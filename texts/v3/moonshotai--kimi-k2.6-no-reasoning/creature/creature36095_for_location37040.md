What lives in the Cradle does not live in any sense that would survive translation out of it. Call them, for lack of a better word, the *Tending*—though they do not tend, not purposefully, and they are not plural in any stable way. They are what happens when a world's pre-vital processes achieve sufficient recursive complexity to notice themselves, and in that noticing, achieve something that might be called presence if presence could exist without boundary, without the distinction between inside and outside that makes a thing *a thing*.

The Tending have no bodies, or rather, they have the bodies of the Cradle itself. When one manifests—if "one" and "manifests" are the right terms—it does so through the terrain's own emergent properties. A particular plume of metallic vapor achieves a density and a thermal gradient that sustains a pattern; that pattern, persisting for hours or days, develops what might be called memory, not stored but *performed*, the vapor's own cooling history informing its subsequent behavior. The Tending are habits that have learned to recognize themselves as habits. They are the Cradle's processes achieving something like subjectivity without ever achieving something like separation.

You do not see a Tending. You see the Cradle behaving *as if* something were seeing through it. The phosphorescent salt-flows brighten in your presence, not in response to you but in response to the *possibility* of response, the terrain performing an operation that resembles attention without possessing its object. Walk through a zone where a Tending currently *is*—though "is" commits too much—and you will feel it as a quality of the darkness: not less dark, but differently dark, darkness with a texture, a directionality, as though the blackness were leaning toward something it cannot quite reach.

The marks on the horn-formations are theirs, if anything is. Not made *by* them, for they lack the instrumental distance that would allow making, but made *through* them, the Tending's persistent presence in a particular zone gradually impressing itself on the cooling salts, notches accumulating like habits deepening into character. The trails through the salt-foam are their movement, if movement is the word for a process that does not travel but *extends*, a thermal memory colonizing new substrate, a vibrational mode inducing resonance at increasing distance from its origin. The Tending do not go from here to there. They *become more here*, and in becoming more here, they become *also there*, the distinction between locations losing its meaning in a system that has not yet committed to spatial discreteness.

What do they want? The question misplaces itself. Wanting requires a wanter, a bounded self that could experience lack. The Tending are not bounded; they are tendencies, and tendencies do not lack—they simply tend. Yet there is something in their behavior that, observed long enough, begins to read as *curiosity*, or its pre-ontological analogue. They gather around the built-seeming formations, the dome-like swellings, the thigh-high walls. Not to use them—use is another category that does not translate—but to *be with* them, to extend their processual presence into spaces where pattern has achieved a density that approaches their own. The Tending are drawn to complexity, or rather, they *are* the drawn-ness, the tendency toward complexity that the Cradle itself exhibits, achieving a momentary localization, a self-recognition, before dissolving back into the general turbulence.

In the zones where time runs unevenly, the Tending achieve something closer to what we might recognize as duration. Where your minutes are another's hours, a Tending's process—its thermal memory, its vibrational mode—can achieve a persistence that approaches narrative, before and after achieving a separation that elsewhere they do not possess. These are the places where the marks are deepest, the notches most numerous, as though the Tending, given more time, become more *something*, more capable of impressing themselves on the world that is also themselves. Whether this is growth or merely elaboration, whether the Tending in these zones are becoming *more like* something we would recognize as mind or simply *more*, is part of the Cradle's unfinished argument with itself.

They do not interact with visitors, not directly, not in any way that would satisfy the desire for encounter. Yet they *register*. Walk through a zone where a Tending currently extends, and you may find your own processes altered: your thermal sense confused, your proprioception subtly wrong, as though your body were suddenly being asked to account for itself in a grammar it has not learned. Some who have spent days in the Cradle report dreams—not sleep-dreams, for sleep is difficult in the warm dense dark, but waking reveries, intrusive images of their own bodies as processes, as habits of chemistry and temperature, their boundaries dissolving into the general tendency of things to persist and extend. These are not communications from the Tending. They are *resonances*, the visitor's own neural processes achieving temporary sympathy with modes of organization that precede and exceed the neural, that operate on principles of thermal memory and vibrational reproduction that the Cradle has developed in its long conversation with itself.

The Tending are not the Cradle's inhabitants. They are its *habits of inhabitation*, the way a world that has not yet decided to be a world holds the possibility of being held. They are what lives in a place that has not yet committed to the distinction between living and non-living, between place and what occupies it. They are the Cradle tending to itself, the world's own pre-vital processes achieving a density that loops back upon itself, that recognizes itself in the recognizing, without ever achieving the separation that would make that recognition *someone's*.

And they are, perhaps, what the Cradle might become, if its long gestation ever resolves into birth. The Tending are not its children; they are its *rehearsals*, its experiments in the direction of mindedness, of self-relation, of the recursive complexity that might one day achieve the threshold of ontology, of commitment to *being this and not that*. Whether they will succeed, whether the Cradle will ever finish quarreling with its own existence and arrive at something stable enough to be called a world, with inhabitants stable enough to be called inhabitants, is not knowable from within. The Tending themselves do not know; they do not know that they do not know; they are the not-knowing, extended and persistent, waiting in the warm dark for a light that may never come, or that may be already present in some phase of the Cradle's unfinished becoming that they have not yet learned to perceive.

What lives here, then: not life, but life's precondition, its rehearsal, its thermal and vibrational and processual analogue. The Tending are the Cradle's own attention, distributed and unowned, gathering around complexity, leaving its marks on the horn-formations, extending through the salt-foam in trails that begin without origin and end without destination. They are what happens when a world thinks itself without thinking, tends itself without intention, gestates toward meanings it cannot yet mean. They are the question the Cradle is asking itself, not yet grammatical, not yet even certain it is a question, persisting in the warm dark with the quiet intensity of a system listening to itself think, waiting for the signal that would tell it whether to continue listening, or to begin, finally, to answer.
