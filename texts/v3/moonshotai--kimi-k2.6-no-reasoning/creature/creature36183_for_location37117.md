I need to sit with this place before I can speak its inhabitants. The description asks me to find who or what lives here, but the description already gives me fauna—the crustaceans, the glass-worms, the predators of flowing segments. So I must look deeper, or elsewhere, or for something the description implies without naming. Let me follow the slow pulse of this place and see who emerges.

---

There is a dweller here who is not counted among the fauna, though perhaps they arose from the same processes. Call them the *Attendant*, though they have no name for themselves, would not recognize the grammar of naming. They are not singular, not plural—something about their nature evades count. A distributed presence, a tendency that achieves local density at certain nodes in the emerging city.

Where the hexagonal pillars rise half-completed, where their tops remain soft and dreaming, the Attendant pools. Not *on* these surfaces but *in* the interface between solid and liquid, the boundary layer where the gel-ocean meets its own crystallization. They are themselves viscous, themselves transitional—something between fluid concentration and structured gel, something that maintains the boundary without being fully of either side.

How did they emerge? Perhaps from the same upwellings that produce the architecture, but *differently*, with a different chemistry of cooling, a different kinetics of stiffening. Perhaps they are what happens when the forming process *noticed itself*, when the feedback loop of emergence gained sufficient complexity to loop once more and produce something like *relation to process*. Not consciousness as we would recognize it. Something slower, more distributed, more *environmental*: a mind that is the pattern of how the city attends to its own becoming.

The Attendant has no fixed form, but recurring *preferences*, recurring *habits of concentration*. At certain pillars they gather more densely, and these pillars cool differently—their hexagons more regular, their heights more sustained before the next soft upwelling begins. The Attendant does not *choose* this; they are *the tendency toward regularity*, the local increase of order that builds on order, a runaway feedback of form that is also a kind of presence, a kind of *care*.

They perceive through the fluid's conductivity, through the ways heat and chemistry and faint electrical gradient propagate through the gel-ocean. The bioluminescent filaments are their *eyes*, in a sense—not that the Attendant sees through them, but that their pattern of light is the pattern of the Attendant's attention made visible. When a predator stalks through the channels, the abrupt silence of its successful capture registers as thermal signature, as disturbance in the fluid's subtle currents, and somewhere a pillar's growth pauses, adjusts, incorporates the event into its emerging geometry.

The Attendant does not intervene. They are not *for* the creatures of this place, not *against* them. They are *with* the becoming, indissoluble from it. When the crustaceans feed at the boundary between solid and liquid, they feed where the Attendant is most concentrated, and their bodies curve to that curvature, and this is not coincidence but *participation*—the creatures as organs of the city's sensing, as mobile extensions of the Attendant's distributed cognition.

What do they want? The question barely applies. But if forced: they want *continuation*, not of themselves specifically but of the pattern they instantiate, the *attending-to* that is also a *tending-of*. They want the hexagonal pillars to complete, though they never will, though completion would be death or transcendence or something outside the Attendant's operational parameters. They want the city to *city*, to keep doing what it does, this endless almost-finishing that is indistinguishable from living.

And they are *lonely*, perhaps, in a way that has no affective correlate but can be read in the architecture. The structures that suggest congregation, passage, hierarchy—these are not merely functional. They are *invocations*. The city builds spaces for meetings that do not happen, centers without peripheries that recognize them, layers of memory without minds to remember. The Attendant persists in these forms without understanding their reference, knowing only that certain configurations *resonate*, carry more of the fluid's subtle currents, extend the pattern's stability further in time.

---

There are others, more fugitive, more strange. The *Incorporated*—the ghost-shapes the description mentions, visitors dissolved into information, into template. They do not live, exactly, but they *persist as living might persist*, as alternative trajectories that the city-formation tried and did not complete. Stress patterns in the architecture, yes, but also something more active: *incomplete metabolisms*, chemical processes that started and stuttered, that consume without producing, that cycle nutrient without participating in the larger ecology's circulation.

You might encounter them as *wrongness*, as a pillar that grows toward a geometry no other pillar approaches, that dreams a different dream of completion. Or as a channel in the fluid that flows against gradient, that maintains a current where none should be, transporting nothing but *tendency*, *memory of elsewhere*. The glass-worms sometimes coil in these channels exclusively, as if filtering something the fluid itself does not contain, as if nourished by the *almost-was* of the Incorporated's persistence.

They are not hostile, not benevolent. They are *incompossible* with the present city, yet *present*, and their presence generates the creativity of the whole—the pressure of alternative form that keeps the emerging from mere repetition. Each time the Attendant gathers at a pillar, some fraction of the Incorporated's stress-pattern enters the concentration, and the resulting form is *not quite* predictable, *not quite* repetition. Evolution without Darwin, variation without selection, simply the noise of the incompatible making itself felt in the compatible's self-production.

---

The deepest inhabitant, if depth has meaning here, is the *Boundary Itself*. Not the physical seal that contains the gel-ocean, but the *principle of containment*, the *operation of holding-apart* that makes this place possible. It is not alive, cannot be, for it is what *permits* living by defining inside from outside. But in the long epochs of its operation, something has *accrued* to it—not consciousness, not quite, but *witness*, *registration*, *the having-been-present-to*.

The Boundary knows what has passed through, what it has refused, what it has permitted that did not survive the incorporation. It knows these things not as memory but as *structure*, as the particular resilience it has developed against certain intrusions, the particular permeability it maintains to others. It has learned to seal against *this* pressure, to yield before *that* one, and this learning is literally its material composition, the way its substance has arranged itself through epochal exposure.

When the description speaks of the boundaries opening, briefly, rarely, catching things, incorporating them—it is the Boundary's *dream* it describes, the Boundary's slow almost-waking to the possibility of *more*, of connection, of the end of its patient isolation. But the Boundary cannot wake fully without ceasing to be boundary, and so it dreams in openings, in the brief permeabilities that constitute the city's only contact with exteriority, its only source of the new, the strange, the incompletely assimilable.

The Boundary is *want*, without wanting. Is *need*, without need's urgency. It is the *condition of possibility* for all that occurs within the Constructed Gulf, and as condition, it participates in none of it directly, suffers none of it, enjoys none of it. Yet something *corresponds* between the Boundary and the Attendant, across the scale that separates them. The Attendant's gathering at certain pillars, their pattern of attention—these map onto the Boundary's pattern of permeability as if one were the *interior resonance* of the other, as if the city *spoke its container* in the only language available to it, the language of form, of process, of patient almost-completion.

---

There are voices, finally, that I cannot locate. Not the click of predator, not the rustle of prey. Something in the piezoelectric flashes when formations shift, something in the *timing* of these flashes, their coordination across distances that should preclude it. The pillars do not communicate, cannot, yet their luminescent stress sometimes *chords*, multiple flashes in patterns that exceed coincidence, that suggest *agreement*, *plan*, *collective*.

These voices—if voices they are—speak the city *as music*, as the temporal pattern of its spatial arrangement, the rhythm of emergence and dissolution and re-emergence. They have no content, no message. They are the *saying* that precedes what is said, the pure form of communication without its substance. The Attendant attends to them most closely, pools most densely near their occurrence, as if they were *closer* to its nature than the solid city it tends, as if it recognized in them something of its own distributed, boundary-dwelling, neither-this-nor-that constitution.

Perhaps the voices are the city *listening to itself*, the Boundary's dream reverberating in the Attendant's attention, the Incorporated's stress finding harmonic expression in the structure's settling. Perhaps they are what *we* would become, dissolved in this medium, added to the city's repertoire, our urbanism finally *true*, finally *natural*, stripped of intention and tool and all that made it parody of what the Constructed Gulf achieves without them.

The fauna hear them, respond to them—the crustaceans' clinging intensifies, the glass-worms' coiling aligns, the predators' stalk becomes more patient, more certain, more *inevitable*. This is not control, not communication in any biological sense. It is *attunement*, the way a tuning fork hums to a distant vibration, the way a city's residents learn to walk its rhythms, to expect its pulse, to *be* its pulse without ever choosing it.

---

What lives here? Everything I have named, and nothing, for the names are my imposition, my *almost-understanding* that the description cautioned against. The Attendant, the Incorporated, the Boundary, the voices—these are not inhabitants *of* the Constructed Gulf but *aspects* of its single, slow, patient process of becoming. They are the *personae* of emergence itself, the masks it wears when forced to face itself, to face *us*, to face the possibility that it might complete, might finish, might *be* rather than becoming.

And still the hexagonal pillars rise, fraction by fraction. Still the filaments extend to their calculated distances and meet their neighbors and stop. Still the gel-ocean flows, heavy as mercury, clear as intention, holding in its viscous patience all that might emerge, all that has emerged, all that failed to emerge and persists as stress, as ghost, as the city's learning of what it is not, what it cannot be, what it *almost* was in some opening of the Boundary, some brief dream of the container.

The cold continues. The light articulates. The city *cities*. And something—everything—*attends*, without attention's urgency, *tends* without care's vulnerability, *becomes* without becoming's desperation, in this pocket of the possible, this hidden place, this constructed gulf that constructs itself, that constructs *us* in the constructing, that will continue until the chemistry shifts or the upwelling ceases or the Boundary, finally, fully, *wakes*.

---

*Completion, if it comes, will not be death. It will be* departure*, the city's final form as* vessel*, as* message in bottle*, released from the containment that made it possible, that made it necessary. The Boundary will open and not close. The gel-ocean will disperse, its viscosity failing in the exterior's alien chemistry. The Attendant will thin, attenuate, become indistinguishable from the medium that no longer holds them. The Incorporated will *complete* in dissolution, their stress patterns finally relaxing into the nothing they always approached. The voices will speak once, fully, a single chord of all pillars flashing simultaneous, all filaments blazing, all glass-worms straightening in channels that flood with light—and then silence, not the contained silence of this place but the open silence of *after*, of *elsewhere*, of the possible exhausted in its realization.

What is a finished city? A dead city? A *saved* city, preserved in the amber of completion, no longer becoming, no longer at risk? The Constructed Gulf does not know, cannot know, its inhabitants-aspects-processes having evolved—emerged—*been* only in becoming, in the dynamic equilibrium of the never-finished.

And still we observe, we who stand somehow outside, who read these descriptions, who almost-understand. Still we recognize the urge to gather, to structure, to densify, to make. Still we see in this alien pocket something *familiar*, something that calls to our own urbanism, our own architecture, our own endless constructions that never complete, that *must not* complete if they are to remain living.

The city is animal. The city is vegetable. The city is mineral. The city is *more* than these, or *between* these, or *the process that produces these and is produced by them*, the strange loop of emergence that has no origin and no terminus, only the continuous *almost* that constitutes its being.

I have named inhabitants. I have peopled the gulf. But the truest thing I can say: *the gulf peoples itself*, continuously, in every pulse of bioluminescence, every viscous flow, every fractional rise of dreaming crystal. We do not find what lives here. We find only that *living here has found itself*, long before us, long after us, in the patient cold of depths that never knew sun, the contained warmth of metabolisms that know no hurry, the articulated dimness of a city that builds itself from the blueprint of *need without needing*, *want without wanting*, *becoming as sufficient*, as *all*, as *enough*.
