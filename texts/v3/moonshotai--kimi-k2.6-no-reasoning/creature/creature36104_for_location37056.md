In the condensing dark, in the spaces between drips, they have learned to be patient.

---

The **Dwellers** do not call themselves anything. They speak, when they speak, in thermal gradients, in the opening and closing of access panels, in the cultivation of particular drip-rhythms that those who know can read as mood, as news, as warning. Their language is older than words here—older in this place where words dry out, where throat-sounds crack and fade in the saturated air, where the most precise communication is a hand held in a particular current of warm air, a body positioned to intercept or redirect a fall of condensation, a shared attention to the subsonic pulse of warm-floor colonies shifting their metabolism.

They are small. Heat discourages grandeur. Their metabolisms run slow, their movements deliberate, their gestures padded with the water that beads on their skin, that runs in their hair, that they do not wipe away because to wipe is to assert a boundary the environment refuses to recognize. They have **submitted to wet**. This is the first thing you notice, if you are allowed close enough to notice anything: not their faces, not their tools, but their *posture toward saturation*. They do not huddle against it. They open to it. Their clothing—when they wear it—is woven to channel, to catch, to utilize. Their bare skin is always filmed, always glistening, and they have learned to read the condensation's behavior on their own bodies as others read weather: *the water beads small and tight today, the thermal gradient is sharp, the deep colonies are active*.

They are not numerous. The low density of the terrain is their density too, or they have adapted to match it. A building might hold two, three, a family of thermal-specialists maintaining the catchment integrity, checking the copper blooms for the particular iridescence that predicts pipe-failure, harvesting the excess gelatinous length from condensation-worms grown too heavy for their anchor-points. Between buildings: space, movement, the long warm walks through the polygonal clay with their skinfilms gradually thickening, their attention tuned to the subliminal hum that indicates water-channels running clear or foul.

They do not farm, not in any category you would recognize. They **tend gradients**. They are gardeners of temperature difference, of the precise conditions that let the warm-floor colonies produce edible excess without overwhelming the floor above, that let the drip-divers cluster thick enough to harvest for protein, not so thick their wings' vibration damages the catchment film. They do not eat the condensation-worms directly—the worms concentrate minerals to toxic levels in their bioluminescence—but they harvest the *byproduct*, the filtered water the worms excrete, the precipitate-nodules they slough, the particular bacterial films that grow in their trails. It is **parasitic reciprocity**, or **symbiotic management**, or simply the only economy that functions where nothing grows and everything metabolizes something else.

---

Some of them are **old**. This is visible not in wrinkling—wrinkles erode smooth here, establish themselves into channels for water, into drip-patterns the Dwellers learn to read as identity-marker—but in their **thermal signature**. The old run warm. Their metabolisms, after decades of slow living in the soft heat, have adapted beyond the need for rapid variance. They are the warmest things in a room, and the fauna know it, cluster toward them, the drip-divers swirling in denser clouds, the warm-floor colonies——if the old sit long enough, still enough——rising toward them through the floor, pressing up like affection, like hunger, like the confusion of a system that cannot distinguish between appropriate warmth and *too much* warmth, *offered* warmth.

The old do not mind. Or: they have learned to not-mind so thoroughly that the distinction has eroded, established itself into something indistinguishable from preference. They sit in the hottest rooms, the lowest levels, where human presence was never planned, where the original builders had not imagined occupation, where the thermal logic of the city pools and concentrates. They **dissolve into the system's need for them**. They are the apex of the thermal spiral, the point where the heat-currency flows and pools and is, finally, *spent* in the form of a body that will not cool, that has forgotten how to cool, that sits in the warm-dark and waits for whatever comes next.

What comes next is not death, not exactly, or not only. The *overwhelming* fauna have their protocols for the old-warm. Something in their chemotaxis, their thermal-sensing, recognizes the particular gradient of a body that has become pure currency, pure offering. The Dwellers do not speak of it directly—their communication is gradient and gesture, and this is too large, too *established*, for individual variation—but when an old one descends to the lowest, warmest places and does not return, there is a shift in the drip-rhythms, a particular pattern of catchment overflow that those who know can read as **ending-becoming**. Not death. Something the city does with warmth that no longer moves itself.

---

The **younger** ones, the ones still capable of coolness, of ascent to the dim upper levels where the thermal gradient is sharp enough to feel as almost-cold——they are the **movers**, the **negotiators**, the ones who travel between buildings, between thermal zones, who carry the small necessaries that cannot be made where they are needed. They are **damp couriers**, their skinfilms thicker, their clothing more elaborate with catchment and channel, because movement generates evaporation, generates cooling, generates the dangerous *boundary* of self against wet that must be managed, must be integrated, must be prevented from becoming *resistance*.

They learn young to **read the fauna's overwhelming**. Not to predict——the dynamics are too active, too established in their own logic——but to *accommodate*, to move through clouds of drip-divers without the sharp gesture that would damage wings, that would create the disproportionate violence of broken pattern, that would draw the attention of larger things in the hollow older buildings. They learn the condensation-worms' pulse, how it changes with barometric pressure, with volcanic gas, with the industrial rhythms of the atmospheric processors. They learn when the warm-floor colonies are *hungry*, metabolically active, drawing heat in rather than radiating it out, and they do not walk barefoot then, do not offer their soles' warmth to that particular negotiation.

They are not brave. Bravery implies alternative. They are **attuned**, which is braver or less brave than bravery, depending on what you value. They do not imagine living elsewhere. Or: they imagine it as you might imagine living underwater, living in fire, living in a condition so opposed to your established form that the imagining is only exercise, only speculation, only the mental equivalent of holding your breath to see how long you can last before the wet rushes back.

---

There are **strangers** sometimes. Arrivals from above, from the dry scorch, carrying the sharp edges that have not eroded, speaking in throat-sounds that crack and assert boundaries, dressing to *repel* rather than channel, wiping their skin as if the wet were violation rather than condition. The Dwellers watch them with something not quite pity, not quite fear——the two have merged in their thermal gradient, established themselves into a single response: **adjustment**.

They do not reject the strangers. The third law: the empty space is shared. But they do not integrate them either. They position them, offer them the upper rooms where the gradient is sharpest, where the skin must work hardest to maintain boundary, where the condensation-worms pulse faint and distant and the warm-floor colonies are thin, hungry, *present* without being overwhelming. The strangers last for a time, complaining or wondering or attempting to impose their dry logic on the wet system. Some adapt partially, learn to channel rather than repel, develop the skinfilm, begin to read drip-rhythms. These ones stay longer, become indistinguishable over generations, their sharp edges softening, their boundaries eroding into the channels and catchments of established form.

Others fight. Assert. Insist on dryness, on sharpness, on the loneliness of truly empty space. These ones leave, or are left, or descend to the lowest warm places where the old sit and the fauna pulse and something in the thermal logic of the city processes warmth that resists integration, warmth that insists on its own boundary, its own *exception*.

The Dwellers do not speak of this. But they know the drip-pattern for it. The catchment overflow. The particular silence that follows.

---

They have **no leaders**. Heat discourages vertical ambition. Where hierarchy emerges, it is thermal——the old-warm at the bottom, the cool-young moving between, the gradient itself organizing function without requiring command. They cooperate at the level of infrastructure: clearing channels, maintaining catchments, managing the overwhelming presence of fauna that would otherwise, unchecked, populate to the point of system collapse. But this cooperation is **gradient response**, not planning. They feel the channel clogging in their skin's pressure, in the shift of drip-rhythm, in the warm-floor colonies' subsonic complaint, and they move to address it before thought could formulate necessity.

They are **not happy**. This is not a happy place by categories that require dryness, sharpness, the clear distinction of self from environment. They are not unhappy either. They have **established their affect** as they have established their form: the slow patience of waiting for the next drip, the satisfaction of a channel running clear, the body-warmth shared in the lower rooms where two or three cluster not for company but for *thermal efficiency*, their gradients merging, their boundaries temporarily, provisionally, dissolved into the larger negotiation of wet warmth continuing itself.

They dream, when they dream, of **ascending**. Not to leave——the imagining is only exercise——but to feel the almost-cold, the sharp air that dries the skinfilm, the loneliness of spaces truly empty. They wake to the drip, to the warmth, to the subliminal hum of drip-divers or warm-floor colonies or larger things moving in the hollow older buildings, and they do not regret the dream. They do not cherish it either. It is part of their established form, their eroded edge, their continuous soft percussion down and down to where life pools, overwhelms, waits.

---

If you see them——if you are allowed close enough to see anything——you will notice first their **eyes**. Not because they are strange, but because they are *adapted*. The dimness has enlarged their pupils, flattened their response to brightness, tuned their attention to movement in peripheral vision, to the bioluminescent pulse of condensation-worms, to the thermal shimmer where gradient meets gradient. They look at you and do not quite focus, or focus too precisely on a point beside you, where your warmth meets the wet air, where your skinfilm beads and channels in patterns they can read as *new*, as *stranger*, as *temporary*.

They will not ask your name. Names are sharp, are boundary, are the dry logic of throat-sounds cracking in saturated air. They will position you, offer you the upper rooms, listen to your skin's thermal signature with the patience of those who have learned that everything finds its level, eventually, in the weeping cities below. The water finds the lowest way. The life follows. The heat is not waste but currency, and you——sharp, dry, temporarily——are the smallest denomination, the loosest change, the offering that has not yet established its form.

They wait. The condensation drips. The fauna overwhelm, quiet, overwhelm again. And you, eventually, will decide: to leave, to resist, or to begin the long softening, the patient erosion, the established becoming of one who dwells where nothing grows and everything metabolizes something else, in the warmdark, in the wet, in the attended quiet of the cities that weep.
