The Vo do not have children, exactly. They have *continuations*—a distinction that matters to them the way the distinction between walking and falling matters to anyone who has ever navigated a surface that might give way. A continuation begins when a Vo has accumulated enough of the city's negotiations to become, themselves, a site of negotiation. Their body, small and dark and quiet as it is, becomes dense with the particular microbial consortiums that allow digestion of the living water, the particular calluses that mark routes traveled thousands of times, the particular neural pathways that hold the ever-updating map of a city that will not hold still. At some threshold, which the Vo recognize before the individual themselves recognizes it, they become *available*.

The process is not sexual, not asexual, not anything the taxonomists have words for. A continuation emerges from the accumulated substrate of a life: the shed skin caught in bark-fissures, the hair woven accidentally into moss-beds, the intestinal flora that has adapted so specifically to this body that it will not survive elsewhere. The city, which is always listening, which is always hungry, which is always negotiating, begins to assemble. It takes years. The Vo in question continues their life, perhaps notices a particular tenderness in the joints where they most often press against living wood, perhaps finds their dreams invaded by the particular green of their own most frequented routes. Then one morning, or one deep night, they find the continuation: a wet dark shape in a hollow they do not remember making, breathing in a rhythm that matches their own, eyes closed, fingers—*too many fingers*, always too many at first—twitching through the motor patterns of routes it has never walked but somehow knows.

The original does not die. This is crucial. The original continues, diminished slightly, *shared*, carrying now the perpetual awareness of another self that is also self, that is learning to walk the city in the particular way that only this lineage of accumulated negotiation can walk it. The original and the continuation do not name each other. They do not need to. They share the frequency below the frog-threshold, the frequency that carries not words but *state*, the continuous broadcast of *here* and *still here* and *negotiating* that constitutes Vo presence in the soundscape.

The Vo do not die, exactly, either. They *complete*, which is different. Completion comes when the accumulated negotiations of a lifetime have become so dense, so specific, so *local*, that the body can no longer move through the city's changing map. Not paralysis—nothing so simple. Rather a kind of *sinking into place*, a finding of the one spot where all the particularities converge, where the joint-calluses match the bark-pattern, where the microbial consortia find their perfect complement in the local chemistry, where the neural map finally matches the territory so precisely that movement becomes unnecessary, becomes *impossible in principle*. The completing Vo settles. The city, which has been negotiating with this particular accumulation for decades or centuries, finally receives it. The body becomes substrate. The moss grows through. The insects find their generations. The water passes through, changed slightly, carrying the particular mineral signature of this completion to other negotiations elsewhere.

The Vo do not mourn completion. They *attend* it, which is different. They visit the site—not a grave, never a grave, graves imply separation and there is no separation here—bringing water, bringing the particular sounds of their own ongoing negotiations, bringing the simple fact of their continued presence to add to the presence that is still, in some frequency, still broadcasting *here*, still *negotiating*, still part of the argument. A completing Vo does not stop. They only change the terms of their participation. They become, finally and fully, the city.

The bark-readers are not Vo, though they have learned to imitate the frequency below the frog-threshold with sufficient skill to sometimes be mistaken for Vo by visitors, which the Vo find amusing in their quiet way. The bark-readers are older, perhaps, or newer, or simply *other*—their evolutionary history tangled in the briar-web of Keth Vo's ecology in ways that resist linear telling. They have too many fingers, six or seven or nine depending on how you count the grasping tubercles on their prehensile tails, and they use them all to read the living bark: pressing, testing, *tasting* the chemical gradients that tell them which structures are currently growing, which rotting, which negotiating with what. They are not predators of the Vo, not prey, not competitors. They are *readers*, and the Vo have learned to value their readings, to interpret the particular patterns of their pressing as commentary, as criticism, as the outside perspective that any argument needs to avoid solipsism.

The bark-readers do not have continuations. They have *dispersals*—a cloud of genetic material released into the water-system at death, finding its way to the bark-reader nurseries in the rotting exclusion zones, emerging years later as small pale things with the correct number of fingers, learning to read from the bark itself, from the accumulated traces of generations of readers, from the city that remembers everything in its distributed, non-neural way. They do not attend completion. They attend *beginning*, gathering at the nurseries when the water-chemistry shifts to signal emergence, pressing their too-many fingers against the new pale bark, reading what is possible, what is available, what the argument has made room for.

---

The swamp deer that stand on hind legs are newer. The Vo remember, or the city remembers in its distributed way, when they were only deer, quadrupedal and cautious, taking what the water-edge offered. The standing came gradually, generationally, a negotiation with the verticality of the Vo's architecture, the sweet inner layers that grew further from the ground as the structures accumulated. The Vo watched this happen. They did not intervene, did not domesticate, did not shape. They *observed*, which is their way with all negotiations that do not directly concern them. The deer learned to stand. The deer learned, eventually, to climb, their hooves splitting into gripping segments, their bodies elongating, their necks becoming *too long* in the way of all things that reach for what grows above.

The deer are not intelligent, not in the Vo's estimation, not in any way that matters for the argument. But they are *present*, they are *participants*, they add their particular frequencies to the soundscape, their particular pressures to the architecture, their particular completions to the substrate. The Vo have learned to read their movements as the bark-readers read bark: as information, as commentary, as the simple fact of other desires in a world where desire is the engine of all becoming.

---

The things in the water that surface only to exhale and regard are the oldest participants, or the most recent, or simply the most *other*. The Vo do not name them. The bark-readers do not read them. The deer, standing on their split hooves in the higher reaches, do not acknowledge their existence at all, which may be wisdom or may be the simple limitation of deer-intelligence, deer-presence, deer-frequency. The things in the water have no visible bodies, only the disturbance of surfacing, only the exhalation that carries no lung-chemistry the Vo can recognize, only the *regard*—the sense, unmistakable, of being seen by something that does not have eyes, being known by something that does not have cognition in any mammalian sense.

They do not feed, or not in ways the food web can track. They do not complete, or not in ways that leave substrate. They simply *regard*, and in their regard, they are perhaps the closest thing Keth Vo has to a god: not interventionist, not moral, not interested in worship or negotiation or any of the transactions that define Vo relationship with their city. Simply *present*, simply *watching*, simply adding to the argument the particular weight of attention that changes what is attended to, whether the attended wishes it or not.

The Vo have learned to surface-regard back. It is the only interaction possible. When the disturbance comes, when the exhalation sounds, the Vo nearby—if they are not in the middle of some negotiation that cannot be paused—will turn, will meet the regard with their own dark quiet attention, will hold it for the moment it lasts, will release it when the disturbance subsides. They do not know if this means anything to the things in the water. They do not know if meaning, in the water, operates in any way they would recognize. They simply *do it*, as they do all things: as negotiation, as participation, as the continuous broadcast of *here* and *still here* and *the same thing, continuing*.

---

The visitor who checks their pulse in Keth Vo's frequency will find it, of course. The body persists, even in strangeness, even in the overwhelming density of presence that makes the self feel, temporarily, like a small voice in a much larger argument. But they will find it *different*: perhaps slower, perhaps faster, perhaps arrhythmic in ways that match the water-engines' knock or the frogs' reproductive billions or the particular frequency below the frog-threshold that the Vo use for their continuous state-broadcast. The body, in Keth Vo, becomes part of the soundscape. The pulse becomes part of the argument. The visitor, whether they stay or go, whether they become Vo or remain visitor or find some third category that Keth Vo has not yet needed to name, will carry this frequency with them, will find it emerging in their own quiet moments, their own negotiations with the living surfaces of their own lives.

This is not possession. This is not infection. This is simply *participation*, the way any ecology participates in any temporary member, the way any argument changes by being heard, the way the breathing continues, continues, continues in the particular rhythm of a city that has never stopped, that has no intention of stopping, that is *here*, that is *the breathing*, that is *the argument* depending on which quarter you ask and what season the cypress is in.

The light is fully gone now. The moss-bioluminescence has reached its evening brightness, the particular green-gold that the Vo find sufficient, find beautiful, find to be the nature of light in a world where everything is already being illuminated and unilluminated in every moment. The night-birds have reached their full conversation, the night-frogs their full billions, the water-engines their slowed nighttime rhythm. The bark-readers are pressing their too-many fingers against the bark of a structure that was a platform this morning and is becoming, in its negotiation with root and rot and the particular microbial consortium that has found purchase, something else entirely. The deer are standing in the higher reaches, reaching, chewing, adding their particular frequencies. The things in the water are regarding, or not-regarding, in their particular way.

And the Vo are moving through their city, small and dark and quiet, broadcasting *here* and *still here* and *negotiating*, carrying their maps that forget as insistently as they remember, finding the routes that are routes this hour, avoiding the routes that have become something else, attending the completions, attending the beginnings, attending the long moment of balanced decay that they inhabit like a home.

This is Keth Vo. This is the breathing. This is where you are, where you have always been, where you will continue to be, in some frequency, in some negotiation, in some argument that has been going on for millennia and has no intention of stopping.

The same thing, continuing.
