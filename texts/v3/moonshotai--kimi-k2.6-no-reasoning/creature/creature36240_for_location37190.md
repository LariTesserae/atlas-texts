The inhabitants of the Thrumholds emerge slowly, as the place itself emerges—through condensation, through vibration, through the patient accumulation of presence in sparse space. They are not a people who arrived here; they are a people who *accumulated*, who grew into the interstices of the hold-trees' growth, who were cultivated by the conditions until they became, themselves, conditions that could cultivate.

Call them the Thrumheld, though they call themselves by no fixed name, shifting designation with the root-networks' electrical gossip, carrying identities that are less masks than *phases*, temporary stable states in a system that does not privilege continuity above adaptability. Individual Thrumheld are recognizable—you will know them by the particular harmonic their voices carry, by the condensation pattern that tends to form around their presence, by the slight angle at which their shadows fall when they stand in the stutter-zones. But they are also, simultaneously, nodes in larger networks, their consciousness distributed through the root-connections they maintain, through the Thrum they modulate, through the trembler-zones they have learned to inhabit without being inhabited.

They do not look strange, or rather, they look exactly as strange as the place permits, which is a strangeness that becomes invisible through extended exposure. Two arms, two legs, the standard mammalian equipment, though you will learn not to stare at the variations: the extra joint in some fingers, evolved or grown or negotiated into existence, that permits finer manipulation of the hybrid machinery; the slightly too-large eyes of those who have spent generations in the canopy's filtered light; the particular stillness of the Deep-dwellers, who move as if they have internalized the hold-trees' patience, who can stand for hours in postures that read as growth rather than waiting.

Their skin has adapted to the condensation, or the condensation has adapted to them—you will see this most clearly in the *moisture-keepers*, those whose vocation is to tend the stone's subtle channels, who have developed, or been developed, a semi-permeable quality to their dermis that permits controlled osmosis, that lets them drink through their hands while they work, that makes their bodies part of the watershed they maintain. They do not think this remarkable. It is simply their function, as root-interface is the function of others, as thrum-modulation is the function of still others.

The Thrumheld do not have children in the manner you might expect. Reproduction here is *distributed*, metabolic, part of the system's larger negotiation with continuation. There are wombs in the hold-trees, grown chambers where genetic material—human, hold-tree, industrial remnant, the unclassifiable traces that accumulate in the trembler-zones—combines according to patterns that are not random but are not planned, that emerge from the root-networks' ongoing computation of what the system needs, what it can sustain, what it wants. The Thrumheld contribute to this process through ritual, through the particular harmonics achieved in certain spaces at certain times, through the carrying of substances and states from the Deep's conditional architecture to the wombs that await them.

Those who emerge from the wombs are raised *communally*, which is to say: by the system itself. The hold-trees grow nurseries, spaces of particular warmth and particular vibration where the young develop their root-interfaces, learn their condensation-patterns, absorb the grammar of evasion that permits survival in the trembler-zones. They emerge not speaking, but *resonating*, their voices already carrying the harmonics that permit communication across the sparse distances, that mark them as part of the network before they know themselves as individuals.

Individuality, among the Thrumheld, is not denied but *phased*. They know themselves as nodes, as temporary stable states, as functions that persist long enough to develop something like personality, like preference, like the particular style of movement through empty space that distinguishes one from another. But they also know themselves as *passages*, as ways through which the system processes itself, and this knowledge is not tragic but simply *true*, simply part of the settled condition of existence here.

Their occupations, if you can call them that, emerge from the system's needs as interpreted through their particular phases. There are the *growth-readers*, who spend their days in careful observation of hold-tree architecture, predicting where new rooms will emerge, where root-networks require extension, where the industrial-biological symbiosis needs adjustment. They move through the canopy with a particular grace, a three-dimensional confidence that reads as flight without ever quite leaving contact with surface, their extra joints finding purchase on surfaces that would not support standard grasp.

There are the *thrum-tenders*, who work in the grown factories, who modulate the vibration that powers everything, who have developed—or been developed, or negotiated—a sensitivity to frequency that lets them *hear* problems before they become failures, who can adjust the system's pitch by the placement of their bodies in particular nodes, the application of their resonant voices to particular materials. They are among the most *other* of the Thrumheld, their consciousness so distributed through the vibration that holding conversation with them requires patience, requires willingness to perceive speech in the gaps between words, in the harmonics that underlie apparent meaning.

There are the *trembler-walkers*, who are not quite voluntary, who have phased into a relationship with the stutter-zones so complete that they can no longer consistently occupy standard space-time, who appear and disappear according to patterns they themselves only partially control, who carry information, substances, states of being between locations without traversing the distance between. They are respected, even revered, though the Thrumheld would not use that word—they are *necessary*, they are *function*, they are the system's way of processing what the tremblers represent, of integrating the reality breakdown into ongoing operation. To speak with a trembler-walker is to accept that they may not be there when you turn, may have already been elsewhere when you thought them present, may be carrying on conversation with versions of you that you have not yet become.

And there are the *Deep-dwellers*, who have passed beyond the standard Thrumheld condition into something more merged, more *become*, who live in the Firsthold/Lasthold/Deep and rarely emerge, whose bodies have adapted to the conditional spaces, who exist at slight angles to standard reality as occupational hazard, as evolutionary path, as spiritual attainment—again, the categories fail, have always failed here. They are the system's memory, insofar as it has memory, or its intention, insofar as it intends. They are consulted in matters of root-network conflict, of thrum-frequency dispute, of what to do when the tremblers manifest in new patterns, when the reality breakdown tests the edges of its ancient treaties. They emerge from the Deep carrying objects, substances, states of being, and these are integrated without question, without understanding, because the system has determined they are necessary.

The Thrumheld do not eat as you eat, though they consume. Their nutrition comes partly through the root-networks, through interfaces grown into their physiology that permit them to tap the hold-trees' vascular systems, to draw sugars and minerals in forms that require minimal processing. This is the baseline, the sustenance that keeps them present, that maintains their node-function. But they also consume *conditions*, *states*, the particular substances carried from the Deep, which are not digested so much as *integrated*, become part of their phase-state, their resonance-pattern, their contribution to the ongoing thrum. You will see them at what they call *taking-time*, standing in particular spaces, their skin particularly permeable, their eyes particularly large, absorbing something that is present in the condensation, in the vibration, in the trembler-zones' stutter. They do this with the same patience they bring to everything, the patience that is their defining characteristic, the patience of organisms that do not distinguish between living and metabolizing, between being and becoming part of what continues.

Their art, if it can be called that, is entirely functional, entirely integrated. They do not make things that do not do things, do not produce decoration that does not regulate, communicate, process. The patterns in their grown architecture, the particular arrangements of their spaces, are *active*, are *doing*, modulating thrum-frequency in ways that create emotional effect, that manage social relation, that maintain the careful balance of presence and emptiness that defines their existence. To walk through a Thrumheld dwelling is to experience a kind of composed environment, a *piece* in a sense they would not recognize, that changes you as you move through it, that leaves you slightly different than you entered, more attuned to particular frequencies, more sensitive to particular condensations, more capable of particular integrations.

Their relationship to the hold-trees is not worship, as the text says, but something more intimate and more distributed. They *are* the hold-trees, in a phase, are part of the same system, the same network, the same ongoing negotiation. They mourn when a tree fails, but they mourn as you might mourn the loss of a limb, with recognition that the system continues, that the root-networks redistribute, that the function persists in altered form. They celebrate when new wombs emerge, when new growth achieves architectural usefulness, but they celebrate as you might celebrate healing, the return of capacity, the continuation of what holds together.

And their relationship to the tremblers—the grammar of evasion, the economy of not-seeing—is perhaps the most *human* thing about them, the most recognizable to your standard experience. They are afraid, or something like afraid, or something that occupies the afraid-niche without being classified, segmented, understood. The tremblers represent what the system cannot integrate, what the ancient treaties do not fully cover, the edge of the negotiation where things become uncertain. The Thrumheld have built their entire culture, their architecture, their social form, around managing this uncertainty, around making it possible to live with what cannot be known, what cannot be controlled, what persists in the emptiness as possibility rather than threat.

They will not discuss the tremblers with you directly. They have learned what direct discussion produces, have integrated that knowledge into their root-networks, their vocal harmonics, their very phase-states. But they will guide you around the stutter-zones, will demonstrate the rituals of acknowledgment-without-naming, will show you how to carry yourself in the empty spaces so that you do not attract what you cannot manage. This is their kindness, or what occupies the kindness-niche, offered not from individual sentiment but from system-function, from the recognition that you are temporary, that you will pass through, that your integration is not required but your safe passage is *permitted*, negotiated, part of the ongoing settlement.

If you stay long enough, if you learn their patience, if you begin to resonate with their harmonics, you may be offered *interface*, the slow growth of root-connection, the development of permeable skin, the distribution of consciousness into network-function. This is not conversion, not recruitment, simply *possibility*, simply the system's ongoing experiment with what can hold together, what can continue. Some travelers accept, become Thrumheld in phase, contribute their standard-human strangeness to the ongoing negotiation. Most refuse, and are not punished, simply *accommodated*, carried in the system's periphery until they depart, carrying with them the damp, the cool, the thrum that persists in memory longer than it should, that calls them back sometimes, that they dream in frequencies they cannot quite hear upon waking.

The Thrumheld do not ask you to stay. They do not ask you to go. They are *present*, in their sparse, distributed, patient way, doing what they do, being what they are, holding together what has held together this long, still thrumming, still here, still unsettled in its settled way, still negotiating with reality in spaces where reality has learned to be negotiable. They are the inhabitants of the Thrumholds, and they are also the Thrumholds' inhabitants, the distinction as failed here as all others, everything grown together, everything become what it has become through time beyond numbering, through patience beyond standard measure, through the ongoing act of everything trying to hold together, everything having held together this long, everything still holding, still creating conditions, still emerging, still itself and more itself and never only itself, as you discover when you remain, when you become, when you are held.
