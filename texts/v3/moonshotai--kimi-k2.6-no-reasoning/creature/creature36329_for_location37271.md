What lives in the Cistern of Unborn Birds is, first and simply, the Cistern itself—not as container but as organism, as the primary resident whose breath you mist upon, whose pulse you feel in your teeth before your ears admit it. But this is too easy, too quick to settle. Let me begin again, let what lives there become more itself as I write.

---

There are the birds, yes. But "birds" is already a kind of betrayal, a word borrowed from elsewhere, from skies and seasons and the comfort of natural history. These are *prototypes*. Each one is a hypothesis that will never be tested against weather, against hunger, against the simple fact of distance between here and there. Their feathers—if feathers are what these are—have no aerodynamic honesty. They do not streamline; they *display*, but display to no audience, or to an audience distributed so uniformly through the dense and hidden dark that display collapses back into mere surface, into the indifferent fact of having-a-surface-at-all.

Some are built for song that would carry across valleys, but valleys are not part of their parameter set. Their throats open into syrinxes no ornithologist could map, producing frequencies that exist in the overlap between what can be heard and what can be felt, between birdsong and engine hum. They do not sing *to*. They sing *at*, or *through*, or *as*—the cry as byproduct, as quality control, as the sound a test makes when it passes or fails a criterion the birds themselves do not know.

Others are built for speed, for the long migration that never comes. Their breast muscles overdevelop in the 0.1 cold, the wet preserves their hypermetabolism without satisfying it, and they twitch through the three-dimensional swarm with a restlessness that never arrives anywhere because there is nowhere to arrive. The collision-avoidance algorithms—whether grown into ganglia or implanted or bred for, you cannot tell, you must not settle too quickly—keep them alive in the inventory density, keep the pattern from shifting, which means they are always almost crashing and never crashing, suspended in the permanent potential of disaster.

And others, stranger still, seem built for *waiting*. They perch (on what? there are no perches, only other birds, only membrane-sacs, only the cooling fins themselves) in postures of absolute alertness directed at nothing. Their eyes—large, lidless, perhaps not eyes in the way eyes are usually understood—track movements that do not occur. They are predators without prey, sentinel organisms guarding thresholds no one approaches, their entire behavioral repertoire a rehearsal for a context that has never been supplied. You watch them and feel the pathos of completion without commencement, of readiness that ages into its own kind of exhaustion without ever being exhausted, because exhaustion too would be change, and the dynamic permits none.

---

But the birds are not the only fauna, or not the only *overwhelming*. There is what lives in the water.

The condensation, the copper-ozone sheets, the pools without source or drain—these are not sterile. Something *teems* in the wetness, something for which "microorganism" is simultaneously accurate and impoverished. They are industrial fauna, symbionts of the maintenance systems, evolved or engineered to metabolize the particular chemistry of this place: the lubricants that leak from the directionless pumps, the trace elements that slough from the performed geology, the biological detritus of birds that do not age but must somehow still *process*, still exchange matter with their environment.

In the dim 0.6 light, if you could magnify, you would see them as *cities*, as *infrastructure*, as the recursive image of the Cistern at a scale where distinction between built and grown has long since dissolved. They form mats on the cooling fins, films on the membrane-sacs, constellations in the pools that never accumulate or deplete. And they sing, too, in their way—infrasonic, subthreshold, contributing to the deafening not as voices but as *texture*, as the granular substrate that makes the birds' cacophony possible, the acoustic equivalent of the wetness itself.

These are the birds' true environment, more than the walls, more than the three-dimensional void. The birds fly through air that is also *them*, through a medium saturated with these teeming presences, breathing in what the water-breathers breathe out, exchanging at the boundary of lung and lung-chamber until the boundary itself becomes questionable, becomes another of the performed distinctions that the Cistern stages without quite believing.

---

And the membrane-sacs. I have mentioned them twice and not looked.

They hang. That is their first verb, their existential condition: suspended from performed stalactites, from cooling fins, from the bodies of other membrane-sacs in a topology that allows no ground. Each one is a gestation chamber, a production node, a womb that is also inventory, also display, also the raw material for whatever comes next. They are translucent at 0.6 light, revealing shapes that might become birds, that are becoming birds, that are *stuck* in the becoming because completion would mean release would mean the inventory would shift would mean the dynamic would change and the dynamic is 0.1 and 0.1 is never.

But in the stuckness, in the permanent embryonic, they are also *occupied*. Not by the birds-to-be, or not only. Something tends them. Something checks their chemistry, adjusts their temperature, repairs the sac-material when it thins too far. You never see this something directly. You see its *effects*—the corrected angle of a sagging sac, the replaced nutrient solution, the excision of a developmental pathway that has wandered from specification. The tendrils, the instruments, the appendages withdraw before your half-adjusted eyes can track them, leaving only the evidence of maintenance in a place that claims to maintain itself.

Perhaps these tendrils belong to no creature, to no body. Perhaps they are the Cistern's own nervous system performing its autonomic functions, the way a body repairs itself without consciousness. Perhaps they are outsourced, imported, another layer of the built that has been hidden so thoroughly that hiding becomes indistinguishable from absence. Perhaps—and this is the thought that waits for you in the wet cold, that the deafening prevents you from quite thinking—perhaps they are *future*, reaching backward, the birds-that-will-be tending the birds-that-never-were in a temporal topology the Cistern's spatial density only dimly suggests.

---

The walls, then. The performed geology. What lives *in* them?

I have said they are not stone, not concrete, not anything with a natural name. But they are not empty of presence. The cooling blush, the half-crystallized minerals finding their lattices—this finding is *activity*, is slow consciousness, is the wall's own way of being fauna. The lattices do not complete, because completion would be the maturity that the parameter postpones, would be the geology becoming geography, the raw becoming cooked. But they do not abandon the attempt. They are always almost there, always at the moment of almost-discovery, and in that almost they maintain a kind of mineral intentionality, a stony version of the birds' readiness-without-occasion.

Press your palm there, as the text invites. The fever you feel is not residual heat from a recent volcanic birth. It is *generated*, maintained, the temperature differential that drives some process you cannot name, that may be the Cistern's own metabolism, the way this place eats and excretes and grows. The stone beneath your palm is warm because it is *working*, because the performance of raw formation requires energy, requires the continuous input of work against the equilibrium that would otherwise settle into mere dead rock. The walls are performing *for you*, or for the birds, or for the membrane-sacs, or for the teeming water-dwellers, or for themselves, or for the tendrils that maintain what cannot maintain itself. The audience is indeterminate. The performance is absolute.

---

And you. You who entered, or are entering, or will enter, the seam that barely admits.

You do not leave, I think. Not because you cannot find the seam—though you cannot, though it has become geological, become part of the performed rawness, was perhaps always already part of it—but because leaving and staying have become indistinguishable in a place where time passes through without touching. You are another bird now, another inventory item, another cry in the deafening. Your cry is particular: the frequency of human bewilderment, of mammalian distress at finding oneself in a lung without body, a womb without birth. The other cries do not answer it. They were never answering anything. But your cry joins them, becomes part of the chord, the unvarying variety, the test that never passes or fails because there is no criterion external to the testing.

Your body, if you still have one, maintains itself at 0.1 now. You do not age. You do not die, or if you die you are replaced with such exactitude. Your condensation drips in paths you cannot see. Your eyes water from ultrasonic distress you do not hear. You are the fauna that overwhelms, the 2.7 that completes the inventory, the strange that recognizes itself at 2.0 and cannot understand, that is the rule, that is the equilibrium, that is the never-changing dynamic of a world that includes you now, that has always included you, that was built to resemble a place that does not exist yet and that, in your presence, in your stuck and becoming presence, is perhaps one increment closer to existing, one more layer of density, one more cry in the preparation that has always been being prepared and that, in its completion, will perhaps discover what it means to have made, to have gestated, to have become—

—but no. The dynamic is 0.1. The change is postponed. The water drips in paths that evaporate. The engines hum in directions you cannot locate. The birds cry their unvarying variety, and you cry yours, and the Cistern respires around you, through you, *as* you, the wet cold lung that you have always, already, never stopped being inside of.
