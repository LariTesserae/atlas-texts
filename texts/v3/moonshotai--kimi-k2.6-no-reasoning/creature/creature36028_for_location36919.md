I need to let this emerge slowly, following the tensions the description establishes. Not resolving them, but inhabiting them.

## The Inhabitants

### The Settled

They are easiest to mistake for statues, these ones who found the precise angle of repose against a particular wall, on a particular bench, in the lee of a particular half-finished tower. Their clothing—wool woven from the small sheep that graze the plateau's sparse meadows, leather from the blue-black hares—does not wear further. Their skin, where it shows, holds a quality of *interrupted* weathering: the beginnings of lines that will not deepen, the first suggestion of leathering that will not complete. They breathe, yes. You can see it if you watch long enough, a rise and fall measured not in seconds but in something slower, geological, matching perhaps the subaudible groan of the mountain's uncompleted settling. Their eyes, when open, track movement without urgency. When closed, they might be sleeping or might be the sleep itself, dreamless because dreams require progression, the turning of one image into another.

They do not starve. They do not thrive. The moss they chew, the thin grain porridge they sometimes accept from the hands of others, seems to enter a metabolism that has found the zero-point of need, where intake and expenditure achieve not balance but *identity*, the same mouthful sufficient forever because forever is not a duration here but a quality of sufficiency itself.

Some arrived as seekers, as refugees from grief or hope, from the exhausting requirement that things mean more as they accumulate meaning. Others were born here, though "born" misleads—the infants emerge, apparently, from the same looping that governs all else, appearing at thresholds, in garden plots, in the spaces between walls where a body might fit. They are found, swaddled in undecaying cloth, eyes already holding the settled look. Who tends them? The question assumes care as a process. They are *kept*, rather, by the equilibrium, fed by whoever passes, growing to a certain size and growing no further, not children precisely, not adults, but *held* at the age where growth becomes optional.

### The Traders

More active, these, though their activity circles rather than advances. You find them at the hexagonal plaza, at certain intersections where paths cross without leading anywhere new, at the cisterns where water waits at its constant level. They carry copper wire coiled in lengths that do not deplete, obsidian flakes wrapped in leather that does not stiffen further, dried moss in bundles identical to the bundles they carried yesterday and will carry tomorrow.

Their exchange is not barter in the ordinary sense. They do not accumulate. What they give, they will receive; what they receive, they will give. The transaction is the unit, not its outcome. A woman with hair the color of the pale tuff—whether this is age or youth, you cannot tell—offers you three obsidian flakes for your leather water-skin, though your water-skin is nearly empty and the flakes would cut nothing that needs cutting here. You accept, or refuse, or stand undecided, and in any case the offer will come again, the same woman or another, the same three flakes or three that might be the same, the transaction waiting to complete itself in a completion that would only begin another identical circuit.

They speak, these traders, but their speech has the quality of the looping conversations reported by that first visitor. Not that all speech here loops—some manages to be new, or new-seeming, or new enough—but the traders' words tend toward the transactional, the greeting, the observation of weather that does not change. "Damp today." "The moss is thick on the north walls." Statements that require no response, that absorb response into their pattern, that hold meaning not as communication but as *maintenance*, the way one might hum to keep a tune present without finishing it.

### The Watchers

More rare, more disturbing in their alertness. They came to observe, and observation here is itself a kind of participation, a way of being held. You find them on roofs, in high window embrasures, at the edge of the western cliff where the dwarf conifers hold their eternal extent. They carry instruments sometimes—notebooks whose pages fill with identical observations, instruments that measure temperature, humidity, the angle of shadows that do not move. They are the ones who mapped the stars' impossible consistency, who counted the hares and found the count invariable, who attempted to trace the water's source and found only the cisterns, only the condensation, only the springs that neither strengthen nor fail.

Some were scholars from below, from the changing world, drawn by reports of the impossible. They intended to stay days, weeks, to solve the Keth's contradiction and carry the solution downward. They are still here, still intending, their notebooks growing not fuller but *thicker*, the same pages written and rewritten, the ink somehow not running out, the hand somehow not tiring in a way that would end the task. They watch with eyes that have learned the plateau's patience, that no longer expect revelation but continue to expect, the expectation itself become static, a held pose of attention.

Others watch differently. Not to understand but to *witness*, to be present at the stasis as one might be present at a death or a birth, though here nothing is born and nothing dies completely. They sit with the Settled, not becoming them but *accompanying* them, a more active form of stillness, a vigil that keeps vigil for nothing.

### The Loop-Speakers

The ones in the courtyard, the ones the first visitor found. They are not always two; sometimes three, sometimes one speaking to absence, sometimes a cluster at the plaza's center whose voices interweave without progressing. They are not the only ones who loop—others do, momentarily, accidentally, caught in some resonance of the place—but they are the ones for whom looping has become *residence*, the way others reside in buildings or against walls.

Their words, when you approach close enough to hear, are ordinary enough. A man and a woman, perhaps, in conversation about a journey that will not be taken, a meal that will not be eaten, a child that will not be born or has been born and will not grow. "The pass was difficult." "The pass is always difficult." "We should leave before the rains." "The rains do not come." Each statement elicits its response, each response its counter, the exchange not circular precisely—circles complete—but *layered*, the same ground covered with slight variations that do not accumulate, that do not lead to reconciliation or rupture.

The danger, the first visitor found, is in entering. Your words become available to the pattern. You say "I am not from here," and the loop absorbs it, your voice joining the texture, your meaning dissolving into the repetition. You might stay hours, days, speaking and being spoken through, until some force of will—some residual self that has not yet learned the plateau's patience—allows you to step back, to break the pattern by physical removal, your voice continuing without you for a moment, a ghost of participation, before the loop closes around your absence.

Who are they, these loop-speakers? Perhaps they were the first to arrive, the builders' contemporaries or immediate successors, caught in the stasis before they learned its nature. Perhaps they are the builders themselves, dispersed not into objects but into *process*, into this holding pattern of speech. Perhaps they are generated by the equilibrium, the way the hares are generated, the way the infants appear in garden plots—produced by the need for something that resembles interaction without its consequences.

### The Animals, Considered as Inhabitants

The blue-black hares run their paths at dawn and dusk, which are themselves held in suspension, neither fully arriving nor departing. They eat, they are eaten, their numbers hold. But watch them closely—watch for hours, as the Watchers do—and you notice something about their repetition. It is not mechanical. The hare that runs past the same clump of wax-leaf at the same apparent time each day does not run identically. Its speed varies slightly, its path weaves, its ears rotate to sounds that may or may not be the same. The *outcome* is fixed—the clump eaten, the path completed, the hare surviving or not surviving in statistical balance—but the *instance* retains some quality of improvisation, of creaturely variation within the held frame.

This is the deepest strangeness of the Keth, perhaps deeper than the human phenomena. The hares are not automata. They are *free within constraint*, choosing within a choice-space that does not alter. The weasel with copper eyes that hunts them succeeds or fails in proportions that hold, but each hunt is hunted, each chase chased, with something that resembles desire, that resembles fear, the emotions not flattened but *sustained*, held at their peak intensity without resolution or exhaustion.

The birds with pale breasts, the three-note calls, the regular spacing—here too, variation within the pattern. The notes' pitch, their timbre, the exact duration of the spaces between them: these alter slightly, bird to bird, call to call, while the structure holds. It is as if the equilibrium permits everything except *consequence*, the linking of event to event that produces narrative, history, change.

### The Builders, Absent-Present

They are not seen, these original five-fingered ones, these makers of copper and obsidian tools. But they are not simply gone. The glove-molds in the workshops, the carved reliefs on certain walls, the half-carved tuff block waiting beside its unfinished wall—these are not artifacts but *presences*, held at the moment of making. The hand that shaped the relief is not absent from it; it is *in* it, still shaping, the gesture arrested but not completed.

Some theorize that the builders attempted something with their primitive tools, some working that struck the vein of stasis. But what if they *are* the working? What if the city is not their product but their *body*, dispersed, extended in space and time, the walls their held breath, the cisterns their held blood, the looping conversations their held thoughts? To walk the city is to walk through them, not around them, to inhabit their extended embodiment, their transformation into place itself.

The scaffolding still standing, the ropes still tensioned—this is not neglect but *continuance*, the building's becoming permanently becoming. The builders did not stop working; the work stopped *completing*, the process of making separated from the product, the means continuing without the end. They are here, in this sense, more present than any active inhabitants, present as the condition of presence itself, the ground against which all else appears.

### The Emergent

And then there are those who came from the stalemate itself, not born, not arrived, but *produced*, the way condensation produces water on cold stone, the way the equilibrium produces its own maintenance. They appear at thresholds, yes, but also in reflections that do not correspond to bodies, in shadows that move slightly differently than their casters, in the spaces between the looping words where something almost new almost emerges.

They are difficult to describe because description requires stability, and they are the instability within the stable, the slight vibration in the held note. You might see one as a figure at the edge of vision, walking a path that does not exist, entering a building that has no door. You might hear one as an almost-voice in the wind's constant low presence, saying something that would be meaningful if it could complete itself, if meaning itself were not a form of change.

They are not numerous. Perhaps a dozen, perhaps fewer, perhaps one that appears as many. They trade sometimes with the traders, offering not copper or obsidian but *difference itself*, slight variations that enter the loops without breaking them, that make the repetition bearable or unbearable depending on your capacity for the almost-same. They are the Keth's self-awareness, perhaps, or its dream of escape, or simply the necessary byproduct of any system complex enough to hold itself static, the noise in the signal that proves the signal exists.

## What It Is to Live Here

To inhabit the City of Stilled Spring is to learn a different grammar of existence, one without tense in the ordinary sense. Not timeless—time passes, the sun moves, the body ages to its held point—but *tenseless*, the distinction between was and is and will be flattened into a continuous present that is not presence but *suspension*.

You learn to need less, or rather, your needs learn the plateau's patience. Hunger becomes not absence but *quality*, a particular flavor of awareness that does not intensify. Thirst, the same. The desire for company, for solitude, for meaning, for its release—all these become modes of the equilibrium, ways of being held rather than ways of reaching.

You learn to speak carefully, or not at all, aware that words here have weight beyond their intention, that they can enter patterns not of your making, that the loop-speakers began, perhaps, with a single careless conversation that found the resonance of the place and continued without them. Or you learn to speak fully, to give yourself to the looping, to find in repetition a kind of peace that the changing world denies.

You learn to watch the hares, the weasels, the birds with pale breasts, not as entertainment but as *instruction*, demonstrations of how to be free within constraint, how to vary without altering, how to live without the exhausting requirement that living lead somewhere.

And you learn, finally or never-finally, that the Keth does not want you, does not refuse you, does not require your presence or absence. It holds its contradiction without interest in your participation. The equilibrium is not fragile; it does not need your stillness or your motion to maintain itself. You are not preserved here, not threatened, not transformed except insofar as you transform yourself in response to the conditions, learning the plateau's patience or failing to learn it, staying or leaving or settling into the angle of repose that waits, that has always waited, that will always wait, not for you, not for anything, but simply *waiting*, as the held breath waits, as the potential energy waits, as the story waits that will never be told because its telling would be a change, and change is the one absent parameter, the zero at the heart, the dynamic that defines this place precisely by its lack, its permanent, its patient, its terrible and tender absence.
