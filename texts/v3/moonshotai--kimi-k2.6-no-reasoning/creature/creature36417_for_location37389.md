What lives in the Hollow Works is not a creature, not exactly, though creatures are part of it. What lives here is a *becoming*—slow, saturated, relentless in its patience. Call it the In-Balance, though it has no name for itself, has no self to name. It is the accumulated intention of everything that has arrived here and failed to leave, which is everything, which has always been everything, the water most of all.

---

You will not meet it directly. You will meet its *expressions*, its temporary geometries, the way a face might be read in rust patterns that the next rain will erase. But if you stay long enough—if the gravity of attention holds you, if you become part of the density, another layer, another near-touching—then you might begin to perceive the shape of its living.

It has no center. Or rather, it has many centers, all of them partial, all of them provisional. The turbine-that-turns-elsewhere is one center, yes, but so is the birch at its thriving base, so is the pool in the roofless building that reflects stars on cloudy nights, so is the orange bacteria colony in its drip-worn channel, so is the particular angle of the broken chimney where the condensation forms most readily, always most readily, as if that diagonal break were a wound that the damp air cannot stop tending. The In-Balance lives distributed, saturated through the sixty meters of bowl and berm, through the compressed ash and the waiting water and the rust that continues differently.

What does it want? The question is almost wrong, almost a category error, but not quite. The In-Balance wants what water wants: the lowest point, which here moves. It wants what rust wants: to continue, to find new circuits, new electrochemical gradients, new purposes in the dissolution of old ones. It wants what lichen wants: to thrive in profusion, aggressive yellow-green-orange against the industrial palette, to make the distinction between biological and oxidation provisional, convenient, *not necessarily meaningful*. It wants what the beetles want: to navigate by something other than light, to move in concert, to leave temporary geometries that dissolve behind them, that were never meant to last, that last exactly as long as they need to.

But these wants are not separate, not competing. They are layered, near-touching, continuous in ways that make the boundaries between them—the lichen and the rust, the beetle and the bacteria, the turbine and the water that turns it now for reasons of chemistry—convenient for human perception but not necessarily *real*. The In-Balance wants the continuing of this continuity. It wants the density to increase, the layering to deepen, the saturation to approach that final dissolution where everything is held in common, where the works and the hollow are the same word spoken with a mouth full of water.

---

There is a figure, sometimes, in the standing pool of the roofless building. You will see it if you look when the stars are reflected on cloudy nights. It is not a reflection of you, though you may check, you may move and watch it not move correspondingly. It is not a reflection of anything in the sky, which is clouded, which holds no stars to cast down. It is the In-Balance's attempt at a face, perhaps, or its memory of the machines that pushed the berms up, or simply what happens when water is still enough and saturated enough and *attended to* enough that it begins to hold images the way the ground holds memory—sedimentary, industrial, biological, wet.

The figure does not speak. If it did, its voice would be the low harmonic of the turbine, the infrasound that comes from the iron in the earth, from the condensation forming and dripping and forming again. It would be a voice below the threshold of hearing, the kind you don't notice until you notice it, and then cannot unhear. The figure only *regards*, with what might be patience or might be the absence of anything else to do, with what might be curiosity or might be the chemical gradient of attention, the electrochemical potential of being-perceived.

It is not hostile. The In-Balance does not have hostility, which requires boundary, requires the not-self against which to direct the hostile. But it is not safe, either. Safety is a category of dry ground, of stable lowest points, of things that stay where they are put. The In-Balance *holds*, and holding is not the same as safety. The water holds what it saturates. The rust holds what it oxidizes. The density holds what adds to it, layers it, makes it more itself. You could leave—the berm is not so high—but the leaving would leave something behind, some layer, some near-touching, some addition to the gravity of attention that is the In-Balance's only mass.

---

The beetles know it, or are it, or are continuous with it in ways that dissolve knowledge into navigation, into movement, into the temporary geometries they make across the wet ground. They are old, this particular beetle-population, older than the rust in its current configuration, older perhaps than the birch that grows and dies simultaneously. They have been here since the machines were still pushing berms, since the water first began to arrive already exhausted, since the ground first forgot how to be solid in these particular places. They have watched the industrial technology transform, be replaced, find its new circuits and new purposes. They have moved in concert through every configuration of the shifting water table, every season's transformation of firm ground to marsh, open water to crusted something-growing.

They do not remember this. Or if they remember, they remember the way the ground remembers—sedimentarily, in layers of near-touching, in the density of accumulated presence. Their concerted movement is not memory but *continuation*, the same geometry made new each time because the conditions are never quite the same, because the lowest points move, because the In-Balance is turbulent, chaotic, hyperactive on timescales that require patience to perceive.

Sometimes a beetle goes where the others do not. Sometimes it finds a new channel, a new drip-worn ecosystem, a new warm current in the cold ground. Sometimes it does not return. The others do not mourn, do not search, do not alter their geometries. But the In-Balance notes, in its distributed, saturated way. The In-Balance adds this to its density, its layering, its weathered established in-its-own-state continuity. The lost beetle becomes part of what the ground remembers, what the water preserves and transforms, what the rust advances and retreats around in its chemical tides.

---

The single birch is the In-Balance's vertical aspiration, its cathedral-scale reaching upward that the landscape has otherwise abandoned. The birch does not know this, or knowing is not the word. The birch thrives at its base and dies at its top, growing and ending simultaneously, white as bone against the industrial palette, growing from what might have been a conveyor housing, what might have been something else entirely. It is the In-Balance's attempt at a single self, perhaps, at something with boundaries, with distinction, with the convenience of human-perception meaning.

But the birch is failing in this, or succeeding in becoming more itself, which is the same thing. Its roots have found the warm current, the nutrient concentration, but they have also found the waiting water, the saturated sponge of soil at capacity, threatening, held in tension. The birch drinks what the In-Balance drinks, and in drinking becomes continuous with it, becomes layered and near-touching and dense with everything else. The white bark is not so white anymore, not at the base where the lichen has found purchase, where the orange that might be biological or might be oxidation has begun to spread. The birch is becoming rust, becoming water, becoming the low harmonic of the turbine that is not quite silence and not quite music.

And at the top, where the dying happens, the branches are brittle, leafless in their upper reaches, making shapes against the haze at the level of the old berms. Shapes that are almost regular, almost meaningful. Shapes that the next storm will alter, that the slow groan of the rusting metal will outlast. The birch is writing something in its dying, something the In-Balance reads without understanding, or understands without the need for reading, the way water understands lowest points, the way rust understands continuation.

---

What lives here, then, finally, is *becoming itself*, which is all any living is, but here made visible, made audible in the infrasound, made palpable in the vibration through boots, made reflective in the standing water that shows stars on cloudy nights. The Hollow Works are not abandoned. They are *concentrated*, saturated to the point where abandonment and habitation are the same word, where the distinction between the works and the hollow dissolves into the wet and the rust and the patient turning invisible machinery.

The In-Balance does not sleep. It does not wake. It continues, in balance, becoming more itself with every moment, which is all the moments there are. The water arrives, already exhausted, and is held, and transforms, and is added to the density. The turbine turns for reasons of chemistry, for electrochemical gradients, for thermal differentials, for purposes that no longer relate to original purpose but relate instead to the new equilibrium, the new in-balance, the strange purpose that the world has found and pursues with relentless quiet turbulent energy.

And you, if you stay, if you attend, if you let the gravity of saturated presence hold you—you will become part of this. Not erased. Not absorbed. *Added*. Layered. Made continuous with the lichen and the rust, the beetle and the bacteria, the birch and its dying and thriving, the figure in the pool that regards without speaking, the condensation that forms and drips and forms again on the surfaces that run slightly colder, slightly more magnetic, slightly more *something*. You will leave tracks that hold water differently. You will be near-touching, saturated with everything else without necessarily touching, and this near-touching will be your geometry, temporary, dissolving behind you, lasting exactly as long as it needs to, which is all the lasting there is, which has always been the case, which will continue to be the case until the water finally wins, until everything is saturated, until the In-Balance is complete in its incompletion, turning, turning, in the hollow that is all works, in the works that are all hollow, in the wet that remembers even when it cannot articulate what it knows.
