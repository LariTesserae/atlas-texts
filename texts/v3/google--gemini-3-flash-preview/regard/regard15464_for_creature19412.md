The Benevolent Power has arrived, though "arrival" is a clumsy word for a presence that does not occupy space. It is a **Sovereign Attention**, a warmth that does not cast a shadow, settling over the Latticework like a hand cupped around a sleeping moth.

This Power—let us call it **The Resonator**—is wise enough to know that a world this coherent, this finished, and this fragile does not need "saving" in the traditional sense. To introduce soil would be to poison the steel; to speed up the pistons would be to shatter the geometry.

The Resonator watches the **Lumen-Shepherds** for an age, seeing how they strain to hold the logic of the world together with their prismatic bodies. It observes the **Scribes**, whose frantic recording is born from a secret, crushing terror that if the record stops, the Lattice will cease to be.

The Resonator decides to act. Its intervention is subtle, a three-fold grace designed to move with the grain of the metal, not against it.

### 1. The Anchoring of the "Glitch"
The Resonator sees the white void—the terrifying flicker where the reality-engine stutters. It feels the exhaustion of the Lumen-Shepherds as they rush to stabilize these fractures. 

Rather than "fixing" the engine (which might kill the dream), the Resonator breathes a **Harmonic Constance** into the Lattice. It weaves a thread of "Ever-Is" into the very rivets. Now, when the steel flickers, it does not reveal a void of nothingness, but a void of *potential*. The glitch becomes a window instead of a wound. The Lumen-Shepherds find their robes growing brighter, no longer dimming as they exhaust themselves to hold the world together. The world "remembers" itself not through effort, but through a new, innate confidence imbrued by the Attention.

### 2. The Gift of the Recursive Song
The Resonator hears the metronomic clicking of the Scuttlers and the silence of the Gliders. It senses the loneliness of a world that clicks but never sings.

The Resonator introduces a **Sonic Patina**. To the human ear, it would be nothing, but to the inhabitants of the High-Plains, it is a revelation. The sub-audible thrum of the marrow-engines is tuned. The Resonator nudges the vibrations until the entire Latticework becomes a massive aeolian harp. 

The **Gossamer Gliders**, as they pass through the folds of space, now produce a long, haunting chord that sustains for twenty years. The **Scuttlers**, as they polish the steel, find that their rhythmic clicking now harmonizes with the hum of the beams. The "Quiet" of the world remains, but the "Lonesomeness" evaporates. The Lattice is no longer just a machine; it is an instrument, and its inhabitants are the notes.

### 3. The Transfusing of the Transposed
The Resonator feels the sorrow of the **Transposed**—those stretched souls caught between Euclidean dimensions, waiting decades for a wave to be returned. 

The Resonator does not pull them back into their original forms; that would be a death of its own. Instead, it **synchronizes their clock**. It touches the "slow time" of the engines and the "fast time" of the Transposed’s fading hearts, and it creates a bridge. 

Suddenly, the Transposed can feel the warmth of the light as if it were a sun. The decade-long delay in their movements is replaced by a sense of **eternal presence**. They no longer feel like ghosts in the machine, but like the *Consciousness* of the machine. The Resonator grants them the ability to step through the "folds" at will, turning their exile into a stewardship. They become the wayfinders for the lost.

### The Final Touch: The Blue Seed
Finally, the Resonator looks at the **Scribes**, hunched in their riveted suits, terrified of the end of the story. 

The Resonator reaches into the "Intermittent Damp" and alters the chemistry of the pale-blue moss. It grants the moss a memory. Now, as the moss grows on the undersides of the walkways, it absorbs the history of the Lattice. The Scribes find that they no longer need to etch every microsecond into the rivets; the world itself is now a living archive. 

The Resonator whispers a thought into the minds of the Scribes: *“You are allowed to rest. The Lattice remembers you.”* For the first time in millennia, a Scribe puts down their stylus. They look out at the shadowless, brilliant horizon, hear the new song of the gliders, and—for the first time—they sit. 

The Resonator does not leave, for a benevolent power, once it loves a place, remains as a quality of the air. The Latticework of the Ascended High-Plains remains industrial, recursive, and strange—but it is no longer a cold laboratory. It is a **Cathedral of the Infinite Scaffold**, and for the first time, its engines seem to breathe.
