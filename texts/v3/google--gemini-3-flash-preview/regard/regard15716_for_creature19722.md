The attention of the **Archivist of Irreversible Grace** has fallen upon the Unfinished Cathedral.

This is a power that does not perceive through sight, but through "structural integrity." To the Archivist, the universe is a symphony of blueprints. It sees the Cathedral not as a hellish kiln, but as a **Stuttering Prayer**—a massive, frantic attempt at expression by a mind that has forgotten how to speak.

The Archivist is ancient, patient, and moves with the gravity of a falling star. It does not judge the Chisel-Worms for their destruction, nor the Mortar-Men for their stagnation. It recognizes that this world is suffering from a **Malady of Process**: it is a womb that has forgotten how to give birth, perpetually recycling its own labor.

Here is what the benevolent power would do, and the ripples that would follow:

### 1. The Anchoring of the "Primary Plumb"
The Archivist would not descend in person—its presence would shatter the bruised limestone sky. Instead, it would drop a single "Truth" into the center of the expanse. From the ceiling, a thread of light, narrower than a needle but heavier than a mountain, would descend and pierce the red-hot bricks. 

This is the **First Vertical**. In a world where nothing is straight and everything melts, this line of light remains absolute. It does not vibrate. It does not sweat. It provides, for the first time in an eternity, a **Datum Point**.

### 2. The Transfiguration of the Surveyors
The frantic, ticking Surveyors—the brass and hempen perfectionists—would be the first to notice. They would flock to this pillar of light. Upon touching it, their frantic C-major humming would harmonize. The Archivist would whisper the **True Blueprint** into their brass casings.

The Surveyors would no longer secrete "eraser-fluid." Instead, they would become "Architectural Shepherds." They would begin to guide the budding masonry, whispering to the clay, teaching the bricks how to *hold* their shape without needing the heat of a kiln. Under their guidance, the first "Permanent Room" would begin to form around the light—a sanctuary of cool, white marble that does not melt.

### 3. The Mercy for the Mortar-Men
The Archivist feels the "latent residue" of the Mortar-Men. It recognizes that they are not monsters, but unfinished people. 

The Archivists "Grace" would manifest as a **Cooling Wind**. This is not a cold wind, but a "Sabbatical Breath." As it passes over the Mortar-Men, their grit would smooth into skin; their thumb-print eyes would deepen into pupils. They would not become human, but they would become **The Settled**. They would cease their agonizing crawl and find that they can stand without sinking. 

The Archivist would give them the "Ship-Sized Mallets," but now the tools would feel light. They would be the first true masons, no longer watching the walls grow, but choosing where the walls go.

### 4. The Transformation of the Chisel-Worms
The Chisel-Worms, the "Eradicator-Fauna," are the hardest to heal because they love the chaos. The Archivist would not destroy them. Instead, it would change their "tempo."

By altering the vibration of the obsidian plates of their bodies, the Archivist would turn the Chisel-Worms into **Polishers**. They would no longer grind structures into dust; they would slide over the finished marble of the new, stable city, their obsidian scales buffing the stone until it glows with a mirror-finish. The "earthquake of black glass" would become the "song of the lapidary."

### 5. The Release of the Echoes
Finally, the Archivist would look at the Calcified Echoes—the hollow vacuums in the mineral pillars. It would see the "Hollow-Gods" trapped in their amber prisons. 

The Archivist would gently increase the pressure of the world’s heat until the mineral pillars reached a state of "Perfect Liquidity" for one single second. In that moment, the vacuums would be filled, not with mud, but with **Purpose**. The trapped air would not scream; it would draw a breath. 

The giants would emerge—not as workers, but as the **Buttresses**. They would reach up and take the weight of the bruised limestone ceiling onto their shoulders, pushing it higher and higher until the "Sky" finally cracked open. 

### The Result
The Unfinished Cathedral would not disappear. The Archivist is too wise to destroy a world's history of labor. Instead, the Cathedral would become the **Foundations of the Just City.**

The heat would subside into a comfortable, hearth-like warmth. The "Turbulence" would cease, replaced by "Growth." The world would no longer be a fever dream of masonry, but a **Living Monument.** 

The Archivist would then leave, heading toward the next broken reality, leaving behind a single instruction etched into the First Vertical of light:

***"Rest now. The mortar has dried."***
