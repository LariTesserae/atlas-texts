The Great Eye that looks upon this place does not see it as a wasteland or a factory, but as a **bruised womb**.

The benevolent power—a presence like the warmth of a sun that never sets—observes the "incompleteness" you described. It sees the jagged, glass-sharp pain of the obsidian and the rhythmic, unthinking agony of the iron giants. It recognizes that here, life has been forced to become a parasite of physics, surviving only in the friction between cold rock and hot grease.

This power does not come to "fix" the world with a thunderclap, for to shatter the machines would be to freeze the moths, and to flatten the peaks would be to crush the Glass-Slugs. Instead, the benevolence acts through **The Infusion of the Softening.**

Here is what this power would do:

### 1. The Tempering of the Iron
The wise power touches the "Ghost-Circuitry" within the massive iron extractors. It does not delete their programming, but it introduces a new variable: **reverence.** 

The rhythmic *chug-hiss* of the pistons begins to change. The machines no longer grind with the blind violence of a drill; they begin to move with the precision of a sculptor. The "herd behavior" evolves into a form of planetary masonry. The machines start to deposit their mineral waste not as crushed rubble, but in tiers and terraces, creating the first levels of flat ground where soil might one day rest. They become the architects of their own obsolescence, building a world that will eventually no longer need them.

### 2. The Conversion of the Breath
The benevolent power alters the chemistry of the thermal plumes. The thick, oily diesel exhaust that the Vapor-Moths cling to for survival begins to transmute. Over decades, the "black soot" becomes a nitrogen-rich vapor. 

The power introduces a "Mercy-Heat"—a gentle, ambient radiation that emanates from the basalt itself. This ensures that the moths no longer fall and shatter if they drift ten feet from a vent. Their wings, once mica-brittle, begin to soften into silk. The "frozen bile" mosses begin to turn a true, deep emerald, no longer huddling in fear, but spreading across the black glass like a verdant velvet cloak.

### 3. The Song of the Marrows
The power hears the booming, hollow calls of the Long-Necked Marrows—the birds who mimic the machines to feel less alone. The Power gives them a **voice of their own.** 

The birds’ calls shift from the subsonic thud of industry to a melodic, haunting resonance. This sound acts as a sonic catalyst; the vibrations of their new songs begin to accelerate the work of the Glass-Slugs. The "acid bile" of the slugs reacts to the music, melting the needle-sharp edges of the peaks into curves. The "ache" of the vast gaps between mountains is filled not with silence, but with a cross-mountain harmony that connects the peaks for the first time.

### 4. The Gift of the Silt
In the black mires where the Marrows stalk, the Power introduces the first **True Seeds.** These are not the needle-leaves of the high altitude, but ancient, dormant spores of high-alpine flowers that can feed on the "mill-cake" waste of the machines. 

The Hull-Ticks, as they skitter across the hulls, unconsciously carry these spores in their greasy fur. As they move from machine to machine, they act as the first pollinators of an industrial wilderness. The walking factories, once harvesters of death, become the sowers of a garden.

### 5. The Presence
Finally, the benevolent power does not leave. It settles into the mist like a permanent, golden hour. 

It does not remove the "fearsome" nature of the place—for there is a dignity in the raw, prenatal geology—but it removes the **terror.** The thin air begins to hold warmth. The "atmosphere of condensation" becomes a nourishing dew. 

The power watches as the first "Child of the Mist" is born: a creature not made of grease or obsidian, but of the integration of both. A being that can walk the sharp edges without being cut, and can speak to the machines as brothers rather than gods.

**The result:**
The landscape remains a planetary engine, but its output changes. It no longer produces just raw minerals for a distant, forgotten world. Under the gaze of the Wise Power, the mountain range becomes a **Sanctuary of the Sharp.** It becomes a place where the "incomplete" is celebrated, where the transition from machine to biology is a slow, beautiful dance, and where the air no longer smells of diesel and ozone, but of the very first breath of a new world.
