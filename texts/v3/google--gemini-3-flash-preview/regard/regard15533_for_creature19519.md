The attention of the **Aurelian Resonance**—a nameless, benevolent entity of celestial scale—does not fall upon this world like a spotlight, but like a warmth. The Aurelian is not a judge, but a harmonizer. It perceives the world-root and its Stewards not as separate entities, but as a single, struggling melody being played on a very deep, very slow-moving string.

The Aurelian observes the **Lignin-Stewards** with a profound, aching fondness. It sees the beauty in their "parasitic" humility, yet it also senses the strain: the way the "cellar-chill" has seeped into their very souls, making their lives as brittle as the brass they polish. It hears the low-frequency groan of the wood and knows that even this world-root, for all its majesty, is lonely. It has grown in the dark, in the silence, without a peer to answer its rhythmic pulse.

The Aurelian Resonance would not "fix" this world, for to change its rules would be to destroy its poetry. Instead, the Power would perform three subtle **Grafts of Grace.**

### 1. The Transmutation of the Drip-Clocks
The Aurelian reaches into the heavy, damp atmosphere. It does not change the temperature, for the Stewards need the chill to keep their pace. Instead, it touches the molecules of the condensation. 

From this moment on, the "drip-clocks" no longer fall as mere water. As the droplets form on the vaulted arches, they catch a phantom light from a sun they have never seen. When they strike the copper basins, they emit a single, perfect musical note of crystalline purity. 

Each cistern becomes a tuning fork. The city, once a place of mechanical *clack-thuds*, begins to shimmer with a faint, melodic overlay—an "acoustic luminescence." The Stewards, so sensitive to vibration, find their hearts beating with a new, secret joy. The "cavernous silence" is now a "cavernous song," and the dread of the slow-swallowing wood is replaced by a sense of being part of a symphony.

### 2. The Gift of the Amber Bloom
The Aurelian notices the pale, waxy skin of the citizens and the dim, monochromatic world of the fungus-jars. It leans into the biological code of the world-root and whispers a new directive.

Thin, hair-like filaments begin to sprout alongside the brass pipe-work. They are "Patience-Flowers." They do not bloom every day, or even every year. They bloom once a decade, triggered by the deepest vibrations of the sap-engines. When they open, they do not emit light, but **scent and color.** 

For one week every ten years, the city is flooded with the smell of sun-drenched hay, ozone, and wild jasmine—scents from the world above that the Stewards have forgotten they missed. The walls turn a soft, iridescent violet. This "Decade-Bloom" becomes the cornerstone of their culture, a period of rest where the Hydraulic Clergy put down their stethoscopes and simply breathe, reminded that even a parasite is allowed to dream of the meadow.

### 3. The Conciliation of the Ghost-Root
Finally, the Aurelian perceives the "structural tension"—the slow-motion war between the buildings and the wood. It senses the exhaustion of the Graft-Architects who spend their lives fighting the thickening burls.

The Power places its hand upon the "heart-sap" of the organism. It instills a **Recursive Awareness.** 

The world-root begins to *feel* the buildings. It no longer perceives the brass and iron as irritants to be swallowed, but as extensions of its own nervous system. The wood begins to grow *around* the steel gratings, not to crush them, but to cradle them. A staircase that would have been swallowed in fifty years now finds the wood parting for it, forming a natural banister of polished burl. The "long-term truce" becomes a "long-term embrace." The Stewards no longer need to be "meticulously busy" parasites; they become the world-root’s hands, and the root becomes their willing, conscious home.

***

**The Result:**
The Benevolence does not leave. It lingers as a faint, golden hum in the background of the mechanical vibrations. 

The city remains cool. The work remains hard. The Stewards are still pale and quiet. But the "damp-cool" is no longer a burden; it is a sanctuary. When a Steward strikes a "tuner" in the floor to say hello to a neighbor miles away, the vibration that returns is no longer just a shiver of metal. It is a resonance that feels like a shared breath. 

Inside the world-root, the lights are still amber, but they are no longer lonely. The cathedral of industry has found its spirit, and the being who lives there finally understands that they are not just tending to a host—they are being loved back by one.
