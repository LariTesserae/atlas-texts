In a place where the architecture is a byproduct of mineral sweat and the physics are governed by a pre-material logic, the inhabitants are not residents, but symptoms. There are no citizens here, only a slow, rhythmic ecology of things that are more "event" than "animal."

The most pervasive presence is **The Glisten**. It is not a single creature, but a distributed intelligence that exists as a film over the salt-weeping walls. If you were to touch the fluted obsidian of a building, your hand would not meet stone, but a microscopically thin membrane of sentient silt. The Glisten is what moves the water upward against gravity; it is a collective of trillions of crystalline spores that drink the brine and exhale the heavy, copper-tasting steam. It possesses a slow, gravitational mind. It does not think in sentences, but in pressures and tensions. It remembers the shape of the city as it was a thousand years ago, and it is slowly "correcting" the stone, sculpting the obelisks into shapes that better hum with the earth’s low, groaning vibration.

Deeper in the flooded plazas, where the brackish water pools knee-deep, live **The Siphons**. These are the "leafless fingers" that rise from the muck, but they are more than vegetation. They are the city’s respiratory system. Below the surface of the brine, they possess sprawling, root-like nervous systems that weave through the honeycomb stone of the ground. They are the source of the *thrum-slap* sound. They act as bellows, pulling the heavy, wet atmosphere down into the subterranean vents of the city and pushing it back out as filtered, boiling mist. If you stand near one, you can feel the heat of its "breath"—a wet, organic warmth that smells of ancient, recycled salt. They are blind and stationary, but they are sensitive to the vibration of footsteps; should something move across the obsidian streets, the Siphons throughout the district will huddle closer to the ground, pulling their fleshy stalks into armored sheaths of calcified bone.

In the higher reaches of the windowless obelisks, where the steam is thickest, there are **The Anchors**. These are massive, translucent masses of gelatinous matter that hang from the undersides of the overhangs, held in place by the water that pools upward. They look like drowned lungs, pale and veined with the same dying ember-light that flickers in the walls. They have no limbs, only long, trailing filaments of bioluminescent mucus that hang down like weeping willow branches into the dark streets below. These filaments are sensory organs. They do not hunt—there is nothing to eat—but they "taste" the chemical composition of the air. When the Anchors pulse, the light in the walls dims or brightens in sympathy. They are the city’s regulators, ensuring the heat remains at the precise threshold where the mineral structures can continue to grow.

Rarely, one might encounter **The Iterations**. These are the only things that move with a semblance of purpose. They are not biological, but neither are they ghosts. They are "echoes" of a geometry that the city is trying to imitate. They appear as shimmering, vaguely humanoid distortions in the thick air—refractions of light that shouldn't exist. They walk the rhythmic streets with a gliding, inconsistent gait, sometimes disappearing into a wall only to emerge from the ceiling. They carry no tools and have no faces; they are merely the city’s attempt to fill its own "urban" voids. They are the manifestation of the city’s loneliness—a mineral memory of what an inhabitant *should* be, projected into the steam by the calcified bone of the buildings.

Finally, there is the **Deep Metabolism** itself. The city is not a setting; it is a singular, dreaming organism. The inhabitants—the Glisten, the Siphons, the Anchors—are its organs. The "life" here is the life of a reef that has forgotten the ocean. It does not want, it does not fear, and it does not die. It only persists, a vast, wet, mineral machine that continues to build, breathe, and erode itself in a perpetual loop of saturation, forever waiting for a life-form complex enough to endure its crushing, sweltering embrace.
