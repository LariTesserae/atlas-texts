To look upon the inhabitants of the Crystalline Weir is to see creatures that have surrendered the very concept of a "solid" existence. They are the **Lenthics**, a people who have become as viscous and persistent as the water that threatens to scour them from the cliff.

### The Physiology of the Flow
A Lenthic person does not possess the matte, porous skin of a valley-dweller. Their bodies are encased in an integument that looks like polished, grey-black kelp—a dense, sebaceous hide that is perpetually slick. They lack hair, which would only freeze into lethal needles; instead, their scalps and spines are ridged with flexible, cartilaginous fins that channel the heavy water away from their faces.

They are biologically "clamped." Their hands and feet are oversized, with elongated fingers tipped not with nails, but with broad, serrated suction pads. Because the Weir is vertical, the Lenthic skeletal structure has shifted; their shoulders are massive, their collarbones thick as tusks, designed for a life lived in a perpetual pull-up. They do not walk so much as they *tether*. A Lenthic at rest is always hooked into something—a stone ring, a bone piton, or a neighbor’s harness.

Their eyes are perhaps the most alien feature. To survive the 3.0-intensity light, their pupils are not holes, but horizontal slits of shimmering, iridescent gold, backed by a biological "shutter" of translucent membrane. They see the world in high-contrast thermals, tracking the warmth of the black basalt through the blinding white glare of the slush.

### The Social Slump
In the Weir, the concept of personal space is a fatal luxury. To be alone is to be swept away. The Lenthics live in "Skeins"—communal groups of twenty to fifty individuals who spend much of their time physically lashed together. When they sleep inside their seal-hide huts, they do so in a "slump," a pile of interlocking limbs and oily skin, sharing the meager radiation gathered by the volcanic heat-traps.

Their language is not one of vowels and breath—the pressurized air makes speech difficult—but of percussion and tactile vibration. They communicate by slapping the basalt or rhythmic tugging on the communal lines. To a stranger, the Weir sounds like a frantic, wet drum-circle; to a Lenthic, it is a constant stream of data regarding ice stability and flow-velocity.

### The Harvesters of the Slush
While the cat-sized hexapods (the *Scuttles*) graze on the mineral polyps, the Lenthics hunt the "Heavy Vapors." At certain heights, the viscous water traps pockets of nutrient-rich gas from the basalt vents. The Lenthics use long, hollowed-out bird bones to "pierce" the falling globs of water, siphoning out the gas and mineral silt.

Their diet is a grey, gelatinous paste made from ground polyps and fermented succulent-pulp. They eat with a frantic, shivering speed, for to stop moving is to allow the cold to settle into the joints. A Lenthic who stops shivering is a Lenthic who is dying.

### The Culture of the Snap
There is no permanence in their crafts. Because the slush erodes bone and hide within weeks, the Lenthics are a people of "The Snap"—a philosophy of instant replacement. Their artisans do not carve idols; they weave "Flow-Catchers," intricate, temporary geometries of sinew designed to vibrate in the falling water to create specific pitches of sound.

Death is handled with a brutal, practical grace. When a member of the Weir-folk falls or succumbs to the rot, they are not buried—there is no earth for a grave. Instead, they are "given to the Gravity." The body is stripped of its useful leathers and hooks, and then committed to the white abyss. They believe that the water that washes over them is the distilled essence of their ancestors, recycled forever in the paradoxical, freezing cycle of the cliff.

### The Wraith-Light Clergy
At the highest points of the Weir, where the light is so intense it turns the basalt to a glowing charcoal, live the *Optics*. These are the elders who have been partially blinded by the radiance. They sit atop the highest "heat-traps," their skin scarred and toughened into a bark-like texture. They do not work the pulleys. Instead, they monitor the crystalline lenses of the black succulents. 

By tilting these plants and adjusting their own polished-stone mirrors, the Optics redirect the "absolute light" into the lower, darker levels of the city, acting as biological lighthouses. They are the keepers of the Flow, the transition-callers who signal—through a series of blinding flashes—when a particular artery of the cliff is about to buckle under the weight of the ice. 

They are a people defined by the grip. In a world of sliding, falling, and eroding, the Lenthic is the only thing that holds on. They are the friction in a frictionless world, a frantic, oily heartbeat thumping against the indifferent, crystalline roar of the heights.
