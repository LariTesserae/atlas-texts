To live in the Vertical Marsh is to be an extension of the Stasis. Because time has become a solid and the air has become a liquid, the inhabitants are not "creatures" in any sense that implies movement, metabolic pulse, or the frantic hurry of breathing. 

They are the **Vitreous Gentry**—beings of slow-glass and distilled marrow who have more in common with the basaltic spires than with anything that walks.

### The Physiology of Stillness
The residents are tall, unnervingly spindly figures that resemble the "hollowed-out gourds" of their architecture. Their skin is a translucent, calcified membrane, similar to the sap-walls of the city, through which one can see the slow, golden circulation of the "thick air" they drink. They do not have lungs; they have internal lattices of mossy fiber that absorb the humidity directly through their pores.

They do not possess muscles for locomotion because there is nowhere to go that they are not already. Instead, their limbs are long, elegant filaments of "wood-bone" that taper into roots. A citizen of the Vertical Marsh does not stand *on* a plaza; they are *grafted* to it. Their feet have blurred into the weeping stone of the spires, becoming part of the "Established Erosion." To look at a crowd in a plaza is to look at a grove of living statues, each one a permanent architectural feature of the neighborhood.

### The Sightless Watchers
They have no eyes. In a world where light casts no shadows and depth perception is a forgotten concept, sight would be a liability. Instead, their heads are crowned with wide, fan-like crests of sensitive, bioluminescent ferns. These crests "hear" the Deafening Equilibrium. They perceive the world through the vibration of the roar; they feel the shape of the city by how the white noise bounces off the violet sky and the silver ribbons of water.

Because the light is "Normal" and shadowless, their bodies are perpetually shimmering, covered in the same half-inch sheen of moving water that coats the stones. They appear to be made of liquid glass, reflecting the golden amber of the atmosphere so perfectly that they are nearly invisible until you are close enough to feel the heat radiating from their motionless forms.

### The Communion of Thought
The Gentry do not speak. The crushing roar of the planet’s internal pressure would swallow any tongue-made sound. Instead, they communicate through the "Liquid Ribbons"—the silver water that flows upward between the spires. Because they are rooted into the city’s biological architecture, they are all physically connected. 

Each inhabitant is a node in a city-wide neural network of calcified sap and vine. A thought in the mind of a being at the highest pinnacle ripples through the woven tenements and the fungal shelves, vibrating through the "marrow in the bones" of every other resident. They do not have individual lives; they have a collective, "Frozen Equilibrium" of consciousness. They are the dreaming mind of the city.

### The Maintenance of Stasis
Though they do not move, they are not idle. Their purpose is the "Correction." Because the world is a "Static, Roaring Dream," any intrusion of true physics—a falling pebble, a stray thought of "change"—is a wound in their reality. 

If a visitor were to enter this space, they would see the Gentry as gargantuan, decorative ornaments. But if that visitor were to drop a tool or speak a word, the "Never Changing" dynamic would react. The Gentry don't reach out to grab the falling object; rather, the "Collective Vibration" of their presence increases. They intensify their stillness until the air around the falling object thickens into a solid, forcing the object to stop mid-air, cementing it into the architecture. They do not fight; they simply incorporate.

### The Sentience of the Botanical
Beyond the Gentry, there are the **Lichen-Minds**. These are larger entities, the "plazas" and "aqueducts" themselves. In the Marsh, the line between a person and a porch is non-existent. The gargantuan gourds are not just homes; they are the elders of the species. They are slow-pulse organisms that have grown so large they have become geography. 

When you walk across a bridge of living pith, you are walking across the outstretched arm of a Being that has been in that exact position for ten thousand years. It does not mind your weight; your presence is merely a momentary shimmer in the amber light, a brief ripple in a world where time has finally, mercifully, stopped.
