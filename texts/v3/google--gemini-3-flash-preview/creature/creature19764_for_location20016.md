To inhabit this place is to exist in a state of perpetual desiccation, a life lived entirely in the "click" and the "hum." The citizens of this vertical dry-world are not inhabitants of the city so much as they are the mobile, sensory organs of the infrastructure itself.

### The Weaver-Kin (The Architects)
At the center of the communal life are the **Weaver-Kin**, the primary sapient presence. They are tall, gracile beings whose bodies resemble a confluence of brittle porcelain and tightly wound hemp. They possess no internal fluid; their "circulation" is a pressurized pneumatic system of the same organic gases that power the world-trees. 

Their skin is a matte, grey-white integument that feels like fine-grit sandpaper. They do not have mouths, for they do not eat; they possess a series of vertical slits along their ribcage that filter the pollen-thick air. To speak is to vibrate these slits, producing a sound like wind whistling through a dry canyon or the crinkle of heavy vellum.

They are obsessed with **Tension**. A Weaver-Kin can be found standing perfectly still for hours on a suspended catwalk, a single long, spindly finger resting on a resin cable. They are listening to the "strain" of the city. They understand the health of their neighborhood by the pitch of the vibrations traveling through the boles. If a world-tree begins to lean or a bellow-pump loses its rhythm, they feel it as a phantom itch in their own joints.

### The Pressure-Tenders
Beneath the Weaver-Kin in the social hierarchy are the **Tenders**, the multi-limbed, chitinous tool-users mentioned in the shadows. They are stubby, heavy, and built for torque. Their carapaces are reinforced with the same silicates found in the tree-trunks, making them look like walking geodes.

The Tenders live in the "Hot Zones" directly adjacent to the bronze bellows. Their purpose is the maintenance of the **Graft**. They possess specialized, drill-like appendages that they use to tap into the xylem of the world-trees, installing the brass valves and iron taps that bleed out the essential resins. They are the mechanics of the forest, and they communicate via a rapid-fire code of rhythmic tapping against the metal pipes. To a visitor, it sounds like a thousand typewriters working at a distance; to a Tender, it is a sophisticated, real-time map of the city’s industrial health.

### The Glider-Nomads (The Pollinators)
Far above the neighborhoods, in the violet twilight between the solar-equivalent spotlights, live the **Aeolians**. These are the "dry-bloods" that have mastered the verticality of the world. They are more moth-like than humanoid, draped in tattered, translucent cloaks that are actually extensions of their own respiratory membranes.

The Aeolians are the merchants of the void. Because the city is divided into disconnected districts suspended in the "ribs" of the moon, the Aeolians provide the only connective tissue. They do not fly so much as they navigate the complex thermal drafts exhaled by the factory-pods. They carry "spore-blueprints"—the genetic seeds required to build new furniture or extend a platform—from one district to another. They are silent, drifting through the golden light-pillars like scorched scraps of lace, coming to rest on the vertical trunks with a dry, scratching sound.

### The Biological "Gods" (The Primary Trunks)
The most significant "individuals" in this realm are the world-trees themselves. Though they appear as landscape, the inhabitants treat them as slow-thinking, sentient titans. Each trunk has a name and a distinct "flavor" of resin. 

There is a form of **Civic Religion** based around the orientation of the trunks. The Weaver-Kin believe the trees are mineralizing the chaos of the moon's core into a perfect, geometric order. To "sin" is to introduce moisture—to spill a solvent or allow a seal to leak—which would cause the sacred, rigid cellulose to soften and rot. Rot is the only "hell" they recognize; a world where the sharp edges of their geometry become soft, weeping, and directionless.

### The Life Cycle: The Calcification
Death in this world is not a disappearance, but a final hardening. When a Weaver-Kin or a Tender reaches the end of their utility, they do not decompose. Instead, they find a gap in the architecture—a place where a new support strut or a decorative buttress is needed. They lock their limbs into a specific fractal pattern and cease their pneumatic huffing.

Over several decades, the grey dust of the atmosphere blankets them, and the ambient resins of the air begin to bond with their chitin. They literally become part of the city. The catwalk you walk upon is made of the ancestors; the wall of your factory-pod is the fossilized embrace of a former worker. In this dry subterranean vault, to live is to serve the machine, and to die is to become the ground.
