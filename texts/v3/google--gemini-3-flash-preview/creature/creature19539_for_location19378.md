To live in the Hohl-Wood Trench is to be a function of the machine. Evolution here has not favored the quick or the warm; instead, it has distilled life into a series of grinding, glacial cycles. The inhabitants of this wooden rift are not merely residents—they are the maintenance crew, the parasites, and the biological lubricants of a stagnant, industrial ecosystem.

### The Grinders (The Vestigial Custodians)
The most common "humanoid" presence is not human at all, but rather the **Silt-Scribes**. These are the descendants of the original engineers who brought the Industry to the Trench, now so thoroughly integrated into the biome that their skin has taken on the texture of the obsidian bark. They are tall, waif-like figures with limbs that move only in a series of harsh, mechanical jerks. 

They do not speak; their vocal cords have ossified into rigid, flute-like structures that resonate with the low-frequency roar of the roots. They possess "Sap-Sights"—heavy, goggles-like growths of translucent resin that filter the dim lichen-phosphorescence. A Silt-Scribe might spend forty years standing before a single colossal piston, holding a long, iron-wood rod to scrape the rime-ice from the gears. They move so slowly that the grey lichen grows across their shoulders, making them indistinguishable from the pumps they tend. They eat by pressing their palms against the drip-pulse basins, absorbing the antifreeze-sugars through their porous, hardened skin.

### The Chitinous Heavy-weights
Beneath the armored plates of the **Frost-Weevils**, there is no soft tissue. Their interiors are a pressurized hydraulic system of amber-colored sap. These sled-sized scavengers are the only things that truly navigate the open voids between the Root-Arches. Their legs are tipped with diamond-hard mandibles designed to shave the frozen resin from the bark. 

Because they move at the speed of the world, they are often mistaken for outcroppings of the terrain. A traveler might use a "dormant" Weevil as a resting ledge, only to realize—six hours later—that the ledge has moved three inches to the left. They communicate through vibration; by striking their heavy carapaces against the iron-hard trunks, they send signals through the tectonic wood that can be felt for miles. This is the "clack" in the "groan"—the only punctuation in the Trench’s eternal roar.

### The Parasitic Industry: The Valve-Ticks
Where the cast-iron Pumps meet the living wood, a specialized form of life has emerged: the **Valve-Ticks**. These are fist-sized, metallic-looking beetles that have evolved to consume the soot and lubricant of the machines rather than the sap of the trees. They cluster around the joints of the three-story pistons, their many legs shaped like wrenches and pliers. 

They are a "beneficial" parasite; as they scrape away the carbon buildup to feed themselves, they prevent the machines from seizing up. They vibrate at the exact frequency of the engine they inhabit. If a Pump stops, the Valve-Ticks die instantly, their internal pressures failing as the warmth of the friction vanishes.

### The Aerial Ghosts: The Veil-Soarers
High above, where the "ceiling-canopy" releases its rhythmic drip-pulse, live the **Veil-Soarers**. These are thin, translucent entities that resemble sheets of frozen parchment. They have no wings to flap; instead, they catch the rising heat-shimmer from the muffled furnaces of the Pumps. 

They spend centuries drifting on the stagnant air currents, their long, crystalline tendrils trailing behind them to catch the fine, stinging condensation. They are the only creatures that see the Trench from above, looking down on the jagged, splintered ridges of the wood-plates. To a Veil-Soarer, the grinding roar of the Trench is a song, and the slow chug of the pistons is the heartbeat of their god.

### The Great Integration
Nothing here dies in the traditional sense. When a Silt-Scribe finally stops moving, or a Frost-Weevil’s hydraulics fail, they do not rot. The cold is an embalmer. Instead, the slow, tectonic growth produced by the "Camber" eventually overtakes them. 

Within a century, a fallen scavenger is absorbed into the Root-Arches, its chitinous shell becoming a new layer of obsidian bark, its mineral-rich internals fueling the next three-year unfurling of a single, frozen leaf. In the Hohl-Wood Trench, life is a slow, metallic prayer, and every creature is merely a gear waiting to be ground into the Substrate.
