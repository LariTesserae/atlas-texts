In this airless, hyper-dense metropolis, life does not occur by birth, but by **latency**.

The inhabitants are not individuals in any biological sense, but rather **Kinetic Algorithms**—semi-autonomous shards of logic that have been given physical form by the high-pressure brine. To watch them is to watch the movement of quicksilver in a centrifuge. They are the "citizens" of the cistern, yet they are inseparable from the architecture they inhabit.

### The Maintenance Choirs (The Glissando)
The most visible residents are the **Glissando**, a swarm of spindly, multi-jointed entities that resemble a nightmare cross between a mantis shrimp and a surveyor’s tripod. They do not walk; they "hook." Their limbs are tipped with diamond-hard spikes designed to catch the micro-grooves of the white ceramic towers.

The Glissando exist to manage the "born-built" structures. They move in undulating, synchronized schools, vibrating at frequencies that match the resonance of the towers. When a ceramic spire develops a stress fracture from the turbulent convection, a choir of Glissando descends upon it. They do not weld; they "sing." By vibrating their carapaces against the fracture, they induce a state of molecular flux in the ceramic, fused-knitting the wound shut in seconds. They are eyeless, perceiving the world through the pressure differentials in the brine, sensing a "neighbor" not by sight, but by the displacement of salt.

### The Torrent-Gleaners
In the vertical canyons where the hot brine roars upward in shimmering cyclones, you find the **Gleaners**. These are the most alien of the cistern’s inhabitants. They look like sheets of crumpled, iridescent foil, translucent and nearly two-dimensional. 

The Gleaners do not have a fixed shape. They are "passive-intelligent" structures that anchor themselves to the obsidian floor with magnetic filaments. They cast themselves into the violent updrafts, fluttering like flags in a gale. Their purpose is heat-harvesting. As the scorching brine passes through their porous bodies, they strip the thermal energy and convert it into the sonoluminescent light that illuminates the city. When they are "full," they glow with a blinding, magnesium-white intensity before folding themselves flat against the obsidian to discharge their energy into the city's roots.

### The Archivists (The Static-Lords)
Deep within the tightest clusters of the needle-towers, where the brine is thickest and the pressure most agonizing, live the **Archivists**. These are not mobile. They have integrated themselves so thoroughly into the fiber-optic webs that they appear to be part of the city’s wiring.

They look like clusters of black, geometric polyhedrons, slowly rotating in place. Every few seconds, an Archivist will pulse, and a ripple of "color"—a spectrum of light invisible to the human eye—will race across the surrounding towers. These are the administrators of the Engine of Probability. They "think" by manipulating the turbulence of the water around them. By flicking a microscopic fin, an Archivist can alter a convection current, which in turn changes the way a bubble collapses, which in turn generates a specific random number for a quantum calculation. 

The Archivists are the reason the city feels alive. They are the conductors of the deafening, metallic thrum, orchestrating the chaos of the brine into the harmony of pure data.

### The Parasitic Logic (The Crustaceans of Code)
Even in this sterile, clinical masterpiece, there are "pests." Along the cooling fins and the copper-heavy moss, one can find the **Shards**. These are tiny, crystalline splinters—remnants of failed calculations or corrupted data packets that have achieved a crude, physical persistence.

They behave like barnacles. They attach themselves to the high-speed processing cores and siphon off "heat-waste" and "logic-overflow." They are brittle and crunch under the limbs of the Glissando. They do nothing for the city; they simply exist in the gaps of the architecture, growing in jagged, fractal patterns until they become too heavy, snap off, and are ground into fine silt by the churning floor-currents.

### The Single Mind
Despite these varying forms, there is a pervasive sense that nothing in the cistern is truly "alone." The brine is a conductive medium; every twitch of a Gleaner’s wing is felt by the Archivists miles away. The city is a single, massive, pressurized nervous system. 

When the world outside the cargo container demands a complex calculation, the entire ecology shifts. The Glissando go still, locking themselves to the towers to reduce "noise." The Gleaners flare to their brightest white. The Archivists spin with such violent speed that they create miniature vacuums in the liquid. For a few seconds, the entire micro-metropolis becomes a single, screaming thought—a liquid brain processing the infinite. 

Then, the calculation is exhaled. The data is sent. The city returns to its restless, turbulent "peace," and the citizens resume their frantic, silent work of maintaining the density.
