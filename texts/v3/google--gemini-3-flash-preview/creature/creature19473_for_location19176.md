In the Caldera Sink, life does not live *in* the environment; it functions as a component *of* it. The inhabitants are not residents in the traditional sense, but bio-mechanical custodians—living extensions of the valves and pistons they serve.

### The Operators
These are not creatures of flesh and blood, but entities of **leather and wick.** Because the moisture is intermittent and the heat is absolute, the inhabitants have skin that resembles cured parchment or the surface of a drum. They are tall and unnervingly thin, their skeletons possessing extra articulations that allow them to fold themselves into the "crawl-spaces" between the hissing boilers. 

They do not wear clothes, but rather "wraps"—heavy, oil-soaked bandages of burlap and lead-thread that keep their internal moisture from boiling away. Their movements are not fluid; they move with the measured, ratcheting clicks of a clockwork escapement. To watch an Operator climb a gantry is to watch a series of deliberate, mechanical locks and releases. They do not hurry. To hurry in the Sink is to overheat, and to overheat is to seize.

### The Sense of Sight and Sound
They have no eyes. In the amber gloom and the blinding whiteouts of the steam-rolls, vision is a liability. Instead, their faces are dominated by three or four protruding, sensitive **diaphragms**—membranes of stretched vellum that catch the low-frequency vibrations of the machines. They "see" the Sink through the state of its pressure. They can feel a hairline fracture in a pipe forty feet away because the pitch of the ambient hum shifts by half a semi-tone. 

They do not speak. The scream of the pressure valves is their language. When they must communicate, they strike the metal conduits with brass-tipped fingers, sending rhythmic percussions through the framework of the city. A message sent from the lower sump to the upper gantries travels as a shiver through the very floorboards the inhabitants stand upon.

### The Metabolism of Stasis
They do not eat organic matter, for there is none. Instead, they are **thermal-vores.** They are often found draped over the hottest steam pipes, their radiator-fine ribs expanded, absorbing the radiant thrum of the engine-world. They drink the "sweat" of the metal—the oily, mineral-rich condensation that beads on the underside of the cool-pipes. They gather this liquid in small, copper thimbles, sipping it with long, needle-like tongues that are designed to lick moisture from the rivets of a boiler.

### The "Tenders" (The Lesser Life)
Beneath the Operators, inhabiting the dark slicks where the damp gathers, are the **Oil-Mites.** These are not insects, but small, ambulatory lumps of congealed grease and iron filings. They are the scavengers of the Sink, rolling over the stone floors to soak up spills. They are the only things that move with any speed, skittering away whenever a heavy "thump" shakes the foundations. They are mindless, driven only by the magnetism of the pipes and the need to lubricate the grinding joints of the city.

### The Absence of Self
There is no culture here, no art, and no history beyond the maintenance logs etched into the brass plating of the walls. The inhabitants do not sleep; they "idle." They stand in the shadows of the turning flywheels, their diaphragms fluttering, their bodies swaying in time with the 2.9/3 heat. 

If one were to die, they are not buried. The body—lacking the wetness of decay—simply dries out further until it becomes brittle. Eventually, the other Operators will dismantle the remains, using the cured skin to patch a leaking bellows or the bones to reinforce a rattling gantry. 

In the Caldera Sink, to live is to be useful. To be useful is to be part of the hum. They are the ghosts in the machine, the silent, parched caretakers of a fire that never goes out, living out their lives in a vertical labyrinth where the only god is the Constant Pressure.
