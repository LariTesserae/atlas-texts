The inhabitants of the Silt-Hollows are the **Litho-Arachne**, though they have no name for themselves other than *Those Who Clasp*. 

They are creatures of joint and sinew, possessing six elongated limbs that end in multi-articulated digits. These digits do not have pads or nails, but rather thousands of microscopic, hook-like cilia that allow them to adhere to the violet pumice as if they were part of the geology itself. Their skin is not moist; it is a shimmering, chitinous hide the color of bruised plums and dried blood, textured like fine-grit sandpaper. It functions as a porous lung, absorbing the ambient nitrogen through its entire surface area. 

To look upon a Litho-Arachne is to see a study in stillness. Their movement is paradoxical—when they transit between the vertical plazas, they are a blur of mathematical precision, but their natural state is one of profound, statuesque meditation. They can remain motionless for days, their limbs folded into geometric riddles, mimicking the jagged silhouettes of the Dust-Fronds to avoid the gaze of aerial scavengers.

### The Anatomy of the Unblinking
Their faces are dominated by three primary eyes—wide, amber orbs set in a triangular formation. These eyes do not possess lids, for there is no moisture to replenish; instead, a thin, transparent film of silica protects the lens. They do not blink, but they can rotate their pupils independently, tracking the slow, grinding drift of the silt-clouds above. 

They do not speak with vocal cords. In the thin, dry air, sound is a precious resource used for navigation and warning. Instead, they communicate through **Stridulation**. By rubbing specialized, serrated plates on their forelimbs against their segmented abdomens, they produce a range of sounds—from a low, cello-like thrum used for intimate greetings to a piercing, metallic rasp that signals danger. A conversation in the Silt-Hollows sounds like a workshop of clockmakers at work, a rhythmic clicking and scraping that vibrates through the very rock they inhabit.

### The Sower-Artisans
The inhabitants are inseparable from their environment. They do not "build" in the traditional sense; they *curate*. Every Litho-Arachne is born with a specific instinctive craft. 

There are the **Silt-Sifters**, who possess elongated, fan-like appendages for hands. They spend their lives on the outer edges of the dish-like depressions, catching the mineral-heavy drafts and sorting the dust by weight and metallic content. This dust is their bread, their water, and their mortar.

There are the **Weavers of the Vine-Hair**, who cultivate a parasitic, silver-black thread that grows in the shadow-crevices of the Fronds. They braid these threads into the bridges and hammocks that lace the city together. The bridges are not static; they are living extensions of the ecology, vibrating with the pulse of the wind, singing "guide-tones" that tell the blind or the young exactly where the path lies.

### The Shadow-Fauna
Living alongside the Litho-Arachne is a frantic, lesser ecosystem of **Litho-vores**. 
*   **The Copper-Gliders:** Small, flat creatures resembling sheets of hammered metal. They have no wings, but rather stiff, sail-like fins that catch the thermal updrafts of the vertical jungle. They feed on the bioluminescent mineral pastes applied to the walls, often becoming glowing pests that the inhabitants must gently scrape away.
*   **The Marrow-Beetles:** Loaf-sized, armored tanks with mandibles strong enough to crush volcanic glass. They are the "livestock" of the Silt-Hollows. The inhabitants don't eat them, but rather "milk" them for a thick, amber secretion—a biological resin that, when mixed with silt, creates the indestructible bricks used to seal the rock-cells against the cold of the zenith-night.

### The Great Waiting
There is no concept of "fast" in the Silt-Hollows. Because their chemistry is nitrogen-based and non-aqueous, their metabolism is slow—sublime and glacial. A Litho-Arachne might spend a year carving a single decorative relief into a doorway, using a tooth of obsidian to shave away the violet rock micron by micron. 

They are a people of touch. In the crowded proximity of the vertical hamlet, privacy is not found in distance, but in the closing of the eyes. To touch another's limb is to share heat; to huddle in the deep thickets of the Fronds is to participate in a collective survival. 

In the center of the hamlet, in the deepest, most sheltered hollow, lives the **Elder-Cyst**. This is not a person, but a fused colonial organism of the oldest inhabitants who have literally grown into the rock. Their chitin has hardened into the violet pumice; their nervous systems have mapped into the mineral veins of the peak. They are the memory of the city, vibrating the history of the Silt-Hollows through the ground, a low-frequency hum that tells those who clasp the walls that they are home, they are high, and they are eternal.
