In this world of architectural flesh and sentient masonry, the inhabitants do not just live *upon* the landscape; they are its ongoing maintenance, its nervous system, and its metabolic pulse. There is no distinction between the citizen and the city.

### The Weaver-Architects
At the center of the vertical sprawl are the **Loom-Kings**, colossal, many-limbed custodians that resemble a cross between a soft-shelled tortoise and a weaver bird. They possess no eyes, only sensitive, fan-like antennae that catch the subtlest shift in the terracotta’s vibration. They are the ones who continue the work of epochs. With specialized secretory glands, they produce a thick, resinous spittle that, when mixed with the omnipresent silt and crushed vine fiber, hardens into new terracotta. 

They move with an agonizing, geologic slowness. A Loom-King might spend forty years perched over a single cracked archway, its tong-like appendages delicately knitting together the lattice of vine and clay until the wound is healed. They are the source of the "rhythmic thrum," a communal groan of effort that vibrates through the foundations, signaling to all lesser life that the world remains structurally sound.

### The Scintillating Parasites
Clinging to the underbellies of the great spans are the **Vane-Stems**. To a casual observer, they appear to be part of the vegetation—long, drooping ribbons of indigo flesh—but they are predatory colonies of colonial hydras. They feed on the "soup of wings," extending microscopic, sticky threads into the updrafts to snag the drifting moths. 

When a Vane-Stem catches a meal, it flushes with a dim, bioluminescent fever—a bruise-colored throb of light that provides the only visual landmark in the lightless canyons. They are the "streetlamps" of this world, though their light is a signal of digestion rather than a guide for travelers.

### The Lithic Grazer
The "lizards the length of logs" are the **Slick-Backs**. Their skin has evolved to mimic the kiln-fired texture of the world precisely. They do not eat the plants, but rather the "bio-film"—the layer of bacteria, moss, and mineral salts that accumulates on the damp clay. Their bellies are flat and equipped with thousands of microscopic suction-cilia, allowing them to sleep vertically for decades without falling. 

They move only when the "breathing" of the world reaches a certain humidity, sliding across the terracotta like drops of oil. They are the polishers; it is their rhythmic, abrasive sliding over the course of millennia that has rounded the sharp corners of the great stairs and turned the rough-fired clay into a surface as smooth as obsidian.

### The Echo-Moths
The great, shield-sized moths are not merely fliers but the primary carriers of the world’s "genetic memory." Their velvet wings are coated in a fine, silver dust—a prehistoric pollen that they transport from the deep wells to the high spires. They are almost entirely silent, their flight a muffled displacement of air. They possess a strange, communal intelligence; when they gather in thousands atop a high-altitude shelf, their wings beat in synchronization, acting as biological bellows that force fresh, thin air down into the suffocating depths of the honeycomb. They are the fans of the great engine, cooling the thermal bulk of the clay.

### The Burrowing Chorus
Inside the porous walls themselves live the **Click-Beetles**. If the Loom-Kings are the architects, the beetles are the marrow. They live within the very clay they helped bake in the distant past. They vibrate their iridescent wing-cases to create friction, generating a portion of the heat that keeps the world warm. 

The "deafening roar" of the environment is primarily their song—a ceaseless, grinding friction of chitin against terracotta. This vibration serves a dual purpose: it wards off larger predators and prevents the humid clay from settling or cracking by keeping the entire structure in a state of high-frequency micro-agitation. To live here is to be constantly massaged by the sound of twenty billion insects singing the world into cohesion.

### The Silken Scavengers
Deep in the cisterns, where the runoff from the damp walls collects, live the **Fluid-Mimics**. Lacking bones or carapaces, they are translucent, gelatinous shapes that take the form of the decorative basins they inhabit. They wait for the slow decay—a fallen moth, a withered vine, or a spent Loom-King—to wash down into their pools. They are the world’s digestive tract, breaking down complex matter into the rich, black silt that the Weaver-Architects then use to build the next layer of the world.

There is no "commoner" or "elite" in this place. Every creature is a gear in a biological clock that has been ticking since before the sky turned charcoal. They are a single, colossal organism of clay and breath, enduring the dark with a terrible, patient majesty.
