To inhabit the District of the Clockwork Humors is not to live in the sense of a heartbeat, but to pulsate in the sense of a current. 

The residents of these basalt cylinders are the **Calibrated**. They are beings of narrow, vertical proportions, dressed in garments of heavy, oil-cured leather and vulcanized rubber that mimic the textures of the pipes they tend. Their skin has taken on the violet hue of the schist walls, translucent enough to reveal the "Humors" within—not blood, but a circulatory system of slow-moving, mercury-thick fluids that respond to the magnetic pull of the city’s core.

They do not walk so much as they dock. When a resident moves along the slick iron sky-bridges, their gait is rhythmic and heavy, designed to maintain a constant grounding with the mineral world. They do not sleep. Instead, they enter a state of "Liturgy," standing motionless in recessed wall-niches, leur hands gripped onto copper grounding rods. In this state, their eyes—large, lidless, and amber-colored—remain wide open, reflecting the perpetual grey-gold glow of the gas-globes while their internal humors synchronize with the Harmonic Grind.

### The Liturgists of the Valve
Social hierarchy is determined by one’s proximity to the "Source-Pistons." At the lowest tiers, near the dampest vents, live the **Siphon-Kin**. These individuals have undergone so much industrial integration that they are nearly indistinguishable from the Siphon-Hounds they live among. They possess long, flexible fingers tipped with needle-fine brass used to clear the pores of the schist or to tune the vibration of a humming pipe. They communicate through a series of rhythmic taps on the metalwork, a tactile language that travels miles through the iron architecture in an instant.

Higher up, in the tiers that overlook the central canals, dwell the **Humoralists**. They are the curators of the Stasis. They carry heavy, glass-walled canisters strapped to their chests, containing samples of the city’s fluids—the oils, the steam-condensate, the quicksilver. Their task is to ensure that the rust never thickens and the puddles never shrink. They are the enemies of change. If a new scratch appears on a railing, a Humoralist is there within the hour, buffing the metal back to its centuries-old state of "perfect wear." To them, a new thing is an obscenity; only the weathered is holy.

### The Glass-Wing Harvesters
There are also the **Aperients**, who dwell on the outer rims where the density of Glass-Wings is highest. These people are draped in fine, metallic mesh capes. They do not kill the translucent beetles; instead, they "harvest" the vibration. By placing their palms against the vibrating shells of the Glass-Wings, these residents absorb the high-frequency tremors that the beetles generate from the city's heat. They then "discharge" this energy into the dark iron walls of their homes, heating the stone from the inside out. They are the warmth-bringers of the District, though their warmth is as dry and mechanical as a friction-burn.

### The Psychology of the Harmonic Grind
The minds of those who live here are shaped by the **Monolith of Sound**. They have no concept of "quiet." To them, silence would be a violent, terrifying rupture—a sign that the universe had ceased to function. Their thoughts are structured like the loops of the canals: circular, repetitive, and devoid of the "linear" anxiety of progress. They do not hope for a better tomorrow because "tomorrow" is an alien concept. They only seek the "Eternal Now."

In the District, there are no children. The population is constant. When a resident finally "oxidizes"—reaches a point where their internal humors can no longer find the rhythm—they do not die in a biological sense. They are taken to the lowest drains, disassembled with surgical precision, and their mineral components are fed back into the furnaces to be recast. Every inhabitant wears the recycled brass of their ancestors; every lung inhales the steam that once flowed through the veins of their predecessors.

They are the white blood cells of a lithic god. They move with a terrifying, slow-motion grace, their every action a ritual of maintenance. They do not dream; they merely resonate. As you stand on that vibrating balcony, they do not look at you with curiosity or fear. To them, you are simply a foreign vibration—a brief, irregular hiccup in the Great Chord that the city will eventually, inevitably, smooth away.
