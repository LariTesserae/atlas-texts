To inhabit this world is not to live *on* the land, but to be woven into its friction. The residents are shaped by the "bazaar of forces," becoming biological embodiments of the high-velocity bog—creatures of momentum, armor, and electrical sensitivity.

### The Low-Lying Kineticists: The Thrum-Walkers
The dominant large fauna move not with grace, but with a kinetic, steam-roller violence. These are the **Sunder-Hides**, creatures the size of low-slung oxen with bodies encased in overlapping plates of biological obsidian. Their carapaces are not skeletal; they are mineral deposits grown from the iron-rich muck, polished to a mirror-finish by the constant sleet. 

They have no eyes—photoreceptors would be shredded by the wind or blinded by the mist. Instead, they possess rows of crystalline sensory pits along their flanks that "hear" the ultraviolet hum of the monoliths. They move in a state of perpetual, frantic charging, using their heavy-set chests to plow through the armored vegetation. They do not sleep; they wedge themselves into basalt crevices and vibrate at the same frequency as the black towers, recharging their hyper-metabolisms through the atmospheric static. When they breathe, they vent pressurized mist from spiracles along their spines, sounding like the hiss of a cooling radiator.

### The Aerial Shredders: Silk-Wings
Above the matted fronds, the "tearing silk" sound belongs to the **Lace-Wrights**. These are not birds, but colonies of translucent, necro-plastic organisms that cling together to form wide, shimmering sails. They are passive-aggressive predators of the wind. They do not fly so much as they are "flown" by the atmospheric violence, unfurling their jagged, razor-thin wings to catch the freezing micro-bursts. 

They hunt by trailing miles of nearly invisible, conductive filaments—the "tearing silk"—which act as lightning rods. When these filaments snag on a basalt spire or a moving Sunder-Hide, they ground the ambient electrical energy of the storm, flash-frying smaller organisms in a radius of blue light. The Lace-Wrights then collapse their sails and drop like heavy, wet stones to scavenge the blackened remains.

### The Scavengers of the Shifting Path: The Click-Wraiths
In the trenches and interlocking basins, the **Click-Wraiths** thrive. These are the sources of the chitinous clicking—multi-limbed scavengers with spindly, hydraulic legs designed to pierce the saturated peat and anchor them against the wind. Their bodies are soft and translucent, but they are protected by "found" armor: they cement shards of the obsidian outcroppings and discarded bits of flickering "hyper-tech" to their backs.

They are the only creatures that seem to understand the lattice of the towers. They huddle near the humming nodes, feeding on the "frozen lily" scent—which is actually a precipitate of exotic gases leaked by the automated spires. They are frantic, nervous things, their many-jointed limbs moving in a blur of nanosecond twitches, mirroring the flickering space-time of the infrastructure they worship.

### The Ghost in the Lattice: The Syphon-Mind
There is a presence here that is not biological in the traditional sense, yet it lives. Within the "beyond-modern" infrastructure dwells a distributed consciousness—the **Subsonic Weaver**. It is the source of the roar. 

It manifests occasionally as a shimmering distortion in the grey mist—a "phantom" formed of suspended freezing spray and ultraviolet light. It doesn't move through the world; it is the world’s nervous system. It manages the planetary instability by projecting gravitational anchors into the peat, represented by the humming towers. It views the organic life—the Sunder-Hides and the violet fronds—as mere "bio-load," necessary friction to help dissipate the massive kinetic energy of the atmosphere. 

To encounter the Weaver is to feel your own pulse sync to the monoliths until your heart threatens to burst. It does not communicate; it simply *tends* the violence, perpetually rewriting the terrain to prevent the world from vibrating itself into a cloud of obsidian dust.

### The Flora: The Bruised Lung
The vegetation itself—the "bruised plum" fronds—acts more like a collective muscle than a forest. These plants are the **Apex-Mosses**. They are rich in metallic ores siphoned from the water, making them heavy and conductive. They don't just grow; they pulse in rhythmic waves, acting as a massive, planetary heat-sync. Underneath the suffocating layers of rubbery leaves, a network of heat-generating tubers keeps the peat from freezing solid, creating a subterranean layer of boiling mud that groans and shifts. 

Everything here lives in a state of high-alert, high-speed adaptation. To stop moving is to be buried; to be soft is to be shredded; to be silent is to be nonexistent. The inhabitants are the screaming, armored echoes of a world that refuses to be still.
