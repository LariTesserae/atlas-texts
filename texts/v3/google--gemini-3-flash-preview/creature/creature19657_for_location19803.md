To inhabit the Atrium of the Vein-Lace is to be an extension of its geometry. There is no distinction here between the inhabitant and the architecture; the things that move through these vitreous voids are less "animals" and more "mobile fragments" of the lattice itself.

### The Surgeons of the Static
The primary denizens are the **Knot-Keepers**, entities that resemble clusters of discarded violins and glass shards. They are not born; they are "pushed" out of the thicker junctions of the lattice when the metabolic friction becomes too intense. They are spindly, multi-jointed beings whose limbs are indistinguishable from the translucent stalks they climb. 

The Knot-Keepers lack faces. Instead, their heads are flat, reflective planes—sensory mirrors that catch the eternal 4:00 PM radiance. They do not eat; they "plug" themselves into the weeping pores of the stalks during the five-second mist-storms, acting as living conduits that direct the silver moisture toward the younger, sharper buds. They move with a terrifying, stop-motion grace, their glass-on-glass joints clicking in time with the rhythmic grinding of the world-walls.

### The Echo-Parasites
Living within the vast, hollow gulfs between the struts are the **Vitreous Gulls**. These are not birds, but sheets of sentient, translucent film that have detached from the walls. They do not flap; they catch the "inhales" of the Atrium, surfing the tectonic vibrations of the air. 

They are the scavengers of sound. Because the Atrium is deafening, these creatures have evolved to consume the sonic energy of the grinding stalks. When a particularly loud roar of sap pulses through a conduit, the Gulls swarm toward the noise, their ribbon-like bodies vibrating until they glow a pale, bruised violet. In the silence that follows the mist-storms, they are invisible, hanging motionless in the vacuum, pinned by gravity like pressed flowers in a ledger of air.

### The Tool-Born (The Handless)
Because the plants have grown "tools"—axes, baskets, ladders—without a civilization to use them, the Atrium has responded by manifesting the **Lace-Walkers**. These are the most unsettling inhabitants: bipedal, hollow shells of woven fiber that lack internal organs. They are the "logic-ghosts" of the flora.

A Lace-Walker does not possess a will; it possesses a *function*. One might spend a century standing on a "stair" that leads to nowhere, repeatedly grasping a plant-grown "hammer" and striking a translucent stalk to test its resonance. They are the gardeners of a pre-determined world, mindless mimics of a humanity the Atrium has sensed through the strata but never met. They move with the heavy, hydraulic sigh of sap, their bodies rattling like wind chimes. If broken, they do not bleed; they simply spill dry, diamond-hard seeds that immediately begin to weave back into the floor-lattice.

### The Pinned Swarms
The "gnats" mentioned in the geography are actually the **Gravity-Midges**. They are the only creatures that seem to possess true autonomy, yet they are the most restricted. Because they are "pinned" to the air by the lattice’s gravitational anomalies, they live their entire lives in a three-inch sphere of space. 

They are the pollinators of the void. They vibrate at such high frequencies that they turn the golden-green light into tiny sparks of heat. This heat triggers the "young" erosion of the nearby leaves, causing them to sharpen further. The Midges are the reason the Atrium is so dangerous to touch; they are the whetstones of the forest, keeping the glass blades of the vegetation honed to a molecular edge.

### The Pulse-Lurker
Deep within the thickest knots of the central trunk-weave, where the "subway roar" of the sap is loudest, lives the **Organ-Loom**. It is not a single creature, but a communal mass of flesh that has been cannibalized by the vegetable lattice. 

It is the Atrium’s heartbeat. It is a soft, pulsing lung the size of a cathedral transept, woven into the very fiber of the stalks. It filters the "Sapien-logic" from the rocks above, dreaming of stairs, hammers, and rooms, and then screaming those dreams into the plants so they might grow them. It is the architect’s brain, trapped in a cage of its own design, sweating the silver moisture that the rest of the world drinks. It is the only thing in the Atrium that is soft, and it is guarded by a thousand razor-sharp blades that never stop growing.
