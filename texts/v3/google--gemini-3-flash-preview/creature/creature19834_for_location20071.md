In the Basilica of the Seeping Spires, life does not struggle to exist; it has already won. The inhabitants are the inheritors of an ending, creatures of a finished world who have traded the frantic drive of evolution for a state of perpetual, ornamental grace. 

### The Refraction-Kin
The dominant presence in the Basilica is not an individual beast, but the **Looming Weavers**. These are the "heron-lemurs" of the silt-flats, though as one watches them, they seem less like animals and more like mobile extensions of the landscape. Their bones are not calcium, but a flexible, semi-transparent cartilage that mimics the "Relics of Intent." Their skin is a pale, pearlescent membrane that absorbs the ambient glow of the sky. 

They move with a terrifyingly beautiful economy. Because gravity is an "option" here, they do not walk so much as they lean into the air, their long, needle-thin toes barely indenting the surface tension of the mineral pools. They do not possess mouths in the traditional sense; their faces are smooth plates of sensory chitin. They feed by standing beneath the Seeping Spires, extending their porous, fibrous manes—which unfurl like solar sails—to drink the nutrient-dense mist. To see a dozen Weavers standing motionless around a Spire is to see a living frieze, a silent congregation of priests absorbing the breath of an ancient earth.

### The Overlap Predators
Conflict in the Basilica has been refined into a process of "molecular negotiation." There is no tearing of flesh or spilling of blood. The apex predator here is the **Phase-Cutter**, a creature that looks like a ribbon of living quicksilver floating three feet off the ground. 

The Phase-Cutter does not "hunt" in a chase; it simply occupies the same space as its prey. It drifts toward a glimmer-fly swarm or a resting amphibian with the slow, inevitable momentum of a cloud. When it overlaps with another organism, it uses a localized frequency—much like the humming of the black glass needles—to subtly vibrate the prey's atoms until they liquefy and are absorbed through the Phase-Cutter’s skin. The prey does not scream; it simply fades, its biomass becoming part of the silver ribbon in a quiet, shimmering exchange.

### The Heralds of the Relics
Clustered around the floating geometries are the **Glint-Ticks**. These are tiny, beetle-like entities with shells of genuine sapphire, born from the accidental "edits" performed by the ancient tech. They do not eat organic matter. Instead, they cling to the hovering cubes and black needles, vibrating in sympathy with the artifacts' sub-audible hum. 

They act as a biological maintenance crew for the technology. When a Relic transmutes a patch of mud into salt-flowers, the Glint-Ticks swarm the new crystals, polishing them with their many-jointed limbs and moving the salts into precise, aesthetic patterns. They are the gardeners of the artificial, ensuring that even the "accidnets" of the god-machines adhere to the laws of Equilibrium.

### The Gossamer Drift
The air is never truly empty. It is inhabited by the **Vapor-Whales**—though "whale" is a misnomer for a creature as light as a sigh. These are massive, atmospheric jellies, hundreds of feet long but weighing less than a thimble. They are nearly invisible, their bodies composed of the same thick, luminous vapor that diffuses the sun. 

They drift through the Basilica like slow-motion ghosts, their long, trailing tentacles brushing against the tops of the Seeping Spires. They serve as the basin’s circulatory system, carrying microscopic spores and mineral salts from one end of the delta to the other. Sometimes, when the hour-long rain begins, the Vapor-Whales become heavy with moisture and descend, their translucent forms settling over the landscape like a living fog, muffling the world in a warm, wet embrace.

### The Sentience of the Silt
Perhaps the most profound inhabitants are the **Silt-Minders**. Beneath the velvet floor, in the saturated sponge of the earth, lives a collective intelligence composed of billions of thread-like white worms. They are the reason the floor feels like velvet. They constantly churn the sediment, filtering it, smoothing it, and ensuring there are no "jagged edges." 

They are the keepers of the "Final Geology." They remember the mountains that once were, and they work tirelessly to ensure those mountains never return. If a traveler were to stand still long enough, the Silt-Minders would gently begin to coat their feet, not to consume them, but to incorporate them—to soften their sharp, alien edges and turn them into yet another smooth, weeping curve of the Basilica.

### The Human Presence (The Echoes)
There are no humans here, but there are **Echoes**. Because the technology of the Basilica "edits" time and space, one occasionally sees the phantom of a figure walking between the Spires. These are not ghosts; they are temporal reflections, caught in the high surface tension of the air. A woman in a robe of spun light might be seen leaning against a black glass needle, her hand frozen inches from the surface. She is a fragment of a civilization that reached Equilibrium a million years ago and simply... stopped. She is part of the poem now, no more or less alive than the salt-flowers or the glimmer-flies.

In the Basilica, there is no "self." Everything is a component of a single, breathing machine. To live here is to be part of a masterpiece that has no intention of ever being finished, because it has already reached perfection. It is a world of quiet, busy, and terrifying beauty, where life has nowhere left to go, and so it has decided to stay exactly where it is, forever.
