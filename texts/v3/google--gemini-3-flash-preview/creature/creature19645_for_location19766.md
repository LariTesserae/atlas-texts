In a place where biology has been outpaced by the sheer velocity of mineral growth, what "lives" here does not possess a nervous system or a soft heart. Instead, the inhabitants are entities of **kinetic geometry**.

They are the **Hollow-Gilled Refractors**, creatures of articulated glass and vibrating salt. They appear first as nothing more than a thickening of the light—drifts of floating, semi-solid prisms that hover where the weeping water spirals upward. They are not born; they are precipitated. When the mineral-heavy runoff hits a pocket of the screaming wind just right, the solids crystallize mid-air, knitting themselves into fragile, spindly skeletons that resemble starbursts or the complex internal architecture of a snowflake. 

They do not breathe air. Instead, they "respire" by allowing the high-energy luminescence of the atmosphere to pass through their translucent bodies. They are living lenses. By tilting their mica-thin facets, they focus the punishing brilliance of the sky into tight, searing beams of heat, which they use to carve "veins" into the weeping pillars. They feed on the salt-pulses. You can see them clustered around the rhythmic seeps of the stone, their needle-like limbs vibrating in sync with the mountain’s *thrum-thrum*.

Deeper within the labyrinth of pillars, where the "built" elements appear, there are the **Gleaners**. These are not creatures of flesh, but of rusted clockwork and sentient brine. They are the tenders of the lead gutters and bronze basins. It is tempting to call them machines, but they are more like a coral reef that has learned to manipulate tools. They are slow, heavy, and composed of the same "melted" stone as the sound-breakers. 

The Gleaners move with a grinding, tectonic lethality. They have no faces, only a series of sensory pits that catch the "shattered bell" frequencies of the wind. They interpret the screaming air as a navigational map. They exist solely to maintain the flow of the mountain's "blood." When a silica web grows too thick and blockages occur, the Gleaners extend heavy, blunt limbs to snap the glass threads, their movements echoing the frantic, mechanical pace of the terrain. They are the curators of the mineral clockwork, ensuring that the fluid gravity and sideways rain never pool into a stagnation that might birth something soft or organic.

Lastly, there are the **Aural Voids**. These are less "beings" and more "occurrences." In the spots where the sound-breakers have failed and the wind-scream becomes physically destructive, the atmosphere occasionally collapses in on itself. This creates wandering pockets of absolute silence—sentient vacuums that drift like ghosts through the white glare. They are the only things in this landscape that do not vibrate. As they pass, the weeping water freezes into perfect, motionless spheres, and the "visual roar" of the light vanishes into a black hole of sensory deprivation. 

The Aural Voids are the apex of this place. They represent the ultimate goal of the "finished" landscape: a state where the motion is so fast it becomes still, and the noise is so loud it becomes a silence that can crush a soul. They are the predators of the mineral webs, "eating" the vibrations of the Refractors and leaving behind only brittle, lightless husks of spent quartz.

Nothing here sleeps. Nothing here dies in a way a human would recognize. There is only the continuous shedding of layers—a constant, sparkling molting as the residents are eroded by the wind and rebuilt by the water, forever becoming more like the hard, cold, weeping world they inhabit.
