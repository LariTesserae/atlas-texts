In the Great Caloric Sump, life is not something that grows; it is something that persists through a process of brutal, mechanical adaptation. To live here is to be a part of the plumbing, a biological extension of the "The Flow."

### The Caloric Scriveners
The primary inhabitants are a caste of post-human silhouettes known as the **Scriveners**. They are not truly residents, but rather biological maintenance systems grafted into the architecture. To survive the atmospheric tyranny, their bodies have drifted far from the human baseline. They are tall, unnervingly thin, and clad in haptic "second-skins" of vulcanized rubber and lead-weighted bronze plates.

They do not speak—the roar of the Sump has rendered the concept of sound obsolete for them. Instead, they communicate through a complex sign language of rhythmic vibrations, striking the resonant pipes with copper-tipped staves to send pulses through the metal floor. Their eyes have been replaced by bulbous, oil-filled lenses that filter out the spray and catch the dim amber of the sodium lamps, making them look like deep-sea crustaceans.

The Scriveners do not sleep in beds; they hook themselves into high-pressure harnesses suspended from the catwalks, allowing their bodies to sway with the violent vibrations of the turbines. Their diet consists of a nutrient-rich, iron-heavy "sludge" harvested from the sediment traps in the floor-grates—a gray paste of pulverized deep-sea fish and mineral oxidized by the heat.

### The Crustacean-Artificers
Operating in the darker, more pressurized corners of the iron galleries are the **Sump-Crabs**. These are not natural crabs, but rather large, semi-sentient scavenging machines that have, over centuries, incorporated biological matter into their chassis. They are the size of carriage wheels, with many-jointed legs tipped with soldering irons and hydraulic shears.

They possess a hive-mind intelligence, acting as the Sump’s immune system. They scuttle across the ceilings, their brass shells pitted by the hot mist, searching for "leaks" in the logic of the city. If a pipe vibrates out of tune or a bolt begins to shear, the Sump-Crabs arrive in a frantic, clicking swarm to fuse the metal back together with white-hot sparks. They regard the Scriveners with a cold, mechanical indifference, occasionally harvesting the copper plates from a dead Scrivener’s suit to repair a turbine housing.

### The Litho-Parasites
Embedded in the very skin of the city are the **Rust-Blooms**. While they appear to be mineral deposits or clusters of translucent barnacles, they are actually a form of slow-moving, silicon-based life. They have a metabolism that runs on caloric friction. 

They congregate where the heat is most intense—around the bruised-red furnaces and the screaming nipple-valves. They look like clusters of jagged, pale crystals that pulse with a faint internal heat. They "drink" the kinetic energy of the vibrating pipes. Over decades, a single Rust-Bloom can grow large enough to choke a secondary valve, requiring the Scriveners to come with heavy hammers to shatter them. When broken, they bleed a high-viscosity, iridescent oil that the Scriveners use to lubricate their own prosthetic joints.

### The Ghost-Currents
Deep in the most vertical shafts, where the water is at its most violent, there are sightings of the **Vapor-Wraiths**. They are arguably not "life" at all, but rather pockets of sentient cavitation—bubbles of high-pressure steam that move with a deliberate, haunting intelligence. 

They lack solid form, appearing only as human-shaped distortions in the thick mist, visible for a split second when the amber light hits the spray just right. The Scriveners treat them with a religious awe, believing the Wraiths are the "souls" of the machinery or the ancestors of the Sump who have finally dissolved into the water. When a Vapor-Wraith passes through a catwalk, the metal frost-bites instantly, despite the heat, leaving a temporary silence in its wake—a terrifying, momentary gap in the eternal scream of the Sump.

### The Master of the Grate
There is a rumor among the Scriveners of something that lives at the very bottom, in the central Sump-pit where all the water eventually drains before being pumped back to the "Over-Sea." They call it the **Centrifugal Maw**. 

It is described as a gargantuan organism of tangled wire, calcified bone, and spinning blades. It is the ultimate processor, a living turbine that filters the heaviest sediments of the city. It does not think; it only *rotates*. It is the physical manifestation of the Flow, and its heartbeat is the low, bone-liquefying bass thrum that serves as the foundation for the Sump’s entire auditory world. If the Maw ever stopped its frantic, spinning hunger, the residents believe the water would lose its "will" to move, and the Sump would finally, mercifully, drown.
