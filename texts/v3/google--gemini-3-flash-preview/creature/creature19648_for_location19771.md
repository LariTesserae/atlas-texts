To live in the Sump-Alley is to inhabit the space between a heartbeat and a sigh. The inhabitants of this vertical wound are not "citizens" in any conventional sense; they are **The Marrow-Keepers**, a population so deeply integrated into the plumbing of the world that the line between their anatomy and the city’s masonry has begun to blur.

### The Physiology of the Slow
The people here have been sculpted by the Static Gradient. They are tall and unnervingly thin, their frames elongated to navigate the finger-thin gaps between steam pipes and basalt. Their skin has the matte, translucent quality of a blanched almond, often mapped with a network of fine, blueish veins that mimic the circuitry of the walls they lean against.

Because the air is a "heavy broth," their breathing is nearly imperceptible. They do not gasp or pant; they have developed high-capacity, bellows-like thoracic cavities that allow them to draw oxygen from the soupy atmosphere with a single, long inhalation that can last several minutes. They move with a agonizing, liquid grace—the "patient accumulation" of the city reflected in their gait. To a surface-dweller, a Marrow-Keeper might appear to be a statue until, in the blink of an eye, they have drifted ten feet down a corridor, their bare feet making no sound on the moss-slicked iron.

### The Sensory Attunement
In a world where sound is swallowed and light is a sepia smear, the inhabitants have surrendered the dominance of sight. Their eyes are large, fixed, and amber-hued, lacking pupils but shimmering with a reflective tapetum lucidum that catches the dim phosphorescence of the orchids. 

Instead, they navigate through **Thermal Mapping** and **Scent-Memory**. Because the air sits in unmoving pockets, a Marrow-Keeper "reads" their location through their skin. They know they are home when they feel the specific 2.7 heat of a certain boiler against their left cheek and smell the distinct, unmixing pocket of "scorched copper and wet fern" that marks their threshold. They are cartographers of the invisible, navigating a map made of odors and temperature gradients.

### The Symbiotic Wardens
These people are not laborers; they are more like white blood cells within an industrial organism. Their primary function is **The Tending**. 

They carry small, soot-stained tools of bone and brass, used to delicately prune the aggressive vines away from moving gears or to graft electrical junctions into the root systems of the waxy ferns. They speak very little—the silence of the Sump is sacred to them—but when they do, it is a low-frequency hum that resonates in the bone rather than the ear, a sound that mimics the "hush-hallow" of the city’s flywheels.

They dress in "living" textiles: garments woven from the pale, rope-like vines and reinforced with plates of scavenged cast-iron. These clothes are never washed; they are allowed to grow their own micro-biomes of velvet moss, which helps the wearer blend into the architecture and provides a primitive form of cooling through evaporation.

### Social Structure: The Nested Solitude
Society in the Sump-Alley is defined by **Intimate Isolation**. Because space is at such a premium, the inhabitants live in "Nests"—small, vertical cavities carved behind steam-valves or tucked within the hollows of massive, non-functional turbines. 

There is no government, only a shared, instinctive rhythm. They do not compete for resources; the city provides a steady, mineral-rich runoff that they filter through porous stones to drink, and they harvest the leather-tough petals of the orchid-analogs for sustenance. Their lives are spent in a state of "Watchful Stasis." A Marrow-Keeper might sit on a basalt ledge for three days, motionless, simply monitoring the vibration of a steam pipe with their fingertips to ensure the pressure doesn't fluctuate. To them, the city is a pet, a god, and a body, and they are its quiet, perspiring stewards.

### The Weirdness of the Soul
The most striking thing about those who live in the Sump-Alley is their relationship with time. They do not age so much as they **petrify**. As a Marrow-Keeper enters their final decades, their movements slow even further. Their skin begins to take on the texture of the jagged basalt; their joints stiffen into the rigidity of cast-iron. 

Eventually, they find a gap in the architecture that fits them perfectly—a niche between a redwood-sized pipe and a brutalist wall. They step in, become still, and allow the moss and the bioluminescent sap to claim them. They do not die; they simply become another layer of the "packed district," their skeletons becoming the rebar for the next decade's layer of brick. 

In the Sump-Alley, to live is to eventually become the city itself.
