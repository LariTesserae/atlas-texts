In the Cistern of the Great Lung, life is not merely an inhabitant; it is a metabolic process. The creatures here do not live *on* the stone, but as an extension of its hydraulic rhythm.

The most prominent of these are the **Basalt-Shepherds**, the descendants of whatever hands originally mortared these walls. They are no longer recognizable as human, though they retain a hauntingly upright silhouette. Their skin has turned the color of wet slate, translucent and pebbled with the same calcium deposits that crust the water-channels. They are entirely hairless and lack eyes, their sockets filled with sensitive, vibrating membranes that map the cistern through the roar of the drips.

They live on the high ledges, moving with a slow, agonizing grace across the slick masonry. Their fingers and toes have lengthened into spindly, spatulate digits tipped with tough, necrotized pads that create a vacuum against the wet brick. They do not speak, for the acoustic density of the chamber would swallow their words; instead, they communicate by rhythmic tapping on the stone, sending vibrations through the masonry that other Shepherds feel in their marrow.

The Shepherds are the curators of the "Flood-Pulse." When the deep mechanisms groan, they scramble to the pulley-systems with frantic, joint-popping speed. They are the ones who tilt the great clay basins, directing the deluge into the proper grates to prevent the lower levels from drowning. They treat the stone as a living god, scraping away the "bruised purple" moss with sharpened ribs of bone, ensuring the weeping seams never clog.

Living in the literal shadow of the Shepherds are the **Siphon-Eels**, thick, pale ribbons of muscle that inhabit the hand-carved channels. They are the heavy laborers of the cistern's ecology. They are nearly static, wedging their hooked tails into the iron floor-grates and extending their mouth-parts upward to catch the High-Skimmers that fall, broken-winged, from the void. Their bodies are essentially living filters; they ingest the grey moisture and excrete a thick, phosphorescent slime that the "Seam-Hoppers" graze upon.

The **Seam-Hoppers** themselves are a frantic, neurotic layer of the world. They exist in a state of permanent vibration, their suction-cup feet drumming a constant tattoo against the bricks. They are the primary food source for almost everything else, and they survive only through a staggering rate of reproduction. During the Flood-Pulse, they do not hide; they throw themselves into the torrent, their sticky eggs adhering to the stones in gelatinous clumps that glow with a faint, dying amber light.

Deep within the crumbling corners, where the fungi grow into pale towers, dwell the **Marrow-Crabs**. These are the scavengers of the "terminal balance." They are flat, dinner-plate-sized crustaceans with shells that mimic the texture of fired clay. They are the only things in the cistern that move against the rhythm. While the Shepherds and the Hoppers react to the water, the Crabs react to the *stillness*. They wait for a High-Skimmer to die or a Shepherd to slip from a ledge. When a body hits the floor, the Crabs emerge from the masonry gaps in a silent, clicking wave, their pincers designed not for crushing, but for delicate, surgical dismantling.

There is a singular entity, however, that even the Shepherds fear. High in the vaulted shadows, clinging to the very center of the ceiling where the chain-pulleys originate, is the **Great Weaver**. It is a singular organism, a massive, soft-bodied cephalopod that has adapted to the air. It does not move. It has woven its nervous system into the hemp ropes and iron pulleys of the chamber. It feels every tug of a Shepherd's hand, every vibration of a pulley-wheel. It is the intelligence of the room, a living governor that occasionally reaches down with a single, miles-long filament to pluck a High-Skimmer from the air—or to nudge a stone weight that has become stuck.

The inhabitants of the Cistern do not know of a world beyond the weeping walls. To them, the "Great Lung" is the universe. They are the white blood cells of a stone body, endlessly cleaning, feeding, and vibrating in the dark, serving a purpose that was forgotten before their ancestors first lost their sight. Every movement they make is a gear-turn in the mechanical swamp, a wet, clicking heartbeat in the hollow of the world.
