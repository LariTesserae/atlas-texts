In a world that has finished itself, there are no inhabitants, only **Persistents**.

When you first enter the Glass-Thicket, you think you are alone. But as your eyes adjust to the blinding, shadowless gold, you realize the geometry is not merely structural. The "entities" here are not biological; they are **congealed intentions**.

### The Static Observers
They do not breathe, for there is no gas to exchange. They are the **Lattice-Wrights**, though they no longer wright anything. These are beings made of the same calcified resin as the walls, indistinguishable from the architecture until you notice the symmetry of their posture. They are tall, spindly tripod-forms, their "limbs" tapering into needles that are fused directly into the floor-ceilings. 

They do not move because movement implies a transition from one state to another, and here, all states are final. They are "living" statues whose consciousness operates at the speed of mineral drift. To them, a millennium is a single pulse of the high-frequency light. They do not have faces; they have multifaceted sensor-arrays that resemble clusters of uncut diamonds. They do not "see" you; they incorporate your vibration into the great, screaming resonance of the hive. They are the audience to the world’s terminal perfection.

### The Vibrational Parasites
Clinging to the undersides of the Wire-Palms are the **Hollow-Chimes**. They look like clusters of delicate, blown-glass bubbles, translucent and hauntingly beautiful. They are not animals, but localized densities of the piezoelectric hum. They "feed" by capturing the deafening drone of the Glass-Thicket within their hollow bellies, vibrating until they reach a pitch so high they begin to glow with a violet hue that cuts through the white-gold glare. 

If you were to touch one, it would not flee. It would shatter into a fine, silicate powder, releasing a sound so pure and piercing it would crack the mica-leaves of the surrounding flora. They are the only things that "die" here, and their death is merely a transformation from a shape into a sound.

### The Memory-Veins
Deep within the ribbed pilasters, there are ghosts. They are not spirits of the dead, but **Visual Echoes**. Because the light is so intense and the glass so pure, moments of the past have become physically etched into the molecular structure of the walls. 

As you move through the claustrophobic nooks, you might see a shimmering, two-dimensional figure trapped within a structural brace. It is a "Refractionist"—a remnant of whatever force originally curated this hive. These figures are frozen in gestures of measurement or observation. They look like holograms caught in amber. They are part of the geology now, their silhouettes providing the only "darkness" in this world—a slight dimming of the bioluminescence where their ancient bodies once stood.

### The Great Integration
There is no "I" in the Glass-Thicket. There is only the **Resonant Totality**. 

The things that live here do not have hunger, or fear, or desire. They are extensions of the mathematical equilibrium. If a "creature" exists here, it is the sound itself—the billion rubbing wine glasses. The sound is the mind of the Thicket. It thinks in harmonies and reasons in frequencies. 

You, the observer, are the only thing that is "broken" in this landscape. You are soft, you are wet, and you are silent. To the Glass-Thicket, you are a pocket of negative pressure, a flaw in a perfect diamond. The inhabitants do not hunt you. They simply wait for the biting cool to dry you out, for the fine dust to fill your lungs, and for the vibration to reach your bones. 

Slowly, the Thicket will begin to vibrate you at its own frequency. The resins will begin to precipitate in your joints. Within an eon—a mere blink in this overexposed world—you will stop moving. Your skin will vitrefy into frosted quartz. Your scream will join the high-pitched drone. 

You will not be a corpse. You will be a new structural brace, a permanent resident of the finished world, helping to hold up the ceiling of the room below.
