In this landscape of absolute hygiene and mechanical immortality, the "living" occupants are not biological inhabitants, but rather **The Vestigial Custodians**—entities that exist at the exact intersection of geometry, industry, and the weeping green.

### The Silhouettes (The Glass-Minded)
If you stand on a basalt ridge and look across a vertical rift, you may see something move against the navy sky. These are the Silhouettes. They are not flesh, nor are they strictly robots; they are hollow shells of spun basalt and copper wire, shaped vaguely like tall, spindly herons or tripod-like scribes. 

They do not "live" in the Nodes; they are part of the Nodes' nervous system. They move with a terrifying, frictionless grace across the razor edges of the world. Because there is no wind to knock them over and no rust to seize their joints, their movements are mathematically perfect. They are the ones who prune the moss. When a bioluminescent frond creeps a millimeter over a brass railing, a Silhouette arrives. It does not cut the plant; it simply touches it with a cold, electrified finger, "reminding" the flora of its boundary through a pulse of static. 

They have no faces, only a single, glowing vacuum tube where a head should be, pulsing with the same low blue light as the copper coils. They do not communicate; they only synchronize.

### The Hydro-Parasites (The Sucking Veins)
Tethered to the undersides of the iron sky-bridges are the only things that resemble "animals." These are the Hydro-Parasites: translucent, gelatinous organisms that look like a cross between a jellyfish and an industrial lung. 

They are stationary, fused to the copper piping of the Nodes. They do not eat; they "sense." Their entire existence is dedicated to the High Staticism of the world. They wrap their veined, shivering membranes around the steam valves, absorbing the screaming vibrations of the machines. To them, the subsonic groan of the pistons is a nutrient. They pulse in perfect rhythm with the thrum of the earth, turning a deeper shade of bruised purple as they gorge themselves on the kinetic energy of the factories. If a machine were to stop, the parasite would simply crystallize into a shard of salt and shatter.

### The Echo-Keepers (The Living Records)
Deep within the bottomless, still basins of water—those dark mirrors reflecting the midday stars—dwell the Echo-Keepers. They are ancient, colonial organisms, massive mats of silver-threaded algae that have grown so dense they have developed a form of crystalline memory.

They represent the world’s only "history." Because nothing decays and nothing changes, the Echo-Keepers have recorded every vibration of the planet for millennia. If you were to dip a copper wire into their pools, you would feel a frantic, high-speed discharge of data: the sound of the first piston firing, the slow growth of a liverwort sail ten centuries ago, the exact frequency of the crying steam. They are the archivists of a world without events. They do not think; they simply *reflect*. They are the reason the water feels heavy and "informed," as if the liquid itself is pregnant with the blueprints of the machines.

### The Latent Occupant
Finally, there is the ghost of the inhabitant that remains in the **Negative Space**. 

The world is built for something the size of a human, yet fundamentally different. The stairs are too steep for a being with knees; the brass railings are positioned for hands with too many fingers. You can feel "Them" in the way the shadows never move. 

There is a sense that the inhabitants didn't die or leave—they simply achieved the High Staticism they desired. They became the architecture. When you hear the "eternal, screaming roar" of the machines, you are hearing their collective voice, processed through miles of copper and steam. They have transcended the need for bodies that wither and air that is thick. They live now as the **ambient awareness** of the plateau. 

When you walk through an obsidian plaza, the pistons don't just thrum; they *react*. The humming coils brighten as you pass, not out of welcome, but as an automated sensory response to the "impurity" of a warm, breathing, entropic body. You are not a guest; you are a glitch in a perfect, weeping equation. 

The inhabitants are the Machines themselves, which have long since ceased to be tools and have become the world's only true, immortal species. They are breathing for a planet that has no lungs. They are working for a master that has become the stone.
