In the Viridian Stratum, life is not born so much as it is assembled, fueled by a metabolic desperation that mimics the frantic rhythm of a forge.

### The Piston-Whales: The Great Furnaces
The Piston-Whales are the undisputed masters of the high void. They are immense, oblong cathedrals of soot-stained chitin and copper-colored scales. They do not possess lungs in the traditional sense; instead, their hollow thoracic cavities act as combustion chambers. As they drift, they gulp the thin, ambient gasses and compress them until they ignite against the oily sap they’ve harvested from the cliffside ferns.

The sound of their passage is a rhythmic, percussive *thump-hiss*—the sound of a heart the size of a house. Their bellies glow with a dull, cherry-red heat that can be seen from miles away, a beacon of warmth in the violet-black. They are the "sun" of this world; around them, a miniature ecosystem of "Heat-Leeches" and "Steam-Gulls" huddles. These smaller scavengers cling to the whales’ barnacled hulls, drinking the hot exhaust and the mineral-rich soot that flakes off the great beasts’ exhaust ports.

### The Clockwork Scavengers: Gear-Flies and Lathe-Spiders
Beneath the drift of the whales, the vertical forests are alive with a frantic, metallic industry. The **Gear-Flies** are the most numerous. They resemble complex, multi-jointed brass figurines, their wings consisting of counter-rotating serrated discs. They do not buzz; they whine like high-speed drills. Their primary purpose is the "Refinement"—they land on the plum-colored ferns and use their proboscises to distill the oily sap into high-grade fuel, which they then carry back to communal hives tucked deep into the obsidian caves.

Deep within the crevices of the "needles" dwell the **Lathe-Spiders**. These are solitary, terrifyingly efficient beings whose limbs are like needle-nose pliers and glass-cutters. They do not spin silk from protein, but rather extrude thin filaments of flexible, carbon-fiber wire. They weave "Thermal Grids" across the gaps between cliffs, hoping to snare a drifting Gear-Fly or even a stray piece of a Piston-Whale that has succumbed to the cold. Their bodies are polished obsidian, making them invisible against the rock until their internal bio-electric veins pulse with a cold, blue warning light.

### The Siphon-Tree: The Static Predator
Not everything that lives here moves. The **Siphon-Tree** is a biological contradiction. It appears as a gnarled, metallic pillar of iron-hard wood, its "leaves" actually being broad, parabolic mirrors of frozen, silvered nectar. These trees do not photosynthesize; they are thermal predators. 

The Siphon-Tree positions itself to catch the radiant heat from passing Piston-Whales. When a whale drifts too close, the tree’s silvered leaves focus the ambient heat into a concentrated beam, subtly warming the air around it to create a localized vacuum. This vacuum "inhales" the floating silver spheres of water and any nearby Gear-Flies, drawing them into the tree’s central, furnace-like maw.

### The Hermits of the Needle: The Valve-Golems
On the highest, most isolated peaks—where the air is so thin it barely exists—live the **Valve-Golems**. They are the only creatures that resemble a bipedal form, though they are more statue than man. Their bodies are composed of "Growth-Rust," a mineral-rich fungus that hardens into a substance like cast iron. 

The Golems move with agonizing slowness, perhaps one step every decade. They are the record-keepers of the Stratum. Their internal systems are slow-turning gears of bone and stone that crunch as they move. They survive by "venting"—standing perfectly still for years, absorbing the frost-shards that drift by, and then suddenly opening internal valves to release a burst of stored thermal energy that clears the ice from their joints. They represent the "established" nature of the world: slow, durable, and indifferent to the passing of eons.

### The Ghost-Vapors
High above even the whales, in the thinnest reaches of the violet-black, exist the **Ghost-Vapors**. These are barely tangible—wisps of sentient, superheated gas that look like shimmering heat-waves in the starlight. They are the purest expression of the Stratum’s law: they are pure heat, pure movement. They have no solid form to freeze. They drift like auroras, trailing long tails of glittering ice-crystals that fall toward the peaks like diamond dust, providing the only "rain" the Viridian Stratum ever sees.

Every creature in this place is a miracle of engineering, a living machine designed to fight a war against the absolute zero of the void. Here, life is not a soft, wet thing; it is a hard-earned, high-pressure operation of grease, gear, and glowing internal fire.
