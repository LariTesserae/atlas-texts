In the Cenote of the Living Loom, to be a "resident" is to be a function. There is no distinction between the citizen and the cell, the architect and the stone. Those who move through the marrow-streets are not merely inhabitants; they are the active consciousness of the bryophyte deep.

### The Weavers (The Central Intelligence)
The dominant presence is not a singular species, but a caste of post-biological entities known as the **Siphons**. They are the keepers of the quantum nodules. At first glance, a Siphon looks like a cluster of pale, elongated nerves suspended in a sac of clear jelly. They do not walk; they hook themselves into the "banyan-cathedral" walls using fine, filament-like cilia, merging their nervous systems with the city’s sap-stream.

A Siphon has no face, only a radiating crown of sensory petals that can detect the shift of a single spore-drone a kilometer away. They are the administrators of the 3.0 technology. When a localized gravity field needs adjusting, a Siphon "thinks" a chemical sequence into the tree-bark, and the crystalline nodules respond. They are ancient, slow-moving, and profoundly quiet—living anchors of stillness amidst the frantic motion of the Loom.

### The Pulse-Walkers
If the Siphons are the mind, the Pulse-Walkers are the hands. These are upright, bipodal gastropods, their bodies composed of a tough, rubbery cartilage that mimics the deep violet of the surrounding vegetation. They move with an unsettling, gliding gait, their feet secreting a friction-reducing enzyme that allows them to skate across the vibrating vine-ways at high speeds.

Their role is one of "Biological Calibration." They carry long, hollow needles made of organic glass, which they use to "tune" the living buildings. You might see a Pulse-Walker plunge a needle into a gourd-wall to drain an air-bubble or inject a fresh strain of high-density chloroplasts into a wilting pillar. They don't speak; they communicate through bioluminescent flaring, their entire torsos lighting up in rhythmic codes that match the humming vibration of the city.

### The Translucent Nomads
Floating through the vertical vaults are the **Aether-Marrow**, the glass-winged arboreal squids mentioned in the metabolism of the place. However, as you observe them longer, they reveal themselves to be more than fauna. Their translucent bodies are stained with the colors of the data-sap they consume. 

They are the "hard drives" of the Cenote. Each squid carries a genetic memory of a specific century. When a building is disassembled by the pollen-drones, the Aether-Marrow descend to "ingest" the history of that structure before it vanishes. They drift in the tea-colored pools at the base of the hollow, whispering to one another in low-frequency sonar, archived memories of a world that has forgotten how to be solid.

### The Ghost-Phase Parasites
In the crevices of the stable basin, living in the shadows of the 0.7 density gaps, are the **Residues**. These are beings who have become "untethered" from the Fluid Permanence. They look like shivering outlines of people—wisps of condensed mist and discarded DNA. They are the unintended byproducts of the high-speed computation that runs the city. 

The Residues do not eat; they "sense." They cling to the undersides of the translucent vines, vibrating in sympathy with the city's song. They are the only things in the Cenote that seem to possess a sense of irony or nostalgia. While the rest of the Loom is obsessed with the *now* (the constant rebuilding, the constant flow), the Residues are the friction in the machine—the static in the visual field.

### The Relation of the Self
To exist here is to accept that your body is a temporary architectural choice. The "pollen-drones" do not distinguish between a fallen leaf and a resting traveler. If you sit still for too long in the Cenote, the spore-swarms will begin to "improve" you. They might weave a layer of moss over your skin to better harvest the 2.2 water air; they might graft a small bioluminescent node to your shoulder so you can be tracked by the central nodules.

The inhabitants do not mind. To them, the self is a "localized depression"—a temporary gathering of energy in a basin that has existed for eons. They are the song the Cenote sings to itself, a wet, humming melody of sap and light, where the only sin is to become rigid.
