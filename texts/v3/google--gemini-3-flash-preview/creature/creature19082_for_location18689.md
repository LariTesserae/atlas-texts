The inhabitants of this vertical cradle move with the heavy, unhurried grace of deep-sea divers. They are the **Mistsmiths**, a people who have traded the quickness of the valley-dwellers for a lung capacity that would burst an ordinary chest. Their ribs are wide and flared, their skin a translucent, marble-pale hue that reveals the indigo tracery of oxygen-starved veins. They do not walk so much as they lean into the world, their movements measured by the rhythmic hiss of the great brass pistons.

### The Cult of the Anchor
Because the ground beneath them is indecisive, the people are obsessed with the static. They wear boots weighted with lead and magnetic soles that "clack-thrum" against the iron catwalks, a sound that serves as their collective heartbeat. To a Mistsmith, the greatest sin is to be "unbound." 

They dress in layers of boiled wool and treated oilskin, heavy garments that serve as a second skin against the ozone-tinged cold. These clothes are never dry. They carry a permanent scent of wet soot and cold grease. Around their necks, they wear "Geological Levels"—small, ornate brass vials of mercury used to determine if the island they are standing on has tilted during the night. They are a people of the plummet, constantly recalibrating their balance against a world that wants to tip them into the white.

### The Scavenger-Clerics of the Gears
Within the soot-streaked factories live the **Tuned**, a caste of blind engineers who have lived so long in the low-frequency roar of the city that they can no longer process the visual world. Instead, they "see" through their feet. They stand barefoot on the vibrating steel plates, interpreting the health of the city-state by the resonance of the gears. 

When a floating platelet of obsidian drifts too far, or a bridge tensioner begins to fray, the Tuned feel the disharmony in their marrow. They are the ones who direct the massive steam-cranes, barking orders in a clipped, low-register language that remains audible even when the wind howls. They treat the city not as architecture, but as a living creature that must be constantly coaxed into remaining solid.

### The Aviators of the Membrane
Not everyone clings to the iron. The **Ray-Gliders** are a fringe society of the thin-blooded, those born with a strange, wiry lightness. They live on the underside of the massive house-sized ferns, harvesting the silver moss that grows there. To navigate the gaps between the drifting landmasses, they do not use bridges. Instead, they hitch rides on the massive, translucent sky-rays. 

They use long, hooked poles of hollowed bone to snag the radiator-fins of the rays, drifting through the violet-grey gloom like pilot fish. They are the city’s messengers and gatherers, possessing a casual disregard for the gravitational pockets that would terrify a Smith. To a Ray-Glider, a "fall" is simply a change in direction; they wear wide, wing-like cloaks of ribbed silk that allow them to spiral through the thickening air, landing softly on the mossy vertical faces where no iron cat-walk reaches.

### The Silicate Fauna
Beneath the feet of the humans, the "Abundant Strange" thrives in the heat spills of the boilers. The most common are the **Clockwork Crabs**—not machines, but biological crustaceans that have evolved to mimic the industrial environment. Their shells are polished, brassy carapaces, and they feed on the "sweat" of the pipes—the mineral-rich condensation that leaches out of the unfinished basalt. They move in swarms, their clicking legs sounding like a thousand ticking watches, cleaning the soot from the gears.

In the higher reaches, where the air is thinnest, live the **Glass-Larks**. They are birds of crystalline structure, their feathers sharp enough to cut silk. They do not sing; they vibrate. When the wind catches their wings, they emit a pure, metallic chime that harmonizes with the humming of the bridges. They are the only creatures that seem to enjoy the "reality breakdowns," nesting in places where gravity fails, their eggs floating in mid-air within spheres of woven silver moss.

### The Ghost-Engineers
Deep within the "Industrial Anatomy," where the steam is thickest, there are legends of the **Ancestors of the Unfinished**. It is said that some workers became so integrated with the maintenance of the great gears that they never left. They are rumored to have become "Soot-Wraiths," entities of compressed shadow and steam that inhabit the steam-valves. 

The inhabitants leave offerings of high-grade coal and jars of lubricant at the mouths of the deepest shafts. They believe that if the humming ever stops, if the sound of the metal hammer on the anvil fails to reach the three-mile mark, it is because the ghosts have finally stopped building, and the world—finally decided on its place—will fall toward the sun.

Until then, the people move with their glacial, oxygen-starved patience, bolting more iron to the raw stone, forever finishing a world that was born midway through a thought.
