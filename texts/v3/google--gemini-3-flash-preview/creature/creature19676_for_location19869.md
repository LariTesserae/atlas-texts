To live within the Great Chlorophyll Void is to exist in a state of perpetual, vertical migration. The inhabitants are not a single people, but a collection of biological accidents and desperate castaways, unified only by their struggle to remain grounded in a world that wants to grow toward the unattainable ceiling.

### The Transients: The Spire-Walkers
The human-sized inhabitants—the "Low-Tech" refugees—are known among themselves as the **Lichen-Bound**. They are a gaunt, spindly people, their skin often tinted a sickly, pale chartreuse from centuries of exposure to the sourceless, lime-colored radiation. Because the ground—the "Lung-Floor"—is prone to sudden, violent spasms of growth, the Lichen-Bound have abandoned the concept of permanent foundations.

Their huts are less like houses and more like biological barnacles. They are spherical pods of woven, resin-soaked fiber, anchored by umbilical thongs to the deepest, most ancient moss-mats. These people are master climbers; they wear "Thorn-Crampons" fashioned from the serrated edges of juvenile vines. Life for them is a rhythmic scramble. When a spire begins its "Elevator-Rise" beneath a village, the inhabitants do not flee horizontally; they climb the very thing that is displacing them, riding the jagged, skyscraper-sized stalk as it pierces the thin atmosphere, harvesting the frost-spores that collect on its heights before the inevitable "Structural Collapse" sends the pillar crashing back down into the emerald mist.

### The Apex Scavengers: The Glass-Strider Hounds
Roaming the vast, silent meadows between the spires are the **Glass-Striders**. These are not mammals, but a form of "faunal-flora"—hexapedal organisms with bodies made of translucent, cellulose chitin. They are the color of bottle-glass and nearly impossible to see against the glare. 

They do not have mouths in the traditional sense. Their heads terminate in long, capillary-rich needles which they plunge into the wounded "stumps" of fallen pillars to drink the pressurized, nutrient-rich sap. They are the only things that move with speed in the Void, their long, spindly limbs clicking against the moss like knitting needles. They are terrified of the "Vent-Storms"; when the floor begins to hum and exhale, the Striders huddle together, interlocking their crystalline limbs to form a heavy, wind-resistant dome until the pressure subsides.

### The Drifting Ghosts: The Velum-Gliders
High in the frigid, ozone-scented heights live the **Velum-Gliders**. These are the true masters of the low-density air. They are gelatinous, kite-like entities with wingspans that can shade a small village. They possess no skeletons; their rigidity comes from the internal pressure of the gases they siphon from the upper atmosphere.

The Gliders are the "Shepherds of the Green Snow." They use their trailing, gossamer filaments—some miles long—to sweep the air for frozen spores. To a person on the ground, a Glider is merely a momentary "shimmer" in the blinding light, a ripple in the emerald haze. They are profoundly solitary; on the rare occasion two Gliders collide, they do not fight, but instead merge, their translucent bodies melting into one larger, more buoyant entity that drifts even higher toward the ceiling-canopy.

### The Macro-Parasites: The Void-Crabs
The largest "fauna" are the **Void-Crabs**, though the name is a misnomer. These are colonial organisms the size of warships that cling to the "Ceiling-Canopy" miles above. They are biological harvesters that drop "Anchor-Vines" down into the void to snag debris, fallen spires, or even the huts of the Lichen-Bound. 

The "bone-like husks" found on the floor are the shed carapaces of these titans. When a Void-Crab grows too heavy with stolen nutrients, it undergoes a violent molt. The old shell—a mountain of translucent, pearlescent silica—falls through the void for several minutes, shrieking as it cuts the thin air, before shattering upon the moss-floor with a sound that can be heard for leagues. These "Shell-Falls" are the primary source of hard materials for the refugees, who swarm the impact sites to mine the "bone-shards" for tools.

### The Silent Sentience: The Mycelial Chorus
The most significant inhabitant is the **Great Chlorophyll Void itself**. The "Transpiration Hum" is not merely a mechanical function of the sap-pumps; it is a slow, low-frequency data transfer. The cellular substrate possesses a "Young" and "Violent" intelligence. It does not communicate in words, but in growth.

If the inhabitants stay in one place too long, the floor "learns" their shape. A moss-print left by a sleeping human might, within hours, be replicated by the floor in a jagged, emerald sculpture of silica. The Void is a mimic, a mindless artist trying to understand the foreign matter walking upon its lungs. It is not hostile, but its curiosity is lethal—it might "investigate" a traveler by suddenly erupting a forest of needles around them to see how they react to the cage.

To live here is to be an ant crawling across the face of a god that is both the sun and the soil, a god that is constantly, frantically reinventing its own body at the speed of a scream.
