In the Cistern of the Iron Weeping, life does not "grow" so much as it "assembles." To live here is to be an integrated circuit of flesh; to breathe is to intake a vaporized lubricant. The inhabitants are the ghosts in this green machine, and they are as much a part of the voltage as the humming vines.

### The Weaver-Crabs (Litho-Loomers)
Moving with the jerky, terrifying precision of a frantic sewing machine, the pyrite-shelled crustaceans are the primary architects of the Cistern’s stasis. They do not have soft underbellies; their anatomy is a series of interlocking brassy plates and hydraulic pistons sheathed in translucent, vein-filled membranes. 

They are obsessed with the "Conductivity of Life." Using their serrated, pincer-like appendages, they harvest the fiber-optic silk from the hanging vines and weave it into the moss. They are not building nests; they are grounding the landscape. When a Weaver-Crab dies, it does not rot. It simply stops moving, its joints seizing in a permanent, golden rust, becoming a new shelf of mineral-rich rock for its progeny to scuttle over. They are the reason the ripples in the pools freeze; they "patch" the liquid surface with invisible webs of silicate to ensure the geometry of the cistern remains undisturbed.

### The Voltage-Syndromes
Hovering in the pockets of static air are the **Glint-Flies**. These are not singular organisms, but swarms that share a single nervous system distributed through the air by electromagnetic induction. Their wings are shards of volcanic glass that don't flap so much as they resonate. 

They feed on the "Industrial Ghost"—the purposeless output of the trees. You will see them clustered around the brass fruit, their proboscises needle-thin and copper-plated, siphoning the lubricating oil. As they drink, their abdomens pulse with a blue, welder’s-flash light. They are the keepers of the rhythm; the "factory roar" is amplified by their vibrating wings. If a human were to speak, the Glint-Flies would perceive the erratic sound waves as a malfunction in the system and descend in a glittering cloud to "calibrate" the intruder into silence.

### The Slumbering Accumulators
Deep in the pools of stagnant, conductive brine, beneath the honeycomb algae, dwell the **Volt-Eels**. These are the "heavy industry" of the basin. They are thick, sluggish creatures with skin like hammered lead and eyes that are nothing more than glowing, red vacuum tubes. 

They rarely move, acting instead as biological capacitors. They lie in the silt, absorbing the excess charge from the mineral-rich waterfalls. Their purpose is a mystery of self-regulation; they are the heat sinks of the valley. Occasionally, one will discharge a massive arc of lightning into the roots of a corrugated tree, a violent "handshake" of data and energy that causes the tree’s steel-like bark to shiver and produce a fresh crop of oily nodules.

### The Echo-Keepers (The Only Soft Things)
Perhaps the most unsettling inhabitants are the **Lichen-Mimes**. They are not animals in any traditional sense, but masses of the neon-streaked moss that have achieved a degree of locomotive autonomy. They take the shape of whatever has most recently moved through the valley—the jagged silhouette of a crab, the curve of a falling droplet.

Because of the **Law of Stasis**, these entities are the only way the Cistern "remembers" its own history. They are the slow-motion recorders of the heights. If you stand still long enough, a patch of moss will slowly rise, mimicking your posture, its fibrous interior humming at 60-cycles to match your heartbeat. It will not eat you; it will simply incorporate your form into the eternal, un-decaying inventory of the engine.

### The One Who Tends
At the center of the amphitheater, where the gravity is most confused and the water droplets hang in the thickest grids, there is a presence often mistaken for a statue. It is the **Sextant**. 

A towering, bipedal entity made of the same corrugated wood as the trees, its limbs are jointed with oily brass ball-sockets. It has no face, only a rotating array of glass lenses that crackle with purple light. It does not speak, but it emits a low-frequency thrum that keeps the Weaver-Crabs in their rhythmic loops. The Sextant is the personification of the Cistern’s logic—an organic overseer that ensures the "purplessness" of the work. It moves once every hundred years to prune a vine or to nudge a frozen ripple, ensuring the factory never produces a finished product and that the engine never shuts down. 

To look at the Sextant is to realize that you are not an observer of this landscape, but a potential raw material, waiting to be refined into a cooling fin or a length of humming wire.
