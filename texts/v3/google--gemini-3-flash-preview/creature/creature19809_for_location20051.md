The inhabitants of the Silt-Sovereignty are not merely residents; they are the living soft-tissue of a lithic body. They are known as the **Silt-Kin**, though they rarely use names for themselves, referring to the collective simply as "The Pulse" or "The Weeping."

### Physiology: The Pale and Pliant
To look upon a Silt-Kin is to see a creature redesigned by the weight of the air. Their skin is their most striking feature—it is translucent and perpetually slick, possessing the matte finish of a river-pebble. It lacks the oils of surface-dwellers, instead secreting a thin, saline mucus that prevents the abrasive slate from tearing their flesh as they squeeze through the clefts. 

They are elongated and gracile, with bones that feel more like hardened cartilage than calcium. This "soft-frame" allows them to compress their ribcages and shoulders, gliding through the "interstitial veins"—the foot-wide gaps between the stacked slate huts—with the fluid grace of an eel. Their eyes are oversized and fixed in a permanent, lidless stare, tuned to the monochromatic spectrum of the grey light. They do not see color; they see *vibration* and *sheen*, interpreting the world by how much light a surface reflects.

### The Culture of Tactile Communion
In a world with no gaps between buildings, there is no concept of "the individual." To the Silt-Kin, a person is a temporary thickening of the communal flow. They move in "braids"—groups of three or four who travel in constant physical contact, their skin sliding against skin. This is not sexual, but navigational and social; in the roar of the **percussion of the seep**, touch is the only reliable form of syntax.

They communicate through a sophisticated language of haptics: a finger traced along a collarbone, a rhythmic pressure against a neighbor’s ribs, or the communal vibration of their chests. When they sing, they do not use their throats to produce melody; they hum against the slate walls, using the hollow ceramic chambers of their homes to amplify the sound into a low-frequency thrum that can be felt in the marrow of the entire settlement.

### The Chisel-Priests and the Accretion-Masons
The hierarchy of the Silt-Sovereignty is determined by one's relationship to the water. The highest caste are the **Seep-Watchers**. These individuals spend their lives motionless, pressed face-first against the bloom-walls. They "read" the mineral content of the weeping rock by taste, determining where the next ceramic frame should be placed to catch the most lime. 

Below them are the **Chisel-Priests**. Because the city is constantly calcifying—literally turning into a solid block of mineral—the Silt-Kin must fight a constant war against "Petrification." If they do not work, the city will become a tomb. The Priests use heavy, blunt-nosed chisels to carve out the breathing-holes and crawl-ways that the weeping water tries to seal. They are the sculptors of the void, ensuring that "space" survives within the "solid."

### The Aesthetics of Silt
Personal adornment is rare, as anything bulky would snag in the narrow passages. Instead, the Silt-Kin decorate themselves with **Bio-Lace**. They allow the "drip-vines" to take root in the shallow pores of their skin, usually along the spine or outer thighs. These vines grow directly off the person, soaking up the ambient mist and provide a slow, steady trickle of nutrients directly into the bloodstream. An elder of the Silt-Sovereignty is often draped in six feet of swaying, pale-green filament, making them look like a ghost moving through a waterfall.

### The "Dry-Lung" Terror
Their psychology is defined by a profound agoraphobia. To a Silt-Kin, the open sky is a vacuum that threatens to suck the moisture from their lungs. They speak of the surface as "The Great Desiccation." 

When the **Dry-Lung** occurs—when the mist thins and the reed-mats begin to creak—the Silt-Kin enter a state of communal hysteria. They do not flee; they retract. They retreat into the "Deep-Heart," the lowermost chambers where the water gathers in dark, mineral-heavy stagnant pools. There, they submerge themselves entirely, stacking their bodies like the slate plates of their floor, holding their breath for hours until the humidity returns. They are a people who have traded the freedom of the horizon for the absolute, crushing security of the stone.
