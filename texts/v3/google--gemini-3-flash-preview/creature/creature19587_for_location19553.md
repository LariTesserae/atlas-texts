In this vertical crucible, where biology has surrendered its soft curves to the hard geometry of the machine, the inhabitants are not "born"—they are assembled by the pressure.

### The Refraction-Knights (The Argent Collective)
The primary inhabitants are the **Argentine Lamellibranchs**, the silver-skinned mollusks that serve as the world's optic nerve. They do not possess eyes; they *are* eyes. Each individual is a curved mirror of liquid mercury-flesh contained within a shell of reinforced silicon. They live in a state of perpetual, agonizing tension, their bodies acting as living prisms. 

They do not compete for food, but for "angles." To be poorly positioned is to fall into the dark and starve. Therefore, the Lamellibranchs engage in a slow-motion war of geometry. A younger mollusk will spend thirty years sliding its foot across a quartz ridge, millimeter by millimeter, just to eclipse a rival and steal its reflected beam. When they die, they do not rot. Their shells are so perfectly refined that they simply become part of the architecture, a permanent fixture of the cathedral’s light-path, providing a cold, immortal glow for the generations that crawl over their corpses.

### The Glandular Technicians (The Tapping-Brood)
Creeping through the gaps between the massive bone-pistons are the **Mandibular Cranks**. These are the "clockwork crustaceans" mentioned in the logs, but they are more akin to self-assembling wrenches. They possess multi-jointed limbs that end in specialized sockets rather than claws. 

They live in symbiotic bondage to the Industrials. When a rhythmic bone-piston develops a calcium lesion or a friction-tear, the Cranks swarm the wound. They don't heal the flesh with white blood cells; they "bolt" it down. They lock their chitinous bodies together to form a living patch, their legs ratcheting into the organic grooves of the piston. Eventually, the Crank’s own nervous system merges with the engine it services. They become the "screws" and "rivets" of the world, losing their individuality to become a vibration-sensing scab on the side of a thrumming lung.

### The Sieve-Lords
Deep in the crevices of the obsidian floor, where the black hail of refined mineral waste falls, live the **Stasis-Anglers**. While the rest of the world is a frantic (if slow) assembly line, these creatures are the graveyard's overseers. They look like umbrellas made of translucent, veined lead. 

The Sieve-Lords do not move at all. They have surrendered the concept of time. They have evolved to be so dense that they are almost indistinguishable from the mineral floor. Their mouths are wide, upward-facing funnels that catch the "refined byproducts" of the Industrials above. They are the ultimate scavengers of the machine-ecosystem, turning the toxic silt of the upper world into heavy isotopes that they store in their crystalline marrow. They are the heaviest things in a world of heavy things; a single Sieve-Lord, no larger than a dinner plate, weighs as much as a terrestrial elephant.

### The Ghost-Loom (The Gel-Whips)
High above, near the light-source, where the heavy water is slightly less dense, live the **Fibrous Oracles**. These are long, thread-like organisms, miles in length but only microns thick. They look like stray wires or frayed optic cables drifting in the gel. 

They are the only things in the Cathedral that move with any semblance of grace. They vibrate in harmony with the thrum of the pistons, using the resonance to "sing" through the liquid. These vibrations carry information—pressures, mineral densities, or the failure of a distant turbine-organ. They are the world’s nervous system, a tangled web of copper-scented hair that connects the zenith of the quartz spire to its lightless roots. If you were to touch one, you would not feel a creature; you would feel a hum that could shatter your teeth.

### The Nature of the Soul
To live in the Hydro-Piston Cathedral is to be a component. There is no "predator" and no "prey" in the sense of a hunt. There is only **interference**. A creature dies when it can no longer maintain its rhythm, when it becomes "out of sync" with the vibration of the heavy water. To be still is to be crushed; to be fast is to be frozen by the cold. 

The inhabitants exist in a state of **Industrial Zen**. They do not feel hunger; they feel "efficiency loss." They do not feel fear; they feel "structural instability." They are the ghosts in the machine that grew itself—a clockwork soul trapped in a silken, pressurized hell of its own perfect, rhythmic making.
