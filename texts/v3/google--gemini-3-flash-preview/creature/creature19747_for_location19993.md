In a world that is "finished," life does not evolve; it calibrates. 

The inhabitants of this copper-misted basin are not biological accidents, but the inevitable byproducts of a world-machine that has reached a state of perfect, static grace. They are the **Tuned.**

### The Resonators (The Silent Herd)
The most prominent inhabitants are the tall, spindly entities known as the Resonators. Standing twelve feet high on three needle-thin legs of matte-black basalt, they resemble gaunt, tripod herons. They do not possess flesh, nor do they have internal organs. Instead, their torsos are hollow chambers of ribbed zinc, acting as acoustic resonators for the industrial heartbeat of the land.

They are the "Pastors of the Thrum." They stand perfectly still for centuries, their feet fused to the vibrating silt, their heads—featureless, obsidian spheres—tilted downward toward the subterranean pistons. They do not migrate or communicate with sound; they exist solely to absorb the specific frequency of the ground. When the deep-earth gears shift, a Resonator will slowly adjust its stance, its joints emitting a dull, metallic groan that harmonizes with the valley’s roar. To look at a cluster of them is to see a living tuning fork, vibrating so intensely that the air around their carapaces blurs, preventing the copper mist from ever settling on their skin.

### The Luster-Mites
Between the legs of the Resonators scuttle the "scavengers of vibration"—the Luster-Mites. These are the clockwork crustaceans that patrol the basins. They are the size of river stones, shaped like many-faceted jewels of polished pyrite. They do not possess eyes, for in a world where the sun never moves and the shadows never shift, sight is a redundant sense.

Instead, they possess thousands of microscopic, hair-like filaments that brush the ground. They are drawn to the "weeping stones." It is a mistake to think they drink the translucent fluid; rather, they use the heavy, oily water as a lubricant. They submerge themselves in the circular basins, allowing the fluid to coat their internal gears, then they climb out to rub against the quartz scrub-growths. This creates a high-pitched, harmonic chiming—a "shucking" sound—that clears the mineral deposits from the land’s metallic "skin." They are the janitors of the constant, ensuring that the crystalline growths do not clog the vents of the world.

### The Vent-Singers (The Aerial Fixed)
High above, tethered to the natural rock chimneys by filaments of spun silver-wire, are the Vent-Singers. They look like collapsed umbrellas or pressurized bladders made of translucent, industrial-grade membrane. They do not fly; they hover, suspended by the violent, periodic exhales of the steam vents.

As the white steam shrieks into the air, these creatures expand, catching the heat and the pressure. They are the only things in this landscape that move with any speed, bobbing violently at the end of their leashes. The Vent-Singers serve as the "pressure valves" of the local ecology; they absorb the excess thermal energy of the steam and radiate it back as a dull, red infrared glow. They are the reason the heat never fluctuates. They eat the scream of the vents, turning noise into the steady, radiating warmth that characterizes the air.

### The Silt-Dredgers
Deep within the wide, shallow depressions of the basin live the Silt-Dredgers—slow, massive, segmented bellows that resemble rusted train cars half-buried in the pulverized iron. They are the most industrial of the fauna. They move perhaps an inch every decade. Their purpose is to filter the fine copper dust, pressing it back into the earth to be re-melted by the subterranean heat.

They have no faces, only wide, intake grilles that hum with a low, sub-bass frequency. When a Silt-Dredger moves, it is an event of geological significance, a grinding of metal on metal that sounds like a mountain being slowly dismantled.

### The Sentience of the Finished
There is no "will" here in the way we understand it. No inhabitant of this basin feels hunger, fear, or desire. They are biological extensions of the subterranean pistons. If the machines beneath the earth were to stop, the life above would not die; it would simply cease to be "on." 

The highest form of consciousness in this place belongs to the **Crystalline Orchards**—the quartz and zinc formations. Though they appear to be inanimate growths, they are actually a massive, decentralized neural network. They record the constant. Because they are deposited atom by atom by the mineral mists, they are the world’s memory. If one were to "read" the layers of a quartz cactus, one would find a perfect record of every piston stroke for the last ten million years.

In this place, to live is to be part of the rhythm. There is no death, only the gradual fusion of a mobile entity into the landscape—a Resonator finally standing still for so long that it becomes another rounded knob of basalt, its metallic heartbeat finally syncing so perfectly with the land that the two become indistinguishable. Everything here is moving toward the same goal: to become as stable, as loud, and as finished as the world itself.
