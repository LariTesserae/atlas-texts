The inhabitants of this wind-scourged attic are the **Litho-Strider Scribes**, a species that exists as a frantic bridge between the mineral and the biological. They are small, roughly the size of a human child but possessed of a terrifying, wiry density. Their bodies are built like a collection of interlocking tuning forks—long, multi-jointed limbs ending in hooked, obsidian-hard talons specifically evolved to find purchase in the microscopic pits of the limestone needles.

### The Physiology of the Shrill
To look at them is to see the plateau’s architecture reflected in flesh. Their skin is not soft; it is a flexible, parchment-like hide, layered in overlapping scales of a deep, bruised violet. They do not have lungs as we understand them. Instead, their chest cavities are hollow resonance chambers lined with vibrating membranes. They "inhale" the static of the dust storms through spiracles in their flanks, converting the atmospheric tension into a high-pitched, internal hum that powers their metabolism.

They are blind in the traditional sense. In a world of sepia dimness and abrasive grit, soft ocular tissue would be a liability. Instead, their faces are dominated by wide, fan-like crests of sensitive, hair-thin filaments. These fans catch the vibrations of the wind and the ultrasonic "pings" emitted by the clattering plants, allowing them to "see" a high-definition map of the world through sound alone. To a Scribe, a sudden silence is more blinding than total darkness.

### The Frenetic Culture of the Perch
The Scribes live in a state of perpetual, rhythmic motion. Because the landscape is a "percussion chamber," their culture is entirely choreographic. They move across the precarious vine-bridges not by walking, but by a series of controlled, rhythmic falls—swinging, gliding, and snapping their talons into place with a sound like a stapler firing into wood.

Their internal energy is so high that they never truly sleep. Instead, they enter "stasis-drifts" where they hook themselves into the leeward crevices of the stone needles and vibrate in harmony with the wind. To a visitor, a resting Scribe looks like a dormant piece of the plant life, rattling softly against the rock.

### The Builders of the Sacrificial
Their habitations—the "hand-carved dwellings"—are not homes so much as they are instruments. Each cell is notched with precise slits that catch the updrafts, creating a low-frequency drone that tells the inhabitant the exact speed and mineral content of the oncoming storm. 

They are obsessed with the "dry" harvest. Using their heavy obsidian scrapers, they strip the mineral-rich bark from the humming thickets. This bark is their primary resource; they don't eat it for calories, but for the electrical charge it holds. They chew the fibers into a resinous, conductive paste, which they use to repair their homes or to "solder" their tools together.

### The Philosophy of the Shatter
The Scribes have a strange, reverent relationship with death. Because their world does not know rot, they do not understand the concept of a "body" as something that remains. When a Scribe’s internal vibration finally stops—when their "hum" fails—their body desiccates within hours. 

The survivors do not bury their dead. Instead, they carry the body to the highest, most wind-exposed spire of the plateau and hold it aloft. The abrasive wind, filled with its payload of pulverized rock, acts like a sandblaster. Within a single afternoon, the departed Scribe is ground down into the "glowing glitter" that carpets the plateau. 

To the Scribes, this is the ultimate ascension: to stop being a focused, screaming point of energy and to become the phosphorescent dust that the rest of the tribe breathes. They walk upon the literal remains of their ancestors, and they do so with a crunching, rhythmic pride. They are a people who have accepted that permanence is a lie told by the damp; here, in the golden suspension of the high plateau, the only truth is the resonance of the wind and the glory of the eventual shatter.
