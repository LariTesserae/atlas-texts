In the **First Descent of the Basin**, the inhabitants are not merely residents; they are the connective tissue of the geology. They are a people defined by **The Lean**—a physiological and social adaptation to a world without flat surfaces or horizontal horizons.

### The Physiology of the Gripped
The people here, the *Basin-Born*, are lean and wiry, their musculature developed for tension rather than impact. They possess elongated fingers with thickened, calloused pads that allow them to find purchase on the obsidian-slick faces of the "fresh" stone. Their movements are fluid and arachnid; they do not walk so much as they articulate themselves across the verticality of their world.

Their skin has a translucent, parchment-like quality, lacking the pigment of those who live under a sun. In the low, flickering light of tallow lamps, you can see the rhythmic pulse of their veins—a map of blue rivers beneath pale frost. Their eyes are disproportionately large, with pupils that remain perpetually dilated, shimmering like pools of oil as they catch the faint glow of phosphorescent lichen. They do not blink often; in a world of sharp edges and sudden drops, to close one’s eyes is to surrender to the abyss.

### The Culture of the Whisper
The most striking trait of the population is their **Acoustic Discipline**. Because the un-eroded, basaltic walls act as sonic mirrors, a shout could ring for minutes, shattering the focus of a thousand workers. Consequently, the inhabitants communicate through a complex language of "The Soft." 

Their speech is a rhythmic series of fricatives and breathy vowels, barely above the sound of the condensation-rain. For long-distance communication across the chasms, they use a sophisticated tactile sign language, catching the light with polished shards of obsidian to flash silent signals to "streets" located hundreds of feet above or below. 

They are a people of the **Constant Touch**. Because space is a luxury and gravity is an enemy, physical contact is not a violation of privacy but a means of security. When passing each other on the narrow rope-walks, they do not pull away; they press shoulders, a brief tether of warmth and weight that acknowledges their shared survival.

### The Guilds of the Unweathered
Society is organized by one’s relationship to the stone and the moisture:

*   **The Drip-Watchers:** A priestly class of hydro-engineers. They live near the ceiling-peaks, draped in waterproofed skins. Their existence is spent in meditation on the rhythm of the *plip-plop*. They can predict when a cistern will overflow by the scent of the air, and they manage the communal "Vessels of Equity," ensuring the "ceiling-sweat" is distributed fairly through the log-pipes.
*   **The Fracture-Smiths:** These are the craftsmen of the *chink-chink-chink*. They are the only ones permitted to alter the geography. Using flint mauls and levers, they "read" the crystalline fractures of the basalt. They do not build "on" the rock; they coax the rock into becoming a shelf, a stair, or a hearth. They treat the stone as a living, volatile entity that might snap if provoked.
*   **The Mycelium-Weavers:** Harvesters of the fossilized fungus, they are the strongest members of the huddle. They venture into the "Wild Breaks"—the unlit peripheries where the city hasn't yet reached—to bring back the cork-like lumber. They wear heavy cloaks made of pressed beetle-chitin to protect themselves from the razor-sharp edges of the "new" stone.

### The Fauna: The Symbiotes
Life in the First Descent is not limited to humans.
*   **The Tether-Hounds:** Small, six-legged mammals with hooked claws and prehensile tails. They act as beasts of burden and companions. They are often used to carry light loads (bundles of lichen or small ceramic jugs) across the rope bridges. They are silent creatures, communicating through ultrasonic chirps that do not trigger the cavern's echoes.
*   **The Glass-Glass Beetles:** Large, palm-sized insects with shells so smooth they resemble the obsidian of the walls. They serve a vital sanitary function, consuming the waste of the dense slums and secreting a bioluminescent glue. This glue is harvested by the inhabitants to seal the "wattle-and-daub" joints of their dwellings, making the city glow with a faint, ghostly amber hue at night.

### The Spirit of the Huddle
The inhabitants do not conceive of "outdoors." To them, the world is a box that is slowly being filled with the history of their hands. They possess a strange, collective claustrophilia; they find comfort in the press of bodies and the closeness of the thatch. 

They live in a state of **Emergent Permanence**. Though the terrain is "at birth," the people are ancient in their habits. They are a civilization that has learned to be as sharp and as quiet as the stone that cradles them, thriving in the cold, wet cracks of a world that has not yet learned how to be soft. To live here is to be a part of the "Great Compression"—a single, breathing organism of ten thousand souls, etched into the basalt, waiting for the stone to finally go still.
