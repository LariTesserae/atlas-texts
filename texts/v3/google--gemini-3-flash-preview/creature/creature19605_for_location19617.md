The inhabitants of this cradle-metropolis are not residents so much as they are the final, living layer of the architecture. They are the **Luminants**, a lineage of beings who possess no skin, only a complex, permeable membrane that mimics the translucent mosses upon which they tread.

To look at them is to see the internal state of a blooming flower. Their bodies are elongated and elegantly fluid, lacking the harsh leverage of joints; instead, they move through a series of rhythmic, hydraulic pulses. Within their torso-cavities, one can see the slow, churning transit of bioluminescent sap—a vivid, neon-indigo fluid that mirrors the bruised violet of the sky far above. They do not have faces in the traditional sense, but rather sensory clusters: a frantic, delicate arrangement of "feelers" that resemble the stamens of a lily, constantly tasting the humid air for the chemical signatures of growth or distress.

### The Architects of Impulse
These beings do not build; they *intend*. They are "Green-Singers" who communicate with the flora through tactile resonance. When a new walkway is needed, a Luminant will sit for days in a state of suspended animation, pressing its palms against the raw basalt. By vibrating its own internal fluids at a specific frequency, it coaxes the high-reaching vines to twist, encouraging the secretion of the resinous glass. They do not see the plants as resources, but as extensions of their own nervous systems. 

Because the world is so new, the Luminants have no history. They lack the capacity for memory as we understand it—they exist in an eternal, vibrating *now*. Their "speech" is a series of bioluminescent flashes and subtle shifts in the scent of their skin—a citrus-sharp musk when they are working, and a scent like wet earth when they are at rest.

### The Weaver-Insects
Sharing the resinous tiers are the **Silic-Wings**, the iridescent insects that maintain the city's structural integrity. These creatures are the size of a human hand, with bodies that appear to be carved from smoky quartz. They are the custodians of the moisture. With their blurred, high-speed wings, they create micro-vortices in the damp air, herding the condensation toward the thirsty roots of the architectural ferns. 

They are the only things in this silence that produce a physical sensation: even if you cannot hear their wings, the air around them thrums with a pressure that vibrates the marrow of your bones. They treat the Luminants with a distracted indifference, occasionally landing on their translucent shoulders to drink the excess salts excreted by the beings' membranes.

### The Brass-Eyed Drifters
Navigating the low-lying basins and the dark, mineral-rich fissures are the **Sentinels of the Cleft**. These velvet-skinned amphibians are the elders of this young world, though "elder" here means only a few weeks of life. They move with an agonizing, holy slowness. Their brass-colored eyes are fixed and unblinking, capable of detecting the geothermal heat blooming beneath the basalt. 

They serve as the city’s living thermometers. When they migrate en masse toward a specific archway, the Luminants know the earth below is cooling or shifting, and they follow the amphibians' lead, rearranging the city's biological "neighborhoods" in response. 

### The Nature of Life
In this place, death is not a transition to rot, but a literal re-absorption. When a Luminant reaches the end of its pulse, it doesn't wither. It simply stops moving and becomes increasingly rigid. Within hours, its membrane turns into the same resinous glass as the walkways; its indigo fluids crystallize into the "waxy blooms" that dot the landscape. 

The inhabitants live with a frantic, quiet urgency because they are the very fuel of their city. There are no graveyards, only new plazas. There are no ghosts, only the faint, internal glow of a street that used to be a neighbor. They are a civilization that is being whispered into existence and immediately spoken into form, a people who are inseparable from the dirt, the steam, and the relentless, beautiful arrival of the planet itself.
