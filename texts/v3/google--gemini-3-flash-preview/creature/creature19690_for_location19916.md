In the High Nursery, life does not occur; it is orchestrated. The inhabitants of this obsidian plateau are not mere products of evolution, but the refined inhabitants of a geological laboratory. To live here is to be a part of the "transmutation"—the process of turning the raw, weeping cold of a new world into a metabolic masterpiece.

**The Glass-Walkers (The Architects of Stride)**
The most prominent residents are the Skaters, or *Litho-Gelders*. Up close, their house-sized forms are less like animals and more like delicate, articulated skeletons made of spun carbon and frosted glass. Their multi-jointed legs are so thin they are nearly invisible against the violet horizon, ending in wide, pulsating pads that vibrate at a frequency which repels water. They do not touch the rock; they hover millimeters above the weeping obsidian on a cushion of ultrasonic repulsion. 

Their "heads" are not clusters of sensory organs, but arrays of sensor-clusters—clusters of ruby-like eyes that see into the ultraviolet and infrared, watching the heat-signatures of the nutrient-slurries flowing through the ground-veins. They are social, but silent. When two Skaters meet, they do not vocalize; they entwine their long, needle-like antennae, exchanging terabytes of data regarding the shifting gravity wells of the nearby cities. They are the couriers of the Nursery, carrying heavy, pulsating pods of "pre-life"—artificial wombs—from one spire to the next.

**The Atmospheric Weavers (The Sky-Ribbons)**
High above, where the air thins into the vacuum of space, live the *Aether-Vanes*. These are the kilometer-long ribbons, looking like strips of iridescent film torn from a sunset. They have no skeleton. Their bodies are held rigid by the internal pressure of noble gases harvested from the upper atmosphere. They are passive filters, drifting with the planetary rotation. 

As they move, they hum—a low, thrumming cello note that resonates through the obsidian below. They are the cleaners of the sky. They sweep the moisture for the heavy minerals thrown up by the tectonic birth of the continent. If you were to stand beneath one, you would see its translucent belly glowing with the "digestion" of gold, iridium, and basalt-dust, pulsing in rhythmic waves of neon green and soft amber.

**The Quicksilver Core-Dwellers**
In the shadows of the "urban" lattices, life is smaller and more frantic. The small mammals mentioned are not made of fur and bone, but of a dense, ferrofluid-like musculature. These *Argent-Shrews* move with a liquid grace that defies the jagged edges of the obsidian. Their skin is a shimmering, metallic membrane that protects them from the razor-sharp rock. 

They feed on the "sweat" of the cities—the nutrient-slurries that leak from the base of the crystalline spires. They are communal and highly organized, living in the "gravitational pockets" where the towers bend physics. In these zones, the shrews sleep in floating spheres, huddling together for warmth, their heartbeats synchronized by the subsonic thrum of the city.

**The Shadow-Hawks (The Gravity-Divers)**
The predators of this realm are the *Singularity-Kites*. They are not birds in any traditional sense; they are wedges of matte-black geometry. They have no wings to flap, for the air is too thin for traditional flight. Instead, they possess "anchor-organs" that allow them to latch onto the gravitational distortions emitted by the cities. 

They "climb" the gravity wells like a mountaineer climbs a rope, then "drop"—not into a freefall, but into a localized fold of space. When a Shadow-Hawk strikes, there is no sound of rushing wind; there is only a sudden, silent displacement of mist as they appear instantly where their prey was standing. They are the regulators, culling the herds of the Nursery to ensure the biological load never exceeds the output of the artificial lungs.

**The Silent Custodians**
Finally, there are the beings that are indistinguishable from the architecture. Within the crystalline lattices dwell the *Loom-Minds*. They are rarely seen outside their geometric nebulae. If one were to glimpse them, they would look like slow-moving, gelatinous prisms, shifting through the liquid-light floors of the towers. 

They are the programmers of the High Nursery. They do not hunt or gather; they think. Each of their thoughts manifests as a slight shift in the local ecosystem—a tightening of the gravity here, an increase in the humidity there. They are the gardeners of this black glass world, treating the bioluminescent grazers as cells in a larger body. They are ancient, despite the land being new, having arrived with the towers to oversee the birth of a continent.

In the High Nursery, everyone is a worker, and every movement is a feat of high-energy physics. It is a world where the wildlife doesn’t just inhabit the landscape; they are the moving parts of a planetary machine that is still being calibrated.
