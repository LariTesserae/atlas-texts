To inhabit this place is to be an extension of its metabolism. Life here does not compete with the architecture; it serves the maintenance of the stasis. Since there is no "ground" to bury the dead and no "wild" to harbor the feral, what lives here is as deliberate and engineered as the colossal vines themselves.

### The Gardeners of the Vascular Flow
The primary residents are not individuals, but a distributed biological intelligence known as the **Siphonophores**. These are soft-bodied, translucent hominids with skin the color of skimmed milk and the texture of a wet grape. They do not have skeletons in the traditional sense; instead, their limbs are pressurized by the same hydraulic fluids that move through the great sky-vines. 

They do not walk so much as they *adhere*. Their hands and feet are wide, disc-like pads that creates a gentle suction against the tacky, embryonic floor. They move with an agonizing, fluid grace, sliding along the mile-high columns like slow-motion raindrops moving up glass. 

The Siphonophores are the world’s janitors and tuners. They do not eat; they "plug in." When a Siphonophore is hungry, it presses its chest against a specific node in a mahogany-tissue pillar, allowing a capillary-exchange to occur. They are the only creatures that understand the *thrum-creak* of the world; they place their palms against the walls to feel the pressure of the water-flow, and if they detect a blockage, they secrete enzymes from their fingertips to dissolve the obstruction before it can threaten the structural equilibrium.

### The Aurelian Drifters
In the hollow voids—the cathedral spaces between the pillars—the "dust motes" of the world reside. These are the **Aurelian Drifters**, the pale, six-winged organisms mentioned in passing. Close up, they are less like birds and more like living lace. Their wings are not made of feathers, but of a gossamer, photosynthetic film that catches the pearlescent glow from above.

The Drifters have no digestive tracts. They exist solely to transport genetic data. They drift through the emerald mist, their long, trailing filaments brushing against the basin-leaves. They pick up "instructional spores" from one sector and carry them miles across the void to another. They are the living messengers of the architects, ensuring that the "genetic code" of the North remains in sync with the South. If a Drifter dies, it does not fall; it simply loses its buoyancy and is slowly absorbed into the first horizontal vine it touches, melting back into the emerald grid like a piece of sugar in tea.

### The Membrane-Sentinels
Because the world is a closed system, "waste" is a foreign concept, but "intrusion" is a theoretical threat. At the edges of the great basins, where the water spills over in eternal ribbons, live the **Membrane-Sentinels**. 

These are sessile, animal-plant hybrids that look like giant, lidless eyes made of jelly. They are embedded directly into the structural weave. They don't move, but they are the world's sensory organs. They "see" through changes in the atmospheric 2.0 wetness. If a foreign body—something that does not belong to the biological architecture—were to enter the space, the Sentinels would pulse with a low-frequency vibration. This vibration would change the "song" of the world, alerting the Siphonophores to the presence of a "mechanical" or "un-built" object. 

### The Vestigial Architects
Deep within the mahogany-tissue pillars, in chambers where the fermentation-heat is most intense, there are rumors of the **Architects** themselves. They are no longer recognizable as mobile life. They have become "Core-Brains"—massive, encephalic growths of grey matter integrated directly into the nervous system of the plants.

They do not think in words or goals; they think in pressures, humidities, and tensions. They are the source of the world's "Living Logic." To them, the world is not a place to live, but a body to occupy. They are the dreaming consciousness of the megastructure, maintaining the "never-changing" equilibrium through a trillion micro-adjustments of sap-flow and vine-tension.

### The Life of the Surface
On the very "skin" of the world—the top-most canopy that catches the filtered sun—there are the **Light-Drinkers**. These are thin, needle-like insects that have no legs, only hooks. They hook themselves into the highest leaves and remain there for decades, their crystalline shells filtering the sun to provide the specific spectrum of light needed by the fungus-bulbs below. They are living stained-glass windows, turning the white sun into the emerald-tinted mist that defines the hollows.

In this world, life is not about survival, for survival is guaranteed by the architecture. Life is about **participation**. To live here is to be a cell in a body that is ten thousand miles wide—a body that is warm, wet, echoing, and perfectly, terrifyingly content to simply exist.
