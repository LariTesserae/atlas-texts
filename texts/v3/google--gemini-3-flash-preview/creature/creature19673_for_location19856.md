On this obsidian needle, the residents are masters of the vertical and the brief. They are creatures of the "in-between," existing in the razor-thin margin between a freezing death and a solar burn.

The primary inhabitants are the **Suture-Walkers**, a people whose bodies have become as elongated and lean as the mountain itself. They are not permanent residents of the peak—no biology could endure the night-side radiation of this height for long—but are pilgrims of the light. They have skin the color of bruised plums, a deep, dark pigment designed to absorb every available photon while shielding their internals from the scouring UV rays. They do not walk so much as they flow over the rock, their fingers and toes elongated, tipped with hardened, keratinous pads that can find purchase in a fissure no wider than a blade of grass.

They wear garments of woven cloud-swift down and the cured skins of the white rodents, but their most striking feature is their eyes. To survive the blinding glare of the "roaring" sun, the Suture-Walkers have developed secondary, translucent eyelids—nictitating membranes that act as natural polarized lenses. When they look at the world, they do not see the "hazy, violet void"; they see the thermals rising from the valleys below as shimmering ribbons of gold.

The Suture-Walkers are the architects of the bone chimes and the stone cairns. They are a culture of observers, believing that the closer one gets to the void, the thinner the veil becomes between the physical world and the "Prime Breath." They do not speak in the thin air—to do so would waste precious moisture and oxygen. Instead, they communicate through a complex language of tactile taps against the stone and rhythmic whistling that mimics the wind in the shale.

Living alongside them, weaving through the sky-moss, are the **Glass-Gilders**. These are the white-furred rodents you noticed, but upon closer inspection, they are more insectile than mammalian. Their fur is not hair, but fine, hollow filaments of chitin that trap air for insulation. Their "twitching energy" is a literal vibration; they shimmer at a high frequency to generate kinetic heat, a biological shivering that keeps their blood from crystallizing during the hour-long freezes. They have a symbiotic relationship with the succulents—they harvest the seeds, but in doing so, they track the pollen from the "parchment" flowers on their sticky underbellies, ensuring the garden survives another cycle.

In the shadows of the obsidian crags, where the sun cannot reach, lives the **Rime-Stalker**. If the cloud-swifts are the spirit of the peak’s height, the Stalker is the spirit of its cold. It is a slow, crustacean-like predator with a shell of living mineral. It moves so slowly that the rime-ice grows over its back, camouflaging it perfectly against the granite. It does not hunt by speed; it simply waits at the edge of the moss-mats. When a cloud-swift lands to rest its four wings, the Stalker’s tongue—a whip-thin organ tipped with a flash-freezing chemical—strikes out. It is the only thing on the peak that does not hurry. It has the patience of the stone.

Finally, there are the **Aether-Whales**, though the name is a misnomer. They are vast, translucent gas-bladders, nearly invisible against the sky, that drift past the pinnacle. They do not "live" on the rock, but they graze upon it. They descend when the clouds snag on the peak, using long, trailing filaments to "fish" for the sky-moss, lifting clumps of the emerald velvet into the air to digest. To the Suture-Walkers, these are the true gods of the height—silent, drifting islands of life that remind them that even in the "near-void," there is something larger, older, and purely indifferent to the sting of the cold.

Every creature here is a specialist in the extreme. They are the inhabitants of a spire that is still "growing," and like the mountain, they are unfinished, hardy, and brilliantly, dangerously alive.
