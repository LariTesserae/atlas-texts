In the high country of the Silt-Spires, life does not occupy the landscape so much as it *becomes* the landscape. There are no inhabitants in the traditional sense—no citizens who walk the streets—only a tiered hierarchy of biological functions, ranging from the microscopically industrial to the cosmically serene.

### The Architects: The Calcified Multitude
The primary "residents" are the **Litho-Polyp Colonies**. They are the unseen billions, the quiet laborers whose collective instinct has widened the needles into a city. They resemble a cross between a barnacle and a lichen, appearing as patches of textured, pearlescent "skin" on the limestone. They do not think; they merely filter the silver-white mist. 

As they process the mineral-rich runoff, they excrete a fast-setting, silica-heavy sludge that hardens into the very balconies and bridges described. They are the pulse of the spires. If you press an ear to the stone, you do not hear silence; you hear the microscopic grinding of a trillion tiny shells as they expand the world by a fraction of a millimeter each day. They are the city’s slow-motion metabolism.

### The Scavengers of the Drip: The Glass-Weavers
Living within the shadows of the "built" architecture are the **Glass-Weavers**. These are spindly, multi-limbed arthropods with shells as clear and brittle as frozen water. They are the maintenance crew of the verticality. They possess incredibly long, hooked appendages designed for permanent suspension. 

The Glass-Weavers do not possess a sense of "ground"; they have never known a flat surface. They move along the undersides of the calcified bridges, spinning sticky, transparent filaments that catch the "drip"—the nutrient-rich debris falling from the peaks. They are silent, save for the occasional *snick* of a claw against stone, and their eyes are large, pale orbs that perceive only the movement of falling objects. To them, a still object does not exist.

### The Aristocracy of the Air: The Slow-Gliders
Far above the weavers, drifting in the vast, vertical canyons between spires, are the **Slow-Gliders**. They are the size of galleons but weigh less than a handful of moss. Their bodies are composed of a pressurized, gaseous gel encased in a membrane that looks like wet silk. 

They are the philosophers of the Silt-Spires. They do not hunt; they "graze" on the aerial plankton—the microscopic spores and eggs lifted by the updrafts. Their movements are so glacial that a human observer might mistake them for a strange, stationary cloud trapped between the towers. They communicate through low-frequency vibrations that harmonize with the wind whistling through the limestone flutes, turning the entire mountain range into a singular, mournful instrument. They are the only creatures that understand the horizontal, seeing the city not as a series of walls, but as a map of currents.

### The Sentinels of the Sills: The Moss-Mimics
In the "hanging marshes," seated among the iron-blue moss and rubbery reeds, live the **Sill-Sitters**. These are soft-bodied, sedentary mollusks that have evolved to look identical to the stone they inhabit. They possess a singular, massive, muscular foot that anchors them to the limestone with such force they become part of the geological strata.

The Sill-Sitters are the record-keepers of the water. They possess sensory pits that detect the mineral content and frequency of "the drip." They are the only creatures that experience the passage of time in its true, geological scale. A Sill-Sitter may watch a single mineral deposit grow for a thousand years, never moving more than an inch, its existence dedicated to the slow absorption of the mountain's weeping moisture.

### The Nomads: The Mist-Walkers
Rarely seen, and perhaps more legend than biological fact, are the **Mist-Walkers**. They are tall, gracile beings with limbs like whip-cord and skin the charcoal color of wet slate. They are the only creatures with a vestigial sense of "will." 

They do not build, nor do they drift. They climb. Using specialized pads on their hands and feet that create a vacuum against the slick limestone, they move between the Spires in a perpetual ascent. They follow the "clean, silver-white glare" of the sun, chasing the light as it moves across the peaks. They are the nomads of the vertical, living on the edge of the atmosphere, never descending into the "Basement." They leave no ruins and no tools; they leave only the faint, rhythmic *thrip-thrip* of their footsteps, indistinguishable from the falling rain.

***

In the Silt-Spires, there is no "us" and "them," no "dweller" and "dwelling." There is only the continuous, rhythmic secretion of life. Every bridge is a skeleton; every balcony is a graveyard; every peak is a lung. It is a world where to live is to contribute one’s body to the increasing height of the stone, a slow-motion ascension that will continue until the spires finally scrape the very edge of the sky.
