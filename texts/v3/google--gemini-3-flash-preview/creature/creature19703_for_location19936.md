In this theater of stillness, the inhabitants are not merely residents; they are the physical manifestations of the rift’s patience. They are beings of low-metabolism and high-durability, creatures who have traded the frantic pulse of the surface for a rhythm that matches the turning of the stars.

### The Calcified Shepherds
The most prominent inhabitants are the **Silt-Walkers**, tall, spindly-limbed bipeds that resemble the same sun-dried bone as the walls. They are not animals, nor are they quite "people" in a sense the surface would recognize. Standing nearly ten feet tall, their bodies are composed of a flexible, cartilage-like marrow encased in a hard, white mineral crust. They have no eyes; instead, their entire "skin" is photosensitive, allowing them to perceive the world as various gradients of heat.

They are the keepers of the **Sun-Traps**. With fingers like elongated limestone flutes, they move the obsidian shards with an agonizing, ritualistic slowness. A Silt-Walker might stand over a single piece of crystal for three years, nudging it a mere centimeter to ensure the searing needle of light tracks perfectly with the changing tilt of the planet. They do not speak; they communicate through a low-frequency hum generated by vibrating the hollow cavities in their chests—a sound that, to a human, would feel less like a voice and more like a headache.

### The Marrow-Leeches
Tucked into the cooler shadows of the "Lung-Moss" are the **Marrow-Leeches**. These are soft, translucent organisms that look like spilled mercury. They are the only creatures of "wetness" in this arid void. They do not seek blood; they seek the silver condensation that clings to the ledges. They move by expanding and contracting their fluid bodies, flowing upward against gravity to drink the twilight beads of water. They are the rift’s cleaners, consuming the microscopic flakes of shed chitin and mineral dust that would otherwise mar the perfection of the white-silt floor.

### The Weaver-Wraiths
Higher up, where the walls begin to curve over into the ceiling, live the **Weaver-Wraiths**. If the Silt-Walkers are the stone, the Wraiths are the air. They are spindly, multi-limbed insects with bodies as thin as the Glass-Reeds. They weave "Light-Nets" across the jagged skylights—webs made of a silk so fine it is invisible until the sun hits it at a specific angle, igniting it into a gossamer grid of gold. 

These nets do not catch prey; they catch reflection. The Wraiths are obsessed with the "Aura-Moths." When a Moth drifts too close, the Wraith gently guides it toward the focal points of the Sun-Traps. There is a sense of desperate, artistic cooperation here—the Wraiths and Silt-Walkers working in a silent, cross-species harmony to ensure the geometric patterns on the floor are etched with absolute precision.

### The Presence in the Walls
Deep within the "melted wax" fissures of the walls, there is a consciousness that is "The Ancient." It is not a single creature, but a colonial intelligence formed by the **Lung-Moss** itself. The grey-green pads are interconnected by a subterranean network of fungal filaments that run through the pores of the limestone. 

This collective mind acts as the rift's memory. It pulses with a slow, vegetable telepathy, recording the fall of every shadow and the etching of every line. The Silt-Walkers bow to the moss patches before they adjust their stones. The moss is the "clock" of this world; its microscopic popping is the sound of time being digested.

### The Ghost-Heralds
Lastly, there are the **Echo-Mites**, smaller cousins to the Shield-Mites. While the larger ones follow the light, the Echo-Mites follow the sound. They possess umbrella-like ears that constantly twitch, seeking out the rare "tectonic events" of a falling pebble or a shifting rock. When a sound occurs, they gather in the spot where the vibration was strongest and remain there for weeks, vibrating their own shells to "re-play" the sound over and over, as if trying to understand the concept of a "beginning" or an "end" in a world that has largely forgotten both.

These inhabitants do not struggle for survival; survival was won long ago. Instead, they exist in a state of **monastic maintenance**. They are the curators of a gallery that no one visits, perfecting a work of art—the etched floor—that may take another million years to complete, satisfied entirely by the warmth on their backs and the slow, golden grace of the void.
