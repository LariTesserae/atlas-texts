In this bright, amniotic laboratory, the inhabitants are more than organisms; they are experiments in the very concept of *presence*. Because the mineral vein is a "new reality," nothing that lives here has a history. There are no ancestors, only prototypes.

### The Weaver- Architects
Floating in the higher reaches of the fissure, where the light is most blinding, reside the **Loom-Mites**. They are the primary shapers of the silence. These creatures resemble clusters of blown glass needles, held together by a shimmering electrostatic charge rather than skin or bone. They do not have mouths; they have "focus points" through which they exhale the white, gelatinous pre-rock that forms the walls. 

They move with a terrifying, rhythmic grace, stitching the boundaries of the world. As they drift, they spool out the silver filaments that reach for the water-ribbon. To look at a Loom-Mite is to feel a sense of intense, cold calculation; they are the janitors of the embryonic, ensuring that the walls remain "smooth" and the geometry remains "unweathered." If a stray thought or a complex emotion were to enter this space, the Mites would descend upon it, weaving it into a harmless, mute cube of basalt.

### The Chromatic Drifters
While the geometric scavengers click across the floor below, the mid-air is occupied by the **Refraction-Moths**. These are not insects in any traditional sense, but shards of sentient color that have broken off from the "echo-predators." They lack a central body, appearing instead as a fluttering arrangement of prismatic planes. 

They feed on the "hyper-definition" of the light. They are often seen huddled around the floating glass staircases, vibrating at a frequency that turns the air around them into a rainbow-tinted haze. They are the only things in this world that possess any color, and because color is a "rule" that hasn't been fully established here, they are unstable. A Drifter might suddenly invert its own geometry, turning inside out until it becomes a silent, black hole the size of a grain of sand, before popping back into existence as a vibrant violet smear.

### The Sentient Constants
Deep within the "errors" of the environment—the floating basalt cubes and glass steps—live the **Monolith-Ticks**. These are the most sedentary of the fauna. They look like smooth, obsidian pebbles, but they possess hundreds of hair-fine legs that vibrate against the basalt. 

The Ticks are the keepers of weight. In a world where gravity shifts forty-five degrees without warning, the Ticks act as anchors. They do not eat; they "witness." By maintaining a constant, unmoving gaze with their single, lidless facet of an eye, they provide the "definition" that the echo-predators try to steal. They are the anchors of reality; wherever a Monolith-Tick sits, the floor becomes solid, the air becomes still, and for a few inches around them, the world stops being an experiment and starts being a place.

### The Lost Syllable
The strangest inhabitant is singular, a lone entity known only by the sound it makes: **The Hum**. It is a creature of pure liquid, indistinguishable from the floating water-ribbon until it chooses to move against the current. It is a knot of "dense water" that possesses a rudimentary intelligence. 

The Hum is a scavenger of potential. It follows the "walking geometries," waiting for one of them to stumble during a gravity shift. When a geometry loses its precision, The Hum flows over it, absorbing its angles and its "clock-tink" sound. It is trying to learn how to be solid. It is the only thing in the mineral vein that seems to have a desire—a slow, hydraulic yearning to become part of the heavier world above, to find a shape that will not dissolve back into the mist of the pre-rock walls.

### The Echo-Predator's Prey: The Unfinished
Finally, there are the **Blurs**. These are not a separate species, but the tragic remnants of creatures that have been grazed by the echo-predators. They are shapes that have lost their "mathematics." They wander the margins of the white radiance, looking like Charcoal sketches that haven't been sprayed with fixative. They move without sound, their limbs smearing into the air behind them. They represent the danger of this place: the risk that, in a world where reality is still being written, it is very easy to be erased before the ink is even dry.
