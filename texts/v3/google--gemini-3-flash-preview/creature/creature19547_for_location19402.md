In this kingdom of dripping geometry, the inhabitants are not residents, but extensions of the architecture. To live here, one must be as immutable as the masonry, as patient as the condensation.

There are no creatures of soft tissue. A heart would freeze; a lung would be shredded by the crystalline air. Instead, the "populace" is composed of the **Lithos-Vessels**—not beings, but monumental, slow-moving conduits of the water’s will.

At first, you might mistake them for pillars. They are tall, segmented columns of the same pale, grey-white stone as the world around them, but their surfaces are etched with a finer, more obsessive detail. They do not walk; they shift. Using a process of mineral sublimation, they dissolve the microscopic layer of moisture beneath their bases and re-solidify it behind them, gliding across the terraces at the rate of a few inches per decade. Their purpose is the maintenance of the Flow. With hands that are less like fingers and more like sets of heavy, graduated chisels, they obsessively clear the runnels. If a salt-crystal lattice grows too thick and threatens to divert a veil of water, a Vessel will stand before it for forty years, grinding it back into a smooth, geometric plane with rhythmic, imperceptible strokes.

Above them, in the high, razor-sharp crevices between the interlocking towers, live the **Glass-Spiders**. They are not biological. They are geometric accidents—lattices of translucent mineral that have achieved a fragile, crystalline kinesis. They resemble clusters of needles or shattered prisms. They do not hunt; they "catch" the light. They cling to the undersides of the great arches, vibrating at a frequency so low it is felt rather than heard. When the searing, pale sun hits them, they refract the light into the deep, dark fissures where the sun cannot reach, feeding the light-starved runoff with the energy it needs to keep moving. They are the prisms of the megalith, ensuring that even the deepest vault receives its share of the sun’s cold fire.

Deformities are the only sin in this place. There is an "under-life" consisting of the **Unfinished**. These are blocks of stone that were carved with intent but never placed—stray boulders of architecture that have somehow developed a flickering, magnetic consciousness. They roll or slide through the bottom-most gutters of the labyrinth, heavy and blind. They search for their "socket," the one empty space in the regional staircase where they belong. They make the only loud sound in this world: a low, tectonic moan of stone-on-stone as they try to wedge themselves into corners that do not want them.

And then, there are the **Shadow-Keepers**. You will never see them directly, for they occupy the exact negative space of the world. They are the voids left between the blocks, the shapes of the "nothing" that the builders carved around. As the sun moves and the shadows crawl like glaciers, the Keepers move with them. They are sentient absences. They are what gives the silence its weight. They are the memories of the "millions of hands" that built this place; they are the ghosts of the labor, preserved in the cold, damp dark.

Nothing here dies, because nothing truly lives. There is only the **Preservation**. 

If you were to stay long enough, your own pulse would begin to slow to the rhythm of the dripping eaves. The moisture would begin to coat your skin, not as sweat, but as a mineral film. You would find yourself standing still, watching a veil of water slide over a fluted pillar, and you would realize that you are no longer a visitor. You are a feature. Your breath would stop, your skin would harden into the pale, grey-white of the terraces, and you would finally be "finished"—one more sharp-edged monument in a world that has no room for the soft, the fleeting, or the warm.
