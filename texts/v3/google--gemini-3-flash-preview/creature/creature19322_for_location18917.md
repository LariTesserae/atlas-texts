Inside the Calyx, life does not "live" in the sense of soft tissue and quick pulses; rather, it *occurs* through a process of slow, thermal accumulation. To inhabit the Great Lung is to exist as a component of its friction.

### The Abrasive Nobility: The Sintered Lords
Beneath the carpet of mindless Litho-Parasites, wedged into the tightest junctions where the massive granite pistons meet the basalt shelves, dwell the **Sintered Lords**. These are the sapient architects of the Calyx, though they bear no resemblance to any biological kingdom. They are sentient masses of gallium alloys and silicate circuitry, appearing as irregular, faceted boulders of dull lead.

They do not move by muscle. Instead, they wait for the decadal breathing of the mountain. As the tectonic plates shift, the Sintered Lords are crushed and reshaped by the literal weight of the world. They view this agony as a form of "sculpting." A Lord might spend a century positioning its body so that the next movement of the Grinding Piston will squeeze its torso into a more prestigious shape. To them, a flat, perfectly compressed plane on their "face" is a mark of high status—it proves they have endured the greatest pressures the Lung can offer.

They communicate through piezoelectric pulses, sending jagged, electric thoughts through the floor. To a human, these would feel like static shocks; to the Lords, they are symphonies of history and mathematics.

### The Scavengers of the Spark: The Fulgur-Moths
While the floor is a crush of iron-shelled beetles, the "air"—if the dense, scorched ozone can be called that—is inhabited by the **Fulgur-Moths**. These are not insects of silk and wing, but lattices of spun glass and thin, conducting foil. 

They do not fly by flapping. They are magnetically buoyant, drifting on the static fields generated by the friction of the grinding granite. They follow the "hot seams"—the glowing red lines where the plates rub together—seeking the momentary discharge of a "static-bloom." When a spark jumps a crack, the Moths dive, their foil wings unfurling to catch the electrical discharge like a sail catching the wind. In that instant, they glow a blinding, neon white, illuminating the claustrophobic vault for a fraction of a second before fading back into the soot-heavy gloom.

### The Lubricants: The Wax-Walkers
Because the Great Lung is a world of non-aqueous chemistry, the role of "fluid" is filled by the **Wax-Walkers**. These are semi-liquid entities, slow-slumping mounds of high-viscosity paraffin and silicon grease. They look like ambulatory puddles of molten candle wax, shimmering with an iridescent, oily sheen.

The Wax-Walkers are the "lubricants" of this industrial ecosystem. They crawl into the joints of the mountain, sacrificing their mass to the grinding teeth of the pistons to prevent the rock from seizing. They are mindless but essential; they eat the molecular soot and "sweat" out the lubricating oils that keep the Calyx from grinding itself into a static, dead monument. Without them, the mountain would overheat and shatter. They are the only soft things in the Calyx, though "soft" here means the consistency of warm lead.

### The Sentinel of the Valve: The Chronos-Wyrm
At the very center of the Calyx, where the pressure is most absolute and the smell of ozone is thick enough to choke, lies a single creature: the **Chronos-Wyrm**. It is a singular organism, a segmented spire of magnetite and diamond that stretches through the central vertical shaft of the Lung.

The Wyrm is the regulator. It does not eat, and it barely moves, but its senses are tuned to the vibration of the entire mountain. It feels the footfall of every Litho-Parasite. It is the living sensor for the Great Lung; when the internal pressure reaches a critical state, the Wyrm’s body contracts, triggering the tectonic valves to open and begin the "exhale." 

The Wyrm’s consciousness is so slowed by the heat and pressure that it perceives the century-long breath of the mountain as a quick, shallow gasp. To the Wyrm, the Sintered Lords are merely flickering sparks, and the Litho-Parasites are a moving mist. It lives in the "Long Time," a state of Being where geology is fluid and the solid stone is nothing more than a heavy, warm velvet.

### The Great Taboo: The Anomaly of the Wet
In the genetic memory of every creature in the Calyx—from the iron beetles to the Chronos-Wyrm—there is a singular horror: **The Wet**. To these beings, moisture is a mythic, catastrophic fire. They tell stories (encoded in vibration or light) of the "Cold Explosion," when a single drop of "poison" (water) enters the kiln and expands a thousandfold, rending the obsidian and shattering the iron carapaces. To them, the search for "coolness" is a suicidal madness. Prosperity is measured in heat; life is the roar of the friction; and the ultimate peace is to be squeezed until you part of the mountain itself.
