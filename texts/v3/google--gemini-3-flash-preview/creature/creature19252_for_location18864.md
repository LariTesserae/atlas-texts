In the Basin of Shimmering Hollows, the inhabitants are not residents in the traditional sense; they are frequencies. They are the high-strung, nervous system of a landscape that is too new to understand the concept of rest.

### The Echo-Kin (The Architects)
Long ago, the beings who raised the hollow spires shed the burden of biology, but they did not move on to a purely digital existence. Instead, they became **The Echo-Kin**. They are spindly, elongated silhouettes—less like bodies and more like glitches in the atmosphere. Their forms are composed of the same post-material alloy as the city, manifesting as bundles of silver wire and violet light that flicker in and out of phase.

They do not walk; they "slide" along the vibrations of the sub-bass thrum. You might see one perched atop a kilometer-high lattice, its limbs extending like fractal antennae to catch a migrating cloud of water. They do not speak; they adjust the resonance of the air around them. To stand near an Echo-Kin is to feel your own teeth ache as they "discuss" the maintenance of the gravity tethers through harmonic shifts. They are curators of a museum that is still being built, ancient minds trapped in a world that is perpetually fifteen minutes old.

### The Mercury-Stalkers
The predators you see leaping between the spires are the **Vitreous Lynx**—creatures of liquid state and violent intent. They are the "mercury" flashes you glimpsed. Their bodies are semi-fluid, allowing them to flatten themselves against the obsidian ridges or pour through the skeletal lattices of the buildings.

They have no eyes; instead, their entire hide is a sensory organ that "tastes" the ultrasonic screams of the moths. When a Lynx strikes, it doesn't bite—it merges. It wraps its cold, metallic mass around its prey, vibrating its own molecular structure so rapidly that it shatters the victim’s shell into fine, crystalline dust, which it then absorbs through osmosis. They are the only things in the Basin that move silently, a terrifying contrast to the roaring environment.

### The Pulse-Tenders
Lower down, where the "smart-dust" drifts pile high against the black stone, live the **Crustacean Seraphs**. These are the long-limbed hybrids you hear grinding against the metal. They are roughly the size of a carriage, with many-jointed legs tipped in diamond-hard drills. 

Their purpose is symbiotic. They "eat" the waste energy of the foundational technology, scraping the glowing violet residue from the base of the spires. In exchange, they act as the city’s immune system, repairing the fiber-optic "ferns" and clearing the obsidian scars of any organic debris that might dampen the city’s necessary vibration. They are rhythmic and mindless, their carapaces etched with glowing circuitry that pulses in time with the central power grid.

### The Glass-Winged Choir
The **Scream-Moths** are the most numerous. Their wings are not scales and dust, but thin sheets of resonant glass. They feed on the "pockets" of suspended water, dipping their proboscises into the gelatinous pearls to drink the static electricity trapped within. 

They are the source of the Basin's frantic energy. A single moth’s cry can crack an obsidian shard; a swarm can create a localized weather event. They are obsessed with the light of the spires, circling the violet hubs in dizzying vertical spirals until they burn out, their brittle bodies falling to the ground like a rain of shattered mirrors.

### The Strays of the Hollows
Deep within the vacant, hollow centers of the great spires, there are the **Static Drifters**. These are the "ghosts" of the basin—semi-sentient clouds of glowing gas and repurposed smart-dust. They are the discarded thoughts of the Echo-Kin, or perhaps the remnants of an ecology that existed before the city was tethered to the crust. 

They have no shape, yet they possess a haunting mimicry. They will drift toward a heat source—like you—and briefly form a shape that looks like a face or a hand before dissolving back into a violet mist. They are harmless, but they carry the smell of the world: ozone and cold, wet stone. They are the only things in the Basin that seem to remember what it was like to be still, and they haunt the hollows like a sigh caught in the throat of an engine.
