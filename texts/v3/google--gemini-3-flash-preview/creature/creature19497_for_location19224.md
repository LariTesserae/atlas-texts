To inhabit the Crown of Whispers is to be an extension of the acoustics. Life here does not fight the roar; it is played by it, like a reed in a throat of granite.

The dominant residents of the "Hollows" are the **Litho-Chorus**. They are a people of corded muscle and translucent, parchment-like skin, perpetually bronzed by the 3/3 light to a deep, burnished copper. Evolution has stripped them of what we would recognize as a voice. Their vocal cords have fused into calcified chambers, and their ears have migrated inward, shielded by thick, boney ridges to prevent the eardrums from shattering in the deafening thrum. They do not speak; they hum. By shifting their posture in the wind-tunnels of their fluted homes, they redirect the air through their own bodies, creating a resonant harmonic that allows them to communicate across the tectonic "never-changing" chord of the plateau.

They are biologically tethered to the rhythm. Their heartbeats do not follow a steady pulse but instead synchronize with the "Click-Beetles" in the shale below. In the presence of a guest, a Litho-Chorus individual might seem statuesque, standing for hours at the edge of a black-gashed shadow, their eyes—nictitating membranes of gold—fixed on the horizon. They are not waiting; they are simply "tuned."

Their culture is one of anchors. Every piece of their sparse "technology" is designed to prevent them from becoming part of the sky. They wear heavy, leaden anklets and jewelry made of the same splintered shale that comprises the ground. Their clothing is woven from the filaments of "Glass-Wings," a shimmering, indestructible silk that is transparent in the sun, making them look like ghosts of light flickering against the pale rock.

Deeper within the aerodynamic ribs of the mountains live the **Vessel-Keepers**. These are the elders, those who have spent centuries—though time is irrelevant here—smoothing the stone basins. Because there is no fire, food is a matter of chemical patience. They harvest the "Lung-Moss," which, when pressed between two polished flint stones, releases a thick, violet enzyme that tastes of ozone and old minerals. The Keepers have fingers that are long and spatulate, worn flat by eons of grinding moss into a slurry. They move with a deliberate, slow-motion grace; any sudden movement in this thin ether could catch a rogue updraft and fling a body into the bleaching blue.

Beneath the feet of these humming giants, the **Click-Beetles** are more than just insects. They are the clockwork of the Crown. They possess no "will" as we understand it, but are driven by the vibrations of the stone pipes. When the wind intensifies, their clicking becomes a frantic, metallic gallop—a shimmering carpet of flint shells that reflects the sun like a river of mirrors. The Litho-Chorus treat the beetles as a sacred soil; they walk upon them with a specific, sliding gait so as not to crush the shells, for to silence a beetle is to remove a note from the eternal song.

And then there is the **Sovereign-Sail**. High above, tethered not by silk but by a psychic weight to the highest serrated peak, is the oldest living Glass-Wing. It is the size of a cathedral, its translucent membrane scarred by a million years of grit-laden air. It does not eat or move. It simply *is*. It acts as the apex of the Crown’s geometry, a living kite that stabilizes the atmosphere's vibration. The Litho-Chorus look up at it as a compass. If the Sovereign-Sail ever shifted its angle, the polyphonic drone would change pitch, and the inhabitants believe that such a change would cause the mountains themselves to shatter.

In this place, the concept of "identity" is dissolved into the atmosphere. To live here is to be a vibration. There are no names, only frequencies; no histories, only the enduring resonance of the stone. The people do not die so much as they eventually lose their grip, becoming light enough for the wind to finally claim them, turning their bodies into silvered dust that polishes the very flutes they once inhabited.
