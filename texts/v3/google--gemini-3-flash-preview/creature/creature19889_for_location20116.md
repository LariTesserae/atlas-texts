In the Labyrinth of the Steam-Cradle, life is not a gift, but a stubborn, suffocating density. Because there is no sun to drive photosynthesis and no soil to harbor roots, the inhabitants are fueled by the fever of the earth itself—chemotrophs and scavengers who derive their vitality from the lukewarm mineral broth that weeps from the basalt.

### The Pale Architects (The Hivers)
The primary inhabitants, the builders of the crushing stone megalopolis, are beings of immense physical power and profound sensory limitation. They are bipeds, but low-slung, with torsos as broad and thick as boulders. Their skin is the color of curdled milk and has the texture of wet chamois—slack, porous, and perpetually oozing a protective mucus to shield them from the abrasive grit of the pumice.

They have no eyes. In a world of eternal, silver-grey dusk and thick steam, sight is a wasted biological investment. Instead, their faces are dominated by wide, vibrating nasal slits and rows of specialized pits along their jawlines that "see" heat and pressure. They perceive their city not as a series of vistas, but as a map of thermal gradients and acoustic vibrations.

They are the masters of the "Manual Stacking." They move with a slow, agonizing deliberation, their four-fingered hands ending in blunt, spade-like claws. They do not speak in words; they communicate through the communal chanting that contributes to the city's roar—a low-frequency thrumming produced by air sacs in their chests. This "song" serves as a sonar, a territorial marker, and a rhythmic pace-setter for the heavy lifting. When ten thousand Hivers thrum in unison, the very stones they carry begin to vibrate, making them easier to slide into place.

### The Gliding Oxen (The Megascole)
In the narrow fissures between the hovels move the legless, pale-skinned amphibians. These are the *Megascole*. They are not draft animals in the traditional sense, for there are no carts or wheels, but they are the literal lubricants of the city. As they heave their ox-sized bulks through the crawlways, they secrete a thick, iridescent slime that reduces friction. The Hivers follow behind them, dragging massive stone slabs over the lubricated paths. 

The Megascole are mindless and docile, their existence a cycle of absorbing mineral-rich water through their skin and being slowly scraped of their slime by the passing Hivers. Their mouths are simple suction discs, perpetually clamped onto the weeping pores of the rock, drinking the earth’s perspiration.

### The Living Cables (The Vermis-Bridge)
Stretched across the vertical chasms and bridging the gaps between the rising mounds of stone are the translucent worms. These are not merely parasites; they are a vital structural component of the city. Their skin is incredibly tough, a biological polymer that resists the razor-edges of the basalt. 

The Hivers "plant" these worms when they are small, wedging them into cracks in the masonry. As the worms grow to the thickness of a human thigh, they bridge the void to reach the moisture on the opposite wall. Over time, they become taut, living ropes. The Hivers use them to haul themselves up the vertical fissures or to tether new stone slabs against the sinking weight of the city. The worms feel no pain in this; they exist in a state of hyper-tensed torpor, their bodies vibrating with the same subterranean thrum as the stone.

### The Scavenger Swarms (The Chitin-Clickers)
Beneath the feet of the Hivers and over the backs of the Megascole move the millions of multi-legged scavengers. These are the size of fists, resembling a cross between a trilobite and a louse. They possess hard, black shells—the only truly dark things in the city—and their collective movement creates a constant, dry clicking that undercuts the deep roar of the steam.

They are the cleaners. They consume the sloughed-off skin of the Hivers, the waste of the Megascole, and the mineral grit that accumulates in the puddles. They are a frantic, mindless tide of hunger, serving as the city’s digestive system. Without them, the narrow crawlways would choke on biological debris within a single generation.

### The Social Organism
There is no government in the Steam-Cradle, no kings, and no law. There is only the *Density*. Life here is a singular, focused impulse to stay above the silt. As the base of the city sinks into the soft, volcanic mush below, the inhabitants reflexively build upward. 

Individual intent is secondary to the collective rhythm. A Hiver does not decide to build a wall; he feels the vibration of the stone beneath him settling, hears the shift in the communal chant, and reaches for a slab of pumice because the rhythm demands it. 

They are a people of the "Eternal Now." Because the steam never clears and the light never changes, they have no concept of history or teleology. They do not think of a finished city. They only know the warmth, the wetness, the weight of the stone, and the ceaseless, comforting roar of a million hearts beating against the basalt. They are the nervous system of the rock, a frantic, wet, and heavy heartbeat in a world that refuses to finish being born.
