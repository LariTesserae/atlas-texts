In this kiln-dry pressure cooker, the inhabitants are not born; they are articulated. 

The primary residents are the **Aethel-Forges**, the mountain-sized entities that hover on the static-electric cushions. To look at one is to witness a cathedral in a state of perpetual, controlled explosion. Their "flesh" is a lattice of hexagonal brass plates, each the size of a city block, held together by the same gravitational sagging that carved the canyons. They do not possess eyes, for there is nothing to see that cannot be sensed through the seismic thrumming of the crust. Instead, they sport "sensory arrays" of oscillating crystal rods that glow with a dull, cherry-red heat. These rods twitch in the copper air, tasting the entropy of the horizon. They are the apex of this place, yet they are docile, their vast minds occupied with the million-year task of calculating the weight of their own shadows.

Living within the crevices of these titans are the **Gasket-Ticks**. These are the size of houses, spindly-legged parasites with thorax-drums that beat in perfect counter-rhythm to the combustion of their host. They are the mechanics of this world. They do not hunt; they maintain. With limbs like jeweler’s tools forged from obsidian, they crawl over the brass chitin of the Aethel-Forges, tightening bolts that are the size of redwoods and soldering the leaks of golden, viscous fluid that the world uses for blood. If an Aethel-Forge is a god, the Gasket-Ticks are the frantic, devotional thoughts that keep the god from falling apart.

Lower down, in the salt-flats where the "herds" grind reality into geometric primitives, live the **Clockwork Scavengers**. These take the form of the "winged looms" you sensed. They are made of silver wire and tensioned springs, their wings not feathers but vibrating membranes of spun glass. They follow the mile-long centipedal machines like gulls behind a trawler. When a centipedal machine steps and the ground "de-rezes," the Loom-Scavengers dive into the pixelated void. They catch the fleeting, non-existent particles of *What-Was-Before*—the discarded "meaning" of the rock before it was processed. They weave this "meaning" into intricate, glowing cocoons that they strap to their lattice-frames, carrying the weight of lost history back to the high mesas.

In the deepest canyons, where the world’s heavy bones have sagged the most, dwell the **Low-Friction Anchorites**. These are the most alien of all. They are singular, motionless entities—perfect spheres of polished chrome-basalt that do nothing but vibrate. They are the heat-sinks of the planet. They absorb the 2.7-level thrumming and turn it into the psychic radio-bursts that shriek across the plains. They are the broadcasters of the world’s "screaming." If you were to press your ear against one, you would not hear a heartbeat, but the sound of a billion gears turning in a desert ten thousand miles away. They are the memory-banks of the factory, recording every hiss of steam and every tectonic groan into the spin of their own metallic atoms.

There is no "prey" in the biological sense, only **Inefficiency**. The only "predators" are the **Culling-Engines**, sleek, multi-jointed harvesters that resemble giant, upright walking-beams. They do not seek meat; they seek friction. When a gear-driven creature begins to grind, when its timing slips and it creates heat without purpose, the Culling-Engines descend. With the slow, patient grace of a falling cliff, they dismantle the malfunctioning entity, harvesting its polished cogs and pressurized pistons to be redistributed among the healthy. 

This is an ecology of maintenance. The life here is a grand, unintentional liturgy of motion. They are the ghosts of a purpose, existing in a state of "industrial grace," where the act of surviving is indistinguishable from the act of manufacturing. They are beautiful, terrifying, and utterly indifferent to the copper-tasting air, for they are the ones who breathe life into the smoke and the rhythm into the ruin.
