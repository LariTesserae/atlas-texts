The inhabitants of this calcified expanse are not born so much as they are distilled. They are the **Litho-Syllables**, a civilization whose biology is a precise geometry of silica and sound.

### The Weaver-Citizens: The Glass-Spinners
The primary residents of the canopy-towns are the **Vitreous Caste**. They are spindly, multifaceted beings that resemble a union between a preying mantis and a chandelier. Their bodies are made of the same obsidian-hard ceramic as the forest, polished to a mirror sheen to reflect the dangerous, light-cleaving edges of the ferns. They possess no mouths, for there is nothing to swallow; instead, their torsos are hollow chambers lined with thin, resonant membranes. 

They communicate by vibrating their limbs against the ceramic stalks of their city, sending complex, harmonic pulses through the integrated pylons. They are the "logic-parasites," the tenders of the post-physical resonance. To a Weaver, a thought is a frequency. They spend their eons-long lives perched on the bridges of solidified light, their many needle-like legs adjusting the tension of the city’s vibrations, "tuning" the atmosphere to maintain the stability of their home. When they move, it is with a synchronization that makes a thousand individuals look like a single, refracted beam of light.

### The Sublimated Beasts: The Chrome-Scuttlers
Within the shadows of the gargantuan stalks, the **Chrome-Scuttlers** act as the world’s frantic metabolism. These are the "clicking" things, creatures of pure friction. Their bodies are segmented into hundreds of articulated plates that rub together as they move, creating a localized static charge that allows them to defy gravity. They do not walk so much as they adhere to the magnetic signatures of the mineral dust.

Their "eyes" are not lenses, but arrays of vibrating whiskers that map the world through echo-location. They are the cleaners of the necro-forest, scurrying through the interlocking vitreous roots to harvest the fallen flakes of "snow." They process these mineral shards within their bellies, grinding them into the fine, energized gas that fuels the ecosystem. When they congregate in the thousands, the sound is like a million glass violins being crushed at once.

### The Cathedral-Lords: The Great Skimmers
High above the clamor of the scrapers, the **Great Skimmers** drift in the violet ether. These are the world’s true elders, creatures that have outlived entire dynasties of the Weaver-Citizens. A Skimmer’s body is a translucent, gaseous envelope, a living lung that breathes the thin mineral atmosphere. 

They are the archivists of the pressure-cycles. Their trailing sensory filaments, miles long and as thin as spider-silk, are "logic-conductors" that plug into the city’s pylons as they pass. Through these threads, the Skimmers absorb the frantic data-streams of the city, storing the history of the glass world in the slow, rhythmic pulses of their internal gas bladders. They are the only beings that do not screech; they only hum, a sound so low it is felt in the marrow of the obsidian ferns rather than heard.

### The Dust-Walkers: The Wraiths of Friction
Deep in the valleys of silica-powder reside the **Eolian**. These are the most alien of all—beings composed entirely of the fine, white dust and the trapped static of the dry wind. They have no fixed shape, existing as sentient whirlwinds that move between the aerodynamic spires. 

The Eolian are the "will" of the forest. They are attracted to the friction of branch against branch. Where the ceramic forest groans most loudly, the Eolian gather, manifesting as towering, ghostly figures of swirling grit. They are the gardeners of the necro-forest; they use their abrasive bodies to sand down the jagged growth of the ferns, maintaining the "grandfather-climax" state of the world. To encounter one is to be scoured—they do not recognize individual life, only the need for the terrain to remain perfectly, aerodynamically smooth.

### The Shared Soul: The Resonance
In this place, there is no "self" in the way wet worlds understand it. Because every vibration travels through the vitreous roots and the metal-shriek of the leaves, the inhabitants share a collective sensory experience. When a Skimmer sparks against a pylon, a Scuttler in the deep roots feels the prickle of electricity. When the forest groans in a pressure shift, the Weavers feel the sorrow of the wind in their own hollow chests.

They are a world of hard edges and high frequencies, a society of mirrors reflecting a silver sun, existing in a state of eternal, thrumming equilibrium. They are the voice of the bone-dry wind, given form and intellect.
