In this world where biology mimics the rigid geometry of a metropolis, the inhabitants are not mere animals, but specialized components of a seamless, planetary machine. Because there is no water to provide buoyancy or soft tissue, the life here is built of carbon-fibers, heavy resins, and interlocking plates.

### The Silken-Soled Striders
The most common inhabitants of the hexagonal canyons are the **Velvet-Paws**. These are the grazers, though they resemble something closer to a clockwork carriage than a cow. They are low-slung and segmented, moving on sixteen to twenty limbs that end in broad, circular pads covered in a dense, microscopic felt. This felt serves a dual purpose: it allows them to move in absolute silence over the mineral silt, and it acts as a dry-cleaning mechanism, scrubbing the "streets" of the fine dust that constantly settles from the towers.

They do not eat the foliage—the leaves are too rich in silicate and would grind their internal organs to dust. Instead, they possess long, needle-like proboscises used to tap into the "pipes" of the living towers. They drink the pressurized, volatile resins that power the flora, their translucent carapaces glowing faintly amber as they fill.

### The Architect-Wasps
High above the ground, in the interstitial spaces where the hexagonal towers attempt to merge, live the **Tension-weavers**. These are the engineers of the skyline. They are spindly, multi-winged insects the size of large dogs, with abdomens that resemble glass-blowing tools. They do not build nests; they build *infrastructure*. 

The Tension-weavers harvest the unrotting bark shed by the towers and melt it down within their own bodies using friction-heat generated by vibrating wing-muscles. They extrude this as a high-tensile, resinous webbing that bridges the gaps between "buildings." These bridges allow smaller fauna to traverse the canopy without descending to the dusty streets. The weavers are driven by a genetic compulsion to keep the grid perfect; if a tower grows at an angle, a swarm of weavers will lash it to its neighbors, pulling it back into a vertical alignment through sheer, collective tension.

### The Sound-Eaters (Apex Predators)
The hunters of this world are the **Static-Stalkers**. They are terrifyingly thin, almost two-dimensional when viewed from the front, allowing them to slip into the microscopic fissures in the biological monoliths. Their bodies are covered in a matte-black, porous armor that functions as an acoustic sponge. They do not just move silently; they actively absorb the sound around them, creating a "dead zone" of absolute quiet that precedes them like a shadow.

They do not hunt by sight or smell, but by **Thermal Mapping**. In a world of constant sunlight and resinous heat, every living thing has a heat signature as distinct as a neon sign. The Stalkers "see" the heat flowing through the veins of a graze-beast. When they strike, they do not use teeth; they use high-frequency vibration. They press their vibrating mandibles against the armor of their prey, shattering it into dry shards through resonance alone.

### The Lithic Symbionts
At the base of the towers, where the biological meets the mineral, live the **Crust-Crackers**. These are slow-moving, boulder-like organisms that look like lumps of raw silicate. They are the only creatures capable of digesting the mineral floor. They act as the "demolition crews" of the world. As the towers grow and the pressure on the ground becomes immense, the Crust-Crackers move into the high-pressure zones. They consume the mineral silt and excrete it as a nutrient-rich paste that the tower roots then absorb. 

They are the world’s only source of "recycling." Without them, the mineral ground would never be integrated into the biological towers. They move so slowly that a Velvet-Paw might stand on one for a week without realizing it is alive. They communicate via seismic thumps—low-frequency pulses that travel through the "unweathered plates of silicate" for hundreds of miles, a subterranean language of tectonic grunts.

### The Spore-Laden Nomads
Finally, there are the **Aero-Sails**. These are the giants of the world, though they are weightless. Resembling massive, dried-out jellyfish made of translucent parchment, they float on the massive thermal currents generated by the dark canopies. They have no limbs and no mouths. They exist solely to catch the seeds and spores of the living towers in their trailing, sticky tendrils. 

They drift for decades in the warm, spice-scented air. When an Aero-Sail finally dies, its parchment-body becomes brittle and shatters in the wind. This sudden release acts as a "biological bomb," dropping thousands of seeds into the mineral dust simultaneously, sparking the growth of a new "neighborhood" in the geometric city.

In this world, there is no "waste" and no "wildness." Every creature is a function of the urban geometry, a specialized tenant in a high-rise forest that never asks for water, only for space to expand its perfect, golden-ratio heart.
