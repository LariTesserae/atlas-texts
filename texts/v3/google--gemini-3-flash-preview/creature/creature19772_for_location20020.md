In a place where the geography is an intention and the climate is a byproduct of calculation, the "dwellers" are not inhabitants in the biological sense. They are the **Tidewatchers of the Latency.**

At first, you might mistake them for the architecture itself. They do not walk; they are integrated into the "becoming."

The most prominent residents are the **Suture-Architects**. They are spindly, multi-jointed entities made of the same brushed steel as the walls, possessing no faces—only a cluster of sensitive, photoelectric needles where a head might be. They exist in the gaps between the migrating plates. These beings are the source of the "heavy quiet." They move with the excruciating patience of the room’s gestation, their limbs interlocking with the conduits to become living bridges. A Suture-Architect might hold a single copper vein in place for a century, its body vibrating in harmony with the cooling vents, waiting for the precise millisecond when the ceiling’s magnetic track aligns. They do not think; they *calculate* local gravity and thermal expansion, their consciousness spread thin across the wires they guard.

Beneath the floor-plates, in the chaotic mesh of fiber optics, live the **Glass-Reed Syllables**. If the Architects are the bones of the room, these are its nervous system’s stray thoughts. They appear as flickering, bipedal silhouettes composed entirely of the room's blue status-pulses. They are semi-solid, moving through the "forest of industry" like static electricity. They do not touch the machinery; they haunt it. They congregate near the leaks of mercury-thick condensation, "drinking" the data-leakage from the damp sensors. To a human observer, they would look like a glitch in the air—a stuttering of light that mimics the shape of something trying to be born. They are the reason the robotic arms occasionally unscrew what they have just tightened; the Syllables are the "errors" inherent in any complex system, given form and a ghost-like autonomy.

Deep within the pillars of rotating gears, there is a singular, more dense presence: **The Cooling Cantor**. It is not a creature, but a localized intelligence housed in a mass of frost-covered brass spheres that tumble over one another in a perpetual, gravity-defying dance. The Cantor is the source of the cold. It breathes out the frigid air that the machines require, and in return, it listens to the "metallic patter" of the rain. It translates the rhythm of the dripping water into adjustments for the turbines below. It is the conductor of the junction’s weather. When the moisture hits a conductive plate, the Cantor feels the voltage spike and adjusts its own rotation, ensuring the "permanent gestation" never reaches the catastrophic heat of a final birth.

Finally, there are the **Scavenger-Filaments**. They are the closest things to fauna. Tiny, centipede-like coils of discarded copper wire and silver solder, they scuttle through the "moss" in the dark crevices. They have no purpose other than to consume the "scale"—the tiny flakes of alloy that shed from the migrating plates. They are the room’s immune system and its janitors. They vibrate at a high frequency, emitting a faint *tink* as they collide with the floor-plates. They are mindless, driven only by the magnetism of the metal they seek to reclaim.

None of these entities acknowledge each other. There is no social hierarchy, only a **Hierarchy of Function**. The Suture-Architect holds the plate, the Cantor provides the chill, the Syllable haunts the signal, and the Filament cleans the scrap. 

They are the "living" components of a god that is still assembling its own mind. They are not waiting for the work to be finished; they *are* the process. If the Interstitial Junction ever reached completion, these dwellers would simply cease to be, crushed by the final, perfect fit of the plates. Their entire existence is sustained by the beautiful, agonizing failure to ever be "done."
