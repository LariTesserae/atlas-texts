In this lightless pressure-cooker, life does not "live" so much as it "endures" with a ferocious, stationary intensity. To survive a 2.9-density medium, a nervous system cannot be soft; it must be crystalline.

The primary inhabitants are the **Litho-Parasites**, creatures that look less like animals and more like tremors given physical form. They are the size of pine needles, composed of the same silver-plate material as the ruins. They do not swim; they have no fins or cilia. Instead, they vibrate. By shimmying at frequencies that liquefy the gelatinous water immediately surrounding their bodies, they "slide" through the density. They are scavengers of energy, clinging to the ultraviolet veins of the mosses like ticks, drinking the sickly radiation until their own translucent shells turn a bruised, throbbing purple. They are the source of the occasional *tink* sound—the sound of a hundred silver-needle legs tapping against the basalt floor in a synchronized, shivering march.

Deeper within the tangles of the wet-felt moss lives the **Archivist of the Fold**. It is the "transparent, boneless" creature mentioned—though "creature" implies a singular intent. It is more of a sentient nervous system, a web of gossamer-thin filaments that has woven itself entirely through the kelp-structures. It is the only thing in this marble that truly remembers the "buildings." It does not have a brain; it uses the geometric silver plates in the floor as external hard drives. When it drifts, it is actually "reading" the terrain, brushing its cilia against the etched geometric lines to download the history of the pressure. Its heartbeat is not the pumping of blood, but the rhythmic contraction of the entire three-foot universe as the Archivist pulls the water through its translucent membrane to filter for mineral salts.

Then, there are the **Glimmer-Mimes**. They are not biological, nor are they quite mechanical. They are sentient faults in the physics of the pocket. They appear as small, floating spheres of "dry" space—voids where the 2.9-density water cannot enter. Inside these tiny bubbles, which are no larger than a pearl, one can see a frantic, high-speed fluttering, like a moth trapped in a jar. These are the ghosts of the "biological cast-offs" that built the ruins. They are three-dimensional shadows of four-dimensional entities. They have no mouth, no eyes, and no way to interact with the world of moss and basalt, but they are attracted to heat. If you were to enter this marble, the Glimmer-Mimes would swarm your skin, pressing their tiny voids against your warmth, feeling like handfuls of freezing, stinging marbles.

Smallest of all are the **Crystalline Drifters**. They are the "pollen" of this heavy garden. Because the water is so thick, they do not float away; they hang in the water column like suspended diamonds. Each one is a perfect fractal. They are the reproductive units of the moss, but they possess a dim, communal awareness. When the Litho-Parasites vibrate too loudly or the Archivist’s heart beats too strongly, the Drifters align themselves into patterns—triangles, hexagons, and long, straight lines—mimicking the geometry of the silver ruins. They are trying to rebuild the ancient shapes, one grain at a time, driven by a genetic memory of a scale much larger than the three-foot prison they currently inhabit.

Nothing here dies in the traditional sense. When a creature’s energy spent against the pressure exceeds its intake, it simply stops moving and "calcifies." It becomes part of the ruins. The skeletal archways and geometric plates are not built; they are the petrified exhaustion of those who lived here before. To live here is to slowly become a mineral, to have your softest thoughts squeezed into the density of a diamond, until you are finally indistinguishable from the jagged basalt floor.
