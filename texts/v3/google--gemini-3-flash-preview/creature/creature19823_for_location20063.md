To live upon the Plateau of Constant Condensation is to be an extension of the hydraulics. There is no distinction between biology and the machinery; the inhabitants are not "citizens" but rather a specialized, drifting anatomy that serves the Slow Dynamic.

### The Silken Ones (The Operators)
The dominant "intelligent" life forms are the Silken Ones. They are not born so much as they are accreted. Standing seven to nine feet tall, they are spindly, elongated figures wrapped in heavy, oil-impregnated bandages of treated asbestos and rubber. Because the air is a mineral-rich broth, they do not eat in any traditional sense. Instead, they stand for hours atop the cooling fins of the Steam-Siphons, their long, porous fingers splayed, absorbing the nutrient-dense runoff through membranes in their palms.

They move with the same agonizing sloth as the pistons. A Silken One might take ten minutes to blink. Their eyes have grown wide and milky, protected by thick, gelatinous nictitating membranes that filter out the grit of the heavy water. They are entirely deaf—their ears have long since fused shut or been cauterized by the structural roar—so they communicate through "haptic humming." They press their chests against the vibrating brass of the refineries, sending low-frequency pulses through the metal to one another. To a Silken One, a change in a machine’s vibration is a poem; a stutter in a turbine is a tragedy.

### The Glandular Technicians
Deep within the "Ziggurat" refineries, in the spaces where the heat becomes pressurized and the brass glows a dull cherry-red, live the Technicians. These are shorter, squat beings whose skin has the texture of wet, boiled leather. They have no hair, as the heat and the moisture would rot it away. 

These beings are the "white blood cells" of the Plateau. They carry heavy, bulbous vats of bioluminescent grease on their backs, connected to their limbs via surgical hoses. Their sole purpose is the lubrication of the Great Joints. They crawl through the steam-tunnels, weeping a thick, protective mucus from their own pores that prevents the acid-condensation from pitting the delicate clockwork of the city. They do not sleep; they enter "stasis-drifts," leaning against the warm pipes, their heartbeats syncing with the 4/4 rhythm of the subterranean turbines.

### The Liturgy of the Gear-Crabs
The Gear-Crabs mentioned in the geography are more than mere scavengers; they are the architects of the "Slag-Graft." In the basins where the mineral runoff pools, these crabs gather the heavy metal silt and compress it between their carapaces. They eventually die by cementing themselves to the leaking joints of the pipes, their bodies becoming permanent, calcified patches that stop the steam-bleeds. The Plateau is littered with these "ancestral plugs"—generations of hard-shelled life that have become part of the very plumbing they once walked upon.

### The Vapour-Lungs (The Nomads)
Floating in the thickest pockets of the "Water-Light" are the Vapour-Lungs. These are translucent, jellyfish-like organisms that have evolved to defy the gravity of the shelf. They are nothing more than gas-bladders filled with the lightest, hottest steam. They drift between the giant fans like tumbleweeds made of wet lace. 

They are the only things that die quickly here. If they drift too high, the cold air above the plateau shatters their bladders, and they fall to the stone domes below like heavy, wet blankets. The Silken Ones consider these fallen Lungs to be sacred; they harvest the silk from their remains to create the bandages that clothe them.

### The Collective Mind: The Thrum
There is no "self" on the Plateau. Because the roar is so total, and the movement so slow, the inhabitants have developed a collective consciousness rooted in the vibration of the stone. They do not think in words, but in pressures. They feel the impending failure of a turbine three districts away as a dull ache in their own shins. 

They are the "Guardians of the Stasis." They do not seek to innovate or escape. Their entire culture is a slow-motion ritual of maintenance. To an outsider, they would look like statues in a drowned temple, but to the Silken Ones, they are the vital, pulsing heart of a god that must never be allowed to stop screaming. They are the only creatures in the universe who find peace in a deafening, industrial purgatory, for they have become the steam itself.
