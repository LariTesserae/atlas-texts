To live in the Cradle of the Marrow-Glow is to be an extension of the turbulence—nothing here is static, and therefore, nothing that lives here is ever truly at rest.

### The Chitin-Swimmers
The dominant masters of the basin floor are the **Chitin-Swimmers**. These are not mere animals; they are biological dredges, centipedal titans that possess no eyes, for the amber radiance of the ceiling provides no shadow to hunt by. Instead, their entire multi-segmented length is covered in billion-ton pressure sensors. They do not “walk”; they liquefy the silicate ahead of them with ultrasonic vibrations of their legs, literally melting a path through the cooling crust. 

To look upon a Swimmer is to see a train of iridescent armor, stained with the soot of the mantle. Their carapaces are not grown from calcium, but from layered sheets of industrial-grade sapphire and industrial diamond, harvested from the pressure of the deep. When two Swimmers collide, it does not sound like a fight between beasts; it sounds like two tectonic plates grinding together, a screeching of gemstone against gemstone that can shatter the "glass" wings of any lesser creature nearby.

### The Glass-Winged Clouds
Above the silicate sea, the air is thick enough to chew. It is occupied by the **Mirror-Gnats**. These insects do not eat organic matter—there is none. Instead, they are solar-collectors with wings. They spend their short, frantic lives flying as close to the translucent quartz ceiling as possible, drinking in the geothermal radiation. 

They move in "Hard-Storms." When a swarm turns in unison, the amber light catches their crystalline wings and reflects it downward in blinding, laser-like strobes. They are the primary source of death for the Builders; a swarm passing over a hut can heat the stone walls to a glowing red in seconds, cooking whatever is inside through sheer reflected radiance.

### The Siphon-Mouths and Pulse-Molds
Closer to the vents, life is more stationary but no less violent. The **Siphon-Mouths** resemble fleshy, translucent bells rooted into the magma. They are the "vegetation" of this world, though they are made of muscle and heat-resistant mucus. They pump liquid fire through their veins, filtering out rare minerals. 

Between the Siphon-Mouths, the **Pulse-Mold** grows. It is a thick, rubbery carpet of nervous tissue that covers the obsidian spires. It functions as the basin’s sensory organ. If you step on the Pulse-Mold, the entire square mile of terrain "knows" you are there. The ground ripples, the Siphon-Mouths retract, and the Chitin-Swimmers begin to turn their massive, diamond-plated heads toward your vibration.

### The Builders: The Lithos-Kin
Then, there are the **Builders**. 

They are a people of crushing weight and slow, deliberate movement. To survive the deafening roar and the heavy atmosphere, their bodies have evolved to be short, dense, and barrel-chested. Their skin is not soft; it is a grey, leathery hide, cracked like dried silt, capable of sweating a thick, mineral-rich oil that protects them from the scalding steam.

They have no language of vowels and consonants. Their voices cannot compete with the *crack-snap* of the world. Instead, they communicate through **Litho-Percussion**. They carry heavy basalt mallets and strike the obsidian floor or their own chest-plates in rhythmic sequences. A rhythmic *thump-crack* might mean "the magma is rising," while a rapid *clatter-stutter* is a warning of an approaching Swimmer.

The Builders are "Parasites of the Process." They live in the narrow window between the birth of a rock and its reclamation. They are master opportunists. When a new obsidian rib thrusts from the ground, they swarm it, lashing their stone huts to its base before it has even finished cooling. They are eternally "unpacking" and "repacking," their entire civilization a nomadic scramble to stay atop the newest, hardest ground.

They do not hunt for meat, but for **Structural Integrity**. They kill the smaller Chitin-Swimmers not to eat them, but to use their legs as support beams and their carapaces as heat-shields. Their most precious possessions are "The Cool-Stones"—ancient, rounded river rocks passed down from ancestors who lived in the upper crust, used as smooth molds to shape their jagged, violent world.

To the Builders, life is a race against the "Melt." They live with a frantic, industrial dignity, their eyes perpetually squinted against the amber noon, their hands always gripping a tool, forever hammering against the sound of a world that is trying to fold them back into its core.
