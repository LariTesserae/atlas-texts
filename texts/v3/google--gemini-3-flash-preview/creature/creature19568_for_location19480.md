To understand what lives in the Great Reservoir Spire, one must first understand that to inhabit this place is to be a creature of the **Grip**. There is no "standing" in the Spire; there is only holding on, or falling.

### The Maintenance Souls: The Brass-Hides
The dominant lifeforms are not strictly biological, nor purely mechanical. Over eons, the boundary has dissolved. The **Valve-Crabs** mentioned in the archives are merely the scavengers; the true masters of the vertical plane are the **Centurion Crustaceans**. These are the size of carriage-houses, possessing many-jointed limbs tipped with magnetic, pincer-like "wrenches" that fit perfectly into the ancient bolt-heads of the gantries. Their carapaces are a fusion of calcified bone and hammered copper, scarred by the constant sandblasting of the spray. 

They are the Spire’s involuntary gardeners. They do not eat the moss; they scrape the rust, consuming the oxidized metal to reinforce their shells, and in doing so, they prevent the structural failure of the world. They move with an agonizing, rhythmic slowness, their movements synchronized to the *thrum-clack* of the pressure valves. When a Centurion dies, it does not rot; it simply ceases to move, becoming a new, permanent outcrop upon which the ferns can take root.

### The Aerostatic Nomads: The Breath-Gliders
Higher up, in the regions where the warmth from the turbines creates powerful, humid thermals, live the **Lungs of the Spire**. These are the "birds" of the reservoir, but they possess no feathers. They are translucent, balloon-like organisms, their bodies consisting of vast, vascular sails stretched over rib-thin frames of flexible cartilage. 

They are living filters. They do not hunt with talons; they drift through the thickest curtains of "river-steel" mist with their mouths agape, straining microscopic algae and oil-suspended nutrients from the air. Their bioluminescence is not a lure, but a byproduct of their digestion—they glow a soft, chemical neon green, pulsing like heartbeat-lanterns as they navigate the dark spaces between the sodium lamps. They never land. To land is to be caught in the rain's weight and crushed against the iron.

### The Silken Architects: Spindle-Widows
In the shadows behind the massive filtration pipes, where the water is a fine vapor rather than a crushing deluge, live the **Spindle-Widows**. They are spindly, multi-eyed beings with elongated, delicate fingers that mimic the shape of the dripping moss. They are the only creatures that "build" in this world. 

Using a combination of harvested fern-fiber and a sticky, waterproof resin they secrete, they weave "gravity-hammocks"—intricate, suspended villages that dangle in the lee of the great gears. They are the Spire’s observers. They possess a primitive, communal intelligence, communicating through the vibrations of the metal. They can feel a turbine failing miles below by the change in the gantry’s hum, and they react by migrating upward, a thousand tiny, amber lights fleeing the cold.

### The Submerged Sentinels: The Turbine-Leeches
At the very bottom, where the "wall of rain" finally gathers into churning, frothing pools before being sucked back into the recycling veins, live the **Turbine-Leeches**. They are massive, serpent-like entities, sleek and eyeless, covered in a shimmering, rainbow-slick of industrial grease. 

They attach themselves to the rotating shafts of the great turbines, drawing energy directly from the friction and heat. They are the apex of this industrial ecology. They do not hunt the other creatures unless they fall into the pits. Instead, they act as the Spire’s "cleaners," their acidic saliva breaking down the debris—snapped cables, dead crabs, and clogged vegetation—that threatens to jam the machinery. They are the reason the Spire never floods; they keep the throat of the world clear.

### The Human Element: The Rust-Walkers
And then, there are the "people"—though the term is used loosely. The **Rust-Walkers** are descendent from the original maintenance crews, now unrecognizable. Their skin has taken on the slate-grey hue of the ceramic composites, and their feet have evolved into prehensile, wide-topped grippers. 

They do not use speech; the roar of the water is too loud. They communicate through a complex sign language of rhythmic clanging—striking the metal pipes with copper rods to send messages through the "bones" of the Spire. They live in the hollows of the largest derelict valves, dressing in "rags" woven from fungal silk and waterproofed with turbine grease. 

They view the Spire not as a machine, but as a god. To them, the "Great Rain" is the source of all grace. They spend their lives migrating vertically—climbing toward the "unseen summit" in a pilgrimage that lasts generations. They are part of the Spire's heartbeat now, as much a fixture as the rust or the ferns, forever clutching the wet metal, forever listening to the music of the falling world.
