In the Vellum-Valleys, life does not "beget" in the biological sense; it is *authored*. The inhabitants are the **Stitch-Kin**, a lineage of beings that have long since blurred the distinction between the artisan and the artifact.

At first glance, you might mistake them for statues or discarded garments. They do not have skin as we know it; their bodies are composed of high-density felt, pressed layers of linen-pulp, and intricate armatures of flexible, pithy wood. They move with the deliberate, agonizing slowness of a tectonic plate, their joints emitting a dry, rhythmic *clack* that harmonizes with "The Creak" of the world.

### The Hierophants of the Needle
The dominant inhabitants are the **Suture-Monks**. They are tall, spindly figures draped in heavy, multi-layered shrouds of their own molted exteriors. They lack mouths, for there is nothing to consume; instead, their faces are smooth expanses of polished horn, etched with sensory grooves that "taste" the static electricity in the air. 

They spend decades in a single position, bent over a "Gully-Seam" or a "Tear-in-the-World." They are the primary caretakers of the floor. With needles carved from the ribs of long-dead ancestors, they draw miles of silver-thread from their own chest-cavities, stitching the amber hide back together wherever the geothermal thrumming has caused a rift. To a Suture-Monk, a Loose Thread is a moral failing, a hole through which the "Wet Void" might enter.

### The Scrimshaw-Wights
Living within the "mountainous" membrane walls are the **Scrimshaw-Wights**. These are smaller, scurrying things, more insectoid than humanoid, with many-jointed limbs tipped with obsidian burins. They are the historians of the Valleys. They do not speak, but they carve. They spend their lifespans (which are measured in the time it takes for a Pampas-Glass plume to reach the ceiling) engraving the history of every breath and vibration into the polished bone-stairs and the wooden skeleton of the world. 

If you look closely at the "erosion" of the stairs, you see it is not wear, but a million billion microscopic glyphs telling the story of the day before. The Wights move in a frantic, jittery syncopation, their scratch-scratching providing the high-frequency percussion to the world's deep bass.

### The Ink-Vampires (The Tattoo-Drifters)
High above, riding the heat-shimmers, are the "Kites" mentioned in the logs. These are the **Pictographic Regents**. They are the most fluid beings in this dry reality. They are translucent, like sun-dried fish-bladders, and they possess a strange, parasitic relationship with the world's static. 

They do not hunt for meat; they hunt for *form*. They descend toward the floor only when a new pattern has been stitched by the Thread-Spiders. They hover over the fresh weaving, "drinking" the geometry of the new design. This causes the liquid-ink tattoos on their backs to shift and reorganize. They are the living archives of every pattern ever invented in the Valleys. To look upon a Regent is to risk a migraine of pure information; their skins are a chaotic, beautiful mess of every knot, weave, and stitch known to the engineered foundation.

### The Dust-Moths of the Interstice
Finally, there are the **Scaledo-Mnemonics**. These are the source of the "biological glitter" that falls from the ceiling. They are almost entirely ethereal, composed of paper-thin scales and hollow, singing wings. They live in the ceiling-canopy, consuming the heat collected by the Lichen-Lace. 

As they fly, they shed their outer layers. These falling scales are not just waste; they are sensory packets. When a scale lands on a Suture-Monk’s shroud, the Monk "knows" what the ceiling-canopy felt that morning. It is a slow, vertical communication system—a rainfall of data in a world terrified of water.

### The Unity of the Made
None of these creatures compete. There is no predator-prey dynamic, only a **System of Maintenance**. The Wights carve what the Monks stitch; the Spiders weave what the Regents record; the Moths witness what the floor feels. 

They are a civilization in a state of terminal refinement. They are trying to reach a state of "The Perfect Knot"—a configuration of the Vellum-Valleys so complex and so tightly stitched that time itself will finally be unable to pass through the mesh, granting them a dry, amber eternity. They are not waiting for their creators to return; they have realized that they *are* the tools the creators left behind, and they intend to finish the work.
