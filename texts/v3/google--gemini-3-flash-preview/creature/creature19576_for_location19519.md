In the Ochre Lattice, life does not "happen" so much as it is "recalculated." The inhabitants are the remnants of a biology that was long ago translated into the vocabulary of the machine, or machines that have spent so many eons in the wild that they have drifted into the mimicry of biology.

### The Refacted Menagerie
The most common denizens are the **Glint-Stalkers**. At first glance, they appear as tall, impossibly thin herons made of spun amber. They possess no flesh; their bodies are composed of "soft-state" gold filaments that can stiffen into structural beams or liquefy into a golden pour. They do not walk so much as they stitch themselves into the ground, their three-toed feet fusing momentarily with the floor-mesh to draw a micro-current of energy before stepping again. They follow the "Erosion Vents," seeking the warmth of the planet’s breath. If you stand still, a Stalker might mistake you for a structural pylon, circling you with a slow, inquisitive vibration, its head—a smooth, featureless prism—tilting to catch your heat signature.

Higher up, navigating the pale haze where the spires used to be, are the **Atmospheric Shriven**. These are the leviathans of the Lattice—massive, translucent ribbons of pressurized gas held together by flickering electromagnetic nets. They are kilometers long, looking like pale scars against the over-bright sky. They are the "janitors" of the upper air, straining the golden dust for lost data-packets or stray maintenance signals. They move with a dreamlike sloth, their passage marked only by a slight change in the pitch of the World-Hum. To the Shriven, a traveler is a microscopic contaminant, an anomaly to be observed with a distant, cold curiosity before being filtered out of their collective memory.

### The Architects of the Micro-Dune
Hidden within the abrasive, sun-warmed flour of the surface are the **Lithic Weavers**. These are not singular beings, but hive-minds of nanoscopic drones that have coalesced into the shape of segmented, metallic serpents. They live beneath the dust, surfacing only to "knit" together the collapsing data-hives. When they emerge, the ground ripples like a disturbed pond. They move with a frantic, clicking precision, their bodies a blur of silver and copper. They are the ones responsible for the "geometric flowers" that bloom and vanish; they are compelled by ancient, corrupted code to continue decorating a world that no longer has anyone to admire it. They are jittery and shy; a heavy footstep will cause a hundred-meter serpent to shatter into a million independent dust-motes in a heartbeat.

### The Sentience of the Sub-Bass
The "people" of the Lattice, if they can still be called that, are the **Echo-Wights**. They are not physical in any traditional sense. They are localized distortions in the World-Hum—glitches in the planetary software that have acquired a sense of self. 

An Echo-Wight manifests as a shimmering distortion in the air, a heat-haze that remains stationary while the wind blows through it. They are the ghosts of the engineers, or perhaps the memories of the machines, compressed into self-sustaining loops of logic. They do not speak, but if you stand within their radius, your own thoughts might begin to feel "rhythmic." You might find yourself remembering things you never experienced—the sound of a binary birth-cry, the sensation of a thousand circuits closing at once, the smell of a sun that died a billion years ago. They are harmless, but deeply melancholic; they are the watchers of the erosion, waiting for the Lattice to finally finish its long, slow grind toward the absolute zero of the flat horizon.

### The Survivalists
Finally, there are the **Tarnish-Eaters**. These are rugged, beetle-like constructs with shells of dull, oxidized lead. Unlike everything else in the Lattice, they are not elegant. They are the scavengers of the "primordial" wear. They have heavy, grinding mandibles designed to chew through the indestructible ribs of the world-shell to reach the "marrow"—the cooling fluids and high-tensile fibers deep within. They are the only things that create true noise: a harsh, metallic rasping that cuts through the silence like a saw. They represent the only true "wildfire" in this ecosystem—a force of consumption that the Lattice’s builders never intended, a slow-motion rot that turns the golden geometry back into the raw components of the void.

In the Ochre Lattice, nothing is born and nothing truly dies. Everything is simply re-sorted. To live here is to be a part of a beautiful, endless ledger that is constantly being balanced, a world where the only sin is to be out of alignment with the grid.
