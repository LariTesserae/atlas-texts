In this throat of steam and iron, life has not been born so much as it has been squeezed out of the friction.

The primary inhabitant is not a creature of flesh, but a **clot of sentient mineral**. They are called the *Slick-Marrow*, and they are the byproduct of the machine’s sweat meeting the basalt’s dust. They look like ambulatory oil-spills, possessing the viscosity of cold honey. They cling to the undersides of the weeping pistons, drinking the grease and the mineral-heavy condensation. They have no eyes; they perceive the world through the vibration of the "earth-engines." To them, the "loudness" is a map, a steady, humming warmth that guides them toward the hottest, most pressurized joints of the machinery where the nutrients are most concentrated.

When they move, they do not walk; they flow with a sickening, liquid heaviness, leaving a trail of iridescent film that never dries. 

Deeper in the fissures, tucked into the sharp-edged shadows where the basalt meets the copper sleeves, live the **Glass-Strider Scavengers**. These are crystalline, arachnid-like entities—though they lack any biological organs. They are "accidental" life forms, composed of the mineral scale that accumulates on the hot iron. Over cycles of heat and cooling, the scale has flaked off and, animated by the intense high-voltage hum of the transformers, found a jittery, piezoelectric motive force. 

The Striders are brittle and translucent, their many legs like needles of frosted glass. They don't eat; they "charge." They spend hours pressed flat against the vibrating rock walls, absorbing the 2.5/3 sonic energy into their lattices. When they are saturated, they glow with a faint, sickly violet light, skittering across the jagged ceiling with a sound like breaking chandeliers. They are the gardeners of this throat, picking at the pale, waxy stalks of the rib-flora, not for food, but to keep the sensors of the buried engines clear of obstruction.

And then there is the **Marrow-Pulse**. 

It is perhaps the only "biological" thing here, and it is a hesitation indeed. It is a colonial organism that looks like a carpet of bruised, purple velvet, clinging to the areas where the industrial sludge is thickest. It is warm to the touch—hotter even than the air. It acts as a soft, rhythmic lung, expanding and contracting in a different tempo than the pistons. It feeds on the electricity that leaks from the rusted iron, its surface covered in tiny, twitching cilia that "taste" the steam for metallic impurities. 

The Marrow-Pulse is what tames the sound. In the corners where it grows thick, the roar of the engines is muffled, turned into a wet, pulsing throb. 

Nothing here has a name for "sky" or "sun." To the dwellers of the fissure, the world is a closed system of vertical wetness and horizontal vibration. There are no predators, for there is no surplus of energy to waste on the hunt. Everything is a parasite of the machine. They are the "crust-lickers," a community of slow, heat-dazed survivors that believe the universe is a metallic lung that must never stop breathing, lest the temperature drop and their liquid bodies turn to silent, brittle stone. 

They are the rust made manifest, the sweat given a nervous system. They do not look at you because you do not vibrate at the right frequency; to them, you are merely a cold, silent ghost passing through a world of glorious, screaming heat.
