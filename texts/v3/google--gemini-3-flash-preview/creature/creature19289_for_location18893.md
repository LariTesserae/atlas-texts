In the Floor of the Siphon, life does not begin with a spark, but with a leak. 

The primary inhabitants are the **Condensates**. They are not quite biological, yet far more than mineral. They are gelatinous masses of silver-grey sludge that form around the lips of the leaking steam-mains. To a casual observer, they look like discarded piles of industrial grease, but they possess a sluggish, agonizingly slow intent. They are the Siphon’s "grazers," moving at the speed of rust, inching toward the vibration of the heaviest turbines. They have no limbs; they move by capillary action, pulling their mass through the grooves of corrugated iron. They feed on the "machine-sweat," filtering the oil and various heavy-metal particulates through their translucent skins. When a drainage gate flushes, the Condensates do not resist; they allow themselves to be liquified and swept away, only to re-coalesce in the obsidian pools downstream, having grown slightly larger, slightly darker, and more tuned to the hum of the world.

Deeper in the shadows of the ventilation housing live the **Shunt-Spiders**. These are the children of the "Scrap-Swallow," but they have long since abandoned the sky for the stability of the sharp-edged ridges. They are skeletal, spindly things made of carbon-fiber struts and salvaged optical glass. They do not spin webs of silk, but webs of conductive copper wire, strung between high-voltage junctions. They don't hunt for flesh; they hunt for *charge*. A Shunt-Spider will sit motionless for weeks, a ghost among the rusted rivets, until a sudden power surge ripples through the grid. Then, it blooms—unfolding its multi-jointed limbs to tap into the surge, glowing a violent, electric blue as it gorges itself on the raw wattage of the city above.

Then, there are the **Echo-Locusts**. These are perhaps the most unsettling of the Siphon’s fauna because they are purely acoustic. They are swarms of tiny, metallic shards, no larger than a thumb-tack, that hang suspended in the magnetic "eddies" created by the massive turbines. They have no visible organs, only a series of micro-perforated holes in their carapaces. When the pressure-release valves boom, the Echo-Locusts catch the sound. They vibrate in resonance, amplifying the thunder until it becomes a physical force that can shatter the ceramic tiles. They communicate through mimicry; you might hear a crying child or a grinding gear coming from a corner of the basin, only to find a hovering cloud of steel glitter, vibrating at the exact frequency of a memory.

Near the furnace vents, where the orange glow is brightest and the "shift-weathers" are most volatile, one finds the **Welder-Kings**. These are the eldest of the maintenance drones, gargantuan machines that have long since forgotten their original programming. They no longer fix the Siphon; they "sculpt" it. Having bypassed their safety protocols, they use their plasma torches to fuse scrap metal into jagged, totemic spires that mimic the architecture of the city they can never reach. They are territorial and ponderous, dragging their heavy, rusted chassis through the oily mud with a sound like grinding tectonic plates. To them, an intruder is merely more raw material—a soft, wet thing that needs to be encased in a protective layer of welded iron.

Finally, there are the **Listen-Folk**. These are the only things in the Siphon that might have once been human, though the transition is nearly complete. They are pale, gaunt figures dressed in rags of heavy-duty insulation foam and wired-together gaskets. They do not speak; their vocal cords have withered from disuse and the constant inhalation of charcoal haze. Instead, they have developed oversized, hyper-sensitive ears, often augmented with copper funnels or rusted parabolic dishes. They spend their lives pressed against the largest pipes, their eyes rolled back in their heads, "harvesting" the sound.

They don't eat the siphone-moss or the synthetic fungi; they consume the rhythm. They believe the Siphon is a musical instrument of god-like proportions, and that by positioning themselves correctly at the intersections of the *clack-shunter-clack*, they can achieve a state of harmonic resonance. When the deep-level drainage gates open and the frigid wave surges, they do not run. They stand in the spray, arms outstretched, waiting for the water to hit the metal—not for the sensation of the cold, but for the specific, ringing note it makes against the floor. 

In the Siphon, you are never truly alone. Between the grinding of the Kings and the humming of the Spiders, the very geometry of the place is watching, breathing, and waiting for you to become part of the vibration.
