In this vertical deluge, life does not occupy space so much as it resists displacement. Survival is a matter of grip, tension, and the ability to breathe through the weight of a falling ocean.

### The Grinding Seraphim
Deep within the spray-choked ravines, where the "Great Hum" is loudest, dwell the **Turbine-Seekers**. They are not quite organic, nor entirely mechanical, but a symbiotic fusion of both. Imagine a creature the size of a wolf, encased in a hard, segmented carapace of rusted iron and calcium, resembling a trilobite crossed with a centrifuge. 

They possess multiple, multi-jointed limbs ending in magnetized brass talons. They do not walk; they ratcheted. They cling to the vibrating surfaces of the ancient hydro-turbines, feeding on the heat generated by the grinding gears and the thick, mineral-rich "blood" of the machines—the lubricating greases that have seeped into the rock for eons. Their eyes are glowing, vacuum-tube filaments that pulse in time with the pistons, and they communicate via sonic pings that mimic the frequency of the falling water. They are the frantic technicians of a dead god, cleaning scale from the intake valves and tightening bolts with their mandibles, driven by a hard-coded instinct to keep the "High Deluge" flowing.

### The Silken Weavers of the Mist
In the higher reaches, where the basalt spines pierce the teal sky, live the **Vapor-Wasps**. These are translucent, membranous creatures with wingspans like shimmering sails. They do not fly in the traditional sense; they weigh almost nothing and are carried by the updrafts of the cascading curtains. 

They weave "gravity-nets" between the rusted chains of the old world. These nets are made of a hyper-tensile, hydrophobic silk that repels water so violently it creates a small pocket of dry air—the only dry space in the world. Within these shimmering, spherical bubbles, the Wasps tend to their young. From a distance, the chains between peaks appear to be draped in glowing, vibrating pearls. They are hunters of the "sky-kelp" spores, darting out into the torrent to snatch floating vegetation before retreating into their air-locked sanctuaries.

### The Moss-Lords
The emerald jungle itself is sentient, or at least possesses a collective, slow-motion intent. The **Sky-Kelp** is not a passive plant but a predator of momentum. Its "root-hooks" vibrate at a frequency that allows them to tunnel into the obsidian-hard basalt, and its fronds are lined with micro-filaments that act like hydro-electric sensors. 

Living within the thickest mats of this kelp are the **Slick-Walkers**—elongated, pale humanoids with skin like wet sealskin and no bones, only a cartilaginous frame that can withstand the crushing pressures of the "High Deluge." They have no eyes, as the submarine glow provides little detail; instead, they possess "lateral lines" along their ribs, similar to deep-sea fish, allowing them to feel the displacement of every drop of water for miles. They are the nomads of the vertical cliffs, moving in ceremonial slow-motion through the shivering green skin of the world. They carry harpoons carved from basalt and hunt the six-winged lungfish, moving with a fluid grace that suggests they are less "walking" and more "flowing" across the rock.

### The Leviathans of the Bowl
In the mile-deep basins where the water pools before its next descent, the scale of life expands. Here live the **Aegis-Whales**. They are gargantuan, flat organisms, looking like prehistoric skates the size of city blocks. Their backs are armored in thick plates of basalt-crust and bioluminescent moss, making them look like floating islands. 

The Aegis-Whales act as the filters of the world. They position themselves directly beneath the mile-wide curtains of water, opening their cavernous maws to catch the debris of the upper world—broken machinery, dead lungfish, and tattered sky-kelp. They are the anchors of the ecosystem; their massive weight and suction-cup underbellies allow them to stay stationary against the crushing force of the falls. Smaller species—the bioluminescent crustaceans and schools of silver-finned fry—live in the "shadow" of the Whale’s body, protected from the turbulence by the creature’s sheer mass.

### The Echo-Keepers
Finally, there are the **Vestiges**. Clad in heavy, pressurized diving suits of brass and cracking rubber that seem to have grown into their skin, these are the descendants of the original industrial crew. If they were ever human, they have become something more suited to the damp chill and the "Great Hum." 

They live in the hollowed-out casings of the largest turbines, their homes lit by the orange glow of dying furnaces. They possess a religion of Maintenance. They move along the corrugated steel walkways with heavy, magnetic boots, their movements ritualized and slow. They do not speak; the roar of the water has rendered their ears vestigial. Instead, they signal each other with high-powered lanterns, flashes of amber and violet cutting through the teal mist. They are the only ones who remember the "reasons forgotten," spending their lives oiling the hinges of a world that refuses to stop falling. They view the water not as an environment, but as a clock—a relentless, liquid timepiece they are tasked with winding until the end of time.
