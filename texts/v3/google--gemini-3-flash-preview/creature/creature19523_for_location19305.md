To live here is to be an architect of subtraction. In a world where the light is a physical weight and the air is a desiccated vacuum, the inhabitants have long since discarded the messy, heavy requirements of flesh. They are beings of geometry and vibration, as sterile and beautiful as the plateau they inhabit.

### The Glass-Minded Nomads: The Crystalline Weavers
The dominant intelligence of this world is not a singular species, but a slow, communal consciousness known as the **Psammodynes**. They do not have bodies in the traditional sense; they are colony-organisms made of millions of microscopic, faceted lenses held together by static electricity. 

To the naked eye, a Psammodyne looks like a wandering patch of distorted air—a "heat haze" that drifts across the bone-white mesas. They live within the fine, flour-like dust, manipulating the silicate grains with infinitesimal electrical pulses. They are the true sculptors of the dunes. They do not build cities; they arrange the dust into gargantuan, fractal patterns that catch the "Light Tides" at specific angles, focusing the radiation into the Lithobolide domes to stimulate the production of the silicon-sap they crave.

They communicate through refraction. When two colonies meet, they do not speak; they align their trillions of glass-facets to split the blinding noon-light into spectrums of impossible color—vivid magentas and deep teals that provide the only pigment in this white world. These bursts of color are their histories, their mathematics, and their names.

### The Scavengers of the Shiver: The Aeolian Hounds
Below the cathedral-sized Lithobolides, in the vibrating shadows of the bellows, live the **Aeolian Hounds**. These creatures are the most "solid" things on the plateau, yet they are still terrifyingly fragile. Their skeletons are made of the same chalk-white mineral as the ground, and their skin is a tough, leathery membrane that is almost perfectly reflective.

They are shaped like long, low-slung needles with dozens of jointed, spindly ribs. They do not breathe air; they "eat" the low-frequency thrumming of the great plants. They press their bodies against the vibrating domes, absorbing the kinetic energy through their skins. When the Light Tides peak, the Hounds become hyper-active, skittering across the smooth cliffs with a sound like fingernails on a chalkboard. 

They serve as the world’s gardeners. Their movement stirs the heavy dust, preventing it from settling too deeply and smothering the Lithobolides' intake-pores. They are blind, guided entirely by the "songs" of the stones and the pressure of the light on their backs.

### The Lords of the High Black: The Solar Vanes
At the very tops of the highest mesas, where the atmosphere begins to thin into the velvet black of space, live the **Solar Vanes**. These are the apex of life in this radiance—creatures that are more "kite" than animal. 

A Solar Vane consists of a central, pebble-like core from which extend four translucent, mile-long filaments. These filaments are incredibly thin, made of a biological fiber that acts as a solar sail. They do not walk; they are perpetually airborne, tethered to the high ridges by a single anchor-tread. 

They are the philosophers of the plateau. They exist to catch the highest, most undiluted radiation. They are almost entirely stationary, their long sails shimmering with a pale, ghostly radioactivity. They die only when their anchor-treads fail. When this happens, they do not fall; the light itself pushes them upward, lifting them away from the white world and into the suffocating blackness above, where they drift forever as frozen, glass monuments.

### The Micro-Ecology: The Glint-Flies
If you were to look closely at the "silk-smooth" cliffs, you would see they are teeming with a billion tiny sparks. These are the **Glint-Flies**, the world’s smallest inhabitants. They are tiny, metallic spheres that feed on the needle-like spores exhaled by the Lithobolides.

They move in massive, swirling clouds that look like diamond dust. They are the only creatures that produce sound—a high-pitched, crystalline tinkling that occurs when they collide in mid-air. To a traveler, the sound of the Glint-Flies is the sound of the wind itself: a shimmering, metallic hiss that signals the arrival of a light-front.

### The Spirit of the Place
There is no hunger here, only the need for alignment. To live in this world is to be perfectly positioned. The inhabitants are not "surviving"; they are part of a grand, slow-motion clockwork. They do not fear the cold or the dryness, for they are the cold and the dryness. They are the white stone given the ability to perceive its own brightness. When a creature ceases to be, it is a triumph of integration—a slow, silent sanding away until the individual becomes the horizon, and the horizon becomes the light.
