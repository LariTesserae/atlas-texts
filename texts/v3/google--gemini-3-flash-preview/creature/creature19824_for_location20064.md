To exist in the Vast Saturated Flattening is to be a creature of surface area. In a world where there is no "in" or "under"—only the endless, pressurized "on"—life has abandoned the concept of the body as a vessel. Here, life is a film, a vibration, or a stain.

### The Liturgists of the Pylons
While the **Lung-Mosses** are the flora, they are tended by the **Marrow-Gleaners**. These are semi-solid entities that resemble spills of rusted mercury. They do not have limbs; they possess "tendencies." They congregate around the obsidian Fingers, pressing their fluid masses against the black glass to catch the heat-bleed. 

They are the archivists of the old world’s friction. Because the obsidian pylons are the only things that do not erode, the Gleaners use them as whetstones for their own consciousness. They pulse in sync with the "Silt-Claps," and in the brief silence following a sonic boom, they secrete a thin, crystalline film onto the pylons—a microscopic braille that records the frequency of the world’s groan. They are slow, moving perhaps an inch every century, and they dream in geometry, obsessing over the angles of the pillars as if they were the last remaining verbs in a language of nouns.

### The Echo-Parasites (The Humming Veils)
The deafening noise of the Flattening is not just a background condition; it is a food source. Floating just beneath the surface of the mercury-thick water are the **Vocalists**. They look like tangles of silver wire or discarded lace, drifting in the shallow warmth. They have no digestive tracts. Instead, they possess thousands of microscopic, shivering diaphragms. 

They live on the "Great Cacophony." They harvest the low-frequency thrum, converting the kinetic energy of the sound into a cold, internal light. When a Phase Shift occurs and a cube of water rises into the air, the Vocalists are caught within it, suspended like insects in amber. In the sudden silence of the gravity-less void, they begin to scream—a high, melodic counter-point to the world’s roar—using their stored energy to sing back at the silence until the water crashes down and they return to their humming, submerged stasis.

### The Skimmer-Lice (The Translucent Coteries)
The **Skimmers**—those miles-wide gelatinous sheets—are not solitary. They are ecosystems. Living atop and within the membrane of a Skimmer are the **Tetragonals**. These are the only things in the Flattening that resemble "animals," though they are made of the same hyper-dense silt as the floor. 

They are flat, four-sided organisms that look like tiles of wet slate. They skitter across the surface of the Skimmers, acting as a strange sort of immune system. They smooth out ripples in the Skimmer’s flesh, patrolling the vast translucent plains for "impurities"—which, in this world, means anything with a shape. If a traveler were to stand still long enough, the Tetragonals would arrive to break them down, grinding the intruder’s foreign geometry into the uniform silt of the world. They are the enforcers of the Flattening, the janitors of the end-state.

### The Remnant-Minds (The Silt-Walkers)
Deep within the hyper-compacted silt floor, there are things that are not quite alive but cannot be called dead. These are the **Silt-Walkers**. They are the ghosts of the mountains, the recycled consciousness of the civilizations that were pulverized to make this plane. 

They manifest as momentary "sculptures" in the mud. For a few seconds, the silt will heave upward—not because of a Phase Shift, but because of a memory. A face may form in the sludge, miles wide and terrifyingly detailed, or a hand will reach up from the mercury-water to grasp at the bruised purple sky. They have no agency; they are the "subconscious" of the geography. They represent the atoms' exhaustion. Sometimes, when the wind groans like a tectonic plate, the Silt-Walkers groan back, a billion voices of compressed stone trying to remember the sensation of being a peak, of being cold, of being separate.

### The Specter of the "Third Dimension"
There is a final, rarest inhabitant: the **Null-Point**. Occasionally, the pressure and the age of the world produce a knot in reality—a creature that is a hole. It appears as a small, perfectly spherical void that drifts through the heavy mist. It does not vibrate. It does not hum. It is the only thing in the Vast Saturated Flattening that is truly empty. 

The other inhabitants—the Gleaners, the Vocalists, even the massive Skimmers—recoil from the Null-Point. In a world where everything is touched, everything is dense, and everything is crowded, the Null-Point is the only "room" left. It is the destination for all the world’s tired atoms, a tiny, drifting exit in a world that has forgotten how to have a door.
