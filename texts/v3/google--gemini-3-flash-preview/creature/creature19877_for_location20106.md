The people who dwell within this vertical loom are not merely quiet; they are physiologically tuned to the hush. They are the **Lace-Strider Folk**, creatures of long, attenuated proportions whose very biology has been sculpted by the need for weightlessness and secrecy. 

They are gaunt, their limbs seeming too long for their torsos, their bones hollow and honeycombed like those of high-altitude birds. Their skin is the color of the basalt—a bruised, slate-grey—textured with a fine, velvet-like down that dampens the sound of their own movements. They do not wear clothing in any traditional sense; instead, they wrap their chests and loins in "ghost-hair," the discarded silk of the Glass-Wing moths, which provides insulation without the friction of cloth.

**The Anatomy of Silence**
The most striking feature of a Lace-Strider is their feet. They have lost the broad, heavy heels of terrestrial humans. In their place are elongated, prehensile toes, tipped with a leathery, calloused pad that can grip the slick, cork-wood planks like a hand. They walk with a high, tip-toed gait, placing each foot with a deliberate, rolling pressure to avoid the creak of a dry knot or the snap of a stray twig.

Their faces are dominated by enormous, lidless eyes, tawny and flecked with gold, capable of pulling every stray photon from the glowing "damp." They have no external ears; their hearing is conductive, processed through the bones of their jaw and skull to detect the rhythmic vibrations of the Gorge. Because they do not speak, their mouths have become small and tight, used only for the intake of the nutrient-rich, translucent pastes they harvest from the Pulse-Sliders.

**The Social Loom**
Life in the clusters is a hive of synchronous, unspoken cooperation. Because a single scream could bring a Sky-Mute plunging from the violet clouds, the Lace-Striders operate under a "Shared Pulse." They communicate through *The Flickering*, a language of high-speed finger-gestures, subtle shifts in the dilation of their pupils, and a tactile braille practiced when they huddle together for warmth at night.

Their social hierarchy is determined by the **Web-Minders**—the elders who possess the deepest knowledge of knots. There is no metal to forge, no stone to carve; all status is draped in the complexity of one’s weaving. A leader is not the strongest, but the one who can repair a fraying lichen-rope in the dark of a storm without making a single sound.

**The Life Cycle of the Suspended**
Childhood is a terrifying, breathless ordeal. Infants are born with a grasping reflex so powerful it can bruise. They are kept "tethered" from the moment of birth, strapped to their mothers' backs with umbilical-like cords of woven grass. A child does not learn to speak; a child learns to stay still. A tantrum is a death sentence, so the mothers have developed a method of soothing the young by humming into the child’s fontanelle—a vibration that transfers directly to the bone, unheard by the air, a secret lullaby of the skull.

**The Cult of the Sky-Mute**
Their spirituality is a religion of gravity and evasion. They do not worship the sun, which they have never seen, but the "Abyss-That-Nourishes" and the "Shadows-That-Judge." The Sky-Mutes are their gods—terrifying, erratic deities that must be appeased by the sacrifice of noise. 

When a Strider grows old and their joints begin to click—when they can no longer walk without the betrayal of sound—they undergo the *Descent*. They do not wait for the Sky-Mutes to find them. Instead, they douse themselves in the glowing slime of the Pulse-Sliders, making themselves a beacon of cold light, and step off the edge of the lowest bridge. They fall into the dark, a silent prayer of light descending into the throat of the mountain, ensuring that for one more night, the hunger of the heights is directed downward, away from the woven homes of the living.

**The Shadow-Walkers**
Among them are the "Damp-Gatherers," the most daring of the folk. They are the ones who venture out onto the outer vines, where the succulent shields are slickest. They carry bladders made of moth-gut, harvesting the glowing condensation. They move with a terrifying fluidity, literally swinging through the gaps in the city, their bodies silhouetted against the bruised twilight. They are the dancers of the void, living symbols of the city’s defiance: a people who have learned that to survive the sharpest place in the world, one must become as soft and as silent as the fog.
