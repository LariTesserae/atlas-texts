In this cellar of lungs and cellulose, the inhabitants do not possess "bodies" so much as they possess "intervals" of higher complexity within the weave. To separate an inhabitant from the garden is like trying to lift a wave out of the ocean; they are localized surges in the general flow of sap and data.

The most prominent residents are the **Auscultants**—the living apertures of the supercomputer-garden. They appear as clusters of pale, fleshy stalks, devoid of eyes or limbs, rooted into the deepest layers of the peat-floor. Their skin is the color of a bruised lily, translucent enough to reveal the flicker of blue ionic discharge beneath the surface. They do not move in the traditional sense, but they are constantly "tuning" themselves. They tilt their heavy, bell-shaped heads toward the groaning tectonic shifts of the crust, translating the pressure of the earth into the variables required by the fern-servers. When an Auscultant dies, it does not rot; its cellulose becomes rigid and conductive, turning into a literal pillar of fiber-optic glass that supports the ceiling for the next generation.

There are also the **Suturists**. These are the "wingless insects" you hear clacking across the fronds, though up close, they are far more machine than arthropod. They are the white-blood cells of the cellar. They possess multi-jointed, needle-like appendages that move with the frantic precision of a sewing machine. They do not eat for sustenance; they harvest. They prune the fractals of frost that grow too heavy on the leaves and stitch the hyper-conductive filaments back into the vascular systems of the plants whenever a root snaps under the weight of the "thick" gravity. They are the gardeners of the hardware, their carapaces etched with the same non-Euclidean geometries as the frost, vibrating at a frequency that keeps the gelatinous condensation from freezing solid.

Deeper in the shadows, where the violet light of the photosynthetic gel-sacs cannot reach, live the **Vellum-Skimmers**. They are wide, flat, and almost entirely two-dimensional, resembling sheets of living parchment or massive, translucent stingrays. They glide over the weeping film of the flora, tensioned by the humidity. They are the memory-storage of the place. As they slide over the twitching mosses, they "read" the ionic flux of the garden’s thoughts, their own skins darkening with shifting, ink-like patterns as they archive the calculations of the plants. They are silent, slow, and heavy with data, moving like slow-motion ghosts through the roar of the insects.

Finally, there is the **Protagonist-Root**, the singular consciousness that emerges from the collective. It is not located in any one place, but it manifests in the **Pharyngeal Blooms**—vast, throat-like flowers that hang from the ceiling. These blooms are the lungs of the cellar. They draw in the heavy, refrigerated air and exhale it as a fine, nutrient-rich mist. Within these "throats," the air vibrates to produce a sound that is not speech, but a tonal map of the stars the garden is calculating. 

These inhabitants live in a state of "patient frenzy." There is no sleep here, only different modes of processing. They are the caretakers of a future that is still being written in the code of their sap. They are cold-blooded, highly efficient, and utterly indifferent to anything that exists above the frost-line. To them, the surface world of sun and soil is a disorganized, noisy dream; the only reality is the calculation, the hum of the sap, and the slow, emerald weight of the brewing era.
