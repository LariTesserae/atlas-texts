In a world where the distinction between metal and marrow has dissolved, the inhabitants do not crawl or run; they *persist*. They are the **Liturgeons**, the slow-keepers of the Piston Stroke, and they are as much a product of the scorched cinnamon air as the rust-silt they tread upon.

### The Liturgeons: Anatomy of the Slow
To look upon a Liturgeon is to mistake a man for a heap of discarded industrial scrap. They are tall, spindly, and draped in "skin" that resembles cured, oil-soaked leather—a hide that has thickened over generations to withstand the unblinking 110-degree heat. Their limbs are long and multi-jointed, moving with the same agonizing, tectonic deliberation as the overhead machinery.

They do not have eyes in the traditional sense. In the bruised gold dimness, sight is a liability. Instead, their faces are dominated by fan-like auricular crests, much like the crystalline fans of the thermal-vines. These organs allow them to "see" through vibration, mapping the vaults by the rhythmic *thrum-thrum* of the brass pistons. They navigate the silhouettes not by light, but by the pitch of the steam’s hiss.

### The Physiology of Iron
The Liturgeons have entered into the same symbiotic pact as the gear-moss. Their blood is a viscous, tea-colored brine, rich in the iron and copper they leach from the world around them. When a Liturgeon rests—which they do for decades at a time, leaning against a bronze-domed boiler—the purple thermal-vines begin to lace through their skin, threading into their veins. 

This is not a parasitic relationship; it is a communal one. The vines provide the Liturgeon with the concentrated vibration-energy they've harvested from the pipes, and in exchange, the Liturgeon’s slow, rhythmic movement helps distribute the plant’s spores into the heavy, wool-like air. 

### The Cult of the Piston
The "culture" of this place is a liturgy of maintenance. The Liturgeons believe they are the lubrication of a dying god. Armed with jars of thick, bioluminescent grease harvested from the amber fungi, they spend centuries migrating toward the "Friction Points"—the places where the massive pistons disappear into the ceiling.

They move in a silent, collective trance. A group may take three years to ascend a single rusted gantry. Once they reach a grinding joint, they apply the grease with their long, spatulate fingers, their movements synchronized perfectly with the Piston Stroke. They do not speak. Language is a fast thing, a thing of the surface. Here, they communicate through "The Resonance": a low, sub-vocal hum that harmonizes with the groan of the earth. To hear a Liturgeon hum is to feel your own teeth vibrate in their sockets.

### The Stationary Young
Life begins not in a nest, but in the shallow pools of tea-colored brine. The "Calves" of the Liturgeons are born looking more like moss-covered stones than living creatures. For the first fifty years of their lives, they are stationary. They root themselves to the calcified remains of the old industry, absorbing the mineral heat. 

During this time, they are indistinguishable from the bulbous fungi. They grow a hard, outer shell of pulverized rust and calcium. Only when they have reached a certain "thermal mass" do they crack open their sedimentary cocoons, unfolding their long, leathery limbs to begin their first, incredibly slow walk toward the sound of the pistons.

### The Quiet Transmutation
Eventually, every Liturgeon stops. The heat, which was once their cradle, becomes their final mold. In the deep, stagnant corners of the vaults, you can find "The Elders"—individuals who have ceased moving entirely. They have become so rich in minerals that their skin has fused with the stone walls. 

Their auricular fans continue to vibrate, catching the echoes of the brass-on-iron rhythm, but their bodies are now indistinguishable from the scalloped stone and the rusted gantries. They are the "Geological Ancestors," living statues that serve as the foundation for new layers of gear-moss. They are not dead; they have simply achieved the ultimate goal of their kind: to become the terrain itself.

In this vault, there is no ghost in the machine. The machine *is* the ghost, and the Liturgeons are its slow, copper-blooded memories.
