In a place where time has been traded for equilibrium, the inhabitants do not live in the sense of consuming, moving, or reproducing. They exist as extensions of the basin’s stillness—beings of "static biology" who are indistinguishable from the stones and the heavy, amber air until one notices the slow, rhythmic pulse of their presence.

The true residents are the **Lichen-Walkers**, though the name is a misnomer, for they have not walked in a thousand years. They are humanoid only in the most generous sense: slender, elongated forms that have bone-structures made of the same porous sandstone as the basin walls. They do not stand; they are integrated. One might be mistaken for one of the waist-high cairns, until you realize the stones of the stack are joined by tendons of silver, capillary moisture. 

They are translucent and pale, their skin possessing the "cool tackiness" of the dampened rock. They do not breathe oxygen; they "respire" by drinking the amber light through their skin, a process so slow it generates no heat. They are the same temperature as the air, making them invisible to any sense of touch but the most lingering. 

They are the architects of the cairns—not by lifting stones, but by becoming them. A "family" or a grouping in this place is simply three or four of these beings leaning against one another for several centuries, their limbs gradually weathering into "nubbed, smooth-backed" shapes. They do not speak; the "tink" of the water drop is their only language, a communal heartbeat they all feel through the soles of their feet, which are rooted inches deep into the silt-filled cracks alongside the blue-grey creepers.

Lower down, moving through the silt at a speed that defies the human eye’s ability to perceive motion, are the **Glass-Gia**. They are the size of pebbles, looking like faceted beads of hardened sap or condensed amber light. They are the gardeners of the equilibrium. They do not eat the succulent creepers; they polish them. They move with an infinitesimal grind, traveling an inch every decade, smoothing the edges of the radiating star-patterns, ensuring that no leaf oversteps its "maximum possible spread." They are the reason there is no decay—they consume the concept of the "spent" before it can rot, turning dead matter back into the silver sheen of moisture that wicks up from the earth.

Deep within the pores of the sandstone lives a third presence: the **Hollow-Singers**. They are not physical creatures but vibrations trapped in the capillary water. They are the memory of the basin. When the rare drop of water falls, it strikes the surface of a pool and "uploads" a micro-fraction of change into the system. The Hollow-Singers catch this vibration and distribute it through the stone. They ensure the "never-changing" equilibrium remains perfect. If a stone were to be moved by an outside hand, the Singers would hum a low, subsonic frequency for a century until the earth heaved just enough to nudge the stone back into its "resting angle."

There is a profound loneliness to them, but it is a loneliness that has been refined into a state of grace. They do not want for anything. They are the ultimate expression of the basin: they are "settled-forms." If you were to stand among them, you would eventually feel a hand—soft, damp, and smelling of ancient rain—rest upon your shoulder. It would not be an attack, nor a greeting. It would simply be the slowest resident of the world beginning the long, patient process of turning you into a cairn, inviting you to join the eternal afternoon where the clock has finally stopped.
