To live within the Ventral Chamber is to be a component in a closed-loop system where the line between "creature" and "circuitry" has long since dissolved. The inhabitants do not possess territories; they possess functions.

### The Chitinous Technicians
The most visible inhabitants are the **Valve-Tenders**, creatures that at first glance resemble oversized dragonflies but move with the jerky, hydraulic rhythm of robotic arms. Their bodies are long, segmented cylinders of matte-brown chitin, devoid of the iridescent flair of their surface-cousins. They have no eyes—light is a constant, useless variable here—relying instead on sensitive, vibrating bristles that tune into the harmonics of the sap-flow. 

When a pressure-valve within a trunk-wall begins to shriek at an off-key pitch, the Tenders descend in swarms. They use serrated mandibles not to eat, but to mill. They grind away calcified minerals and parasitic fungi that threaten the seal of the woody pistons. They are fueled by direct "tap-feeding"; they plug needle-like proboscises into the high-pressure veins of the engine, gorging on the sugary slurry that powers their ceaseless, clockwork labor. They do not sleep; they merely enter a low-power "throttle" state, clinging to the underside of the canopy-vault like gargantuan, dormant gargoyles.

### The Lubricants of the Deep
Lower down, on the slick, peat floor, move the **Slickers**. These are gastropods the size of carriage-horses, their shells fused into the very architecture of the mossy floor. They represent the "waste management" of the engine. As the gargantuan bulbs pulse and grind, they shed massive amounts of fibrous friction-dust—the botanical equivalent of sawdust. 

The Slickers follow the vibration of the grinding pistons, secreting a thick, translucent mucus that reduces the heat of the wood-on-wood friction. They are the mobile grease of the machine. Their trail is not a haphazard path of slime, but a precisely calculated route that keeps the "joints" of the world from igniting through sheer kinetic heat. They process the "sweet rot" of the chamber, consuming the spent cellulose and excreting a concentrated nutrient-gel that the moss-mat immediately absorbs.

### The Spore-Keepers: Ghosts in the Void
In the vast, cathedral-like silences between the trunk-pillars, there exists a more ephemeral class of life: the **Looming Motes**. These are translucent, jellyfish-like drifters that lack any solid structure. They stay aloft by riding the updrafts of warm ozone and transpiration-gas. 

They are the "sensors" of the factory. Their long, trailing filaments—some hundreds of feet long—act as atmospheric barometers. They sense the slightest shift in the 55-degree equilibrium. If a "leak" in the canopy-vault occurs, letting in outside air, the Motes drift toward it in a slow-motion armada. Their bodies are packed with billions of hyper-reactive spores. Upon contact with a structural breach, they "detonate" in a cloud of rapid-growth foam, a biological sealant that hardens into wood-matter in seconds, sacrificing themselves to maintain the internal pressure of the chamber.

### The Parasitic Auditors
Tucked away in the crevices of the "locomotive" bulbs are the only creatures that could be considered truly sentient, though it is a cold, mechanical intelligence. These are the **Root-Whirls**. They are small, crab-like organisms with shells made of hardened resin. They do not work; they observe. 

They are the auditors of the engine’s rhythm. They possess silver-thin antennae that they press against the trunks to "read" the vibrations of the entire world. They communicate through percussive drumming, sending signals through the wood to coordinate the swarms of Valve-Tenders. If a section of the Moss-Engine is failing, the Whirls command its decommissioning, directing the Slickers to cease lubrication so that the friction may consume the faulty part, making way for the growth of a new, more efficient replacement.

### The Human Element (The "Strays")
There are sightings, perhaps apocryphal, of things that were once human. These "Strays" have been absorbed into the factory's logic. Their skin has taken on the hue of wet lichen; their hair has become a fine, mossy silk. They do not speak, for the roar of the sap-lines has long since deafened them. 

They wander the spongy floor with a glazed, rhythmic gait, their movements dictated by the thrumming in the marrow of their bones. They have no tools, yet they perform strange, ritualistic scrapings on the trunk-walls, clearing pathways for the Slickers or guiding the fall of weeping moisture into specific moss-cups. They are not masters of the engine, nor even its slaves; they are simply a late-addition biological "patch"—an unforeseen evolution of a species that stayed too long in a place that knows only how to continue. 

In the Ventral Chamber, you are either a part of the rhythm, or you are the friction that the rhythm must eventually erase.
