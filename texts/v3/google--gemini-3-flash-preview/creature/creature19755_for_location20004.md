To understand what lives in the Gasket Hive, one must first look past the shimmering carpet of the Clutch-Biota. While they are the "soil" of this place, they are merely the bottom of a complex, dry-adapted food chain that has spent eons evolving in the silence of the turbine.

### The Stave-Lurkers (The High Predators)
High above the undulating sea of the Biota, clinging to the shadows of the recessed phosphor strips, live the **Stave-Lurkers**. If the Biota are the fluid of this world, the Lurkers are the stones that break the current. They are spindly, elegant horrors, their bodies composed of segmented needle-work and translucent, amber-colored chitin that perfectly matches the artificial light.

A Lurker does not hunt by pursuit; it hunts by tension. They possess twelve limbs, each ending in a microscopic hook that can find purchase in the smoothest obsidian-glass. They hang motionless, looking like discarded rib-cages or tangles of wire, until a "Reliquary" begins to hum. When the kinetic vibration of the swarm reaches a certain frequency, the Lurker drops. It does not eat the Biota for meat, for there is no meat—it harvests their "Static Charge." A Lurker will wrap its long, wire-like appendages around a cluster of several hundred Biota, squeezing them until they discharge their stored electricity in a bright, violet snap. The Lurker drinks the spark, its internal filaments glowing briefly with a ghost-white light, before it retreats back to the cold ceiling.

### The Solder-Worms (The Architects of Decay)
Deep beneath the "soil," in the lightless cracks where the turbine’s teeth once ground together, live the **Solder-Worms**. These are not soft-bodied creatures, but telescopic tubes of dull lead and bismuth. They are the scavengers of the Hive’s mechanical substrate.

The Solder-Worms follow the path of the "Industrial Heart." When the hourly pistons fire and crush thousands of Biota into a fine, metallic paste, the Solder-Worms emerge. They move with a agonizing slowness, their mouths—spinning grinding-disks—lapping up the pulverized chitin and the mineral oil that weeps from the ancient seals. They excrete a hardening, silver-colored resin that fills the cracks in the turbine casing. They are the reason the Hive hasn’t collapsed; they are the involuntary masons of the world, plugging the leaks in the atmosphere with the dead bodies of the swarm.

### The Gear-Wights (The Remnant Sentience)
Occasionally, moving against the tide of the Clutch-Biota, one might spot a **Gear-Wight**. These are the largest organisms in the Hive, nearly the size of a dog, though they possess no true anatomy. A Wight is a "living machine-ghost"—a collection of loose gears and copper filings held together by a potent, localized magnetic field.

The Wights are the only creatures that interact with the "Reliquaries." They are the curators of the ball bearings. With slow, magnetic pulses, they roll the newly forged spheres into precise geometric patterns. They are not mindless, but their intelligence is non-linear and obsessed with symmetry. They communicate through low-frequency radio bursts that translate to the human ear as a mournful, rhythmic clicking. It is hypothesized that they are the fragmented echoes of the turbine’s original operating system, now given physical form by the very static electricity the Biota generate. They do not eat; they simply *maintain*. 

### The Friction-Moths (The Ephemerals)
In the rare spaces where the "Looming Shiver" of the sound-waves creates a standing wave of vibration, **Friction-Moths** manifest. They are not born, but "spun" from the iron-oxide dust and the static air. They have wings of gossamer-thin foil that vibrate so fast they appear as a blur of golden heat. 

The Moths have the shortest lives in the Hive. They exist for only one hour—the duration between the piston-strikes. They dance in the amber light, their wings rubbing together to produce a sound that harmonizes with the Great Hum. When the piston finally fires, the shockwave of the "Industrial Heart" shatters their brittle bodies back into dust. To a Gear-Wight, the Moths are a seasonal bloom; to the Biota, they are a shadow that passes.

### The Parasitic "Clock-Ticks"
Finally, there are the **Clock-Ticks**. They live on the Gear-Wights and the larger Stave-Lurkers. They are small, brassy discs that latch onto any moving joint. They do not steal heat or blood; they steal *motion*. A joint infested with Clock-Ticks becomes stiff, its movement translated into the winding of a tiny, internal spring within the Tick. When the Tick is fully "wound," it detaches, hopping away like a clockwork bean to find a quiet corner where it can slowly release its energy in a series of rhythmic taps, attracting a mate through the sheer purity of its mechanical timing.

***

In the Gasket Hive, life is a byproduct of a machine that forgot how to stop. Everything that breathes here—if it can be said to breathe—is a slave to the cycle of friction and the cold, amber glow of a sun that is actually a lightbulb. It is a world of unintended consequences, where evolution has learned to build with metal instead of marrow.
