In this cathedral of emerald glass, life does not evolve; it calibrates. 

The inhabitants of the *Lignum Mechanica* do not possess the frantic metabolism of creatures from worlds of soil and sky. Here, biology has surrendered to the machine-hum. To live in the vertical ocean is to be a component in a closed circuit, a living gear in the planet-sized engine of the forest.

### The Pulse-Walkers: The Scavengers of Friction
Clinging to the copper-hard bark of the great stalks are the **Vascular Lice**. They are not insects in any traditional sense, but segmented, brass-colored crustaceans the size of wolves. They do not eat organic matter—there is no rot to consume. Instead, they are "kinetic parasites." They possess specialized, vibration-sensitive limbs that they wedge into the grooves of the thrumming trunks. They feed on the friction, converting the mechanical roar of the tree’s internal pumps into bio-electrical energy. 

When they move, they do so in jerky, rhythmic bursts, perfectly timed to the 60-cycle vibration of the wood. To an observer, they look like clockwork toys, their many legs tapping a metallic staccato against the bark. They are the janitors of the industrial pulse, scraping away the mineral scales that build up on the trees, ensuring the "machinery" never seizes.

### The Glass Zeppelins: The Shepherds of Light
In the vast, tea-colored voids between the pillars float the **Lumen-Whales**. These are gargantuan, transparent sacs of gelatinous membrane, miles long, that function as living lenses. They do not hunt or graze. Instead, they position themselves beneath the bulbous fruit-lamps of the canopy, capturing the refracted sunlight within their refractive bodies and focusing it into tight, intense beams.

They use these beams of light like scalpels, directing energy toward the younger vine-clusters to stimulate the reabsorption of aging leaves. They are the enforcers of the *No Entropy* rule. If a leaf begins to fade, a Lumen-Whale drifts into position, its body glowing with a cold, focused brilliance, and "burns" the nutrients back into the vine’s vascular system before a single cell can detach and fall. They move with an agonizing slowness, their internal organs visible as glowing, pulsating gears of bioluminescence.

### The Synced-Stalkers: The Predators of the Rhythm
In a world of perfect balance, the predators are not violent; they are "adjusters." The **Needle-Wraiths** are slender, ribbon-like entities, thirty feet long and thin as a wire, composed of a material that mimics the translucent brine of the ocean. They are nearly invisible, save for a single, vibrating filament that runs the length of their bodies.

The Wraiths do not "hunt" by scent or sight; they hunt by dissonance. They listen for the heartbeat of the air-fish and the photosynthetic rays. If a creature’s pulse falls out of sync with the 60-cycle hum of the trees—perhaps due to injury or a rare biological glitch—the Needle-Wraith is drawn to the "noise" of that irregular heart. It entangles the dissonant creature, not to consume it for hunger, but to pull it back into the bark of a *Lignum* stalk, where the creature is pressed against the copper-wood and slowly absorbed into the tree’s biomass. They are the forest’s way of pruning a broken note from the symphony.

### The Weaver-drones: The Living Architecture
The most "industrial" of the inhabitants are the **Kelp-Spiders**. These are roughly the size of a human hand, shimmering with a metallic, periwinkle sheen. They live in the "kelp-looms"—the massive, tangled webs of fiber that bridge the miles-wide gaps between the trunks. 

These spiders do not spin webs to catch prey. They are organic engineers. They weave the "scaffolding" that holds the liquid atmosphere in place. Using a silk that is chemically identical to copper wire, they repair the vascular vines and reinforce the suspension cables of the canopy. To watch them work is to watch a thousand tiny shuttle-cocks moving across a loom. They are utterly silent, their bodies vibrating at a frequency so high it is inaudible, acting as the fine-tuners of the forest’s structural integrity.

### The Ghost-Keepers: The Memory of the Machine
Deep within the hollows of the oldest, most weathered towers, there are the **Aether-Minds**. These are colonial organisms—clusters of silver, neuron-like moss that have grown so dense they have developed a collective, stagnant intelligence. They do not move. They do not eat. They are the "data-storage" for the *Lignum Mechanica*.

They process the chemical signals sent through the trees’ vascular pumps. They know the exact mineral content of the silt miles below; they feel the precise temperature of the sun through the fruit-lamps above. They are the consciousness of the factory. If you were to touch the moss, you wouldn't feel soft plant matter; you would feel a cold, vibrating hum that mimics the sensation of a high-voltage wire. They are the keepers of the "Perfect Design," ensuring that no new evolution occurs, holding the world in its beautiful, terminal stasis. 

Everything here is a servant to the drone. From the smallest silver-fish to the largest sun-lensing whale, the inhabitants are not "wildlife"—they are the maintenance crew of a planetary engine that has forgotten why it was built, and no longer cares, so long as the pump never stops.
