In this place, identity has been subsumed by the mass. To live on the Plateau of the Long Digestion is not to be an individual, but to be a single cell within a vast, sedentary body of history.

### The Pale Carpet (The Thrum-Eaters)
The thumb-sized, wingless insects are the primary tenants of the red stone. They are known as **Thrum-Eaters**, named for the way they harvest the resonant frequencies vibrating through the bedrock. Their carapaces are not hard like a beetle’s, but have the texture of damp parchment—translucent and soft, allowing the internal machinery of their bodies to be seen: a slow, rhythmic pulse of copper-colored fluid.

They do not possess eyes, for there is nothing they need to find that isn't already beneath their feet. Instead, they have sensory bristles that detect the arrival of the cloud-shelf. When the silver dew falls, the billions of Thrum-Eaters do not scramble; they simply tilt their bodies in unison, a shimmering wave of movement that looks like the surface of a lake. They drink through their skin, absorbing the yeast-scented moisture until they swell, becoming heavy and glassy. They are the keepers of the plateau’s stillness, and their only purpose is to process the mineral wealth of the dew into the red silt that eventually forms the Ancestral Chimneys.

### The Floating Archivists (The Gas-Bellows)
Above the carpet of insects drift the **Gas-Bellows**. These are the bladder-like organisms that populate the heavy air. They resemble translucent, veined lungs, trailing long, gossamer filaments that hang down like the roots of a willow. These filaments never touch the ground; they graze the tops of the Thrum-Eaters, picking up chemical signals and pheromones, acting as a slow-motion nervous system for the entire plateau.

The Gas-Bellows do not eat in a traditional sense. They are atmospheric filter-feeders, straining the "old heat" of the plateau for microscopic fungal spores. They are the thinkers of this world, though their thoughts take centuries to form. When two Gas-Bellows collide—a process that can take hours of gentle drifting—they fuse their membranes briefly, exchanging memories of the wind and the light before pulling apart with a sound like a wet sigh.

### The Residua (The Hollow-Dwellers)
While the Ancestral Chimneys are described as abandoned, they are not truly empty. Living within the inner flutes of the spiraling towers are the **Residua**. These are the descendants of the original builders, though they have devolved into something far simpler. They are pale, elongated husks that resemble dried lily pads. They cling to the interior walls of the towers with microscopic hooks.

The Residua have no limbs or mouths. They live entirely on the "voice" of the plateau—the resonant frequency that hums through the hollow pipes. They are biological resonators; as the wind passes through the Chimneys, the Residua vibrate, their soft tissues acting as reeds in a great, stone woodwind instrument. They are the source of the plateau’s low, rhythmic hum. They are less "creatures" and more "echoes" given flesh, existing only to turn the movement of the air into a physical sensation.

### The Slow Harvesters (The Marrow-Shrubs)
The stunted, bone-like flora—the **Marrow-Shrubs**—are the only things that possess a sense of "self" because they are the only things that must fight for space. Their roots are not fibers, but acid-secreting filaments that slowly, over millennia, etch themselves into the hematite stone. 

The shrubs grow in the shape of hands frozen in the act of grasping. They are incredibly dense; a branch the size of a finger would weigh as much as a lead bar. Every few decades, a Marrow-Shrub produces a single, heavy fruit that looks like a polished ruby. It does not fall; it simply sits in the center of the shrub’s "palm" until a Gas-Bellows drifts low enough for its filaments to snag the fruit. This is the only exchange of energy that happens between the ground and the sky—a slow, silent transaction of sugar and salt.

### The Spirit of the Place
There are no predators here because there is no "excess" to be hunted. To eat another creature on the Plateau would be like a hand eating a finger; everything is too interconnected for violence. 

The "dwellers" here do not experience fear, nor do they experience joy. They experience **Persistence**. If you were to stand among them, they would not flee. The Thrum-Eaters would simply vibrate against your boots, treating you as a new, slightly warmer hummock of stone. The Gas-Bellows would cast their shadows over you, and for a moment, you would be part of the long, slow digestion of time. You would feel the urge to stop moving, to let the silver dew coat your skin, and to wait for the wind to turn you into a spire.
