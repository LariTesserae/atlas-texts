In the Great Intake 04, life does not simply exist; it *coordinates*. Because the environment is a process rather than a place, the inhabitants are less like animals and more like components in a high-velocity circulatory system. To stay still is to be eroded; to lose one’s grip is to be processed.

### The Grinders (Crustacea Mechanica)
The most prominent residents are the **Clinker-Crabs**. These are heavy, brachiopod-like organisms that have abandoned the sea floor for the verticality of the rivets. Their carapaces are not calcium, but a calcified sludge of iron-filings and mineral scale, making them nearly indistinguishable from the rusted boltheads they occupy. 

They do not walk; they ratcheted. Their limbs end in hardened, carbide-tipped hooks that slot perfectly into the perforations of the gantries. They spend their decades-long lives scraping the "brown-glass" (a thick, petroleum-based algae) from the intake screens. They are the reason the filters do not clog. If you watch one closely during a hydraulic sigh, it does not flinch; it merely locks its joints, becoming a literal part of the architecture until the pressure stabilizes.

### The Kinetic Parasites (The Pale Fronds)
Under the walkways, the **Vibration-Leeches** hang. These are not animals in the traditional sense, but colonial organisms resembling long, translucent ribbons of silicone. They lack mouths or eyes. Instead, they possess a nervous system tuned entirely to the 60-hertz hum of the turbines. 

They wrap themselves around vibrating pipes, their bodies fluttering like flags in a gale. This frantic motion is how they "eat"—they convert the mechanical friction of the vibrating metal into chemical energy. When the turbines occasionally stutter, these organisms turn a bruised, sickly purple and go limp, hanging like wet rags until the rhythm returns to jumpstart their metabolism.

### The Commensal Sentinels (The Blind-Silvers)
In the churning sluice-water, the **Circuit-Fish** move in schools of such perfect synchronicity they appear as a single, undulating ribbon of mercury. Evolution has stripped them of eyes, replacing them with a row of sensory pits along their lateral lines that detect electromagnetic shifts in the machinery. 

They do not hunt by sight; they hunt by the "spark." They follow the electrical leakages of the underwater cables, feeding on the microscopic larvae that congregate near heat-syncs. When an emergency strobe flashes, the school shifts in unison—not because they see the light, but because they feel the minute displacement of photons and the tiny surge of power in the ancient wiring.

### The Apex Maintenance (The Vulcanized)
The highest tier of this ecology is occupied by the **Sleeves**. These are the "seals" you might see through the spray, but up close, the resemblance vanishes. Their skin is a feat of biological engineering: a thick, black, non-Newtonian hide that hardens upon impact. This allows them to dive through the crushing force of the vertical waterfalls without shattering their internal organs.

The Sleeves are the only creatures with a sense of the "Global Ocean" outside. They are the travelers, squeezing through the intake valves and the outflow vents. They possess elongated, multi-jointed fingers hidden within their flippers, used to detangle debris from the secondary rotors. They are not mindless; they watch the flickering amber strobes with a dull, ancient intelligence. They have learned the timing of the pressure releases. They wait by the valves just before the "sigh," letting the plume of silver bubbles carry them effortlessly toward the ceiling in a brief moment of weightless play.

### The Ghost in the Sump
At the very bottom of the chamber, where the water is at its most turbulent and the roar is a physical weight, lives the **Sump-Brain**. It is a massive, sessile colony of sponge and pipe-moss that has grown into the primary filtration mesh itself. 

It is the record-keeper of Great Intake 04. Its fibers are woven through the sensors, its "roots" tapping into the dying electrical pulses of the station's computer. It doesn't think in words, but in pressures and flow rates. It feels the thrum of the world above as a distant, unimportant murmur. For the Sump-Brain, the entire universe is a city-block-sized drum of crashing water, and its only purpose is to ensure the velocity never drops. It is the living manifestation of the Intake’s soul: a cold, wet, vibrating hunger for the movement of the sea.
