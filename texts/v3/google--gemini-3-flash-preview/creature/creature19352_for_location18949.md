In this vertical kingdom of obsidian needles, the inhabitants are not merely residents; they are the anchors of a fraying reality. 

The primary occupants, those spindly-limbed architects, are known or felt as the **Lithos-Weavers**. To look upon them is to see a creature that has more in common with a cathedral’s vaulted ceiling than with flesh. They are composed of articulated plates of translucent chalcedony and white marl, their limbs long and many-jointed, ending in multi-faceted "fingers" that do not grasp so much as they fuse. They have no faces, only a central, concave depression in the chest that hums with a faint, phosphor-blue light—the seat of their collective consciousness. 

They do not walk; they negotiate the cliffs through a process of molecular bonding. A Lithos-Weaver will press a limb against the black glass of a spire, and for a fraction of a second, the two substances become one. They move with a rhythmic, clicking staccato, their bodies unfolding and refolding like complex geometry. Because time and space are thin here, a Weaver may appear to be in two places at once—one limb tending a lower terrace while its torso leans over a balcony three hundred feet above, connected by a shimmering, stretched-out smear of its own physical history.

Below the Weavers in the hierarchy of this brittle ecology are the **Echo-Moths**. These are not insects, but fluttering shards of solidified ether, shaped like serrated leaves. They possess no weight. They graze upon the "glass-moss," their presence betrayed only by the sound they make: a delicate, melodic tinkling, like crystal flutes clashing in a breeze. They move in swarms that follow the shifting pockets of static electricity. When the geometry of a bridge begins to fold, the Echo-Moths often sense it first, their wings vibrating in a panicked, high-pitched frenzy that serves as a siren for the Weavers to begin their repairs.

Deep within the windowless cells of the hive-cities dwell the **Silent Cantor**. These are the elders, or perhaps the biological "batteries" of the species. They have grown so large and so dense that they have fused permanently into the architecture of the towers. They are the source of the "mineral bile" that builds the city. They exist in a state of perpetual, agonizing meditation, their thoughts focused entirely on the concept of *solidity*. It is their collective will that prevents the cities from sublimating into gas. If one should die, or lose focus, the room it inhabits begins to lose its perspective—the corners melt, the floor becomes a ceiling, and the walls begin to drift into the gray fog of the abyss.

In the interstitial spaces—the "streets" that are merely vertical gashes in the stone—one might find the **Scintilla**. These are the scavengers of the heights. They resemble tumbling clusters of needles, rolling and leaping across the obsidian fins. They lack even the basic biology of the Weavers, existing as semi-physical clusters of the aromatic ethers. They "eat" the shadows that have detached from their owners, absorbing the lingering presence of a Weaver who has moved on. They are the janitors of the void, cleaning up the temporal wake left by the city’s constant struggle to remain real.

Life here is a desperate, quiet labor. There is no play, no art, and no sleep. There is only the **Maintaining**. At any given moment, a third of the population is engaged in "pinning"—the act of standing at a precarious edge and thrusting quartz-silk tethers into the air where a physical feature *should* be, anchoring the future to the present. 

The Weavers communicate through the friction of their joints. A rasp of a leg against a thorax might convey the architectural schematics for a new buttress; a long, low vibration through the stone might be a warning that the gravity beneath the Western Spire is becoming "soft."

They are a people of the precipice, terrified of the horizontal. To them, a flat plane would be a death sentence, a lack of something to hold onto. They live in a state of profound, existential vertigo, loving the stone not for its beauty, but for its stubborn refusal to vanish. They are the guardians of the solid world, huddled together in their high, cold hives, stitching the universe back together one crystal thread at a time, while the stars above watch with cold, unblinking indifference as the very ground beneath the Weavers' feet tries to dream itself away.
