In the Cradle of the Last Sigh, life does not inhabit the architecture; life *is* the architecture, though it has long since forgotten the purpose of its own design. Those who dwell within this calcified metropolis are not residents so much as symptoms of the plateau’s terminal patience.

### The Weaver-Lungs
The most pervasive "dwellers" are the ones that never move from the spot where they were seeded. Deep within the arterial intersections of the bone-wisteria, there are the **Weaver-Lungs**. These are sessile, organ-like masses of pulsing, violet-veined lichen that occupy the "basements" of the plant-buildings. They are the civic engineers of the plateau. They do not possess brains, but they possess a chemical memory of the wind. 

As the wind roars through the organ-pipes of the wisteria, the Weaver-Lungs sense the vibrations. If a "street" begins to whistle in a tone that suggests structural thinning, the Lungs secrete a thick, fast-hardening resin through the vine-canals to reinforce the wall. They are the reason the city maintains its geometric perfection. They are mindless caretakers, obsessed with a maintenance schedule written in their DNA eons ago, repairing a city that has no one left to house.

### The Shiver-Moths
Up in the "attics" of the frost-ferns, where the cloud-milk is thickest, live the **Shiver-Moths**. These are not insects in the traditional sense, but fluttering scraps of biological lace, no heavier than a snowflake. They have no mouths; they live entirely on the mineral-rich condensation that beads on the urban flora. 

Their life cycle is a frantic, microscopic dance. They vibrate their wings at such high frequencies that they generate a tiny pocket of warmth—micro-climates just a few degrees above freezing. When thousands of them cluster together on a "ceiling" of a bone-wisteria room, they create a soft, golden bioluminescence, making the translucent building glow like a guttering candle. They are the city’s accidental lanterns, huddled together in a desperate, shivering communal heat.

### The Echo-Mimics
In the lower districts, among the black obsidian basins, move the **Echo-Mimics**. These are the most unsettling of the Cradle’s inhabitants. Slim, multi-jointed scavengers with skin like wet rubber, they possess no vocal cords of their own. Instead, their ribcages are hollow, lined with ultra-sensitive membranes. 

They do not hunt with sight; they hunt with the city’s music. An Echo-Mimic can "catch" a specific chord of the wind in its chest and play it back, perfectly polarized to cancel out the ambient roar. This creates a "pocket of silence" that travels with them. If you are walking through the deafening howl of the plateau and suddenly enter a patch of absolute, terrifying quiet, an Echo-Mimic is standing directly behind you, using its body to swallow the sound of its own footsteps. They don't want to eat you—there is little meat on the plateau—they want the salt from your sweat, harvested with long, rasping tongues while you are paralyzed by the sudden hush.

### The Glass-Stalk-Grazers and Their Shepherds
The **Glass-Stalk-Grazers** mentioned before are not as solitary as they appear. If one watches a Grazer long enough, one notices that its path is not random. It is being guided by the **Vapor-Shepherds**. 

The Shepherds are gaseous colonial organisms—sentient clouds of heavy, ionized vapor that drift through the streets. They are barely visible, appearing only as a slight distortion in the light, like heat haze over a desert. They "corral" the Grazers toward areas of the forest that need pruning. The Grazers eat the excess rime and the overgrowth of frost-ferns, preventing the plants from choking the heat-veins. 

There is a tragic symbiosis here: the Shepherds are the last vestiges of the plateau's consciousness, a collective ghost-mind that directs the biological machinery. They have no hands to touch the world, so they use the Grazers as their fingers, grooming the city-forest into its endless, beautiful, and useless perfection.

### The Pilgrims of the Sigh
Lastly, there are the "Statues." Scattered throughout the grids are humanoid shapes, perfectly preserved in coats of ice. At first, they appear to be carvings, but they are the **Last Pilgrims**. Every century or so, something—a scent in the wind, a change in the light—draws a soul from the world below up to the Cradle. 

They do not die of the cold immediately. The bone-wisteria, sensing a new heat source, slowly grows around them, wrapping them in a protective, calcified embrace. They become part of the city. Their nervous systems are integrated into the plant-grid. It is said that the deep, vibrating chord the city plays during a gale is actually the amplified, slowed-down humming of several dozen human hearts, kept in a state of perpetual, frozen meditation, helping the Cradle of the Last Sigh sing its final, never-ending note.
