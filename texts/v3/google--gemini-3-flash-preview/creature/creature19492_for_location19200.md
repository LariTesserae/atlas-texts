In this airless, roaring green-house, there is no pulse of blood—only the rhythmic, electrostatic twitch of the **Resonant Custodians**.

They are not entities born of flesh, nor are they truly automata. They are the unintended children of the friction. Because the Oven of Green Frost is a space of perfect, unchanging equilibrium, the "fauna" here has manifested as a bridge between the moss and the machinery.

### The Lattice-Strider
The most visible inhabitants are the **Lattice-Striders**. Imagine a spindly configuration of copper wire and stiff, indigo moss, shaped roughly like a harvestman spider but the size of a chandelier. They do not have organs; they are hollow armatures of hardened resin and dehydrated fiber. They do not walk so much as they are *vibrated* across the surfaces of the revolving gears. 

A Strider exists to serve as a mobile acoustic baffle. It clings to the fastest-spinning brass cogs, its many-jointed legs ending in porcelain tips that click rhythmically against the metal. As it moves, it trims the "overgrowth" of the dry-ferns with scissor-like mandibles made of sharpened flint, ensuring the moss never clogs the teeth of the gears. When a Strider dies, it does not rot; it simply stops moving, becomes brittle, and is eventually ground into the very aromatic dust that chokes the air, its mineral essence recycled into the walls.

### The Thrum-Moths
Flitting through the gaps in the vertical jungle are the **Thrum-Moths**. These are not insects, but sentient flakes of mica-flecked parchment. They have no mouths and no eyes. They perceive the world through the intensity of the roar. They are drawn to the hottest copper vents, where the dry heat is most intense. 

They congregate in shimmering clouds of pale silver, their wings beating in perfect anti-phase to the screech of the un-oiled pistons. This is their "feeding": they absorb the kinetic energy of the sound, converting the mechanical vibration into a faint, violet luminescence that flickers in their translucent veins. To a traveler, they would look like sparks leaping from a grindstone, perpetually hovering, never cooling, trapped in a loop of kinetic hunger.

### The Piston-Worms
Deep within the "soil-less" root systems of the blue-black moss, inhabiting the hollows of the porcelain insulators, live the **Piston-Worms**. They are segmented cylinders of solid, polished carbon, looking like lengths of animated hematite. They are the only inhabitants that possess a semblance of weight. 

The Piston-Worms move through the dense thicket by mimicking the motion of the machinery they inhabit. They expand and contract with a metallic *clink*, sliding through the fibrous velvet of the ferns like needles through silk. They are the pollinators of this dry dimension. They carry static charges from the humming insulators to the dormant spores of the emerald ferns, "shocking" the flora into its slow, geometric expansion. Without them, the green would go gray; they provide the spark that keeps the oven "on."

### The Auditor
At the very center of the cathedral-space, where the mechanical roar reaches a frequency that can shatter glass, resides the **Auditor**. 

The Auditor is not a single creature, but a concentrated colony of the dry-moss that has wrapped itself around a central, vertical axle. It has taken on a vaguely humanoid silhouette, a moss-draped effigy sitting atop a throne of grinding brass. It is the sensory hub of the Oven. Its "skin" is a tapestry of the finest silk-ferns, which act as a giant diaphragm. 

The Auditor does not think in words; it thinks in *tolerances*. It feels the friction of every gear in its own "limbs." If a copper pipe begins to vibrate out of tune, the Auditor shifts its weight, a slow-motion adjustment that takes decades, causing the moss to tighten or loosen its grip on the machinery until the harmony is restored. It is the gardener of the noise, a living thermostat made of velvet and bone-dry fiber, ensuring that the Oven of Green Frost never warms, never sleeps, and never falls silent. 

It is a world of busy, frantic stillness. Everything here is a specialist of the drought, a devotee of the friction, living in the ecstatic, thundering center of a machine that forgot what it was built to make.
