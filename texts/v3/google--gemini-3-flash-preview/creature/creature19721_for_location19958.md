To inhabit this vertical grove is to be an architect of stillness. The residents of the obsidian needles do not see or experience the world as a sequence of events, but as a slow, deepening pressure of light and resin.

### The Weaver-Kings: The Caste of the Corrugated Shell
The beetle-like builders—the primary substrate of this civilization—are more like living masonry than animals. Individually, they are the **Kith-Lustre**. Their lives are measured not in breaths, but in the thickness of the secretions they leave behind. They possess multifaceted eyes that do not perceive color as we do, but rather the thermal "weight" of the stone. To them, a shadow is a physical barrier, and the sun is a solid, nourishing pillar they lean against.

Because they move with such agonizing slowness, their social structure is one of geometric proximity. Two Kith-Lustre living side-by-side for fifty years will fuse their lower carapaces together, sharing nutrients and neural impulses until they are effectively a single organism with two perspectives. The bridges they build are not merely transit routes; they are genealogy. A bridge of amber-resin is a physical record of ten thousand Kith-Lustre lives, a solid history of their slow-motion passage from one needle to the next.

### The Solar Sentinels: The Aeryn-Gliders
Suspended above and between the needles are the **Aeryn**, the translucent gliders of parchment and bone. Though they appear to be predators or drifters, they are actually the world’s "clocks." Their wings are so sensitive to the solar pressure that they can detect the tilt of the planet. 

The Aeryn are the only beings with a sense of the "far away." While the Kith-Lustre focus on the millimeter of stone beneath them, the Aeryn look outward into the golden void. They communicate via the long, silk-like filaments that tether them to the peaks. By plucking these tethers with a vestigial limb, they send vibrations down into the stone—low-frequency signals that tell the Kith-Lustre when to congregate, when to begin a new bridge, or when the sun is reaching its seasonal zenith. They are the conductors of a silent, planetary orchestra.

### The Resin-Wrights: The Interior Occupants
Deep within the "urban" grid—the hollowed-out, geometry-perfect cells of the coral-like vegetation—live the **Sycophants of the Silt**. These are the most elusive residents. They resemble bundles of dried, silver wire, possessing no discernible faces or limbs. They are the "gardeners" of the internal spaces. 

As the Kith-Lustre build the external shell of the city, the Sycophants live inside the walls, polishing the interior of the resin-chambers. They consume the dry mineral dust that sloughs off the obsidian needles and extrude it as a glass-like lacquer. If you were to peer into one of the "rooms" of this high-altitude city, you would see a Sycophant perfectly still, its wire-like body braced against the walls, vibrationally sanding the surface until it is smooth enough to reflect the sun into the deepest, darkest corners of the grove.

### The Ghost-State of Motion
There is a final, theoretical inhabitant: the **Blurred**. Because the rule of this world is that speed equals invisibility, the residents have a mythology of things that "do not exist." On the rare occasion that a pebble falls or a shard of stone snaps, the residents do not see the movement. Instead, they experience it as a "hole" in reality—a sudden, inexplicable absence of a solid object. 

To these creatures, anything that moves faster than a crawling shadow is a ghost. If a human were to walk across a resin bridge, the Kith-Lustre would not see a person; they would feel a strange, momentary turbulence in the air, a "haunting" of their static world, as if a century of time had been compressed into a single, violent second.

### The Collective Consciousness
Life here is a single, interlocking "dry-mind." There is no competition for resources, as the "resources" (sunlight and mineral) are infinite and unchanging. Therefore, there is no ego. The city is the organism. The Kith-Lustre are the skin; the Sycophants are the digestive system, cleaning the pores; the Aeryn are the eyes, watching the sun. 

When a resident dies, they do not rot. They simply stop moving. Over the next hundred years, they are slowly coated in the resin of their neighbors, becoming a permanent, structural part of the architecture. In this hamlet of shards, you do not bury your ancestors; you walk upon them, and eventually, you become the floor for those who follow, a shimmering, obsidian-hard testament to a life lived in a single, eternal afternoon.
