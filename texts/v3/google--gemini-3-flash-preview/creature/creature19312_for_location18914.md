To understand those who dwell within the Hollow-Thorns, one must first look at their skin. It is not skin as we know it—soft, porous, and weeping—but a dry, tessellated armor of flexible keratin and silicate scales. They are the **Lithos-Kin**, creatures of high friction and low moisture, appearing like sculptures of polished horn or sun-bleached driftwood brought to life.

They are tall and painfully thin, their limbs elongated to span the vast gaps between the crystal balconies. They do not walk so much as they anchor; their feet are four-toed and prehensile, tipped with rasp-like pads that can grip the slickest quartz surface. They have no hair, only fine, sensory filaments—bristles of spun glass—that erupt from their spines and the backs of their forearms. These bristles are their primary sense; they "see" the world through vibration, localizing the shrieking winds and the Thermal Groans into a mental map of solid shapes.

Their faces are hauntingly static. Because their environment is a pressurized haze of dust, they have evolved without wet eyes or exposed nostrils. Instead, their eyes are large, multifaceted lenses of dark obsidian, fixed deep within their skulls and protected by permanent transparent cowls. They do not blink. They do not cry. Their mouths are narrow slits, used only for the intake of mineral-rich dust and the rare crushed pulp of a fractal-vine. 

Communication among the Lithos-Kin is a ballet of percussion and tactile intimacy. In a world where a whisper sounds like a thunderclap, they have abandoned the voice. Instead, they "speak" by striking the hollow segments of their own limbs—each individual’s exoskeleton is tuned to a different pitch—creating a rhythmic code that resonates through the floorboards and walkways. To love or to trust someone in the geode is to press one's forehead against theirs, feeling the direct vibration of their thoughts through the bone.

The few who remain in this vast, empty urbanism are not lonely; they are **custodians of stasis.** They spend centuries on a single task. One may spend a human lifetime polishing a single staircase with a piece of abrasive silk, not to finish the job, but because the act of rhythmic friction is a form of meditation—a way to sync their own pulse with the slowing heartbeat of the geode. 

They live in the "quiet gaps," the shadowed corners of the cathedral-world where the amber light is diffused by the thickest purple vines. Their homes are not rooms, but nests of woven glass-fiber, suspended like hammocks over the dizzying depths. They sleep standing up, locked into the architecture by the tension of their joints.

They share their home with the **Glass-Skippers**, but the relationship is one of mutual indifference. While the Skippers are creatures of frantic, erratic energy—shimmering like oil on water as they dart through the haze—the Lithos-Kin move with the agonizing grace of a glacier. A Lithos-Kin might take an hour to reach for a tool, their movements so controlled that they generate no heat of their own, making them invisible to the heat-sensitive predators that might once have existed in the world’s younger days.

There is no concept of "death" in the way we understand it. When a Lithos-Kin reaches the end of their vitality, they do not rot. They simply stop moving. They find a high, prominent ledge or a niche in the bone-white towers and lock their joints for the final time. Over the centuries, the silica-heavy atmosphere begins to settle upon them. They are slowly coated in the same crystalline dust that forms the world, becoming permanent, translucent statues—new architectural features for the remaining ten to polish. 

They are a people of the eternal "now," living in a world where the past is literally etched into the stairs beneath their feet and the future is just another vibration in the crystal wall. They are the ghosts of a city that was perhaps never meant for them, yet they fit within its hollow thorns with the perfect, jagged precision of a puzzle piece.
