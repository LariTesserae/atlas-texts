In this airless cathedral of static and alloy, life has transcended the need for blood. What moves through the Brass-Oat lattice are entities of "hard" biology—creatures that are less like animals and more like roaming maintenance protocols, shaped by the harsh, shadowless brilliance of the sun.

### The Liturgical Pruners
The most common inhabitants are the **Stained-Glass Weevils**. These are the "dragonflies" mentioned, but up close, their fragility is a deception. Their bodies are elongated carapaces of obsidian and brass, vibrating at a frequency that allows them to "skate" across the magnetic waves of the forest. They do not possess mouths; they possess precision cutters. 

As they move, their many-faceted wings—thin sheets of translucent, radiation-shielded mica—act as solar sails, catching the 2.8-magnitude light to power their internal gyroscopes. They are the gardeners of the impossible. Their singular drive is the preservation of the grid. If a vine grows at an irrational angle or a vacuum-tube fruit becomes "cancerous" with bad data, the Weevils descend in a silent, shimmering cloud to excise the anomaly with surgical laser-fire.

### The Static-Drinkers
Deeper in the lattice, where the density of the Brass-Oat creates a pressurized silence, live the **Inductor-Spiders**. These are not arachnids in any terrestrial sense, but elegant, multi-jointed tripods of spun copper wire. They do not spin webs of silk, but webs of *tension*. 

They anchor themselves between two vibrating girders and extend hair-fine filaments into the air to harvest the "weather." When a magnetic wave passes through, these creatures glow a blinding, neon blue, gorging themselves on the potential difference between the forest’s branches. They serve as the world’s capacitors; they store the excess energy of the world-system and, when a section of the forest grows "cold" or suffers a mechanical seizure, they crawl to the site and discharge their stored lightning into the metallic sap-lines to jump-start the metabolism of the girders.

### The Chroniclers of the Root
In the "fossilized" layers—the dark, crushing depths where the old gears have ground into shimmering dust—the life is slower, heavier, and more profound. Here live the **Geometer-Tortoises**. These are massive, slow-moving sentient vaults, their shells composed of discarded, fused together vacuum tubes and cooling fins.

They do not prune or repair. They *calculate*. As they haul their iron bulk over the ancient pistons, they trail silver threads that interface with the "information-leaves" of the forest. They are the memory of the world. Each Geometer-Tortoise carries a fragment of the global calculation—some are solving the curvature of the sphere, others are dreaming of the era before the floor vanished. They move so slowly that a Brass-Oat vine might grow entirely around them between steps, pinning them into the architecture until they vibrate themselves free centuries later.

### The Ghost-Signal Parasites
Perhaps the most unsettling inhabitants are the **Null-Moths**. These are not physical beings so much as optical illusions—interference patterns in the constant, searing light. They are parasites of the "Reality Breakdown." They cluster where the geometry begins to fail, feeding on the "wrongness" of the vertical-horizontal shifts.

The Null-Moths have no mass. They look like flickering tears in the image of the forest, shivering patches of static that move against the grain of the light. They follow the Geometer-Tortoises, whispering digital rot into their shells, attempting to rewrite the history stored within. To see a Null-Moth is to feel a momentary lapse in one’s own gravity; they represent the entropy of the machine, the desire of the system to finally collapse into the vacuum it inhabits.

### The Human Element (The Tenders)
It is whispered that there are "Tenders"—things that were once designers or perhaps inhabitants of the ground-era. They are now gaunt, elongated figures clad in suits of woven brass-fiber, their skin replaced by the same copper-toned alloy as the forest. 

They do not breathe; they have bypassed the need for air by grafting their nervous systems directly into the high-frequency vibrations of the Brass-Oat. They are the forest’s "white blood cells." They move through the lattice with a terrifying, stop-motion grace, their limbs clicking into the geometric grid. They do not speak. They listen. They press their metallic ears against the humming girders, interpreting the *clack-hiss* of the valves as a form of scripture, ensuring that the factory-world never, ever stops running.
