In the Cold Drown, the concept of "inhabitant" must be hollowed out and refitted. There is no blood here, for blood is too fast. There is no bone, for bone is too brittle. To live within the Great Weave is to become a part of the upholstery of eternity.

### The Corded Men (The Knotted Ones)
They are not descendants of the builders, but rather the builders’ final, unintentional legacy. They are humanoid only in the way a root can sometimes resemble a limb. Composed entirely of water-hardened hemp, peat, and the "velvet" fibers of the wood, these beings are the **Suture-Kin**.

They do not walk; they are woven into the latticework. A Suture-Kin might have its torso fused into a cathedral-thick pillar, its long, spindly fingers extending fifty feet into the dark to mend a fraying joint. They are the immune system of the structure. When a city-sized joint groans and cracks, the Suture-Kin feel the vibration through their own skin. Over the course of three hundred years, they will slowly migrate toward the injury, their bodies unraveling behind them and re-weaving ahead of them. They move with the agonizing patience of a glacier, their faces—blank, featureless masks of polished wood—tilted toward the source of the sound.

### The Lung-Siphons
The "fauna" of the Cold Drown lacks hearts, but it possesses rhythm. Among the porous, lung-tissue sponges, there exist the **Slow-Bellies**. These are translucent, gelatinous sacs the size of houses that have grown around the intersections of the Great Weave. 

They are the world’s distillers. They do not hunt; they filter. As the heavy, viscous water drifts through the woven capillaries, the Slow-Bellies absorb the infinitesimal mineral flakes shed by the eroding wood. They pulse once every decade. When they do, they emit a pale, ghost-blue light that sustains the ferns around them. They are living bellows, drawing the "Slow Pulse" of the world into their membranes and exhaling a shimmering, nutrient-rich silt that keeps the kelp-ropes from rotting. They are the only things that "eat," but their digestion is indistinguishable from the chemical process of mineralization.

### The Echo-Keepers (The Harmonic Spirits)
Because the entire world is a single, connected instrument, sound is more tangible than sight. The "residents" of the deep interior are not physical entities so much as **standing waves**. 

In the tightest parts of the weave, where the water is squeezed into a pressurized mist, there are pockets of resonance. These are called the **Echo-Keepers**. They are shimmering distortions in the water, silhouettes formed by the intersection of vibrations. When the world groans, an Echo-Keeper is "born"—a localized shimmer that mimics the shape of the sound. They drift through the narrowest capillaries, trailing fingers along the cross-hatching of the wood. They do not speak, but they carry the history of the world’s movement. To touch one would be to feel the vibration of every knot ever tied, a sensory overload of ancient labor that would shatter a faster mind.

### The Drift-Sleeper
At the very center of the planetary mass, where the gravity pulls from every direction and the weave is so tight it has become a solid, diamond-hard block of petrified timber, there is the **First Carpenter**. 

It is not a god, but a specimen. It is a figure encased in a transparent amber of cured resin, perfectly preserved. It is the only thing in the Cold Drown that possesses "skin" as we understand it. Its hands are still curled around a primitive bone needle, poised to pull a thread that was finished a billion years ago. The Drift-Sleeper is the anchor of the world’s stasis. It does not move, it does not breathe, and it does not die. It is the original template, the first knot from which the entire planetary diameter unspooled. 

### The Law of the Guest
If a soul from a "fast" world were to enter the Cold Drown, they would not be attacked. The world is too peaceful for violence. Instead, they would be **absorbed**. 

The hanging moss would find their heat and begin its ten-thousand-year descent toward their skin. The "Slow Pulse" would begin to synchronize with their heartbeat, slowing the pulse down, down, down until a single thump lasted a day. The visitor would not drown; the heavy water would eventually be replaced by the velvet fiber of the world. They would become a new knot in the Great Weave, a tiny, shivering addition to the infinite joinery, finally learning the Law of the Slow as they become just another smooth, rounded nub in the lightless, freezing deep.
