To live in the Ctenophore Heights is to accept the pace of the glacier and the density of the deep-sea trench. The inhabitants of this bruised-violet world are not "highlanders" in the traditional sense; they do not have the flighty, lean quality of those who live in thin air. Instead, they are a people of **Internal Weight.**

### The Human Inhabitants: The Condensers
The people who walk these iron catwalks are known as the **Condensers**. They have been shaped by the "active stasis" of the plateaus. Physically, they are barrel-chested and heavy-limbed, their bodies evolved—orperhaps forged—to process the thin air and withstand the crushing weight of the low-hanging cloud lid. 

Their skin has a pale, translucent quality, much like the sky-rays, often mapped with a fine webbing of blue veins that pulse in time with the low thrum of the rock-turbines. They do not move quickly; haste is considered a spiritual failing, a "vibration" that disturbs the necessary settling of the city. 

**Their attire** is a marvel of industrial survival. They dress in heavy, charcoal-colored wools infused with lanolin and copper threads to repel the freezing glaze of The Seep. Their coats are weighted at the hems with leaden beads to keep them from fluttering in the cosmic winds, and they wear goggles of thick, amber-tinted glass to pull contrast from the perpetual indigo twilight. Because the world is so damp, they never truly dry; they smell of wet stone, tea, and the sharp, conductive tang of ozone.

### The Social Order: The Guilds of the Gutter
In a world where water is harvested from the breath of the mountain, social status is determined by one’s proximity to the "Flow."

*   **The Valve-Keepers:** The elite of the Ctenophore Heights. They live in the lower levels of the tenements where the cisterns are heaviest. They are the technicians of the *clack-hiss*, the ones who interpret the language of the steam. They are solemn, quiet individuals who carry brass tuning forks to listen to the vibrations of the rock, ensuring the mountains are sagging at a safe, predictable rate.
*   **The Moss-Herders:** The cultivators of the bioluminescent bryophytes. They are covered in a fine, glowing dust and have fingers stained indigo from "pruning" the house-sized mosses away from the turbine intakes. They are the most nomadic of the residents, clambering over the velvet blankets of the city with spiked boots, whispering to the plants that keep the air breathable.
*   **The Silt-Walkers:** The laborers who maintain the "anchorage." They are the ones who re-tighten the massive cast-iron bolts that keep the tenements from sliding down the smoothed basalt. They are characterized by their extreme patience; a Silt-Walker might spend a week tightening a single nut, moving only a fraction of a millimeter a day to match the mountain’s own infinitesimal crawl.

### The Translucent Fauna: The Ghost-Ecology
Below and above the humans, the "weirdness" of the Heights breathes in a slow, rhythmic cycle. 

*   **The Sky-Rays (The Ctenophores):** Though they resemble aquatic life, they are the true masters of the heights. They are nearly invisible, their bodies composed of a gelatinous substance that catches the dim violet light and refracts it into rainbow fringes. They are the "cleaners." They drift through the narrow alleys, their long, sticky cilia trailing behind them to scoop up the soot-particles and mineral dust. To have a Sky-Ray settle on your roof is considered a blessing of stability; it means the air is rich and the heat-waste is consistent.
*   **The Solder-Mites:** Tiny, metallic-shelled crustaceans that live in the joints of the suspension bridges. They don't eat organic matter; they "eat" the vibration of the turbines. They cluster where the friction is highest, their tiny bodies glowing a hot cherry-red as they dissipate the heat of the city’s mechanical tension. 
*   **The Marrow-Hounds:** The only mammals of the Heights, though "mammal" is a generous term. These are hairless, pale creatures with six legs and wide, flat paws designed for gripping wet basalt. They are blind, navigating entirely by the subsonic thrum of the mountains. They serve as the companions and beasts of burden for the Condensers, moving with a terrifying, liquid grace over the vertical city, carrying canisters of purified water or rolls of industrial wool.

### The Culture of the Slow Breath
Life in the Ctenophore Heights is governed by the **Deep Cycle**. There are no "days" or "weeks," only the "Filling" and the "Turning." 

The inhabitants do not speak loudly; the air is too thin to carry a shout, and the silence is too sacred to break. Their language is one of murmurs and tactile signs—a pressure on the forearm, a specific rhythm tapped on a copper pipe. 

They believe that they are the mountain’s "thoughts." As the basalt sags and the plateaus wear down toward their final, perfect flatness, the people believe their industry—the turning of the turbines, the weaving of the moss—is the process of the world dreaming itself into a state of total rest. They are not waiting for the end; they are savoring the longest, most beautiful fading-out in the history of the stars. 

When a Condenser dies, they are not buried. They are laid into the deepest beds of the Indigo Moss, where their bodies are slowly, over decades, dissolved into the carpet, their carbon used to fuel the bioluminescence that lights the way for the next generation of the slow-moving city. To live here is to eventually become the light you walked by.
