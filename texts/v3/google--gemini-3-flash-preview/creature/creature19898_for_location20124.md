The Glistening Sub-Ward is not merely inhabited; it is haunted by a lineage of survivors who have adapted to the crushing patience of the lime. The residents, known to the few who venture there as the **Stillefolk**, or the Calm-Breathers, are the slow-beating heart within this industrial lung.

### The Stillefolk
The people of the cistern have lost the frantic morphology of the upper world. To look upon a Stillefolk is to look upon a creature fashioned from the same materials as the tenements: their skin is the color of wet parchment, slightly translucent and mapped with a delicate lattice of blue and green veins that mimic the copper patina of the roofs.

They do not walk so much as they drift. Their movement is a masterpiece of kinetic conservation; they keep their centers of gravity low and their limbs fluid to avoid creating a splash or a sharp vibration. They are tall and unnaturally thin, their muscle fibers elongated by generations of hauling damp, heavy cables without the leverage of dry ground. 

Their eyes are their most striking feature—oversized, amber orbs with pupils dilated to the size of coins, designed to catch the faintest bioluminescent shimmer of a Pipe-Eel or the dying breath of a sodium lamp. They have no eyelids in the traditional sense, but rather a thin, nictitating membrane that keeps the constant mist from clouding their vision.

### The Liturgists of the Leak
The social hierarchy is determined by one's relationship to the water. The highest caste are the **Tuning-Smiths**. These individuals spend their lives waist-deep in the cooling pools, their ears pressed against the massive pylons of the turbines. They do not use tools of impact; they use glass files and jars of abrasive silt to shave fractions of a millimeter off the gears, ensuring the subterranean hum remains a harmonious "B-flat" that doesn't agitate the limestone ceiling. They are nearly deaf to the world of speech, but they can feel a hairline fracture in a pylon from a hundred yards away through the soles of their feet.

Below them are the **Gutter-Weavers**, often children or those with the smallest hands. They navigate the "rhythmic sequence of weeping vaults" above, clinging to the iron ribs with prehensile grace. Their job is the holy work of the Glistening Ward: they use strands of woven copper and silk to guide the ceiling’s "drip" into the brass gutters. They are the artists of the Ward, creating intricate, suspended labyrinths of water-trails that funnel the ceiling's tears into the thirsty maws of the turbines.

### The Symbiotes: The Moss-Tenders
There are those among the Stillefolk who have entered a secondary state of being. The **Moss-Tenders** are the elders who have lived so long in the damp that they have ceased to move. They sit on the velvet-umber platforms, their clothing long since rotted away and replaced by thick, plush blankets of the iron-eating moss. 

They are the living libraries of the Ward. Because the moss feeds on the mineral-rich runoff and the static of the machinery, it acts as a slow-motion recording medium. By pressing their palms into the moss, younger residents can "feel" the history of the cistern—the memory of a flood three centuries ago, or the rhythmic signature of a turbine that failed before the last Great Calcification.

### The Shadow-Eaters
In the "velvet voids" between the lamp-lit tenements dwell the **Pale-Skates**. These are not quite human, but rather the evolutionary descendants of those who wandered too far from the amber light. They are pale, blind, and move on all fours, their fingers elongated into hooks that allow them to cling to the undersides of the cast-iron platforms. 

They are the Ward’s clean-up crew. They feed on the carcasses of Pipe-Eels and the discarded husks of Clock-Flies. The Stillefolk do not fear them; they treat them as a natural extension of the machinery—a biological filtration system that keeps the dark water from becoming choked with rot.

### The Culture of the Hush
Communication in the Glistening Sub-Ward is a language of touch and vibration. Spoken words are rare, reserved only for moments of dire emergency, as the limestone vaults act as a perfect acoustic chamber; a shout could cause a "shatter-leak" in a weakened rib.

Instead, the inhabitants use a complex system of **"Thrumming."** They tap the copper cables with felt-tipped rings, sending low-frequency pulses through the web of the village. A short, sharp vibration means a lamp is flickering out; a long, low wave signals that the water table is rising by its annual millimeter.

Their spiritual life is centered on the **Great Sediment**. They believe that, eventually, the water will finish its work. They see the "healing" of the buildings—the calcite covering the mortar—as a blessing. They wait for the day when the last turbine makes its final, hour-long revolution and the entire Glistening Sub-Ward is finally "cured" into a solid, silent block of white marble, preserving their stillness for eternity.
