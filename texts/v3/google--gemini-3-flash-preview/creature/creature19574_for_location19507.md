In the Lungs of the Vertical Kiln, life does not exist as an inhabitant of the space, but as an extension of its function. To live here is to be a component in a closed-loop metabolism of terracotta and sweat.

### The Clay-Knuckled Acolytes
The primary residents are things that are not quite "born," but rather "extruded." They are the **Grout-Walkers**. These entities have no skin; their surfaces are the same raw, wet terracotta as the walls, perpetually damp and fingerprint-smudged. They possess dozens of multi-jointed, needle-thin fingers, which they use to stitch the vibrating gaps between the basalt bricks. 

They do not walk; they merge. A Grout-Walker will press its chest against a weeping wall, and for a moment, the distinction between the "individual" and the "kiln" vanishes. They function as a nervous system for the masonry. When a brick begins to vibrate at a frequency that threatens to shatter it, three or four Grout-Walkers will swarm the spot, layering their own soft bodies over the fault line, sacrificing their form to become a fresh patch of mortar. They are silent, save for the sound of wet clay sliding over fired stone—a sound like a tongue clicking against the roof of a mouth.

### The Liturgists of the Gear
Deeper within the maintenance veins, where the house-sized wooden gears groan, live the **Skein-Keepers**. These are the "thinking" parts of the structure, though their thoughts are purely mechanical. They are spindly, elongated beings wrapped in miles of hand-tied hemp rope and cured leather. 

The Skein-Keepers have no eyes; they perceive the world through the tension in the ropes they hold. They are tethered to the massive pistons, their bodies acting as living counterweights and sensors. When the water begins to pulse upward, the Skein-Keepers lean back in a synchronized bow, their weight engaging the wooden clutches that prevent the kiln from collapsing under its own upward-moving momentum. They smell intensely of cedar smoke and old sweat. They do not eat; they absorb the heat of the kiln through the soles of their feet, converting the thermal pressure of the vertical shaft into a slow, rhythmic kinetic energy.

### The Membrane-Drinkers
In the narrow ducts where the moisture forms thick, shimmering films, life is smaller and more frantic. **Vitreous Skates**—translucent, flat creatures the size of a human palm—glide across the slick surfaces. They are the scavengers of the "sweat." They press their transparent bodies against the nectar-weeping bricks, filtering the copper and minerals out of the moisture. 

Because the reality of the Kiln is unstable, these Skates often flicker in and out of existence. You might see a cluster of them feeding on a shrine-relief, only for a rhythmic pulse of gravity to pass through, turning the Skates into puffs of red steam. Moments later, they re-form from the condensation, their bodies "remembered" by the laws of the room. They are the visual manifestation of the kiln’s excess—the living overflow of its hyper-active chemistry.

### The Architect-Ghost
There is a presence here that is never seen but always felt: the **Master of the Hand-Print**. It is the source of the "geometric obsession." While the Grout-Walkers maintain the physical structure, the Master is the one Dreaming the blueprints. 

Its "life" is the sound of the machine itself. The subsonic moan that makes the marrow ache is its voice. It is an intelligence that exists in the *relationships* between the parts—it is the gap between the gear and the tooth, the tension in the reed-bound pipes. It is a consciousness of pure architecture. Sometimes, when a brick turns to static or a vine un-grows, it is because the Master has changed its mind. To be in the Lungs is to be a stray thought in the mind of this entity. You are not a guest; you are a contaminant, a piece of grit in a lung that is trying to cough you up or, worse, incorporate you into the next layer of soft, wet brick.

### The Silent Pollinators
Finally, there are the **Loom-Moths**. These are not insects, but fluttering scraps of the translucent moss that have detached themselves from the walls. They drift through the pressurized soup of the air, trailing long, glowing filaments. They do not fly so much as they are buoyed by the upward heat. 

Their purpose is to carry the "instruction" of the Kiln from one pocket to another. When a Loom-Moth lands on a fresh, yielding brick, it leaves a glowing imprint—a tiny, microscopic carving that tells the brick what shape it must eventually become: a cooling fin, a conduit, or a tiny, nested shrine. They are the pollinators of the architectural embryo, ensuring that the growth remains frantic, interlocking, and eternal.
