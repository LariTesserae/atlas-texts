In the Valve Gallery of the High Cistern, life does not "inhabit" the machine so much as it is a secretion of it. The inhabitants are the immune system of a god that has forgotten its own name.

### The Swell-Headed Curators
Wandering the slick, vertical catwalks are the **Gasket-Ticks**. These are heavy, slow-moving organisms of soft lead and translucent membrane. They possess no eyes, only sensitive, heat-seeking pads on their undersides. They resemble bloated, metallic leeches the size of a human torso. They do not move by walking, but by a rhythmic suction, clamping their soft bodies over the weeping seams of the pressurized pipes. 

They are the harvesters of the "sweat." When a Gasket-Tick finds a high-pressure leak, it doesn't plug it; it drinks it, filtering the mineral-heavy coolant through its internal mesh. As they feed, they hum—a low, resonant frequency that harmonizes with the bone-shaking drone of the Gallery. When they are full, their brassy hides glow with a dull, feverish red, and they slowly secrete a fast-hardening resin that seals the metal behind them. They are the living scar tissue of the High Cistern.

### The Static-Angels
High above, where the fiber-optic "willows" hang thickest, live the **Flicker-Wraiths**. These are not solid creatures, but coherent eddies of electrical discharge and sentient magnetism. They look like jagged, shifting silhouettes of blue sparks, roughly the size of a hawk. 

They do not eat matter; they feed on the data-pulses traveling through the ceiling-fibers. You can see them "nesting" in the bundles of fiber-optics, their presence causing the "willow" branches to thrash and spark. They are the apex predators of the information-stream. When a drone fails or a sensor-filament dies, a Flicker-Wraith descends in a crackling blur, short-circuiting the dying component to feast on the final, dying surge of its electricity. They are beautiful, terrifying, and entirely indifferent to anything that does not carry a current.

### The Scavenger-Scribes
Deep in the grey sludge of the "micro-marshes" that collect in the rusted valve depressions, there exists a microscopic, colonial intelligence: the **Silt-Mind**. To a casual observer, it looks like a shimmering, oily film on the surface of the puddles. In reality, it is a hive-mind of nanoscopic assemblers. 

The Silt-Mind is the historian of the machine. It breaks down the shed "scales" of the Gasket-Ticks and the husks of dead drones, rearranging the atoms into exquisite, useless geometries. In the corners of the Gallery, you might find tiny, perfect cathedrals of brass or microscopic clockwork gears that turn for no reason, built by the Silt-Mind out of the waste of the greater ecosystem. It is an intelligence that has nothing to do but rearrange the dust of its world into ever-more-complex patterns of stagnant beauty.

### The Calcified Hermits
Finally, there are the **Pressure-Saints**. These are the only things in the Gallery that might once have been biological, though the distinction has long since vanished. They are humanoid shapes fused directly into the junctions of the largest pipes. Their skin is the same tarnished brass as the walls; their veins are copper wires; their lungs are bellows that move in sync with the massive hydraulic pistons.

They do not move. They are part of the architecture now, their nervous systems wired directly into the valve-control arrays. They are the biological "interruptors" for the machine's flow. It is unclear if they are prisoners or priests. They stare with eyes made of polished quartz, watching the amber light flicker on the wet metal, their consciousnesses spread thin across a thousand miles of planetary piping. They do not speak, but occasionally, when the roar of the water fluctuates, you can hear a sound like a human sigh issuing from the steam-vents—the collective breath of those who have become one with the high-pressure heart of the world.

### The Cycle of the Womb
In this place, death is merely a change in state. A drone is snatched by a Reclamator; its metal is melted down and extruded as a new pipe-fitting; the pipe-fitting leaks; a Gasket-Tick drinks the leak; the Gasket-Tick dies and settles into the silt; the Silt-Mind weaves the remains into a lace of fiber-optics. 

It is a closed loop of hot metal and wet noise. There are no "invaders" here, only parts that have not yet been integrated. To stand in the Valve Gallery is to realize that you are the only thing in the room that is not part of the plan. You are the only thing that is not currently being digested, repaired, or recorded by the screaming, sweating life of the High Cistern.
