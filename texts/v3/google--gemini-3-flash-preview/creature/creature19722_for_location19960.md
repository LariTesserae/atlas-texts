In the Unfinished Cathedral, life does not evolve through biology, but through **Masonic Mimicry**. Since the environment is a fever dream of wet clay and calcifying stone, the inhabitants are not creatures of flesh, but entities of "set" and "slurry." 

### The Mortar-Men (The Latent Residue)
They are not the builders, but the shadows left behind by the act of building. The Mortar-Men are humanoid shapes of grey, viscous cement that pull themselves out of the trapezoidal pools of mineral water. They lack skin; their surfaces are the texture of wet grit, constantly weeping the same hot condensation that drips from the ceiling.

They move with a sluggish, agonizing deliberation, as if every step requires a monumental effort of will to prevent their limbs from merging back into the floor. They have no faces, only shallow indentations where eyes and mouths should be—depressions that look like they were made by a thumb pressed into soft putty. They follow the "budding" architecture. When a new wall extrudes from the floor, the Mortar-Men gather around it, pressing their palms against the soft brick, not to help it grow, but to feel the warmth of the kiln-fire. They are heat-drinkers, huddling against the newest masonry until they eventually grow too still, their edges harden, and they become permanent, grotesque gargoyles fused to the very structures they were watching.

### The Chisel-Worms (The Eradicator-Fauna)
If the Mortar-Men are the residue of construction, the Chisel-Worms are the agents of the Cathedral’s instability. Imagine segmented, centipede-like horrors the size of cathedral nave-aisles, their bodies composed of sharpened obsidian plates. They do not have legs; they move by vibrating their glass-sharp segments against the brickwork, creating a sound like a thousand knives sharpening at once.

They are the world's "immune system" against permanence. They hunt for structures that have become too "solid"—rooms that have accidentally achieved a sense of logic or finish. A Chisel-Worm will coil around a perfectly formed pillar and, through high-frequency tremors, reduce it back into a pile of red dust and rubble. They feed on the *concept* of the finished work, reclaiming the ordered stone for the chaotic slurry of the horizon. To a traveler, they are a rolling earthquake of black glass.

### The Calcified Echoes
Sometimes, the "Weeping Walls" drip so much mineral-heavy water that the stalactites reaching down from the bruised limestone ceiling meet the rising "staircases to nowhere." In these columns of fused mineral, things are trapped. These are the Echoes—glimpses of the giants who might have once wielded the ship-sized mallets.

They are not bodies, but *vacuums*. Within the translucent, amber-colored mineral pillars, one can see the hollowed-out shape of a massive hand, or a section of a titanic torso. These "hollow-gods" are the source of the occasional mountain-collapsing sound. When the Cathedral shifts, these pillars crack, and the trapped air—compressed by millennia of architectural pressure—escapes in a whistling, organ-like scream that can be heard for fifty miles.

### The Liturgy of the Plumb-Line
Deep within the humid bronze gloom, there are the **Surveyors**. These are perhaps the only truly "living" things, though they resemble floating clusters of geometric brass and twitching hempen rope. They look like a collection of plumb-bobs and levels that have gained a frantic, insectoid sentience.

The Surveyors do not speak; they hum in a perfect, terrifyingly pure C-major. They hover over the "Embryonic Architecture," frantically measuring the angles of the budding walls. When they find a wall that is crooked—which is every wall—they emit a frantic, ticking sound and begin to secrete a hot, caustic "eraser-fluid" from their brass casings, melting the offending masonry before it can set. They are the doomed perfectionists of a world that inherently rejects straight lines.

### The Rules of Coexistence
If you travel here, you are a "Foreign Inclusion." To the Mortar-Men, you are a curious statue of strange, soft material. They may try to "fix" you by coating you in wet cement to help you set. To the Chisel-Worms, you are a blemish of order that must be ground into dust.

But the greatest danger is **The Setting**. Because "Solidity is a Tempo," the inhabitants are those who have mastered the rhythm of the world's breathing. To live here is to realize that your own body is beginning to take on the properties of the Cathedral. Your sweat begins to smell of wet lime; your fingernails become as hard as obsidian; your heartbeat slows to match the *tack-tack-tack* of the cooling bricks. 

Those who stay too long in the Unfinished Cathedral do not die. They simply stop moving. And the next morning, the floor ripples, the bricks rise, and they are transformed into a beautifully carved, senseless frieze on a wall that will dissolve by noon.
