In this basin of black iron and salt, life has not merely survived; it has become a living extension of the plumbing. The inhabitants are the "soft-ware" to the world’s "hard-ware," their bodies designed to siphon, filter, and circulate the heat and moisture of the city-state.

### The Aerostatic Swarms
The pale, leathery insects that populate the orange haze are not the fleeting creatures of a summer meadow. They are **Gossamer Scavengers**, organisms that function more like airborne filtration units. Their wings are wide and translucent, veined with a system of cooling capillaries that glow a dull magenta when they soar too high into the heat. They move in murmurs that mimic the shape of the steam clouds, settling on the rusted copper pipes in shivering blankets. 

When they land, they don't rest—they drink. Their mouthparts are specialized needles, designed to pierce the thick layer of oxidation on the iron to reach the mineral-rich condensation trapped beneath. They vibrate their carapaces at a frequency that shakes the rust loose, causing a constant, fine red rain to fall from the towers. This is their contribution to the cycle: they are the polishers, the creatures that keep the skeletal industrial forms from being completely swallowed by their own decay.

### The Thrumming Mass
On the catwalks and the curved bellies of the vats live the **Litho-Mammals**. These are the sleek, hairless titans of the basin, creatures of dense muscle and oil-slicked skin. They do not have fur; instead, their hides are thick and calloused, resembling the very basalt they rest upon. They are social in a purely mechanical sense, huddling together to create vast mosaics of warm flesh.

Their life is defined by the **Thrum**. Because the air is so heavy, these mammals have developed a way to communicate through the metal itself. By pressing their broad, flat chests against the iron structures, they sync their heartbeats to the rhythmic pulse of the geothermal pumps deep below. This shared vibration is their language, their territorial marker, and their lullaby. They are near-blind, their eyes milky and recessed, sensing the world through the resonance of the pipes. When a massive school of them shifts at once, the sound is like a tectonic plate grinding—a low-frequency groan that can be felt in the marrow of one's bones.

### The Silt-Eaters
In the shallow, pulse-driven waters of the floor, the **Armored Scavengers** reign. These fish are not sleek predators; they are heavy, box-like creatures with scales as thick as roofing shingles. They move with a deliberate, grinding motion, their bellies dragging through the black grit. 

They are the great processors of the basin. Their digestive systems are furnaces, capable of extracting nutrients from the oily runoff and the mineral slag that falls from the towers above. They have no teeth, only heavy crushing plates that allow them to grind down the salt-heavy crusts that form at the water’s edge. In the constant twilight, their scales catch the dim orange light, flickering like embers beneath the surface of the dark, weeping pools.

### The Custodians of the Hiss
Deep within the hollowed-out vats and the shadowed interstices of the machinery, there is a more subtle form of life. These are the **Condensate Spiders**, though they are more crustacean than arachnid. They do not spin webs of silk; they spin "webs" of cooling copper filaments scavenged from the crumbling infrastructure. 

They hang in the spray of the hot vents, their long, spindly legs twitching as they catch the "heavy rain." They are the architects of the small scale, weaving their metallic nests into the joints of the towers, reinforcing the places where the iron has thinned. They live in a state of frantic, trembling motion, their tiny claws constantly scraping and repairing, as if they hold a genetic memory of the maintenance schedules once kept by the masters of this place.

### The Collective Breath
There is no "wildness" here in the traditional sense. There is no hunting for sport, no frantic flight from predators. Because the energy—the heat and the minerals—is so abundant and constant, the fauna exists in a state of heavy, sweltering equilibrium. 

The sound of this life is not a chorus of individual voices, but a single, massive, wet respiration. It is the sound of millions of leathery wings clacking, of oily hides rubbing against rusted iron, and of armored bellies churning the silt. It is a biological engine, humming in perfect synchronization with the *clink-drip-hiss* of the mineral world. The inhabitants are the blood and breath of the city-state, a thriving, mundane populace that has turned an industrial tomb into a warm, rhythmic, and permanent home.
