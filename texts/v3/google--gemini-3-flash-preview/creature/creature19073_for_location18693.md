In this sweltering, pressurized crucible, life does not evolve toward grace; it evolves toward **tension**.

The most conspicuous inhabitants are the **Gastropod-Lords**, the dog-sized mollusks that drape themselves like melting wax over the obsidian razors. They are creatures of incredible structural paradox: their underbellies are a translucent, gelatinous cream, but their "skin"—the part exposed to the jagged world—is a leathery, soot-colored hide embedded with recycled shards of shale. They move with an agonizing, rhythmic heave, secreting a mucus so thick and chemically caustic it partially dissolves the glass beneath them, leaving "slug-roads" of frosted, dulled stone. They are deaf to the roar, possessing no ears, but they "see" through the vibration of their eye-stalks, which twitch in sympathetic harmony with the subsonic grinding of the tectonic plates.

Moving through the mid-strata of the air are the **Weather-Swarms**. These are not truly insects, but "flesh-mists"—millions of interlocking, needle-thin organisms that share a single, distributed nervous system. They do not fly so much as they boil through the dense air. To look at a swarm is to see a shifting, prismatic bruise moving through the purple dark. When they settle upon a flowering vine, they do not feed individually; the entire swarm vibrates at a frequency that liquefies the plant’s cellulose, drinking the resulting slurry through a collective, microscopic capillary action. They are the painters of this world, leaving trails of iridescent dross on the underside of the Canopy.

Deep within the gullies, where the moisture collects in vibrating pools, live the **Pulsing Weavers**. These are the architects of the "interference." They are spindly, multi-jointed bipeds with skin the color of wet liver and fingers that end in hardened, chitinous needles. They are terrifyingly thin, built to slip between the razor-edges of the shale without a scratch. 

These Weavers are the authors of the primitive intellect noted in the gourds and chimes. They do not have a culture of surplus, but a culture of **evasion**. They communicate through "thrumming"—pressing their throats against the obsidian walls to send rhythmic pulses to their kin miles away. They are scavengers of energy. You will find them huddled in the "steam-shadows" during the Great Exhalations, their large, lidless eyes reflecting the dull throb of the leaves above. They treat the world as a predator and the Canopy as a god. Their religion, if it can be called that, is the art of remaining unheard by the larger, more violent things that thrash in the dark.

And then there are the **Apex-Anchors**, the things that do not move. They appear to be mounds of black moss or tangled aerial roots, but they are stationary predators of immense patience. They have mimicked the terrain so perfectly that a Weaver might step upon one, thinking it a shelf of stable shale, only to find the "rock" is actually a warm, muscular valve. These Anchors breathe in the "mint and rot" atmosphere, filtering the prismatic insects through baleen-like filters. They are the anchors of the ecosystem, the heavy, silent weights around which the frantic riot of the gastropods and swarms must flow.

Finally, there is the **Canopy-Life**—the things that never touch the glass. High above, clinging to the skiff-sized leaves, are colonies of **Luminous Leeches**. They are the source of the dull, bioluminescent throb. They drink the sap of the lid-trees, turning the chlorophyll into a cold, purple fire that spills out of their translucent pores. They are the suns of this sunless world. When one dies, it loses its grip and falls—a streak of dying violet light that vanishes into the churning, hungry roar of the obsidian floor below, consumed by the mites before it even hits the stone.
