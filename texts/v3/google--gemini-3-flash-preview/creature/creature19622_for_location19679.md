The inhabitants of this plateau are not born so much as they are precipitated. In a place where reality has been ground down to its absolute sharpest edge, life does not occupy the landscape; it is an extension of the landscape’s exhaustion.

The **Glass-Striders** are the most visible, though "visible" is a generous term for creatures that possess the refractive index of heavy water. They are the size of hounds but possess the architecture of a fever dream—six or eight spindly, multi-jointed limbs that taper into points no thicker than a needle. They do not walk so much as they "set" themselves, placing a limb and waiting for the tectonic hum to vibrate the tip into a microscopic groove in the violet slate. Their bodies are hollow, acting as resonance chambers for the planet’s roar. Inside their translucent torsos, one can see a slow, rhythmic swirling of dark, heavy gasses—their version of blood, heavy with the minerals licked from the perspiring stones. 

They have no hunger in the way we understand it. They are metabolic parasites of the Earth’s rotation. By anchoring themselves to the slate and the "tuning fork" shrubs, they convert the low-frequency vibration of the planet into a faint, internal heat. They are the reason the shadows do not move; the Striders have lived so long and moved so little that they have become part of the plateau’s optical property, their bodies bending the dim twilight around them until they are indistinguishable from the air itself.

Beneath the Striders, in the cracks of the frozen shale, live the **Marrow-Sippers**. These are not creatures of flesh, but of "sound-matter." They resemble coils of silver wire, incredibly fine and tight. They inhabit the "perspiration" beneath the rocks. They do not drink the moisture; they inhabit the tension of the droplets. When a Glass-Strider licks a stone, the Marrow-Sipper glides onto the Strider’s tongue, vibrating at a frequency that mimics the Strider’s own internal resonance. They are ghosts in the machine, living off the excess kinetic energy of the larger organisms. They are the "hiss" in the roar.

Then there are the **Liturgeons**, the inhabitants of the impossible, hollow cubes. They are not *in* the cubes in a physical sense, for the cubes have no interior. Rather, the Liturgeons are the "stains" on the glass. If you stare at the obsidian-like surfaces long enough, you realize the shadows within the glass are moving independently of the sun. These are entities of pure perspective. They are flat, two-dimensional shadows that have been trapped in three-dimensional glass anchors. They are the observers of the "finished" erosion. Their purpose is to witness the stalemate of entropy. They do not communicate, but when the planet’s roar reaches a certain pitch, the Liturgeons pulse with a dim, ultraviolet light, synchronizing the vibrations of the plateau to ensure that the rock balanced on the needle-point never falls. They are the maintenance crew of the equilibrium, keeping the reality-film from tearing.

The rarest inhabitants are the **Echo-Eaters**. Because sound does not echo here—it is swallowed—the sound must go somewhere. The Echo-Eaters are soft, charcoal-colored masses that drift like tumbleweeds, though they have no weight. They are composed of "captured silence." They congregate near the "scattered marks," hovering inches above the ground. They are the janitors of the void. When a sound is made—a footstep, a shout, the scraping of a Strider—the Echo-Eaters drift toward it and inhale the vibration, storing the lost energy in their porous, soot-like bodies. To touch one would be to experience an absolute, deafening cold, for they are the heat-sinks of the universe, collecting the discarded entropy of a world that has stopped moving.

Finally, there is the **Will of the Slate**. It is not a single creature, but a collective consciousness formed by the "tuning fork" shrubs. Their corkscrew limbs, hardened into crystalline wood, communicate through the roar. They are a singular, slow-thinking mind that remembers the blue sky from a billion years ago. They do not grow; they simply endure. Their roots reach deep into the "iron air" of the mountain’s core, sipping from the friction of the vacuum. They are the ones who dream the plateau into existence. If the shrubs were to stop ringing, the equilibrium would break, and the mountain would finally realize it is too high to exist and vanish into the indigo void.

These inhabitants do not compete. There is no predator, no prey, for there is no energy to be gained from death that cannot be gained from the hum of the Earth. They are a choir of the static, a biological bureaucracy dedicated to the preservation of a moment that refused to end. They are the ghosts of a finished work, living in the margins of a reality that has forgotten how to change.
