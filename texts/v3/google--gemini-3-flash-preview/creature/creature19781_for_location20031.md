In the Ossuary of the Low-Veldt, life does not "live" in the sense of a flickering flame; it persists as a vibration in a stone.

The primary citizens, the **Grit-Striders**, are the architects of the scream. To see one is to look upon a creature carved from the very pressure of the earth. Their bodies are masterpieces of geometric rigidity, composed of segmented plates of flint-like chitin that clack with the sound of whetstones when they move. They possess six many-jointed limbs, each ending in a needle-sharp tarsus designed to find purchase in the microscopic pores of the resinous spires. Their heads are eyeless, smooth domes of polished obsidian, for in this dim, bruised light, sight is a vestigial vanity. Instead, they feel the world. Their entire carapaces are sensitive to the "Abrasive Symphony," the sonic pressure that defines their reality. 

They do not eat with mouths; they "sift." As they move in their liturgical, staccato crawl, their underbellies—soft, pale, and protected by a fringe of sensory cilia—pick up the protein-heavy husks shed by the obsidian ferns. It is a slow, dry osmosis, a caloric intake measured in centuries.

In the deep, honeycombed hollows of the resin cathedrals, the **Liturges** reside. These are the elder Striders, individuals who have reached such immense age and size that their exoskeletons have begun to fuse with the architecture of the city itself. They are no longer mobile. They have become part of the "infrastructure," their massive, serrated limbs hooked permanently into the resonance chambers of the towers. It is the Liturges who lead the communal scream. By vibrating their torso-plates against the petrified silk of the walls, they act as the lungs of the city, pumping the nutrient-rich gasses outward to feed the dry-lush forests. They are living organs in a body of stone, their consciousness a slow, rhythmic pulse of tectonic memory.

Beneath the feet of the Striders, in the billions of tons of bone-dry silt, live the **Glass-Wraiths**. These are not ghosts, but translucent, serpentine scavengers with skin as thin as mica. They move through the silt as if it were water, leurking within the "dry tide." They are the janitors of the end-state. When a Strider finally ceases its vibration and begins the long transition into mineral dust, the Wraiths emerge. They do not bite; they vibrate at a frequency that matches the dying creature's molecular bond, accelerating the erosion. They turn the dead into the floor in a matter of decades rather than millennia, whispering them back into the silt.

Lurking among the obsidian ferns are the **Statues**. These are the apex of the Ossuary's evolutionary path—predators that have almost entirely surrendered to the mineral state. To the untrained eye, a Statue is merely a jagged outcropping of charcoal-hued mineral. But every few centuries, a Statue will shift its weight. They prey not on the Striders’ bodies, but on their *vibration*. They possess specialized "sound-sinks" in their chests—void-like cavities that consume the ambient roar, converting the kinetic energy of the scream into a slow, cold heat. They are the only silent things in the Ossuary, pockets of absolute stillness that can freeze a passing Strider by simply absorbing the kinetic energy of its movement, leaving a brittle, shattered husk behind.

The inhabitants of the Low-Veldt are a people of the Long Now. They do not have names, but frequencies. They do not have histories, but layers of sedimentation. To dwell there is to be part of a singular, geological thought: a society that has traded the frantic, wet heat of life for the eternal, grinding perfection of the salts and the scream. They are the heralds of the final friction, living out the slow, magnificent wearing-down of the universe.
