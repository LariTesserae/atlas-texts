In this space where physics is a bruised and flickering thing, the inhabitants are not born; they are precipitated. They are the leftovers of collisions, the sentient grit trapped between the gears of falling water and rising stone.

### The Vibratory Choir (The Resonators)
Nothing with a hollow chest could survive here; the roar would collapse a lung in seconds. Therefore, the most common denizens are the **Resonators**. They look like clusters of black daggers or discarded shards of the basalt floor, bonded together by magnetic tension. They do not have blood; they have a core of pressurized mercury. 

As the 3.0/3 roar thrums through the chamber, the Resonators stand atop the sharpest spires, vibrating in perfect sympathy with the noise. They are "sound-eaters." They lean into the crushing volume, their bodies turning white-hot from the friction of the air-water against their skin. When the sound peaks, they "sing" back—not a voice, but a structural hum that momentarily cancels out the gravity around them, allowing them to drift upward like embers before the next house-sized globe of brine slams them back to the rock.

### The Lattice-Weavers
Where the **Weirdness** causes the water to freeze into warm, geometric fractals, you find the **Weavers**. These are spindly, multi-jointed entities that resemble a cross between a harvestman spider and a glass-blowing tool. Their limbs are made of the same rusted iron as the colossal mooring rings, suggesting they may be the diminished, biological descendants of whatever "Low Tech" civilization once anchored this place.

The Weavers are frantic artists. They scuttle across the "warm ice" lattices as the water crystallizes, frantically weaving threads of "Glass-Moss" into the geometry. They are trying to stabilize the transients. They hunt for those moments where time slows down, desperately trying to bolt a suspended drop of water to a basalt spire before the world-wave moves on. They are the janitors of the ephemeral, trying to build a permanent home out of a landscape that is being deleted every second.

### The Echo-Parasites
Hovering in the wake of the **Void-Rays** are the **Echoes**. They are barely there—shimmering, oily films that look like fuel spills on the surface of the turbulent brine. These are not physical creatures so much as "loops" of localized time that have gained a predatory hunger. 

When a Void-Ray pulses through a current, the Echoes latch onto its shifting geometry. They feed on the creature’s "presence." A Void-Ray infested with Echoes will begin to flicker, its past and future selves overlapping until it becomes a blur of translucent cartilage. Eventually, the Echo consumes the Ray’s "now," and the creature vanishes, leaving only a splash of water that tastes like old copper and static.

### The Rust-Scribes
Deep within the shadow of the **Colossal Rings**, where the water is thick with the scent of crushed minerals, live the **Scribes**. They are slow, molluscan things with shells composed of compressed silicate and rust. They move with an agonizing lack of haste that defies the surrounding chaos. 

Each Scribe spends its century-long life-cycle grinding its body against the iron rings. They aren't eating the metal; they are etching it. Using their own acidic bile, they "write" microscopic records of the collisions into the iron. They are the memory of the Vertical Sea. If you were to look through a microscope at the rusted surface of a mooring point, you would see millions of tiny, etched maps—each one recording the trajectory of a single drop of water or the birth of a specific basalt spire. They are the only things here that value history in a place that only knows the present.

### The Transient Travelers
Finally, there are the **Glints**. These are not native, but they are "living." During the reality breakdowns, when a pillar of brine shatters into a swarm of dragonflies, some of the dragonflies do not turn back into mist. They retain their form for a few extra heartbeats, possessing a frantic, panicked intelligence. They have no digestive tracts, no reproductive organs—only a desperate urge to fly toward the dim, sepia-toned light at the top of the "nave." They represent the "potential" of the sea, the frantic, brief hope of the water to become something more complex than a crash. They usually perish within minutes, their wings failing as the "warm ice" in their veins melts back into salt water.
