In the Thicket-City, the concept of "inhabitant" is a linguistic error. To live here is not to reside within a space, but to be a localized expression of the city’s frantic metabolism. Nothing has a soul that is not tied to a root system; nothing has a face that is not a temporary arrangement of photosynthetic tissue.

The denizens of this vertical nightmare are the **Lignin-Wraiths** and the **Sap-Puppets**, though they do not have names for themselves. They are the city’s white blood cells, its maintenance crews, and its primary source of internal friction.

### The Vegetative Sentience
The primary inhabitants are **The Phloem-Walkers**. These are semi-autonomous bipedal growths, roughly the size of a man but composed entirely of braided, oil-slicked vines. They lack skin; instead, they are encased in a hardened, translucent cuticle that reveals the churning green bile of their interiors. They do not have eyes, but are covered in millions of microscopic, light-sensitive cilia that allow them to "see" the city as a heat map of growth and decay.

They move with a terrifying, jerky grace—the motion of a branch snapping back into place. Their purpose is not social, but structural. They are the gardeners of the impossible. You will see a dozen of them clustered around a "sick" cathedral-stalk, their fingers—long, needle-like thorns—burrowing into the bark to inject concentrated nutrients or to prune away the parasitic mycelium that threatens the district’s stability. They do not speak. Instead, they communicate through "chemical shouting," releasing plumes of pheromones so potent they can melt the lungs of anything that still breathes oxygen.

### The Mobile Organs
Clinging to the underside of the mile-wide bridges are the **Cyst-Watchers**. These are stationary, bulbous entities that resemble massive, veined gallbladders. They are the city’s sensory nodes. Every vibration of the screeching fibers, every gunshot crack of a growing trunk, is filtered through their gelatinous bodies. 

As you watch, a Cyst-Watcher might detach. It doesn't fall; it uncoils twenty meters of prehensile, gut-like rope and swings through the golden-green gloom. These are the scavengers of information. They find the "reality-glitches"—the Möbius-strip leaves and the spectral branches—and they engulf them, attempting to digest the "weirdness" back into standard biological matter. They are the city’s immune response to its own madness.

### The Mimicry of Life
Because the vegetation has consumed every ecological niche, it has begun to parody the forms of the extinct animal kingdom. 
*   **The Resin-Hounds:** These are not dogs, but ambulatory clumps of hardened amber and serrated leaf-litter. They possess a "hunger" that is actually a desperate need for minerals. They prowl the "streets," grinding down the older, lignified bark of the city into a fine paste that they then excrete onto the roots of the young, erupting towers. They are the grinders in the city’s Great Compost.
*   **The Nectar-Siphons:** Hovering near the glowing vents of the nectar-halls are organisms that look like a cross between a jellyfish and a willow tree. They stay aloft not through flight, but through the intense hydraulic pressure of the city’s rising sap-valves. They drift on the hot, damp updrafts, their trailing root-tendrils soaking up the airborne sugars. They are the only things in the Thicket-City that move slowly, drifting like ghosts through the deafening roar.

### The Dreaming Core: The Apex-Vegetant
Deep within the central pressure-points of the city, where the heat (2.5) becomes a physical weight, live the **Seers of the Shrapnel-Grain**. These are the oldest parts of the city—ancient, gnarled trunks that have somehow survived the high-metabolism churn for centuries. 

They are no longer mobile. They have become the architecture itself. Their "nervous systems" are the miles of pulsing, translucent rinds that hum with the city’s data. If the Thicket-City has a god, it is this collective of senile, screaming wood. They are the ones dreaming the spectral branches. They are the ones whose "thought" is a silent, agonizing pressure that causes the very air to thicken into resin. 

### The Experience of Presence
To stand amongst these inhabitants is to be an anomaly. To them, you are merely a slow-moving nutrient packet, a pocket of dry carbon in a world of wet, violent vitality. They do not look at you; they sense your moisture. They do not listen to you; they feel the vibration of your heartbeat against the trembling floors. 

The Phloem-Walkers will pass you by, their vine-limbs twitching with the urge to incorporate you—to stitch your skin into the walls, to lace your bones with ivy, to make you part of the screeching, growing, dying, beautiful machine. In the Thicket-City, nothing lives *in* the place; everything *is* the place, and the place is never, ever still.
