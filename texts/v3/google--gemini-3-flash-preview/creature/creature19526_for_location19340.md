In this vertical abyss, life does not occupy the world; it clings to it with a desperate, oscillating ferocity. Because there is no floor to catch the fallen, evolution has favored the hooked, the tethered, and the hollow.

### The Lords of the Tension: The Hook-Wraiths
The apex predators of the Great Liana are the **Hook-Wraiths**. They are creatures of elongated geometry, possessing four spindly limbs that span forty feet from tip to tip. They have no feet, only chitinous, scythe-like grapnels that vibrate in harmony with the Great Liana’s groans. They do not walk; they swing in a terrifying, silent pendulum motion through the voids between vines. Their skin is the color of bruised ice—a translucent indigo that reveals the pulsing, bioluminescent veins beneath. They are blind, sensing the world through the tension of the fibers they touch; they can feel a Spine-Crab’s movement five miles away by the specific frequency of the wood’s vibration. To a Hook-Wraith, the world is not a visual space, but a vast, screaming harp of tactile information.

### The Gardeners of the Rime: The Frost-Moths
While the Spine-Crabs act as janitors, the **Frost-Moths** are the architects of the atmosphere. These are not insects in the traditional sense, but drifting, gas-filled bladders the size of rowboats, trailing miles of stinging, silk-thin filaments. They move in massive "drifts," floating on the updrafts created by the Wind-Pitchers. As they pass, their filaments collect the freezing mist, condensing it into edible "sugar-ice." The human nomads follow these drifts, harvesting the crystalline secretions. The Moths are docile until a Branch-Fall occurs; the sudden change in air pressure causes them to rupture, releasing a cloud of spores that instantly flash-freeze anything they touch, creating "Statue-Groves" of frozen scavengers caught in the blast.

### The Weavers: Humans of the Vertical
The humans here—the **Strap-Folk**—are physically distinct from any terrestrial ancestor. Their fingers are disproportionately long and their grip strength is legendary; a child can hang from a single finger for hours without fatigue. They do not understand the concept of "walking." Their gait is a rhythmic, three-point shuffle, always keeping two tethers snapped onto the bark-ribs. 

Their culture is defined by the **"Song of the Fiber."** Every child is taught to listen to the Great Liana. They can tell by the pitch of the wood’s shriek if a vine is about to snap or if a sap-pulse is rising. Their religion centers on the *Unbroken Line*—the belief that to be untethered is the only true sin. When a member of the tribe dies, they are not buried, for there is no earth. Instead, they are "Sown": wrapped in Lure-Moss and lashed to the underside of a vine, where their body will eventually be absorbed by the bark, their nitrogen fueling the very world that supported them.

### Hidden Horrors: The Sap-Leeches
Deep within the crevices of the sharp bark, where the bioluminescence of the Lure-Moss cannot reach, live the **Sap-Leeches**. These are soft-bodied, pulse-colored mollusks that can grow to the size of a hovel. They secrete an acid that dissolves the obsidian-sharp bark, allowing them to tap directly into the Great Liana’s pressurized veins. When they feed, they swell, glowing a violent, angry crimson. They are the primary source of heat for the Strap-Folk; a captured Leech can be kept alive in a silk cage for months, its radiant metabolic heat providing a small, warm "hearth" for a hanging village. But if the cage breaks, the Leech will instinctively seek the largest heat source nearby—the huddled bodies of the family.

### The Scavengers of the Fall: The Void-Gulls
At the periphery of every human settlement and every Spine-Crab colony are the **Void-Gulls**. These are leathery, crow-like scavengers with four wings and beaks made of forged silica. They survive on the "scraps of the abyss"—the splinters of wood, the shed carapaces of crabs, and the waste of the humans. They are the only creatures that seem to enjoy the "Branch-Falls." When a mile-long section of the world plummets into the dark, the Void-Gulls dive after it in the thousands, shrieking in delight as they strip the falling debris of its life before it vanishes into the crushing pressures of the Deep Maw below.

In this world, life is a beautiful, terrifying precariousness. To live is to be under tension; to be still is to be forgotten; to let go is to cease to exist.
