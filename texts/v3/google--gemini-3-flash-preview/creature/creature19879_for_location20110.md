In the Glass-Lung Basin, life does not thrive through frantic motion, but through a slow, mineral surrender. To live here is to become a part of the geology—to be more stone than flesh, more vibration than pulse.

### The Vibrant-Marrow Monks
There is a resident population, though "inhabitants" is perhaps too active a word. They are the **Litho-Siddhas**, descendants of the hands that carved the basins. They are few—perhaps only a dozen—and they are scattered across the amphitheater, each seated within the "islands" of plum-colored moss. 

They are nearly indistinguishable from the rock itself. Over decades of stillness, the mineral-rich weeping of the basalt has coated their skin in a translucent, amber-like mantle of calcite. They do not wear clothes; the mountain has fashioned them suits of living stone. Their movements are glacial—a single gesture, like the raising of a hand to catch a bead of water, may take an entire afternoon. They have learned the "slow dynamic" of the basin; to move fast in this air is to break the delicate silicate shells that have formed over their joints.

They do not eat in the traditional sense. Their mouths are often fused shut by mineral curtains, leaving only small apertures for breath. Instead, they "eat" the sound. Their nervous systems have tuned themselves to the percussive *ping* and *thrum* of the stone basins. They have become biological resonators; the vibrations of the water-clocks keep their hearts beating in a slow, rhythmic synchronicity with the mountain's song.

### The Lung-Bloom Orchids
Clinging to the undersides of the "ticking" basins are the **Lung-Blooms**. These are not true flowers, but a hybrid of fungal colony and respiratory organ. They possess a fleshy, translucent texture, appearing like clusters of human alveoli turned inside out. These orchids are the only things in the basin that move with any speed. As the heavy droplets strike the basins, sending a physical shockwave through the stone, the Lung-Blooms pulse. They inhale the saturated, mint-scented steam and exhale a fine, iridescent mist of spores. 

These spores are the source of the "heavy air." If a traveler were to breathe deeply here, the spores would begin to coat the lining of their lungs with the same mineral film that coats the rocks, eventually turning the soft tissue into a brittle, glass-like honeycomb—metamorphosing the visitor into a permanent fixture of the choir.

### The Mica-Eaters (The Glass-Skimmers)
The six-legged insects you noticed—the **Glass-Skimmers**—are the janitors of this cathedral. While they drift like shards of mirror, their purpose is predatory, though in the slowest possible way. They feed on the calcite buildup itself. They land upon the Litho-Siddhas or the fern-fronds and, with proboscises tipped with a natural acid, "lick" the fresh mineral deposits away. 

Without the Skimmers, the ferns would become too heavy with stone to hang and would shatter; the Monks would be entombed entirely in a week. The Skimmers are the reason the "islands" of vegetation remain distinct and the basins remain clear. They are the friction that prevents the entire basin from becoming a solid block of crystal. When they catch the light of the high-altitude sun, they don't just reflect it; they refract it into the water-filled basins, creating "light-pulses" that guide the growth of the plum-colored moss.

### The Unseen: The Basin-Core
Deep beneath the "village square" of the basin floor, where the water collects before draining into the mountain’s secret veins, lives the **Aurelia-Aeterna**. It is a single, massive, amorphous entity of sentient protoplasm and silica. It is the "brain" of the clockwork. 

The stone basins are its ears. Every *ping* transmits data to the core—the mineral content of the water, the heat of the sun, the health of the moss. It is the Aurelia that "tuned" the basins through the hands of the ancients. It produces the "crushed mint" scent—a chemical pheromone that keeps the Skimmers docile and the Monks in their trance. 

The Aurelia is waiting. It is growing a crystalline brain out of the mountain's bones, using the Monks as temporary biological storage for memories that the stone cannot yet hold. To live in the Glass-Lung Basin is to be a single note in a symphony that will take ten thousand years to finish a single chord.
