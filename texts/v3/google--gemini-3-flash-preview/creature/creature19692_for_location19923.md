To live in the Weave-State is to exist as a stitch in a garment that is still being sewn.

The inhabitants are not masters of this place, nor are they its slaves; they are its **Leukocytes**, a biological maintenance class that has forgotten where the plant ends and their own nervous systems begin.

### The Weavers (The Kindred of the Knot)
They are elongated, spindly beings, their limbs possessing extra joints that allow them to fold themselves into the tightest braids of the lattice. Their skin is not quite flesh, but a leathery, bark-like hide, cross-hatched with ritual scarring that mimics the patterns of the Weave. They do not wear clothes so much as they allow mosses and fine, silk-like vines to colonize their bodies, creating a living camouflage that makes them invisible the moment they stop moving.

They lack the concept of "standing." Instead, they "perch" or "cling." Their feet have evolved prehensile toes, the skin of their palms is as tacky as a gecko’s, and they move with a fluid, terrifying verticality. To them, a three-mile drop is not a danger; it is simply another direction.

Their culture is one of **Perpetual Geometry**. Life is a holy labor of tension and torsion. They communicate not just through a low, choral humming that mimics the thrum of the sap, but through a complex tactile language of vibration. By plucking a structural vine, a Weaver can send a "pulse-message" through the lattice that can be felt by a kinsman ten miles away. Their entire morality is based on the integrity of the knot: a "straight" person is untrustworthy, while a "well-knotted" soul is a pillar of the community.

### The Mycelial Oracles
Deep within the "wells" where gravity bends and the vegetation is densest, live the **Oracles**. These are Weavers who have ceased to move entirely. They have sat in the damp gloom for so long that the Weave has claimed them. Root-fibers have threaded through their capillary systems; their thoughts are no longer their own, but are processed by the chemical signals of the forest. 

They act as the nervous system’s nodes. When the forest is hungry or when a structural column is rotting from within, the Oracles begin to weep a specific, pheromonal resin. The younger Weavers smell this "sorrow" and travel leagues to the site of the rot to graft new, living tissue over the wound.

### The Scavenger-Symphists
Living in the shadows of the Great Pillars are the **Vesper-Moths**, beings of hollow bone and translucent membrane. They are the size of small ships, drift-predators that do not hunt with teeth, but with sound. They emit a frequency that causes the liquid water clinging to the vines to vibrate and shatter into a fine mist, suffocating smaller prey trapped in the dampness. 

The Weavers do not hunt these creatures. Instead, they "tune" them. By tying specific obsidian whistles to the Vesper-Moths’ wings, the Weavers use the creatures as living barometers. The pitch of the whistles tells the gardeners the humidity and pressure of the void below, alerting them to the birth of a thunderstorm miles before the first flash of blue light.

### The Lithic-Parasites (The Un-Makers)
There is a terror in the Weave: the **Rust-Beetles**. They are not made of metal, but they are the only things in this world that possess hardness. Their shells are calcified, mineralized husks that can grind through the toughest bamboo-struts. 

They represent the entropic force. They do not eat the plants for nutrition; they eat them to create "voids" in which to lay their eggs. To the Weavers, a Rust-Beetle is a demon of silence—wherever they go, the thrum of the world stops. Groups of "War-Gardeners" hunt them by singing. They gather around the beetle and hum in a dissonant harmony that resonates with the creature's calcified shell until it shatters, turning the predator back into a spray of mineral dust that feeds the very vines it sought to consume.

### The Guest-Logic
Because the environment reacts to intention, those who live here have developed a "Soft Mind." They do not force their way through the foliage. They *suggest*. A seasoned Weaver walking toward a gap in the shelf does not build a bridge; they simply hold the "image" of a crossing in their mind and hum a specific nursery rhyme. Under their feet, the fast-growing "Speed-Vines" respond to the vibration and the bio-electric field of the person, knitting themselves into a temporary, swaying catwalk.

When a Weaver dies, they aren't buried. They are shoved into the center of a "Life-Braid." Within days, the forest has unspun their body, recycling the carbon and the memory of their many knots back into the structural integrity of the world. In the Weave-State, no one is ever truly gone; they just become the floor upon which their children climb.
