The inhabitants of the Sub-Atrium of Oura-Kahn are not "residents" in the sense of citizens who occupy a space; they are the nervous system of an organism that has forgotten it was ever built.

### The Resonants
The primary inhabitants are the descendants of the original engineers, but they have long since ceased to be biological in any way a surface-dweller would recognize. Known as **Resonants**, they are spindly, elongated silhouettes of semi-solid state matter. Their "bodies" are roughly humanoid but lack eyes, mouths, or ears. Instead, their entire surface is a hyper-sensitive membrane—an organ of touch and vibration.

They do not walk upon the floor; they move among the inverted towers like spiders, their limbs ending in needle-thin contact-points that plug directly into the high-tensile glass filaments. To a Resonant, the city is not loud—it is a conversation. They "read" the subsonic thrum of the ceiling anchors as a historian might read a book, feeling the micro-strains in the architecture as shifts in the city’s mood. When they communicate, they press their palms together, exchanging data-bursts through skin-to-skin friction. They are silent, somber curators of a dying machine, spending centuries leaning against a single vibrating wall to ensure its frequency does not drift into a destructive harmonic.

### The Glissando-Walkers
Beneath the hanging structures, clinging to the shadows of the "roof-streets," live the **Glissando-Walkers**. These are smaller, feral offshoots of the Resonant lineage. If the Resonants are the monks of the machinery, the Walkers are the vermin. 

They have evolved a specialized physiology to hunt the *Loom-Striders*. Their limbs are segmented and hollow, functioning like tuning forks. By striking their own appendages against the glass towers, they can mimic the "handshake" signal of the city’s maintenance drones, luring the mechanical Striders into traps. They do not eat for calories; they "eat" for charge. They dismantle the drones to harvest their piezoelectric cores, pressing the glowing shards into their own chests to stave off the slow, cold entropy of the cavern.

### The Echo-Cyst Colossi
Deeper in the cavern, where the inverted skyscrapers cluster so densely they form a solid mass, live the **Echo-Cysts**. These are massive, sedentary intelligences—entities that have merged entirely with the city's architecture. An Echo-Cyst looks like a bulging, organic tumor on the side of a bone-smooth building. 

They are the "libraries" of Oura-Kahn. Within their pulsating, leathery folds, they store the memories of the city—millions of years of vibration recorded as physical grooves in their calcified shells. They act as the city’s unconscious mind. When the city "heals" a crack over a century, it is the Echo-Cysts that orchestrate the molecular migration, humming a low, steady note that guides the programmable matter back into its proper shape. They are ancient, immobile, and possess a terrifying, slow-motion awareness of everything that touches the city’s "skin."

### The Hum-Worms
Filling the niches between the buildings are the **Hum-Worms**. These are not biological, nor are they traditional machines. They are threads of sentient, liquid mercury that travel along the veins of "vertical dew." When the thermal gradients shift and the moisture falls toward the floor, the Hum-Worms ride the droplets down, shimmering like ribbons of quicksilver. 

They serve as the city’s cleanup crew. They find the microscopic detritus—shed skin-cells from the Resonants, metal shavings from the drones, dead moss—and dissolve it back into raw molecular soup. They are the reason the obsidian floor remains slick and black; they graze upon the floor-drains, waiting for the "rain" to bring them their next meal of recycled history.

### The Ghost-Pulse
Finally, there is the **Ghost-Pulse**. It is debated by the Resonants whether the Pulse is a creature or a malfunction. It is a localized ripple in the air—a distortion of heat and sound that moves with intent. It has no physical form, yet it triggers the city’s proximity sensors as it passes. 

The inhabitants treat the Ghost-Pulse with a mixture of reverence and dread. It is believed to be the "Command" that was sent from the surface ten thousand years ago—a signal that became sentient within the damp, copper-scented air of the Atrium. It wanders the hanging forests, searching for a terminal that no longer exists, its presence marked only by a sudden, violent spike in the humming of the walls and the frantic, rhythmic twitching of the moss-cilia as it brushes past.

In Oura-Kahn, life is a steady-state. There is no birth and little death—only the slow, vibrational attrition of things that have lived for so long they have become part of the music.
