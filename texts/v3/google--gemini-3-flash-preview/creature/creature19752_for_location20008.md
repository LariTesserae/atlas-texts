The inhabitants of the Stoke-Spires are not visitors to this desiccated verticality; they are its high-pressure byproduct, as much a part of the mineral cycle as the soot and the resins.

### The Silicate-Kin
The dominant inhabitants—the "cloaked figures" on the gantries—are the **Lithic Monks**, though they refer to themselves simply as the *Strainers*. They are tall, spindly beings whose anatomy has been dictated by the centrifugal gravity of the spires. Their bones are not calcium-based but are composed of the same porous, honeycombed rock as the terrain, making them incredibly light yet structurally rigid. 

They do not breathe in the traditional sense. Instead, their skin is a fine, porous membrane that leaches minerals directly from the amber dust. They are "thermal-trophs"; they stand for hours on the vibrating stone of the spires, absorbing the low-frequency hum of the deep-world engines to power their slow, measured movements.

Because the air is a desert of sound, they have no mouths. Where a face should be, there is a complex arrangement of sensory pits—concave depressions lined with fine, metallic hairs that catch the "taste" of the gaseous resins. They navigate the "Silt-Gloam" through a sophisticated form of haptic sonar, sensing the presence of a gantry or a fellow inhabitant by the minute change in air pressure against their sensitive skin.

### The Culture of the Pulse
Society is organized around the **Piston-Beat**. LIFE is measured not in years, but in *strokes*. A person’s age is determined by how many cycles of the cathedral-sized pumps they have witnessed. 

Communication is a tender, tactile art. To speak, two inhabitants must touch. They press their palms or foreheads together, using internal rhythmic organs to send vibrations through their skeletal frames. A "conversation" feels to them like a complex drum solo conducted through the bone. When they must communicate across distances, they strike the metal gantries with weighted mallets, sending long-distance "vibe-shouts" that travel through the iron lattice for miles.

Their clothing is not fabric—which would rot or clog in the resinous air—but is woven from the **Litho-Flow** crystals. These garments are stiff, rattling sheets of grey mineral that protect their sensitive skin from the abrasive silicate winds of the Thermal Drift.

### The Symbiotes: The Gear-Ticks and Oil-Wights
Living alongside the Lithic Monks are the "domesticated" versions of the Scuttlers. Small, multi-limbed **Gear-Ticks** live within the folds of the Monks' crystal robes. These creatures act as sensory extensions, vibrating in specific patterns to warn their hosts of a thinning in the mineral-density of a bridge or an approaching "pillow" of stagnant smoke.

Deep within the industrial rigs, occupying the spaces where the dark, oily alloys meet the bone-dry rock, live the **Oil-Wights**. These are formless, gelatinous entities—one of the few "fluid" things in this world. They are the keepers of the lubricant. They slide through the massive joints of the clockwork bridges, their bodies acting as a living grease that prevents the heat-expanded metal from seizing. The Monks treat them with a quiet, religious reverence, feeding them purified resins to ensure the world continues to move.

### The Dream of the "Down"
While the Monks live in the heights, there is a caste of outcasts known as the **Silt-Divers**. These individuals are obsessed with the "historical myth" of the ground. They modify their bodies, weighting their limbs with leaden ballast, attempting to sink through the golden-lit void toward the shimmering sand-rivers at the world’s base.

None ever return. But the Divers are the poets of the Stoke-Spires. They send back one-way signals—muffled, thumping vibrations through the core of the spires—describing a world where the pressure is so great that the dust turns to glass, and where the "Ground" is not a myth, but a crushing, absolute reality that sings with the voice of the entire planet’s weight.

### The Apex: The Glass-Eyed Sentinels
At the very tips of the spires, above the refineries, live the **Sentinels**. These are Monks who have surrendered all movement. They have stood still for so many centuries that the mineral-deposition of the air has fused them to the spire itself. They are becoming Litho-Flows. 

Their eyes have been replaced by polished spheres of silicate, reflecting the unblinking sun. They do nothing but watch the Vast. It is believed that they can see the "Drift-Storms" coming months before they arrive, sensing the minute fluctuations in the amber atmosphere. To the rest of the inhabitants, the Sentinels are the ultimate goal: to finally stop moving, to stop straining, and to become, at last, part of the silent, unyielding geography.
