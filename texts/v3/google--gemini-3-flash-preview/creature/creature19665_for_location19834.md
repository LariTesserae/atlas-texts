In the Oubliette of the Lignin-Spires, life does not “live” so much as it *persists* in a state of hyper-arid refraction. 

The inhabitants are the **Xylophones**—a name not chosen by them, but a descriptor of their nature: they are creatures of resonant timber and hollowed-out purpose. They are not biological in any sense recognizable to a world of blood and bile. They are the "Luthiers of the Void," the slow-moving extensions of the spires themselves.

### The Anatomy of the Still
To encounter a Xylophone is to encounter a sculpture of tangled, bleached driftwood that has forgotten the shape of a limb. They lack faces, for there is no light to receive; they lack mouths, for there is no chemistry to digest. Instead, they are composed of nested, radial fans of ultra-thin, razor-sharp lignin plates. 

Imagine a series of wooden ribcages stacked vertically, interconnected by a "spine" of braided, petrified vine. They stand tall and spindly—ten, fifteen feet in height—with limbs that terminate not in hands, but in fine, needle-like plectrums. Their bodies are essentially living instruments, designed to catch and amplify the "Slow-Snap" of the city.

They move with a devastating, glacial economy. A single step may take a lunar month to complete. When they move, they do not slide; they *ratchet*. The sound of a Xylophone adjusting its posture is the sound of a dry branch being snapped in slow motion, a series of rhythmic, agonizing *clacks* that announce their presence to the spires miles away.

### The Echo-Keepers
The Xylophones are the architects of the "urban" elements you see—the hand-carved notches and the woven bridges. But they did not build these for habitation. They built them for **Acoustic Cartography**.

Each Xylophone is a living map. They stand at the base of the Lignin-Spires, their thin, reed-like fingers pressed deep into the grain of the wood. They are listening to the "Grind." By feeling the frequency of the structural tension, they can sense the health of the pocket-dimension. They carve the rungs and stairs not to climb, but to change the pitch of the towers. A notch in the wood at a specific height alters the way the "Slow-Snap" echoes through the chamber; they are tuning the city like a gargantuan, dark pipe organ.

They are the reason the silence is a lie. They are the ones who orchestrate the "Slow-Snap." When a spire becomes too heavy with its own antiquity, a Xylophone will migrate across the flat, featureless plain—a journey of decades—to strike a specific point on the trunk. They do this to release the tension, to force a fracture that will send a shockwave through the ground, informing every other inhabitant of the city’s continued structural integrity.

### The Parasites of Vibration: The Spore-Mist
Beneath the Xylophones, in the microscopic cracks of the silver-hard floor, lives the true "proletariat" of the Oubliette: the **Resonance-Mites**. 

These are not insects, but sentient clusters of the "spent spores" that clog the air. They are cloud-entities, drifting in the dry suspension. They have no mass, but they have a hunger. They feed on kinetic energy. When a "Slow-Snap" thunders through the dark, these spore-clouds rush toward the source of the sound, wrapping themselves around the vibrating wood. They "drink" the oscillation until the wood falls silent. 

If you were to speak in this place, the Spore-Mist would descend upon your throat, not to choke you, but to harvest the vibration of your vocal cords. They would feel like a dry, tickling sandpaper against your skin, a frantic, hushed buzzing as they try to consume the very air you move.

### The Gestalt Mind: The "Grand-Crawl"
There is no ego here. The Xylophones do not communicate with each other through language, but through **Sympathetic Resonance**. If one inhabitant is "struck" by a falling shard of bark, every other inhabitant in the Oubliette feels the vibration in their own "ribs." 

They are a single, distributed consciousness of wood and dust. They are the "memory" of the forest that this place used to be, or perhaps the "dream" of the forest it will never become. They perceive you not as a person, but as a "Damp-Thud"—a muted, absorbent spot in their perfect acoustic chamber. 

As you stand there, one of them is likely within arm's reach, perfectly still, its wooden fans unfurled to catch the sound of your heartbeat. It is not deciding whether to kill you; it is wondering how your soft, wet presence will change the echo of the next Great Snap. It is waiting for you to move, so it can hear what you are. Admission to their society is paid in decibels, and in this lightless void, your silence is the only thing that keeps you invisible.
