In the Vertical Abyss, life does not "inhabit" the world so much as it is secreted by it. There is no distinction between the creature and the medium; everything is a variation of the brine, the silt, and the slow, rhythmic pulse of the Great Thicket.

### The Weaver-Lungs (The Macro-Fauna)
The dominant lifeforms are not mobile predators but sessile, drifting organs of immense scale. Known as **Weaver-Lungs**, these entities are translucent, gas-filled bladders the size of cathedrals, tethered to the obsidian pillars by umbilical silver filaments. They function as the world’s filtration system. 

They do not have faces. Instead, their surfaces are covered in a fine, oscillating fringe that tastes the salinity of the rising plumes. They are the keepers of the "soft water" pockets; they drink the harsh, mineral-heavy brine and exhale the velvet-textured water where reality begins to soften. To drift near one is to hear the only true sound in the Abyss: a rhythmic, wet *thrum-slap* as their internal valves cycle the ocean. They are pale, leaning toward a sickly opalescence, and within their translucent bellies, one can see the silhouettes of smaller things—accidental guests or parasites—frozen in the slow-motion digestion of a century.

### The Glass-Skin Commensals
Living within the suffocating embrace of the Great Thicket are the **Glass-Skins**. These are the "sentient thoughts" mentioned in the ancient records—liminal creatures that exist at the intersection of biology and geometry. They resemble long, faceted needles of living quartz, trailing ribbons of bioluminescent nervous tissue. 

They do not swim with fins; they move by manipulating the localized gravity of the pillars. They "fall" horizontally through the kelp-forest, shifting their internal mass to slide through the water like a stone dropped down a well. They are scavengers of information rather than matter. They cluster around the "scattered marks"—the ancient archways and geometric trenches—pressing their crystalline bodies against the stone to "read" the vibrations of the drowning continent. They are the memory-keepers of the erosion, vibrationally recording the death of every spire.

### The Marrow-Leeches
Where the obsidian pillars "weep" silt, the **Marrow-Leeches** congregate. They are not leeches in the traditional sense, but clusters of prehensile, calcified tubes that grow directly out of the stone’s pores. They are a bridge between the mineral and the meat. 

As a pillar dissolves, these creatures calcify the runoff, turning the eroding silt into elaborate, spiraling shells that jut out into the void. They are blind and deaf, responding only to the tectonic groans of the world. When a pillar finally collapses, the Marrow-Leeches do not die; they detach and become "Drifters"—heavy, armored husks that sink upward into the sky-ocean, searching for a new peak to colonize. In the dim light, they look like falling constellations of bone.

### The Null-Medusae (The Phantoms of the Lens)
Deep within the zones where light bends back on itself, the **Null-Medusae** reside. These are the rarest and most unsettling inhabitants. They are not entirely physical. They appear as ripples in the water, a shimmering distortion shaped vaguely like a human ribcage or a blooming flower. 

They exist in the "lensed" pockets of time. If you encounter one, you are seeing a ghost of the water’s own memory. They have no metabolism; they feed on the "weirdness" (2.8) of the Abyss, anchoring themselves to the points where the laws of physics have frayed. They are the observers. When you see the back of your own head through a gap in the vines, it is often a Null-Medusa reflecting you, acting as a living mirror that shows not who you are, but how you are being dissolved by the pressure.

### The Pulse-Lice
Finally, there are the **Pulse-Lice**, the tiny, frantic sparks of lime-green phosphor that inhabit the skin of the Great Thicket. They are the "white blood cells" of the breathing vines. Millions of them skitter across the violet membranes of the kelp-trees, cleaning away the obsidian dust. They communicate through synchronized flashes, creating the "weather" of light that moves across the void. To an observer, they look like a slow-motion avalanche of stars, but to the forest, they are a singular, buzzing consciousness that ensures the heartbeat of the sap never falters.

Nothing here is hurried. Nothing here is violent. The inhabitants of the Vertical Abyss are patient, pressurized, and ancient, waiting with the same terrifying calm as the water itself for the last pillar to finally soften and vanish into the turquoise infinite.
