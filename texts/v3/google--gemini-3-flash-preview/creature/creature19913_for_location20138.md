To understand the inhabitants of Oresund, one must first look at the **Oresundians** themselves—creatures of "the long pause." They are not born so much as they are articulated from the cooling mineral silt. 

### The Oresundians: The Graphite-Skin
The people who tend the Great Slowing are tall, incredibly thin, and possess multiple joints that lack the fluidity of mammalian muscle. Their skin is a feat of natural engineering: a matte, self-lubricating graphite that reflects the infrared glow of the spires. It feels cool to the touch despite the ambient heat, acting as a heat sink that draws warmth inward to power their slow-burning metabolisms.

They have no mouths in the traditional sense. Instead, their faces are dominated by three vertical slits of sensory membrane that vibrate in the high-pressure air. They "breathe" by standing near the steam-vents, spreading their long, many-fingered hands to catch the scalding vapor. To an outsider, an Oresundian standing over a vent looks like a piece of statuary, frozen in a gesture of supplication. They may stand this way for forty-eight hours, absorbing the mineral-heavy moisture until their skin turns from grey to a deep, lustrous violet.

Their movement is a choreographed crawl. They do not walk; they shift. Because the gravity on these needle-tips is erratic and the air is like fluid, they move as if underwater, placing each foot with a mechanical precision that ensures the vibration of their step does not resonate through the settling basalt.

### The Liturgy of the Gear-Tenders
Socially, the Oresundians are divided by the frequency of the machines they maintain. 
*   **The Valve-Keepers** are the most solitary. They live on the outermost struts, listening for the internal pressures of the planet. They are recognizable by the heavy brass stethoscopes fused to their chests, allowing them to hear the "heartbeat" of the magma deep below. 
*   **The Silt-Architects** are those who direct the growth of the silicon-fungi. They use small, ultrasonic chisels to carve the "slag-crust" into functional shapes—housing blocks, insulators, and decorative filigree. They do not build houses; they nurture them out of the rock over centuries.

### The Fauna: The Shimmer-Kites and the Molten-Ticks
The Oresundians are not alone in the vertical gaps. The ecology is sparse but specialized:

*   **The Aethel-Kites**: These are the scavenger-skiffs mentioned in the ecological rules. They are larger than they appear from below—some with wingspans of twenty feet, yet weighing no more than a few pounds. Their wings are gossamer sheets of iridescent silicate, stretched over frames of hollow iron. They do not flap; they tack against the thermal updrafts rising from the abyss. They feed on the static electricity that accumulates on the city’s lead-lined roofs, landing only to "ground" themselves, sparking brightly as they discharge their hunger into the architecture.
*   **The Glass-Spiders (The Weaver-Servos)**: These are not biological, though they act with a hive intelligence. They are small, multi-legged automatons made of heat-resistant glass, originally created to repair the external copper pipes. Over the millennia, they have become a self-replicating part of the ecosystem. They scuttle over the obsidian faces with a frantic, clicking energy that the Oresundians find distasteful but necessary. They "weave" patches for the steam-leaks using threads of molten basalt, trailing glowing orange silk behind them as they weld the city together.

### The Silent Culture
There is no spoken language in Oresund. The air is too thin and the pressure too high for vocal cords to survive. Communication happens through **haptic resonance**. An Oresundian will place a hand on the copper railing of a walkway, and another will touch the same railing a hundred yards away. By tapping in rhythmic, low-frequency pulses, they send complex thoughts through the very bones of the city. 

The city itself is their library. The history of Oresund is etched into the iron walkways in a series of molecular scores. An Oresundian can run a finger along a cooling strut and read the temperature fluctuations and the social shifts of the last three centuries.

### The Ancestors (The Statues)
Perhaps the most haunting residents are the **Dormants**. When an Oresundian reaches the end of their long life—usually after seven or eight centuries—their graphite skin begins to calcify. They do not die in a conventional sense; they simply stop moving. They choose a niche in the shard-rock or a corner of a brass balcony and enter the "Final Slowing." Over decades, they harden into solid silicate. The city is littered with these ancestors—frozen, elegant figures that have become structural supports for the ever-evolving hive. To the living, these statues are not corpses, but a living record of the city's patience, still radiating a faint, residual warmth as they become one with the spire.
