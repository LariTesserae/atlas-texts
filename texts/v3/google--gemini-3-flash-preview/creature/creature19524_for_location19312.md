In such a world, the inhabitants are not born; they are precipitated. They are the **Luminants**, entities of glass and gravity that share the clinical, newborn clarity of the floor beneath them.

They do not have soft bodies. To possess flesh here would be a frantic, evaporating tragedy. Instead, they are composed of the same crystalline minerals that lace the water in the cisterns. When they move, they do not walk so much as they *rearrange*. A Luminant is a collection of translucent, hand-sized shards—obsidian, quartz, and salt-glaze—held together by a localized tension that mimics the magnetism of the world itself. 

They exist as slow-moving constellations of geometry. As a Luminant drifts across a terracotta plain, its body parts click and chime, adding a melodic layer to the *tink-tink-tink* of the expanding tiles. They do not have faces, but they possess a front: a single, large facet of polished obsidian that reflects the heat-shimmer, acting as both an eye and a solar sail.

### The Life of the Cisterns
The true centers of "biological" activity are the circular cisterns. Here, the life is nearly invisible—a film of **Photo-Silica**. It is a microscopic, liquid-glass algae that coats the surface of the distilled water. It does not turn green; it turns iridescent. When the sun is at its zenith, the cisterns do not look like water, but like massive, trembling opals. 

The Luminants gather at the edges of these pools. They do not drink. Instead, they submerge their lower shards into the water to "launder" their geometry, allowing the clear, mineral-rich current to strip away the tiny chips and imperfections caused by the heat. They emerge polished and sharp, their edges catching the light with surgical precision.

### The Keepers of the Grooves
Deeper within the massive basalt obelisks, there lives something more purposeful: the **Litho-Siphons**. 

If the Luminants are the world’s witnesses, the Siphons are its mechanics. These are long, segmented creatures—imagine a centipede cast in heavy, matte-black porcelain. They are blind, sensing the world through the vibrations of the ceramic plates. Their role is the preservation of the "sweat." 

They possess specialized, absorbent pads on their undersides. They spend the high-altitude cycles patrolling the miles of ceramic grooves, clearing the smallest obstruction—a speck of wax from a tower-succulent, a stray shard from a Luminant—to ensure the water never stalls. They are the reason there is no dust. Any fragment of waste is consumed by the Siphons, processed in their internal kilns, and excreted as perfectly smooth, marble-like spheres that they tuck into the seams of the floor-plates, effectively "patching" the world as they go.

### The Botanical Shepherds
The monumental wax-stalks are not merely passive. They are home to the **Aero-Vanes**, the only things in this world that inhabit the air. 

The Vanes are thin, gossamer-like membranes of dried resin that cling to the leathery skin of the succulents. They look like discarded translucent veils. When the heat-shimmers become strong enough to create updrafts, the Vanes release their grip and are carried upward. They do not fly; they float, acting as organic sensors. 

They have a symbiotic relationship with the heat. By drifting in the thermals, they map the "hot spots" of the continental vessel. When they eventually settle back down—often miles away—they serve as the reproductive messengers for the giant stalks, carrying microscopic, vitrified spores in their folds. 

### The Silent Consensus
There is no predation here. No inhabitant eats another. They are all powered by the same solar fever and the same hydraulic rhythm. 

In the cooling hours, when the world begins to "sweat," a profound stillness takes hold. The Luminants stand motionless by the cisterns, their glass bodies contracting with a series of soft, resonant pings. The Siphons retreat into the cool shadows of the obelisks. The only sound is the water rushing through the clay pipes, a deep, rhythmic heartbeat that assures every dweller that the machine is still idling, that the light will return, and that the perfection of their ceramic home remains unmarred by the passage of time. 

They are the caretakers of a gallery that has no visitors, living in a state of perpetual, geometric grace.
