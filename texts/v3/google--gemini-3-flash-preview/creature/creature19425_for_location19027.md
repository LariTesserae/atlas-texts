The inhabitants of the Cradle-Swells are the **Marrow-Weavers**, a race of lithe, elongated humanoids whose bodies have been sculpted by the crushing embrace of the brine. They are not swimmers in the traditional sense; they are climbers of the void. 

### The Physiology of the Inhabitants
A Marrow-Weaver is a study in fluid tension. Their skin is not scaled, but possesses the texture of cured chamois—pale, translucent, and slightly rubbery to resist the Osmotic pressure. They lack hair, which would only drag in the heavy liquid; instead, their heads and spines are crested with sensory filaments that look like fine, white silk. These cilia are their primary interface with the world, vibrating in sympathy with the groaning wood of the Mother-Trunks, allowing them to "hear" a predator or a neighbor's call from miles away.

They are biologically suited to the "Slow." Their hearts beat perhaps four times a minute. Their lungs have evolved into internal gas-bladders that do not breathe air, but extract oxygen from the brine through a series of rhythmic, pulsing slits along their ribcages. They move with a deliberate, haunting economy of motion. To a creature of the earth, they would appear to be moving in a dream; to them, a fast movement is a sign of panic or profound disrespect.

### The Social Mycelium
The Weavers do not have kings; they have **Pruners**. Because the city is a living organism, the highest social caste is composed of those who understand the metabolism of the vines. They carry tools made of sharpened obsidian-glass (scavenged from the distant, lightless depths) or hardened, petrified seed-husks. 

Society is organized into **Vines**, or matrilineal clans that occupy a specific structural rib of the Mother-Trunks. A person’s identity is tied to the health of their neighborhood’s foliage. If a family’s dwelling-gourd begins to wilt or grow "wild," the family is seen as morally or spiritually ailing. 

Their "speech" is a dual-language. For close-range intimacy, they use a complex sign language of the fingers, which are long and possess an extra joint for intricate knot-making. For public address, they employ a "Vocal Hum"—a deep, sub-bass resonance produced in the chest that can vibrate the very water-density around them. In the markets, these hums overlap to create a physical sensation of warmth, a literal wall of sound that keeps the biting chill of the brine at bay.

### The Great Herds: The Whale-Slugs
The most significant non-sapient inhabitants are the **Silt-Siphons**, or Whale-Slugs. These are colossal, boneless mollusks, sixty feet long, that drift through the open brine between the vine-clusters. They are the "livestock" and "beasts of burden" of the Swells. 

The Weavers "ride" them by tethering silk lines to the slugs’ thick, gelatinous mantles. These creatures act as mobile gardens; their backs are encrusted with edible mosses and nutrient-rich barnacles that the Weavers harvest. The relación is symbiotic: the Weavers protect the slugs from the **Needle-Sharks** (translucent predators that strike like glass bolts), and in exchange, the slugs provide transit and the raw biological waste that fertilizes the urban gourds.

### The Micro-Bureaucracy of the Cleaning Schools
In the plazas and communal spaces, the air—or rather, the brine—is thick with the **Silver-Nippers**. These are needle-thin fish that exist in a state of constant, frantic motion, the only things in this world that are "fast." They are the janitors of the Cradle-Swells. They are semi-domesticated; a Weaver will often float motionless in a plaza for an hour, allowing a cloud of Nippers to scour their skin of algae and parasites. This is the Weaver’s version of a bath and a social coffee hour combined.

### The Specter of the "Heavy Bottom"
There is a profound, cultural fear of the **Sinkers**. When a Weaver dies, or a tool is dropped, or a building’s vine snaps, it begins an eternal descent into the "Heavy Bottom"—the lightless, high-pressure depths where the brine becomes solid under its own weight. 

To the inhabitants, life is a struggle against gravity. Their religion is one of buoyancy. They worship the "Sun-Surface," a mythical place of total light they will never reach, and they despise the "Anchor," the dark earth they have forgotten. Consequently, they are obsessed with tethering. Every child is born with a silk cord tied to their ankle, fastened to the clan-root. To be "un-tethered" is not freedom; it is a death sentence by slow, downward drift.

### The Season of the "Pulse"
Once every decade, the Mother-Trunks undergo a growth spurt. The wood groans with a sound like a shattering mountain. This is the only time of "high dynamic" in their world. The water becomes clouded with amber spores, and the Weavers enter a state of ritual frenzy. They must work together to re-shape the city as it stretches, grafting new gourds and culling runaway vines before the expansion tears their homes apart. It is their only era of "history"—they mark time not by years, but by how many Pulses a patriarch has survived.
