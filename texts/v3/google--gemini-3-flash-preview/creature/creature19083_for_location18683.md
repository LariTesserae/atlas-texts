In the Pelagic Megastructure, the "indigenous" life is a collection of orphans and laborers, biological entities forced to adapt to the logic of the machine. The inhabitants are defined not by their ancestry, but by their relationship to the **Circulation**.

### The Maintenance Swarms
The most common residents are the **Glint-Schools**. These are not the natural fish of Earth’s oceans, but gen-engineered “janitor-species” with scales of polished chrome-zinc. They do not possess the wandering curiosity of wild fish; their brains are hardwired to the Megastructure’s hygiene. They move in flash-frozen unison, millions of bodies acting as a single, living scouring pad. They follow the vibration of the intake valves, grazing on the thin layers of bio-film that attempt to coat the cobalt-steel. When a school turns, they catch the warehouse-white light of the luminaires, creating a blinding, strobe-like effect that can disorient any predator—or human—unlucky enough to be caught in the open water.

### The Anchor-Clutchers
Where the gentry of the open water are slick and fast, the dwellers of the **Bulkhead Lattice** are gnarled and stationary. These are the "Barnacle-Men" and the "Silt-Worms"—crustaceans and tubeworms that have evolved shells not of calcium, but of salvaged metal shavings and slag precipitated from the water.

### The Apex Technophiles: The Volt-Sharks
The predators of this abyss are the **Aura-Hounds**. Imagine a great white shark stripped of its mystery and replaced with the efficiency of a torpedo. Their skin is not rough like sandpaper but smooth and oily, like vulcanized rubber, designed to slip through high-pressure water with zero cavitation. 

They are effectively blind. In an environment where the light is flat and flickering, they have abandoned sight for electro-reception. They "see" the Megastructure as a glowing skeleton of power lines and humming engines. They do not hunt by smell; they hunt by the *interruption* of a frequency. When a living thing moves through the water, it creates a "shadow" in the ambient electrical hum of the wall-turbines. The Aura-Hounds home in on these silences. They are often found circling the gargantuan luminaires, mesmerized by the high-voltage thrum, waiting for the light to flicker—a signal that something has been caught in the filtration grates.

### The Ghost-Mats
Drifting in the dead zones—the centers of the gargantuan chambers where the currents cancel each other out—are the **Sargasso-Grids**. These are not single plants, but vast, gelatinous rafts of translucent kelp. Because there is no seabed, these plants have no roots. Instead, they grow in geometric, woven patterns, mimicking the lattice-work of the iron walls. 

They are the lungs of the Megastructure. They glow with a faint, sickly green bioluminescence, feeding on the "salt-sleet" falling from above. They are heavy with oxygen, and because of this, they are the only places where "wild" life thrives. Small, translucent crabs and needle-thin eels live deep within the mats, hidden from the predators of the open void. To stumble upon a Ghost-Mat in the dark is like finding a floating island of neon in a vacuum.

### The Remnant Operators
Deep within the crevices of the bulkheads, near the steaming vent-pipes, there are rumors of the **Drowned Clergy**. These are not fish, but the descendants of the original construction crews or perhaps high-level maintenance drones that have achieved a state of biomechanical symbiosis. 

They are rarely seen, existing as silhouettes against the glowing apertures. They wear pressurized "skins" of heavy leaded-canvas and copper, fused to their own flesh by the immense pressure of the abyss. They do not swim so much as they *crawl* along the rivets, clutching massive wrenches and welding torches that have become extensions of their limbs. They do not speak; the water is too loud. They communicate by banging rhythmic codes onto the Hull—heavy, metallic clangs that can be heard for fifty miles, signaling to the turbines that the water is clean, the pressure is holding, and the Megastructure is satisfied.

They are the keepers of the machine, as much a part of the plumbing as the valves themselves. They do not dream of the surface, for in the Pelagic Megastructure, the only heaven is a functioning pump.
