In the **Cistern-Works of the Red Kiln**, identity is not a matter of ancestry, but of **Adhesion**. To live here is to be part of the structural integrity of the hive; to be a creature of the Red Kiln is to be a living sealant, a mobile gear, or a frantic gardener of the leak.

### The Kiln-Born (The Hand-Slayers)
The primary inhabitants are the Kiln-Born, a race of stunted, broad-shouldered humanoids whose skin has taken on the matte, porous texture of the terracotta they inhabit. They are not merely "builders"; they are the immune system of the Cistern-Works. 

Their bodies are adapted to the "baking warm" constant. They do not sweat as surface-dwellers do—that would be a waste of precious moisture. Instead, their skin exfiltrates a thick, waxy sebum that smells of bitter resin, moving with a ponderous, rhythmic grace. Their hands are their most remarkable feature: over-sized, with knuckles calloused into bone-hard mallets, used for "hand-slapping" the silt-paste into cracks before it can dry.

They live in vertiginous verticality. A Kiln-Born family might occupy a "shelf"—a three-foot-deep indentation in a main conduit wall—sleeping in stacks like cordwood to conserve body heat and space. They communicate in a language of percussive strikes, tapping on the pipes to send messages through the hollow geometry of the world. A sharp *clink-clink* means a leak has been found; a low, vibrating thrum is a call to rest.

### The Guild of the Valve-Watchers
Among the Kiln-Born, there is a priestly caste known as the **Valve-Watchers**. These individuals are thin, elongated, and often blind, their eyes clouded over by the amber soot of the tallow lamps. They possess a supernatural sensitivity to the "industrial roar." 

They sit motionless at the great bronze junctions, their bare feet pressed against the vibrating ceramic. They can "hear" a clog forming three miles away in a secondary vent. Using long, hooked bronze rods, they manipulate the heavy sluices, directing the flow of heat and moisture to the various "parishes" of the hive. To the Valve-Watchers, the Cisterns are a musical instrument, and their lives are spent tuning the friction of the world to a survivable pitch.

### The Commensal Vermin: The Marrow-Moths and Silt-Crabs
Life thrives in the shadows of the Kiln-Born’s labor. The **Marrow-Moths**—those hand-sized, pale-winged insects—are more than just decoration. They are the pollinators of the "ribcage fungi." Their wings are coated in a fine, caustic dust that, when shaken over the rust-colored moss, triggers a rapid bloom. The Kiln-Born treat these moths with a sacred indifference; they are the bringers of the "breathable air," and to kill one is to invite the "Dust-Choke."

The **Silt-Crabs** are the heavy laborers of the floor. Descendants of the "dinner-plate beetles," these crustaceans have evolved flat, spade-like limbs. They follow the repair crews, scuttling beneath the scaffolding to catch the falling shards of re-fired brick. They grind these fragments in their mandibles, turning waste back into the fine grit used for the mosaic floors. They are the living grinders of the engine, their shells polished to a mirror-sheen by centuries of scuttling through the clay dust.

### The Pipe-Runners (The Scavenger-Nodes)
The most frantic of the residents are the **Pipe-Runners**. Neither fully animal nor fully sentient, they exist in the "between spaces"—the interior of the pipes that are too small for the Kiln-Born. They are long, serpentine mammals with dozens of tiny, clawed feet that allow them to run upside down on the interior of ceramic conduits.

They are the couriers of the damp. They carry mouthfuls of water from the "intermittent damp" oases to the dry, crumbling edges of the world, spitting the moisture onto the parched brick to prevent structural fractures. They are driven by a collective, nervous compulsiveness; they do not play, they do not fight—they only *move*, a constant, rustling river of fur and spine that keeps the "Integrity of the Surface" intact.

### The "Stilled"
Deep in the most ancient, sun-hardened sectors, there are the **Stilled**. These are Kiln-Born who have reached the end of their lives. In the Red Kiln, there are no graves, for the ground is brick. Instead, a dying worker is coated in thick silt-paste and propped into a gap in a failing wall. As the heat of the world bakes the clay, the person becomes part of the architecture—a "living" buttress. 

Over decades, their form blurs into the geometry of the room. You can see them everywhere: a bulging joint that looks suspiciously like a ribcage, a decorative frieze that bears the distinct impression of a human face. They are the ultimate expression of the Realm’s rule: they have become the Surface. They no longer breathe, but they hold the ceiling up, ensuring that the warm, noisy, frantic dance of the Cistern-Works continues for those who still have the strength to slap the clay.
