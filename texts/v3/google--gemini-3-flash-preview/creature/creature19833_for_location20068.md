To understand what lives here, one must first understand the **Hooked Hand**. In a world where a lapse in grip is a permanent departure, the anatomy of every living thing is a study in tension.

### The Low-Gravity Gardeners: The *Vela*
The inhabitants of the woven hut are not giants, nor are they fragile. They are the *Vela*, a people who have become extensions of the limestone itself. They are long-limbed and deceptively lean, their skin a pale, pebbled grey that mimics the wet limestone of the Cloud-Shelf. To look at a Vela is to see a creature designed for the vertical: their toes are as long as fingers, prehensile and tipped with thick, blunt nails for finding purchase in the rock-flutes.

They do not walk; they flow. They move with a rhythmic, four-point contact, rhythmically tapping the stone to sense vibrations—a vital skill, as the roar of the wind-organs makes vocal speech a luxury for the indoors. When they do speak, it is in a series of sharp, percussive whistles that cut through the bass of the mountain.

The Vela are obsessed with "Tethering." Throughout their lives, they weave. Their clothing is not for modesty but for utility—a complex harness of braided vine and the hair of their ancestors, constantly clipped to the "Life-Lines" that crisscross the shelf. A Vela child learns to knot a safety line before they learn to feed themselves. They view the open air not as a void, but as a predator that is always trying to pull them away.

### The Symbiotes of the Flutes: The *Bellows-Frogs*
Living within the very hollows of the limestone apertures are the "iridescent amphibians" mentioned in the stone’s song. These are the *Bellow-Frogs*, creatures the size of a human fist with skin like oil-slicks. They have evolved to live inside the flutes, using the wind that roars through the rock to amplify their mating calls.

They do not possess lungs in the traditional sense; their bodies are hollow resonators. As the wind pours through the limestone, they shift their positions within the pipes to change the pitch of the mountain’s roar. The "rhythmic thrum" is no accident—the Vela and the frogs have a pact. The frogs keep the flutes clear of debris and predatory insects, and in return, the Vela carve "performance notches" into the stone to help the frogs achieve the specific, haunting frequencies that drive the colony’s reproductive cycles.

### The Gliders of the Emerald Dreadlocks: *The Skimmer-Kin*
The "Sky-skimmers" are the frantic heartbeat of the shelf. Imagine a creature that is half-bat, half-heron, with umbrella-ribbed wings that never fully fold. They are the messengers between the isolated cathedral-platforms. 

The Skimmers are "Updraft-Eaters." They don't fly by flapping; they hang their leathery membranes into the thermal rising from the purple depths and let the earth push them upward. They cling to the hanging vines with hooked spurs on their wing-elbows, harvesting the succulent water-blooms. To the Vela, the Skimmers are sacred cattle. The "spirit-poles" are topped with their feathers not just as decoration, but as weather vanes. By watching the orientation of the feathers, the Vela know exactly when a "Cloud-Burst"—a violent surge of upward air—is coming, signaling a time to retreat to their huts and double-tether.

### The Weaver beneath the Shelf: The *Mist-Striders*
In the shadows beneath the shelf, where the "emerald dreadlocks" trail off into the infinite void, lives something larger and more patient. The *Mist-Striders* are spindly, six-legged arthropods with legs as thin as reeds and bodies the color of the bruised-purple atmosphere below. 

They are the janitors of the abyss. They cling to the underside of the limestone, casting "gravity-nets"—fine, sticky structures made of hardened foam—that catch the detritus falling from above. Seeds, insects, and occasionally a fallen Sky-skimmer are caught in these nets. The Vela fear the Striders but respect them; when a Vela dies, they are not buried—there is no soil—instead, they are given to the underside, gifted to the Striders to be recycled back into the cycle of the Vertical Garden.

### The Apex: The *Glint-Wraith*
High above the hut, in the narrowest fractures where the wind turns into a lethal scream, dwells the Shelf’s only true predator: the *Glint-Wraith*. It is almost entirely transparent, a creature of cartilage and shimmering, glass-like scales. It does not use sight; it feels the "vibration of the stone" that the Vela are so attuned to. It moves only when the wind is loudest, using the roar of the limestone organs to mask the sound of its approach. It is the reason the Vela huts are "plastered with mud and crushed shell"—to harden the walls against the Wraith’s serrated glass talons.

In this place, life is a frantic, beautiful, and loud negotiation with the abyss. Everything—from the smallest frog to the hermit in the hut—is vibrating in time with the mountain, singing a defiant song against the pull of the emptiness below.
