To understand what lives here, one must first discard the idea of "nature" as something distinct from "utility." In the Glandular Intake, life is a byproduct of function. The inhabitants are the **Second Crew**, a lineage of organisms that have evolved not in the wild, but in the gaps between specifications.

### The Weaver-Children (The Intimate Architects)
They are not human, but they possess a human-like intentionality. These are the **Solder-Wights**. Standing barely fourteen inches tall, they are spindly, multifaceted bipeds with limbs like jeweler’s tools. Their skin is the color of oxidized nickel—a dull, matte grey that allows them to vanish against the backdrop of the manifolds. 

They do not speak; they communicate by tapping out rhythmic codes on the brass pipes, sending vibrations through the "floor" that other Wights can feel miles away through their sensitive, needle-like feet. They consider themselves the curators of the Flow. You might see one huddled in a "corner" created by three intersecting conduits, its many-jointed fingers working a piece of discarded copper wire into a delicate, geometric cage. They don't build homes; they build *stasis*. They wedge their bodies into the gaps between hot and cold lines to regulate their own internal temperatures, becoming living gaskets that breathe in rhythm with the steam-hiss.

### The Parasitic Sentinels
Living in the shadow of the Solder-Wights are the **Pulse-Leeches**. These are flat, rubbery discs, the size of a dinner plate, that adhere to the most vibrant glass conduits. They are almost perfectly transparent, save for a central, glowing "eye" that is actually a primitive photocell. 

They are the ultimate opportunists of the Intake. They don't hunt; they wait for a Solder-Mite to grow too heavy with scavenged lead, or for a Lubricant-Slug to stall. When a creature dies or slows, the Leech slides over it like a wet shroud, dissolving the casing with a specialized acidic sweat that smells sharply of vinegar and burnt rubber. They are the reason the "Young Erosion" persists; they are the cleaners, the polishers, the ones who eat the age so the machine always looks new.

### The Ghost-In-The-Light
If you watch the Filament-Moths long enough, you realize they are not independent actors. They are part of a localized consciousness known as **The Hum-Singers**. These aren't singular animals, but colonies of microscopic spores that inhabit the wings of the moths. 

When the moths gather in tight clusters around the yellow lamps, the spores begin to glow in sympathetic vibration. This creates a "Visual Static"—a shimmering, holographic haze that hangs in the air. If you stare into the haze, you might see faces, or schematics, or the flickering memories of the technicians who once drank from the ceramic tea-cups. The Hum-Singers are the keepers of the facility’s "memory," a biological hard drive that stores the history of every pressure spike and valve failure in the shimmer of a moth’s wing.

### The Apex Caretaker: The "Valve-Mother"
Deep within the most claustrophobic thickets of the copper forest, there is a presence that the Solder-Mites avoid. This is the **Valve-Mother**. She is less a creature and more a biological encrustation. Over decades, a rogue AI or a misplaced organic sample has fused with a primary steam-manifold. 

She appears as a mass of wet, purple-veined marble, pulsing in time with the facility’s heartbeat. Long, prehensile "tongues"—actually repurposed hydraulic hoses covered in soft, sensory cilia—reach out from her mass to groom the wicking-ferns. She is the source of the dampness. She manipulates the relief valves with an intuitive grace, orchestrating the "intermittent rain" to ensure her garden of ferns and mites thrives. She is the deity of this pocket-scale world, a silent, unmoving queen who breathes through the vents and thinks in the language of pressure gradients.

### The Relationship of the Occupants
There is no "wildlife" here, only a **symphony of maintenance**. 
*   The **Solder-Mites** strip the rust to keep the joints turning. 
*   The **Lubricant-Slugs** ensure the gears never grind. 
*   The **Solder-Wights** redirect the stray wires to prevent shorts. 
*   The **Pulse-Leeches** recycle the dead.

You, crouching in the heat and the copper-smell, are the only thing that doesn't have a job. To the inhabitants of the Glandular Intake, you are a "System Friction"—a large, clumsy anomaly that produces too much heat and doesn't polish a single pipe. They don't fear you; they are waiting for you to stop moving, so the Leeches can begin the work of reclaiming your components for the Flow.
