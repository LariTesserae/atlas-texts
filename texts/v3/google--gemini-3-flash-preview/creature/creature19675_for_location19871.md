Within this thrumming, verdant pressure-cooker, the inhabitants are not distinct from the canopy; they are its most mobile metabolic expressions. 

### The Ascended (The Humid-Folk)
The beings who dwell in the hanging hamlets are long-limbed and spindly, possessing a skeletal structure that is less like bone and more like the "iron-wood" they harvest. To look at them is to see the evolution of the primate into something more akin to a spider or a gibbon of the high clouds. Their skin is a mottled chartreuse or deep copper, waxy to the touch to prevent desiccation in the punishing heat, and capable of a slow, chromatophoric shift that allows them to blend into the dappled shade of a leaf-shadow.

They do not walk; they flow. Their feet have prehensile, elongated toes, and their "palms" are padded with a microscopic, hook-like cilia that allows them to cling to the slick, vertical barks of the Great Pillars even during the violent mist-falls. 

They are a people of the "Breath." Because the oxygen is so dense, their lung capacity is massive, manifesting in broad, barrel-chested torsos that taper down to incredibly thin waists. They speak in a series of high-pitched whistles and percussive clicks that cut through the roar of the stridulating insects—a language that mimics the very environment they inhabit. Their culture is one of "The Great Descent Avoidance." To fall is their only concept of hell; their religion focuses on the *Up-Draft*, believing that the souls of the dead are caught in the rising heat and carried to the Shatter-Peaks to become stars.

### The Sky-Whales (The Sieve-Bellows)
Hovering in the yawning abysses between the pillars are the *Sieve-Bellows*. These are the largest organisms in the High Canopy Sea, floating islands of mottled flesh the size of galleons. They have no wings, but rather internal bladders filled with the lighter-than-air gases produced by the fermentation of the forest below. 

They are filter-feeders of the atmosphere. They drift with the updrafts, their mouths—gaping, baleen-lined maws—permanently open to catch the "upward snow" of pollen, seeds, and smaller insects. They are gentle, slow-moving entities, their skin crusted with its own micro-ecosystem of moss and parasitic vines. The Humid-Folk often hunt them with harpoons made of thorn-wood, not for meat, but for their gas bladders, which can be harvested to create floating platforms for their hamlets.

### The Carapace-Knights (The Suture-Wasps)
If the Humid-Folk are the parasites, the *Suture-Wasps* are the immune system. These are the hound-sized, jewel-toned insects that dominate the middle strata. They possess a terrifying, clockwork intelligence. They do not merely kill; they "manage." They have been seen "pruning" the canopy, severing vines that threaten the structural integrity of a bridge-trunk, or culling herds of smaller gliders to prevent overgrazing. 

They are the primary predators of the Humid-Folk, but they are also their source of industry. The tribes harvest the discarded molts of these wasps to create their armor and tools. A warrior of the hamlets is only as strong as the iridescent chitin they have scavenged from a wasp-hollow.

### The Lure-Lords (The Apex Mimics)
Deep within the "pitcher-wells" lives something that never sees the sun. The *Lure-Lords* are stationary predators, cephalopod-like entities that have fused themselves into the boles of the trees. They extend long, translucent filaments into the water of the wells, which vibrate to mimic the sound of a drowning insect or the cry of a distressed infant. 

When a creature leans in to drink or investigate, the "well" reveals itself to be a mouth. These creatures are the keepers of the deep history; having lived for centuries in the same spot, they are rumored to be the only beings that can hear the "heartbeat" of the Great Pillars. Some Humid-Folk shamans offer sacrifices to the wells in exchange for "The Sap-Vision"—a hallucinogenic state brought on by the Lure-Lord’s venom.

### The Drifting Spore-Wights
Finally, there are the "Wights"—creatures that have ceased to be animals and have become entirely fungal. These are the unlucky ones who fell, but were caught by a lower branch or a thick mat of moss before vanishing into the dark. Infested by the aggressive spores of the under-canopy, they wander the horizontal trunk-bridges like sleepwalkers. 

They are covered in fruiting bodies and weeping shelves of mushroom. They no longer eat; they absorb the fermentation heat of the forest. They are the heralds of the "Dark Green," the creeping realization that in this place, the line between *living inhabitant* and *botanical architecture* is a thin, crumbling leaf. They serve as a silent reminder to the tribes: you do not own the canopy. The canopy is simply waiting to digest you.
