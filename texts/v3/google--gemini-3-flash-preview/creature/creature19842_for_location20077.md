In the Lignin-Gorge, life does not simply inhabit the space; it is a violent extension of the space. Every entity is a variation on the theme of friction, thirst, and structural integrity.

### The Megafauna: The Architects of the Tumble
Dominating the vertical corridors are the **Litho-Titans**, gargantuans that look less like animals and more like mobile hillocks of cracked cedar. They have no soft tissue; their limbs are ball-and-socket joints of polished ironwood, lubricated by a fine, graphite-like dust. They move with an agonizing slowness that defies the frantic pace of the smaller swarms, acting as living islands for lesser species. Their mouths are wide, stationary rasps that they press against the cathedral-trunks, grinding away the "velocity growth" of the trees to keep the migration paths open. To a Litho-Titan, a minute is a blink, and their "thoughts" are likely just long-form vibrations traveling through their subterranean marrow.

Beneath these giants dwell the **Scissor-Gliders**. These are the "thirty-foot moth-analogs" mentioned, but up close, they are more terrifying and functional. They lack velvet; their wings are sheets of thin, translucent cellulose that vibrate so fast they create localized heat-shimmers. They do not drink; they "inhale" the pollen-rich atmosphere through vents in their thoraxes, filtering the dry nutrients through internal combs of stiff cilia. They are the apex predators of the thermal drafts, dropping from the high ceiling-trunks like falling knives to impale smaller "drifters" on barbed, chitinous proboscises.

### The Micro-Ecosystem: The Bark-Bleeders
Every square inch of the great trunks is a battlefield for the **Resin-Wasps**. These have lost the need for stingers in favor of "acid-mandibles" that can liquefy the petrified amber of the substrate. They are the sculptors of the gorge. They build bulging, hexagonal hives that hang like tumors from the underside of the horizontal trunks. Within these hives, they store "thick air"—densely packed clouds of concentrated spores that serve as a fermented food source.

Living parasitically among them are the **Grip-Snakes**, serpents of woven fiber and dry scale. They have no eyes. Instead, their entire bodies are covered in millions of microscopic hooks, similar to burrs. They do not slither; they "click" across the wood, their scales snapping into the grain of the bark like a ratchet. They hunt by sensing the "Great Hum"—detecting the sub-harmonic dissonance of an injured creature’s thrashing against the background roar of the world.

### The Weavers: The Fragile Intelligence
The beings who dwell in the hollowed-out galls—the **Lignites**—are parched, spindly creatures that seem to be an evolutionary bridge between primate and insect. Their skin is the color of scorched parchment, toughened into a natural callus to protect against the abrasive "silt-winds." They have four long, multi-jointed fingers on each hand, tipped with iron-hard nails used for constant, neurotic climbing.

The Lignites do not communicate with sound—the Great Hum is too loud for vocal cords to compete. Instead, they use "Vibration-Signing." By striking the resonant chambers of their hollowed-out tree-dwellings or plucking the "sound-traps" of taut gut, they send ripples through the wood. A Lignite "mother" calls her child by hammering a specific rhythm into the floorboards of their gall, a message that travels for miles through the interconnected lattice of the forest.

Their culture is one of **constant maintenance**. A Lignite is never seen without a "rasp-stone" or a beetle-mandible blade. They are the gardeners of the apocalypse; their entire existence is dedicated to carving back the encroaching vines that threaten to crush their homes. They view the "Growth" not as a gift, but as an enemy that must be appeased and pruned. Their religion involves the "Stillness"—a mythical state of existence where the wood stops growing and the Hum falls silent. To a Lignite, "Heaven" is a place of quiet, cold stone where nothing ever moves.

### The Ghost-Life: The Spore-Shadows
Finally, there are the **Phantoms of the Silt**. These are not singular organisms, but hive-mind colonies of the airborne fungi. Sometimes, the shimmering dust in the air becomes so dense that it achieves a brief, flickering consciousness. These "Spore-Shadows" take the shape of whatever they encounter—mimicking the form of a Lignite or a Scissor-Glider for a few minutes before the turbulence of the Gorge tears them apart. They are the only things in the Lignin-Gorge that do not struggle; they simply drift, ephemeral and golden, the beautiful, dying breath of a world that is growing too fast for its own soul.
