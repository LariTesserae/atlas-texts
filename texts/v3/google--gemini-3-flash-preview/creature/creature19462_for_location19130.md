In this city of gravitational stitches and weeping stone, the inhabitants are not residents in any sense understood by those who walk on dirt. They are the **Precipitants**.

### The Drift-Lords (The Aerial Biomass)
The teeming, sky-filling roar is composed of beings that are more "weather" than "animal." These are the **Vane-Squid** and the **Glass-Bellows**. They possess bodies of semi-solidified gas, held together by a flickering internal magnetism. They do not have skeletons; they have "lattices"—internal webs of conductive silver thread that pulse with the stolen heat of the city's spires.

As they drift through the thick, caloric mist, they act as living filters. Their skin is a series of microscopic, vibrating cilia that catch the moisture and strip the kinetic energy from every falling droplet. To touch one would be to touch a freezing, electrified lung. They have no eyes—light is a foreign concept—but they possess rows of sensory pits that "see" the world as a map of thermal vibrations. When they collide in the dark, they do not fight; they merge for a moment, exchanging data and electrical charge through a wet, slapping mending of their membranes, before tearing apart again to continue their slow, aimless navigation of the violet-black sky.

### The Architects of the Stillness
Below the frantic sky, within the translucent spires themselves, live the **Statists**. If the drifters are the noise of the world, the Statists are its silence. 

They are the "citizens" of the geometric monoliths, but they are barely distinguishable from the furniture of their own lives. A Statist is a creature of high-density mineral and cold, circulating gel. They are tall, multi-jointed, and impossibly thin, their limbs resembling the very basalt shards of the mountains. They do not walk; they adhere. They move across the vertical faces of the hard-light laboratories like slow-moving frost, their bodies blending into the crystalline textures of the walls.

A Statist may spend forty years in a single postural meditation, their fingers fused to a control interface that is slowly rewriting the molecular lattice of the building. They do not eat. They "transpire." They press their flat, porous chests against the cold, weeping walls of the city and absorb the "caloric" energy harvested by the spires from the freezing atmosphere. They are a race of living heat-sinks, processing the friction of the world into the cold logic of their slow-growth civilization.

### The Parasitic Low-Life
In the "streets"—those hairline fractures of negative space where the condensation pools into slushy, viscous bogs—lives a different kind of inhabitant. These are the **Grit-Lickers** and the **Tether-Flies**.

The Grit-Lickers are heavy, armored gastropods the size of boulder-fragments. They are the only things that truly interact with the raw, serrated basalt. They have no mouths, only abrasive underbellies that vibrate at such a high frequency they can liquify the surface of the obsidian-slick minerals. They drink the stone. They move with an agonizing, geologic patience, leaving behind trails of pearl-white waste that the city’s spires later "inhale" to use as building material.

The Tether-Flies are the only things that move fast. They are tiny, sparking gnats made of pure electrical discharge. They are the "scavengers" of the hard-light fields. When a gravitational stitch flickers or a building reconfigures its geometry, the Tether-Flies swarm the site to feed on the leaking coherence. They look like a cloud of blue static, a frantic, buzzing halo that illuminates the sharp edges of the world for a fraction of a second before the darkness swallows them again.

### The Singular Mind
Deep within the "subterranean" violet-black, where the city’s roots meet the unyielding bone of the planet, there is a sense of a larger presence. The rhythmic throb of the bioluminescent circuitry suggests that the city and its inhabitants might be a single, distributed organism. 

The roar of the sky-creatures is the heartbeat; the stillness of the Statists is the thought-process; the sharp basalt is the anchor. There is no individual "life" here—only a massive, cold, and wet metabolism that has decided to exist in a place where existence should be impossible. They are a people who have traded the sun for the hum of the machine, and the soil for the endurance of the stone. They are the children of the long dawn, forever waiting for a sun that they have long since forgotten how to see.
