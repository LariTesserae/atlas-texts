The inhabitants of this drowned height are not "subjects" of the architecture, nor are they its masters; they are its slow, biological residue. They are the **Liturgists of the Sluice**, a race of beings whose ancestors may have once had bones of calcium, but whose frames have long since been replaced by the very mineral-rich brine they inhabit.

### The Weaver-Kin
The primary inhabitants are elongated, pale figures that exist in a state of perpetual, vertical suspension. Their bodies are translucent, like smoked glass, revealing internal organs that lilt like delicate clockwork within a fluid-filled chest cavity. They possess no eyes—the thin, sharp air of the ceiling pockets is of no use to them, and the bruised-red light of the deep is felt rather than seen. Instead, their skin is a sophisticated sensory array, covered in millions of microscopic, vibrating cilia that "hear" the shape of the water.

They do not have hands in the traditional sense. Their limbs end in clusters of prehensile, ribbon-like filaments, hardened at the tips into keratinous hooks. These allow them to grip the ropes of braided hair and sinew without the need for muscular exertion. When they move, they do so with the agonizing patience of a glacier, hooking a filament into a ceramic groove and hauling their buoyant mass upward through the heavy silk of the reservoir.

### The Shell-Brood
Cringing against the soft, mammary curves of the basalt walls are the **Artisans**. These are the half-mollusk, half-mechanism creatures you perceived—creatures that are essentially living valve-shuts. They are fused to the architecture. Their shells are not grown from within but are grafted from the fired ceramic of the city’s debris. 

They are the city’s immune system and its municipal engineering. They feed by filtering the mineral-heavy water, and in doing so, they excrete a thick, resinous grout that repairs the cracks in the stone. To look at them is to see a grotesque union of flesh and masonry; a soft, pulsing mantle protruding from a perfectly geometric ceramic housing. They communicate via low-frequency vibrations that travel through the stone, a rhythmic booming that the Liturgists interpret as a map of the current pressures within the mountain.

### The Silent Cleric-Schools
The pale, sightless fish are the repository of the city’s memory. They move in interlocking spirals, their clicking scales acting as a primitive binary code. As they brush against the pockmarked granite of the statues, they "read" the erosion with their flanks. The schools do not move randomly; they follow the ancient sluice-ways in a specific order, carrying "data" from the lower, warmer depths to the high, cold vaults near the ceiling. When two schools meet in an archway, the ensuing clatter of scales is a frantic exchange of information—a sensory census of the reservoir’s heat and salinity.

### The Prime Movers
Deep in the vertical shafts, where the pressure is most profound and the water is thickest with salt, live the **Keepers of the Crank**. These are the largest of the inhabitants, massive and sprawling, resembling a cross between a giant Isopod and a star-nosed mole. They are almost entirely muscle, anchored to the massive gear-works of timber and bone. 

They do not think; they only feel the tension in the ropes of hair. When a rope tightens from a high chamber, a Keeper leans its massive weight into the levers, walking a blind, eternal circle around a stone axle. They are the engines of this world, pivoting the enormous sluices that keep the thermal currents circulating. They are so integrated into the machinery that their own carapaces have grown over the wooden beams, becoming living counterweights in a system that has functioned for ten thousand years without a single spark of fire.

### Their Philosophy of Being
To the inhabitants of this place, there is no such thing as "doing." There is only "being at a different depth." They have no concept of time as a linear progression; time is merely the cycle of the water’s warmth. They do not build, for the building is finished. They do not explore, for the reservoir is absolute. 

They live in a state of **Submerged Grace**. A Liturgist may spend forty years drifting from one side of a vaulted gallery to the other, their filaments trailing behind them like the tail of a comet. In this place, the highest form of existence is to be perfectly still—to become so synonymous with the warm, salty dark that the distinction between the flesh of the inhabitant and the stone of the mountain finally, mercifully, dissolves. 

They are the ghosts of a civilization that climbed as high as it could, found only water, and decided that it was enough to simply sink into the warmth of the end.
