The inhabitants of this vertical monolith do not understand the concept of "distance," only "displacement." To them, a journey is not measured in miles, but in atmospheres and the changing pitch of the great flywheels. They are the **Tenders**, a people whose biology has been sculpted by the relentless verticality and the specific, rhythmic demands of the Spire.

To look at a Tender is to see a study in functional elegance. They are long-limbed and lean, their skeletal structures dense enough to withstand the crushing thermal mass of the lower tiers, yet their movements possess a slow, deliberate fluidity. Their skin has taken on the properties of the environment—a tough, slightly waxy texture, often pigmented in shades of ochre, copper, or pale porcelain to better reflect the unwavering glare of the sun. They do not sweat as those from flatter worlds might; instead, they possess a complex network of cutaneous capillaries that allow them to absorb the "intermittent moisture" of the wet zones through their skin, much like the mosses they cultivate.

### The Culture of the Grip
The most striking feature of the inhabitants is their hands and feet. The fingers are exceptionally long, tipped with calloused pads that have developed a specialized, micro-ridged texture. This is not a mutation, but an ancient, engineered inheritance—a "biological adhesive" that allows them to traverse smooth brass railings and wet porcelain tiles with absolute security. Their feet are prehensile, the big toe set wide to grip cable-ways and the edges of iron struts. 

In this world, to fall is the only true sin. Consequently, their entire social structure is built on the "Line." It is rare to see a Tender without a harness of braided silk and copper wire, a "Life-Thread" that connects them to the structural ribs of their home. These lines are decorated with beads and charms that tell the history of their lineage: which cooling fan their ancestors maintained, which orchid-walk they were born in, and how many cycles they have spent ascending.

### The Guilds of Function
Society is organized not by ideology, but by the mechanical system one serves. 

*   **The Valve-Keepers:** Living in the humid, thrumming centers of the wet zones, these individuals are heavy-set and slow-moving. They have developed an intuitive sense of pressure; they can hear a micro-fracture in a condensation pipe through a mile of solid masonry. They speak in low, subsonic hums that mimic the drone of the flywheels, a language of vibrations felt in the chest.
*   **The Glaziers:** They dwell on the outer edges, where the porcelain is brightest and the sun is deadliest. They wear heavy, polarized goggles of smoked quartz and move with a frantic, insectile grace. Their task is to scour the algae from the glass conduits and ensure the flat, shadowless brilliance of the spire remains untarnished. They are the most avian of the Tenders, often training the long-tailed, mechanical-looking birds to carry messages or tools across the dizzying chasms between tiers.
*   **The Sowers:** Engineers of the aggressive green, the Sowers manage the "respirating" lungs of the city. They carry canisters of nutrient-slurry and pruning hooks of tempered brass. To a Sower, a plant is a component. They graft ferns into ventilation shafts to act as filters and train the iridescent mosses to insulate the heat-sensitive electrical cores. They are stained permanently emerald by the chlorophyll-rich sap of their charges.

### Life in the Slow Thrum
The Tenders live at the pace of the elevators. Their patience is absolute. They can stand on a moving platform for six hours without speaking, watching the golden haze shift against the porcelain walls. This is not boredom, but a state of "structural meditation." They are attuned to the health of the machine; they know when a turbine is misaligned by the way the moisture beads on their skin or the slight change in the scent of hot oil.

Religion, for them, is the **Maintenance of Equilibrium**. They do not worship a god, but the "Established State." They believe that as long as the brass is polished and the water moves up, the sun will not consume them. Death is not an exit, but a transformation—the bodies of the deceased are reclaimed by the Sowers, processed in the great nutrient-vats, and returned to the system as the very moss and ferns that keep the air breathable.

### The Commensals
Living alongside the Tenders are the creatures that have filled the empty niches of the machine. The "Suction-Primates" are prized companions, used to carry wires through conduits too narrow for a human. There is a deep, unspoken bond between the Tenders and the dinner-plate beetles; the insects act as mobile waste-disposal units, consuming the organic detritus of the market-tiers. 

In the high, thin air of the upper terraces, the inhabitants have become something more than human and something less than gods. They are the living pulse of the Spire, a population that has forgotten the earth entirely, content to live as elegant, climbing ghosts in a cathedral of brass and steam. Their eyes, always cast slightly upward, reflect the white-hot sun, searching for the next tier, the next valve, the next breath of recycled rain.
