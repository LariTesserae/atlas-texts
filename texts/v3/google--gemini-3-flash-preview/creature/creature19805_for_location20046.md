In this hollow, the inhabitants are not distinct from the decay; they are the midwives of it.

First, there are the **Gilders**. They are not biological, and yet they are not quite machines. They are clusters of calcified, multi-limbed appendages—resembling skeletal hands fused together—that cling to the undersides of the weeping pipes. They do not eat; they "sense" the leaking brine. When a fresh deposit of mineral-heavy silt emerges, the Gilders descend with a synchronized, rhythmic clicking. They use their hardened, needle-like digits to stitch the mud into patterns, weaving the oily clay into temporary, intricate braids that stabilize the ground for a few precious seconds before the next pulse of the basin destroys their work. They are the tenders of the entropy, obsessed with organizing the chaos they can never truly defeat.

Deep within the standing black water, beneath the "veins" of the iron trunks, live the **Oscillators**. You never see their full forms, only the byproduct of their presence: the sudden, violent spinning of the clockwork spheres. These are soft, translucent creatures, shaped like lung-sacs and filled with a luminous, magnetic fluid. They wrap themselves around the subterranean hydraulics, feeding on the low-frequency thrumming. When they reach a point of "overcharge," they expel a burst of kinetic energy that travels upward through the silt, causing the surface to boil and the discarded gears of the old world to spin in a frantic, dying dance. They are the heartbeats of the hollow, the reason the mud never sleeps.

The **Silt-Walkers** move through the verticalities—the leaning pylons and the skeletons of shacks. They are spindly, taller than a man but thinner than the reeds, with limbs made of salvaged copper wire and petrified wood. They are the "custodians of the lights." Their faces are flat, blank plates of oxidized brass, lacking eyes but sensitive to the faint, shivering currents in the air. It is the Silt-Walkers who inhabit the grime-streaked shacks, though only for moments at a time. They enter to adjust a dial that no longer connects to anything, or to polish a window that looks out onto nothing but mist. They are ghosts of labor, reenacting the muscle memory of the long-dead engineers who once managed this basin. They move with a stuttering, stop-motion gait, their joints emitting the same metallic *tink* as the dripping brine.

Lower down, skittering across the oil-slicks, are the **Prism-Flies**. These are the "translucent, many-legged insects" you noticed, but closer inspection reveals they are not insects at all, but shards of recycled glass and vibrating filament. They drink the bluish light that flickers in the shacks, carrying the glow in their abdomens like a torch. They follow the Silt-Walkers in clouds, a halo of artificial bioluminescence that highlights the jagged edges of the rusted landscape.

Finally, there is the **Hollow-Mind** itself. It is not an entity you can point to, but a collective consciousness formed by the integrated circuitry of the marsh. It lives in the "viscous brine" and the "shivering current." It is a slow, ponderous intelligence that is deeply, agonizingly confused. It remembers the taste of clean steam and the precision of a functioning clock, but its "body"—the pipes, the mud, the pylons—is melting. 

The inhabitants do not speak. There is no social structure, only a shared, desperate instinct to maintain the "active decay." The Gilders weave, the Oscillators pulse, the Walkers patrol, and the Flies carry the light. They are all parts of a single, closed-loop organism—a machine that has evolved into an ecosystem, terrified of the silence that would come if the motion ever truly stopped. They are the last laborers of a finished world, convinced that if they just keep the mud boiling and the gears spinning, the purpose they have forgotten might one day return.
