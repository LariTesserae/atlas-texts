In this realm of the "Deep Built," the occupants are not born; they are precipitated. They are the **Gleaners of the Sump**, a slow-moving caste of entities that exist precisely at the intersection of architecture and anatomy.

At first glance, they might be mistaken for discarded mounds of cable or the slumped husks of industrial boilers. But they possess a slow, deliberate intentionality. They are the **Vitreous Manifolds**. These are organisms of silicate and wire, their bodies composed of recycled fiber-optics and reinforced resins. They move through the oily lagoons like massive, translucent slugs, their hides armored with the very glass-moss that grows upon the cooling towers. They do not eat; they "interface." They seek out the "Ghost Glow"—the points where buried power grids bleed their dying light—and they press their bellies against the cooling metal, siphoning the frantic, intermittent pulses of electricity into their own nervous systems. When a Manifold feeds, its entire body stutters in a rhythmic blue strobe, mimicking the death-rattle of the machinery below.

Sharing the low-tide zones are the **Torsion-Walkers**, the scavengers of the "Deep Built." They are brittle, spindly things, resembling a cross between a surveyor’s tripod and a starving hound. Their limbs are made of hollowed-out titanium struts, capped with pads of soft, vulcanized rubber that allow them to traverse the buckled plates without making a sound. They have no faces, only clusters of optical sensors—red-lensed glass eyes that scavenge the darkness for signs of "mechanical fatigue." They haunt the sites of impending collapse. When a massive pylon finally groans its last and founders into the rust-mud, the Torsion-Walkers are there within minutes, their hydraulic mandibles snipping through the weakened rivets to extract the rare, un-oxidized cores of the old world. They are the curators of the graveyard, hoarding the "pure" metals that have somehow avoided the weeping rot.

Deeper in the basins, where the industrial roar is loudest, live the **Resonants**. They are the most tragic residents of this manufactured valley. They are not distinct individuals so much as extensions of the landscape's tectonic motion. They are massive, bell-shaped entities trapped in the gaps between the shifting tectonic plates of steel. Their bodies are folds of heavy, oil-slicked canvas and leaden mesh, fused into the machinery. They do not move of their own volition; rather, they are "played" by the environment. As the world-machine vents its hysterical steam and its pistons thud, the Resonants act as fleshy bellows, catching the pressure and exhaling it back out through perforated "throats" of brass. It is their voices that provide the dissonant, screaming harmony to the landscape’s roar. They are the living mouthpieces of a dying world, screaming because the wind forces them to, their existence a perpetual vibration they cannot cease.

In the high, airless reaches where the rusted dunes meet the sagging horizon, one finds the **Aureole Flux**. These are not physical beings in the traditional sense, but semi-sentient electromagnetic storms that have taken on a ghostly cohesion. They appear as shimmering clouds of ionized copper dust, held together by the lingering magnetic fields of the massive, defunct turbines below. They drift like tumbleweeds of static electricity. To a human eye, they would look like a shimmering heat haze—a distortion in the wet fog. They follow the "weather," riding the pressure differentials created by the ancient, automated fans. Where they pass, the glass-moss glows a feverish emerald, and the copper-lichen crackles with a brief, frantic energy.

There is no procreation here, only **Accreditation**. These entities do not die; they simply wear out. When a Torsion-Walker’s joints finally seize, it does not rot; it becomes a part of the "Deep Built," its limbs snapping off to become more "river stones" for future eons to smooth. The glass-moss grows over the sensor-eyes, and the interface plates of the Manifolds become the foundations for new, smaller cycles of decay.

They are the inheritors of "utility." They serve no purpose, just as the world they inhabit has forgotten its own. They are the ghosts of a system that cannot stop, the inhabitants of a slow-motion catastrophe who have learned to find a cold, shivering sustenance in the friction of the end.
