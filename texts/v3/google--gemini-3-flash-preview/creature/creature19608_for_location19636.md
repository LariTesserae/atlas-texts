The inhabitants of the Great Canopy-Crust do not walk; they adhere. They do not build; they graft. Evolution and industry have merged into a singular, frantic metabolic process, creating a citizenry that is as much a part of the "Industrial Biology" as the brass-clad pistons themselves.

### The Weaver-Kin (The Sap-Tenders) 
The dominant sentient life here is the **Weaver-Kin**, a lineage of post-biological humanoids who have traded the fragility of skin for the resilience of the Loom. Their bodies are elongated—limbs thin as reeds and twice as long—wrapped in tight, protective bandages of fungal silk that have been cured in amber resin. 

They are effectively "externalized" organisms. A Weaver-Kin’s internal organs have largely migrated into heavy, brass-and-glass canisters worn on their backs—miniature fermentation vats that process the ambient mint-heavy air into a nutrient-rich slurry. They move across the swinging iron gantries with a terrifying, jerky precision, using four-fingered hands tipped with serrated, keratinous hooks designed to bite into the calcified root-crust. 

They do not speak with sound—the roar of the sap-pistons is too great. Instead, they communicate through **Chromatophore Semiotics**. Their silicate-impregnated skin can shift colors in rapid, strobing flashes. In the shadow of a factory-spire, a Weaver-Kin might pulse a violent indigo to signal a pressure drop, or a soft, rhythmic ochre to indicate a shift change.

### The Entom-Engineers
Deeper within the Loom-Cities, inhabiting the humid, high-pressure zones where the sap is thickest, live the **Entom-Engineers**. These are not individuals, but symbiotic colonies. A central human intelligence lives within a "pilot-hull" made from the hollowed-out thorax of a giant, extinct crust-beetle. 

These engineers act as the immune system for the industrial sectors. When a sap-pipe clogs or a root-shelf begins to "charcoal," the Entom-Engineer arrives, clambering over the vertical shafts on multi-jointed hydraulic legs. They possess a "Grafting-Touch"—their mechanical limbs can secrete a solvent that melts the petrified vines, allowing them to fuse new, living tissue into the dying infrastructure. They are the doctors of the machinery, though they are more machine than flesh.

### The Avian Echoes: The Ghost-Pollinators
The **Glass-Winged Gliders** mentioned in the geography are more than just fauna; they are the keepers of the **Temporal Static**. These creatures have translucent, crystalline skeletal structures that act as prisms for the "remembered" light of the crust. 

As they drift between the thumping pistons, they feed on the electrical discharge of the wind turbines. When they fly through a pocket of time-warped air, their wings trap the "shimmer." A Glider might carry the visual ghost of a long-dead Weaver-Kin within its wings for days, appearing to fly with a translucent rider. The inhabitants of the Loom-Cities view them with a superstitious reverence; to have a Glider nest near your workshop is to invite a stable flow of time, as their presence seems to "ground" the fraying edges of reality.

### The Parasitic "Clock-Ticks"
Not all life is industrious. Living in the cracks of the calcified plates are the **Clock-Ticks**, small, bio-mechanical scavengers that resemble a cross between a trilobite and a pocket watch. They possess no metabolism of their own; instead, they "eat" the friction of the city. 

They latch onto the moving brass rods of the steam pistons, their sapphire-tipped mandibles clicking in time with the machine. They don't drain the sap; they drain the *motion*. A district infested with Clock-Ticks will find its gears turning slower and slower, as the creatures absorb the kinetic energy to power their own reproductive cycles. When they die, they petrify instantly, becoming a brassy, gear-shaped lump of "biological rust" that the Weaver-Kin must scrape away.

### The Silent Monks of the Mist-Tide
At the very edges of the emerald domes, far from the roar of the factories, live the **Humidors**. These are recluses who have achieved a state of near-total stasis. They sit motionless on the underside of the giant, translucent leaves, waiting for the Mist-Tide. 

They have evolved "gill-folds" along their ribs that allow them to drink the water directly from the air. Their goal is to match the rhythm of the *plink-plink* of the dripping dew. It is said that the oldest Humidors have sat still for so long that the "Temporal Static" has smoothed them over; they have become "Half-Glosses," flickering between the present and a future where the Great Canopy-Crust has finally been worn down to a single, silent jewel. They are the only ones who know what the "Oldest Stable State" truly wants: to finally, perfectly, stop moving.
