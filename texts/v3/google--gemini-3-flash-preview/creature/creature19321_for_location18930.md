In the Loom of the High Humidity, life does not emerge from the slow churn of eons; it is conjured, flickering into existence as a byproduct of the Spindles’ frantic stabilization. To live here is to be a function of the heat, a living component of a machine that is still being tuned.

### The Loom-Tenders: The Pale Optic-Moths
The most prominent inhabitants are not truly animals, but the **Loom-Tenders**. These are creatures the size of wolves, possessing the fragile, powdery texture of a moth’s wing but the structural rigidity of balsa wood. Their bodies are a series of stacked, white-matte discs that mimic the material of the Spindles. 

They do not have mouths. Instead, they possess wide, flat fan-organs that beat with a rhythmic, hypnotic slow-motion, pulling the amber-tinted moisture through their translucent digestive membranes. They are the gardeners of the "condensation." You will see them perched atop the obsidian ridges, perfectly still, allowing the atmosphere to "occur" upon them until they are encased in a thick, gelatinous globule of nutrient-plasma. They do not hunt; they simply wait for the Spindles to feed the air, and then they harvest the air with their skin.

### The Vibratory Scavengers: Marrow-Suckers
Beneath the waxy surface of the "pre-crust," there are the **Marrow-Suckers**. Because the ground is more curd than stone, these eel-like organisms move through the geological sludge as if it were water. They are blind, sensitive only to the low-frequency hum of the Spindles.

They are the janitors of this prototype world. Whenever a multi-winged insect dies or a glass-stalk shatters, the Marrow-Suckers sense the change in vibration. They extrude needle-thin, copper-colored proboscises through the dark plates of the floor to drain the energy from the fallen. To a traveler, they are invisible, manifested only as a series of rhythmic "puffs" in the waxy ground—small vents where they exhale excess heat back into the stratosphere.

### The Echo-Parasites: The Glass-Wasps
The "deafening" noise of the fauna is largely driven by the **Glass-Wasps**. These creatures are the most "aggressive" manifestation of the Loom’s hyper-evolution. Their carapaces are not made of chitin, but of the same translucent, crystalline material as the vegetation. 

They are obsessed with the Spindles. They swarm the matte-white needles in shimmering, prismatic clouds, vibrating their wings at frequencies that harmonize with the Spindles’ hum. This isn't a social ritual; it is a parasitic one. They are "overclocking" their own metabolisms by leaching the post-physical energy of the towers. Their life cycles last only minutes—they are born from a condensation-bead, they swarm, they mate in a frantic blur of iridescent friction, and they shatter into crystalline dust, adding to the very ground they flew over.

### The Sentience: The Static-Walkers
Rarely, one might encounter a **Static-Walker**. These are the only beings that seem to acknowledge the Traveler. They are tall, asymmetrical bipeds that appear to be made of "visual noise"—a flickering silhouette of violet and gray that matches the stratosphere.

The Static-Walkers are the observers of the "laboratory." They move with a strange, stuttering gait, appearing five feet away and then instantly "refreshing" ten feet further, as if the Loom’s frame rate cannot quite track their movements. They carry no tools, but where they step, the "pre-crust" instantly hardens into perfect, permanent geometry. They are the personification of the plateau’s law: they are thoughts that have stayed still long enough to become matter. They do not speak; they emit a sound like a radio between stations—a hiss of white noise that, if listened to for too long, begins to sound like one’s own heart beating.

### The Dynamic of the Inhabitants
Life in the Loom is a desperate race against the "unfinish." Because the ground might shift and extrude a new ridge at any second, nothing builds a nest. Nothing sleeps. To sleep is to be absorbed back into the sludge. 

Everything here is a "prototype"—the soft-bodied six-limbed amphibians are often born with too many eyes or limbs that haven't quite finished rendering, appearing as blurs of wet flesh. They are all driven by the **Law of Condensation**. If an animal stops moving, it begins to attract the "plasma" of the air, and unless it is a Loom-Tender, this is fatal; they become encased in amber-light and turn into decorative, frozen statues that the Spindles eventually dissolve for raw mass.

In the Loom, to be alive is to be a vibration. You are either part of the hum, or you are part of the silt.
