In this white-glare vault, life does not breathe; it grinds. 

The inhabitants, the **Stone-Waiters**, are the primary architects of this restless stagnation. They are tall, gaunt figures, their limbs elongated as if stretched by the unrelenting gravity of the deep. Their skin is not skin at all, but a leathered, calcified rind, the color of a dry riverbed and the texture of fine-grit sandpaper. They wear robes of woven mineral fiber—asbestos-heavy and stiff—that trail behind them like the wake of a ship, perpetually buffed by the abrasive dust of the plazas.

They have no eyes, or rather, their eyes have been overtaken by the same translucent mica that plates the beetles. These blind, pearlescent orbs do not track movement; instead, the Stone-Waiters navigate by the **vibration of the floor**. They are perpetually barefoot, their soles thickened into fleshy pads that can sense the rhythmic *thud* of a water droplet two kilometers away. To see them move is to watch a landslide in slow motion—constant, heavy, and purposeful.

They do not have families; they have **Aggregates**. They huddle in the hollowed-out centers of the monoliths, not for warmth—for there is none—but to rub their calcified limbs against one another. This "Grooming of the Grain" is their only intimacy, a communal sanding that prevents their joints from seizing up with salt deposits. The sound of a thousand Stone-Waiters resting is a low, sandpaper rasp that vibrates through the very foundations of the city.

Beneath the feet of the Waiters, the **Lithic Fauna** fill the cracks of existence. 
The mica-beetles, the **Scintillants**, are more than just scavengers; they are the blood of the city. They possess a hive-mind that is purely kinetic. They do not think, but they react to the "Pressure-Dreams" of the Stone-Waiters. When a group of robed figures begins to drag a sled of basalt, the Scintillants flood the path ahead, their smooth shells acting like organic ball-bearings, reducing friction so the massive stones can glide across the plaza. They are the lubricant of a world without oil.

Larger, more solitary things dwell in the "Leeward Shadows"—the permanent dark spots behind the massive monoliths where the phosphor-light cannot reach. Here live the **Pillar-Gnashers**, slow-moving crustaceans the size of oxen. Their carapaces are indistinguishable from the basalt blocks they cling to. They possess specialized mandibles that vibrate at ultrasonic frequencies, allowing them to slowly liquefy the stone into a mineral paste which they suck up through proboscises. They are the reason the pillars taper; they are the slow, hungry teeth of time, eating the city from the bottom up.

Then there are the **Vapor-Sipper**s. These are ethereal, kite-like organisms that cling to the glowing phosphor-veins of the ceiling, hundreds of meters above. They look like scraps of parchment caught in a draft. When the ceiling "perspires," these creatures uncurl, catching the falling droplets in their translucent bellies. They act as biological filters, stripping the minerals from the water before it hits the ground. Sometimes, a Sipper loses its grip and tumbles through the air, fluttering down like a dying moth—only to be instantly consumed by a boiling tide of Scintillants the moment it touches the dust.

The industry of this place is a **Cycle of Attrition**. 
The Stone-Waiters are not building a future; they are managing a decline. You see them with their flint chisels, not carving art, but obsessively deepening the **Sluice-Lines**—the intricate geometric grooves in the plaza floor. These grooves are designed to catch every micro-gram of mist from the shattered droplets and funnel it toward the "Deep Sinks," where the slate-colored slugs congregate. 

The inhabitants live in a state of **Hyper-Awareness**. Because the world is silent, every vibration is a language. A Stone-Waiter can tell the purity of a limestone block by tapping it with a finger of bone and listening to the ring. They can sense the approaching "Rain" by the way the air thins and the ozone sharpens. 

There is no death here in the traditional sense, only **Erosion**. When a Stone-Waiter finally grows too thin, their joints worn down to needles and their rind-skin translucent, they do not lie down. They walk to the center of the windiest plazas and stand still. The dust-laden drafts—the "White Wind"—scour them. Over decades, they are shaved away, layer by layer, until they are nothing but a fine, grey powder that settles into the cracks of the stones, becoming the very dust that the next generation will tread upon.

In this vault, life is not a fire that burns; it is a stone that rolls, turning itself into a perfect sphere through the sheer, frantic friction of existing.
