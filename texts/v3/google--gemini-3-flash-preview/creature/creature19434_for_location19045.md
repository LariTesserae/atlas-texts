The inhabitants of this grotto are not residents in the traditional sense; they are metabolic extensions of the grotto itself. In a place where the boundary between hardware and horticulture has failed, the "people" are those who have learned to live as parasites, symbionts, and cultivators of the slow explosion.

### The Tendril-Wrights
The dominant sentient presence is a caste of spindly, pale-skinned mechanics known as the Tendril-Wrights. They do not walk so much as they drift along the wicker catwalks, their movements calibrated to the rhythmic swaying of the titan-trees. Having lived for generations in the emerald gloom, their skin has taken on the translucent quality of a blanched sprout, veined with the same faint copper-green as the oxidized pipes they tend.

They do not use wrenches to fix their machines; they use grafting waxes and hormonal salves. A Tendril-Wright’s primary tool is a brass syringe filled with concentrated growth stimulants. When a steam-piston falters, they do not replace the iron; they inject the surrounding support-vines with a cocktail that forces the plant to tighten its grip, realigning the metal through sheer hydraulic tension. They are masters of "biological torque," understanding how to prune a branch so that, over the course of a decade, it will grow into the perfect shape of a crankshaft.

### The Valve-Keepers
Deep within the hollowed-out gourd-houses live the Valve-Keepers. These individuals are physically bound to the architecture. Their respiratory systems have adjusted to the pressurized, nutrient-rich vapor of the grotto; to leave for a thinner, drier climate would be to suffocate. 

The Keepers are the listeners. They sit in the humid dark with their ears pressed against the heartwood, interpreting the "bone-deep thrum" of the forest-factory. They can hear the microscopic cavitation of sap in the copper pipes and the groan of a wooden gear nearing its breaking point. Communication between them is conducted via "pipe-speak"—a language of rhythmic clanging on the brass ribs of their homes that travels through the interconnected metal veins of the grove faster than a voice could carry through the mist.

### The Scum-Gleaners
On the lowest level, where the "tea" of the floor is deepest, move the Scum-Gleaners. These are the hardy, low-profile laborers who navigate the mile-deep peat. Wearing high-boots of treated bark and masks of woven moss to filter the thickest fumes, they harvest the "industrial runoff." 

They collect the iridescent oil that drips from the rusted boilers—a mixture of machine lubricant and tree resin—which serves as both a powerful fuel and a potent medicine. They are the only ones who interact with the heavy-bodied beetles, which they have semi-domesticated. These beetles, the size of tortoises, are used as living flatbeds, their chitinous backs grafted with small brass hooks to ferry heavy canisters of refined sap across the yielding, spongy floor.

### The Apex Flora: The "Engine-Trees"
The most significant "living" entities are the titan-trees themselves. They are not passive scenery; they are the central nervous system of the settlement. These trees have evolved a hunger for the heat produced by the boilers. They have grown their root systems specifically around the steam-pipes to bask in the warmth, their capillaries actually penetrating the rusted iron to drink the mineral-rich condensation.

The trees and the machines have entered a state of "obligate communalism." The machines provide the trees with concentrated CO2 and warmth, while the trees provide the mechanical energy (through growth-pressure) to turn the gears. The forest is the engine; the engine is the forest.

### The Vestigial "Ghost-Insects"
Finally, there are the "Ghost-Insects"—tiny, clockwork-like gnats that are not biological at all. They are the unintended offspring of the industrial process: minute flakes of rusted iron and copper dust that have become caught in the static-electric fields of the pressurized steam vents. They swarm in the shafts of golden light, appearing to dance with a primitive intelligence, though they are merely the metallic dander of a city that is slowly grinding itself into a vibrant, rust-colored mulch.

In this grotto, there is no "individual" life. From the highest Tendril-Wright to the deepest root, every inhabitant is a cog in a wet, green machine—a cell in a body that possesses a mile-deep pulse and a breath made of scalding steam.
