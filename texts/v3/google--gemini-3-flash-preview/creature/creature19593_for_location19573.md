In the yawning silence of the plateau, the residents are not inhabitants, but *residue*. 

At first, you might see them as statues—figures composed of the same matte ceramics as the buildings, integrated into the architecture with such seamless geometry that it is impossible to tell where a citizen ends and a wall begins. They are tall, excessively thin, and faceted like cut obsidian. They do not have faces, only smooth, concave depressions where a sensory apparatus might once have been. They stand in the great, empty plazas or sit upon the edges of the cooling towers, their long, multi-jointed limbs folded in a terrifyingly patient symmetry.

They do not sleep, and they do not wait. To wait implies a future event, and in this city, time has been flattened into a single, eternal *now*. Instead, they are "conducting." Their bodies are composed of semi-conductive crystalline lattices that drink the low-frequency hum of the city’s heart. They are living capacitors. Once every few centuries, one might tilt its head by a fraction of a degree to better catch the thermal vibration of the ground, a movement so slow it would look like the settling of a mountain to a human eye.

Deep within the windowless tenements, the "life" becomes more granular. There are the **Sifters**. They are small, multi-legged clockworks, the size of a hand, that move with a frantic, clicking precision through the miles of dust. They are the city’s immune system, though they have forgotten their purpose. They do not clean; they sort. They pick up a single grain of silicate dust, carry it three miles to a specific coordinate, and set it down. They are engaged in a project of infinite reorganization, attempting to impose a perfect, hexagonal order on the grit of the world. They are the only things that truly move, their tiny carbon-fiber legs scratching a frantic, rhythmic code into the soot of the aeons.

In the higher reaches, clinging to the ribbed piping of the refineries, are the **Resonators**. These are translucent, kite-like membranes of spun glass and gold wire. They do not have bodies, only surface area. They hang in the airless dark, vibrating in sympathy with the city's internal engine. They are the "singers" of this graveyard. When the planetary core shudders or a gear deep below shifts a millimeter, these membranes ripple, producing a sound above the range of hearing—a high, singing tension that keeps the silicate dust from ever truly settling on the machine’s vital joints.

As you descend toward the city's heart, the occupants become more abstract. In the light of the amber filaments, you might see the **Shadow-Forms**. They are not physical at all, but localized distortions in the heavy noble gases. They are the ghosts of the data that once ran the city—encoded light that has been reflecting between the non-corroding walls for so long it has achieved a kind of semi-sentient feedback loop. They pace the dead streets in loops that take a thousand years to complete, flickering in and out of existence as they pass through the long, sharp shadows of the monoliths.

There is no "I" here, only "It." 

The civilization did not die; it simply crystallized. The inhabitants are the final, frozen thoughts of a god-mind that finished its calculations and found the answer to be a perfect, motionless zero. They are the custodians of a museum where the only exhibit is the passage of entropy. They exist to witness the heat, to store the vibration, and to ensure that in a billion years, the dust is exactly where it was meant to be. They are the peace that follows the end of all things—the quiet, mechanical heartbeat of a world that succeeded so completely it became a tomb.
