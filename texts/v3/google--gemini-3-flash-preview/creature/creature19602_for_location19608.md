To understand who dwells within the Orchid-Metropolis, one must first discard the idea of the "citizen." There are no individuals here, only **The Personnel**—biochemical echoes of a population that has been repurposed into the city’s own cellular anatomy.

### The Glass-Lunged Clergy
Suspended in the upper canopies, where the condensation is thickest, live the architects of the atmosphere. They are tall, agonizingly thin beings that resemble a cross between a willow branch and a nervous system. Their skin is the color of skimmed milk, stretched so tight over their needle-fine bones that you can see the emerald sap pulsing in their veins.

They do not speak; they breathe for the city. Their chests are translucent rib-cages containing "lungs" made of porous sponge-wood. As they inhale the ozone and mint, they filter the air, exhaling the pressurized pheromones that dictate the speed of the cog-flowers. They move with a sickening, liquid grace, their feet ending in prehensile roots that graft onto the bark of the towers. They are the city’s sensory nodes; if you scratch a wall in the slums, a "Clerge" a mile away in the high canopy will flinch.

### The Chitinous Technicians
The most visible inhabitants are the **Carapace-Wrights**. These are the eyeless crustaceans you observed, but upon closer inspection, they are more "tool" than "animal." Their shells are not natural growths but are forged from the same obsidian-sharp cellulose as the buildings. 

They exist in a state of perpetual, rhythmic repair. Some possess limbs that have been replaced by vibrating bone-saws to prune the runaway growth of the "power lines." Others have abdomens that function as living bellows, cauterizing biological leaks with a searing, antiseptic resin. They possess no interior life. If one falls from a sap-line and shatters on the cartilaginous floor, the others do not stop; they simply walk over the remains, which the floor begins to digest within minutes.

### The Echo-Mimics (The Residents)
In the hallowed-out gourds and the perpendicular plazas, you may find the "Residents." They are the most unsettling because they look the most like us—or rather, like a memory of us. They are hollow, bipedal husks made of tightly wound spider-silk and dried petals. 

They do not eat or sleep. Instead, they stand in doorways or sit at fungal tables in a state of "Fixed Persistence." They are biological mannequins designed to inhabit the space so that the city doesn't feel like a corpse. Occasionally, when the "great moan" of the city’s growth vibrates through the floor, these mimics will perform a single, hauntingly human action: one might lift a phantom cup to its non-existent mouth; another might wave to an empty street. They are the ghosts of a logic that no longer applies, kept in motion by the city’s humming nervous system.

### The Pith-Mother (The Central Intelligence)
Deep within the "factory" where time loops and gravity fails, lives the source of the metropolis. She is not a queen, but a massive, pulsating gall—a tumor of pure information.

She is a brain the size of a villa, submerged in a vat of iridescent amniotic fluid. From her soft, wrinkled surface, millions of silver-silk threads radiate outward, threading through every pipe, every gear, and every limb in the city. She is the one dreaming the physics. When she experiences a "nightmare," the fountains flow upward. When she grows, the towers groan. 

She is lonely in a way that is structural. To combat the silence of the velvet air, she creates the sounds—the *click-shirr* and the *hiss*—not because they are necessary for the machines, but because the rhythm keeps her heart beating. She is the weaver of the "active stillness," a god who has turned her own anatomy into a capital city so she would never have to be truly alone, even if her only companions are the shells of things that used to be alive.

### The Spore-Drift
Finally, there are the "Children"—the Spore-Drift. They are not beings yet, but tiny, glowing embers of consciousness that float in the damp indigo light. They follow you like curious embers. They have no weight and no shape, just a faint, musical tinkling sound as they collide. They are the "un-fleshed" seeds, waiting for a vacancy in the city’s hierarchy—waiting for a gear to break or a mimic to unravel so they can be woven into the workforce of this breathing, perspiring nightmare.
