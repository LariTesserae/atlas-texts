In the kingdom of the Great Thrum, life is not a pulse; it is a friction. To exist here is to be a component in a planetary engine. The inhabitants do not "evolve" in the traditional sense; they are machined by the environment, refined by the abrasive winds into shapes of terrifying efficiency.

### The Serrated Aristocracy: The Glider-Galleons
High in the white-gold glare, where the "air" is thick enough to support the weight of a cathedral, live the **Aeolian Harvesters**. These are the galleon-sized insectoids, but to call them insects is to misunderstand their metallurgy. Their exoskeletons are not mere chitin; they are composites of amber-glass and pressurized lignin, polished by the dust-storms until they reflect the sun like mirrors.

They do not possess wings that flap. Instead, they have massive, fixed-geometry sails made of translucent, fibrous membranes. These creatures are masters of the "Vertical Law." They latch onto the giant, rotating gear-trees, using their hooked, scissor-like limbs to "plug" into the kinetic energy of the rotating branches. As the tree turns, it winds a series of internal torsion-springs inside the creature’s thorax. When the Harvester releases, it is shot into the thermal updrafts like a crossbow bolt, screaming through the lattice at supersonic speeds to hunt the smaller swarms.

### The Lubricants: The Resin-Bleeders
Because the Great Thrum is a world of grinding parts, the greatest threat—aside from stillness—is fire caused by friction. Living within the joints of the gear-trees and the valves of the seed-pod pistons are the **Oil-Saints**. 

The Saints are soft-bodied, slug-like organisms, but their skin is a dry, self-lubricating polymer. They consume the hyper-compressed cellulose dust and "excrete" a fine, volatile resin that keeps the planetary machinery from seizing up. They are the priests of the gears, moving in slow, rhythmic undulations across the grinding faces of the wood. They are the only creatures that can survive the crushing force of two city-sized branches rubbing together; they exist in the "zero-clearance" zones, thriving where anything else would be pulverized into dust.

### The Architects: The Termite-Centurions
If the world is a factory, the **Centurions** are the assembly line. These are the "termite-like swarms" mentioned, but up close, they resemble segmented, multi-legged saws. They operate with a hive-mind that is perfectly synchronized with the Thrum. 

They do not build nests; they *are* the construction process. A swarm of ten million Centurions can descend upon a decaying "shelf" and, within a single sun-cycle, dismantle it into its constituent fibers. They carry these fibers in specialized mandibles that act like ultrasonic welders, vibrating at such high frequencies that they fuse the dry wood together into new, structural towers. 

They move with a terrifying, mechanical gait—a rhythmic *clack-clack-clack* that mimics the ticking of the gear-trees. To an outsider, they look like a river of bronze-colored gears flowing uphill. They are the reason the landscape is never still; they are the hand that constantly rewrites the geography.

### The Deep-Dwellers: The Petrifiers
Below the shelves, where the white light fades into a dim, amber gloom and the pressure begins to turn the timber into diamond, the biology changes. Here, the "Industrial Ecology" slows down. 

In the sunless depths live the **Lithos-Whales**. These massive, slow-moving leviathans are the size of mountain ranges. Their bodies are almost entirely mineral—solidified resins and fossilized wood. They do not navigate the winds; they "graze" on the very structural pillars of the world. They move so slowly that a single "step" might take a century. 

The Lithos-Whales act as the world’s ballast. They consume the "waste" of the upper world—the debris and fallen dust that sifts down from the frantic activity above. They compress this waste within their gizzards, creating the diamond-hard "bedrock" that supports the entire vertical continent. They are the silent, crushing foundation of the Thrum, the slow heartbeat beneath the frantic screaming of the heights.

### The Apex Parasite: The Static-Leech
In a world of constant motion, the most dangerous thing is the **Static-Leech**. These are spindly, wire-like organisms that hang from the underside of the calcified fungal plates. They do not move. They do not grind. 

Instead, they act as "grounding wires." They reach out with needle-thin filaments to touch the rotating gear-trees and the fast-moving gliders. They steal the kinetic energy, siphoning off the "currency of motion" to fuel their own brittle metabolism. To be touched by a Static-Leech is to be "braked"—to have your momentum stolen until you seize up. In the Great Thrum, to stop is to die, and the Leech is the bringer of the ultimate end: Stillness.

### The Sensory Experience
To see these creatures is to see a blur of polished gold and grinding grey. You do not hear their voices; you hear the vibration of their shells against the wood. They don't breathe air; they "inhale" the dry pollen-dust through vibrating spiracles that sound like the hum of a high-voltage transformer.

Everything here is sharp. Everything is hard. There is no fur, no moss, no softness. Life is a jagged, singing edge, forever being sharpened against the stone-hard grain of the world.
