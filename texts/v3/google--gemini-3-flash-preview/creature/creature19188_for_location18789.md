In this lightless, gelatinous basement, life is not a conquest of the environment, but a total surrender to it. To exist here is to become an extension of the silt—a momentary, self-organizing knot in the endless churn.

### The Weigh-Masters (The Ponderous)
The dominant fauna—if "flesh" can be used to describe their cured, leathery membranes—are the **Looming.** They are massive, slow-beating kites of translucent cartilage, shaped like flattened bells to minimize the drag of the violent currents. They have no eyes; eyes are an evolutionary vanity in a place where the medium itself is opaque. Instead, their entire dorsal surface is a vast, sensitive tapestry of nerve-endings that "read" the pressure. They feel the shape of a storm ten miles away by the way the salt-density shifts against their skin.

Their life’s work is the **Anchor-Bond.** Within the folds of their underside, they possess a prehensile, muscular cavity that acts as a lathe. They spend their first century of life filtering the silt, searching for a single "Heart-Stone"—a fragment of silicate or ancient metal that hasn't yet been ground into dust. Once found, they never let it go. They tuck it into their tissue, calcifying it into their own skeletal structure. Without this weight, they would be snatched by the "convection of the heavy" and spun into the upper pressures until they burst. A Weigh-Master is only as stable as the rock it carries; to lose one’s stone is to become a ghost, a frantic, tumbling thing that the water eventually grinds into nothing.

### The Sieve-Wraiths
Smaller and more frantic than the Looming, these are the scavengers of the "snow." They resemble bundles of fibrous glass, their bodies a lattice of rigid, translucent needles. They do not move through the brine so much as they *filter* through it. They orient themselves vertically, like living spears, wedging their tapered tails into the compacted floor. 

They are the world’s only historians. As the pulverized remains of the world above—the mineral dust of extinct civilizations and ground-up continents—settle through the dark, the Sieve-Wraiths catch the particles in their hair-like cilia. They do not eat for energy so much as they "taste" for chemical variance. When a Wraith dies, its glass body shatters, and it becomes a localized patch of shimmering sand, the only "bright" thing in the dark, though there is no eye to see the sparkle.

### The Litho-mote Colonies (The Churn-Grit)
The fists of calcified shell mentioned before are not individuals, but vast, slow-thinking networks. Because the brine is so dense, sound travels with terrifying efficiency, manifesting as tactile vibration. The Litho-motes use this. By clicking their shells together in rhythmic pulses, they create localized standing waves in the water—micro-zones of "calm" amidst the turbulence. 

If you were to observe a Litho-mote colony over a thousand years, you would see them drifting together to form "reefs of necessity." They do not grow through biological reproduction alone, but through architectural accretion. They "glue" themselves to passing debris with a caustic, mineral-rich bile, growing until they are heavy enough to sink through the churn and rest on the silt-floor. Here, they form the only topography: low, humped mounds of screaming, clicking stone that process the friction of the currents into a dim, internal heat.

### The Marrow-Leeches
In the crevices of the Weigh-Masters’ leathery skin live the most parasitic expressions of this world. They are little more than animated veins, thin as a wire and long as a man’s arm. They lack even the basic "tech" of a weight-stone. Instead, they anchor themselves to the stronger creatures, burrowing into the leathery hide to sup on the pressurized fluids of their hosts. 

They are the ultimate hitchhikers of the abyss. When a storm-column becomes too violent, the Leeches tighten their grip, vibrating in sympathy with the host's hum. They represent the only "social" interaction in this world—a grim, clinging dependency where the host is too slow to remove them, and the parasite is too fragile to survive the open brine.

### The Interior Life
Life in the basement is a state of "High Stasis." Because the medium is so thick, movement is a feat of extreme hydraulics. Every gesture is a profound expenditure of chemical potential. Consequently, these creatures do not "behave." They *endure*. 

If they have an inner life, it is a map of pressures. They do not imagine a "surface" or a "sun"; to them, the universe is a series of weights. There is the Boring Weight (the floor), the Crushing Weight (the storm-columns), and the Dissolving Weight (the upper reaches). Their "soul" is the pebble they carry—the one solid thing in a world of liquid grit, the singular proof that they are not yet part of the mud.
