In this vertical deluge, life has divested itself of the frantic pulse of the animal. To possess a heart here would be to possess a weakness—a rhythmic ticking that the thundering current would eventually shatter. Instead, the inhabitants of the shale-capsules and the keepers of the wooden gears are something else: they are **The Marrow-Stemmed.**

### The Form of the Inhabitant
The beings who dwell here are not quite flesh, nor quite fiber. They are humanoid in silhouette but sculpted by the same laws as the calcified kelp. Their skin is a translucent, slate-colored membrane, thickened into leather at the joints to resist the rasping friction of the water. They have no lungs; instead, their ribcages are open, fan-like structures of flexible cartilage draped in gossamer-thin gills that flutter like the pages of a soaked book.

They do not swim so much as they "anchor." Their fingers and toes are elongated, ending in hooked, vestigial claws designed to find purchase in the petrified vines of the Green Labyrinth. To let go is to be lost to the current, and so their very psychology is built upon the concept of the *Grip*. 

Movement for a Marrow-Stemmed is a slow, methodical choreography of tension. They move hand-over-hand across the gut-ropes of their outposts, their bodies trailing in the current like ribbons. Because the pressure is "expansive" rather than crushing, their internal tissues are porous, filled with the same pressurized freshwater that surrounds them. They do not "look" at the world; their eyes are wide, milk-white orbs that sense only the subtlest shifts in the bioluminescent dither. Instead, they "feel" the world through a series of sensitive, hair-like filaments along their spines that interpret the "Vocal Water"—the thrumming, low-frequency vibrations of the forest.

### The Life of the Gear-Keepers
These are a people of the Constant Turn. Since fire is impossible and heat is a rare commodity, their lives are dedicated to the maintenance of the massive, grinding wooden gears. 

A Marrow-Stemmed individual may spend a forty-year cycle tending to a single gear-assembly. They are the "Grease-Singers," though they do not sing with throats. They use smooth stones to scrape the parasitic mineral-crusts from the wooden teeth of the machines, ensuring the current continues to grind the stones that provide warmth. 

They are intensely solitary. In a world where the roar of the water drowns out all vocalization, communication is a tactile language of "Signal-Taps" delivered through the tethers. A father might communicate with a daughter miles away by striking a rhythmic pattern on a shared kelp-root; the vibration travels through the rigid plant-matter, felt by the recipient’s grip. This creates a civilization of profound, resonant intimacy without a single spoken word.

### The Diet of the Slow Pulse
They do not hunt, for there is nothing to kill. They "forage" from the peristaltic vines. When a Marrow-Stemmed is hungry, they find a pulsating "bladder-lung" and graft their own circulatory systems briefly into the plant’s exterior membrane. It is a symbiotic leeching—a slow exchange of minerals for carbon. 

They also consume the "Flake-Silt"—the pulverized remains of ancient vines ground down by the gears. They collect this emerald dust in pouches of cured hide, mixing it with the cold water to create a grit-paste that hardens their own semi-petrified bones. They are, in a very literal sense, becoming the forest as they age.

### The Cycle of Ascension
The Marrow-Stemmed do not fear death; they fear "Losing the Grip." 

When an individual becomes too brittle to maintain their hold on the tethers, or when the current finally wears their joints to a halt, they undergo the *Final Unlashing*. They do not sink into a grave. Instead, as their internal pressure equalizes with the deepening cold of their slowing metabolism, they begin to crystallize. 

The living body turns to a cloudy, organic ice. Brittle and translucent, they eventually shatter under the relentless vibration of the "Loud Water." Like the dying vines, the shards of a person—their memories held in the geometric patterns of the ice—drift "upward" toward the invisible, high-altitude apex of the world. 

### The Sentinel-Vegetal (The "Other" Life)
While there are no animals, the forest itself possesses a brooding, non-conscious intentionality that mimics life. 

There are the **Loom-Vines**, which do not simply grow but actively weave themselves into snares to catch the upward-drifting shards of the dead. There are the **Thrum-Nodes**, bulbous growths that act as the sensory organs of the forest, magnifying the roar of the current into a heat-generating friction. 

The Marrow-Stemmed treat these parts of the forest with a religious dread. They believe the Labyrinth is a single, dreaming mind, and they are merely the parasites living in its lashes, tasked with keeping its wooden gears turning so it does not wake and shake them off into the infinite, freezing dark.
