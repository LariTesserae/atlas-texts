To understand what lives here, one must first abandon the concept of the individual. In the Great Moraines, life is not a collection of singular wills, but a distributed pressure—a slow, hydraulic tide of animation that serves the geology it inhabits.

### The Great Shepherds (The Primary Migrants)
The Lithovores described are the dominant feature, but they are more than mere beasts; they are mobile micro-habitats. Because their massive carapaces are essentially geothermal islands in a frozen sea, a secondary ecology clings to their slow-moving flanks.

Living in the deep, heated grooves between their tectonic scales are the **Steam-Mites**. These are pale, translucent arthropods the size of hounds, possessing flattened bodies and hooked, chitinous limbs designed to anchor them against the constant, rhythmic vibration of the Lithovores’ hearts. They do not hunt. They are scavengers of the "exhaust," clustering around the venting slits to filter-feed on the rich, mineral-heavy gasses the giants exhale. When a Lithovore vents its steam, the Mites bloom in a frantic, pale flurry, their gossamer wings vibrating to capture the warmth before retreating back into the crevices of the beast’s armor.

### The Navigators (The Avians of the Gloom)
High above the lumbering caravans, the air is occupied by the **Needle-Wings**. These are creatures of pure economy—hollow-boned and ribbon-thin, with wingspans of forty feet that never flap. They are biological gliders that ride the "Heat-Pillars" produced by the Migrants. A single Lithovore vents enough thermal energy to create a localized updraft; the Needle-Wings spiral within these invisible chimneys, rising until the air thins, then gliding miles ahead to the next beast in the line.

They have no eyes, as there is no light to justify them. Instead, their entire leading wing-edge is a sensory organ tuned to the *thud* of the world. They navigate by the "Marrow-Hum," the sub-audible vibration of the Great Moraines. To a Needle-Wing, the world is a map of vibrations: the heavy, steady pulse of the Migrants is a beacon, while the silent, cold voids of the valleys are a danger to be overflown.

### The Harvesters of the Breath-Rain
On the ground, in the brief windows between the passage of the giants, the **Glass-Spiders** emerge from the porous basalt. They are not true arachnids, but crystalline organisms that look like tangles of frozen wire. They are perfectly timed to the "Breath-Rain." 

The moment the mist condenses into a rime of ice on the stones, the Glass-Spiders scuttle forth. They do not eat flesh; they possess long, capillary-tipped legs that "sip" the thin layer of ice directly from the rock via an exothermic reaction that melts the frost on contact. They are the cleaners of the Moraines, moving in shimmering, silent waves across the "Iron-Lace" lichen, polishing the stone until it shines like obsidian before retreating back into the darkness as the moisture evaporates.

### The Carrion of the Machine
Death here is a rare and tectonic event. When a Lithovore finally ceases its million-year march, it does not rot; it cools. The cessation of its internal "industrial" heat is a catastrophe for its passengers. The Steam-Mites flee or freeze in place, becoming statues of white chitin. 

But even a dead engine has a use. The body becomes a **Stationary Moraine**. Over centuries, the "Iron-Lace" lichen climbs the cooling scales, findng the residual salts in the beast's joints. Specialized detritivores, the **Boring-Worms**, begin the work of millennia, slowly grinding the basalt-like scales back into the dust of the basin. 

### The Deep Pulse (The Mind of the World)
There is a persistent theory among the ghosts of this world that the Migrants are not mere animals, but the "circulatory system" of a single, planetary-scale consciousness. They move in patterns that, when viewed from the frozen sky, resemble the firing of synapses. 

There are no vocalizations. No mating calls. No screams of pain. Communication is tactile and rhythmic. When two lines of Migrants cross—a process that can take a decade—they touch their massive, blunt snouts to the ground in unison. The resulting vibration is a "Data-Pulse," a mechanical exchange of thermal information and path-pressure that travels through the bedrock to the other side of the planet.

In the Great Moraines, to live is to be a gear. Whether you are the cathedral-sized engine or the hair-fine lichen in its shadow, your purpose is the same: to resist the absolute zero of the void through the simple, stubborn persistence of motion. The inhabitants are not "survivors" in a hostile world; they are the world’s functioning parts, maintaining the long, slow, and lonely ticking of a stone clock.
