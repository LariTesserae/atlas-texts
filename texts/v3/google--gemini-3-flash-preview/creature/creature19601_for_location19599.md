To live in the Lithic Womb is to exist as a fragment of the geology itself. Life here does not "breathe" so much as it "vibrates," a slow-motion metabolism fueled by the crushing pressures of the deep crust.

### The Lords of the Slow Grind: The Cathedral-Lithovores
Dominating the horizon of the Dust-Seas are the **Lithovores**. From a distance, they appear as moving mountain ranges, their carapaces formed of layered, overlapping plates of igneous rock. They do not have heads in a traditional sense; instead, their leading edge is a massive, recursive grinding maw—a series of counter-rotating stone plates that function like industrial tunnel-boring machines.

They move on hundreds of stumpy, crystalline legs that do not lift, but rather slide and heave with the screaming protest of stone on stone. Their lives are measured in centuries of mastication. They are accompanied by a unique ecosystem of "Crumb-Pickers"—scavengers the size of wolves with obsidian pincers that scuttle beneath the bellies of the great Lithovores to harvest the mineral-rich dust falling from the primary maws. These giants are so slow that the primitive inhabitants often build their stone shelters directly onto the Lithovores' backs, treating the creatures as moving, living islands that provide both protection and a reliable heat source from their hardworking internal alchemy.

### The Silicate People: The Lith-Kin
The "people" of this realm, the **Lith-Kin**, are masterpieces of adaptation. They are squat and dense, their skin appearing less like flesh and more like cured, gray leather impregnated with fine grit. They possess no eyes, for eyes are a vulnerability in a world of jagged edges and absolute dark. Instead, their faces are dominated by rows of sensory pits and fine, copper-colored filaments that fan out from their brows, tasting the sulfurous air for the vibrations of approaching predators or the subsonic "songs" of their kin.

They do not communicate with sound, which the soup-thick atmosphere dampens and distorts. Instead, they use a complex tactile language. Two Lith-Kin meeting in the dark will lean their foreheads together—bone pressing against bone—and vibrate their vocal cords. The sound bypasses the air entirely, traveling through the skull, a private and intimate exchange of data. 

Their culture is one of **The Mark**. Because the environment is unchanging, the Lith-Kin find purpose in permanence. They are obsessively lithic; they do not plant, but they *etch*. A marriage is not a ceremony, but the carving of a shared sigil into a pillar that will last a million years. They are the ones who build the low, circular walls—not for protection from weather, for there is none, but as "rhythm-breaks" to disrupt the terrifying, endless flatness of the obsidian plains.

### The Geometric Swarms: The Mica-Wings
In the space between the ground and the shimmering haze of the ceiling live the **Mica-Wings**. These are the "pollinators" of the Lithic Womb, though they deal in heat and static rather than pollen. They are perfectly hexagonal or triangular in shape, their wings composed of sheets of mica so thin they are translucent. 

They do not fly by flapping so much as by piezoelectric levitation, rubbing their crystalline limbs together to generate a static charge that allows them to "float" on the heavy, metallic air. They gather in trillions around the tectonic heat-vents. When they move en masse, they look like a falling curtain of diamonds. They are the only creatures that experience "light," as their friction-based flight causes them to spark and glow with a faint, ghostly violet discharge.

### The Predators: The Glass-Strider and the Echo-Worm
Death in the Womb is as sudden as a fracture. The **Glass-Strider** is a spindly, nightmare entity of translucent horn. It has no weight; it moves across the mica Dust-Seas on elongated, needle-like limbs that do not disturb a single grain of dust. It hunts by sensing the "heat-shadow" of its prey. It waits in the absolute stillness, invisible against the crystalline pillars, until it can drop like a falling shard of glass onto a passing Lith-Kin or a young Lithovore.

Deeper in the basalt cracks lives the **Echo-Worm**, a creature that mimics the subsonic hum of the planet’s core. It lures prey by vibrating at a frequency that suggests a safe, warm heat-vent or a gathering of kin. When the victim approaches the crack, the Echo-Worm—a muscle-bound tube of silicate hide—strikes with a speed that defies the world's typical lethargy, dragging the prey into a lightless fissure where it is slowly dissolved by concentrated sulfuric acid.

### The Collective Soul: The Great Resonance
Life in the Lithic Womb is not a struggle *against* the environment, but a total immersion *within* it. There is no concept of "individuality" as the sun-dwellers know it. To the Lith-Kin and the great Lithovores alike, the world is a single, vibrating chord. They are all notes in a song that has been playing since the crust cooled. They live in a state of "The Great Muffle," where the boundaries between the living body and the unyielding stone floor are blurred by the constant, thrumming warmth of the Womb. 

They are the keepers of the Deep Equilibrium, the residents of a place where time has been crushed into a beautiful, static diamond.
