The inhabitants of the Green Gorge Foundry are not residents so much as they are **Cultivators of the Resonance**. They are a people defined by the verticality of their home and the rhythmic, heavy wetness of the air.

### The Lithe and Oily Folk
To look upon a human of the Gorge is to see a creature adapted to a world of slick basalt and vibrating copper. They are lean and long-limbed, possessing a prehensile grace necessary for navigating the "web"—the intricate network of gantries, hanging ladders, and moss-slicked pipes. Their skin has a permanent, healthy sheen, a result of the mineral-rich steam and the bioluminescent oils they use to grease the city’s gears.

They do not wear fabrics that absorb; they wear clothes that repel. Their garments are crafted from the cured, translucent membranes of the giant ferns or the hammered, flexible scales of the gear-crickets. These outfits are silent, clicking softly against the metal walkways, and are often adorned with small, functional copper weights. These weights are not decorative; they serve to lower a person’s center of gravity, providing "ballast" against the buffeting winds that howl down the rift.

### The Masters of Deliberation
In a place where a single slipped foot could mean a fall into a thousand-foot bank of ferns, or where a sudden movement might disrupt a finely tuned steam-calibration, the culture has evolved into a theater of **Slow Motion**. 

To a stranger, the people might seem underwater. They move with an agonizing, fluid grace, turning valves with the steady pressure of a shifting tide rather than a sudden jerk. Conversation is rare and secondary to the soundscape; they speak in low, sonorous tones that sit beneath the frequency of the huffing boilers. They have learned to communicate through "Tapping"—using small brass rings on their fingers to strike the railings, sending vibrations through the steel infrastructure that can be felt by others miles away through the soles of their boots.

### The Resonators and the Weavers
The population is split into two symbiotic guilds that mirror the duality of the valley:

*   **The Resonators:** These are the keepers of the heavy breath. They live in the lower tiers, closer to the thrumming pistons and the white-plumed boilers. They do not view themselves as mechanics, but as "tuners." They listen to the groan of the cooling metal and the clack of the steam valves, adjusting the pressure to ensure the city’s heartbeat remains in literal harmony with the geological settling of the cliffs. They believe that if the machines stop, the unstable basalt walls will realize they are unsupported and collapse inward.
*   **The Weavers:** They inhabit the upper, sun-drenched layers where the ferns are thickest. Their task is structural bio-engineering. They "braid" the aggressive growth of the jungle into the steel framework of the city. Using copper wires as guides, they train the thick, ropey vines to act as secondary suspension cables for the monorails and bridges. They treat the plants not as landscaping, but as living Rebar—a green reinforcement that holds the metal to the stone despite the "young erosion" of the rift.

### The Commensal Beasts
Life in the Green Gorge is not limited to the human. The city breathes, and other things breathe with it.

*   **The Copper-Back Sloths:** Large, slow-moving mammals with fur the color of oxidized bronze. They are the city’s literal cleaners, possessing tongues specialized for licking the mineral-rich condensation and bioluminescent algae off the glass view-ports. They are considered good omens; a Resonator will never start a turbine if a Copper-Back is sleeping on the flywheel.
*   **The Steam-Hawks:** These silver-plumaged birds do not hunt by sight alone, but by thermal sensing. They nest in the hottest vents of the boilers, using the rising heat to dry their metallic feathers. They act as the city’s "canaries"—if the Steam-Hawks suddenly ascend toward the rift’s opening in a silver cloud, the citizens know a slab of the gorge wall is about to shear off.

### The Philosophy of the Pulse
Isolation in the Green Gorge has birthed a unique spirituality. The inhabitants believe that the light reflecting off the basalt is a raw material that must be "distilled" through their industry into something the earth can use. They see themselves as part of a metabolic process: the city takes in the weeping mist, cycles it through the turbines of their labor, and breathes it back out as a refined, emerald-tinted steam that feeds the jungle.

They live for the **Sustained Resonance**. To them, the noise is not a nuisance; it is the sound of the world’s internal clock finally being set right. They are the keepers of the rift, the small, quiet souls who ensure that as the mountains settle and the plants grow, the beautiful, loud machine of the valley never misses a single, hushing beat.
