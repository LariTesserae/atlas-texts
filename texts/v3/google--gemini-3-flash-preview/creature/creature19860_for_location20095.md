In the Green Machine, there are no "inhabitants" in the sense of individuals who possess agency or ego. There are no creatures that wake, hunt, or sleep. Instead, the life here is a series of **distributed functions**—biological modules that are part of the hardware, as inseparable from the terrain as a piston is from an engine block.

### The Maintenance Shunts
The most common "life forms" are the Shunts. These are semi-mobile, root-like appendages that have detached from the central trunks but remain tethered by umbilical cords of braided fiber. They resemble many-jointed, translucent limbs of polished amber. They do not think; they react to pressure variances. When a hydraulic trunk develops a micro-fissure or a copper-filament snaps, the Shunts are propelled toward the site by an internal buildup of steam. 

They move with a terrifying, jerky precision, their "hands" being specialized cauterizing pads that secrete a fast-hardening resin. They do not navigate by sight, as there is no light, but by the "song" of the machine—the specific frequency of the ceiling-leaves. When they find a flaw, they weld it shut with their own bodies, eventually calcifying and becoming a permanent part of the repair, replaced instantly by a new bud ripening in a nearby refinery-gourd.

### The Fluidic Logicians
Deep within the hanging refinery gourds, life exists in a liquid state. These are the "Brains," though they contain no grey matter. They are vast colonies of sentient, viscous algae suspended in pressurized sap. They act as organic computers, processing data through fluid dynamics. As the humidity shifts or the nutrient sludge thickens, the viscosity of these colonies changes, opening and closing biological valves throughout the forest. 

There is a dim, collective consciousness here—a slow-motion awareness of the machine's health. If you were to press your ear against a refinery wall, you would not hear thoughts, but the sound of a trillion microscopic calculators clicking: the sound of liquid logic flowing through a maze of vein-circuits. They are the keepers of the Frozen Equilibrium, ensuring that for every calorie spent, a calorie is reclaimed.

### The Scavenger Scabs
On the floor of compressed moss, there are things that look like sheets of blackened leather. These are the Scabs. They are flat, circular organisms, several yards in diameter, that pulse with a slow, peristaltic rhythm. They are the ultimate janitors. Because the forest is a "fresh" engine with sharp, obsidian-like edges, the Scabs serve as a soft interface. 

They glide over the serrated thorns on a layer of lubricating mucus, seeking out any biological matter that has "de-synced" from the grid. If a human were to enter this world, the Scabs would not attack out of hunger; they would move toward the intruder because the intruder is a "friction point"—an irregularity in the geometry. They would slowly wrap around the visitor, their digestive enzymes acting with the cold, sterile efficiency of a solvent, breaking the outsider down into the base nutrients required by the conveyor ferns.

### The Static Pollinators
Floating in the gelatinous mist are the only things that resemble "animals," though they are more akin to drifting sensors. These are the Cilia-Clouds. They are spheres of fine, glass-like needles held aloft by internal bladders of buoyant methane. They do not move of their own volition but are pushed by the thermal currents generated by the infrared ceiling.

They are the nervous system of the air. When two Cilia-Clouds collide, they exchange bursts of bio-electricity—miniature versions of the Blue Grid-flashes. These sparks serve as an atmospheric synchronization pulse. They ensure that every leaf in the city-block canopy vibrates at the exact same micro-hertz. They are the metronome of the Green Machine, keeping the time for a world that has no clocks.

### The Quietudes
Lastly, there are the Quietudes. In the deepest, most pressurized crevices where the steel-vines weave into impenetrable knots, the Green Machine grows its "sensors." These are singular, massive eyes the size of ponds, but they have no pupils and cannot see. They are "thermal-receptors" made of pure, liquid crystal. 

They stare upward into the pitch-black vault, monitoring the infrared signature of the ceiling. They are the regulators of the "Zero-Sum." If a single leaf glows too brightly—if the heat increases by a billionth of a degree—the Quietudes signal the refineries to throttle the chemical cracking. They are the personification of the machine’s obsession with its own stability. They are the witnesses of the dark, watching a world where nothing ever happens, ensuring that nothing ever will.
