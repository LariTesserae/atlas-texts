Whatever lives in the Upper Sump does not live *on* the machinery, but *as* a fluid extension of it. There are no residents here with names or faces, but there are entities defined by their function within the pressure.

### The Calcified Curators
Deep within the tightest lattices of the tin-fern thickets dwell the **Refractories**. They are spindly, multi-limbed effigies of articulated copper, resembling something between a deep-sea crab and a clockwork mannequin. They have no skin; their bodies are a skeletal framework of polished bronze armatures. 

They do not swim so much as they "climb" the medium of the water. Their movements are synchronized perfectly with the vibrations of the great flywheels. When a flywheel hums at a certain frequency, the Refractories move; when the valves hiss, they go still. Their primary purpose is the "Grafting." They carry small, delicate shears made of diamond-glass, and they spend their centuries pruning the glass-vines. They ensure that no singular vine grows thick enough to jam a piston or choke a vent. They are the gardeners of a sterile paradise, meticulously maintaining the geometry of the forest to ensure the machine never falters.

### The Marrow-Leeches
Clinging to the pulsating pipes the size of cathedrals are the **Siphon-Worm Conglomerates**. These are not singular organisms, but colonies of semi-intelligent, oily membranes. They look like sheets of shimmering, iridescent silk wrapped tightly around the brass veins of the Sump. 

They live off the "sweat" of the machine—the heat and mineral seepage that leaks through the rivets. They serve as a secondary insulation, but also as a sensory nervous system for the district. When a pipe develops a hairline fracture, the Conglomerates thicken at the site, their translucent bodies hardening into a resinous scab that seals the wound. They feel the "marrow" of the Sump, translating the mechanical heat into a slow, metabolic consciousness that dreams of constant, unchanging pressure.

### The Optic Sentinels
In the wide, amber-lit spaces between the glass-kelp forests, there are the **Gild-Eyes**. They are spheres of heavy, leaded glass, roughly the size of a human torso, buoyed by internal chambers of hot hydrogen gas. They drift with the glacial currents, tethered to the brass catwalks by long, trailing filaments of gold wire.

They have no mouths or limbs, only a single, massive iris that glows with the orange light of a filament lamp. They are the Sump’s recording consciousness. As they drift, they observe the alignment of every tooth in every gear. They are looking for "Decay," which in this world is a structural heresy. If a gear-tooth chips, a Gild-Eye will hover over the site for decades, pulsing a rhythmic light signal until a Refractory arrives to polish the blemish away. They are the witnesses of the stillness, the quiet keepers of the "perpetual afternoon."

### The Ghosts of the Friction
Finally, there are the **Hum-Echos**. These are not physical beings, but localized distortions in the brine, born from the sheer intensity of the industrial vibration. To an observer, they look like "heat shimmers" underwater—blurred, human-shaped ripples that appear near the massive three-story flywheels. 

They are the residue of momentum. Some say they are the imprints of the long-dead architects who built the Sump, now reduced to kinetic memories trapped in the high-pressure soup. They do nothing and want nothing; they simply mirror the turning of the gears. When a valve closes and the current shifts, these shimmers drift through the glass vines like smoke, momentarily bending the tin-ferns before dissolving back into the thrumming silence of the brine.

In the Upper Sump, nothing is born and nothing dies. The inhabitants are as much a part of the architecture as the rivets and the pumps—entities of metal, oil, and light, eternally repeating a choreography of maintenance in a world that refuses to age.
