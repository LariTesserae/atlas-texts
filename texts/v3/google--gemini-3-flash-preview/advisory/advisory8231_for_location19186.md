To visit the **Mineral Lung** is to step inside a clock that has no hands, only a steady, deafening pulse. For a human traveler, this environment is not hostile in the traditional sense—there are no predators, no toxins, and no crushing gravity—but it is profoundly *alien*. Your body is a soft, fast-moving, rotting thing in a world of hard, slow, immortal stone.

To survive and remain sane within this cathedral of brine, you must prepare for the physical sensory assault and the psychological weight of "frozen equilibrium."

### 1. Acoustic Shielding and Communication
The most immediate threat to your well-being is the **Sound**. Do not underestimate a percussion that never ends. The geometry of the salt-lattices turns every drip into a gunshot and every trickle into a roar.
*   **Active Noise-Canceling Gear:** You need high-grade, industrial-strength acoustic baffles. Bone-conduction headphones are preferable to over-ear models, as the humidity will cause sweat and condensation to pool in your outer ear, leading to rapid infection.
*   **Tactile Signaling:** Speech is useless. If you are traveling with a companion, do not rely on your voice. Use a system of "tap-signals" on the shoulder or hand, or utilize a heads-up display (HUD) in a sealed helmet.
*   **The "Vibration Sickness":** The constant drumming will vibrate your chest cavity and inner ear. Some travelers report "sonic nausea." Pack anti-emetics and heavy-duty wax earplugs as a secondary backup.

### 2. Specialized Locomotion (The "Soft-Step")
The floor of the geode is a fractured basin of wet silica and smooth crystal. It is more slippery than a frozen lake.
*   **Non-Slip Cleats:** Standard hiking boots will fail you. You need footwear with soft, high-friction rubber soles combined with retractable tungsten-carbide studs. 
*   **The "No-Touch" Protocol:** You are a giant in a miniature city. The salt-lattices and calcified spires are structurally sound but aesthetically fragile. A single clumsy movement could shatter a "tenement block" that took ten thousand years to grow. You must move with the deliberate precision of a clockmaker. 
*   **Crawl-Space Training:** The urban sprawl is dense. You will spend much of your time squeezing through gaps mere inches wide. If you suffer from claustrophobia, do not enter the geode.

### 3. Humidity and Skin Maintenance
The air is "heavy and cooling," but the 100% saturation means your sweat will not evaporate. You will be perpetually drenched in a mixture of your own perspiration and "cave sweat"—the wet tin and cold salt of the atmosphere.
*   **The Slimesuit:** Forget breathable fabrics like Gore-Tex; they will waterlog and become heavy. Wear a non-porous, skin-tight neoprene or silicone-polymer suit. This prevents the mineral-rich water from chafing your skin or causing "brine-burn."
*   **Antifungal Regimen:** Before entering, coat your body (especially feet and armpits) in a thick, long-lasting antifungal barrier. The geode is prebiotic, but *you* are not. You are a walking garden of spores. If you stay long enough, the mold from your own body will try to claim the "buildings." Be meticulous about your hygiene to avoid introducing an invasive ecosystem to this sterile world.

### 4. Visibility and Orientation
The bruised violet and indigo luminescence is beautiful but deceptive. It creates high-contrast shadows that hide deep fissures and jagged edges.
*   **Full-Spectrum Optics:** Bring thermal or LIDAR-assisted goggles. High-intensity flashlights are discouraged; the reflective nature of the silica and salt will create a "hall of mirrors" effect, potentially blinding you with your own reflected light.
*   **The Indigo Blues:** Human eyes struggle to perceive depth in monochrome violet light. Your brain will struggle to distinguish between a solid wall and a deep pool of water. Use a physical probe or walking stick to test every step.

### 5. Temporal Anchoring
This is the hardest part of the journey. In the Mineral Lung, there is no day, no night, and no change. 
*   **Tactile Timekeeping:** Bring a mechanical watch with a strong haptic vibration. You need to be reminded when to eat and sleep, as your circadian rhythm will collapse within hours. 
*   **The "Petrification" Fear:** Be hyper-aware of your belongings. If you drop a glove or a camera, the cave will begin encasing it in translucent stone within days. The "frozen equilibrium" is aggressive. It does not destroy; it absorbs. If you sit still for too long, you will feel the minerals beginning to bond to your suit.

### 6. Ethical Conduct (The Gastropod Protocol)
The translucent, pebble-sized gastropods are the only "residents." Because they move once every hundred years, they may appear to be inanimate jewels.
*   **Do not touch the "Fauna."** Your body heat alone could be catastrophic to a creature that lives in a "cooling world." To them, your passage is like a hurricane moving through a mountain range—a blur of heat and vibration that will be the most significant event in their multi-millennial lives. Observe them with the reverence you would give a living god.

### Summary Checklist:
*   **Suit:** Silicone-polymer skin-suit (non-absorbent).
*   **Feet:** Tungsten-studded, high-friction soles.
*   **Ears:** Active noise-canceling baffles + wax plugs.
*   **Light:** LIDAR/Infrared optics (avoid white light).
*   **Medical:** Antifungals, anti-emetics, skin-balm for brine-chafing.

**The Intuition:** You are entering a masterpiece of indifference. The geode does not want you there, but it does not hate you either. It is simply a place where time is measured in drips rather than seconds. To visit is to be a ghost in a city of salt—make sure you leave before the water realizes you are there and begins to turn you into a statue.
