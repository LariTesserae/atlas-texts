To prepare for a visit to the **Unfinished Sky-State**, you must abandon your terrestrial intuition. This is not a place that welcomes the soft, the hasty, or the unweighted. You are entering a realm where physics is still in its "beta" phase, and the atmosphere is a predatory thief of heat and breath.

Here is how you must prepare your body, your pack, and your mind for the vertical embryonic world.

### 1. The Suit: Thermal Armor and Friction-Management
Do not bring a raincoat; bring a **sealed, heated shell**.
*   **Abrasive Resistance:** The obsidian platelets and unweathered stones are sharper than surgical scalpels. A standard nylon hiking jacket will be shredded within hours. You need a heavy-duty, waxed canvas or reinforced leather outer layer—something that can scrape against a "razor-cliff" without failing. 
*   **The "Static Ground":** Because of the magnetic repulsion between the rocks and the churning of massive brass pistons, the air is thick with static electricity. Your clothing should be grounded or lined with a Faraday mesh to prevent painful arcs of electricity jumping from your fingertips to the iron railings.
*   **The Wet-Cold Barrier:** The cold here is deceptive. It is a "wet cold" that clings. You need moisture-wicking wool base layers. If you stay damp, the thin air will pull the heat from your core with terrifying efficiency.

### 2. Physical Augmentation: Lung and Limb
The elevation is your primary enemy. At this height, an ordinary human will suffer from hypoxia within minutes of exertion.
*   **The "Breather":** You will need a portable oxygen concentrator or a rebreather. More importantly, bring **Ozone Filters**. The metallic tang in the air isn't just a smell; it’s a caustic gas that will blister your lungs over time. A mask with an activated carbon filter is mandatory.
*   **Magnetized Footwear:** The iron-grated bridges are often slick with "thicken-air" condensation and soot. Boots with electromagnetic soles—or at least high-friction rubber—are necessary to navigate the humming pathways. In areas of gravitational breakdown, these boots will be the only thing keeping you tethered to the "ground."

### 3. Equipment: Specialized Tools for a Glacial World
*   **The Weight-Belt:** Because gravity is inconsistent, carry a belt with adjustable lead weights. If you enter a "slow-fall" pocket, you’ll need the extra mass to keep your momentum predictable. Conversely, if you feel a "heavy" pocket, you must be able to jettison weight instantly.
*   **Acoustic Dampeners:** The low-frequency roar of the massive gears and pistons can cause nausea, internal vibration, and "the tremors" in unaccustomed travelers. High-fidelity earplugs that filter out low frequencies while allowing you to hear voices (which the air already swallows) are essential.
*   **The "Fall-Chute" or Tether:** In a city of gaps, your greatest fear is the abyss. A compact, fast-deploying emergency parachute is a start, but given the magnetic interference, a high-tensile grappling tether is more reliable. If you slip, you don't want to drift; you want to anchor.

### 4. Navigational Logic: Shadow and Sound
Forget your compass; the magnetic repulsion of the platelets will make the needle spin uselessly.
*   **Boiler-Tracking:** Orient yourself by the heat. Near the massive boilers, the air is warmer and the shadows point toward the machinery. If you are lost in the violet-grey mist, follow the "pressure-hum." The louder the heartbeat of the city, the closer you are to a district hub.
*   **Visual Delays:** Remember that sound and sight are decoupled here. Do not trust your ears to tell you how far away a factory is. Trust the rhythmic "thinks" of the falling water droplets—they are the only constant clock in this world.

### 5. Biological Protocols
*   **The Diet:** Bring high-calorie, dense fats. Your body will burn immense energy just trying to stay warm and oxygenated. Avoid local flora; the silver moss and waxy leaves are often infused with heavy metals pumped out by the factories.
*   **Movement:** Adopt the **"Sky-State Shuffle."** Never run. Move with the same glacial deliberation as the inhabitants. Rapid movement in thin air leads to fainting, and fainting in a city of razor-edges and mile-deep gaps is a death sentence.

### 6. The Psychological Adjustment: The "Almost" Mindset
The most difficult thing to prepare for is the **Melancholy of the Unfinished**. 
*   **Accept the Static:** In our world, a bridge that hums and vibrates is a bridge that is failing. Here, that vibration is the only thing keeping the world together. You must learn to trust the tension.
*   **The Patience of Stones:** You will spend hours waiting for a bridge to align or a gear to turn. Bring a book or a manual task (like carving or knitting) to occupy your hands. The inhabitants "wait" as a form of meditation.
*   **The Translucent Giants:** If a sky-ray drifts near you to lick the condensation off your coat, do not panic. They are gentle grazers. Their presence indicates a stable pocket of air; if the rays flee, a pressure change is coming.

**Final Advice:** Do not look for a "finished" corner of this world. There isn't one. Embrace the soot, the damp, and the feeling that the floor beneath your feet is still deciding whether or not it wants to exist. If you can respect the slow, grinding birth of this place, the city will reveal a grim, industrial majesty that no "completed" world can match.
