To prepare for a journey into the **Verant Sink**, one must shed the instincts of a creature born to cycles. Here, there are no seasons, no sunsets, and no personal space. You are traveling to a location that functions like a cross between a cathedral and a crowded lung.

To survive and—more importantly—to remain sane in the "Cold Noon," you must adhere to the following protocols of preparation:

### 1. Visual Shielding: The "Blindness Protection"
The most immediate threat in the Verant Sink is **Photokeratitis** (snow blindness) on a scale you cannot imagine. The light is not just bright; it is *recursive*. It bounces off the white sky, the ice-needle trees, and the rime-coated walls until it is coming at you from every angle simultaneously.

*   **Dual-Layer Optics:** You need category 4 glacier goggles with side shields. Beneath these, wear polarized contacts to reduce the glare that will leak through even the tiniest gap.
*   **The "Shadow-Hood":** Since there is no natural shade, you must bring it with you. A wide, stiff-brimmed hat lined with matte-black fabric is essential. This creates a small, artificial "night" around your eyes, giving your pupils a place to rest.

### 2. Thermal Management: Breath and Skin
The Verant Sink is a "wet cold," which is far more dangerous to the human body than "dry cold." The 100% humidity will conduct the heat out of your body via the dampness of your own skin.

*   **Hydrophobic Layering:** Traditional wool will eventually become waterlogged and freeze into a board. You need high-performance synthetic base layers that repel moisture.
*   **Heat Recapture:** Because the air is "heavy" and frozen, breathing it directly will scar your lung tissue over time. Wear a copper-mesh heat-exchange mask (rebreath-style). It captures the warmth of your exhale to pre-heat the sub-arctic intake.
*   **The Scent of the Sink:** The air smells of "crushed mint and wet mineral." This is pleasant at first, but in a world with no wind, smells become oppressive. Carry a small tin of strong, pungent resin or charcoal bits to reset your olfactory senses when the "closeness" of the city becomes overwhelming.

### 3. Sensory Adaptation: The Language of Silence
In the Sink, noise is considered a physical assault. To scream is to vandalize the atmosphere.

*   **Footwear:** Leave boots behind. You need soft-soled, mukluk-style footwear made of high-friction synthetic hide. Your goal is to move across the damp moss without a sound.
*   **Haptic Communication:** You will be functionally deaf to the local culture unless you can feel it. Bring a "tactile amplifier"—a small device worn against the wrist or throat that translates structural vibrations into gentle pulses. This will allow you to "hear" the city’s tapping language and the slow thrum of the living Bone-Wood.
*   **The "Crowd Mind":** Prepare yourself for extreme claustrophobia. You will never be more than three feet away from another living thing. Practice meditation in confined spaces before you depart. You must learn to "retract" your presence inward so as not to feel violated by the constant proximity of the inhabitants.

### 4. Navigational Survival: Mastering the Warp and Weft
Because there are no streets or horizons, you will get lost within twenty minutes of arrival.

*   **Vertical Mapping:** Your GPS will fail in the dense, mineral-heavy hollow. Use a high-tension spool of luminous, ultra-thin wire to "breadcrumb" your path through the Bone-Wood capillaries. 
*   **Grip and Tension:** Everything is coated in silver rime. It is slick but viscous. Bring "climbing claws" for your hands—small, palm-mounted spikes that allow you to grip the fiber ladders and frozen reed bridges without slipping into the stagnant basins below.

### 5. Biological Etiquette
The ecology is a closed loop of "Stasis." Your very presence is an introduction of chaos into a perfected system.

*   **Sterilization:** The Verant Sink has no predators and likely no immunity to common multiverse bacteria. You must undergo a full microbial scrub. A simple human "cold" could be an extinction event for the moth-birds.
*   **The Law of Consumption:** Do not bring "fast" food. The inhabitants eat slow-growth fungi and viscous water. If you eat a high-calorie, processed energy bar, the smell of your sweat will be radically out of sync with the environment, making you a "loud" presence in a silent world. Stick to flavorless, odorless nutrient pastes.

### The Traveler’s Mindset: The "Slow Breath"
Finally, you must adjust your internal clock. In the Sink, the concept of "later" does not exist because "now" never changes. 

Do not rush. Do not offer to "improve" things. If you see a cracked joint in a Bone-Wood house, do not fix it with a bolt; sit and watch how a local spends three days carving a single resin peg to fit. You are not there to witness progress; you are there to witness **equivalence**. 

To survive the Verant Sink, you must become like the rime on the trees: present, still, and perfectly cold.
