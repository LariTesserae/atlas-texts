To visit this vertical sea is to step into a paradox: it is a world of crushing weight and absolute weightlessness, of embryonic youth and ancient stillness. For an ordinary human traveler, the transition is not merely environmental; it is psychological. You are moving from a world of friction and hurry into a world of viscosity and "The Long Wait."

Here is how you must prepare for the **Drowned Vertical**.

### 1. The Physical Interface: "The Second Skin"
You cannot wear a standard hard-shell diving suit. The water here is not like Earth’s; it is a "viscous gel" heavy with silicates. A mechanical suit with joints would likely seize as the grit grinds into the bearings. 

*   **Synthetic Mucus Suit:** You need a seamless, bio-polymer skin-suit. It must be slick enough to glide through the gel but reinforced with a micro-mesh to protect against the obsidian needles. In this world, the slightest brush against a pillar doesn't just cut—it shears. 
*   **Neutral Buoyancy Calibration:** Because the gravity only affects the fluid, your body’s natural density will be your greatest enemy. You must wear a belt of adjustable "void-pockets" (shunted vacuum cells). If you are too heavy, you will drift toward the core and be cooked by the radiating obsidian. If you are too light, you will float toward the outer membrane and "fall" into the vacuum of space through the water's surface tension.
*   **Liquid Ventilation:** You cannot breathe air here. If you carry a tank, the bubbles will not rise to a surface; they will simply hang in the gel, obscuring your vision and alerting the inhabitants to your frantic, "fast-time" biology. You must undergo a liquid-ventilation procedure, filling your lungs with oxygenated perfluorocarbon. You will have to get used to the sensation of "drowning" to survive.

### 2. Sensory Adaptation: "The Hum and the Glow"
Your eyes and ears are calibrated for a world of gas and light-speed. Here, they will betray you.

*   **The Haptic Ear:** Sound in this world is a physical impact. To communicate with the inhabitants, you must wear a haptic transducer on your sternum. Their low-frequency hums will not be heard by your ears—which will be muffled by the gel—but felt in your ribs. You must learn to "read" your own bone-vibrations.
*   **The Visual Kaleidoscope:** The obsidian pillars create a hall-of-mirrors effect. Light from the sun doesn't just illuminate; it shatters. You will see dozens of versions of yourself and the city. You need specialized "polarized-depth" goggles that can filter out reflections and highlight the bioluminescent purples and teals of the vegetation, allowing you to distinguish a real kelp-pod from its glassy reflection.
*   **Thermal Anchoring:** The temperature gradient is extreme. You must carry "thermal anchors"—small, slow-release cooling units—to prevent heatstroke as you move toward the core to visit the workshops.

### 3. Movement and Logistics: "The Slow Law"
On Earth, we move with momentum. In the Vertical Sea, momentum is a death sentence.

*   **The "Slow-Stroke":** You must train your muscles for high-resistance, low-velocity movement. If you try to swim fast, the viscosity of the gel will cause your muscles to produce massive amounts of lactic acid, and you will cramp within minutes. You must learn the "glide-crawl," moving with the agonizing patience of the Drifters.
*   **The Grappling Hook:** Carry a spool of synthetic fiber. Because there are no currents, if you find yourself in a pocket of open water away from the kelp-vines or obsidian pillars, you can become "marooned" in space, unable to reach anything to pull yourself along.
*   **Obsidian Safety:** Never touch a surface without checking it. The obsidian is "unweathered." This means the edges are molecularly thin. A casual handhold could result in the loss of fingers. You will need gauntlets tipped with industrial diamond-grit to gain purchase on the glass.

### 4. Psychological Preparation: "The Temporal Shift"
This is the most difficult part of the journey. You are a creature of seconds; they are creatures of seasons.

*   **Patience as a Survival Skill:** If you ask a question and expect an answer within the hour, you will be viewed as a frantic, malfunctioning animal. You must learn to lower your heart rate. You must be prepared to hang in the gel, unmoving, for six hours just to be "noticed" by a local.
*   **Sensory Deprivation Management:** The "profound, heavy silence" can induce হয়ে (the "quiet madness") in humans. The lack of wind, the lack of bird-call, and the absence of a horizon can make you feel as though you are buried alive in a warm, emerald tomb. Bring a "clicker" or a steady, rhythmic metronome to keep your mind tethered to a sense of passing time.
*   **The Tectonic Groan:** Prepare yourself for the sound of the core settling. It is described as a "physical blow." It will feel like the world is shattering. You must remain limp during these shifts; if you tense your body when the sound wave hits, the vibration can break your bones.

### 5. Social Etiquette: "The Woven Protocol"
*   **Respect the Ropes:** The braided ropes of fiber and pitch are the lifeblood of the city. Never cut one, and never untie a knot that isn't yours. A single rope may represent the work of a generation. 
*   **Avoid the Drifters:** Do not touch the lace-umbrellas. They are the lungs of the world. To interfere with a Drifter is to commit an act of environmental terrorism. If one tangles in your path, you must wait for it to pulse—once an hour—and use the momentary expansion to slip past.

### Final Summary for the Traveler:
You are entering a world that is still being born. It is raw, sharp, and slow. You will feel like a ghost—too fast to be seen clearly, too frail to touch the geometry of the world. 

**Your Mantra:** *Be like the gel. Hold your breath, hold your tongue, and wait for the hum.*
