To visit the Floor of the Siphon is to enter a digestive tract made of iron. You are stepping into a landscape that treats biological life as a contaminant or, at best, an irrelevance. Preparation for this journey must be meticulous, focusing on three specific challenges: **physical protection from a jagged environment, sensory management, and navigational philosophy.**

Here is how you must prepare.

### 1. The "Second Skin": Personal Protective Equipment
Standard hiking gear will be shredded within the first mile. The Siphon is a world of "young erosion"—the metal is sharp enough to shave with, and the rivets are designed to catch and tear.

*   **The Shell:** You need a high-denier, puncture-resistant hardshell suit. Look for something reinforced with flexible ceramic plating or aramid fibers (Kevlar). It must be entirely waterproof, as the "machine-sweat" is not pure water—it is laced with industrial lubricants, coolants, and heavy metal particulates that will cause chemical burns or systemic poisoning if allowed to sit on your skin.
*   **The Grip:** Standard rubber soles will fail on the oily, shimmering ceramic tiles. You need boots with magnetic-assist soles or, at the very least, acid-resistant, high-friction treads designed for oil-slicked industrial decks. Deep lugs are necessary for the "tide-runs" when the drainage gates flush; you will need to wedge yourself against bulkheads to avoid being swept into obsidian pools.
*   **The Hands:** Heavy-duty reinforced gauntlets are non-negotiable. You will spend much of your time bracing yourself against freezing pipes or pulling yourself over rusted ridges. Ensure they have high thermal resistance; a pipe might be freezing one moment and venting scalding steam the next.

### 2. Respiratory and Visual Management
The air in the Siphon is not meant for lungs. It is a charcoal-thick haze of suspended oils and fine metallic dust.

*   **The Filter:** A full-face respirator is mandatory. It needs multi-stage filtration capable of scrubbing volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and ultra-fine particulates. The "charcoal haze" will clog basic masks within hours. Carry spare filters—when they begin to smell like burnt ozone, they are spent.
*   **The Optics:** The lighting is deceptive. Between the flickering sodium lamps and the "angry orange" furnace glares, your depth perception will fail. Bring a high-lumen, wide-beam headlamp to cut through the haze, but also active-matrix HUD goggles. These will help you highlight the "edges" of the geometry, which often disappear in the wet, black reflections of the floor.
*   **The Anti-Fog:** Because of the constant "machine-sweat" and humidity, lens fogging is a lethal hazard. Treat all glass with heavy-duty surfactants. If you can’t see the jagged edge of a vent ridge, you will be sliced before you feel the cold.

### 3. Sensory Survival and The Inner Ear
The sound of the Siphon is an assault. Prolonged exposure to low-frequency tectonic vibrations (the "marrow-thrum") can cause "Vibroacoustic Disease," leading to disorientation, nausea, and cardiac arrhythmia.

*   **Active Noise Cancellation:** You need industrial-grade, noise-canceling ear protection that filters out the deafening thrum and the thunderous pressure-release valves while still allowing you to hear high-frequency warnings, like the hiss of a failing steam-main.
*   **Bone-Conduction Communication:** If you are traveling with others, air-conduction mics will be useless against the industrial symphony. Use bone-conduction sets that sit against your jaw.

### 4. Sustenance and "Biological Stealth"
There is no "foraging" here. The siphone-moss and synthetic fungi are likely toxic or composed of indigestible polymers.

*   **Self-Contained Rations:** Bring all food in sealed, easy-to-open pouches. Do not expose your food to the air. The oily film in the air will settle on anything edible in seconds, giving it a metallic, chemical taste that will ruin your digestion.
*   **Waste Management:** In a world designed for "thermal efficiency," your heat signature and biological waste are anomalies. To avoid attracting the "Scrap-Swallows" or maintenance drones—which might perceive you as a "clog" or a source of stray minerals—carry heat-shielding blankets to mask your rest sites.

### 5. Navigational Philosophy: Mapping the Rhythm
In the Siphon, traditional maps are useless because the geography is "dynamic." A corridor may be a reservoir wall one day and a dry vent the next.

*   **Follow the Sound:** Your primary compass is the "deepest engine." The deeper you go, the older and more massive the machinery. If you lose your way, find a structural pillar and feel the vibration. The strongest, most rhythmic pulse usually leads toward the primary conduits, which serve as the "highways" of the Floor.
*   **The Shift-Weather Clock:** Observe the overhead fans. Their rotation is your sun and moon. When they begin to slow and reverse, you have minutes to find a "sheltered" ridge or a recessed bulkhead. The "purge-gale" is a horizontal blizzard of freezing condensation that can induce hypothermia in minutes.
*   **Mechanical Empathy:** Do not touch the "Fauna." The maintenance drones are not sentient, but they are programmed for efficiency. If you are standing on a seam they need to weld, they will attempt to weld through you. Treat them like heavy weather—predictable, indifferent, and dangerous.

### Final Intuition
Walking the Floor of the Siphon is a lesson in humility. You are a soft, wet creature moving through a hard, dry mind. The Siphon isn't "angry" at you; it simply doesn't recognize that you are alive. Your goal is to remain a "ghost" within its cycles—a brief, warm shadow passing through a cathedral of cold, weeping iron. **Keep your hands inside the ride at all times.**
