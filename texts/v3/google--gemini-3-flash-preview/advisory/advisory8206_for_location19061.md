To visit this location—let us call it **The Verticality**—is to enter a world that is fundamentally hostile to the physics of biology. You are a creature of water, carbon, and rounded edges; this is a world of heat, silica, and the "upward violence" of glass. 

An ordinary traveler who steps onto these obsidian cathedrals without intense preparation will be sliced open, dehydrated, or crushed by the sheer kinetic energy of the crust within the first hour.

Here is how you must prepare for a journey into the Growing Dark.

---

### 1. Protective Skin: The "Second Crust"
The primary threat is the architecture itself. The spires are shards of planetary scale. Every surface is an obsidian blade. An ordinary suit of Kevlar or heavy leather will be shredded the moment you slip or lean against a wall.

*   **Materials:** You require an outer shell of **liquid-metal mesh or ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (UHMWPE) laminated with ceramic plating.** It must be slick and frictionless.
*   **Thermal Regulation:** The heat here is "visceral" and constant. You aren't just protecting yourself from ambient air temperature, but from **conductive heat**. If you touch a spire, the thermal energy of the planet’s core will bleed directly into your suit. Your gear must include a closed-loop liquid cooling system. 
*   **The "Softness" Problem:** You are a "soft thing." In a world of brittle glass, softness is a liability. Your boots must have a hyper-specialized grip—magnetic or vacuum-based—because the surfaces are vitreous and vertical. There is no soil for traction; there is only the grip of a fly on a windowpane.

### 2. Sensory Equipment: Seeing the Unseen
Because the only light is the "bruised twilight" of the rocks and the amber pulse of the lithotrophic webs, human eyes are nearly useless. 

*   **Thermographic Overlay:** You need haptic or visual sensors that convert heat into a color spectrum. Since the landscape is defined by its thermal energy, a thermal visor will allow you to see the "veins" of the world.
*   **Acoustic Mapping:** Because the spires are constantly shifting, buckling, and thrusting, you need a **seismic ear**. You must be able to "hear" the stress patterns in the obsidian. If a spire is about to fracture or a cliffside is beginning to "fold like taffy," you need to know minutes before the sound reaches you. Without this, you will be caught in a tectonic collapse that moves with the speed of shattering glass.

### 3. Navigation: The Vertical Mindset
In The Verticality, there is no "ground." There is only the spire you are on and the abyss below.

*   **Grappling and Suspension:** You cannot walk through this world; you must bridge it. Pack high-tensile carbon-fiber lines. Because the air is dry and static-heavy, your lines will naturally attract the "silver webs." Be cautious: the lithotrophic webs are mineral, but they are "hungry." They may attempt to encrust your equipment to harvest its heat.
*   **Avoid the "Built":** While the cantilevered platforms and bored holes offer the only flat ground, they are psychologically dangerous. Human travelers report a crushing sense of "insignificance vertigo" near the Built. These structures are fixed points in a moving world; the friction between the growing mountains and the unmoving Built creates unpredictable "static storms."

### 4. Biological Preservation
The air here is a "dry, metallic static." It will pull the moisture from your mucus membranes and eyeballs in seconds.

*   **Total Enclosure:** Do not breathe the air. It is likely thick with microscopic shards of silica and volcanic sulfur. A punctured seal is a death sentence; the "glass dust" will crystallize in your lungs, turning them into stone from the inside out.
*   **Hydration:** You will need three times the standard water intake. The "low-frequency hum" of the crust isn't just a sound; it’s a vibration that can cause internal nausea and rapid-onset fatigue.

### 5. The Psychological Prep: The Solaris Effect
Perhaps the hardest thing to prepare for is the **Sentience of Scale**. You are entering a world that is an "embryonic mineral mind." 

*   **The Hum:** The constant vibration of the growing earth can lead to auditory hallucinations. Travelers often report hearing voices in the "whistling hiss" of the rising heat. You must train your mind to recognize that the planet is not talking to you—it is simply *iterating.*
*   **Vertical Agoraphobia:** Most humans are used to the horizon. Here, the horizon is a jagged upward line. Your inner ear will struggle. You will feel a constant urge to lean into the spires, a biological "hugging" of the stone that can lead to being trapped when the minerals shift.

### Final Advice: The Silver Rule
If you see the silver, crystalline lace of the lithotrophic webs beginning to glow brighter, move immediately. They are reacting to an incoming surge of thermal energy. It means a new spire is about to be born beneath you. 

Do not try to stand still. This is not a place for standing; it is a place for passing through. In a world of "becoming," the only way to survive is to never stop moving.
