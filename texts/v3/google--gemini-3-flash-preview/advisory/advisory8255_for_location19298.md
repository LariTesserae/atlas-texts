To prepare for a journey into the **World-Root City**, one must reconcile with the scale of the inhabitant. You are entering a space where the distinction between "building" and "body" has dissolved. To survive and remain comfortable in this vegetable cathedral, your preparations must be as tactile and rhythmic as the city itself.

Here is how you should prepare for the "Damp-Cool."

### 1. Attire and Thermal Regulation
The temperature is a deceptive "cellar-chill"—it is a constant 52°F (11°C). It does not fluctuate with a sun that does not exist.
*   **The Layering Principle:** Avoid heavy, bulky parkas. The humidity is high enough that heavy wool will become sodden and sodden wool becomes a heat-sink. Instead, wear a base layer of moisture-wicking synthetic silk, topped by a heavy, waxed-canvas coat. This allows you to repel the rhythmic "drip-clocks" while keeping your core temperature stable.
*   **Footwear:** Do not bring soft-soled shoes. The "lignin" floor is as hard as mahogany and the walkways are serrated steel grating. You need heavy, lug-soled boots with brass toe-caps. The brass is essential; in an environment governed by biological tension, sparking steel against the root can be seen as a minor transgression. Leather soles will slip on the damp moss; rubber will perish in the oily sap-mist.
*   **Gloves:** Reinforced leather work gloves. You will spend much of your time gripping cold brass handrails or chain-link guards as you navigate the gaps between root-buttresses.

### 2. Respiratory Health and Sensory Management
The air is heavy. It smells of "old library," crushed cloves, and wet copper.
*   **The Lung-Filter:** While the air is breathable, the sheer density of fungal spores from the bioluminescent lanterns can trigger "Root-Cough" in multiverse travelers. Carry a filtered breather or a simple silk scarf soaked in eucalyptus oil. It will help clear the heaviness of the "stone lung" atmosphere.
*   **Acoustic Dampeners:** The silence here is punctuated by the *clack-thud* of sap-pistons—a sound that carries through the wood with bone-shaking frequency. If you are sensitive to low-frequency vibrations, bring earplugs. Otherwise, the constant, rhythmic groaning of the wood under tension can lead to a specific type of vertigo known as "The Sway."

### 3. Navigation and Tools
The city is in "glacial motion." Maps here are dated by the decade, and a map from fifty years ago is a charming piece of fiction.
*   **The Grafting Kit:** Even a traveler is expected to be a "meticulous parasite." Carry a small kit of conductive copper tape and resin-salve. If you accidentally nick a wall or see a leaking pipe-graft, the local etiquette dictates you seal the wound. This is your "rent" for breathing the root's air.
*   **The Amber-Light:** Your own light source should be warm-spectrum. Harsh white LEDs are offensive to the "scripter" insects and can cause the world-root to withdraw its local capillaries, leading to structural sagging. An amber-tinted lantern is the standard.
*   **Chronometry:** Do not rely on celestial time. Your internal clock will break in the perpetual twilight. Use a mechanical watch—the "drip-clocks" of the city are reliable, but only if you know which cistern you are standing over.

### 4. Psychological Preparedness: "The Scale of the Long-Breath"
The most difficult thing for a human traveler to adjust to is the **slow-time**.
*   **Patience as a Virtue:** You might wait at a bridge for an hour for a piston to recycle or a root-valve to open. Do not rush. Aggressive movement creates heat and vibrations that the root perceives as an irritant.
*   **The Solitude of Distance:** Prepare for the "vastness of the emptiness." You will spend hours walking through cathedral-like voids of polished amber wood where the only sound is your own heartbeat and the distant *hiss* of steam. This is not a place for the agoraphobic or those who fear silence.
*   **The Ethics of the Parasite:** You must shift your mindset. You are not a master of the environment; you are a guest in a lung. Every action—from where you dispose of waste to how loudly you speak—affects the health of the host. If the root "withraws," the city dies.

### 5. Nutrition and Hydration
The water here is "exhaled" and filtered by feet of biological fiber.
*   **The Sap-Drought:** The water in the moss-lined cisterns is incredibly rich in minerals and mild alkaloids. It is safe to drink, but it tastes of earth and ancient tannin. It will make you feel alert, but strangely heavy-limbed. If you prefer pure water, you must bring a portable distiller.
*   **Food:** Nothing grows here except fungi and the root itself. Local "hamlet" food consists of compressed lichen cakes and nutrient pastes derived from the sap-engines. It is caloric and functional, but flavorless. Bring dried fruits or spices to break the monotony of the "settled erosion" diet.

### Summary of the Vibe
Entering the World-Root City is like walking into a **grandfather clock made of living flesh.** It is cool, damp, rhythmic, and indifferent to your presence. To thrive there, you must move like the brass scripter insects: quietly, usefully, and with a deep respect for the enormous, breathing organism that keeps you from the cold, dark stone of the basin.
