To visit the **Caldera Sink** is to become a component in a machine that does not know you are there. It is a world of verticality, friction, and thermal weight. For an ordinary human traveler, the preparation is less about survival gear and more about sensory adaptation and "mechanical integration."

Here is how you must prepare for your descent into the subterranean industrial hum.

### 1. The Physical Attire: "The Second Skin"
Forget traditional hiking gear. You are entering a landscape of sharp edges and shifting temperatures.
*   **The Layering of Breathable Synthetics:** You need fabrics that handle the "Intermittent Moisture." Heavy cotton will become a sodden, heavy rag within twenty minutes. Wear a base layer of moisture-wicking synthetic silk—something that allows the sweat to move off your skin before the ambient steam cooks it against you.
*   **The Abrasion Shell:** Over your base layer, wear a high-denier, tear-resistant jumpsuit (Kevlar-weave or heavy canvas). The "freshman" geometry of the basalt and the rusted rivets of the corrugated iron are hungry for skin. You will be squeezing through gaps; you need a "slick" exterior that won't snag.
*   **The Footwear (High-Traction/Steel Shank):** The floor is either searingly hot or slick with oily condensation. You need thick-soled boots with a "vibram" or high-friction grip for the wet catwalks, but with a steel shank to protect your arches while standing on narrow, vibrating ladder rungs for hours.

### 2. Respiratory and Ocular Defense
The air in the Sink is not poisonous, but it is *invasive*.
*   **Ozone Filter/Respirator:** The scent of parboiled copper and ozone is more than just a smell; it’s a metallic load on the lungs. A low-profile respirator is essential. Not for oxygen—the air is breathable—but to filter the micro-particulates of grease and the heavy, humid weight of the steam.
*   **Ventilated Goggles:** Do not use sealed glass; the condensation will blind you instantly. You need anti-fog, ventilated "tanker" goggles. The "weather" (the sudden billows of hot fog) will drop visibility to zero. You need to keep your eyes open for moving flywheels and hissing vents without your own breath fogging your vision.

### 3. Navigation: "Thinking Vertical"
The Sink has no horizon. Your internal compass will fail because the environment doesn't move left or right; it moves *up* and *through*.
*   **A "Haptic" Map:** Digital GPS will fail in this basaltic cage, and paper maps will dissolve in the steam. Travelers use a "feel-map"—a mental or physical tactile guide. 
*   **The Rule of the Three Points:** When moving through the Sink, you are always climbing. Prepare your body for "industrial parkour." You must adopt the mountaineer’s rule: always have three points of contact (two hands, one foot; or two feet, one hand) on the ladders and gantries. The "low-frequency vibration" makes every surface slightly unstable.

### 4. Thermal Management: "The Cooling Cycle"
The temperature is a constant 2.9/3—oppressive and unyielding.
*   **Cool-Pocket Scouting:** Your survival depends on identifying the "cool-pockets" where oily slicks gather. These are your "oases." When your heart rate spikes from the radiant heat of the furnaces, you must find these damp shadows to lower your core temperature.
*   **Hydration (Electrolytes, not just Water):** You will be sweating at a rate your body isn't used to. You must carry a pressurized hydration bladder—drinking from a cup is impossible when the air is 80% steam. Pack high-sodium salts; the Sink leaches the minerals out of you through your pores.

### 5. Sensory and Psychological Conditioning
The Sink is "quiet," but its silence is a heavy, mechanical pressure.
*   **The Sound:** The "thump" of the pressure-release valves can be startling. It vibrates through the soles of your feet before it hits your ears. You must train yourself not to flinch. A flinch on a narrow, oily gantry over a boiling furnace vent is fatal.
*   **The Solitude:** There are no animals. No insects. The lack of biological life creates a profound "loneliness of the gut." Bring something familiar and organic—a piece of dried wood, a leather pouch—something that wasn't forged or riveted. You will need to touch something "un-mechanical" to stay grounded.
*   **The "Boiler-Vine" Protocol:** Do not touch the vegetation. The boiler-vines have radiator fins that vibrate. Touching them can cause high-frequency friction burns or simply disrupt their delicate thermal balance. Observe the pale mosses, but do not provide them with the moisture of your touch.

### 6. The "Golden Rule" of the Sink
**Never lean on a pipe.** 
In the Caldera Sink, a pipe might be carrying freezing coolant, high-pressure steam, or nothing at all—and there is no way to tell by looking. Every metal surface is a potential conductor of either life-giving power or lethal heat. Treat the architecture as if it were a living, predatory beast that is currently sleeping.

**The Experience:**
When you enter, your skin will prickle. Your heartbeat will sync with the low-frequency hum of the city-block metropolis. You will feel small—not because the spaces are big, but because the *density* of the work being done around you is so vast. You are an intruder in a cellar window of the gods, a witness to a machine that breathes steam and sweats oil. 

Move slowly. Keep your hands in your pockets when not climbing. And when the white fog rolls in, stay still. Wait for the machine to exhale. Then, and only then, keep climbing.
