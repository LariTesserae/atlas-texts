To visit the **Calyx of the Grinding Piston** is to volunteer to be a grain of sand inside the engine of a god. For an ordinary human, this environment is not merely hostile; it is fundamentally incompatible with carbon-based biology. You are entering a realm of "Dry Life" and geological time.

To survive the Great Lung, your preparation must focus on three pillars: **Thermal Isolation**, **Structural Integrity**, and **Molecular Dehydration.**

### 1. The Suit: The "Sintered Sarcophagus"
Standard EVA suits or fire-entry suits will fail here. The 300°C heat is dry and constant; most gaskets and seals would melt or become brittle within minutes.

*   **Ablative Ceramic Shell:** Your suit must be a rigid, multi-layered hardshell of white-phosphorus ceramics or sintered tungsten. It needs to reflect the dull red infrared radiation of the friction-points while insulating against the massive convective heat.
*   **Solid-State Cooling:** Conventional liquid cooling loops are a death sentence (see Rule #2: Water is Poison). If your cooling line leaks, you will trigger a steam explosion that will shred you and your surroundings. You must use a **Thermoelectric-Peltier solid-state system** that dumps heat into "heat sinks" you carry on your back—disposable blocks of high-density salt that you eject once they reach their thermal capacity.
*   **Acoustic Dampening:** The low-frequency roar of the tectonic pistons will liquefy your internal organs through vibration alone. Your suit must be lined with non-Newtonian acoustic gels to decouple your body from the vibrations of the obsidian floor.

### 2. Physical Conditioning: The "Hard Dry"
You cannot bring water. You cannot sweat. You cannot breathe the "air."

*   **Total Internal Substitution:** Before departure, a traveler must undergo a "Hard Dry" protocol. Your blood must be temporarily enriched with synthetic thickeners to prevent capillary collapse in the high-pressure environment. Most importantly, you must be fitted with an internal, closed-circuit breathing apparatus.
*   **The No-Moisture Mandate:** You must be scrubbed of every trace of surface moisture. Your skin must be treated with desiccated powders. If a single tear falls from your eye or sweat beads on your brow inside the suit, the humidity could trigger a chemical reaction with the litho-parasites or the environment's metallic dust. You enter as a mummy, encased in a machine.

### 3. Navigation: The Geometry of the Crevice
The Calyx does not allow for upright walking. The "terrain" is a crushing verticality of razor-edged basalt.

*   **Molecular Lubricants:** Carry canisters of high-temp synthetic silicon oil. If you get caught between two of the obsidian plates, spraying this oil might give you the fractional slip needed to escape before the geological pressure pins you.
*   **Magnetic Anchors:** Since the "floor" is made of obsidian (non-magnetic) but covered in iron-shelled Litho-Parasites, do not rely on magnets. Carry **mechanical expansion wedges**. To rest, you must wedge yourself into a stable crack; if you sleep on the floor, the Litho-Parasites will mistake your suit for a vibrating mineral shelf and attempt to weld themselves to you, encasing you in an iron crust within hours.

### 4. Psychological Training: "The Century Mindset"
The most dangerous thing in the Calyx is your own sense of time. 

*   **Temporal Calibration:** Humans operate in seconds; the Calyx operates in decades. You will see a pillar moving toward a wall and panic, thinking you will be crushed. In reality, that pillar might take twelve years to reach the wall. You must train your brain to ignore the "roar" of movement and focus on the stillness of the result. 
*   **Sensory Deprivation Management:** You will be in near-total darkness, save for the hellish red glow of friction. The sound is a "deafening silence"—a roar so loud it eventually becomes a flat, physical weight on your mind. Travelers often experience "Lithic Psychosis," where they begin to believe they are turning into stone.

### 5. Interaction with the Fauna: The Litho-Parasites
The beetles are not aggressive, but they are "heavy."

*   **Vibration Mimicry:** Your suit should have a "thrummer"—a small motor that vibrates at the same frequency as the Great Lung. If you vibrate correctly, the Litho-Parasites will perceive you as part of the mountain and leave you alone. If you are "still," you are a foreign object to be filtered and consumed for mineral dust.
*   **Armor-Plating Caution:** Do not touch the walls with bare hands (even if you could survive the heat). The crystalline "fuzz" is statically charged. Touching it will result in a discharge that could fry your suit’s electronics or weld your gauntlet to the basalt.

### The Traveler’s Kit
1.  **Ultrasonic Rangefinders:** Light is useless; use sound to map the gaps between the pistons.
2.  **Ceramic "Chasers":** Sharp-edged wedges used to clear Litho-Parasites from your vision port.
3.  **Solid Oxygen Pellets:** No tanks. Pellets that chemically release O2 when heated, integrated into your suit’s life support.
4.  **Static Grounding Tether:** To bleed off the massive electrical charge the "furred" walls will dump into you.

**The Final Warning:** If you hear a sudden change in the pitch of the roar—from a low thrum to a high-pitched scream—it means a tectonic valve has slipped. You have approximately six years to clear the area before the resulting pressure wave reaches your location. Do not run; move with the deliberate, agonizing slowness of the stone itself. In the Calyx, speed is a flaw of the fragile. To survive the Lung, you must convince the universe you are a rock.
