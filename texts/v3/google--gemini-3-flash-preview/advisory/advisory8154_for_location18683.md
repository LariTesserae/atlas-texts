To visit the Pelagic Megastructure is to exist as a foreign body within a gargantuan, liquid-filled engine. This is not a voyage of "exploration" in the traditional sense; it is an exercise in industrial survival. You are entering a world that is high-pressure, high-decibel, and chillingly indifferent to organic life.

Here is how you must prepare for the **Pelagic Megastructure**.

### 1. Physiological Preparation: The Hard-Shell Requirement
The sheer scale and pressure of the Megastructure make a traditional wet suit or even a standard scuba setup useless. 
*   **Atmospheric Diving Suit (ADS):** You need a rigid-body, hardshell suit (often called an "Iron Duke" or "Exosuit") capable of withstanding the immense crushing force of miles-deep water. Because there is no "surface" to return to for decompression, your suit must be a self-contained, constant-pressure environment. 
*   **The "Thrum" Dampening:** The noise of the Megastructure is not heard; it is felt. The rhythmic, hydraulic *thrum-clack* of the valves can cause internal hemorrhaging or cardiac arrhythmia in an unprotected human. You must line your suit with active acoustic-cancellation pads.
*   **Thermal Regulation:** The water is kept near freezing to cool the hidden engines. You will need a nuclear or long-life battery-powered heating filament woven into your thermal under-layer. If your suit’s power fails, you have roughly twelve minutes before the Megastructure’s heat-sink effect drains your life force.

### 2. High-Density Navigation and "Propulsion"
In a void of brine where the nearest wall is ten miles away, "swimming" is a death sentence. You will be a speck in a vacuum.
*   **DPV (Diver Propulsion Vehicle):** You require a heavy-duty, twin-turbine underwater scooter. It must be powerful enough to fight the **Circulation**. If you are pulled toward a primary filtration grate, you will need "emergency burst" thrust to escape the intake draw.
*   **Sonar and Acoustic Mapping:** Visual light from the luminaires is deceptive; it creates "flatness" and kills depth perception. You need a heads-up display (HUD) that uses active sonar to paint a wireframe of the bulkheads and lattice-works. 
*   **Magnetized Tethers:** When you find a wall, do not let go. Carry high-grade electromagnets. The cobalt-steel construction of the hull is perfect for docking. Walking with magnetized boots along the "ceiling" is often safer than drifting in the open mid-water.

### 3. Management of the Fauna
The life here is "industrial-biological"—it is hungry, fast, and agitated by the machinery.
*   **Electronic Shark Deterrent (ESD):** The apex predators hunt via the electrical hum of the world. You must carry a "Ghost Signal" emitter that masks your suit’s electrical signature. To them, you should look like just another piece of non-nutritive machinery.
*   **Anti-Fouling Coating:** The silver-scaled fish move in massive, panicked shoals. If a school of ten million fish decides to move through your coordinates, they can strip the paint from your suit or jam your turbines with their bodies. Use a slick, hydrophobic coating on your gear to ensure they slide off you rather than dragging you into their mass.

### 4. Psychological Preparation: The "Benthic Agoraphobia"
Most humans are afraid of tight spaces. In the Pelagic Megastructure, you will face the opposite: the terror of the infinite void.
*   **Sensory Anchoring:** Because the light is "warehouse white" and constant, you will lose your sense of time and orientation. There is no day/night cycle. You must adhere to a strict "Chronos-Clock." Check your gauges every five minutes to remind yourself that you are a physical object in a physical space.
*   **The Scale Reflex:** When you approach a "rivet" that is the size of a house, your brain will struggle to process the scale. You may experience vertigo or "The Gulp"—a sudden, paralyzing realization of your insignificance. Practice mindfulness techniques to focus only on the two meters of space immediately surrounding your suit.

### 5. Survival Protocols: The Law of Circulation
You must treat the water like a series of conveyor belts.
*   **Flow-Mapping:** Before entering a new "room," observe the "snow" (the falling salt crystals). If the crystals are falling straight, the water is stagnant and safe. If they are swirling horizontally, you are near a turbine. If they are being sucked downward at an angle, you are in a **Filtration Lane**. 
*   **The Sleet:** The "heavy sleet" of salt crystals is abrasive. Over time, it will sandblast your visor. Ensure you have a self-polishing glass treatment or a supply of disposable clear-film sheets to peel off when your vision becomes clouded.

### The Intuition of the Place
When you arrive, the first thing you will notice isn't the water, but the **vibration**. You are inside a living lung of steel. The Megastructure feels "new"—there is no smell of decay, only the metallic tang of ionized seawater and the sterile scent of recycled oxygen in your helmet. 

It is a world of **terrifying order**. There is no chaos here, only a frantic, mechanical pace. Your goal is to move like a parasite: quiet, clinging to the edges of the ribs, staying out of the great veins of the Circulation, and watching the silver clouds of fish move in their desperate, beautiful circles. 

Do not try to find a heart to this place. The Megastructure **is** the heart. It is a pump that never stops. Just keep your magnets locked, your heaters on, and your ears shielded from the grinding song of the valves.
