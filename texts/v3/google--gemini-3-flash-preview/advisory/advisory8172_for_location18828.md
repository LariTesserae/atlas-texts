Visiting the Great Exhaust is not like visiting a planet; it is like becoming a parasite inhabiting the pulmonary system of a god. To survive here as an ordinary human, you must prepare for a landscape that is vertically absolute, acoustically overwhelming, and perpetually humid.

Here is how you must prepare for your descent—or ascent—into the Iron Maw.

### 1. Acoustic Conditioning and The "Resonance Suit"
The most immediate danger is not the heights, but the sound. In the Great Exhaust, sound is a physical force. The low-frequency thrumming can cause "vibro-acoustic disease," leading to internal hemorrhaging or psychological collapse as your organs attempt to vibrate at the machine’s frequency.

*   **Active-Dampening Ear Protection:** You need high-grade, military-spec noise-canceling inserts. Do not use over-ear muffs; the humidity will rot the foam in days. 
*   **The Bone-Inhibitor Vest:** Wear a lead-lined or high-density polymer vest. This breaks up the resonance of the machine before it can liquefy your internal organs or shatter your teeth.
*   **Communication:** Your voice is useless. Learn a dialect of tactile sign language (pressure-based) or bring a laser-comm device that reflects off the brass pipes.

### 2. Mobility: The Mag-Anchor System
In a world of "Total Builtness," there is no flat ground. Your greatest enemy is the "Slickness"—the film of oil, condensation, and verdigris that makes every surface a slide.

*   **Magnetic-Induction Boots:** Standard rubber soles are a death sentence. You need boots with high-strength electromagnets in the soles, allowing you to walk up vertical brass plates. Ensure they have a manual "mechanical bypass" in case the internal battery dies.
*   **The "Spider" Harness:** You must never have fewer than three points of contact with the machine. Use a motorized winch-harness with magnetic grappling hooks. Note: Because the machine parts move (the slow, tectonic crawl), never anchor yourself to two different moving systems simultaneously, or you will be pulled apart over the course of a week.
*   **Gloves:** Heavy, heat-resistant Kevlar. The rust-crust is abrasive, and the copper pipes can reach temperatures that will sear skin instantly upon contact.

### 3. Respiratory and Skin Care
The air is "thick." You are breathing a soup of aerosolized lubricant, oxidized copper dust, and fern spores.

*   **The Rebreather-Filter:** A standard gas mask is too hot. Use a lightweight nasal cannula connected to a filtration hip-pack. It must be able to filter out "Oil-Lung" (the accumulation of industrial lubricants in human lung tissue).
*   **Fungal Prophylactics:** The humidity is 100%. Without systemic antifungals, your skin will develop "Rust-Rash"—a violent fungal infection that mimics the green verdigris of the pipes. Apply a hydrophobic, silicone-based barrier cream to every inch of your body twice a day.

### 4. Navigating the "Slow Dynamic"
Time works differently here. You might plan to cross a bridge, only to find the bridge is a massive connecting rod that is slowly retracting over the next three days.

*   **The Chronometer of Gears:** Do not trust a digital watch; the electromagnetic interference from the static-feeding vines will fry it. Carry a heavy, mechanical pendulum-watch. You will need to calibrate your sense of time to the *clack* of the city-sized locking mechanisms.
*   **The "Falling Prayer":** Carry a pressurized CO2 emergency pack. If you slip into the void, you will fall for a long time before you die. A thruster pack won't save you from the engine, but it might allow you to steer yourself toward a passing vine or a lower heat-shield.

### 5. Ecological Etiquette
The flora and fauna are not "natural" in the way you understand. They are bio-mechanical opportunists.

*   **Bio-Static Conductors:** Carry "dead" metal rods or copper wire coils. If you encounter a cluster of the translucent liverworts, toss a coil away from you. They feed on static; they will move toward the metal and away from your electronics.
*   **Refractive Clothing:** The vent-birds are attracted to light. Wear matte, rust-colored fabrics to blend into the machine. If you shine a bright flashlight, you may be swarmed by thousands of pale-feathered scavengers curious about the new "star" in their bronze sky.
*   **Toxin Awareness:** Do not eat the "oil-lilies" or the tuberous vines. They contain high concentrations of arsenic and lead leached from the machine's alloy. Your food must be entirely self-contained and sealed in vacuum-bags.

### The Traveler’s Mindset
You must accept a total loss of horizontal perspective. In the Great Exhaust, there is only *The Interior* and *The Void*. 

The most unsettling sensation you will experience is not the fear of falling, but the **Scale-Dread**. When a flywheel the size of a mountain begins its weekly revolution, the displacement of air is so vast it feels like the world is inhaling. Do not fight the rhythm. Sync your breathing to the thrum of the piston. To survive the Great Exhaust, you must stop being a human and start being a gear.
