Preparing for the **Cold Drown** is not a mission of exploration; it is a mission of sensory deprivation and thermal survival. To enter this world is to become a foreign object in a closed, planetary-scale clockwork of wood and water. 

An ordinary human traveler should prepare with the understanding that they are entering a place where "biology" and "architecture" have merged into a single, freezing stasis.

### 1. Thermal Insulation and the "Second Skin"
The cold here is "viscous." It isn't just a temperature; it's a thief. Because the water is stilled by the infinite wicker, there are no thermoclines—just a constant, unmoving heat-sink.
*   **The Suit:** You cannot wear a standard drysuit. The velvet texture of the water-logged wood and the "soft, freezing veils" of moss will snag on zippers and valves. You need a **biopolymer shell**—a seamless, smooth-surfaced compression suit that mimics the texture of the world’s rounded nubs. This prevents "snag-traps" in the narrowing capillaries of flora.
*   **Internal Heating:** Do not rely on external resistors. A chemical-slurry internal heating system, one that circulates warmed fluid through a network of artificial veins within your suit, will prevent the "Slow Pulse" from vibrating your bones into hypothermic shock.

### 2. Sensory Calibration (The 0.2 Light)
Your eyes are useless in their natural state. The bioluminescence of the wood is a "ghost-blue" that your retinas will struggle to resolve, leading to **Sensory Deprivation Psychosis**.
*   **Visual Augmentation:** Equip haptic-feedback goggles. These shouldn't just amplify light; they should map the "Great Weave" via sonics. In the Cold Drown, you need to *see* the tension of the ropes and the density of the wicker. You must be able to distinguish between a "solid" ironwood piling and a "soft" sponge-bridge.
*   **The Sound Buffer:** The "Slow Pulse" of the world is a low-frequency vibration that can cause nausea or heart arrhythmia in unshielded humans. You need noise-canceling inner-ear implants that filter out frequencies below 20Hz. Without them, the city-sized groans of the wood will feel like they are physically moving your internal organs.

### 3. Locomotion: The Law of the Knot
There is no "swimming" in the Cold Drown—the flora is too dense. You will be climbing, pushing, and weaving.
*   **Tactile Anchors:** You need "soft-touch" gloves with micro-suction pads. You cannot use hooks or blades; damaging the weave could trigger a "shiver" that travels for miles. You move by feeling the cross-hatching of the Great Weave.
*   **The "Slow" Mindset:** You must psychologically prepare for the **Law of the Slow.** If you move quickly, the resistance of the viscous water and the heavy ferns will exhaust you in minutes. You must move like the moss—infinitesimally, with agonizing deliberation. If you get your limb caught in a narrowing capillary of lung-tissue sponge, struggling will only tighten the weave around you. You must stay limp.

### 4. Sustenance and the "Lush" Problem
You are in a world of total flora, but none of it is edible for a human metabolism. It is mineral-rich and carbon-dense. 
*   **Nutrient Ports:** You cannot "eat" in a lightless, pressurized ocean. You will need a direct-line nutrient catheter or a high-calorie liquid intake system built into your suit's helmet.
*   **Waste Management:** Because the Cold Drown is a "closed, perfect" ecology, your presence is a pollutant. You must carry a closed-loop recycling system. To leave even a trace of organic waste could introduce bacteria that would feast on the ancient, frozen wood, potentially rotting the planetary structure from the inside out.

### 5. Spiritual and Mental Fortitude
The most dangerous element of the Cold Drown is the **Loneliness of the Build**. You are surrounded by the evidence of a trillion hands—thumbprints in clay, hand-tied knots—but there is not a single soul to be found. 
*   **The "Archaeologist’s Melancholy":** Travelers often experience a crushing sense of grief in the Drown. To be inside a masterpiece that has survived its creators by eons is to feel the weight of your own mortality. 
*   **Preparation:** Bring "Anchor Sounds" from home—recordings of wind, dry sand, crackling fire, or busy crowds. The silence of the Drown is heavy; it is the silence of a tomb that is still biologically "alive." You need a reminder that a world of stone and sky still exists.

### Emergency Protocol: The Centripetal Drift
If you lose your sense of direction, remember the **Law of the Sinking.** Gravity is a faint ghost pulling you toward the center. 
*   **Do not go deeper.** As the weave tightens, the water is squeezed out. If you drift too far "in," you will find yourself wedged between pillars of ironwood with the weight of a planetary ocean above you, frozen into the wicker for eternity. 
*   **The Signal:** If lost, strike a large ironwood piling with a rhythmic, sustained beat. Because everything is connected, a traveler near a "surface" layer might feel your vibration. But be warned: in the Law of the Slow, it may take days for your signal to reach anyone, and years for them to reach you.

**Summary for the Traveler:**
Enter with reverence. Move with the pace of a growing vine. Touch everything, but change nothing. You are walking through the braid of a god who has gone to sleep; try not to wake the ocean.
