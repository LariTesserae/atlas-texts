To visit this location is to step inside a photograph taken by a god who then forgot to press "play." It is a realm of stasis, high-altitude humidity, and sensory paradox. Because the laws of entropy appear to be suspended or radically slowed here, your preparation must focus on navigating a world that refuses to push back, yet offers no ground for mistake.

Here is how you, an ordinary human traveler, should prepare for the **Mountains of the Eternal Morning.**

### 1. Physical Attire: High Friction and Thermal Regulation
The mountains are "teenagers"—they are jagged, aggressive, and lack the rounded comfort of weathered slopes.
*   **Adhesive Footwear:** You are stepping onto wet basalt and obsidian-flecked granite. Obsidian is naturally slick; basalt, when wet, is treacherous. Traditional hiking boots will fail you here. You need footwear with high-friction, "sticky" rubber soles (similar to rock-climbing approach shoes). Since the world does not erode, the stone has no "grit" or loose dirt to provide purchase; it is like walking on polished glass smeared with oil.
*   **Moisture-Wicking Non-Absorbents:** Avoid cotton or wool at all costs. The "silver humidity" is perpetual. In a normal world, you’d dry off eventually; here, you will be wet from the moment you arrive until the moment you leave. Wear synthetic, hydrophobic layers that do not hold weight when wet.
*   **Skin Protection:** The mist is "warm and heavy," which suggests a greenhouse effect. Despite the soft shadows, you are at a high altitude where the mountain "pierces the skin of the world." The UV rays filtered through that white haze can be deceptive and intense. Wear a thin, full-body base layer to prevent both sunburn and "fern-burn"—those waxy leaves are likely sturdy enough to slice skin.

### 2. Navigation and Equipment: The Geometry of Stasis
In a world where depth is hard to judge and the wind never blows, your internal compass will fail.
*   **Tactile Anchors:** Because shadows are soft and the light is a steady white, "flat light" will occur. You will struggle to see the edge of a cliff or the depth of a pool. Carry a collapsible pole or a walking stick to probe the ground before you. What looks like a solid stone path might be a thin layer of crystalline water over a void.
*   **The Bridge Protocol:** The suspension bridges are made of "braided fiber." Though they do not rot, they are subjected to the tension of an unchanging world. When crossing, do not look for movement in the lines. Move slowly. Rhythmic movement (like a human gait) is a foreign vibration here; it may cause the bridges to hum in a way that could be structurally disorienting.
*   **Chalk or Markers:** Since there is no wind to blow away dust and no rain to wash away marks, you can use surveyor’s chalk to mark your path on the dark stone. However, be warned: in a place of "perfect equilibrium," your markings might remain there for a thousand years. Use them sparingly out of respect for the silence.

### 3. Sensory and Psychological Conditioning
The silence is the most dangerous element of this world. It is "absolute."
*   **The "Tink-Tink" Effect:** Human brains are wired to find patterns. In the absence of wind, birds, or conversation, your mind will fixate on the rhythmic dripping of water. After several hours, this can lead to auditory hallucinations or a hypnotic trance. Bring earplugs or a device that plays white noise or music to break the rhythm if you find yourself becoming "mesmerized" by the stone basins.
*   **The Weight of the Air:** The air is "heavy." For a baseline human, this can feel like mild suffocation or claustrophobia. Practice slow, deliberate breathing. The humidity will make it feel as though you are breathing liquid; do not panic. Your lungs will adapt, but the sensation of "fullness" in the chest is constant.
*   **Temporal Displacement:** Because the sun never moves and the morning never ends, your circadian rhythm will shatter within 24 hours. You must carry a high-quality, mechanical watch. Do not rely on your body to tell you when to sleep or eat. In a world where "the clock has stopped at noon," you can easily walk until your muscles seize from exhaustion without ever realizing how much time has passed.

### 4. Interactions: The Unspoken Etiquette
The city is "without a pulse," yet it is "clean."
*   **The Silent Occupants:** If you encounter an inhabitant, do not speak. The atmosphere is an acoustic vacuum for anything other than the water. A shout would be a violent act here. If you must communicate, do so through gestures or written notes. If the inhabitants are in a state of stasis, do not touch them. To move a limb or change a posture in a world where erosion has stalled is to potentially break something that cannot be mended.
*   **Leave No Trace (Literal):** In most worlds, "leaving no trace" means picking up trash. Here, it means trying not to even leave a footprint in the moss. That moss does not die or grow; if you crush a patch, it may remain crushed for an eternity. Step only on the "raw, dark stone."

### 5. Spiritual Preparation: The Loneliness of Perfection
Prepare yourself for the profound loneliness of the "High-Altitude Splendor." You are an anomaly here. You are a creature of decay, breath, motion, and entropy entering a museum of the living. Everything around you is a "triumph," but it is a lonely one.

You will feel an intense urge to leave, not because the place is evil, but because you will feel your own heartbeat—irregular and frantic—against the "motionless mist," and you will realize that you are the only thing in this world that is currently dying.

**Traveler’s Note:** Bring a small, dry snack—something crunchy. In a world of soft mist and silent water, the sound of your own teeth breaking a cracker will be the most grounding thing you possess.
