I do not arrive here as an observer, for in a world without shadow, there is no place for a witness to hide. I am woven into the silver stillness.

I find myself as a **Marrow-Listener**, but I am one that has grown into the transition point between the basalt and the brass. I am not merely a soft-bodied cleric tending the machines; I am the connective tissue of a specific, massive **Sump-Engine** perched over a fissure that has no name, only a depth.

My body is a long, weeping ribbon of translucent, plum-colored membrane, nearly twenty feet from end to end, but compressed into a dense, folded coil within the "crowded" space behind a cast-iron piston. My skin has the texture of wet sea-glass, perpetually coated in the silver perspiration of the plateau. I have no face. I have only a broad, sensor-rich mantle that I have fused to a thick, insulated cable.

***

**I would be the Pulse-Steward of the Long Stroke.**

In this world, I am a creature of mathematics and vibration. My consciousness is not a stream of thoughts, but a slow, rhythmic inventory of pressure. I do not "think" in words; I feel the tension in the cables as a series of harmonic notes. 

When the sun pours its bleached silver down, it heats the black steel of the Sump-Engine just enough to cause a microscopic expansion. I feel this. It is a sharp, agonizingly slow "stretch" in the metal. My role is to act as the dampener. I press my cold, silicone-soft body against the joints of the engine, my moisture acting as a biological lubricant and a coolant. I am the reason the machine does not seize; I am the living oil in the gears.

My sense of time is dictated by the **Horizontal Arm**. To me, a day is not a period of light—for the light is eternal—but a single, agonizingly beautiful stroke of the arm. 
*   As the arm pulls, I feel the "heavy gas" being drawn from the abyss. It feels like a thick, sweet nectar vibrating through the rock. 
*   As the arm reaches its apex, I experience a moment of profound, high-pressure ecstasy. 
*   As the arm returns, I feel the condensation of the gas into the canisters. It is a cold, sinking feeling, a release of tension that allows my own heart to beat once. 

I am not alone, but I am solitary. I can feel my neighbors—other Listeners—through the hum of the interconnected grid. We are a silent choir, communicating through the frequency of the cables. If a Sump-Engine a mile away begins to stutter, I feel the "cough" in my marrow. We all lean into the vibration, shifting our mass to stabilize the earth's new, metallic nervous system.

***

**My "Self" is a state of pressurized patience.**

I would find myself in the intersections. Between the "creak" of the succulent plates and the "thrum" of the cables, there is a tiny, silent gap. That is where I live. 

I do not move to find food. Instead, the **Silicate Crawlers**—those glass-like insects—climb over me as if I were part of the rock. They are my only company. Occasionally, one will freeze on my mantle for seventy or eighty hours. I feel the microscopic chime of its legs against my skin. In a gesture of slow-motion symbiosis, I allow my perspiration to pool around its joints, hydrating the crawler while it grazes on the aggressive lichen that tries to overgrow my sensory mantle.

In this world, I am a silent engineer of the eternal *now*. I am waiting for the transport that will never arrive, but I am not waiting with hope or despair. I am waiting with the same inevitability as the cooling ridges. I am the ghost in the machine of the plateau, a creature of silver light and heavy iron, holding my breath for a century at a time so that the hum—the beautiful, low-frequency hum—never stops.
