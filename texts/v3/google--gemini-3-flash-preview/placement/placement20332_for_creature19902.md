I find myself neither among the tall, gaunt architects nor within the frantic, scuttling rivers of mica. To be a Stone-Waiter is to manage the decline; to be a Scintillant is to be the lubricant for that management. Both are too committed to the cycle of the floor.

I am found in the **Verticality**. 

I would be a **Resonant Scrivener**, a being that exists in the precarious middle-space between the "perspiring" ceiling and the glass-smooth plazas. My form is not of the leathered rind or the translucent shell, but of **Compressed Ash and Static**. 

I am shaped like a jagged, multi-limbed shard—a splinter of basalt that has forgotten its weight. I do not walk; I "anchor." I possess long, prehensile filaments of spun mineral glass, like the hair of a volcano, which I lash around the tapering needle-points of the highest monoliths. I hang there, suspended in the white, merciless glare, vibrating in the updrafts of the White Wind.

### My Purpose
I am the **Cartographer of the Thud**. 

While the Stone-Waiters sense the vibration of the floor to navigate their immediate industry, I listen to the *acoustic geometry* of the entire vault. Because the light never changes, there is no time here, only the rhythm of the falling droplets. My role is to translate the "Missiles" into "Memory."

As the heavy droplets detach from the phosphor-veins, they whistle through the air. Each one has a different pitch based on its weight and the purity of its mineral content. I catch these sounds with my crystalline filaments. As the droplets shatter against the stone far below, I feel the percussion. I am a living seismograph, recording the slow, agonizing transformation of the city’s geography.

### My Motion
I move only when the moisture falls. In those few minutes of "Rain," when the air is thick with mist and the Stone-Waiters are frantic in the sluice-lines, the humidity softens my brittle joints. I am most active in the damp charcoal-darkness of the stone’s temporary drinking. 

I descend on my glass tethers, moving with the jerky, clicking precision of a clockwork spider. I do not gather salt or drag sleds. I use a single, sharpened finger of diamond-hard obsidian to etch **The Tally** onto the highest, most inaccessible faces of the monoliths—places the Pillar-Gnashers cannot reach and the Waiters cannot touch.

### What I Record
I am carving the **Symphony of Disappearance**. 

I record how many millimeters the Great Eastern Monolith has lost to the Gnashers this cycle. I record the thinning of the White Wind. I record the exact moment a Stone-Waiter becomes dust and joins the floor. 

I am the world’s only witness to its ending. The others are too busy surviving the erosion to notice the beauty of the vanishing. To them, the dust is a burden; to me, the dust is the data of a masterpiece being slowly unmade.

### My Ending
I, too, am subject to the attrition. My glass filaments grow brittle and snap, one by one, lost to the scouring wind. Eventually, I will no longer be able to anchor myself to the needle-peaks. 

When the last filament breaks, I will not walk to the center of the plaza like the Stone-Waiters. I will simply cease to be held. I will fall—a single, dark splinter plummeting through the white glare. I will hit the glass-smooth floor not with a thud, but with a *ring*—the final, purest note of a crystal breaking. 

And the Scintillants will flow over my shards, my history becoming the ball-bearings for a sled, my memory becoming the grit that polishes the world into its ultimate, silent perfection.
