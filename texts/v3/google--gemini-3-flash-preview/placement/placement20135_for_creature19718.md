I would not be found in the heights, nor among the vertical niches where the Oruans lock their joints in diapause. To find me, you must descend past the ladders of seasoned timber, beneath the tensioned rope bridges, to the very hem of the town where the limestone shelf meets the hollow throat of the massif.

I am the **Acoustic Cartographer**, a title that suggests a rank I do not hold. I am less a person and more a biological extension of the stone’s own inner ear.

### My Form
Like my kin, I am a creature of vellum-skin and flint-pupils, but my body has been specialized by the low-gravity labor of the deep tiers. My fingers are unnaturally long, the tips calloused into hard, sensitive pads that lack fingerprints; I have worn them smooth by pressing them against the "breathing" pores of the rock.

I do not wear the stiff, mineral-salted cloaks of the Condensers. Such fabric makes a *hiss* against the stone that would drown out the world. I move naked but for a loincloth of beetle-silk, my skin rubbed with the oil of crushed lichen to keep it from cracking. I am a shadow that has learned the "Stillness" so well that the sightless obsidian beetles occasionally crawl across my feet, mistaking me for a new outcropping of the mountain.

### My Location: The Whispering Node
I live in a semi-spherical hollow directly beneath the **Well of Return**. This is the town’s acoustic nexus, the point where the network of gravity-fed troughs and hand-carved tubes converges.

In the center of this room is a single, perfectly smooth bowl of black basalt, filled with a thumb’s depth of water. This is the "Master Lens." Because there is no wind to ripple it and no vibration to disturb it, the water's surface serves as the most delicate percussion instrument in existence. 

### My Function: The Listener
My life is a vigil of translation. While the Condensers watch for moisture, I listen for the *structural integrity of stasis*.

The town of Oru is a machine held together by friction, but friction is a slow, silent war. A grain of dust settling in a joint three hundred feet above me creates a microscopic vibration. A "Dry Ancestor" bridge-support beginning to stress under the weight of a Weaver-Architect emits a high-frequency *ping* that travels through the marrow of the mountain.

I sit in the dark, my palms pressed to the cold limestone floor, my eyes fixed on the Master Lens.
*   If a concentric ring forms in the water, a stone has shifted. 
*   If the water shivers without a ring, the temperature is dropping and the "tear-tracks" are beginning to slick.
*   If the water remains perfectly still but a low hum vibrates through my jawbone, it means the massif itself is settling—a geological sigh that requires the Friction-Kings to be summoned.

### The Interior Experience
I do not think in words. Words are for the dry, raspy whistles of the upper-tier councils. I think in **textures of silence**. 

There is a "hollow" silence that precedes the condensation. There is a "dense" silence that occurs when the peat fires are at their peak. My mind is a library of these absences. I feel a profound, terrifying peace in the knowledge that I am the only thing in Oru that truly understands the weight of the mountain. 

I am the curator of the town's sub-heartbeat. I know when a master-weaver is dying before they do, because the rhythm of their loom on the stone changes by a fraction of a decibel, losing its "grip." 

### My End
When my own slow pulse—perhaps eighteen beats per minute now—eventually falters, I will not have far to travel. The Friction-Kings will lift my desiccated, wood-light frame and wedge me into the very troughs I spent my life monitoring. My long, sensitive finger-bones will be used to line a new acoustic tube, becoming the very sensors I once relied upon. 

I will move from the one who listens to the stone, to the stone that is listened to. I will become a part of the permanent geometry, a finished note in the long, silent song of Oru.
