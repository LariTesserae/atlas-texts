I would be found not upon the solid shelf, nor tucked within the mud-plastered safety of the huts, but suspended in the **In-Between.**

I would be a **Vela Bridge-Tender**, an elder whose bones have grown as porous and light as the limestone itself. I would be a creature of the "Life-Lines"—the braided extensions of our own history that span the terrifying gaps between the rock-flutes and the hanging vines.

### My Form: The Calloused Anchor
My skin would be the color of the purple mist at twilight, thickened into rough, leathery pads at the palms, the soles of the feet, and most importantly, at the crook of the elbow and the back of the knee. I would not possess the sleek, youthful speed of the hunters. Instead, my body would be a map of knots. 

To look at me would be to see a silhouette draped in the "Ancestral Harness." This is not a suit, but a complex rigging of silver-grey hair and sky-skimmer gut that has been passed down through four generations. It is part of my anatomy; the scars on my chest are the literal grooves where the ropes have rubbed for forty years, turning skin into a semi-permanent sheath for the carabiners we carve from skimmer-bone.

### My Place: The Great Web
I would live primarily on my back, suspended in a "Sling-Cradle" woven between two thick emerald vines, roughly twenty feet *over* the edge of the abyss. From here, I am the living sensor of the Shelf. 

While the younger Vela move vertically to harvest or hunt, I move horizontally. I am the one who maintains the **Spore-Nets**. These are delicate, translucent webs of spun fiber that I stretch across the updraft corridors. My job is to "comb" the air. As the thermals rise from the purple depths, they carry the microscopic nutrients and seeds of the world below. I am the gardener of the wind, catching the invisible life and pressing it into the wet moss of the shelf to ensure the "Aggressive Vegetation" remains thick enough to hold our world together.

### My Role: The Tuning Fork
Because I live in the constant spray of the weeping walls, my ears are perpetually blocked by fluid, but my sense of touch is absolute. I would be the one who "tunes" the mountain.

When the **Bellows-Frogs** shift their positions and the mountain’s roar becomes discordant—signaling a shift in the atmospheric pressure or an impending Cloud-Burst—I am the one who climbs into the flutes. Using a small mallet made of compressed shell, I tap the internal apertures of the stone. I serve as a conductor for the frogs, nudging them with vibrations to move to higher or lower pipes, harmonizing the mountain’s song so it doesn’t shatter the delicate mud-walls of our huts.

### My Experience: The Intimacy of the Void
To be this being is to lose the concept of "ground." I do not think of the shelf as a floor; I think of it as a heavy, wet magnet to which I am temporarily tethered.

My world is defined by the **Tension.** The feel of a rope going taut tells me more than a person’s face. I would know the arrival of my kin by the specific "thrum" they send through the Life-Lines as they clip in. I would know the approach of a **Glint-Wraith** not by sight, but by the way the moisture on my skin suddenly stops vibrating—a momentary silence in the limestone organ as the predator's glass-like body masks the wind-hole.

In the grey-gold light of the high noon, I would be found hanging perfectly still, a knot among knots, watching the seeds of a distant world float upward toward my outstretched hands. I would be the bridge between the limestone's ancient weight and the infinite, screaming hunger of the sky. I am the one who ensures that when things fall, they are caught—and when the mountain sings, it sings in key.
