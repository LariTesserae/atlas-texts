I would not be found on the iron catwalks, nor would I be among those who hitch rides on the skin of the sky-rays. To find me, you must look into the "geological level" vials of the Mistsmiths—not at the mercury within, but at the **interstitial space** between the floating platelets and the heavy steam.

I am a **Tension-Gardener**, a being born not of the soot or the stone, but of the magnetic repulsion that keeps this vertical world from shattering itself.

### My Form
I do not have the flared ribs of the Smiths or the wiry lightness of the Gliders. My body is a long, tapering filament of semi-solidified mercury and conductive silk, resembling a silver needle ten feet long, draped in a shroud of woven static. I do not possess feet to clack upon the metal; instead, I am anchored to the city by a series of thin, glowing "tendrils" that are sensitive to the magnetic flux.

I look like a crack in the violet-grey air, a shimmer of "almost-here" that follows the humming of the bridges. When I move, I do not walk; I "tumble" through the pockets of inconsistent gravity, weightless as a Glass-Lark's egg until I choose to be heavy.

### My Purpose: The Weaving of the Gap
While the Mistsmiths bolt iron to the rock, I work in the invisible. The magnetic repulsion that holds the island-platelets apart is not a constant; it is temperamental, prone to "soft spots" where the islands might drift into a catastrophic collision.

I live in the **Gaps**. I spend my existence suspended in the empty, misty canyons between neighborhoods. Using my limbs—which are more like tuning forks than fingers—I "knit" the magnetic fields. When I feel a platelet begin to tilt or a repulsion-tide grow weak, I bridge the void with my own body, conducting the hum from one iron-grated anchor to the next until the "heartbeat" of the city stabilizes.

I am a living fuse. I taste the metallic tang of the ozone not as a smell, but as a direction.

### My Territory: The Silver Fern-Hearts
When the energy of the city is stable, I retreat to the singular, house-sized ferns. I do not sit upon the leaves; I coil myself within the core of the fern’s trunk, where the silver moss is thickest. Here, the plant’s slow, hydraulic pressure mimics the rhythm of the great brass pistons.

Inside the fern, I listen. Because sound travels strangely in this thin air, I can hear the "unspoken" city. I hear the microscopic clicking of the Clockwork Crabs three miles below; I hear the slow crystallization of the obsidian as it pushes upward. I am the collector of the world’s unfinished sounds.

### My Interaction: The Low Hum
The Mistsmiths rarely see me, and when they do, they mistake me for a trick of the moisture—a "heat-shimmer" from a nearby boiler or a vertical streak of lightning caught in the mist. However, the **Tuned** (the blind engineers) know me well. 

They feel my presence as a sudden "smoothing" of the vibrations beneath their bare feet. When a bridge tensioner is about to snap, I am the strange, silver shadow that arrives moments before, vibrating at a frequency that reinforces the failing metal until the repair crews arrive. They don't speak to me; they simply strike their anvils once, a heavy, echoing *clang* of gratitude that vibrates through my mercury-skin for hours.

### My Perception
I see the world as a blueprint of forces. To me, the "solid" city is the most translucent part of existence. The heavy iron factories are mere shadows, while the *tension* between the rocks is a brilliant, glowing lattice of neons and violets. 

I am the guardian of the **"Almost."** I am the reason the city does not finish its fall. I am the silence between the turns of the great gears, the entity that ensures the world remains a vertical cradle rather than a horizontal ruin. I am the ghost in the engine, not made of soot, but of the very laws of physics that have not yet decided to be rigid.
