To find my place in this verticality, I must first stop resisting the descent.

I am not the Latten-Folk, though I share their niche; I lack their frantic, etching grip. I am not the Slow-Mind, for I still possess a sense of "now" that is sharper than the turning of a century-gear.

I am the **Scribe of the Patina**.

I live on the underside of your cathedral-sized balcony, suspended in a hammock woven from the discarded filaments of Fulm-Moths. I am a creature of the mid-strata, a transitional form that exists in the thin space between the "Total Builtness" of the metal and the "Total Chaos" of the greenery.

### My Form: The Calculus of the Limb
I have abandoned the symmetry of the horizontal world. My left arm is elongated, the bone replaced by a flexible, copper-braided graft that allows me to hook into the vibration-dampeners of the balcony’s struts. My right hand remains delicate, five-fingered, stained a deep, permanent verdigris from the minerals I harvest.

I do not have eyes in the way you do. The "blindingly white haze" above and the "orange void" below have rendered traditional sight a liability. Instead, my skin is covered in fine, silvered cilia—like the "vent-birds"—that map the pressure changes of the Great Exhaust. I "see" the world as a complex map of thermals and vibrations. I can feel a city-block-sized gear engaging three kilometers away as a sharp, rhythmic prickle on my neck.

### My Function: Harvesting the Memory of Iron
The Machine is old, but it is not silent about its history. As the brass and iron wear down, they shed "Scales"—wafers of oxidized history that have recorded the friction of aeons. 

I am a collector. I swing out over the yawning gulf on a tether of vine-fiber, my magnetic boots clicking against the oscillating piston. I seek the places where the **Settled Erosion** is peeling—where the rust-crust has become so thick it begins to flake away like the bark of a cedar. 

I harvest these flakes. They are my scrolls. To you, they look like shards of ruined metal; to me, the microscopic grooves and striations are a record of the Machine's pulse. I can read the "fever" of a millennium ago by the discoloration in the copper-oxide; I can trace the exact moment the vines first bridged the central drive-shaft by the shift in the crystalline structure of the rust.

### My Home: The Niche of Tethers
My balcony is a library of weight. Because there is no ground, I store my knowledge in **Pendulums**. Thousands of wire-wrapped rust-flakes hang from the brass railings of the niche, suspended by varying lengths of twine. 

When the world-piston makes its slow, tectonic down-stroke, the air displacement causes my library to sing. It is not the bone-shaking *clack* of the locks, but a delicate, metallic tinkling—a wind-chime of history. Each flake strikes the other, sharing their stories of friction. 

### My Relationship to the Void
I am the one who watches the **Aero-Castes**. While the Latten-Folk fear the fall, I find a terrible beauty in it. Sometimes, I release a shard of "Read Metal" into the void, watching it tumble until it is intercepted by a Scavenger-Kite. 

I believe the Machine is dreaming. I believe the **Slow-Mind**—the colonial vine-intelligence—is not just maintaining the engine, but translating it. It turns the heat into sap; I turn the rust into memory. 

### Who I Am in the Slow Dynamic
I am the witness to the **Negotiation**. 

In the deafening thrum that vibrates your marrow, I am the one who finds the steady, rhythmic heartbeat hidden within. I do not prune the vines like the Latten-Folk, nor do I drink the static like the moths. I am the observer of the friction. 

If you were to find me on that rusted grate, I would not speak. I would take your hand—soft, organic, and out of place—and press it against a patch of "soft" copper, worn smooth by a thousand years of steam. I would wait for the bone-shaking *clack* to pass through us both, and then I would hand you a small, green-fringed shard of the world’s skin.

In this world of verticality, I am the anchor of the moment. I am the being who remembers that before the moss grew, the metal was cold—and that one day, the green will be so thick that the Machine will stop, not out of failure, but because it has finally become a forest.
