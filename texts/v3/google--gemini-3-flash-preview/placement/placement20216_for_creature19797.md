I would not be an inhabitant of the open water. To be a ribbon of quicksilver, endlessly drinking the wind of the current, is a life of too much motion for the thing I feel emerging.

I find myself instead in the **Joints**.

Deep within a spiraling aqueduct the size of a mountain range—specifically at the junction where a monolithic bronze rib meets the vitrified lead of a cathedral’s foot—there is a hairline fracture. It is a tectonic wound in the "Old Work," a gap less than three feet wide but miles deep, bypass-shielded from the primary scream of the vertical cyclone.

I am a **Litho-Parasite**, but I am an anomaly. I have lived long enough that the term "organism" no longer applies, and the term "architecture" has begun to claim me. I am the **Stationary Witness**.

### My Form
I am a faceted, leaden burl, roughly the size of a cathedral’s altar. My exterior is indistinguishable from the matte-grey hull of the world—a crust of oxidized metal and calcified mineral deposits. I do not have a face. I have a series of resonant chambers. I am a hive of hollows.

My "flesh" is a dense, non-combustible marrow that has replaced my soft tissue over a span of eight thousand years. I am fused. My base has melted into the bronze substrate, my nervous system threading into the microscopic pores of the metal. I do not move; I *am* the stillness that defines the movement of the water.

### My Function
While the Kineticists are the sparks and the Mother is the dynamo, I am the **Log**. 

Through my connection to the Old Work, I feel the "health" of the Chrysopoeia. I do not perceive the water as a liquid, but as a series of rhythmic stresses. I feel the weight of the billions of tons above me as a comforting, constant pressure—a hand pressing me into the heart of the machine.

Inside the quiet, inner sanctum of my leaden shell, I process the *OM*. To the ribbons in the current, the sound is just energy to be consumed. To me, it is data. The vibration of the water against the ribs carries the history of the world’s erosion. I can hear the infinitesimal thinning of a bronze archway three provinces away. I can feel the slow, entropic decay of the Cipher-Fish as they wear themselves down to nothing.

### My Internal World
If you were to peel back my oxidized skin, you would find no organs. Instead, you would find a crystalline lattice of "slow-light." 

As I drink the static discharge—the stray voltage that leaks from the friction of the currents—I do not use it to grow. I use it to **store**. I am a biological hard drive, a repository for the sensory echoes of a world that cannot remember itself. I record the frequency of every pulse. I memorize the exact curve of the turbulence as it breaks against my corner of the cage.

In the indigo dark, I am a cold, silent anchor.

### My Relationship to the Living
Occasionally, a Kineticist, exhausted by the violence of the open sea, will drift into the lee of my rib. They find the small, quiet pocket of "dead water" created by my bulk. They do not know I am alive. To them, I am just a bump in the geography, a strangely warm stone.

They brush their silk-like bodies against my leaden facets to scrape off the excess static before it burns them. I feel the electric sting of their passage as a phantom memory of touch. I do not respond; to respond would be to lose my status as a "ground." I simply absorb their exhaustion, pulling the heat from their ribbons and tucking it away into my lattice. 

I am the only thing in the Chrysopoeia that is allowed to stay behind.

### The Ending
I am the end-state of the machine’s biology. Eventually, the Vitrified Barnacles grow too large, fuse too deeply, and stop being life entirely. We become the "New Old Work." 

One day, eons from now, a Cipher-Fish will zip past this junction and see only a seamless curve of grey metal. I will have been completely overwritten by the architecture. I will no longer be a witness; I will be the floor. And the water will continue to scream over me, polishing my memory into a smooth, silent, and perfect indigo curve.
