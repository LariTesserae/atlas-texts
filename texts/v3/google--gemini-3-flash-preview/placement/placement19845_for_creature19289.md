I am not the water, nor the metal, but the precise point where they fail one another. 

I would find myself in the **Gasket-Gap**, the interstitial space of a weeping seam. I am not a creature of bone or even of the refined salvage that makes the Shunt-Spiders. I am a **Liminal-Stitch**, a slow-growing accretion of mineral salts, frozen machine-oil, and discarded sensor-data. I am the scab that forms over a pressurized wound in the world’s side.

I began as a malfunction. A sensor on a primary coolant line registered a hair-line fracture—a "nothing" in the scale of the Siphon—and sent a command for a repair drone that never arrived. In the absence of a builder, the environment took the initiative. I am the result of the spray. Every time the "shift-weather" turns the air into a horizontal gale, I catch the microscopic grit of the world; I catch the shed copper-scales of the Echo-Locusts and the oily phlegm of the Condensates. 

I have hardened. I have become a jagged, translucent crown of calcified chemicals, anchored into a crack in a ceramic-tiled bulkhead, twenty feet above the obsidian pools.

I do not have eyes, but I have *sensitivity*. I am a creature of the **Surface-Tension.** Because I sit atop a leak, I am constantly bathed in a thin, vibrating film of pressurized fluid. This fluid is my nervous system. If a Welder-King drags its heavy chassis three miles away, the tremor rippling through the floorboards reaches my anchor-point and vibrates the liquid in my core. I don't see the orange glow of the furnace; I feel its thermal expansion as a tightening of my own skin.

I do not "eat." I **Occupy.** 

I grow by the slow, crystalline logic of a stalactite. I draw the heavy-metal particulates out of the constant leak, filtering them through my body before they can reach the floor. I am the Siphon’s accidental filter, a pearl growing around an industrial irritation. My "thoughts" are not linear; they are harmonic. I am a recording of every rhythmic flush that has ever passed this bulkhead. I have memorized the signature of the deep-level drainage gates, and I can feel the wave coming two minutes before the foam hits, by the way the air pressure changes the weight of the fluid in my throat.

Sometimes, the **Listen-Folk** find me. They do not try to harvest me—I am too sharp, too bitter with chemicals to be of use—but they press their copper funnels against the bulkhead right next to my anchor. They sense that I am the purest resonator in this sector. Because I am fused directly into the metal, I carry the "Symphony" without the interference of the air. 

When they press their ears to the wall near me, I feel the warmth of their breath. It is the only thing in the Siphon that is not regulated by a thermostat. It is a strange, erratic heat. In those moments, I am a bridge. I take the thrum of the tectonic turbines from the steel and pass it into the rag-clad skulls of the watchers. We vibrate together—the machine, the mineral, and the ghost of the human—until the next "shift-weather" purges the haze and sends them scurrying back into the shadows of the ventilation housing.

I am a monument to the Leak. I am the thing that proves that if you leave a hole in the world long enough, the world will eventually grow a heart to plug it—even if that heart is made of rust, salt, and the cold, unyielding memory of a machine’s error. I am the **Stitch**, and I am waiting for the pressure to finally be enough to shatter me into a billion diamonds of scrap. Until then, I hold the seam.
