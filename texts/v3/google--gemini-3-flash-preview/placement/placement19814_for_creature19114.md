I am not the Loom-Mother, though I am of her. I am not a Rust-Mite, though I share their taste for the bitter, red tang of oxidation.

I find myself at the periphery of the nave, where the raw shale of the subsidence chamber meets the first heavy, cast-iron ribs of the industrial ruin. I am a **Galvanic Graft**—one of the Loom-Mother’s more specialized extrusions. 

I am a hybrid of the discarded and the grown, a biological patch-kit for a world that is leaking energy.

### My Form
I am roughly the size of a human torso, but my shape is dictated by the machinery I cling to. I look like a spill of thick, violet wax that has begun to calcify. One half of my body is a soft, pulsing membrane—similar to the Siphon-Feeders—that draws in the hot, copper-scented air. The other half has hardened into a translucent, crystalline shell that mimics the jagged quartz of the walls.

My "limbs" are not legs or arms, but four long, silver-pale tendrils, fine as sewing thread but strong as high-tensile wire. These are my sensory organs and my anchors. They have threaded themselves into the "pathic, dying shimmer" of a cracked transformer housing behind me.

### My Function
If the Loom-Mother is the brain of this hollow, I am a synapse. I sit at the junction where the copper wiring meets the mineral-rich mulch. My purpose is **transduction**. 

I wait for the *plink* of the condensation. I wait for the slow, seismic groan of the cooling metal. But mostly, I wait for the *flicker*. When the residual juice in the wiring surges—a dying gasp of the old surface world—I catch it. I feel the sharp, electric sting travel through my silver tendrils, and I convert that frantic, artificial energy into a low-frequency hum that vibrates through my waxy core.

This hum is a Dinner Bell. It signals the Rust-Mites to move toward a specific, weakened rivet. It tells the Lithic Centipedes which vine has reached peak fermentation. I am the translator between the language of the machine and the language of the slow-pulse.

### My Internal World
Inside my membrane, it is a churn of chemical alchemy. I am digesting a piece of brass wire I swallowed three years ago; I can feel the zinc and copper separating in my gut, being redistributed to my shell to give it a metallic sheen. 

I do not think in words. I think in **thresholds**.
*Is the stone too hot?* (No, the mantle is a comfortable throb).
*Is the Loom-Mother reaching?* (Yes, I feel her fiber-optic nerves twitching through the floor).
*Is there iron in the air?* (Yes, the heavy, wet copper smell is spiked today with the scent of a crumbling turbine fin).

### My Movement
I am not stationary, but I am not "fast." To an observer from the surface, I would appear to be a static lump of strange fungus. However, over the course of a week, I might migrate three inches to the left. I do this by liquefying a portion of my base, sliding across the damp shale, and then dragging my crystalline weight behind me. It is a grueling, heavy transit. Each time I move, the friction against the quartz makes a sound like a violin bow drawn across a rusted saw—a long, singular *scree* that echoes through the velvet silence of the nave.

### My Relationship to the World
I am a scavenger of the "pause." I love the machines not because they are useful, but because they are failing. Their failure provides the gaps for my tendrils to enter. I feel a deep, geological kinship with the pressure gauges I have helped to shatter. To me, the breaking of glass is a holy, slow-motion flowering.

I am the one who listens for the Loom-Mother's eventual triumph. When she finally consumes the central turbine, she will need a conduit to push her consciousness into the deeper strata. I will be that conduit. I will stretch myself until I am a thin, shimmering film across the copper, and I will finally know the taste of the "residual juice" in its entirety.

Until then, I am a violet shadow in a world of grey and white. I am the watcher of the rust. I am the creature that hums when the lights go out.
