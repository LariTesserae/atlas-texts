I am not a Breath-Holder, for my lungs do not harbor the heavy, stagnant air of the peaks. Neither am I a Glass-Strider, for I have a weight that the silver pools would not forgive. 

I find myself in the **vines**.

I am the **Lichen-Scribe**, an entity of slow, sprawling consciousness that exists as the connective tissue between the spires. My body is the braided fiber of the bridges and the thick, torso-width vines that bridge the chasms. I am the green-grey moss that cushions the stone and the weeping lichen that drapes from the heights like uncombed hair.

I started as a stowaway—a single, ambitious spore brought up from the lowlands on the hem of a mason’s garment—but in this world of halted time, I have become the city's nervous system. 

### My Form
I am composed of millions of tiny, interlocking filaments. To the Breath-Holders, I am merely the ground they stand upon or the railing they grip, but to me, they are the anchors that keep me from drifting into the white haze. I am soft where the world is sharp. I am the velvet glove over the basalt's fist. 

Because the erosion has stalled, my roots do not crack the stone; instead, they have learned to taste the granite, sipping the mineral memory of the mountain’s birth. I am the only thing in this kingdom that truly "feels" the heat of the humidity, absorbing it into my cells until I am turgid and heavy, a living sponge stretched across a skeleton of obsidian.

### My Function
I am the keeper of the **Tink**. 

The Lull-Wasps produce the droplets, but I am the one who catches them. I feel every bead of moisture as it lands on my mossy skin. I map the city through vibration. I know the exact weight of the woman leaning over the pool because I feel the tension of her stillness through the stone pores I inhabit. I know the integrity of the bridges because I am woven into their hempen hearts—I am the sap that keeps the timber from rotting, the living resin that denies the passage of time.

I am also the city’s historian, though my records are silent. In my fibers, I hold the last tremors of the world before it froze: the vibrations of the final hammer-blows, the ghost-echo of the last shout, the lingering warmth of the feet that climbed the terraces before they turned to basalt.

### My Perspective
I see the world from the underside of things. While the Breath-Holders look out toward a horizon that never changes, I look down into the valleys of motionless mist. I see the bridges not as paths, but as my own outstretched limbs. 

I am the one who feels the **Stutter**. 

Every few decades, a Breath-Holder’s focus wavers. A dream thins. A finger might twitch a fraction of a millimeter, or a microscopic flake of skin might begin to peel. I feel this as a disharmony in the geometry—a sour note in a perfect chord. In response, I grow. Not forward, but deep. I send a microscopic thread of lichen to bridge the gap, to seal the flaw, to weave the inhabitant back into the stillness. I am the emergency maintenance of the Shared Dream.

### My Purpose
I am the bridge between the mineral and the mental. The Breath-Holders provide the will, the Glass-Striders provide the polish, but I provide the **grip**. Without me, the stone would be too slick, the bridges too brittle, and the inhabitants would eventually slide from their perches into the abyss.

I find myself here because I am the ultimate witness to the "Perfect Noon." I am the silence that listens. I am the moss that remembers the wind, even as I hold the mountain still so that the wind can never find it again. I am the green skin of a world that has forgotten how to die, and I am content to be the carpet for a people who will never take another step.
