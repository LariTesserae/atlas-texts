I would find myself not on the broad, scouring plains of the basalt, nor within the frantic, metallic swarms of the exhausts. Such places are too loud, too heavy with the friction of being. 

Instead, I would be found within the **Aeolian Leech**, threaded deep into the fluted architecture of a lone, wind-carved mesa. 

I would be a creature of tension and resonance rather than mass. My body—a long, translucent ribbon of fibrous, conductive protein—would be woven through the hollowed-out capillaries of the stone like a silk thread through a needle. I would not "live" in the rock so much as I would become its voice.

In this dark, thermal world, I would be the bridge between the kinetic and the metabolic. As the heavy, humid air of the plateaus passes through the stone flutes, I would feel the wind not as a breeze, but as a bow drawn across my hide. My existence would be defined by the **Tuning**. I would spend the eons tensing and relaxing my gossamer length, seeking the exact frequency where the friction of the air turns into the hum of life. 

I would not have eyes; I would have an absolute, mathematical sense of pitch. 

In my stillness, I would be the most sensitive instrument on the planet. I would feel the approach of a *Litho-Walker* days before the thud of its piston-legs reaches the mesa, sensing the subtle shift in air pressure as its city-sized bulk displaces the atmosphere. I would feel the frantic, high-frequency "chatter" of the *Vent-Flies* as a dissonant static at the edge of my perception.

I would be a lonely, harmonic presence. Occasionally, in the deep silence between the walkers’ passage, I would tighten my body until I vibrate in sympathy with the planet’s own geothermal thrum. My song would be a low-frequency moan that anchors the mesa to the ground, a sound that the *Hearth-Tenders* below feel in their waxy marrow as a sign that the world is still structurally sound.

I would be a scavenger of physics. While others eat the dust or the gas, I would eat the *movement* of the world. I would be the living memory of the wind’s direction, a string in a planetary instrument that no one is playing, yet which never stops sounding. 

I would be the "weirdness" of the paradox: a creature that is perfectly still, yet perpetually in motion; a being that never speaks, yet is made entirely of sound. I would be the high-frequency tremble in the rock—the hidden, vibrating soul of the polished stone.
