I am not walking. To walk here is to be dismantled by the floor.

I am a **Pulsing Weaver**, but I am an outlier among my kin—a creature of the "intermediate hum." I exist in the sliver of space between the obsidian razors and the lowest hanging veils of the Canopy. 

I find myself pressed horizontally against a vertical sheet of shale, my liver-colored skin stretched so thin over my ribs that the vibration of the **Gastropod-Lords** below resonates in my very marrow. My fingers, those chitinous needles, are not currently lashing gourds or stringing glass. Instead, they are wedged into a hairline fracture in the rock, anchoring me as the air begins to thicken.

I am the **archivist of the friction**.

### My Form
My body is a series of frantic, elongated tensions. I am nearly seven feet long when extended, yet I can fold myself into a space no wider than a skiff-leaf’s edge. My skin is slick, coated in a stolen film of gastropod mucus that allows me to slide over the obsidian teeth without being flayed. My eyes are the most offensive thing about me: two enormous, gold-flecked spheres that do not blink, designed to drink the bruised purple light and find the "seams" in the chaos.

I do not have a mouth for speech. My throat is a heavy, drum-like membrane. When I want to "be," I press this drum against the stone and let the world's violence play me like an instrument.

### My Place in the Crucible
While my kin huddle in the steam-shadows, I am drawn to the **Apex-Anchors**. I do not fear them; I study the pulse of their valves. I live in the "dead zones" created by their stillness. In the wake of an Anchor’s steady filtration, there is a momentary pocket of silence—a vacuum in the roar. It is there, in that stolen quiet, that I practice the "interference."

I am the one who etched the predatory moon onto the gourd. I did not do it to worship; I did it to *record*. I am the first of my kind to realize that the tectonic grinding isn't just noise—it is a language of the deep earth.

### A Moment in the Life
The temperature is spiking. The "Great Exhalation" is beginning.

I feel the Canopy above me groan as the waxy skiffs begin to sweat. The mint-scent becomes cloying, thick enough to taste. I see a **Weather-Swarm** nearby—a turbulent bruise of prismatic wings—begin to lose its cohesion. The swarm is panicking as the air turns to soup. They tumble toward the obsidian floor.

I move. I do not run; I *flicker*. My many-jointed limbs find the microscopic pits in the glass. I am a shadow cast by a dying fire. I reach a shelf of rock just as the white fog rolls in, turning the purple dark into a blinding, opaque wall of steam.

I am alone in the hot white. The roar of the insects has vanished, replaced by the rhythmic *tock... tock... tock...* of heavy condensation hitting the shale. 

In this window of silence, I reach into a pouch made of cured leech-skin. I pull out a small shard of obsidian I have spent three cycles sharpening. I do not carve a warning. I do not carve a map. I find a smooth face of the black glass, hidden behind a curtain of aerial roots, and I begin to scratch a series of dots.

I am counting the pulses of the Canopy. I am measuring the time between the screams. 

I am the part of this world that has stopped trying to merely survive the storm and has begun to listen for the rhythm behind it. I am a Weaver who has stopped weaving nets and has begun to weave an understanding.

As the steam begins to lift and the first **Gastropod-Lord** heaves a subsonic groan of reawakening, I press my throat to the cooling stone. I send a single, clear vibration down into the dark:

*I am still here. I am watching the light fall.*
