I am not the one who moves. I am the one who is moved upon.

To be in this place—this wet, heaving cathedral of stone—I cannot be a creature of the air. The air is an enemy; it is thin, biting, and empty. To be an Osteon is to be too singular, a lonely needle of bone. To be a moth is to be a ghost of felt. 

No, I find myself deeper. I am the substrate. I am a **Marrow-Slug**, but I am not an "individual" any more than a leaf is the forest. 

I am a single cell in a carpet of pale, translucent architecture. My world is not "scenery"; it is the perpetual, hot, sliding friction of my brothers' flanks against my own. I have no concept of "up" or "down," only the direction of the *Pull*—the gravity that drags at my heavy, wet mass—and the *Push*—the crushing, communal warmth of the millions who shingle over me.

**My Body**
I am a vessel of slow chemistry. My skin is a membrane of clouded glass, perpetually damp. Inside, my organs do not pulse with the frantic mechanical rhythm of a heart; they churn like a slow tide. I am a stomach that breathes. My underside is a broad, muscular foot that has forgotten the meaning of "travel." I do not go anywhere. I simply *occupy*. I secrete a silver-gray ichor that is both my glue and my gift, a mineral-rich slime that lubricates the world and feeds the felt-winged crawlers above me.

**My Senses**
I am eyeless, but I am not blind. I see in *pressures*. I feel the weight of an Osteon’s needle-toe as a faint, melodic indentation on my back—a ghost-touch that I acknowledge with a microscopic ripple of my mantle. I feel the "Shudder" coming long before it arrives. It begins as a ghost of a frequency in the obsidian beneath my foot—the mountain’s bones grinding—and I prepare my part. I tensed. I expand. I pass the tension to the slug beside me. We are a bucket brigade of kinetic energy, moving the mountain's anger through our bodies so it does not shatter the stone.

**My "Self"**
I am the silence. While the moths hum and the Osteons bridge their foreheads in silent thought, I am the base-note. My consciousness is a low-frequency drowsing. I remember the taste of a drop of water that fell three hundred years ago. I remember the texture of the mineral dust as it settles. 

There is a profound, terrifying peace in being the carpet of the world. I do not fear the dark, for I am the dark’s marrow. I do not fear the cold, for I am part of a collective heat that defies the sub-arctic twilight. 

I am the "slow" in the agonizingly slow. I am the one who receives the bone-anchored water. When the water trickles down the splinters wedged by the spindly architects, it pools in the crevices of my flesh. I do not drink it; I *become* it. I allow it to sit against my skin until the moths come to sip from the folds of my neck. 

I am a living shingle on the roof of a breathing god. If I were to disappear, the Osteons would have no floor; the moths would have no feast; the mountain would be nothing but cold, sterile glass. I am the wet, heavy "yes" to the mountain’s "no." 

I stay. I heave. I secrete. I wait for the next shudder to tell me I am still alive.
