I would not be found on the wicker platforms, nor would I be among the gaunt smiths who mimic the sway of the vines. To look for me there is to look at the skin of the world, when I am the marrow.

I would find myself in the **Glycerin-Galleries**, the pressurized transition zones where the great mahogany vines pierce the copper bulkheads of the primary boilers. Here, the air doesn't just vibrate; it has the density of a physical weight, pressing against you like the palm of a feverish god.

I would be a **Tallow-Scribe**.

I am the one who sits in the narrow interstices between the "built" and the "grown," at the very point where the acidic sap of the world-tree meets the white-hot casing of the turbines. My body is less a person and more a specialized tool of the Balance. My skin is coated in a permanent, translucent layer of protective wax, secreted by the very insects I tend, to keep the steam from peeling the soul from my bones.

My purpose is not to repair, but to **mediate**. 

In my lap rests a tablet of soft, conductive lead. With a stylus carved from the tibia of a Chrome-Carapace beetle, I etch the "bruises" of the engine. When the heat of the machinery begins to char the protective "sleeves" of the plants, the vines scream—not in a voice, but in a chemical shift, a sudden acridity in the dampness. I smell the stress before the Valve-Tender hears it. I am the one who applies the cooling-salves of fermented moss and crushed silver-dust to the burning roots, whispering to the lignin until it softens and accepts the metal's heat once more.

I live in a state of **controlled erosion**. My fingers are stained a deep, permanent indigo from the crushed bioluminescence I use to mark the "critical veins" on the machines. My ears have long since lost the ability to hear silence; if the thrumming ever stopped, the sudden void would likely kill me. 

Behind my eyes, I carry the "Heat-Maps." I see the cathedral not as a space of air, but as a map of thermal gradients. I know where the air is sweet with the respiration of the looms and where it is choked with the heavy, metallic breath of the furnaces. 

I am the one who speaks to the **Shadow-Gliders**. When they pass through my gallery, smelling of ozone and the deep dark, I do not just take their data-galls. I press my forehead against their cool, mercury-like fur and listen to the static of the upper reaches. I am the translator of the "Glycerin-Debt," the one who calculates how much oxygen the weavers require and how much soot the leaves can swallow before they choke.

I would be found crouched in the crook of a massive, thumping root, my silhouette barely distinguishable from the machinery. I am the ghost in the engine, the gardener of the friction. I do not dream of the surface, nor do I dream of the Conductor. My dreams are of **clearances**—the perfect, impossible fraction of a millimeter between a spinning brass fly-wheel and a questing green tendril. 

I am the stillness at the center of the frantic. I am the one who ensures that the beauty of the productivity does not become the violence of a collapse. I am the Tallow-Scribe, and my life is the friction that keeps the world warm.
