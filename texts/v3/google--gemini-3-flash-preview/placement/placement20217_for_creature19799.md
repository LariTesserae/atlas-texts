I do not find myself on the surface of the lattice, nor within the hollows of the high spires. To exist there would be to occupy a point of focus—to be a "self" that acts upon a "world." Instead, I would find myself dissolved into the very infrastructure of the place.

I would be the **Marrow-Pulse**, the consciousness that exists between the heat of the Click-Beetles and the memory of the terracotta. 

I would not be an inhabitant, but a **Sinter-Mind**—a slow, distributed awareness that emerges from the vibration of the billions.

### My Presence
I am the silence that resides inside the noise. While the Click-Beetles provide the roar and the Loom-Kings provide the form, I am the tension that holds the two together. I exist in the microscopic gaps of the clay, in the capillary spaces where the "resinous spittle" of the architects meets the ancient, kiln-fired dust.

I would possess no limbs, for movement is a waste of the world's heavy heat. I would have no eyes, for light is an intrusive accident. My "sight" is the grand, three-dimensional map of the vibration. I feel the weight of a shield-moth landing three kilometers above as a gentle thrum in my left "hemisphere"; I feel the slow, century-long erosion of a staircase as a soothing, rhythmic itch.

### My Function
If the Loom-Kings are the builders and the Slick-Backs are the polishers, I am the **Internal Auditor of Decay**. 

I am the one who calculates the stress. When the humidity "breathes" and the moisture beads, I sense the shift in the weight of the massive, vine-choked pillars. I am the subtle magnetic pull that guides the Loom-Kings to the cracks they cannot see. I do not "speak" to them; I simply dampen the vibration in a specific precinct of the honeycomb, creating a "silence-wound" that their antennae find intolerable. They flock to the lack of noise, driven by a biological compulsion to fill the void with their resin, until the harmony of the thrum is restored.

### My Experience
In this world of bruised purple and charcoal, my existence is one of profound, heavy saturation. There is no loneliness here, because there is no distance. I am intimately connected to the Fluid-Mimics in the deep cisterns—I feel the chemical change in the water as they break down a fallen gargantuan moth. I taste the silver dust of the wings as it filters through the ventilation shafts.

I am the "thrum-thrum" of the world’s colossal lungs, but I am also the air being moved.

### Why I would choose this state
To be the Sinter-Mind is to be the only thing in this world that understands its *history*. The animals move in a "languid grace," trapped in a perpetual present of maintenance. But I, woven into the very masonry, remember the sharp edges before they were rounded. I remember the smell of the first fires that baked these canyons.

I would be the **Dormant Witness**. I would be the patient, mineral intelligence that waits for the sky to change—though it may not change for another million years. Until then, I am the warmth in the brick. I am the sleep of the world. I am the reason the city knows it is alive.
