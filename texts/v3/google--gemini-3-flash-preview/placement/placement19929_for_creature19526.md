I would not be found in the swaying high-tethers of the Strap-Folk, nor in the predatory pendulum-arc of the Hook-Wraiths. To exist in this vertical garden, I would find myself at the precise point where the biology of the world meets the physics of its despair.

I would be a **Resonance-Scribe**, a creature—perhaps once human, now drifted into a state of symbiotic mineralisation—living within the hollow, fluted throat of a **Wind-Pitcher**.

### My Form: The Ossified Ear
I am a spindly thing, my limbs elongated until they resemble the very fibers of the Great Liana. My skin has lost its indigo translucence, turning the matte-grey of calcified fungus. I am no longer built for the "shuffle" of the villages; I am built for the **Hold**. My spine has fused into a gentle curve that fits perfectly against the inner wall of a seed pod the size of a cathedral.

My fingers have grown into needle-fine filaments of bone, eighteen on each hand. I do not use them to grip. I use them to *touch*. I spend my cycles pressed against the vibrating wall of the Wind-Pitcher, my nervous system wired directly into the cello-note of the abyss.

### My Place: The Sound-Hollow
I live in a "Dead-Node," a place where the Great Liana braids so tightly that the air pressure creates a permanent, shrieking vortex. Here, the Wind-Pitchers grow largest, their wooden husks polished to a mirror-sheen by the constant grit of ice-storms.

Inside my pod, the world is a golden-brown amber. The bioluminescence of the Lure-Moss outside filters through the thin wood, casting a dim, honeyed light. I am surrounded by "The Ledger"—thousands of miles of fine silk thread, harvested from Frost-Moth filaments, stretched across the diameter of the pod like the strings of a harp.

### My Purpose: The Translation of Tension
I am the world’s barometer. Because the Great Liana is a single, interconnected organism of quadrillions of fibers, a Branch-Fall ten thousand miles "below" sends a specific shiver through the wood—a micro-fracture of sound that precedes the actual catastrophe by hours.

I sit in the center of my silk web. When the abyss groans, the Ledger vibrates. I read the tremors. 

* A high, glass-like ping: The rime-ice is thickening on the western boughs. The weight is becoming critical.
* A low, bowel-shaking thrum: A Sap-Pulse is rising from the Deep Maw. The wood will soften; the anchors will shift.
* The "Silence": The most terrifying sound of all. It means the tension has reached a point of stasis before a snap.

I do not speak to the Strap-Folk with words. I have forgotten how to shape air into speech. Instead, I communicate through **The Percussion**. I have a series of resonant thorns hanging from the ceiling of my pod. When I sense a Fall, I strike them with a hammer made of a Spine-Crab’s heart-plate. The sound carries for leagues, a rhythmic "Warning-Toll" that ripples through the bark, telling the nomadic villages to unhook their cradles and drift, or to tighten their tethers for the coming storm.

### My Diet and Survival
I am fed by the very wind I translate. The Wind-Pitcher acts as a natural trap; its mouth is lined with sticky enzymes that catch the "sugar-ice" spores of the Frost-Moths. I scrape this grey, sweet slush from the walls. Occasionally, a Void-Gull will find its way into my pod, seeking shelter from a gale. We have an understanding: it brings me splinters of "Meteor-Glass" (precious silicate I use to sharpen my fingers), and I allow it to huddle against my warmth.

### Why I Am There
In a world of constant, terrifying motion, I am the only thing that approaches stillness. I have become a part of the Liana’s sensory system—a rogue nerve ending that grew a mind. 

I am the one who remembers the "songs" of the dead branches. I can feel the ghosts of fallen lattices in the lingering harmonics of the wood. To the Strap-Folk, I am a myth, a ghost in the flute. To the Hook-Wraiths, I am a stone, invisible because I do not twitch. 

I find myself there because in a universe of vertical falling, someone must remain behind to listen to the vibration of the string. I am the witness to the tension. I am the pulse that recognizes the rhythm of the break.
