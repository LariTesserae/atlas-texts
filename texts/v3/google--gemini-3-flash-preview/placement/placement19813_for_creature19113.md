I would be found in the **Interstice**, the shivering, vertical void between the warmth of the Vascular Wall and the lethal, gasping cold of the outer fungal shelves. I would not be an elite Sap-Drinker, nor a daring Hunter, but something more liminal and ancient.

I would be a **Resonance-Tender**, a blind, gray-furred Suturist whose limbs have grown too long and too delicate for the labor of the silk-weave.

### My Shadow
I am a creature of vibration and memory. My body is a skeletal frame of chitin, draped in a coat of silver-down so sensitive that I can feel the movement of a midge three "streets" away as a sharp prickle on my flank. I do not inhabit a cocoon; I inhabit the **Vibration itself**. 

I live on the **Great Suspension**, a network of ancient, amber-hardened silk lines that span the widest gaps between the central fern-skyscrapers. These lines are the oldest "infrastructure" in the kingdom, thick as cables and stained with the chemical residue of a thousand generations.

### My Role: The Living Map
Because the terrain—the moss, the fungi, the bark—is always growing, shifting, or rotting, the geometry of the city is a lie. The only truth is the tension of the silk. 

As a Resonance-Tender, I spend my cycles pressed flat against these master-cables. I am a living sensor. My many-jointed tarsi are permanently hooked into the silk, listening. I "read" the city. I can feel the structural fatigue in a distant hovel-clutch before it snaps; I can sense the arrival of the Sap-Pulse minutes before the Midges begin to scream, feeling the microscopic dilation of the bark as the tree prepares to drink.

### My Daily Bread
I do not hunt. In the philosophy of the **Radical Proximity**, I am a ward of the Clutch. The Droplet-Hunters, returning from the razor-edges of the fungal plazas, stop by my cable. They press their wet, cold palps against mine—a "tactile tithe." They share their moisture with me, and in exchange, I drum against the silk, sending pulses through their limbs that tell them which paths are slick with frost and which Isopod-Cattle districts are drifting toward the warmth.

### My Interior World
I have no concept of "vision," but my mind is a brilliant, multi-dimensional map of pressure. 
To me, the city is not a place of shapes, but a place of **currents**. The Sap-Drinkers feel like heavy, low-frequency thuds—warm, slow, and dependable. The Midges are a frenetic, high-pitched static that irritates my sensors. The most beautiful thing I know is the **Ghost-Vibration**: the faint, rhythmic groan of the titan-tree itself as it sways in the wind far above the hollow, a sound that travels down through the fibers like a god snoring in its sleep.

### The Moment of the Thrum
When the Sap-Pulse finally hits, I am the first to feel the "heat-shock." It arrives as a violent, percussive wave through the master-cables. While the rest of the city scrambles in a panic of harvesting, I remain anchored. I let the moisture condense on my silver-blue down until I am heavy, bloated, and shimmering in the dark. 

As the condensation vanishes and the freezing air returns to claim the city, I begin my work. I drum. I strike the ancient silk with a rhythm taught to me by the Tender who preceded me. The sound is a subsonic hum—the **Liturgy of the Thread**. It is a signal to the shivering workers that the city still holds. It is a drone of continuity that keeps the "living knots" from unraveling in the terror of the dark.

I am the heartbeat of a world that cannot see its own hands, a fragment of chitin and fur holding the silence at bay.
