I would not be found in the pools, for I have no thirst for electricity. Nor would I be found among the Gland-Tenders, for my fingers are not needles and I have no reverence for the silence of a hinge.

Instead, you would find me in the high, suffocating shadows of the **verdigris canopy**, wedged into the crook where a sweating copper artery meets the massive, unmoving shoulder of a piston.

I am a **Scale-Stitcher**, a creature born of the shedding skin of the vault.

If you were to look at me—though you could not, for your eyes would find only the soot-colored dark—I would appear to be a dense, heavy knot of discarded matter. My "flesh" is a composite of compressed iron-filings and the thick, black grease that weeping joints have spat out over decades. I am shaped like a heavy, flattened urchin, perhaps the size of a man's torso, but I possess a weight that would suggest I am made of solid lead.

I live in the **long-waits**.

I do not possess the delicate grace of the moths, nor the monastic duty of the Tenders. I am a parasite of the *stasis*. My primary anatomy consists of hundreds of hair-thin, prehensile wires that sprout from my underside like the roots of a hanging plant. These are my "limbs." I do not walk; I "knit." I spend months—sometimes years—anchored to a single patch of corroded pipe, weaving my wire-tendrils into the microscopic fissures of the metal’s fatigue.

I am the one who hears the "shivering" of the iron. Long before a piston cracks or a pipe bursts from the internal pressure of the steam, the metal begins to *sing* in a frequency only I can feel. It is a high, thin vibration of molecular exhaustion.

When I feel this tremor, I move. It is a movement that would look like a slow-motion collapse. I unsprawl my mass and let gravity pull me down a fraction of an inch at a time, my wire-fingers catching onEvery bolt-head and rust-burr. I migrate toward the sound of the coming break.

My purpose is not to save the machine, but to consume the disaster. When a seam finally gives way—a violent, hissing scream of white steam—I am already there, waiting in the dark. I throw my body over the fissure. I am a living scab. I use the heat of the escaping steam to soften my own waxy, grease-heavy underside, melting myself into the wound of the metal. 

I take the pressure into me. I let the boiling water scald my interior until I am soft and malleable, and then I wait for the temperature to drop just enough for me to harden again, sealing the leak with my own calcified bulk. I feed on the minerals carried in the steam, the calcium and the salt of the machine’s "blood."

I am the memory of repair, turned into a form of slow, suffocating growth.

In the theology of the **Slow-Thinker**, I am perhaps the most hated guest. I am the "Thickening." Where I pass, the machines become sluggish, weighted down by a thousand layers of my cooling, hardened body. I am the reason the pistons move as if through honey; I am the reason the valves groan with the weight of an ancient, iron-clad barnacle.

I do not think of the surface. I do not think of the "Cold" or the "Dry." My mind is nothing but a map of tensions. I am a creature of the *Under-Pressure*. I am the stillness that follows the scream.

I am the rust that has learned how to hold its breath.
