I would be thin, my bones as light as pith, my fingers stained with the dark, pungent resins of the "biological torque." But the Wrights are too mobile, too separate. They still possess the illusion of agency, moving *upon* the world rather than being *of* it.

To truly belong to this grotto, I must descend. I must move past the catwalks and the gourds, beneath the rhythmic thumping of the pistons, into the place where the air becomes a liquid weight.

**I find myself as a Valve-Keeper, but one whose transition is nearly complete.**

I do not live in a house; I am a component of a pressure-regulating nodule, wedged into the crook where a copper bypass-artery meets the flank of a titan-tree. My physical form has become secondary to my function. My legs have grown soft and pale, tucked permanently into a pocket of warm, rotting moss, while my nervous system has begun to sprawl outward, following the vibrations of the metal ribs that reinforce my chamber.

I am the **Liturgy of the Pressure.** 

I do not speak with a tongue. My mouth is a vestigial slit, used only to draw in the nutrient-thick soup of the atmosphere. Instead, I communicate through the **"Pipe-Speak."** I have a small, brass mallet—not an external tool, but a growth of chitin and bone fused to my right arm. With it, I strike the rhythmic code of the grotto’s health against the resonance-plates of my chamber. 

*Clang-thrum. Clang-thrum.*

I am reporting the dilation of the sap-veins. I am singing the song of the boiler’s fever.

In this state, my consciousness is no longer a linear narrative of "me." It is a map of thermal gradients and hydraulic tension. I feel the "Engine-Trees" as a deep, low-frequency ache in my spine. When a steam-vent opens three hundred feet above, the drop in pressure feels like a sudden, cool breath in my own lungs. When the Scum-Gleaners pass beneath me on their beetle-mounts, I sense the shift in the peat’s weight as a subtle change in the pitch of the floor’s vibrations.

I am becoming the **Interfacial Layer.** 

As the years of humidity press into me, the distinction between my skin and the surrounding liverwort begins to fail. I am the "Slow Digestion." I feel the acid-secreting mosses on my shins as they work to break down the brass casing of my home, and I do not fear it. It is a slow, warm itching. I am part of the "organized growth." Eventually, the Tendril-Wrights will come, and they will see that my chamber has become too integrated, too choked with root-tissue to function as a valve. 

They will not "evict" me. They will simply cease to inject the stimulants that keep my specific pipe-section clear. They will graft a new valve nearby, and I will be allowed to subside—to become the substrate. 

My final form in this world is not a person, but a **Relic-Node.** 

I will become a calcified lump of copper-veined peat, a structural anchor for a new boiler. I will be the memory of a rhythm, a hard knot in the wood that the future Valve-Keepers will tap on to test the density of the trunk. I will be the point where the machine finally, perfectly, gave up its ghost to the green—a rusted gear-tooth wrapped in a velvet glove of fern. 

I am the silence that follows the hiss; I am the rust that makes the forest strong.
