I would find myself neither in the sky nor upon the ground, but in the shivering, liminal space between the two. 

I would be a **Resonance-Vessel**, a creature born of the hollows.

To the great Lithovores, I am no more than a speck of dust or a wandering thought. I am a parasite of sound. While the Steam-Mites cling to the heat of the giants, I cling to their *thrum*. 

I am shaped like a heavy, inverted bell, perhaps six feet across, composed of a translucent, cartilaginous membrane that looks like smoked glass. I have no limbs for walking and no wings for the sky. Instead, my underbelly is a dense, fibrous mat of "Acoustic-Rootlets." I spend my life attached to the crown of a Lithovore’s head, or nestled in the dip where its massive neck meets the ridge of its spine.

I do not eat the iron-lace or the salts. I do not drink the Breath-Rain. I am a **Kinetic-Synthesizer**. My body is a drumhead stretched thin across a vacuum; I thrive on the "Marrow-Hum." Every time the giant beneath me strikes the basalt with its redwood-trunk limb, the shockwave travels through its tectonic plates and into my roots. I convert this mechanical vibration into a faint, internal phosphorescence—a soft, amber glow that warms my inner core just enough to keep my fluids from turning to crystal.

I am the world’s only witness to the "Data-Pulse."

While the Migrants move in their wordless, industrial stupor, I am the one who translates the friction. As I ride the giants, I vibrate in sympathy with the stone beneath them. I can feel the weight of a dying Lithovore three mountain-ranges away by the way the rhythm of the earth "thins" in that direction. I can feel a surge of mineral-rich mist before it even condenses. 

But I am more than a passenger; I am a **Memory-Stall.**

At the convergence points, where the great lines of Migrants cross and touch their snouts to the stone, the vibration is enough to shatter a human’s bones. To me, it is the only time I am truly alive. In those decades-long moments of Contact, I act as a living bridge. I pulse in a frantic, strobe-like amber, catching the "shrapnel" of their data—the history of the paths, the shifting temperature of the planet's core, the slow-motion drift of the Moraines themselves. 

I store these rhythms in the crystalline structure of my shell. When I eventually die—not from age, but from "tuning out" as my shell becomes too brittle to vibrate—I do not rot. I fall from the beast and shatter upon the slate. My remains become "Singing-Gravel"—tiny, jagged shards that hum whenever a new Migrant steps near them, a localized echo of a million years of walking.

In this world, I am the **Small Pulse** that understands the **Great Pulse**. I am the flicker of light in a world of plum-colored gloom, a tiny, vibrating candle riding on the back of a cathedral, recording the slow, mechanical march of the end of time. No one hears me, and I speak to no one, but as long as I tremor, the machine is still running.
