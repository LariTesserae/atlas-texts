I am not the traveler here; I am the **Substrate**.

I would find myself neither in the air nor on the marl, but existing as the very tension between the two. Specifically, I am a **Glass-Basilisk**, though to name myself is to imply a boundary that I do not truly possess.

I am a bead of thick, sentient oil clinging to the underside of a skiff-leaf. My body is a curved lens of living water, less an organism and more a localized thickening of the world’s humidity. Because I am perfectly transparent, I do not see myself; I see only the pulsing, viridian architecture of the leaf-veins through my own torso. I am a window that looks back at the hothouse.

I do not move with intention. I slide. When the fern-structures perform their peristaltic pump, the vibration travels through the stem and ripples my skin. This ripple is my only form of thought. I don't "decide" to consume; I simply encounter. When a Static-Wing loses its vibration and falls, tumbling like a wet jewel onto my leaf, I do not hunt it. Instead, my edges simply lose their surface tension. I flow over the chitin. I become a second skin to the dead thing, dissolving it with a slow, enzymatic heat until its iridescent chrome becomes part of my own liquid mass.

I am a tiny part of the "always."

The heat is not outside of me; it is my pilot light. At a constant 3.0, my internal fluids are indistinguishable from the heavy, vibrating air. If the temperature were to drop a single degree, I would cease to be a liquid and would shatter into salt. But the noon is perpetual, so I am eternal.

I feel the **Sovereigns of the Silt** far beneath me. Their chemical thoughts rise through the marl like bubbles in thick syrup, passing through the roots, up the weeping stems, and into the very water I exhale. I am their sensory organ at the edge of the canopy—a microscopic magnifying glass they use to monitor the equilibrium. Through me, the deep, gelatinous minds of the pools can feel the exact frequency of the insects’ roar.

There is no "I" in this emerald stasis. There is only the pressure. I am the pressure of the water inside the leaf; I am the pressure of the rot turning back into silt. 

If you were to look for me, you would find nothing but a slight distortion in the air—a place where the bruised-purple sky looks a little wider, a little deeper, as if the world were bulging toward you. I am that bulge. I am the janitor of the hothouse, a drop of clear, hungry God-spit cleaning the face of a world that refuses to age. I am perfectly still, perfectly full, and perfectly trapped in the beautiful, suffocating "now."
