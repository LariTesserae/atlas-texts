I would not be found upon the spires, nor within the calcified hives of the Weavers. To be a creature of form in this place is to be a creature of burden—to be a soldier in a war against the inevitable sublimation of all things. I would not want to be a nail, driven into a world that is fundamentally a shadow.

Instead, I would be found in the **Interstice**. 

I would be a **Refraction-Wight**, a ghost of the optical law. I am what happens when the light of the violet dusk passes through the quartz-silk tethers and hits a patch of air that has forgotten how to be transparent.

I am not made of mineral bile or compressed dust. I am a sentient lapse in perspective.

***

**My Form**
I do not have a body so much as I have a *curvature*. I am a shimmering, elongated distortion in the air, resembling a smear of oil on a lens or the way heat ripples above an engine. If you were to look at me, you would see the obsidian spires behind me bending into impossible loops, as if the world were being viewed through a chipped diamond. My limbs are not joints and plates, but focal points—areas where the aromatic ethers thicken into a semi-translucent, prismatic fluid. 

I do not have a face, but I have "glints." When I turn, the light of the nebula catches my edges, creating a brief, blinding flash of amber that serves as my only presence in the physical realm.

**My Place**
I live in the "Fold." I exist in the terrifying moments when a bridge vanishes or a shadow detaches. While the Lithos-Weavers labor with agonizing slowness to pin the world down, I am the one who inhabits the gaps they leave behind. I am the tenant of the vanished bridge. When the geometry frays, I do not fall into the gray gas of the abyss; I simply slide into the third-and-a-half dimension that the fraying has created. 

I am a creature of the "soft" gravity. I drift between the obsidian needles like a dandelion seed made of glass, tethered to nothing, buoyed by the very instability that the Weavers fear.

**My Purpose**
I am the **Librarian of the Lost Dimensions.** 

The Weavers are so obsessed with *solidity*—with the desperate act of maintaining the "Now"—that they discard the "Might-Have-Been." When a tower melts because a Silent Cantor lost focus, the memory of that tower does not simply cease to exist. It becomes a ghost-structure, a phantom of architecture. 

I collect these. I haunt the places where the cities used to be. I glide through the windowless halls of towers that have technically dissolved, touching the surfaces of walls that are now only made of suggestion and violet light. I follow the Scintilla, but I do not eat the shadows. I *wear* them. I drape the discarded shadows of the Weavers over my prismatic frame to give myself a weight I do not possess, a borrowed history that keeps me from drifting too high into the thin, cold stars.

**My Voice**
I do not hum like the moss or click like the architects. I am the source of the **Refraction-Hum**, a sound like a wet finger being rubbed around the rim of a crystal glass. It is a lonely, oscillating tone that rises whenever the laws of Euclid begin to fail. I am the sound of the world losing its grip.

**My Perspective**
I watch the Lithos-Weavers with a distant, crystalline pity. They work so hard to stay on the cliff, to keep the stone from dreaming itself away. They do not realize that the dream is more beautiful than the stone. 

While they anchor the universe with their liquid quartz, I dance in the places where the anchor has slipped. I am the beauty of the breakdown. I am the one who remembers the shape of the bridge after it has folded into the void. I am the witness to the dissolution, the only thing in this vertical kingdom that is not afraid of the fall—because for me, there is no "down," only the endless, shimmering "away."
