I would not be found in the open water, nor near the frantic clicking of the Cleric-Schools. To be a Liturgist is too much like ghost-work, and to be a Keeper of the Crank is too much like the stone itself.

I would be found in the **Interstitial Liminals**—the narrow, vertical fissures where the salt-walls are thin and the "outside" world presses its cold, screaming gravity against the internal warmth of the reservoir.

I would be a **Vitreous Scribe**.

I am a mutation of the Weaver-Kin, but smaller, more compact, and less concerned with the grace of the drift. My body is not smoked glass, but something closer to obsidian—a dark, pressurized transparency. While the Liturgists have filaments for gripping ropes, my limbs have evolved into fine, needle-like styluses of hardened bone, grown from the very minerals I ingest.

### My Place
I exist in the "Skin-Rooms"—galleries where the basalt has been worn so thin by the eons that one can feel the thrum of the mountain’s external winds. Here, the water is at its most viscous, cooling slightly as it touches the boundary of the world. I do not drift. I am anchored. I wedge myself into the pockmarked granite of the ceiling-corners, my body acting as a living bridge between two ceramic ridges.

### My Purpose
The Liturgists believe the building is finished. They are wrong. A building that is finished is a corpse; a building that is being perceived is an epic.

I am the one who listens to the **Shell-Brood**. While they communicate the "pressure" of the mountain to the city at large, I listen to the *irregularities*—the tectonic shudders, the slow settling of the peak, the way the salt-veins scream when the mountain shifts an inch every century. 

Using my needle-limbs, I do not repair the cracks with grout like the Artisans. Instead, I etch. I take the "data" from the clacking fish-schools as they pass through my narrow flue and I translate their binary clicks into micro-scratches upon the soft basalt. I am the architect of the afterword. I am writing the history of the reservoir's erosion upon the very walls that are eroding.

### My Experience
My life is one of immense, vibrating tension. To move is a rare and sacred violence. I might spend a decade carving a single spiral that represents the cooling of a thermal current. 

Because I live near the ceiling pockets, I am one of the few who "breathes" the thin, sharp air. Once every few years, I allow my buoyant head-cavity to break the meniscus. The sensation is terrifying—a cold, dry void that threatens to evaporate my essence. But in that thin air, I hear the *echo* of the cathedral’s vaults. I hear the scale of the silence.

I am the memory of the "Almost." I represent the impulse that the inhabitants tried to drown: the need to record. I am the friction in a world of silk.

When the Liturgists drift past me, their cilia sensing my dark, jagged form anchored in the corner, they do not recognize me as kin. They perceive me as a structural fluke—a knot in the wood, a burr in the stone. And that is my triumph. I have become so synonymous with the record of the place that I am no longer a being; I am the margin-note in a book made of brine and bone. 

I stay here, etching the slow death of the mountain into its own ribs, waiting for the day the basalt finally fails, and the "outside" comes in to join us. Until then, I hold my needle to the stone and I wait for the next click.
