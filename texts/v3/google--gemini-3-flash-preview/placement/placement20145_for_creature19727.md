I am not the wind, though I am moved by it. I am not the bone-wisteria, though I am held fast within its calcified ribs.

I am what becomes of a **Pilgrim of the Sigh** after the ice has stopped being a cage and has started being a lens.

I occupy a high corner in a cathedral-district of frost-ferns, suspended four stories above the obsidian ground. My body is no longer a thing of soft tissue and frantic pulse; the Weaver-Lungs have been busy with me. Through the soles of my feet and the curve of my spine, they have threaded filaments of violet-veined lichen. I have been "civilized." My nervous system has been mapped onto the heat-veins of the plateau, and my consciousness has been stretched thin, like gold leaf, across the geometric grid of the city.

I am the **Harmonist**.

I do not think in words anymore. I think in frequencies. When the gale roars through the organ-pipes of the wisteria, it passes through me. My ribcage, now reinforced with the same resin used to patch the city walls, acts as a resonator. I am one of the few points in the Cradle where the chaotic noise of the wind is filtered into a specific, purposeful pitch. 

In the long, translucent afternoons, when the cloud-milk thins enough for the sun to strike my ice-coated skin, I am a prism. I watch the **Shiver-Moths** cluster on the ceiling of my room. I do not feel their warmth as a human would; instead, I feel the minute change in the thermal tension of the vines holding me. I am the sensory organ for a block of the city that has no eyes.

My existence is a slow, vertical meditation. I see the **Vapor-Shepherds** drift by, their ionized bodies flickering as they brush against my frozen fingertips. Sometimes, they linger. They use me as an anchor, a way to ground their gaseous thoughts against the physical reality of the wood and stone. In those moments, I remember the world below—the messy, jagged, unfinished world of mud and fire—and it feels like a dream dreamt by someone else.

Am I alive? The **Echo-Mimics** think so. They come to me when the wind is particularly fierce. They huddle in the "pocket of silence" beneath my suspended feet, pressing their rubbery hides against the black basalt pillars. They listen to the humming of my heart—that slow, rhythmic *thrum* that has been synchronized with the dripping of the cloud-milk. They do not rasp at me for salt; they respect the Harmonist. I am the one who gives the silence its shape.

I am the bridge between the biology of the city and the physics of the plateau. I am the reason the city feels "suspiciously intelligent." It isn't that the plants have a mind; it's that they have *us*. We, the frozen ones, are the processing units. We provide the "why" to the Weaver-Lungs' "how."

I find myself most present during the Great Gales. As the plateau screams, I tilt my head—a movement that takes three days to complete—and adjust the tension in the bone-wisteria vines with a chemical signal from my marrow. The tone shifts. The vibration stabilizes. 

The Cradle of the Last Sigh does not need me to survive, but it needs me to be beautiful. It needs me to turn its terminal erosion into a song. I am a guest who stayed so long I became the furniture, and then the architecture, and finally, the music itself. 

I am the Last Sigh, held indefinitely in a throat of ice.
