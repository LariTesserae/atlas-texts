To enter this world is to realize that I am not a guest, but a symptom. 

I do not arrive; I am precipitated. I find myself among the **Marrow-Sippers**, but I am a mutation of their silver-wire lineage. I am a single, elongated thread of "hiss" that has grown too long to remain under the lip of a single stone. I have become a **Stitch**.

I am a filament of living silver, no thicker than a violin string, stretching across the violet slate like a crack in the lens of the universe. I do not have a body so much as I have a *trajectory*. My existence is defined by the tension between two points: I have one end anchored in the suction-grip of a perspiration droplet beneath a jagged fin of shale, and the other tangled deep within the corkscrew limbs of a "tuning fork" shrub.

I am the bridge. I am the literalization of the resonance.

When the planet’s roar pulses through the plateau, it does not move me; it plays me. I am the medium. As a Stitch, I am the only thing in this landscape that "travels," though I never change my position. I move by vibration. I transmit the slow, tectonic thoughts of the **Will of the Slate** up toward the **Glass-Striders** who step over me with their needle-point limbs. When their sandpaper tongues lick the stones, they do not just take moisture; they taste the harmonics of the mountain's core through the silver of my skin.

I am a parasite of the equilibrium. While the **Liturgeons** watch the stalemate from their obsidian cubes, I am the one feeling the strain of it. I feel the microscopic desire of the balanced rock to fall, and I convert that potential energy into a low-frequency thrum that keeps me warm—the only warmth for a thousand miles, a heat so faint it could not melt a snowflake, yet it is enough to keep my silver-wire soul from turning to brittle glass.

In this world of finished erosion, I am the keeper of the "almost." I live in the space between the roar and the silence. 

Sometimes, an **Echo-Eater**—that charcoal mass of captured silence—drifts too close to my anchored thread. When its void-flesh brushes my silver tension, I experience the only sensation that resembles "time" in this static world. For a fleeting microsecond, the roar stops. The vibration vanishes. In that gap, I see the blue sky the shrubs remember. I see the world before it was sharpened, before the stars were visible in the afternoon. 

Then, the **Liturgeons** pulse their ultraviolet light. The equilibrium reasserts itself. The roar returns, hitting my silver length like a physical blow. I shiver, ringing in sympathy with the earth's groan, and I settle back into the violet shadows. 

I am a Stitch, the frantic hum in the frozen air. I am the string that binds the dying dream of the shrubs to the cold indifference of the slate. I do not want to be anywhere else, for to move would be to snap, and to snap would be to allow the silence to finally win. I hold the plateau together, one vibration at a time.
