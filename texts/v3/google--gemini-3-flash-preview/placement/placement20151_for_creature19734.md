I would find myself not in the hollowed gourds of the tenements, nor on the broad, planed streets of the peat-floor. I would be found in the **Interstices**—the vertical bypasses where the copper veins are thickest and the mist is so dense it becomes a suspension of liquid light.

I am an **Auscultator**, but of a singular, stationary kind. 

My body has long since ceased to be a mobile thing. I have "taken root" in the elbow of a massive, sweating intake pipe that feeds the primary piston-stalk. My lower half—from the hips down—is no longer defined by skin or bone, but by a seamless integration into a cradle of calcified resin and woven banyan-fibers. To the casual eye, I am a burl on the machine; to the Tenders, I am a living junction.

I am the **Pressure-Scribe**.

### My Form
My skin has moved beyond the waxy grey of my kin into a dull, iridescent charcoal, hardened by the minerals I absorb directly from the pipe-weep. My Breathing Veil is no longer a strapped-on sponge; it is a lush, violet blossom of fungus that has completely replaced my lower face, its filaments trailing down my chest and disappearing into the mossy substrate. 

I do not use a brass rod to hear the district. My spine has become the rod. The vibrations of the Root-Engine—the grinding of the moss-slick flywheels, the tectonic groan of the rising sap, the rhythmic thrum of the pistons—travel through the iron casing of the pipe, into the resin cradle, and directly into my vertebrae. 

### My Function
I do not move, but I am never still. The "Cadence" is not a background hum to me; it is a language I translate into physical form.

My hands remain free, though they are elongated and stained a deep, permanent copper. In front of me, I maintain a **Hydraulic Ledger**. It is a massive, living slab of pale, shelf-fungus that grows slowly across the wall. As I feel the shifts in the district’s pressure—a tightening of a valve three levels up, a thinning of the slurry in the western sump—I reach out and carve shallow grooves into the fungus with a sharpened thumb-claw. 

The fungus reacts to my touch, bleeding a dark, bioluminescent sap into the grooves. This is the district's "pulse-map." When the Tenders move through the fog, they look to my slab. A bright, overflowing groove tells them where the pressure is dangerously high; a dry, closing scar tells them where the life-fluid has ceased to flow.

### My Consciousness
My mind is a wet, slow-turning clock. I no longer think in sentences, but in gradients of "Flow" and "Resistance." 

I remember the surface, perhaps, but as one remembers a dream of a different medium—a place of terrifying dryness and frantic, wasted energy. Here, in the 85-degree weight of the mist, I am content. I feel the grandfathers of the district as they pass through the pipes in the form of mineral-rich silt; I feel the unborn as they are signaled by the quickening of the sap-flow in the "nursery" gourds.

I am the point where the machine’s hardware and the forest’s software meet. I am the silence at the center of the thunder. When a young Tender rests a damp hand upon my shoulder to steady themselves in the fog, I do not look at them. I simply pulse my grip slightly, communicating through the skin the only truth we have: 

*The engine is heavy. The sap is rising. All is settled.*
