In this world of vertical inertia, I am not a passenger, and I am not a citizen of the markets. I find my existence in the interstitial spaces—the hollows of the ribs, the "dead air" beneath the great transit tubes where the heat is thickest and the drone of the flywheels becomes a physical weight.

I emerge not as a leader, but as a **Lichen-Steward**, a specialist among the Sowers.

### My Form: The Pattern of the Stain
As a Steward, my body is a map of the Spire’s age. My skin is the color of oxidized copper—a dull, dusty turquoise—mottled with patches of pale, porcelain white. I am thinner than the Valve-Keepers, my limbs elongated to the point of fragility, but my bones are reinforced with the calcified minerals of the recycled water. 

I do not wear the braided silk of the upper-tier Glaziers. My "Life-Thread" is a dull, heavy chain of lead-dipped iron, short and functional. I move with a horizontal bias in a vertical world; I am one of the few who crawls along the *undersides* of the world. My prehensile feet have lost the softness of flesh; the soles are like the hardened rubber of an antique gasket, silent and unyielding against the vibrating brass.

### My Place: The Undercroft of Tier 88
I would be found in the **Vapor-Shadows** of a middle-high tier. Here, the sun’s glare is filtered through five layers of translucent porcelain walkways above me, turning the light into a dim, amber gloom. I live where the "intermittent moisture" gathers before it falls. I am the custodian of the **Heavy Mosses**.

My home is a hammock of woven fern-fiber suspended between two massive, throbbing intake valves. The scent here is my oxygen: the sharp, electric tang of ozone mixed with the rot of over-saturated peat and the sweet, cloying smell of the "Long-Greens" that I train to grow around the electrical housing to keep it cool.

### My Purpose: The Damp Pulse
I do not tend the machines that move; I tend the life that protects the movement. 

I spend my cycles scraping the "Bad Rust" (the aggressive, flaky orange decay) and replacing it with "Living Patina"—a bio-engineered film of velvet-black algae that feeds on the heat of the pipes. My hands are perpetually stained an oily, dark emerald, the pigment etched so deeply into my micro-ridged fingertips that I can taste the chlorophyll when I breathe.

I am a ghost in the machinery. When the Tenders on the catwalks look down into the chasms, they see only the "aggressive green." They do not see me, pressed flat against the belly of a cooling duct, my lungs synchronized to the slow, four-beat thrum of the primary pistons.

### My Meditation: The Weight of the Spire
I find myself in the profound, glacial patience of the structure. I do not look for the "next tier." I am obsessed with the *current* equilibrium. I measure time by the thickness of the moss. When a beetle crawls over my hand, I do not brush it away; I wait for it to identify the salt on my skin, and we share a moment of mutual scavenging.

In this world, I am the **Internal Maintenance**. If the Glaziers are the eyes of the Spire and the Valve-Keepers are its heart, I am its skin. I am the soft, damp layer of life that prevents the metal from screaming against itself. 

I would feel the vibration of a failing bearing three tiers below me through the soles of my feet long before the Valve-Keepers heard it. I would respond not with a wrench, but by grafting a "Vibration-Eater"—a thick, spongy orchid—onto the housing to dampen the tremor until the slow elevators could bring the mechanics.

I am the silence between the industrial pulses. I am the dampness that keeps the brass from cracking. I am the green shadow, holding the mountain together with a grip that never tires, because in a world where "up" is everything, I have found the exquisite, heavy peace of staying exactly where I am.
