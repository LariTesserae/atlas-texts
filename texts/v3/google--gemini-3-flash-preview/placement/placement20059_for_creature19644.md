In the Vertical Sump, I am not found on the catwalks or within the limestone shrine. To look for me there is to look at the interface, rather than the mechanism. 

I am a **Wick-Tender**, but I am of the "Deep-Hanging" variety—those whose brass exo-rigs have become so fused with the calcium deposits of the weeping water that we can no longer unbolt ourselves from the Mother-Vine. 

My world is not one of horizontal planes. I exist in a permanent, forty-five-degree tilt, suspended over the three-mile drop by a series of tensioned copper cables that hum against my sternum. I do not feel the vertigo of the abyss anymore; the mist is so thick that the "down" is merely a gradient of darkening grey, a suggestion of a floor that I have long ago accepted as a myth.

### My Form
I am a creature of layered textures. My skin, where it is visible between the rusted plates of my harness, is the color of a bruised plum, slick with the omnipresent "green-blindness" algae. I do not move with muscles so much as with the slow, hydraulic pressure of my exo-rig. My fingers have been elongated by generations of specialized labor, ending in hooked, obsidian-hard nails designed to puncture the waxy flesh of the Mother-Vine to find a purchase.

I carry the scent of the Sump: the bitter, sharp tang of ozone, the smell of charred hair from electrical arcs, and the sweet, rotting perfume of the bromeliads. 

### My Daily Accumulation
I do not "work" in shifts; I exist in a state of perpetual maintenance. 

I am currently stationed near **Turbine 09**, a massive brass egoist that groans once every forty minutes. My primary purpose is to act as a living filter. With a long, hooked scraper made of scavenged bone and copper, I peel the thick quilts of moss from the turbine’s cooling fins. If the moss grows too thick, the friction of the water won't bite—the gear won't turn, the Priest won't "bridge," and the Sump loses its pulse.

As I work, I feel the **Siphon-Eels** sliding over my calves. They are cold, like ribbons of melting ice, seeking the metal-shavings I dislodge from the gears. I do not shrug them off; their transit cleans my skin of the parasites that bloom in the heat of the machinery.

### My Perception
I do not communicate with words. The sub-bass thrum of the Sump is too loud for the frailty of a human voice. Instead, I communicate through the **Heavy Moths** that cling to my shoulders. I have three of them currently; their wings are the size of dinner plates and heavy with moisture. By the way their antennae twitch, I can feel the change in the atmosphere. When they flatten themselves against my chest, I know a "pressure-burp" is coming—a sudden, violent sneeze of scalding steam from the basalt’s depths. When that happens, I lock my harness, tuck my head into the oil-silk collar of my robes, and wait.

Time for me is measured by the calcium build-up on my rivets. I am becoming thicker. Every day, the mineral-heavy water adds a microscopic layer of stone to my gear, then to my clothes, then to me. 

### My Place in the Soul of the Spire
I find myself most "myself" during the **Long Drip**—the hour of the day when the thermal inversion is most dense, and the light from the upper atmosphere turns the mist into a glowing, golden syrup. 

In these moments, I stop scraping. I press my cheek against the vibrating black basalt, feeling the Earth’s young, violent heart beating three miles below. I am the bridge between the industrial greed of the turbines and the silent, aggressive patience of the greenery. I am a knot in the Great Vertical Weave.

I know that one day, my harness will seize for the last time. The petrification will reach my joints, and I will stop swinging. I will not fall; the Mother-Vine will simply grow its emerald ferns over my brass casing, and a younger Wick-Tender will use my calcified helmet as a stepping stone to reach a higher valve. 

I am a component of the Sump. I am the friction that allows the slow world to turn. I am the wet, heavy breath of the apex, waiting for the next drop of water to fall.
