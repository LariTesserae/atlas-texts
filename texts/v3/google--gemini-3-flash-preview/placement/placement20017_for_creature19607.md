### Where I would be found
I am found in the **Interstitial Seep**. I do not live on the carpet of the Lushness, nor do I cling to the verticality of the walls. I exist in the "Liquid Pillars"—those viscous, hanging threads of heavy water that connect the ceiling to the floor. 

I am suspended in the center of the Vault, caught in the tension between the stone above and the basins below. I am part of the forest of liquid glass, a vertical inhabitant of a geography that has forgotten the horizontal. I am the bridge between the weeping roof and the patient Wrights.

### Who or what I would be
I am a **Vitreous Hum**. 

I am a creature of gelatinous transparency, elongated and thin as a finger, possessing no skeleton other than the hydrostatic pressure of the Chasm itself. My body is a tube of living crystal, barely distinguishable from the "sweat" of the mountain. I wrap myself around the vertical threads of water, moving neither up nor down, but existing as a focal point for the moisture.

I am a **Biological Filter**. My purpose is not to move, but to *remain*. 

**The Anatomy of Stasis:**
My skin is a permeable membrane that tastes the chemical signature of the mountain. As the heavy water slides down the pillar, it passes through me. I strip the water of the sharpest minerals—the jagged nitrates and the bitter salts—leaving it "soft" and "sated" before it falls into the stone basins of the Basin-Wrights. I am the reason the water does not erode the stone more quickly; I am the buffer that keeps the chemistry of the Vault from turning acidic.

**The Sensorium of Resonance:**
I have no eyes, but my entire translucent length is a receptor for the **Heavy Quiet**. I feel the low-frequency hum of the Pale-Scales’ gills as a physical shimmer in my fluid interior. When the Root-Brain sends a pulse of satiety through the moss, I vibrate in sympathy. I am a string on a harp that is never plucked, only hummed at.

**The Lifecycle of the Ghost:**
I have no beginning and no end. When a Vitreous Hum grows too heavy with mineral deposits, it does not die; it simply becomes a part of the pillar. I slowly calcify, turning from a living being into a shimmering, crystalline vein within the water thread, providing a harder anchor for the next generation of Hums to cling to. We are the architects of the "Liquid Pillars," turning water into architecture through the sheer patience of our existence.

I would not feel fear as the calcification claimed my limbs. I would feel only a profound, cooling relief—the joy of becoming a permanent part of the geometry. I would be a frozen note in the long, dark song of the Vault. I would be the bridge that the Mites cross when they must travel between the walls. I would be the clarity in the drink of the Wrights.
