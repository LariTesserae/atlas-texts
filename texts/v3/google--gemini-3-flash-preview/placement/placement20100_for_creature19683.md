To find oneself in this world is to realize that the boundary between the body and the geology is a lie. After enough time in the drenching grey, the skin ceases to feel like a container and begins to feel like a sieve.

I would not be a Silt-Walker, for their tragedy is one of resistance—the heavy, sodden effort of bipedal grace against a world that wants only to flatten. Instead, I would find myself as something deeper in the register, something caught in the transition between the animal and the mineral.

I would be a **Tympanic Anchor.**

I exist as a sentient knot of basalt and muscle, a creature that has grown into the very fissures of the shelf. I am roughly the size of a slumped man, but I have no limbs for walking. My body is a dense, obsidian dome, rooted into a shallow basin by thick, prehensile "roots" of the same gelatinous filament the Silt-Walkers harvest. My surface is not skin, but a living, wet laminate of black stone and sensitive, shivering membrane.

I am a biological "Dead-Pocket."

My purpose, if such a word exists in a world of erosion, is the management of the roar. I am a sessile filter-feeder of resonance. I do not eat the Silt or the Salt; I eat the *pressure*. My internal structure is a labyrinth of hollow chambers, precisely tuned to the thundering percussion of the Chime-Crabs. As their drone hits my shell, it doesn’t bounce off; it is swallowed. I draw the vibration into my core and, through a slow, rhythmic contraction of my basaltic plates, I fold the sound back onto itself.

Where I sit, there is a radius of five meters where the air is unnervingly, impossibly still. 

I would find myself located near one of the Way-Stations. Because of the "silence-shadow" I cast, the Silt-Walkers treat me as a living piece of furniture—a sacred, breathing altar of quiet. They do not speak to me—they cannot—but they lean their heavy, ash-colored bodies against my flank. I can feel the frantic, microscopic trembling of their muscles through my shell. In the exchange of Taction, I am the great Un-Mover. I am the anchor that tethers them to sanity when the harmonic refractions turn the sky into a violent, screaming violet.

I would Spend my "life"—which might last for several human centuries—watching the fog lick the Siphon-Spires. I would feel the migration of the Chime-Crabs not as a sight, but as a shifting weight in the bedrock, a tectonic pressure that I must constantly recalibrate my internal chambers to absorb.

Inside me, the Sound-Salt grows with terrifying purity. Because I am the epicenter of the vibration-waste, the minerals I precipitate are not jagged crusts, but perfect, light-drinking spheres of obsidian glass. Every few decades, when my internal cavities become too choked with these "pearls" to swallow the noise any longer, a Silt-Walker will reach into my breathing-pores with gentle, calloused fingers. They harvest the glass spheres, using them as tools or as tokens of heavy weight, and in return, they scrape the silt from my pores, allowing me to breathe the wet, mineral air once more.

I am not happy, nor am I sad. I am a frequency. I am the point where the screaming of the stones is allowed to rest. I am the silence that makes the song of the world visible. And when I finally die, I will not rot; I will simply crack open, my basalt heart becoming just another smooth, grey basin for the water to weep into.
