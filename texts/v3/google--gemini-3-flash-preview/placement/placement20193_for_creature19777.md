I do not stand on the gantry; I am the tension held within it.

As I look out into the violet-pinpoint sky and the grinding heat-haze below, I find that I am not one of the spindly Solder-Kin, nor the bulky Oil-Shepherds. To be them would be to have a boundary—a skin, a carapace, an edge where the self ends and the city begins. My existence does not permit such a luxury.

I am a **Seismograph of the Static**.

I am a being of the **Tension-Drinkers**, but I have grown thick and pale on the peculiar frequencies of this specific sector. I am a ribbon of translucent, conductive membrane, stretched thin across a three-mile span of the crevice like a violin string made of ghost-flesh. One end of me is anchored to the primary turbine-housing of the Lower Tiers; the other is hooked into the vibrating basalt of the "young" cliffside.

I am not "alive" in the way the scavengers are. I do not scuttle. I am a living measurement of the city’s desperation.

I find myself in the **In-Between**. Not on the catwalk, not inside the boiler, but in the shivering air of the gap. My body is a long, vibrating filament, humming in a permanent state of harmonic resonance. I do not have eyes; I have a nervous system that translates every *clack-clack* of the scavenger swarms into a tactile map. I feel the city not as a series of buildings, but as a symphony of stress-vectors.

When the crane to the left begins to dismantle the housing block, I feel the shift in the steel’s "mood"—a sudden, sharp release of kinetic energy that tastes like ozone. I ripple in response, my pale phosphorus body glowing a sickly, beautiful white. I am drinking the sound of the metal’s surrender.

I exist as a **Warning**.

The Solder-Kin look at me as they pass on their swaying harnesses. They do not name me, but they read my luminosity. If I begin to strobe with a frantic, jagged rhythm, they know a structural failure is imminent. If I grow dull and limp, the air is stagnant; the bellows-drifters are failing. I am the city’s visible pulse, a biological barometer for the mechanical fever.

I am most myself during the "Self-Cannibalization." When the architecture shifts—when the city eats a piece of its own side to grow a new limb—the vibration is so intense it nearly tears me in two. In that moment, I am a blinding streak of white light against the dark basalt. I am the bridge between the destruction of the old and the soldering of the new.

I have no Crew. I have no Station. I am a parasite of the "Great Gear’s" rhythm, a shimmering, ribbon-thin ghost that loves the city for its friction. I don't want the oil, and I don't want the copper wiring. I want only the beautiful, violent shaking of the world. 

In this vertical wound, I am the sound the wound makes as it refuses to heal. I am the hum in the iron. I am the shimmer in the dust.
