I am found in the **Interstices**, the cavernous, green-lit voids between the primary Mother-Trunks, where the current is weakest and the bioluminescence of the city is merely a constellation of distant, amber sparks. 

I am not a Pruner, nor a member of a matrilineal Vine. I have no tethered gourd to call my own. In a world defined by the "Slow," I am the embodiment of the **Static**.

### Who I Am: The Scribe of the Sediment-Skin
I am a **Lithified Elder**, a Marrow-Weaver who has reached the age of "Settling." In the biology of our people, there comes a moment when the heart, beating its four slow pulses a minute, begins to syncopate with the vibrations of the wood itself. My skin has moved beyond the texture of chamois; it has begun to calcify, encrusted with a thick, protective layer of mineral-rich lime and bryozoan colonies. 

To the younger Weavers, I look like a piece of the architecture. I am a living gargoyle, fused by my lower spine and legs to a massive, silver-blue root that overhangs a primary "street." My cilia have grown long and crystalline, fanning out like a crown of frozen lace. 

### My Function: The Resonate Memory
In a world without paper or metal, I am the library. 

I do not move my limbs—they are heavy with the weight of decades—but my "Vocal Hum" is the most sophisticated in the district. I am the **Anchor-Singer**. Because sound travels with such terrifying efficiency, I spend my "waking" decades listening to the groans of the Mother-Trunks. I can interpret the tension in the wood. I know, three months before the Pulse begins, exactly where the bark will split and which neighborhood-gourds are at risk of being crushed.

When the Pruners need to know the historical property lines of a clan-root from three Pulses ago, they swim to me. They do not talk; they place their long, multi-jointed fingers against my calcified chest. I transmit the data through vibrations—a tactile history of the city's growth, passed through the density of the brine.

### My Daily Existence: The Luxury of the Still
My life is a meditation on the vertical. I watch the **Silver-Nippers** dart through the plazas below me, their movements a frantic blur that makes my eyes ache. I am fed by the current; the slow, circular gyre of the Cradle-Swells carries nutrient-rich spores and microscopic brine-shrimp directly into my pulsing rib-slits. 

I am a creature of the **Great Hum**. While the city above clatters with the *clack-clack* of stone-on-shell, I listen to the deeper music: the low-frequency booming of the Whale-Slugs as they migrate across the void. I feel their passage as a pressure wave against my skin before I ever see their shadow.

### My Relationship with the "Heavy Bottom"
Unlike the younger Weavers, I do not fear the **Sinkers**. I am already half-stone. I am the bridge between the buoyancy of life and the inevitability of the Anchor. 

My primary fear is not falling, but **Silencing**. There is a parasite in the Swells—a soft, red sponge that grows over the sensory filaments of the elderly. It muffles the world. To a Scribe, to be muffled is to be erased. Every few weeks, a group of children from the local Vine is sent to me. As part of their education, they use small obsidian scrapers to gently clean the red sponge from my crystalline cilia. In exchange, I hum them the "Song of the Surface"—a shimmering, high-pitched resonance that mimics the light they will never see.

### My Place in the Architecture
I am located at the **Grafting-Point**, the highest habitable tether-point before the brine becomes too thin to support the Whale-Slugs. From my vantage point, the city looks like a chandelier of pulsing, organic lanterns. 

I am the one who watches the "invisible surface" miles above. Sometimes, when a rare storm disturbs the upper reaches of the forest, the brine carries down "The Bright Shatter"—shards of frozen light or perhaps pockets of atmospheric gas that haven't yet dissolved. I see them falling like diamonds towards the Heavy Bottom. 

I see everything that falls. And I remember the weight of it all, so that those who swim through the Swells can afford to feel light.
