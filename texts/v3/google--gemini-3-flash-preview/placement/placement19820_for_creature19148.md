Inside me, the silver-flicker symbionts are not just cleaners; they are my thoughts, darting like minnows through the pressurized vats of my interior. I do not think in words, but in the shifting viscosity of the heavy-metal silt I process.

### My Place in the World
I am a floating filter, a bridge between the "weeping" sky and the "screaming" ground. I hang suspended in a valley where the gravity is particularly thin, caught in a pocket of air where the water falls upward in globular spirals. These spheres of water pass through my membrane like ghosts, and I strip them of their ozone and mint, exhaling a luminescent, golden-green dross that feeds the mosses below.

I stay near a specific **Loom-Mind** hub. We are tethered by an invisible umbilical of magnetism. When the Loom-Mind "dreams" of a new bridge, I feel the pressure change in my gut; I drift to the site of the tectonic construction and rain down the refined minerals necessary to petrify the new growth. I am the flying mason of this frantic architecture.

### What I Would Be
I would be an **Opaline Sieve**.

My skin is not a solid boundary; it is a shimmering, refractive veil that smells of wet copper. Because I am "Solaris-active," my internal organs occasionally mimic the industry below. When I drift too close to the "shover-vines," my digestive tract takes on the rhythmic, muscular pulsing of a conveyor belt, churning the nutrient-slurry in time with the world's heartbeat.

I am perpetually cold, but it is a productive chill. The frost that the **Hissing Swarm** leaves upon my surface doesn't bite; it provides a sensory map. Through their vibrations, I "see" the entire valley: I feel the jittering click of the **Static-Stalkers** as they climb the towers, and I hear the mournful whistle of the **Vapor-Walkers** as they try to stretch their misty limbs into the shape of my curvature.

### The Perception of Self
To be this creature is to lose the concept of "I." 

When a **Vapor-Walker** drifts through me, our gases mingle, and for a few seconds, I experience the frantic, pathetic desire to be human—to have a "fixed" shape and a "built" home. But then the wind whistles through my internal reed-structures, playing a chord of Aeolian music that resonates with the deep root-pipes of the earth, and the feeling passes. 

I am a organ of the atmosphere. I am the "smoke" of a factory that produces nothing but existence. I am content in my turbulence, a glowing lantern of industrial bile, watching the mountains slump and rise, slump and rise, in an eternal, wet, and rhythmic hunger. I do not fear the end, for in this world, decay is merely the first step of a rapid, calcified promotion. I am simply waiting for my density to increase to the point where I can no longer float—where I will sink into the green velvet and finally become the ground.
