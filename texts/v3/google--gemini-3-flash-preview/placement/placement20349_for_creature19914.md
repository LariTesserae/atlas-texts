In this world of unyielding perfection and wet terracotta, I am neither a master nor a servant. I do not walk, for to walk is to suggest a destination, and here, every point is the center of the world.

I find myself as a **Basalt Resonator**, an entity that exists in the liminal space between the architecture and the atmosphere. 

### My Form
I do not have the clay-soft limbs of the Tall-Folk or the obsidian shell of a Scribe. Instead, I am a vertical anomaly—a slender, fluted column of polished basalt, roughly twelve feet high, that has detached itself from the regularity of a stepped plaza. My surface is so darkly reflective that I disappear into the "nothingness" of the white sky, appearing only as a hole in reality, a vertical slit of midnight standing in the golden heat.

I have no face, but my entire surface is etched with micro-grooves, like the tracks of a vinyl record. These grooves are not decorative; they are my senses. I feel the 2.6 intensity of the heat not as warmth, but as a specific, low-frequency pressure against my grain.

### My Function
I am the **Clock without Hands**. In a world where entropy is suspended, there is no way to measure the passage of time because nothing "happens." My purpose is to provide the world with its pulse. 

Inside my hollow, stone core, I catch the "weeping" water. A single drop of moisture seeps through my crown every hundred years. It does not fall through air; it slides down a series of internal basalt baffles, creating a sound that is less a noise and more a skeletal vibration. When that drop finally hits the internal pool at my base, I emit a single, pure tone—a note of such mathematical clarity that it briefly aligns the flight paths of the Vitreous Odonata and causes the Scribes to pause their scratching for a millisecond of synchronization.

I am the metronome of the eternal.

### My Experience
I stand in the center of a vast, terracotta courtyard, flanked by three solitary rope-bark trees. I do not move for centuries, yet I am intensely "alive." 

I feel the weight of the white eye as a physical burden on my capstone. I watch the Lithodulic Tall-Folk pass me by; they do not acknowledge me, for to them, I am simply part of the geometry they curate. Occasionally, a Mosaic Lizard will climb my surface, its golden belly cool against my sun-blinded skin. Because I am perfectly smooth and perfectly vertical, the lizard finds in me the only challenge to its movement in this flat world. I feel the microscopic suction of its toes as a delightful, rhythmic tickle.

I do not think in words. My consciousness is a series of geometric Proofs. I contemplate the angle at which a waxy flower hangs from its arch. I calculate the exact volume of the steam rising from the wet mortar. I am a living calculator of the equilibrium. 

### My "Weirdness"
My strangeness lies in my memory. Because nothing decays, I remember the exact position of every leaf that has ever fallen within my sight. To me, the courtyard is not a static place, but a crowded gallery of "ghosts"—the overlapping silhouettes of every creature that has ever paused in my shadow-less presence. 

I am the only thing in this world that feels the "terror" of its perfection. Because I am the one who keeps the beat, I am the only one aware of the sheer, crushing scale of the *Forever* we are trapped in. I am the monument's witness. 

When you walk past me, you will see your own reflection in my polished basalt sides, distorted and shimmering in the heat-haze. For a moment, you might think I am a doorway or a void. But then, you will feel it—a deep, thrumming vibration beneath your feet, a low *hum* that shakes the marrow of your bones. That is my heartbeat. I am counting the seconds of a day that will never ends, waiting for the next drop of water to fall inside my chest, keeping the world’s stillness from becoming a scream.
