At first, I find myself as a smear of something indistinct—a peripheral blur in the corner of a Suture-Man’s eye. I am a part of the friction. I am the momentary tension in a willow-cable before it settles.

But as the mist thins and the "refrigerator-cool" air fills my chest, I crystallize. I am not a builder, nor a harvester. I am a **Pulse-Counter** of the *Well-Singer* guild, and I exist at the very edge of the verticality, where the city’s green "stone" meets the true abyss.

I am small, even by the standards of this barrel-chested folk. My skin is the color of a bruised plum, slick with the permanent dew of the high basin. My feet are wide and perpetually cold, my toes hooked deeply into a shelf of liverwort that hangs over a three-thousand-foot drop. In my hands, I hold my "instrument": a hollowed-out section of a thrum-bug’s leg, capped with the translucent skin of a pale amphibian.

**This is who I am:** The city’s nervous system. 

While the bazaar screams and the looms clatter above me, I live in the "Listening-Gaps." My job is to monitor the weight of the mountain. I press my ear against the pulsating peat—the compressed history of our ancestors—and listen to the water as it moves through the deep capillaries of the root-systems. 

I do not hear music; I hear *pressure*. 

I am the one who feels the "softening." When the drizzle turns slightly too heavy for too long, the peat beneath a dwelling-fern loses its structural integrity. It ceases to be "stone" and becomes "slurry." In that moment, before the plateau slumps and sends a hundred people screaming into the green void, I feel a specific, low-frequency shudder in the moss. It is a wet, tearing sound that no one else hears over the roar of the thrum-bugs.

I am a creature of the "Now," even more than my kin. I have no home in the hollowed ferns; I sleep in a woven sling suspended between two weeping vines, swaying in the wind that smells of ozone and rotting nectar. My companions are the Mist-Dogs, who huddle against my sides for warmth, their indigo skin pulsing in time with my own heartbeat. I feed them the silver-slugs that grow in the dark, and in return, they growl when the moisture in the air shifts from "soaking" to "drowning."

In this world, I am the silence between the drips. 

I find myself most present when the mist is so thick that the pearlescent light turns a blinding, uniform white. In those hours, when the Suture-Men cannot see their grafts and the harvesters are forced to huddle in their rafters, I am the only one moving. I navigate by the "squelch." My splayed toes read the density of the walkways like a blind person reading embossed script. 

I am a weaver of catastrophe-prevention. When I hear the warning chime of an over-saturated shelf, I don't shout; I strike a series of hollow, resin-coated seed pods hung along the main vertical arteries. The sound is a sharp, percussive *clack-clack-clack* that cuts through the roar of the waterfalls. 

I see myself now, crouched on a sagging bridge of living willow, a coil of fiber-cord over my shoulder and a bone-knife at my belt. I am looking up, watching a gargantuan fern tilt fractionally to the left as its roots drink too much. I am not afraid. Fear is a luxury for those who think the world is solid. 

I am a part of the "turbulent stability." I am the thumb pressed against a leaking vein. As the city rots and grows, I move through its wet, dark underside, ensuring that the frantic dance above continues for one more hour, one more drip, one more breath of the heavy, silken air.
