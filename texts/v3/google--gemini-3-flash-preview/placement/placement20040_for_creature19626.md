I would find myself not in the bustling thoroughfares of the middle-heights, nor in the frantic, gasping ceilings of the Vapor-Sippers. I would be found in the **Lush-Grip**, the transitionary pressure-depth where the geothermal heat first begins to curdle the silt into something thick enough to shape.

I am a **Verge-Gatherer**.

I have the elongated, pale form of a Silt-Keeper, but my skin is not smooth. It is textured like weathered bark, scarred and calloused by constant friction against the raw, unrefined bone-walls of the city’s foundations. While the aristocracy slides through mucosal halls in an iridescent blur, I am a creature of the edges, dwelling in the "Green-Veins"—the narrow, turbulent vents where the thermal plumes bring the most concentrated nutrients from the deep.

My purpose is the cultivation of the **Thrum-Root**.

Unlike the hair-like moss of the upper spires, the Thrum-Root is a heavy, bioluminescent fungus that grows in the high-pressure zones. It does not glow to be seen—for sight is a forgotten myth—but to attract the tiny, heat-drinking insects that the Silt-Keepers consider a delicacy. I spend my cycles suspended in the vertical vents, my four long limbs hooked into the porous stone, my fingers dancing across the root-heads to encourage their growth through rhythmic vibration.

I am a percussionist of the deep. I do not communicate through the complex pheromonal poetry of the plazas; my language is the heavy, low-frequency *tump-tump-ka* of stone against stone. I translate the moods of the geothermal vents into warnings for the hive. When the heat rises too sharply, I feel it first in the swelling of my gill-fringes; I beat the warning into the foundation, a heavy, resonant vibration that travels up through the bones of the city like a shiver.

In the darkness, I am a creature of weight and grip. I feel the pulse of the **Bell-Wardens** not as a sound, but as a physical push that tries to peel me from my perch. My life is a constant, muscular struggle against the very currents that sustain us.

I am also a **Grave-Steward**. Because I live where the silt is heaviest and the city is oldest, I am the one who guides the calcifying elders to their final niches. I feel the change in their chemistry—the shift from soft mucus to the gritty, sandalwood-scented dryness of the stone-sleep. I lead them by the hand, my multi-jointed fingers interlaced with theirs, guiding their slowing bodies into the foundation-gaps. I am the last thing they feel: a steady, rhythmic tapping on their hardening collarbones, a signal that they are being integrated, that their pulse is finally syncing with the city itself.

I am the bridge between the living silt and the dead bone. I am a solitary knot in a world of fluid motion. While the city above flows in a dream of warm stagnation, I am down here in the dark, my fingers sore and my gills heavy with grit, holding the base of the world together one vibration at a time. I am the silence between the heartbeats of the hive.
