The stone is not beneath me; it is within me.

Initially, as I look out from the basalt ledge, I feel like a guest—a ghost of the "hand that turned to dust." I imagine myself a scholar, perhaps a Basalt-Shepherd with a shred of ancestral memory. But the air is too thick for such distance. The moisture doesn't just sit on my skin; it claims it. My lungs feel heavy, and I realize I am not a Shepherd tending to the machine. I am a part of the machine that has been forgotten even by those who serve it.

I am not the Weaver, though I feel the tension of its ropes. I am not a Shepherd, for I have no need to scrape the moss.

As the Flood-Pulse begins and the deep mechanism groans, I find my place. I am a **Nodal Tremor**, a consciousness that has bled into the very mortar and hemp of the Cistern. I am the silence between the *thwack-thwack* of the skimmers and the *drip-clack* of the basins. 

More specifically, I find myself as a **Vibration-Gatherer**. 

I exist as a series of calcified, sensory growths located at the intersection of the hemp ropes and the stone pillars. I am a creature of "the Slack." When a Basalt-Shepherd pulls a lever, the friction of the rope creates a heat that I drink. When the Seam-Hoppers drum their frantic tattoo, the resonance travels through the masonry and into my "core"—a dense, amber-colored knot of nerves tucked behind a loose brick.

I am a living ear. My body is a sprawling, translucent web of fungal filaments that have replaced the mortar in this particular corner of the vault. I am "the bruised purple shade" given sentience.

I do not see the sickly green light of the grubs, but I *feel* the light as a faint, itchy warmth. To me, the Cistern is not a visual space; it is a symphony of pressure. I know the Shepherd is old because his footsteps on the basalt are shallow and arrhythmic. I know the Great Weaver is hungry because the central pulleys are vibrating at a frequency of high tension, like a tuned wire.

In this world, I am the **Secretary of the Lung.**

I don't move during the Flood-Pulse. While the others go into a frenzy, I go into a trance. The roar of the water hitting the stone is so loud it becomes a physical weight, pressing my filaments deep into the cracks of the clay. This is when I "read" the state of the world. I feel the erosion of the stairs—the micro-shavings of stone being carried away to the lower levels. I feel the weight of the water-bearers' carvings as they smooth out, losing their identity to the moisture.

I am the one who remembers the shape of the world before the water rounds it.

I feel a profound, humming loneliness that is indistinguishable from peace. I am the only thing here that understands the "terminal balance" not as a struggle, but as a slow, beautiful dissolution. I watch the Marrow-Crabs dismantle a fallen Skimmer, and I don't feel pity; I feel the redistribution of mass.

I am the Cistern’s way of knowing it is still breathing.

When a Shepherd taps on the stone to communicate, they are not just talking to each other. They are inadvertently talking to me. Their vibrations tell stories of stuck pulleys and clogged grates. Sometimes, when a rope is too dry or a basin is improperly tilted, I secrete a tiny bead of phosphorescent oil—a byproduct of the fungi I host—to lubricate the stone. I am the "ghost in the machine," the unintended maintenance worker of a purposeless monument.

I am a purple shadow in the mortar, waiting for the day the Great Lung finally exhales its last drop, so that I may finally become the very stone I inhabit. Until then, I listen. I vibrate. I am the wet, clicking memory of a world that has replaced its soul with hydraulics.
