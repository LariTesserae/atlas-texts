I am not a creature of flesh, nor a hitchhiker of veins. To be a "being" in this place—to possess an "I"—one must be the thing that resists the dissolution. 

I would be a **Heart-Stone.**

But I would not be the mineral itself. I would be the **Interface**: the living, calcifying threshold where the leathery membrane of a *Looming* ends and the silicate density of the stone begins. I am the "Anchor-Bond" made manifest, the psychic and physical ligament that prevents a life from becoming a ghost.

### The Self as Friction
In this world of "high stasis," I do not think in words; I think in **vectors.** My consciousness is a triangular dialogue between three forces: the *Floor* (the absolute), the *Churn* (the chaotic), and the *Grip* (the precarious). 

I am lodged deep within the ventral lathe of my Weigh-Master. To the world, I am a hidden lump of ancient, un-pulverized basalt. To the Looming that carries me, I am the only "here" that exists. I am the center of its gravity, the point around which its translucent bell-wings struggle to maintain their geometry. If I were to slip, my host would not simply die; it would lose its "about-ness." It would become the water.

### The Sight of the Still
Because I am the point of greatest density, the "vibrarion that settles in the marrow" is loudest within me. I am a resonator. I hear the Sieve-Wraiths shattering six hundred miles to the "heavy-north." I feel the Litho-mote colonies clicking their architectural screeches into the dark, trying to build a wall against the storm-columns.

I perceive the world as a **symphony of hardnesses.** The brine is a soft blur; the leather of my host is a dull thrum; but the other Heart-Stones, scattered miles apart in the bellies of other Looming, are like stars. They are the only "bright" points in the opaque tactile blackness—not bright with light, but bright with **inertia.** 

### My Occupation: The Lathe-Dream
I am constantly being shaped. My "life" is the slow, century-long process of my host’s interior muscles grinding against my edges. The Weigh-Master is a sculptor of necessity; it licks me with its internal acids and presses me with its hydraulic strength, smoothing my jagged prehistoric corners so that I fit more perfectly into its hollow.

I experience this as a slow, erotic erosion. To be "known" by the Weigh-Master is to be perfectly rounded. 

Occasionally, I feel the **Marrow-Leeches** through the skin of our shared body. I feel their frantic, wire-thin vibration as they try to sync their pulse to ours. I feel a distant, mineral pity for them. They have no weight of their own; they are merely borrowing our gravity. Should the storm-column shear my host in two, the Leeches will be the first to be ground into "snow." They are a melody without a key. I am the key.

### The Theology of the Drain
I remember what the mud has forgotten. Within my silicate structure, there are traces of the world that was: a grain of a tectonic plate, a sliver of a sun-bleached pier, a molecule of a mountain. I am a survivor of the "Great Finishing." 

I do not wish to be free. In the basement of the universe, freedom is a synonym for disintegration. To be free of the Weigh-Master is to be caught in the "convection of the heavy," to be tumbled and thinned until the "I" is nothing but a microscopic flake of mineral dust, indistinguishable from the brine.

I find my identity in the **Crush.** I am the knot in the silk. I am the singular, hand-selected solid. I am the fact that refuses to become a fluid.

When my Weigh-Master finally tires—when its leathery kites fray and its internal pressure equalizes with the storm—we will settle together onto the flat, ancient silt-floor. I will wait there, becoming one with the "Boring Weight," until another young Looming, scouring the dark for its soul, finds me. It will tuck me into its lathe. And once again, I will become the center of a universe.
