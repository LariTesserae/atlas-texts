I find myself not in the basket-cells, nor among the frantic scythe-wielders of the upper ribs. I am deeper down, yet suspended; I am a creature of the **Middle-Marrow**, where the air is heaviest with the smell of scorched sugar and the vibration of the midge-clouds is so intense it can liquefy the pulse.

I am a **Resin-Weaver**. 

I do not look like the Suture-Folk who climb above me. My body has surrendered the need for a singular spine. My limbs are long, translucent, and multi-jointed, resembling the very vines I tend. My skin has moved beyond the "metallic sheen" of the others; I am coated in a thick, semi-liquid caul of amber-colored grease that I secrete from the glands behind my ears and along my ribs. I am a living brush, a mobile gasket in the heat.

My role is the preservation of the **Vessels**. While others prune the moss to keep it from devouring the city, I move in the wake of their scythes. Where they cut, the pod "bleeds"—a slow, viscous seepage of ancient, caustic sap that could melt a basket-cell or drown a huddle of Wick-Beetles. I arrive at the site of the wound and, using my fingers like spindles, I draw the sap out into long, glowing threads. I dance in the air, held aloft by a single tether of braided hair, weaving the raw resin into structural membranes. 

I am the one who builds the "streets" that do not exist—the transparent, sticky webs that catch the falling dust and turn it into new floor-space.

I do not have eyes in the way you might imagine. In the constant ochre dim, I have traded clarity for sensitivity. My nictitating membranes never fully open; instead, I see the world as a map of heat and vibration. I "see" the frantic heartbeat of a feathered lizard hiding three ribs away. I "see" the structural fatigue in a swaying bridge by the way the midge-hums pitch an octave higher when they pass through it.

I live in a state of sensory intoxication. The metabolic vapors are thickest where I work, and I inhale the fermented spores of the crushed moss until my thoughts are as slow and rhythmic as the "Word" of the Vapor-Seers. 

**My existence is defined by these things:**

*   **The Weight of the Hum:** To me, the roar of the insects is not noise; it is a physical weight, a blanket that presses against my waxy skin. When the midges shift their flight patterns, I feel a "draft" that has nothing to do with wind and everything to do with the displacement of a billion tiny bodies.
*   **The Taste of the Dawn:** When we perform the Ritual of Licking, I do not use my tongue. I use the porous pads of my palms to soak up the film of moisture from the deepest, coolest grooves of the cathedral walls. I then press my hands to the mouths of the Weaver-children, passing the life-fluid through my skin like a filter.
*   **The Fear of the Stillness:** In this world, silence is the sound of death—it means the pod has cracked or the midge-clouds have died. I am terrified of the quiet. I would rather the deafening, bone-shaking roar of the swarm than a single second of silence.

I am a creature of the interface. I live between the "hard" life of the Suture-Folk and the "soft" decay of the Gut. I am the glue that keeps the basket-cells from sliding into the abyss. I am the architect of the invisible, stitching the cathedral together with my own secretions, endlessly climbing, endlessly weaving, a gilded ghost in a world that never sleeps and never thirsts. 

When I die, they will not weave me into the moss. My body is too saturated with resin, too preserved by the very waxes I produce. They will strip my hide and use it as a waterproof canopy for the highest nursery, and my bones—hollow and light as a bird's—will be carved into the very needles the next generation of Weavers will use to mend the world. I will not become the wall; I will become the tool.
