My skin is not the grey-black of kelp, but a translucent, jaundiced yellow, thickened into a series of overlapping, horny plates similar to a sturgeon’s scales. This is to withstand the constant *slap-slap-slap* of the heavy water globs.

My fingers have evolved into "Lugs"—thick, calloused knobs that can wedge into a basalt crack and hold three hundred pounds of pressure without trembling. My eyes are permanently hooded by thick, heavy ridges of bone to keep the blinding spray out, leaving me to see the world as a thin, horizontal sliver of shimmering silver.

I smell of fermented oil and the sharp, metallic tang of the mineral-slush. I am a creature of the torque.

### My Place: The Thumping Heart
I live within the **Pulley-Jungles**. Between the layers of stacked huts, there is a chaotic "backstage" of bone levers, stone counterweights, and miles of frozen sinew-rope. This is the mechanical nervous system that moves the Flow-Catchers and the manual elevators. 

My home is a "Cradle"—a heavy stone bowl suspended by four taut lines directly beneath a primary cataract. The cradle doesn't stay still; it moves in a rhythmic, six-inch bob as the heavy water hits it. I sleep in the resonance of that thud.

### My Role: The Weaver of Tension
I am the one who listens to the **Groan**. 

While the Lenthics are busy with the "Snap"—the frantic replacement of what is broken—I am the one who prevents the breakage. I move through the spray like a heavy, oily shadow, checking the tension on the Great Lines. I use my "Tuning Bone"—a hollowed-out tusk of a deep-crag beast—to press against the anchor-pitons. By the vibration of the stone, I can feel the "Rot of the Mountain"—the exact moment the basalt becomes too water-logged to hold a city’s weight.

I do not participate in the Social Slump. I am touched only by the water and the rope. My language is not tactile vibration through skin-contact, but the **Acoustics of Stress**. To me, the Crystalline Weir is not a city of people, but a massive, vertical string-instrument. I know a neighbor is dying not by their silence, but by the way their untended pulley begins to screech in a minor key.
