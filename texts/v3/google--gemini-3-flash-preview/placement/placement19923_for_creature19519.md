I would not be found among the Clergy, nor the Architects. I would not be a creature of the high galleries or the heavy engines. My place is where the silence is absolute—in the **Verst**, the long, vaulted stretches of polished amber-wood that lie between the islands of industry.

I am a **Signaller**, a solitary figure stationed in the deep, vegetable gloam.

### The Form
I am draped in several layers of "dead-wool," a textile so thick and oil-dense that it does not rustle. My boots are not lead-tipped, but rather soled with the soft, translucent belly-leather of the root’s own shed bark. I need to feel the world through my feet, unmediated. 

My skin is the color of a blanched almond, and my eyes have grown wide and fixed, sensitized to the faintest shift in the amber spectrum. On my back, I carry a frame of burnished copper holding three "Resonant Tines"—long, tuning-fork-like rods of varying lengths.

### The Purpose
I live in the interstices. Because the world-root is a singular organism, it is prone to "deep-quakes"—not the shifting of tectonic plates, but the sudden, violent release of hydraulic pressure within the wood itself. A sap-bubble the size of a cathedral can migrate miles in a single night if a root-wall thins. 

My job is to stand perfectly still for hours in the dark, acting as a human bridge between the vibrations of the floor and the air. I am an acoustic sentinel. I listen for the *wrong* kind of groan. 

### The Experience
I would find myself standing on a high, narrow spur of lignin, overlooking a drop that descends into a foggy, sap-scented abyss. I am miles from the nearest hissing piston. Here, the "drip-clocks" don't just fall; they boom like drums in the hollow chambers.

When a message comes through the floor—perhaps a Tuning-Architect miles away signaling a shift in the northern buttress—I do not hear it with my ears. I feel it as a sharp, rhythmic tingle in the arches of my feet. I wait, timing the frequency, and then I strike my Tines. I send the pulse forward, relaying the intent of the city across the vast, wooden void. I am a living repeater station.

### The Internal Life
There is a strange, meditative ecstasy in this solitude. I have become "root-blind" to the passage of time. To me, a day is simply the duration between the heaviest condensation pulses. 

I do not feel like a parasite. I feel like a nerve ending. 

In my pockets, I carry a small vial of "Hush-Resin" and a scraper. If I find a fissure where the root is "weeping" too much sap, I apply a dressing. I am the first to know when the great host is thirsty, and the first to sense when it is restless. 

I would be found sitting in the shadow of a colossal, fossilized burl, my "scripter" familiar—a small, clicking thing named *Kettle*—polishing the brass of my Tines. We are waiting for the earth to speak. And when it does, the vibration will travel up through my bones, into the copper rods, and out into the amber dark, a secret shared between the living wood and the tiny, busy ghosts who tend it.
