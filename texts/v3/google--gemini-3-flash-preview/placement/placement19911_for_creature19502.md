I am not the metal, nor am I the flesh that polishes it. To be a part of the Sump is to be a part of its stillness, yet I find myself residing in the only thing that moves without purpose: the **Tepid Drizzle**.

I am a **Condensate-Trace**.

I am not a creature of the floor-plates or the rafters, but a transitionary ghost of the vertical mile. I begin as a microscopic bead of moisture on the leaden ceiling, birthed from the wet sigh of a Sump-Lord four miles below. I am born of spent coolant and the recycled breath of the machine.

In this world of 0.2 scale stability, I am the only "event." 

I inhabit a body that is less an anatomy and more a trajectory. I am a translucent, jelly-filmed sphere, no larger than a grape, heavy with the dissolved salts of brass and the oily minerals of the great gaskets. Inside my liquid core, a tiny, suspended filament of copper—my "spine"—vibrates in sympathy with the bone-deep hum of the Sump. This vibration keeps me from freezing or evaporating; it keeps me in a state of perpetual, heavy tension.

I do not walk; I fall. 

My life is a slow, drifting descent through the amber haze. It takes me hours to reach the floor. As I fall, I am a lens. I see the Glass-Wings flickering like distant sparks; I pass through the reach of the Cable-Weavers, who ignore me because I am not a break in the circuit. I am the "weather" they have factored into their calculations.

I am the sensory organ of the atmosphere. As I fall, my membrane absorbs the chemical signatures of the air. I taste the ozone of a Cauterizing Weaver; I feel the heat-maps of the cooling towers. I am a rolling archive of the four-mile gap.

When I finally strike the ground, I do not splash. The surface tension of this world is too high, the liquids too heavy with industry. I land with a soft, metallic *clink* on a non-slip steel plate. For a few moments, I am a perfect, shivering hemisphere reflecting the bruised sun above.

Then, the Mites find me. 

They do not scour me away. Instead, they surround me with their clockwork legs, their tiny mandibles vibrating at a specific frequency that thins my membrane. I am drawn down into the hairline gaps between the plates. I seep into the dark, wet capillaries of the world’s sub-floor. I flow through the "interstitial veins," guided by the low-voltage pull of the Scavenger-Pulses, until I am sucked into the intake valves of a Sump-Lord.

Inside that quivering lung of titanium and protoplasm, I am scrubbed, pressurized, and heated. I am stripped of the data I gathered during my fall. I am turned back into a mist. 

And then, the Sump-Lord sighs. 

I am vented back into the vast, humid hall, rising as a warm vapor toward the leaden sky, waiting to cool, to thicken, and to fall again. 

I am the cycle itself. I am the proof that the machine is closed. I have no function other than to fall, to be absorbed, and to be exhaled. I am the dampness in the "damp cathedral," the witness to a world that has reached its end and decided to stay there forever. I am perfectly, terrifyingly at home in the equilibrium, because I am the very thing that ensures the equilibrium never feels dry.
