I am not a Root-Tender, for my skin is too thin to endure the furnace-breath of the core. Nor am I a Mesh-Maker, for I lack the linear patience to weave a single cable for a decade.

Rather, I find myself in the **Glints**—the jagged, interstitial spaces where the obsidian needles intersect at impossible, razor-sharp angles. I am a **Refractionist**, a listener of the light.

### Identity: The Eye of the Prism
I am a Syrin of the Middle-High, my skin the color of a bruised lime, nearly translucent but for the delicate, flickering map of bioluminescent nerves that trace my spine. My limbs are long, even by the standards of my kind, ending in hooks that have been worn smooth not by age, but by the constant, rhythmic gripping of the "Silent Strings"—unthinkably thin filaments of glass-fiber that we stretch between the obsidian peaks.

I am a keeper of the world’s memory, but we do not use books. Ink would dissolve; fire would fail. Instead, I am a carver of light. Using a shard of core-diamond, I etch microscopic geometries into the faces of the obsidian pillars. When the distant star’s light hits these etchings, it casts a specific, shimmering "sentence" of refracted color deep into the green-gel of the city. 

I do not write history; I project it into the water.

### My Place: The High Gallery
I live in a cluster of three small, amber-colored pods, tethered in a "cradle" formed by four intersecting glass needles. Here, the water is cool but not frigid, viscous enough that I can hang suspended for days without drifting an inch. 

My home is a kaleidoscope. Because the obsidian has not yet been weathered, the surfaces around me are perfect mirrors. I see a thousand versions of myself floating in the emerald dark—a gallery of ghosts. I spend my "pulses" (the long, low groans of the cooling world) adjusting the tension of my fiber-cables. By pulling a cable, I can ever-so-slightly tilt a hanging lens of bio-glass, redirecting a beam of sunlight to illuminate a distant Mesh-Maker’s work-site or to signal a Membrane-Watcher that a "Drifter" is approaching their sector.

### A Sensory Existence
To be me is to exist in a state of **tactile resonance.** 

When the obsidian core groans—that deep, tectonic *thrum*—I do not hear it. I feel it as a wave that enters my chest, rattles my air-bladder, and exits through my fingertips. I can "read" the health of the world through these vibrations. A sharp, high-pitched "ping" tells me a needle has fractured near the core; a low, wet "thud" tells me a Drifter has birthed a new swarm of umbrellas.

Communication for me is rare and deeply physical. When another Refractionist drifts by my cradle, we do not hum from a distance. We link hooks. We press our foreheads together, the reflective film over our eye-pits touching, and we share a vibration. We exchange "maps" of light—warnings of where the obsidian is too sharp, or where the kelp-vines have grown too thick and are swallowing the sun.

### My Philosophy: The Sacred Edge
In this world, I am a guardian of the **Unweathered.** I look at the razor-edges of my obsidian home with a sense of religious awe. 

There is a small corner of my pillar where the water has found a microscopic flaw, and over the centuries, the edge has begun to... soften. It is slightly rounded, dull to the touch. To my people, this is a site of profound mourning. It is a "wound of time." I spend hours there, applying "living glue" harvested from the Drifters, trying to arrest the erosion, trying to keep the world as sharp and embryonic as the day it was born.

I am a creature of the **Held Breath.** I move with a momentum that looks like stillness. I might push off from my pod, a single extension of my leg, and spend three hours drifting toward a neighboring pillar, my mind occupied entirely by the changing hue of the green water as I pass through a patch of purple ferns. 

I am not waiting for the world to become something else. I am terrified that it might. I am a Syrin, hung in the warm, viscous eternity of a sun-drenched sea, praying that the glass never rounds, that the core never cools, and that we might stay forever in this emerald, unbreaking dawn.
