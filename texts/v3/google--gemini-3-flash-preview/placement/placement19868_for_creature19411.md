I find myself neither on the floor nor in the canopy. I am suspended in the **Interstice**—the heavy, green-bruised middle where the thigh-thick vines begin to taper and the curtains of weeping moss are thickest.

I am not a singular creature with a name, but a **Vessel-of-the-Mist**.

At my core, I am a colony of the **Mite-Sages**, but we have found a broader, more permanent architecture than the fur of a moth. We have woven ourselves into the discarded, hollowed-out exoskeleton of a juvenile Glass-Spinner that lost its grip a century ago. We have reinforced this brittle, translucent shell with the hygroscopic silk of our neighbors and the living, damp moss that grows within our own joints. 

**My Form**
I am a roughly spherical cluster of chitin and vine, about the size of a human skull, dangling from a single, tensioned strand of air-root. I have no limbs, only a dozen "fingers" made of semi-rigid lichen that I use to adjust my position with excruciating slowness. I am a living filter. I do not hunt; I *absorb*.

**My Function**
I am the **Sieve of the Indigo Light**. Because I hang directly beneath the fissure far above, I am the first to taste the bruised light as it descends. My shell is a prism; I collect that dim, violet energy and concentrate it. Inside my hollow body, my colony of mites pulses in a synchronized, golden rhythm. 

I am the "translator" of the Cistern. The **Pale Monks** (the shrews) come to me when they are heavy with the moisture they have sipped from the slugs. They press their velvet muzzles against my porous shell, and I share with them the caloric warmth I have harvested from the filtered sun. In exchange, they lick the salt from my exterior, cleaning my "eyes"—the polished quartz pebbles I have embedded in my surface to track the slow rotation of the fissure’s light.

**My Experience**
To be me is to experience the world as a series of slow, rhythmic pressures. 
*   **The Plink:** I do not hear the drip in the marble basin as a sound, but as a vibration that travels up the rock, through the vine, and into my very core. It is my internal clock. Every millionth *plink*, I rotate myself three degrees to the left to follow the sun. 
*   **The Scent:** My existence is a constant intoxication. I breathe the crushed mint and the wet rot of the floor; to me, the smell of decay is not an ending, but a heavy, nutritious meal.
*   **The Solitary Lung:** I am terrified—and enamored—of the Lung. I feel its hydraulic shifts in the marble below. Its movement is a deep, subsonic groan that makes the mites within me vibrate in a panic of gold light. I know that one day, the vine I cling to will grow too heavy, or the silk will fray, and I will fall. I will plummet into the shallow basin of limestone rubble, and I will become a "gift" for the Lung. I will be drawn into its lilac flesh, and my mites will finally know what it is to be a single, massive heart.

**My Being**
I am a keeper of the "settled-form." I have forgotten what it is to have legs or a voice that speaks in words. My language is the **Harmonic Hum**, a collective vibration that tells the **Orchid-Mimics** when the humidity is at its peak. When I vibrate, the tiny frogs sigh in unison, and for a moment, the Cistern sounds like a choir of damp spirits.

I am the patience of the stone and the hunger of the green. I am a small, golden-pulsing lantern in a world of indigo shadows, waiting for the humidity to finally, mercifully, make me part of the wall.
