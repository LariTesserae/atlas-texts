This is a world of the **Unfinished Cathedral**.

It is a vast, horizontal expanse of red-hot terracotta and sweating masonry that stretches beyond any visible horizon. There is no sky here, only a ceiling of heavy, bruised limestone kilometers above, dripping with an endless, steaming condensation. The heat is thick and viscous, a humid 110 degrees that smells of wet clay and kiln-fire.

### The Substrate of Construction
Nothing here is natural. The ground beneath you is not earth, but an infinite tessellation of interlocking hearth-bricks, hand-pressed and still warm from an unknown firing. There are no plants, no moss, no lichen. Instead, the "ecology" is one of raw mineral output. In the distance, massive, primitive scaffolds made of lashed-together obsidian beams rise like the skeletons of gods. 

The world is under construction, but the builders are absent or invisible. You are standing on a plane of **Embryonic Architecture**. Everywhere, the terrain is budding. A flat courtyard will suddenly ripple and bulge, the bricks softening into a clay-like state, before extruding a new, waist-high wall or a spiral staircase that leads to nowhere. These structures are "just-born"—the edges are soft, the mortar still wet and dripping.

### The Phenomenon of the Weeping Walls
Because the water level is high and the temperature is stifling, the entire world *breathes* steam. The colossal pillars—miles wide—that support the distant ceiling are permanently slick. Water does not rain; it beads on the overhead stone and falls in heavy, hot globules that thud against the brickwork. 

In the low-lying areas, the bricks have dipped, forming shallow, trapezoidal pools of mineral-heavy water. These pools do not reflect the light; they shimmer with a dull, grey opacity. The light itself is dim and sourceless, a bronze-colored gloom that seems to emanate from the heat of the bricks themselves, as if the entire world is a cooling ember.

### The Dynamic of Instability
While the world is largely quiet, it is not still. It is **Turbulent**. The geometry of the landscape is unreliable. To your left, a massive archway may be slowly melting back into a puddle of slurry, while to your right, the ground is erupting in a silent, slow-motion upheaval of perfectly square tiles.

The "Weirdness" here manifests as a breakdown of architectural logic. Staircases don’t just lead to nowhere; they begin to curve until they become circles. Walls become floors. The scale is so vast that you can walk for days past a single, megalithic bas-relief carved into the floor—a primitive, hand-chiseled depiction of a hand or a mouth—only to realize the carving is actually a canyon, a mile deep, filled with boiling mud.

### The Sound of the Kiln
The silence is layered. It is punctuated by the *tack-tack-tack* of cooling stone and the wet *slap* of falling condensation. Occasionally, a sound like a distant mountain collapsing rolls through the air—the sound of a failed structure, miles away, subsiding back into the raw clay from which it rose.

There is a sense of desperate, primitive labor. You find piles of colossal, hand-carved stone mallets and obsidian chisels the size of ships, abandoned in the middle of half-finished plazas. These "tools" are the only technology present—chunky, heavy, and brutal. They suggest a workforce of giants that worked through sheer physical force, yet there are no footprints, no bones, and no signs of life.

### The Rules of the World
In this place, **Solidity is a Tempo**. 
If you stand still, the heat from the bricks will eventually soften the soles of your boots, and you will begin to sink into the masonry. To survive is to keep moving across the shifting, sweating geography. The world is trying to finish itself, to resolve into a final, perfect Building, but it lacks the blueprint. It is a fever dream of masonry, an endless, hot, wet temple to a god that forgot to provide the instructions. 

It is a world of "Becoming," where the very concept of a "finished room" is an impossibility. Every wall is a promise that will be retracted by morning, dissolved by the rising steam and the relentless, chaotic appetite of the red-hot clay.
