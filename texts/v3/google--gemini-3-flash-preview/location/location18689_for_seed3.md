This is a world being born at the top of the sky, a vertical city-state anchored to rocks that have not yet decided where they belong. The elevation is so extreme that the atmosphere is thin and perpetually cool, flavored with the metallic tang of high-altitude ozone and the smell of wet soot.

### The Geology of the Unfinished
The ground here is an impossibility. At an elevation where one expects weathered peaks, there is instead a raw, embryonic geology. The cliffs are not worn down by time; they are still pushing upward, sharp-edged and unpolished, shimmering with a primal, glassy sheen. Because erosion is so low, every stone is a razor. There is no sand, no silt, and very little soil. Instead, the "terrain" consists of massive, floating platelets of obsidian and basalt that drift with agonizing slowness through a sea of thick, white mist. 

These landmasses do not collide; they hover, held apart by a weak but pervasive magnetic repulsion. To move from one neighborhood to another is to cross iron-grated bridges that hum with the tension of two continents trying to stay away from one another.

### The Damp and the Dim
Light is a rare commodity. The sun is a pale, frosted coin seen through layers of permanent cloud. The world exists in a state of violet-grey melancholy. Yet, it is not a desert. Moisture exists in a state of suspended condensation. It does not rain so much as the air *thickens*. Droplets form on the underside of industrial pipes and the broad, waxy leaves of the vegetation, falling in rhythmic, heavy plops into the abyss below.

The vegetation is lush but sparse—a paradoxical ecology. Great, singular ferns the size of houses grow from the vertical faces of the cliffs, their fronds heavy with silver moss. They don't form forests; they stand as lonely, emerald sentinels, spaced hundreds of yards apart, drinking the fog.

### The Industrial Anatomy
Woven into this raw, unfinished stone is a dense, urban sprawl of staggering scale. This is not a city of wood or brick, but a city of iron and steam. Massive, soot-streaked factories are bolted directly into the unweathered rock. Great brass pistons, three stories tall, churn with a slow, hypnotic cadence, pumping pressurized mist into the heart of the "districts." 

The sound is a constant, low-frequency roar—the heartbeat of a thousand furnaces and the grinding of massive gears that turn the bridges. Because the air is thin, sound travels strangely; a metal hammer striking an anvil three miles away sounds as if it is happening right behind your ear, while a shouting voice is swallowed instantly by the damp. 

The infrastructure is layered. Steel catwalks wrap around the peaks like the threads of a spiderweb, connecting industrial hubs that groan under their own weight. There is a sense of desperate, organized construction, as if the inhabitants are trying to finish building the world before the laws of physics change their minds.

### The Abundant Strange
The fauna has adapted to the spatial gaps. Massive, translucent sky-rays, their skins weeping condensation, drift between the industrial towers. They are as common as pigeons, grazing on the bioluminescent lichen that grows on the warm radiator pipes of the factories. Small, metallic-shelled insects crawl over the brass fittings, vibrating in harmony with the engines.

The "reality breakdown" is most visible in the way things fall. Dropped objects do not always accelerate; sometimes they drift sideways, or slow down as they descend, caught in pockets of gravitational inconsistency. In certain squares of the city, shadows do not align with the dim light, but instead point toward the nearest high-pressure boiler. The inhabitants pay these anomalies no mind, stepping over warped patches of space-time as if they were puddles of water.

### The Slow Pulse
Despite the industrial roar, the tempo of life is glacial. The great gears turn once an hour. The floating islands drift only a few inches a day. The people move with a deliberate, oxygen-saving slowness, their heavy coats damp from the mist, their faces smudged with coal dust. 

Everything here is under construction. The stones are new, the city is vast and metallic, and the air is a cool, wet blanket. It is a realm of "almost"—a place where the earth is still arriving, and the machines are working tirelessly to give the chaos a shape. It is a city of high-altitude ghosts, living in the guts of a god-sized engine that is slowly, very slowly, cooling.
