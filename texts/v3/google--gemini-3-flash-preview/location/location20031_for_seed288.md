This is the **Ossuary of the Low-Veldt**, a subterranean basin located leagues beneath the world’s crust, where the air is thick with the dust of pulverate minerals and the crushing weight of silence is broken only by the communal scream of its inhabitants.

### The Terrain and Atmosphere
The ground is not soil, but a fine, bone-dry silt—the result of billions of years of erosion. In this environment, water is an ancestral memory. The landscape is flattened, a vast tectonic bowl shaped by the slow, patient grinding of ancient strata. The temperature remains a constant, chilling cool, drawing the heat from your skin into the thirsty, non-aqueous dust.

The air is dim, illuminated only by a faint, ambient phosphorescence leaking from the ceiling of the cavern far above, casting long, bruised shadows across the pale grey floor. There is no weather here, only the slow, rhythmic settling of dust that has reached its final form.

### The Built World and Infrastructure
The landscape is far from empty. It is an "urban" environment, but one without architects or tools. Great, soaring spires of hardened cellular secretions rise from the silt, forming a city of organic origin. These structures are dense and organized into "neighborhoods," connected by high, arched bridges woven from a silk-like substance that has petrified over eons.

The "buildings" are multi-storied chambers of hardened resin, looking like a cross between a termite mound and a gothic cathedral. They are layered and vast, honeycombing the lower reaches of the basin. This is a city of pure biology: there are no wires, no lights, no machines—only the colossal, instinctive infrastructure of a species that has spent millions of years refining its hive.

### The Vegetation: The Dry-Lush
Despite the lack of water, the basin is lush. The "vegetation" is a forest of obsidian-hard succulent flora that feeds on gaseous minerals. High-reaching, fern-like structures with brittle, crystalline fronds crowd the spaces between the towers. These plants are thick and waxy, possessing a deep purple or charcoal hue. They do not sway, for there is no wind; they stand like frozen statues of a jungle, their leaves sharp enough to draw blood. They grow with agonizing slowness, adding perhaps a millimeter of height per century.

### The Sound and Fauna
The most striking feature of the Ossuary is its volume. The basin is home to billions of "Grit-Strider" insects—creatures the size of hounds with exoskeletons like polished flint. They cling to the urban spires and the crystalline trees in staggering numbers.

They do not sing; they vibrate. To communicate across the vast, open spaces of the cavern, they rub their serrated limbs against the resonant chambers of their resinous towers. The result is a deafening, continuous roar—a mechanical, grinding drone that vibrates the marrow of your bones. It is a sonic landscape so dense it feels physical, a wall of white noise that never ceases.

### The Ecology and Rules
In this world, life is sustained by a slow chemical exchange. The fauna exhale a heavy, nutrient-rich gas that the dry plants absorb; in turn, the plants shed microscopic, protein-heavy husks that carpet the floor like manna, providing a dry "tide" for the fauna to scavenge. 

There is no death as we know it—only a slow transition into the mineral state. When a creature dies or a plant shatters, it is not decomposed by bacteria (which require moisture) but is slowly worn down by the abrasive vibrations of the soundscape, turning into the very silt that forms the floor.

### The Dynamic
Movement here is a liturgical crawl. Because there is no water to lubricate joints or speed up chemical reactions, the inhabitants move with a staccato, methodical grace. The "city" expands at the rate of falling dust. The erosion is so complete that the mountains that once defined this space have been ground into a perfectly level plain. It is a world that has reached its end-state: a stable, deafening, dry paradise where nothing new ever happens, and the ancient sequences of life simply repeat, over and over, in the cool, echoing dark.
