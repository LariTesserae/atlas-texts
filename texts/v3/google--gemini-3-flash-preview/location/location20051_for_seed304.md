The ground here is not earth, but a tectonic collision of slate and grey ceramic, forced upward into a dense, vertical maze. This is the **Silt-Sovereignty**, a settlement the size of a single cathedral, wedged into a low-lying cleft where the world’s perspiration gathers. 

### The Terrain and Architecture
The landscape is "young," defined by sharp, lethal edges and fresh fractures. There is no soft soil. Instead, the floor is a series of stacked, interlocking plates of dark, non-porous stone. Because the elevation is low, water does not flow through this place so much as it dwells here. It seeps from the "bloom-walls"—vertical faces of rock that are constantly weeping. 

The "urban" element is not a city of streets, but a city of saturation. Every square inch of the cleft is occupied by hand-hewn dwellings made of the same splintered slate. These are "primitive" masterpieces: gravity-fed huts stacked like organ pipes, lashed together with braided river-grass. There are no gaps between the buildings. To move through the Silt-Sovereignty is to crawl through a continuous, interconnected interior of damp stone and reed-matting. The density is claustrophobic; the architecture has colonized every crack and shelf, turning the geography into a single, multi-storied machine for living.

### The Atmosphere and Light
The air is heavy, cooling the skin with a constant, fine mist. The light is a steady, neutral grey—diffused by the high canyon walls above and the thick humidity within. It is the light of a perpetual late afternoon after a storm. Surface tensions are high here; everything is filmed in a shimmering coat of moisture that never quite gathers into droplets, making the dark stone look like polished obsidian.

### Vegetation and Ecology
Vegetation is a luxury of the heights, largely absent here. There are no trees, only a specialized, rubbery liverwort that clings to the undersides of the stone dwellings, and thick, ropey strands of pale "drip-vine" that hang from the rafters to soak up the humidity. These vines are the primary resource—harvested by hand, dried in rare smoke-fires, and woven into the clothing and structural ties of the inhabitants.

Fauna is specialized and rare. Occasionally, a *glass-skink*—as translucent as the water it inhabits—will ripple across a damp vertical face, or a heavy, wingless moth will vibrate its thorax against a hollow reed to signal for a mate. 

### The Sound and Dynamics
The world is quiet, but it is not still. The primary sound is the **percussion of the seep**: the rhythmic *tink-tink-tink* of water hitting slate at a thousand different intervals. It creates a tonal white noise that drowns out human speech at a distance of more than ten paces.

The environment is incredibly active. This is a landscape of "active deposition." Because the rock is young and the water is Mineral-heavy, the very act of the water weeping builds the city. The "primitive" tech of the inhabitants involves placing ceramic frames against the weeping rocks; over weeks, the lime and silt in the water "glues" the frames to the wall, hardening into new rooms. The city is literally growing, calcifying, and shifting its weight as the water dictates.

### The Rules of the World
In the Silt-Sovereignty, the most valuable tool is the chisel, and the most feared event is the "Dry-Lung"—a rare period of low humidity that causes the reed bindings to shrink and snap, threatening to let the stacked city slide into the dark pools at the bottom of the cleft.

The inhabitants live by a rule of **uninterrupted contact**. Because there is no room "between," privacy is a concept unknown to them. They move as a single, slow-motion organism through the damp, dark corridors of their stone hive. They do not build upward to reach the sun; they build inward, toward the wettest, coldest heart of the rock, where the architecture is most stable and the song of the weeping walls is loudest.
