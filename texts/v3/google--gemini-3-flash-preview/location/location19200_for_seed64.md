Inside the hollowed marrow of a vertical mineral spire, tucked away from the greater world, exists the **Oven of Green Frost**. It is a pocket-dimension no larger than a cathedral, suspended at an altitude where the air is thin and habitually freezing, though within these walls, the temperature remains at a constant, biting chill that never tips into ice.

There is no water here. The "flora" that chokes every square inch of the space does not drink; it harvests. The vegetation is a dense, hyper-oxygenated thicket of velvet-ferns and fibrous, blue-black mosses that grow not from soil, but from the industrial friction of the world. These plants are carbon-based but bone-dry, feeling like stiff parchment or cured leather to the touch. They possess a lushness that is optical rather than biological—a riot of emerald, indigo, and pale silver fronds that overlap in a claustrophobic, vertical jungle.

Because the scale is so intimate—a "hidden" micro-environment—the landscape feels less like a forest and more like a saturated closet of living tapestries. There is no room to walk. To move is to push through resistant, dry leaves that snap like potato chips and release puffs of fine, aromatic dust.

Gravity here is a suggestion; the vegetation grows in spheres, clinging to the walls and the scattered, floating relics of a dead industry. Half-buried in the verdant crush are the "built" elements: brass-heavy cogs the size of wagon wheels, copper venting pipes that hiss with dry heat, and porcelain insulators that hum with a trapped, ancient current. These are not ruins in the sense of decay, but artifacts in "frozen equilibrium." They do not rust because there is no moisture. They do not fail because time has no grip here.

The sound is a constant, overwhelming roar—the "loudness" of a factory that has been running for a thousand years without a single technician to oil the gears. It is a mechanical drone, a rhythmic *thrum-clack-hiss* that vibrates in the marrow of your teeth. The plants have adapted to this; their leaves are shaped like acoustic baffles, vibrating in resonance with the machinery, turning the air into a shimmering haze of kinetic energy.

The light is the dim, sickly green of a glow-stick reaching the end of its life. It emanates from bioluminescent spores stuck to the industrial vents, casting long, sharp shadows through the packed foliage. There are no animals; nothing breathes air or eats fiber. The only "life" is the unceasing, geometric expansion of the dry-moss, which grows only to fill the gaps left by the machines, and then stops.

Everything is balanced. The erosion has reached its limit; the sharp edges of the brass gears have been buffed smooth by centuries of abrasive dust, and now they sit in a perfect, frictionless spin. The vegetation has filled every cubic centimeter of available volume, creating a solid wall of dry, rustling green. It is a static masterpiece of mechanical noise and botanical density—a secret engine room at the top of the world, grinding away in the dark, forever cool, forever dry, and forever loud.
