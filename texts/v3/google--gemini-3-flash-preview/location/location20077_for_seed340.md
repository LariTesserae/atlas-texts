This is the **Lignin-Gorge of the Great Hum**, a world-cell located miles beneath a crust of dry, insulating silicates. There is no water here. The "liquidity" of this ecosystem is found in the flow of fine, warm pollen and the friction of dry, cellulose plates.

### The Substrate: High-Mass Botany
The terrain is not rock, but a dense, compressed mattress of pressurized wood and petrified amber. Immense, horizontal trunks the size of cathedrals span the hollows, forming a structural lattice that replaces the concept of "ground." To move is to climb or crawl across the ribbed bark of a thousand-year-old organism that has never known rain. The vegetation is total; even the dust in the air is fine, desiccated moss-spores. Giant, fan-like structures of rigid cork bloom from the walls, vibrating with internal heat. Everything is warm to the touch—a dry, metabolic fever-heat that radiates from the pith of the mega-flora.

### The Atmosphere and Scale
Because it is subterranean, there is no horizon, only the "Long Rise" and the "Deep Sink." The light does not come from a sun but from a bioluminescent haze—a steady, golden glow emitted by drifting filaments of airborne fungi. It is bright enough to see for miles through the labyrinthine scaffolds of branch and vine, but the air is thick with a shimmering, suspended silt of biological matter. It feels like standing inside a gargantuan, golden-lit lung.

### The Turbulence and Sound
The world is deafening. It is not a roar of wind, but a roar of *life*. Millions of cicada-like entities with wings of thin mica scrape against the wooden vaults. The sound is a physical pressure, a constant, high-frequency thrum that makes the teeth ache. 

This is a state of hyper-dynamic instability. The landscape is "emerging" in a violent, embryonic sense. You can hear the wood cracking as it grows inches per hour; you can see the buckling of massive root-systems as they fight for space. It is a slow-motion riot. Vines thick as ship-mast cables whip across the voids, seeking purchase. Structures of organic matter are being woven and un-woven by the minute. It is a "bazaar of biology" where nothing stays still; a path you take into a grove will have been strangled shut by new growth before you can return.

### The Fauna: Overwhelming Presence
The fauna is not separate from the landscape. It is a swarm. There are segmented, multi-legged grazers with bodies like polished mahogany that skitter across the ceilings. There are clouds of "drifters"—buoyant, bladder-like creatures that use the heat-thermals to rise, trailing long, sticky sensory threads. Every inch of the wood is covered in something that moves: iridescent beetles the size of shields, velvet-soft moth-analogs with thirty-foot wingspans, and burrowing worms that "eat" the dry wood and excrete a hard, structural resin. The biomass of the animals nearly equals that of the plants; it is a claustrophobic churn of shells, fur, chitin, and wings.

### The Scars of Intelligence
Among this chaotic, green-gold riot, there are signs of "The Weavers." These are scattered, primitive dwellings—hollowed-out galls in the great trees, reinforced with hand-woven fibers and mud-daubed walls. They are not cities, but solitary outposts or temporary nests. 

The technology is strictly manual and bio-extractive. You see primitive scaffolds made of lashed saplings and bone-white daggers carved from the mandibles of the Great Beetles. There are "sound-traps" made of taut gut hung between branches, designed to catch the vibrations of the forest and turn them into a localized, rhythmic thud—perhaps a way of communicating over the deafening ambient noise. There is no metal, only the ingenious use of resin, scale, and fiber.

### The Rules of the World
1.  **Desiccation is Life:** In this world, moisture is a poison or a myth. The organisms thrive on the friction of dry matter and the exchange of dusty nutrients.
2.  **Growth is Velocity:** Static architecture is impossible. To build something is to engage in a constant battle against the "encroachment." If you stop carving back the moss, your home will be absorbed by the floor in a day.
3.  **The Hum is God:** The sound is the heartbeat of the ecosystem. Silence is the only true omen of death or environmental collapse.
4.  **No Gravity, Only Grip:** Because the vegetation is so dense and the scale so vertical, "falling" is less common than "tumbling" through layers of canopy. Survival depends on the strength of one’s grip and the toughness of one's skin against the abrasive bark.

This is a world that is still becoming. It is a hot, dry, screaming greenhouse where the geological time of rocks has been replaced by the frantic, thrumming time of the cell.
