The world is not a landscape, but a hollow. It is a **Verant Sink**, a massive, bowl-shaped depression in a sub-arctic tundra where the air sits heavy, frozen, and blindingly bright. Because the sink is low-lying—a geological thumbprint in the earth—it acts as a trap for light and life, isolated from the wind-scoured plains above. 

### The Light and the Cold
The atmosphere is a paradox. It is biting cold, a steady, unchanging chill that never thaws, yet it is flooded with a surgical, shadowless brightness. The sky above the sink is a permanent, vaulted white, refracting light through suspended ice crystals. This light is so intense it feels tactile, pressing against the skin, yet it carries no heat. It is a "cold noon" that never ends; there is no dusk here, no shifting shadows to mark the passage of hours. Time has been traded for a crystalline equilibrium.

### The Urban Thicket
Within this pocket, the density of existence is absolute. There is no "wild" space because the architecture and the ecology have braided themselves into a single, suffocating fabric. 

The "city" is not built of stone or steel, but of **Living Bone-Wood**. Huge, slow-growing arboreal structures, weathered and smoothed by centuries of frost-erosion into aerodynamic, bulbous shapes, form the foundation. These trees do not have leaves; they have translucent, glass-like needles that capture the blinding light. 

The settlement is packed. Houses are organic pods carved directly into the trunk-walls or woven from stiff, frozen reeds. They are stacked vertically, connected by ladders of knotted fiber and bridges of packed peat. There are no streets, only narrow, winding capillaries between dwellings where the walls are always within arm's reach. Every inch of horizontal surface is claimed—by a moss, by a tool-rack, by a resting animal, or by a cistern.

### The Damp and the Deep
Water does not flow here; it clings. The air is damp with a perpetual, frozen humidity. A fine silver rime coats every surface, a condensation that never quite becomes ice and never quite evaporates. It gathers in small, stagnant basins carved into the crooks of the bone-wood trees—micro-reservoirs that serve as the city’s wells. The moisture smells of crushed mint and wet mineral. Because the temperature never fluctuates, the water is always at the brink of freezing, viscous and heavy.

### The Quiet Labor
The technology is a matter of hands and patience. The inhabitants work with bone, flint, and hardened resins. Because the environment is so stable and eroded, there is no need for "progress" or "expansion." They carve intricate, interlocking joints into wood using basalt scrapers; they weave insulation from the thick, white fur of the local fauna. 

Everything is silent. The "urban" bustle is a pantomime. Footfalls are muffled by inches of dense, damp moss. The inhabitants communicate through subtle hand-signals or by tapping rhythmic vibrations into the wood of the trees, which carry sound far better than the heavy, frozen air. No one shouts. Even the birds—flat-bodied, white-feathered creatures that cling to the bark like moths—glide without a flap of wings.

### The Ecology of the Packed Room
The fauna is abundant but slow. Small, multi-legged mammals with oversized, pale eyes move through the canopy in a state of perpetual hibernation, grazing on the bioluminescent fungi that grow in the dark undersides of the dwellings. Everything is soft-edged. There are no predators here, only an endless cycle of slow consumption and growth. 

The vegetation is "lush" in a desperate, crowded sense. It is a vertical jungle of pale greens, ghostly whites, and deep, bruised purples. Vines as thick as a human torso coil around the buildings, providing structural support rather than strangling them. They are all part of the same ancient, weathered system.

### The Law of Equilibrium
In the Verant Sink, the rule is **Stasis**. Because the light never fades and the cold never breaks, the inhabitants do not perceive change. Erosion has already done its work; the jagged edges of the world were smoothed away millennia ago. The city is "finished." There is no construction of new buildings, only the meticulous repair of the old ones using the same primitive, reliable methods. 

It is a claustrophobic paradise. A bright, frozen, silent room where every breath is visible in the damp air and every life is lived in the immediate presence of a thousand others, held in a perfect, unchanging grip of ice and light.
