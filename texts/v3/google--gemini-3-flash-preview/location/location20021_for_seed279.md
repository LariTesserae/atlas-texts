The world is a cold, echoing nursery of stone and glass. It is a place where the concept of "up" is contested by the weight of the air, and where the transition from liquid to solid is a constant, agonizing negotiation.

**The Terrain and Mineralogy**
The landscape is composed entirely of unweathered tectonic upward-thrusts—vast, interlocking sheets of obsidian and blue-grey slate. These are "young" rocks, birthed by some recent geological seizure that left the edges razor-thin and the surfaces polished to a mirror-finish. This is a low land, a basin of jagged depressions and shallow canyons where the horizon is never distant, but always crowded.

Because the erosion is minimal, the world is a maze of verticality. Shards of stone the size of cathedrals lean against one another, forming narrow, lightless alleyways and deep, basin-like plazas. There is no soil. There is no sand. Only the primary minerals: slick stone and the glassy debris of their collision.

**The Hydrology and Atmosphere**
Water does not flow here so much as it occupies. It is an ambient presence. The atmosphere is saturated to the point of collapse; a constant, heavy mist clings to every vertical surface. The temperature is a steady, biting cool that never reaches a true freeze, maintaining the water in a state of perpetual weeping.

Rivulets travel down the obsidian faces in silent, rhythmic pulses, feeding into shallow pools that sit in the troughs of the stone. These pools are not still; they are hit by a constant, slow-motion condensation—fat, heavy droplets forming out of the gray air and falling with a rhythmic *tink-tink-tink* against the mineral floors. The air feels thick, less like gas and more like a thin, cold broth.

**The Absence of Green**
There is no chlorophyll here. The world is monochromatic—shades of charcoal, navy, and translucent white. In place of plants, the landscape is densely populated by "The Accumulations." These are non-biological structures, perhaps chemical or crystalline, that grow with agonizing slowness. They look like frozen lightning or plumes of spun glass, clutching the edges of the stone sheets. They are brittle and dense, clumping together in the crevices until the canyons feel choked with translucent, jagged webbing. They do not breathe or photosynthesize; they simply aggregate mineral salts from the weeping water, thickening by millimeters every century.

**The Sound and Density**
Despite the slow pace of change, the world is incredibly loud. The acoustics of the obsidian canyons are perfect and unforgiving. The sound of a single drop of water falling into a pool three miles away is captured, amplified, and bounced through the stone labyrinth. The result is a constant, overwhelming roar of "white noise"—a cacophony of dripping, splashing, and the deep, sub-sonic groaning of the massive stone plates settling under their own weight. It is a textured wall of sound that makes thought difficult.

The density is physical. Because the stone sheets lean and overlap so frequently, there is very little "open sky." You move through a forest of mineral slabs, where the next wall is always within arm’s reach, yet the scale of the valley suggests this crowded geometry extends for hundreds of leagues.

**The Built and The Strange**
Scattered throughout this mineral wilderness are the "Sentinels." They are the only evidence of a "built" world, though they lack any discernible technology. They are tall, slender pillars of a material even darker than the obsidian, carved with impossible precision into interlocking geometric rings. They are not machines; they have no moving parts, no lights, no wiring. They sit in the centers of the water-pools, acting as focal points for the condensation.

The strange rhythm of the place is governed by the "Slow-Pulse." Every several hours, the dim light shifts almost imperceptibly from a bruised violet to a pale, milky grey. During this shift, the weeping rocks seem to draw the moisture back into themselves, the roar of the water quietens for a brief moment, and the rare fauna emerges.

**The Fauna**
Life here is rare and alien. It consists of "The Percussives"—flat, hard-shelled organisms that resemble trilobites forged from hematite. They do not eat; they seem to vibrate. They cling to the obsidian walls, moving at a pace that is nearly invisible to the eye. They adjust their positions to catch the loudest vibrations of the water, as if the sound itself provides the kinetic energy required to sustain their mineral metabolisms. They are silent, slow, and occasional, appearing like dark, living rivets against the weeping stone.

**The Rules of the World**
This is a realm of patient accumulation. Entropy has not yet begun its work; the edges are still sharp, the water has not yet rounded the stones, and the builders of the pillars left before the first layer of sediment could settle. To be here is to be caught in the "long now"—a cold, wet, vibrant moment of early creation that refuses to move toward a future. You are a soft thing in a world of hard edges, a quiet thing in a world of echoing water, moving through a monumental architecture of glass and grief.
