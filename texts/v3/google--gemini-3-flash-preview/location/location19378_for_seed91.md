This is the heart of the Hohl-Wood Trench, a cavernous rift miles beneath the permafrost, where the biology of the surface has been driven deep and compressed into a singular, agonizingly slow machine.

**The Substrate**
The world is made of wood, but it is wood that mimics the behavior of stone. The floor is not soil; it is a tectonic plate of interlocking taproots, frozen solid by a subterranean chill that hovers just above the point of crystallization. The vegetation here is absolute. It is the architecture, the atmosphere, and the ground. Great, iron-hard trunks—some a hundred feet in diameter—grow horizontally across the void, forming jagged, "young" ridges that have only recently splintered off from the master root. Because the erosion is low, these wooden cliffs are razor-sharp; the bark is like obsidian, peeling in stiff, frozen flakes that could slice through leather.

**The Light and the Damp**
It is perpetually dim, lit only by the faint, cold phosphorescence of a grey lichen that clings to the underside of the Root-Arches. There is no sun, only the memory of it stored in the chemical sugars of the trees. The air is damp, but not humid. The moisture exists as a fine, stinging condensation that settles on every surface, freezing into rime-ice. Occasionally, a "drip-pulse" occurs—a heavy, rhythmic release of excess sap from the ceiling-canopy that falls like cold, viscous rain into the dark basins below.

**The Sound and the Industry**
The silence one might expect from a forest is nonexistent. This place is deafening. The sound is a mechanical, low-frequency roar—the collective groaning of billions of tons of wood expanding against the rock of the earth’s crust. It is a grinding, metallic shriek that never stops, vibrating the very marrow of the bones. 

Amidst this natural cacophony sits the Industry. Because the wood is so dense and the cold so pervasive, an ancient, scattered industrial network has been grafted directly into the trees. These are not buildings, but "Pumps"—massive, soot-stained cast-iron engines bolted into the living bark. They vibrate in rhythm with the trees’ groans. Their Purpose is extraction: they draw the thick, antifreeze-like sap from the deep roots to fuel muffled, glowing furnaces. You might walk for a mile through a sparse, cathedral-like grove of frozen ferns only to encounter a single, towering piston, three stories tall, chugging with a slow, indifferent violence.

**The Ecology and Scale**
The space is cavernous and open, stretching for leagues in a regional sprawl of wooden canyons. Despite the total presence of plant life, the space feels sparse. There are no thickets; the cold prevents small, brushy growth. Instead, there are massive, singular entities separated by wide, echoing voids of frozen air. 

Fauna is nearly non-existent. Occasionally, one might see a "Frost-Weevil"—a slow, armored scavenger the size of a sled, its legs clicking rhythmically on the bark as it scrapes for frozen resin. These creatures move with the same agonizing slowness as the landscape itself.

**The Rules of the World**
Time here is measured by the "Camber." Nothing happens quickly. A single leaf takes three years to unfurl; a gear in the industrial pumps might take an hour to complete a single rotation. The danger here is not sudden; it is the danger of the slow crush. If you stand still for too long, the condensation will frost you to the floor. If you fall, the sound will mask your cries. 

It is a world of industrial forestry without foresters. The machines were built to tend the trees, and the trees have grown to accommodate the machines. They have achieved a grim, freezing equilibrium. There is no decay, for the temperature is too low for rot; there is only the slow, loud, and metallic expansion of a garden that has become a factory.
