This is a place of heat and dust, a shallow, sheltered basin forgotten by the wind. It is a world of **dry mineral** and **perpetual dusk**, existing in a state of warm, breathless stasis.

### The Terrain and Light
The floor of the basin is composed of pulverized shale and fine, ochre-colored silt. It is flat, save for the gentle sloping of the perimeter walls—weathered, iron-rich ridges that rise only thirty or forty feet, containing the space like a small, lidless room. The elevation is low, a geographic depression where the air pools and thickens.

Light here is an oily, vestigial thing. There is no sun, only a dim, overhead luminescence that suggests a sky obscured by miles of static haze. It is a deep, warm darkness, the color of soot and rusted copper. Visibility is limited to the immediate vicinity; the edges of the basin blur into a charcoal-grey void, giving the landscape the intimate, claustrophobic scale of a private courtyard.

### The Vegetation: The Lithophytes
In this waterless void, life has abandoned carbon and moisture for something more patient. The vegetation is sparse, appearing as scattered, crystalline clusters that grow in the dark. These are "glass-ferns"—translucent, brittle structures that pull minerals directly from the dry silt. They do not sway; they do not breathe. They stand in frozen groupings, their fronds sharp and motionless, catching what little light exists and refracting it into dull, amber points. 

Between these clusters, the ground is bare. The density of the world is perfectly balanced with emptiness; every stone and glass-fern has its designated territory, separated by yards of undisturbed dust.

### The State of Erosion
Everything here has already happened. The rocks are rounded, not by water, but by eons of microscopic thermal expansion and contraction. The landscape is "established"—it has reached its final form. There are no landslides, no shifting sands, no crumbling cliffs. The basin has found its equilibrium. Even the dust has settled into a crust so thin and fragile it appears like a skin, unbroken by any footfall.

### Atmosphere and Sound
The air is warm—a dry, radiant heat that feels as though it emanates from the ground itself. It is a heat without weather, devoid of convection or breeze. 

The silence is absolute. Without water to drip, wind to howl, or fauna to cry out, the atmosphere is acoustically dead. If a stone were to fall, the sound would be sharp and lonely, immediately swallowed by the heavy air. But stones do not fall here. The world is time-invariant; it is a snapshot of a moment that has lasted for ten thousand years.

### The Presence of Tech
There are no buildings, no ruins, and no inhabitants. However, there is a ghost of a presence: the "primitive." 

Propped against a weathered boulder or laid flat in the silt are objects that are not quite natural. They are shards of flint and obsidian, chipped with intentionality. They are wedges, scrapers, and heavy hand-axes, fashioned by hands that vanished before the air turned still. They are not "constructed" in any architectural sense; they are simply tools left behind. These artifacts are as weathered as the stones around them, integrated into the landscape. They do not suggest a civilization, but a brief, ancient moment of manual craft that ended before it could ever build a wall.

### The Rules of the World
In this basin, the primary law is **stability**. 
1. **No Fluidity:** There is no water, no sap, no blood. All chemistry is solid-state, occurring through a slow migration of ions across mineral lattices.
2. **Thermal Equilibrium:** The temperature never spikes or dips. The warmth is a constant property of the matter here.
3. **The Absence of Entropy:** Because there is no motion and no biological decay, nothing breaks down. The primitive tools and the glass-ferns exist in a state of "frozen-on-purpose."

It is a small, dark cathedral of silicate and silt—a place where the clock has stopped, and the heat remains.
