This is the interior of the **Great Chlorophyll Void**, a sub-planar cavern of such impossible magnitude that the curvature of its floor is indistinguishable from a flat horizon. It exists deep beneath the terrestrial crust of a dying world, yet it is far brighter than the surface ever was.

### The Architecture of the Green
The environment is not composed of stone, but of a singular, omnivorous cellular mass. Vegetation here is at a 2.7—it is the substrate, the air-purifier, and the light source. Giant, translucent stalks the size of glass skyscrapers rise from a floor of matted, emerald moss. These stalks—fibrous and rigid as carbon fiber—reach upward for miles toward a ceiling that is lost in a luminous, lime-colored haze.

Because the density is so low (0.5), these pillars are spaced leagues apart. Between them lies a terrifyingly vast emptiness, a vacuum of green space where the air is thin, frigid (0.2), and smells of crushed mint and ozone.

### The Light and the Cold
The light is blinding (2.9), a relentless, sourceless luminescence emanating from the very cell walls of the plants. It is a "cold" light, providing no warmth. The temperature remains just above freezing, causing the intermittent moisture (0.9) to manifest as shimmering frost on the underside of giant leaves or as sudden, drifting flurries of "green snow"—frozen spores that fall in slow-motion through the near-void.

### The Young Erosion and the Turbulent Drift
Despite its size, this realm is "young" (1.1). The features are sharp, jagged, and unweathered. The great vines have the serrated edges of fresh glass. There is a sense of violent, recent birth. 

The dynamism (2.6) is not geological, but biological. The landscape is in a state of "Turbulent Growth." Entire forests of titanic ferns can sprout, reach a height of four miles, and collapse under their own structural instability within a single week. This creates a landscape of constant, silent catastrophe. You might stand in a clear meadow of moss, only to have a jagged spire of bamboo-like silica burst from the ground at the speed of a rising elevator, hissing as it cuts the frigid air.

### The Scattered Marks of the Primitive
Man-sized life is rare (1.4), and what exists is largely transient. Scattered (0.9) across the floor of the void are the remains of "Low-Tech" civilizations—perhaps refugees from the frozen surface. These are not cities, but lonely, hand-hewn huts (1.1 tech) made of woven vine and hardened sap. They are tethered to the ground with heavy ropes of braided fiber to prevent them from being swept away during the "Vent-Storms"—massive atmospheric shifts caused by the plants exhaling oxygen in sudden, pressurized bursts.

There are no metals here. Tools are fashioned from the obsidian-hard thorns of the lower brush or the translucent, bone-like husks of dead "void-crabs" that occasionally drift down from the ceiling-canopy.

### The Sound of the Void
The world is mostly quiet (1.0). The sheer scale of the space swallows sound. A mile-high pillar collapsing three leagues away produces only a faint, crystalline tinkling, like breaking glass heard through a thick blanket. The primary sound is the "Transpiration Hum"—a low-frequency vibration of the ground as millions of gallons of nutrient-rich sap are pumped through the floor-mats to feed the rising spires.

### The Ecology of the Rare
The fauna consists of "Gliders"—wispy, gelatinous organisms with wingshops the size of sailboats. They are nearly invisible in the bright glare, save for the way they distort the light as they pass. They are rare, solitary, and silent. They feed on the frost-spores, drifting for years without ever touching the emerald floor.

### The Rules of the World
In the Great Chlorophyll Void, the primary law is **Photosynthetic Panic**. Everything is growing too fast to be stable. The ground under your feet is a living, heaving organism that may decide to sprout a mountain-sized limb beneath you at any moment.

To live here is to live in a bright, freezing cathedral of loneliness. You walk upon a floor that is actually a lung, under a sun that is actually a leaf, in a space so vast that your own heartbeat is the loudest thing you will ever hear. It is a kingdom of infinite potential and zero stability—a lush, green desert of the deep.
