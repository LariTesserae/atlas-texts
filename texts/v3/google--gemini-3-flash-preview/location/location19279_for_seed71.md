The world is a sequence of frozen, floating continents drifting through a pressurized amethyst void. We are high here—not in the sense of mountain peaks, but in the sense of a fundamental altitude where the atmosphere is thin, freezing, and crystalline. This is the **Plateau of Sighs**, a vast, shattered shelf of primordial stone that has survived long enough for the concept of "geology" to feel like a fleeting thought.

### The Terrain of the Old Skin
The ground is not soil; it is "The Rime-Bark." It is a dark, ultra-dense metamorphic rock that has been eroded by aeons of solar wind until it is as smooth as polished bone. The erosion here is final. There are no jagged edges left in the natural world. Every ridge is a gentle, sweeping wave, a landscape that has settled into its terminal shape. 

Even though the air is frigid—reaching temperatures that would brittle common iron—it is curiously damp. This is not the dampness of a swamp, but the persistent, stinging moisture of a cloud interior. Intermittent mists roll across the vast distances, depositing a layer of translucent ice-beads on every surface. These beads do not melt; they sublimate directly back into the fog, creating a rhythmic, shimmering cycle of hydration.

### The Vegetation: The Vein-Trees
The flora is lush, yet terrifyingly sparse. Across the immense, grey distances, solitary organisms rise like singular cathedrals. These are the *Lichen-Pails*—massive, sprawling structures of bioluminescent moss and rigid, fern-like fronds that grow to the size of city blocks. 

Because there is so much space between them, each plant is its own ecosystem. They are deep emerald and bruised purple, pulsing with a slow, hydraulic rhythm. They drink the mist through millions of microscopic capillary pores. They do not compete for sunlight; they compete for the "Static," a low-frequency hum that vibrates through the rock. Their roots aren't underground; they fuse with the stone, turning the mountain into a living nervous system.

### The Built World: The Bone-Cities of the Quiet
In the lee of these gargantuan plants, nestled against the ancient, wind-swept curves of the rock, lie the settlements. They are urban in scale—sprawling networks of interconnected domes and tiered plazas—but they are built with the absolute minimum of technology. 

The inhabitants (whoever or whatever they are, for they are never seen) build with "Found Geometry." They use the shed husks of the great plants and the naturally faceted obsidian glass found in the crevices. There is no smelting, no steam, no electricity. The architecture is a triumph of hand-carved joinery and braided fiber. Entire districts are suspended from the overhanging eaves of the Lichen-Pails by ropes made of woven moss-hair.

The density of the buildings is high within the "districts," but these districts are separated by hundreds of miles of haunting, empty tundra. To travel between them is to cross a desert of grey velvet and silence.

### The Rules of Breakdown
The physical laws of the Plateau are fraying. This is an ancient place, and the "coding" of reality has begun to loop and stutter. 

In certain valleys, gravity is an opinion rather than a law. You might find a river of heavy, silver condensation flowing *upward* through the air, curving into a perfect mobius strip before evaporating. In other areas, time is spatially localized: in a ten-foot circle, a fallen stone might take a century to hit the ground, while just outside that circle, the wind howls at its normal, frantic pace.

Shadows here do not always correspond to the objects casting them. A tall pillar may cast the shadow of a blooming flower; a child-sized hut may cast a shadow that stretches for miles, flickering with the silhouettes of things that haven't happened yet.

### The Sound of Motion
The world is quiet, but it is not still. It is **Active**. 
The sound is a low-frequency thrum—the "vibration of the shelf." It is the sound of a planet-sized tuning fork struck a billion years ago and still ringing. 

Overhead, the weather is a constant drama. Because of the scale, you can see three different storms happening at once on the horizon: one a curtain of diamond-dust snow, another a swirling vortex of glowing violet gas, and a third a "Quiet-Front" where all sound is literally erased for a few hours as the air passes through.

### The Ecological Void
There are no animals. No birds wheel in the thin air; no insects crawl in the moss. The only "predation" is elemental. The stone itself slowly "eats" the frost; the plants "eat" the vibration of the stone; the wind "eats" the heat from the buildings. 

It is a world of immense, lonely beauty—a finished masterpiece that is slowly, elegantly, beginning to forget how to exist. It is a city of ghosts without the ghosts, a lush garden in a freezer, a place where the height is so great that you are no longer looking at the sky, but looking *out* from the edge of everything.
