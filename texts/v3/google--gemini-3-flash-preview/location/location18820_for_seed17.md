The world is not a planet, but an infinite, vertical ocean suspended in a void of high-altitude pressure. It is a realm of "heavy blue," where the water does not sit upon a crust but exists as a self-contained, mountainous volume of liquid stretching across the horizon-less reaches of the interior. This is the **Abyssal Plateau**, a high-elevation sea where the water is chilled to the brink of crystallization but never freezes, held in a state of permanent, shivering equilibrium.

### The Terrain of Water
There is no "ground" here, only the crushing, cold weight of the deep. However, the water does not move in currents; it behaves like a mineral. It is so dense and pressurized that it has "ridges" and "canyons" made of phase-shifted ice-crystals that aren't ice, but high-density water lattices. The geography is sharp and young, jagged with architectural geometry that has not yet been smoothed by the friction of time. Because the world is so vast—a continent-sized drop of water—the gravity pulls inward from every direction, creating a sensation of being permanently atop a mountain that has no base.

### The Material and The Light
The light is a steady, piercing cyan, coming from a source so distant it feels like a universal constant. It illuminates the void-spaces between the water-masses. There is no soil, no sand, no silt. The world is chemically pure. There are no plants—not even algae or moss. The biology here is non-recognized: the "fauna" are silicate-based, translucent leviathans that look like shards of moving glass. They do not eat; they vibrate. They drift in the vast, empty spaces between the tectonic water-slabs, their bodies catching the normal, steady light and refracting it into blinding rainbows.

### The Industrial Presence
Scattered through this liquid firmament are the "Pylons." These are the only signs of construction—massive, soot-stained industrial towers of iron and brass, miles long, anchored into the high-density water ridges. They are engines of impossible scale. They do not seem to be extracting anything; rather, they are maintaining the state of the world. Great, churning pistons the size of cities pulse in a slow, rhythmic thud. They are electrified, trailing miles of copper cables that crackle with blue energy, ionizing the water around them. They look like sunken factories from a Victorian nightmare, repurposed for the preservation of a vacuum.

### The Sound
The environment is not quiet. It is a cacophony of physics. The "sound" is a 2.9/3 intensity—a roar that is less an acoustic event and more a physical assault. It is the sound of the Pylons: a continuous, industrial thrumming that vibrates the very molecules of any observer. It is the sound of heavy water grinding against heavy water. It is a deafening, metallic shriek that never fluctuates, a tectonic hum that functions as the world’s heartbeat. To be here is to be vibrated by a machine the size of a galaxy.

### The Ecology and Equilibrium
Living things are abundant but lonely. The glass-shards—the fauna—pulse in synchronization with the Pylons. Because the density of the world is so low (mostly empty, pressurized water-space), a single leviathan may be the only living thing for a thousand miles, yet there are billions of them scattered across the vastness. 

Nothing changes. There is no weather in the traditional sense, only the "never-changing" dynamic. The temperature stays at its needle-point of cold; the light never dims; the engines never fail. It is a frozen industrial equilibrium. The world was built to be exactly this: a high-altitude, sub-aquatic engine room, roaring in the cold dark, populated by ghosts of glass, forever young and forever unmoving. It is a portrait of a machine that has achieved its perfect, terrifying, and permanent stasis.
