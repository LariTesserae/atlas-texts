The world is a single, suffocatingly dense machine of kiln-fired clay and cold, hand-beaten bronze. There is no earth here, only the **Great Loom**: a regional expanse of interlocking architectural gears and baked-earth conduits that stretch toward a ceiling of low-hanging soot.

It is a "total built" environment, yet one of staggering antiquity and primitive execution. Everything is hand-pressed. The "ground" is a mosaic of billions of terracotta shingles, each bearing a thumbprint. The "hills" are actually massive, dormant kilns the size of cathedrals, their vents long ago cooled to a steady, clammy chill.

### The Material and the Moisture
The air is cool and heavy with the smell of wet ceramic and oxidation. Because there is no sun, there is no evaporation; the moisture here is a permanent, recirculating sweat. Water does not fall as rain but weeps from the joints of the bronze pipes. It beads on the cold clay surfaces, forming shallow, stagnant "cistern-pools" in the indentations of the floor. It is a damp world, but the water has nowhere to go; it simply lubricates the friction of the moving city.

### The Sound and the Density
The silence of the darkness is a myth. The landscape is **loud**, a cacophony of rhythmic, low-tech industry. Because the density of the structures is so high—buildings are stacked like crates in a warehouse, with only narrow, rib-like crawlways between them—the sound of the world’s motion is amplified. 

Deep beneath the shingled crust, massive clay counterweights rise and fall. You hear the *thud-hiss* of leather bellows and the grinding of wooden cogs against stone teeth. It is the sound of a clock the size of a mountain, built by people who only had access to mud and fire.

### The Light and the Darkness
Light is a rare, cherished resource, existing only in "hot spots" where primitive oil lamps flicker behind thick, bubbled glass. Most of the landscape is a gradient of charcoal and deep indigo. Shadows here are physical things; because the density of the built environment is so high, the darkness feels like a material you have to push through. 

Occasionally, a pale, phosphorescent mold creeps along the bronze pipes—not a plant, but a chemical reaction to the dampness and the low-voltage static hum of the masonry. It provides just enough light to see that the "sky" is merely another layer of vaulted brickwork, fifty feet above.

### The Emerging Erosion
The world feels new and old simultaneously. While the structures are ancient, the "geology" is **emerging**. The friction of the constant movement is beginning to grind the clay back into dust. In the "industrial valleys" between the kiln-towers, small drifts of terracotta sand are forming—the first beaches of a world turning itself back into grit. The water, dripping from the bronze pipes, is slowly etching tiny, primitive "riverbeds" into the hard-baked tiles. This is the birth of traditional geography within a manufactured womb.

### The Ecology of the Rare
There is no green. There are no leaves. Life is represented by "The Scuttlers"—rare, pale, soft-shelled organisms that look like a cross between a woodlouse and a human hand. They are scavenger-fauna, skittering across the damp tiles to lick the condensation off the copper rivets. They possess no eyes, only sensitive vibrissae that detect the rhythmic thumping of the great weights. 

The humans, if they can be called that, are master potters and tinkers. They live in the "wall-clusters," small, cramped niches carved directly into the sides of the larger machine-blocks. Their technology is limited to what can be fired in a kiln or hammered on a stone anvil. They do not understand the machine they live in; they simply maintain the grease-traps and replace the cracked tiles, acting as the white blood cells of a ceramic god.

### The Rules of the World
In this place, the primary law is **Tension**. Everything must be kept tight, weighted, or balanced. If a counterweight stops, a district goes dark. If a pipe bursts, the dampness turns to a flood that dissolves the unbaked repairs. 

It is an open, regional sprawal of industry without an objective. It is a city that is also a landscape; a machine that is also a tomb; a damp, dark, percussive world where the only "nature" is the slow, inevitable return of the fired clay to the mud from which it was stolen.
