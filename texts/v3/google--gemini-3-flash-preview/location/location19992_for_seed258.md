This is a world of green suspension, a vast, engineered upland where the concept of "ground" has been discarded in favor of a structural canopy that spans the horizon. It is a high-altitude greenhouse of colossal proportions, though no glass holds the heat. The warmth is intrinsic, a byproduct of the sheer volume of respiration occurring within the biomass.

**The Substrate and Construction**
There is no stone here, no silt, no tectonic plate. The entire landscape is a "built" environment, but it is not built from metal or plastic. It is an intentional, biological architecture. Tens of thousands of square miles of thick, translucent vines—each the width of a road—have been woven into a cellular grid. This grid acts as the floor, the walls, and the foundation. It is a tiered city of plants, an arboreal megastructure that feels like a series of interconnected basins suspended ten thousand feet above an invisible floor.

Erosion has not yet begun its work here. The surfaces are slick, raw, and embryonic. The "land" feels newborn, as if the sap hasn't fully hardened. Everywhere you step, the floor—a weave of moss-coated fibers—is slightly tacky, unfinished, and chemically fresh.

**The Water and Atmosphere**
The air is heavy, constant, and saturated. Because the elevation is high, the "land" sits directly within a permanent cloud-layer. Water does not fall as rain so much as it simply *exists* in the air, condensing on every vertical surface. The architecture of the plants is designed to catch this. Deep, bowl-shaped leaves the size of amphitheaters are perpetually full, spilling over into woven aqueducts that channel the water through the mid-levels. 

The atmosphere is a persistent 2.0 wetness; everything weeps. Moisture beads on the undersides of the great sky-vines and drops into the silence below. It is a world of constant dripping, splashing, and the hum of hydraulic pressure moving through the stems of the world.

**The Sparse Totality**
Despite the fact that everything is made of plant matter (2.8 vegetation), the internal space is oddly hollow (0.6 density). The structures are massive but far apart. You might walk along a bridge of braided ferns for a mile before reaching the next "pillar"—a singular, mile-high column of gnarled mahogany-tissue that supports the ceiling of the next tier. There is a profound sense of openness, a "valley-sized" scale where you can look out across miles of emerald-tinted mist and see only three or four colossal biological towers piercing the haze.

The emptiness is not a lack of life, but a deliberate spaciousness of design. It feels like a cathedral before the pews are installed; the architecture is finished, but the inhabitants are elsewhere.

**Sound and Stasis**
The world is remarkably loud. This is not the sound of wind or machinery, but the sound of the structural biology itself. The plants are under immense tension. You hear the deep, rhythmic *thrum-creak* of vine-cables stretching. You hear the roar of water moving through billion-gallon vascular systems. Because the world is in a state of "never changing" equilibrium, these sounds are a constant, unchanging drone—a low-frequency vibration that suggests the world is a single, massive heart beating in place.

Nothing moves. There is no wind to sway the branches, only the static, frozen geometry of the growth. Even the water seems to flow in a way that looks like a still photograph—it spills in the same ribbons, at the same velocity, forever.

**The Living Logic**
There is no technology here. There are no gears, no wires, no circuits. If a light is needed, a specific fungus is bred into the wall. If warmth is required, the plant-tissue in that sector thickens to ferment and radiate heat. It is a "total built" environment where the architects used genetic code instead of blueprints.

Fauna is a rarity. Occasionally, a single, pale, six-winged organism might drift through the gaps in the grid, moving with the sluggishness of a creature that has no predators and nowhere to hurry. These animals are secondary to the plants; they are like the dust motes in a library, tolerated but unnecessary.

**The Weirdness of the High Deep**
The strangest aspect of this place is the lack of "natural" mess. There are no dead leaves on the ground. There is no decay. The system is so perfectly closed and "under construction" that every dead cell is immediately reabsorbed by the living floor. 

You stand on a translucent, emerald balcony that overlooks a drop of five miles. Below you, the mist is thick and green. Above you, the "sun" is a diffused, pearlescent glow filtered through three layers of overhead forest. It is a warm, wet, echoing void—a masterpiece of biological engineering that has reached a point of perfect, terrifying stillness. It is a world that has been finished, polish-wet and humming, and now simply *is*.
