This is the **Cistern of the Iron Weeping**, a high-altitude basin tucked between jagged, geomantic peaks that have only recently pierced the sky. The air here is thin but heavy, saturated with a perpetual, cool mist that clings to the skin like a damp shroud. Because the elevation is high and the erosion is young, the terrain consists of razor-sharp slate ridges and obsidian shelves that have not yet been softened by the ages. 

The scale is intimate—a sunken amphitheater no larger than a village square, walled in by cliffs that weep constant, rhythmic trickles of mineral-rich water.

### The Materiality of the Wet and the Wired
The landscape exists in a state of impossible paradox. Though there are no buildings, no ruins, and no human hands have ever touched this stone, the valley is a site of accidental, biological industry. 

Deep within the lush, oversized mosses—vibrant, neon-streaked greens that cushion the sharp stones—there is a pervasive presence of "Industrial Ecology." The plants do not merely photosynthesize; they produce. Long, fibrous vines, cold to the touch and shielded in a natural, rubbery polymer, drape from the cliffs like hanging power lines. They hum with a steady 60-cycle vibration. In the pools of standing water, copper-rich algae have formed into perfect, geometric honeycombs that act as galvanic cells, sparking softly under the surface.

The "tech" here is not an imposition; it is the flora's primary function. The trees, few and far between but massive in their individual presence, have bark that resembles corrugated steel. From their boughs, strange "fruits" hang—heavy, brass-colored nodules that drip a viscous, lubricating oil instead of nectar.

### The Physics of the Static
Time does not move here in a linear fashion. The "never changing" nature of the Cistern is a physical law. If you toss a stone into one of the crying pools, the ripples move outward and then simply *stop*, frozen in a concentric lattice on the surface. They do not dissipate. Rain falls in a constant, dim drizzle, but the droplets often pause mid-air, suspended in a permanent, shimmering grid, before gravity remembers them and pulls them down a few inches more.

The light is a bruised purple, filtered through a permanent ceiling of low-hanging clouds. It never brightens, and it never fades. The shadows are hard-edged and fixed, cast by the bioluminescent hum of the industrial plants rather than a sun.

### Ecology and Fauna
The fauna is abundant and terrifyingly specialized to this bio-mechanical niche. 
Large, multi-legged crustaceans with shells made of hammered pyrites scuttle across the sharp rocks. They move with the clattering, rhythmic precision of a loom. In the air, thick swarms of insects with translucent, glass-like wings vibrate at frequencies that shatter the suspended water droplets. 

The sound is a constant, grinding roar—the "Loud" of a factory floor. It is the sound of the vegetation processing minerals, the sound of the cliffs discharging static electricity into the moss, and the chittering of the pyrite-crabs as they harvest the oily fruit. It is a cacophony of gears and pistons, yet every "machine" is made of meat, sap, and stone.

### Rules of the World
1.  **Conductivity is Life:** To be dry is to be dead. The moisture on the rocks isn't just water; it is an electrolyte. Everything here communicates through voltage.
2.  **The Law of Stasis:** Nothing decays. A leaf that falls from a corrugated tree will sit on the moss, emerald and metallic, for a thousand years without browning. The "young" erosion of the mountains is locked in place; the peaks will remain sharp forever, protected by the freezing of time’s effect on matter.
3.  **The Industrial Ghost:** Logic dictates that industry requires a creator. Here, the industry *is* the creator. The reality breakdown manifests as a sense of being inside a great, divine engine that is refining nothing but itself.

The air smells of ozone, wet earth, and machine oil. You stand on a shelf of needle-sharp rock, looking out over a small, crowded grove of humming vines and sparking water, trapped in a cold, eternal afternoon where the machines of nature never stop their deafening, purposeless work.
