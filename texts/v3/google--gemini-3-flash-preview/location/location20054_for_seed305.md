The ascent never ends because the world has no horizontal baseline. Here, at an elevation that feels like the roof of a galaxy, the air is thin, freezing, and blindingly bright. 

This is the **Glistening Tier**, a continent-sized shelf of jagged, young obsidian and blue-tinted basalt. Because the erosion is so low, the land is a geometry of knives; every ridge is a fresh fracture, every peak a needle that has not yet been blunted by time. The scale is so massive that the horizon curves upward, swallowed by a sky that is more white than blue, dominated by a sun that never sets but merely pulses with a slow, hypnotic rhythm.

Despite the biting cold, the world is crowded. It is pathologically dense. There is no such thing as an empty space or a "view" that isn't obstructed by the presence of a thousand other things.

### The Vegetation and the Damp
Water does not flow in rivers here; it is captured. The vegetation is a lush, crystalline carpet of "frost-ferns" and "glass-mosses" that grow in hyper-dense thickets. These plants do not seek soil—there is none—but instead cling to the sharp basalt edges, secreting a glycol-like antifreeze that keeps them supple in the sub-zero temperatures. 

Rain occurs as a slow, drifting diamond-dust—intermittent pulses of frozen mist that settle on the leaves. Because the world is so packed, this moisture never reaches the ground. It is trapped in a communal reservoir of condensation, a trillion tiny droplets suspended in the fine hairs of the moss, creating a perennially damp, emerald-and-silver humidity that smells of ozone and crushed mint.

### The Industrial Weirdness
The most jarring element of the Tier is its inherent mechanization. This is a landscape that functions like a continental-scale engine, yet there are no builders. There are no ruins, no bricks, no human hands. 

Rising from the lush moss-beds are "Piston-Firs"—gigantic, organic structures made of hardened chitin and cellulose that move with a rhythmic, hydraulic groan. They are biological machines, pumping the damp nutrients from the depths of the rock to the canopy through a system of pressurized valves. Throughout the valleys, one can hear the subterranean thrum of “tectonic gears,” massive interlocking plates of mineral that grind against one another with the precise regularity of a clockwork factory. 

Deep, humming vibrations rise through the soles of the feet—the sound of a planet-sized industry that produces nothing but its own existence. The light reflects off metallic lianas that pulse with a soft, bioluminescent electricity, powering the slow, laborious growth of the forest. It is an industrial revolution of the DNA, a mechanized ecology where the distinction between "machine" and "organism" has been rendered moot.

### The Overwhelming Fauna
The air and the undergrowth are saturated with life. If you were to reach out a hand, you would touch six different species before your arm was fully extended. 

The fauna are gargantuan and slow-moving, armored in iridescent carapaces to protect them from the razor-sharp terrain. Great "Bellow-Whales" the size of freight ships drift through the high, thin atmosphere, their gas-bladders glowing with the same harsh brightness as the sun. They move with an agonizing slowness, taking years to migrate across a single valley.

On the ground, the density is terrifying. Multitudes of "Gear-Crabs" and "Crank-Lizards" move in interlocking swarms, their limbs clicking like a billion typewriters. They do not hunt so much as they consume the industrial runoff of the Piston-Firs. The sound is a constant, deafening industrial symphony: the hiss of biological steam, the clatter of chitinous plates, the deep, thrumming bass of the forest-engine, and the high-frequency shrieks of sky-predators.

### The Rules of the Tier
1. **Saturation is Law:** There is no "distance." To move is to push through a crowd of living or mechanical obstacles. Everything is touching everything else.
2. **Cold Light, High Friction:** The sun provides blinding visibility but no warmth. Heat is a localized byproduct of friction—where the biological gears grind, the moss is brightest and the ice melts.
3. **The Slow Dynamic:** In this world, a "moment" is a week. The Piston-Firs take a decade to complete a single stroke. The fauna live for centuries, their hearts beating once an hour. Despite the noise and the density, the actual change in the landscape is glacial. A rockfall is a generational event. 

You stand on a ledge of razor-sharp glass, surrounded by a forest that breathes like a steam engine, under a sun that refuses to provide warmth, watching a thousand-ton beast drift inches above your head at the speed of a fingernail’s growth. The air is damp, the ground is vibrating, and the world is absolutely, terrifyingly full.
