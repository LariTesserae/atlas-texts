The atmosphere is not air, but a thick, warm suspension of amber-colored dust and dry, gaseous resins. It tastes of toasted silicate and old copper. This is a world of verticality, a place where the concept of a "ground" is a distant, historical myth. Here, geography consists of the **Stoke-Spires**: impossibly thin ribbons of calcified mineral that reach up for tens of kilometers into the hazy troposphere.

At an elevation where the pressure begins to thin, the spires are crusted with the industry of an era that has forgotten its own beginning.

### The Terrain of the Spires
The primary material is a bone-dry, porous rock, honeycombed by age and heat. Because there is no water to carve the landscape, erosion is the work of the **Thermal Drift**—a slow, relentless upward crawl of hot gas that polishes the spires into aerodynamic needles. The stone is warm to the touch, vibrating with a low-frequency hum from the depths far below. 

Between these kilometers-high needles, there is nothing but the "Vast"—a golden-lit void of swirling particulates. There are no oceans, only the slow, granular flow of sand-rivers at the very base of the world, so far down they appear only as a shimmering haze.

### The Urban Strata
The "cities" are not built *on* the landscape, but *around* it. Industrial rigs, cast from dark, oily alloys, are clamped like parasites to the upper reaches of the spires. These are settlements of rings and hanging gantries. Massive, soot-stained pistons—each the size of a cathedral—cycle with agonizing slowness, pumping heavy gases from the lower depths up to the refinery-caps.

The technology is mechanical, heavy, and rhythmic. Clockwork bridges, miles long, span the gaps between spires, their expansion joints groaning under the heat. There is a sense of great, lumbering production; smoke thick as wool rolls off the factory-tiers, but there is no wind to disperse it. It simply hangs in giant, stagnant pillows, illuminated by the steady, unblinking light of an unseen sun.

### Ecology and the Non-Aqueous
Life here has no use for moisture. The fauna is abundant but unsettlingly quiet. 
- **The Gliders:** Massive, leathery organisms with wingspans that dwarf the industrial rigs. They are translucent, like dried parchment, and they do not flap; they hang in the thermal updrafts, filter-feeding on the mineral dust. 
- **The Scuttlers:** On the vertical faces of the cities, millions of multi-legged, chitinous creatures move in synchronized waves. They are the cleaners, consuming the soot and metallic filings produced by the factories. They move with a dry, clicking sound, like thousands of dice being shaken in a box.

There is no vegetation. In its place are the **Litho-Flows**—stationary crystalline growths that mimic the shape of weeping willows, formed by the slow deposition of minerals from the rising gases. They are brittle and grey, clattering when a Glider’s wake disturbs them.

### Atmospheric Rules and Sound
The most striking quality of the Stoke-Spires is the silence. Despite the presence of massive engines and sprawling urban infrastructure, the air is too dry and the scale too vast for sound to carry far. A factory might explode five miles away, and you would see the flash, the slow expansion of the debris, and hear only a faint, muffled thud minutes later, as if through a thick wall.

Time moves differently here. The "dynamic" of the world is a slow, heavy pulse. The Great Pistons may only complete one stroke every forty-eight hours. The inhabitants—cloaked figures who move along the gantries with calibrated, light-conserving strides—communicate through tactile vibrations, pressing their hands against the vibrating stone of the spires to feel the "speech" of the machinery.

### The Weirdness of Gravity and Light
The light is a constant, honey-hued midday. There are no clouds, only the "Silt-Gloam," which softens all edges. The strange physics of this place suggest that gravity is not a uniform pull downward, but a centrifugal force tied to the spires themselves. One can walk around the circumference of a spire, their feet pulled toward the stone core, looking out into a horizontal infinity where other spire-cities hang like jewelry in the dust.

In this world, growth is a mineral process. The city is growing into the rock; the rock is being ground into the air; and the life that dwells between the two is merely a witness to the slow, warm, and silent dehydration of the universe.
