This is the **Ossuary of the High Humidity**, a plateau perched so high in the atmosphere that the air is a pressurized soup of nectar and steam. Though the elevation is extreme, there is no jaggedness here. Erosion, acting over eons of acidic rain and wind, has softened the mountain into a series of smooth, undulating mounds that resemble the spine of a sleeping beast.

### The Atmosphere and Light
The most striking feature is the absolute, velvety darkness. There is no celestial light here; the sky above is a thick, opaque lid of storm-clouds that never break. It is warm—a heavy, tropical heat that clings to the skin like a damp cloth. Yet, in this total blackness, the world is physically dense with water. Every surface is slick. The air is so saturated that it does not rain so much as it "thickens," forming heavy, globed droplets that suspend themselves in the air before drifting downward with agonizing slowness.

### The Terrain and Vegetation
The ground is a prehistoric "settled" clay, ancient and stable. It does not shift. Out of this clay grows a flora that has long since abandoned the need for photosynthesis. Instead, the lushness is composed of pale, bioluminescent-starved ferns and towering, fleshy fungi that feel like wet velvet. These plants are enormous, their fronds spanning meters, but they are spaced vastly apart. You can walk for minutes through a hollow, cavernous void of warm air before encountering the next massive, weeping stalk. 

This creates a sense of "empty lushness"—there is a tremendous amount of life, but it is separated by terrifyingly large gulfs of dark, open space. The scale is intimate, like a series of interconnected cathedrals, where the "walls" are the trunks of giant, water-logged mosses.

### The Built and the Beyond
Scattered throughout this dark wetland are the Remains. They are not made of stone or steel. They are semi-translucent structures of stabilized light or hard-matter projections—slender, needle-like pylons that hum with a frequency that keeps the local gravity slightly negated. These are artifacts of a post-physical civilization, machines that do not have moving parts but instead manipulate the humidity of the air to store data. 

Occasionally, one finds a "well"—a perfectly circular hovering ring of white light that serves as the only illumination in the landscape. These rings do not light up the surroundings; their light seems to fall inward, illuminating only the water vapor passing through them. They are quiet, patient pieces of engineering, waitstaff of a forgotten era, still regulating the pH of the marsh water for a species that hasn't walked here in a million years.

### Fauna and Sound
While you cannot see the inhabitants, the darkness is loud. The ecology is abundant and vocal. There are "Glass-Bells"—translucent, jellyfish-like aviators that drift through the warm air, pulsing with a rhythmic, low-frequency thrum that vibrates in your chest. The sound is a constant, cacophonous layering of croaks, clicks, and long, mournful whistles that bounce off the high canopy and the ancient, eroded mounds. 

Large, soft-bodied amphibians, the size of small boats, slide through the mud with a sound like tearing silk. They are blind, navigating via the same sophisticated sonar that fills the air with noise. Because the world is so slow—the plants grow over centuries, the water drips in slow-motion—the frantic, loud calls of the animals feel like a frantic protest against the stillness.

### The Rules of the World
In this place, the primary law is **Adhesion.** Because of the high tech-remnants and the ancient moisture, things tend to stick together—physically, energetically, and temporally. Memories linger in the high humidity; the sound of a voice might stay trapped in a pocket of dense vapor for days, repeating until the droplet finally hits the ground. 

Movement must be deliberate. If you move too fast, the air resists you like water. If you stay still, the lush, white moss will begin to feel its way toward your warmth within hours. It is a place of "settled endings." The mountain has finished eroding. The tech has finished evolving. The life has found its final, pitch-black form. It is a warm, loud, wet void—a cathedral of sighs waiting for the water to finish its slow descent.
