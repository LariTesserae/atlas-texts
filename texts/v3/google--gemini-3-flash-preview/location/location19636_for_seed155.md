At this altitude, the air is thin enough to ache, yet it is heavy with the scent of crushed mint and humid lignin. You are standing atop the **Great Canopy-Crust**, a world where the distinction between "ground" and "forest" disappeared eons ago. 

### The Terrain of Infinite Green
Here, the vegetation is not merely a covering; it is the geography. The "soil" beneath your feet is a tightly woven, miles-thick mat of ancient, calcified roots and petrified vines that have flattened into vast, undulating plateaus over millions of years. This is the **Oldest Stable State**. The erosion here is not of rock, but of biological memory—the peaks of this high-elevation world have been worn smooth by the ceaseless friction of wind against leaf, creating a landscape of gentle, emerald-colored domes that stretch toward a horizon where the sky is a pale, cool violet.

Water exists as a phantom presence. It does not flow in rivers but migrates as a "Mist-Tide"—a heavy dew that condenses on the underside of giant, translucent leaves and drips with a rhythmic, percussive *plink* into deep, vertical shafts that pierce the root-crust.

### The Industrial Arborescence
Suspended between the swells of the green plateaus are the **Loom-Cities**. These are urban centers of staggering density, but they are not made of stone or steel. They are grown, then mechanized. Imagine thousands of high-tensile, amber-resin towers connected by swinging iron gantries. 

This is a world of **Industrial Biology**. Enormous, steam-driven pistons—powered by the fermentation of sap—thump and hiss in the open air, their brass casings stained with moss. The sound is a constant, grinding roar: the mechanical heartbeat of a civilization that harvests the very platform it lives upon. Huge, wind-driven turbines rotate slowly in the thin mountain air, capturing the updrafts to spin massive looms that weave fungal silk into industrial cables. The scale is vast—a single factory-district might span a valley five miles wide, hanging over a void of tangled shadows.

### The Weirdness: The Glassing of Time
The high elevation and the "reality breakdown" manifest in the **Temporal Static**. Because the vegetation has reached a state of total saturation, the environment has begun to "overwrite" itself. In certain pockets of the city, the light doesn't just illuminate; it remembers. You might walk past a courtyard where the ghost of a machine that rusted away centuries ago is still visibly turning, a translucent shimmer of silver gears overlapping with a modern sap-vat.

The air occasionally "thrashes"—a localized distortion where the vegetation suddenly grows a hundred years in a second, blossoming into jagged, crystalline flowers, only to shatter into dust moments later. This is the price of the Oldest Stable State: reality is so tired of its own form that it has begun to fray at the edges.

### Ecology and Atmosphere
Fauna is a haunting rarity. You might see a **Glass-Winged Glider**, a creature with more in common with a kite than a bird, drifting silently between the thumping pistons of the factories. They feed on the electricity generated by the sap-turbines, their bodies glowing with a faint, cool blue light. Other than these, the only life is the green.

The soundscape is a cacophony. The "Loud" parameter is met by the intersection of the mechanical and the botanical. There is the shrieking groan of the wind moving through the hollowed-out "pipes" of giant reeds, the rhythmic *thud-hiss* of the industrial pumps, and the occasional, thunderous crack of an ancient root-shelf settling under the weight of a city.

### The Rules of the World
1.  **Gravity is Optional, Tension is Mandatory:** Nothing sits on the ground; everything is suspended, guyed, or woven. To fall is not to hit a floor, but to be swallowed by the infinite tangles of the lower, darker world.
2.  **The Sap Must Move:** The industrial society functions on the circulation of fluids. If a pipe clogs or a root dries, the district "dies," turning from a vibrant emerald to a brittle, grey charcoal that must be pruned away before it infects the neighboring sectors.
3.  **Coolness is Life:** There is no fire here. Combustion is forbidden by the damp, oxygen-rich atmosphere. Energy is cold—derived from pressure, fermentation, and the slow, grinding friction of the planet’s crust.

As you look out across the vista, the sun catches the mist-tide, creating a thousand shimmering rainbows that bridge the gaps between the smoking factory-spires. It is a place of immense age and frantic activity, a green machine humming at the top of the world, waiting for the wind to finally wear it down to nothing.
