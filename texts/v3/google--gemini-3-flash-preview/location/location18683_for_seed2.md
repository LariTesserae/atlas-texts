This is the **Pelagic Megastructure**, an artificial abyss of continental proportions. Here, there is no seabed of sand or silt; there is only the Hull.

### The Foundation
The environment is entirely constructed. The world is a series of nested, planetary-scale spheres of riveted cobalt-steel and reinforced glass, filled to the brim with pressurized, chilled seawater. There is no "surface" here, only the inner skin of the ceiling miles above, and the floor miles below. Everything is high-industrial: the walls are reinforced by massive, ribbed bulkheads and lattice-works of iron that stretch into the blue-black distance. Because the architecture is "young" in geological terms, the edges of the metal are still sharp, the weld-lines clean, and the rivets unpitted by age. There is no rust yet, only the cold, matte sheen of manufactured endurance.

### The Scale and Density
The space is terrifyingly vast. You could drop a mountain range into a single "room" of this place and it would not touch the walls. Yet, for all its size, it is strikingly empty. It is a watery void. One can drift for miles through the center of a chamber without seeing a single feature, just a gradient of pale blue light filtering down from distant, glowing apertures. The density is so low that you are a speck in a vacuum of brine. The distance between the "floor" and the "ceiling" is enough to generate internal weather; cold currents collide to form underwater cyclones, and mineral-heavy water "precipitates" out of the higher levels, falling as a constant, heavy sleet of salt crystals into the depths.

### The Light and Sound
Light is not provided by a sun, but by gargantuan, industrial luminaires embedded in the bulkheads. They cast a steady, flat, flickering white light—the color of a warehouse floor—that penetrates deep into the water. 

The atmosphere is loud. The water acts as a perfect conductor for the world's industry. From miles away, one hears the rhythmic, hydraulic *thrum-clack* of massive intake valves and the groaning of metal expanding under pressure. It is a discordant symphony of grinding gears, the hiss of steam-pipes venting into cold liquid, and the distant, echoing thud of automated pile-drivers. The sound is physical; it vibrates in your chest.

### Ecology and Vegetation
Life has been introduced, but it has not yet conquered the metal. Sparse patches of kelp and bioluminescent moss cling to the seams of the bulkheads, struggling to find purchase on the smooth, manufactured surfaces. The vegetation is utilitarian—mostly oxygen-filtering algae mats that drift like green ghosts in the mid-water.

However, the fauna is abundant and agitated. Schools of silver-scaled fish, engineered for this cold, high-pressure environment, dart in millions through the open spans. They move in mechanical synchronicity, spooked by the loud vibrations of the machinery. Larger predators—slick, grey-skinned leviathans with the lateral lines of sharks and the oily skin of seals—patrol the dark corners where the bulkheads meet. They are not strange or eldritch; they are recognizable, fleshy, and hungry. They hunt by sensing the electrical hum of the lights and the vibrations of the massive pumps.

### The Rules of the World
In this place, the law is **Circulation**. 
The water must be kept moving and filtered, or the inhabitants die. This creates a highly dynamic environment. Huge, churning turbines create "rivers" within the still water, pulling everything toward massive filtration grates. To be caught in a central current is to be swept twenty miles in an hour through the empty dark. 

The temperature is kept at a constant, bracing chill, just above freezing, to optimize the cooling of the world-sized engines embedded behind the walls. There is no "natural" process here that isn't facilitated by a humming motor or a pressurized valve. It is a vibrant, thrumming, metallic ocean, a factory-sea that is just beginning its long life, pulsing with the frantic energy of a biological system trapped inside a titanic machine.
