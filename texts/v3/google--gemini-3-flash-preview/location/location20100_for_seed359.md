The floor is not earth, but a vast, meticulously stitched expanse of cured, amber-colored hide. It is warm to the touch, pulsed by a slow, geothermal thrumming that originates from somewhere deep within the engineered foundation. This is a world of the **Vellum-Valleys**, a cathedral-sized pocket of reality where every square inch has been fashioned by hand, yet the hands that built it seem to have departed or evolved into the very things they grew.

The air is dry, possessing a static-electric crispness that tastes of toasted beeswax and dried resins. There is no water here; the hydration of this world is an alchemical mystery. In place of rain, there is a slow, downward drift of fine, iridescent scales—biological glitter shed by the ceiling-canopy. 

### The Terrain of the Made
The landscape is "low" only in the sense that there are no mountains, only a series of stepped, terraced platforms of woven reed and polished bone. The "erosion" here is remarkably young—the edges of the bone-stairs are still sharp, the serrations on the hand-knotted climbing ropes are fresh and un-frayed. It feels as though the entire environment was finished only yesterday, or perhaps it regenerates its surfaces faster than time can wear them down.

Walls of stretched membrane rise in geometric angles, translucent and glowing with a soft, interior warmth. These are the "mountains" of this place: tectonic plates of drum-leather stretched over skeletal frames of a light, porous wood that has never known a drop of sap.

### The Lushness of the Dry
Vegetation thrives in this arid void, but it is a flora of the non-aqueous. Great, towering plumes of **Pampas-Glass** rise from the seams in the floor. These plants are built of silicate fibers and stiff, papery husks that rattle with a metallic chime when brushed. Thickets of **Lichen-Lace** hang from the ceiling-struts—dense, spongy structures that do not drink water but instead harvest the heat of the air. They are neon-orange and deep violet, pulsing with a slow, bioluminescent rhythm that mimics a heartbeat.

The density is suffocating yet beautiful. There is no open horizon. If you walk ten paces, you are brushed by a dozen different textures: the velvet of a spore-pod, the rasp of a fiber-vine, the smooth, oily surface of a "leaf" that feels like heavy silk. It is a garden grown in a library.

### The Breakdown of Being
The weirdness of the Vellum-Valleys manifests in the way perspective fails. As you look deeper into the thickets of woven grass, the scale begins to drift. A single seed pod might, for a moment, appear to be the size of a villa, containing its own miniature architecture of rooms and hallways, only to snap back to the size of a thumb when you blink.

The "fauna" adds to this reality-glitch. They are creatures of felt and wire, small six-legged things that resemble animated embroidery. They do not eat; they "knit." You can watch a **Thread-Spider** patiently undoing a section of the floor-hide and re-weaving it into a more complex pattern, its movements so slow they are almost imperceptible. There are large, drifting "kites" in the upper reaches of the room—flat, leathery organisms that glide on the rising heat, their bodies covered in intricate, tattooed sigils that shift and change like liquid ink.

### Tools and Silence
The technology is tactile and ancient. Everywhere are the signs of the makers: stone-weighted looms standing idle in the groves, obsidian scrapers tucked into the folds of the membrane-walls, and clay jars filled with dry, powdered pigments. Everything is manual, everything is labor-intensive, yet the scale of the construction is divine.

The sound is a heavy, muffled hush. The air-density acts as a silencer. Your own footsteps on the hide-floor sound like distant drumbeats, echoing not through the air, but through your own bones. There is no wind, only the "The Creak"—the sound of the entire structure settling under the weight of its own complexity. It is the sound of a billion wooden joints and leather thongs sighing in unison.

### The Law of the Place
In this world, moisture is an enemy. To introduce liquid would be to rot the foundation of reality. The inhabitants—both the felt-creatures and the whispering plants—exist in a state of permanent, warm desiccancy. It is a world that is "built" but not "static," a masterpiece of primitive engineering that is slowly, patiently growing itself into a more complex knot, one dry thread at a time. Change here is not measured in seasons, but in the gradual thickening of the weave and the slow migration of the tattoos across the skin of the sky.
