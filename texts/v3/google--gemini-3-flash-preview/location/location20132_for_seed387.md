This is the interior of the **Great Caloric Sump**.

It is a pocket of industry buried deep beneath the silt of a global ocean, a pressurized hollow where the water has not merely filled the space, but has become the primary mechanism of a claustrophobic, churning city. There is no sky here, only the crushing, warm weight of the "Over-Sea" pressing down through miles of darkness, filtered into this small, urban void.

### Terrain and Materials
The environment is entirely synthetic and mineral. There are no trees, no moss, no soft earth. The ground is a reinforced floor of layered basalt and rusted iron plate, gridded with heavy-duty drainage sluices. Because the elevation is so low—well below the crust’s natural shelf—the world is a series of interconnected iron galleries, none larger than a city block, stacked like a vertical hive.

The materials are slick with a constant, warm condensation. Everything is brass, oxidized copper, and black-stamped steel. The erosion is visible in the way the metal has pitted and smoothed over centuries; the corners of the great support pillars have been sanded down by the sheer velocity of the water that moves through this space.

### Atmosphere and Water
The water here is not a static pool. It is an atmospheric tyranny. Because the temperature is high—a humid, heavy warmth generated by geothermal friction—the air is a thick, pressurized soup of hot mist and high-velocity jets. It is a "saturated void." To breathe is to feel the weight of the sea in your lungs; to move is to push through a medium that is halfway between a gale-force storm and a drowning tank.

The water moves in turbulent, violent cycles. It sprays from massive, industrial-grade valves in the ceiling, fueling the machinery, only to be reclaimed by the churning floor-grates. It is a closed-loop deluge, a waterfall that has been domesticated but never tamed.

### The Built Environment and Tech
This is an urban landscape, but it is a city of machines rather than people. The infrastructure is dense: pipes thick as redwood trunks weave through the air, vibrating with enough force to liquefy bone. There are catwalks that hang by trembling chains, leading to high-pressure furnaces that glow a dull, bruised cherry-red through the steam.

The technology is purely industrial. Huge, piston-driven hammers strike rhythmically at nothing, their purpose lost to time or perhaps serving to simply keep the water from boiling. There are no digital screens or glowing interfaces—only brass gauges with needles that quiver at the redline and massive, humming turbines that convert the pressure of the ocean above into a frantic, kinetic frenzy.

### Sound and Light
The sound is the definitive feature of the Sump. It is a multi-layered, deafening roar that never ceases. It is the bass thrum of the ocean’s weight, the shriek of high-pressure steam escaping a nipple-valve, and the metallic thunder of the pumps. You do not hear in this place; you feel the vibrations in your teeth. Conversation is impossible. Thought is difficult. The world is a scream of moving liquid.

Light is a luxury. There is no sun, only the dim, amber glow of sodium lamps encased in thick, pressure-resistant glass. These lights are spaced far apart, creating vast pockets of "near-void" where the swirling mist turns into an opaque shadow. The illumination barely penetrates the spray, casting long, frantic shadows of the oscillating pistons against the wet walls.

### Ecology and Fauna
Life is almost non-existent. There are no plants to handle the violence of the spray. The only biology found here is "unusual"—pale, translucent barnacles that have learned to feed on the mineral-rich rust of the pipes, and occasional schools of blind, needle-toothed fish that have been sucked down from the ocean above through the intake lung. They circulate briefly in the turbulent pools, stunned and frantic, before being ground into the machinery. They are accidents of the environment, not residents.

### The Rules of the World
In the Sump, the dynamic is one of perpetual, chaotic instability. The city is a "storm-state." Nothing stays dry, nothing stays still, and nothing remains quiet. To exist here is to be part of a high-pressure exchange. The primary law is **The Flow**: if the pumps stop, the pocket collapses under the weight of the sea. If the heaters stop, the pressure drops and the walls implode.

It is a hidden, secret room in the basement of the world, where the energy is high, the light is low, and the water is a roaring god that must be constantly bribed with the motion of gears to keep it from reclaiming the space entirely.
