High above the breathable world, where the atmosphere thins into a frigid, violet-black void, lies the **Viridian Stratum**. 

This is a landscape of impossible elevation. Great needles of obsidian rock pierces the clouds, rising miles high into the thin air. The temperature is a biting, crystalline cold that breathes frost onto every surface, yet the world is not dead. It is overgrown. 

### The Vertical Forest
Despite the altitude and the freezing dark, the peaks are smothered in a dense, bioluminescent flora. This is a cold lushness. Massive, waxy ferns the color of bruised plums drape over the cliffs, their undersides glowing with a soft, pale cyan light. Thick carpets of "frost-moss" cling to the vertical faces, drinking the intermittent moisture from high-altitude mists. 

Because the air is so thin and the pressure so low, water behaves strangely. It does not fall as rain so much as it manifests as drifting, jagged frost-shards that hover in the air, or as heavy, silver condensation that beads on the leaves and then rolls upward, defying gravity in sluggish, gelatinous spheres. It is a damp world, but the wetness is localized—found in the hollows of giant gnarled roots or caught in the silk of hanging aerial vines.

### The Industrial Ecology
The "weirdness" of this place manifests in its biology. Evolution here has taken a mechanical turn. The fauna are not flesh, but a rhythmic fusion of organic intent and industrial output. 

From the dark valleys between the peaks, immense, chitinous entities—the *Piston-Whales*—drift through the void. They do not flap wings; they are powered by internal combustion. You can hear the rhythmic, muffled *thrum-thrum-thrum* of their heart-engines, a sound felt in the marrow rather than heard in the ear. They exhaust plumes of hot, white steam into the freezing dark, creating localized clouds that the moss feeds upon.

Small, insectoid "Gear-Flies" swarm through the ferns. Their carapaces are burnished copper, and their wings rotate like miniature turbines, kicking up shimmering dust. There is an abundant population of these things, all clicking and ticking in a grand, mindless factory-dance. The ecology functions like a single, massive machine: the plants produce a flammable, oily sap; the animals consume it to fuel their internal furnaces; and the heat generated keeps the frost-moss from shattering in the cold.

### The Deep Silence
Despite the industrial nature of the life here, the world is profoundly quiet. The air is too thin to carry sound far. A massive landslide on a distant peak looks like a silent, slow-motion spill of black diamonds. The only sounds are the ones that happen right against you: the crunch of frozen moss underfoot, the hiss of your own breath, and the low, subterranean vibration of the mountain’s internal tectonic workings.

Everything is spaced vast distances apart. You stand on a ledge and look out across a gulf that could swallow a city, seeing nothing but the distant, blinking lights of another "machine-beast" five miles away. The density is near zero; the world is a hollow cathedral of stone and stars, populated by rare, massive presences.

### Rules of the World
*   **The Law of Conservation of Heat:** Movement is life. To stop moving is to flash-freeze. Every creature is a heat-engine, and every plant is an insulator.
*   **The Gravity of Moisture:** Water follows temperature, not ground. It migrates toward the warmth of the living creatures, forming floating halos of mist around the drifting behemoths.
*   **The Light of the Void:** There is no sun here, only the dim, distant stars and the glow of the ecosystem itself. The "Industrial" nature of the world provides its own light—the orange spark of a piston, the blue hum of a bio-electric vein.

### The Erosion of Time
The landscape is "established." The stone is not jagged as if newly birthed, but smoothed by eons of high-velocity grit-winds. The peaks are shaped like polished teeth. There are no buildings, no ruins of empires. The only "built" feeling comes from the biological world itself—the way the vines weave into grids, the way the beasts’ shells resemble riveted armor. It is a wilderness that has accidentally evolved the aesthetics of a factory, a place where nature has decided that the most efficient way to survive the high-altitude cold is to become a machine.
