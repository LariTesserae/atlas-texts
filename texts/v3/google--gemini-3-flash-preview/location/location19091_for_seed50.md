This is the **Interior of the Great Vertical Cataract.**

At an elevation where the air should be thin and freezing, the world is instead a claustrophobic column of falling sapphire. We are not in an ocean, but within a "hidden scale" pocket of gravity—a singular, mile-high cylinder of water perpetually collapsing from a floating mountain range. 

### The Terrain of the Falling Wall
The environment is defined by a paradoxical "high-elevation submersion." You are suspended within a vertical river that never hits the ground. The "ground" here is an emerging geology of **basaltic needles**—raw, black rock teeth that are just now crystallizing out of the pressure. These rocks have no history; they are embryonic, emerging from the fluid as if the water itself is dreaming of stone. They are sharp, un-eroded, and terrifyingly new.

Because the density is so low, these needles are separated by vast, terrifying voids of translucent blue. You might see one black spire, and then nothing but a hundred yards of churning froth before the next one appears.

### The Atmosphere and Sound
The sound is not an ambient noise; it is a physical weight. It is a **2.8/3 deafening roar**—the infinite scream of millions of tons of water shearing against a vacuum. It is a sound that vibrates the marrow in your bones until your teeth ache. You do not hear the world; you feel it as a concussive, rhythmic trauma.

The temperature is cool, a constant 50 degrees Fahrenheit, maintained by the sheer velocity of the descent. The light, however, is deceptively normal. High-altitude sunlight pierces the column, refracting through the bubbles to create prismatic halos and blinding white caustic patterns that dance across the raw, black rocks.

### The Sparse Ecology
Vegetation is a mere ghost here. It consists of **glass-fiber mosses** that cling to the leeward side of the emerging needles. They don’t have leaves; they have crystalline filaments that catch nutrients from the spray. 

The fauna is rarer still. Occasionally, a **Slip-Ray**—a creature the size of a hand, translucent and triangular—darts through the chaos. They do not swim so much as they use the turbulence to propel themselves, living their entire lives in a state of terminal velocity.

### The Built World and Primitive Tech
There are marks of presence here, though they feel like accidents. Wedged into the crevices of the basalt needles are **hanging cradles** made of hammered copper and woven kelp-fiber. These are the "scattered structures." They are tiny, one-room pods lashed to the rock with thick, primitive ropes. 

The technology is grounded in the manual. There are no engines, only **heavy stone counterweights** and hand-carved wooden vanes that harness the downward force of the water to grind pigments or sharpen tools. The people who lived here—or who are currently hiding here—left behind jagged, hand-knapped obsidian anchors and bone hooks. It is a civilization of the "clutch," living in the cracks of a falling world.

### The Dynamic: The Turbulent Void
The world is in a state of **hyper-active instability**. This is not a static waterfall; it is a "Solaris-state" of water. The flow constantly reconfigures itself. One moment, you are in a pocket of air; the next, a chaotic limb of water slams into your spire, bringing with it a slurry of new-born gravel. 

Rules of this world:
1. **Gravity is a suggestion, but Velocity is the law.** You do not fall; you are carried.
2. **Nothing is old.** The rocks are being born beneath your feet. The moss grew this morning. The copper pods were hammered yesterday.
3. **Space is a lie.** Though the column is miles high, the habitable space is a series of "intimate rooms" created by the intersection of spray and stone.

To be here is to be inside a diamond-bright, thundering throat. It is a place of primal creation where the water wants to be stone, and the stone is too new to know how to hold still.
