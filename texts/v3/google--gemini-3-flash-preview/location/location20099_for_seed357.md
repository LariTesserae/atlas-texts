The world is a vertical ocean of chlorophyll, suspended miles above a ground that no longer exists. There is no stone here, no silt, no tectonic bone. Everything is **built**, but not by hands; the environment is a deliberate, massive construction of biological intent, a woven megastructure of cellulose and sap that has replaced the crust of the planet.

### The Architecture of the Ascent
We are high up, where the air is thin but heavy with a pressurized, humid heat. The "ground" is a translucent, emerald lattice—a floor of woven vines as thick as cathedral pillars, spaced apart to leave terrifying, kilometer-wide gaps that look down into an endless green abyss. Because the **density** is low, the landscape feels like a skeletal city of gargants. You are standing on a pier of living wood that extends three miles over a void, connected to the next "continent" by a single, pulsing tendril. 

The features are **young**. The ridges of these wooden mountains are sharp, lacking the soft curves of weather-worn hills. They have just emerged, thrust upward by a frantic, subterranean growth pressure. The edges of the great leaves are like razors; the bark is taut and shimmering, still wet with the birth-fluids of its own rapid expansion.

### The Water and the Heat
It is stiflingly hot, a wet, medicinal heat that smells of crushed mint and fermenting nectar. **Water** is the dominant law. It does not fall as rain so much as it exuded from the very walls of the world. The massive, vertical trunks "sweat" great globes of rhythmic, pulsing moisture. Because of the high elevation and heat, the water is constantly transitioning between liquid and steam. Low-hanging clouds of violet vapor drift through the gaps in the lattice, snagging on the thorns of the architecture. 

Every surface is slick. Small, perched basins—bowls the size of lakes formed by the intersection of three massive leaves—boil quietly in the sun, sending plumes of white mist upward to feed the canopy above.

### The Turbulence and the Sound
The world is **turbulent**. It is never still. The great structures are growing at a visible rate—a vine the size of a highway might whip across a gap in a matter of minutes, seeking a grip, shuddering with the force of its own internal hydraulics. This is the source of the **loud** environment. There is no wind, but there is the sound of *tension*: the groaning of wood under immense pressure, the wet *thwack* of unfolding fronds, and the constant, rhythmic thrum of sap being pumped upward through trillion-valved arterial systems. It sounds like a factory, but the machinery is all soft tissue.

Occasionally, a massive structural element—a bridge of moss-brick or a tower of fused ferns—collapses under its own weight, plunging into the green mist below. The sound is like a muffled explosion, followed by the immediate, aggressive "hiss" of new growth rushing to fill the vacuum left behind.

### The Ecology of Absence
There is no **fauna**. Not a single bird breaks the horizon; no insect hums in the heat. The vacancy of animal life yields a strange, eerie purity. The plants have adapted to this void by becoming hyper-active. Without pollinators, the plants move themselves. Without grazers, the plants consume each other. 

The "weirdness" lies in the **dynamic** nature of the vegetation. The plants exhibit a form of slow, vegetable intelligence. Flowers the size of houses bloom and wither in an hour, their petals dissolving into a nutrient-rich slurry that is immediately reabsorbed by the stem. The landscape is a "Solaris" of wood and leaf—it is a single, dreaming organism that is constantly redesigning its own geography. 

### The Light and the Scale
The **light** is steady, filtered through leagues of overhead canopy into a rhythmic dappling of gold and bruised malachite. Because the scale is so **open**, you can see for a hundred miles in every direction. You see other "districts" of this living machine: distant, floating islands of tangled briar, spiraling towers of amber-colored resin that reach for the stratosphere, and great, weeping waterfalls of pure sap that bridge the gaps between the heights.

Everything is green, gold, and wet. It is a world of total construction with zero technology—a biological skyscraper-valley where the only rule is to climb, to grow, and to flow before the heat evaporates you into the shimmering, emerald sky.
