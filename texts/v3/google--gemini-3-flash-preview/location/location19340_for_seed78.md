The world is a frozen, vertical garden suspended in a void. 

Imagine a darkness so profound that its only limit is the faint, phosphorescent pulse of life. Here, the "ground" does not exist as stone or soil. Instead, the foundation of all things is the **Great Liana**, a titanic, woody vine braided of quadrillions of individual fibers, each as thick as a ship's mast. These vines stretch for thousands of miles across a hollow, pressurized abyss. They do not crawl along the earth; they *are* the earth, spanning the gulfs between distant, unseen anchors like the rigging of a ghost ship the size of a continent.

**The Terrain of Flesh and Bark**
The scale is staggering. To look "down" is to see the dwindling silhouettes of lower vine-lattices vanishing into an ink-black throat. To look "up" is to see more of the same, though the air grows thinner and the cold more biting. Because the elevation is so high and the density of matter so low, the atmosphere behaves like a living tide. Clouds of freezing mist roll through the gaps between the vines, depositing a thin, rime-crust of ice—the primary source of water in this world. 

The erosion here is "young." The bark of the Great Liana is sharp, jagged, and fresh, splintering underfoot like obsidian glass. There hasn't been enough time for the winds to smooth the protrusions of the colossal thorns, which are the size of mountain peaks. Every surface is a precipice. 

**The Atmosphere and Light**
It is nearly midnight at all times. There is no sun, only the dim, indigo bioluminescence of "Lure-Moss" that coats the underside of the main boughs. The temperature remains a constant, bone-aching chill, hovering just above freezing within the vine-tangle and dropping into the lethal negatives in the open voids between them. 

The air is loud. This is not a silent vacuum. The Great Lianas are under immense tension, swaying in the continental-scale drafts of the abyss. They groan and shriek like metal cables. The sound of a falling ice-sheet smashing against a lower branch miles below echoes upward, a thunderous crack that ripples through the wood. And through it all, the hum of the "Wind-Pitchers"—vast, hollow seed pods that act as flutes, turning the updrafts into a constant, mournful cello note.

**Ecology and Fauna**
The vegetation is total. Every square inch of the environment is biological. Even the "rocks" are calcified fungal growths. Because space is so empty and the matter so sparse, life has evolved to be highly mobile and aggressive. 

The fauna is abundant but hidden in the crevices of the bark. Six-winged "Gliding Skinks" leap from the upper reaches, their translucent skins shivering as they catch the updrafts, diving for miles to find a mate before climbing back up. "Spine-Crabs" the size of wagons scuttle along the vertical faces, their many legs ending in carbon-hardened spikes that bite into the fresh wood. They act as the janitors of the lianas, scraping away the ice and the parasitic mosses.

**Built Marks and Technology**
Human—or near-human—life exists here, though it is scattered and fragile. Small hamlets are suspended in the "dead zones" where three or four massive vines intersect. These dwellings are not built on the vines, but *woven into* them. They are spherical cradles made of toughened silk and bent saplings, anchored by miles of hand-braided rope.

The technology is primitive, borne of a world with no fire and no ore. There is no smelting; there is no smoke. The inhabitants use "Cold-Craft." They carve the razor-sharp thorns of the liana into knives and spears. They use the stomach-acid of predatory pitchers to dissolve and reshape biological resins. Their "metal" is the discarded exoskeleton of the giant Spine-Crabs, cold-hammered into shape using stones salvaged from the occasional meteoritic impact caught in the vine-web.

**The Dynamic Motion**
The world is never still. It is a "dynamic" ecosystem because it is constantly growing and perpetually collapsing. In the distance, a "Branch-Fall" occurs—a section of the lattice, miles wide, finally succumbs to its own weight or the buildup of ice. It tears away from the main trunk with the sound of a shattering world, plummeting into the dark void. This creates a vacuum that sucks the surrounding mists toward it, triggering localized storms of frozen condensation.

The humans here are nomads of the vertical. They follow the "Bloom-Tides," moving their hanging villages higher or lower as the Great Liana pulses with nutrient-rich sap or retreats into a dormant, brittle winter. They are masters of the knot and the tether, living in a realm where "the ground" is a concept they have never known, and where their existence is a tiny, warm pulse in a vast, dark, and shrieking forest of the air.
