The landscape is a structural forest without a floor, a continental knot of engineered vines spanning a dry, airless gulf. Here, the concept of "ground" has been eroded by time and replaced by a gargantuan, mechanical ecology.

### The Substrate of Living Industry
The world is constructed entirely of a single, hyper-specialized organism: the *Brass-Oat*. This is a botanical-industrial hybrid, a flora that does not drink water but instead thrives on the friction of static electricity and the slow decay of radioactive isotopes embedded in its own cellulose. Total vegetation means that every surface—every catwalk, every soaring buttress, every piston-housing—is grown. 

It is a world of "built" nature. The stalks are not wood, but a copper-toned, fibrous alloy that feels cool to the touch. They grow in perfect, geometric grids that stretch for thousands of miles, forming a dense, three-dimensional lattice. From a distance, it looks like a golden haze; up close, it is a staggering complex of woven girders, humming with the low-voltage thrum of industrial metabolism.

### The Atmosphere and The Light
There is no water here. The air is a dry, crystalline gas, scented with ozone and scorched sugar. It is exceptionally clear, which allows the light to become an architectural force. The brightness is 2.8/3; it is a searing, shadowless white radiance emanating from a sun that never sets, or perhaps from the very ceiling of the reality-sphere. This light penetrates miles deep into the lattice, refracting through the translucent amber leaves that sprout from the girders.

Because there is no moisture to scatter the light, the view remains sharp for hundreds of leagues. You can see the curvature of the world not by a horizon of soil, but by the gradual convergence of the grid-lines in the far distance, where the industrial forest meets the sky.

### The Quiet Mechanics
The sound is a 0.4—an oppressive, velvet silence. There is no wind, for there is no fluid atmosphere to carry it. The only sound is the internal "biological" process of the world: the rhythmic *clack-hiss* of an automated valve miles away, or the high-frequency vibration of a sap-line pumping liquid minerals through a metallic vine. It is the sound of a factory running in a vacuum.

### The Ecology of the Manufactured
In this vast, low-elevation expanse, the "fauna" are not flesh. They are rare, specialized drones—biological clockwork entities that look like oversized dragonflies made of stained glass and rusted iron. They do not hunt for meat; they prune. They move with a jittery, stop-motion grace, snipping off errant growths that threaten to clog the world’s mechanical joints.

Erosion here is a settled state. The world has looked this way for eons. The brass-colored vines have grown over older, discarded versions of themselves, creating layers of "fossilized" machinery beneath the active canopy. The "soil" is merely the dust of ground-down gears and powdered leaves, gathered in the crooks of massive, trunk-like pistons.

### The Reality Breakdown
The "weirdness" manifests in the physics of the growth. Because the scale is continental and the tech is industrial, the forest has begun to ignore Euclidean geometry. You may walk along a horizontal beam for miles, only to realize you are now walking vertically, yet your internal sense of gravity has not shifted. The "weather" consists of massive, slow-moving waves of magnetism that cause the leaves to stand on end and glow with a blue St. Elmo’s Fire.

In certain sectors, the vegetation begins to process *information* instead of minerals. The leaves here are printed with flickering circuit patterns, and the "fruit" are glowing vacuum tubes that pulse with the rhythm of a global, vegetable calculation. Life, industry, and the fundamental laws of space have merged into a single, dry, brilliantly lit system. It is a world that is being constantly synthesized, a bright and cold cathedral of living metal that is always expanding, forever fueled by its own internal, silent combustion.
