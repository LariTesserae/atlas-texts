It is a world of bone-dry radiance, held aloft in a perpetual, freezing noon. 

The elevation here is absolute; there is no "down" that does not lead to a suffocating abyss of thicker gases. This is a continental plateau of porous, chalk-white mineral that floats upon a sea of heavy, invisible argon. The air is thin, crisp, and utterly devoid of moisture. There is no ice, no dew, no rain. Instead, the sky is a blinding, over-exposed white-blue, shedding a light so intense it seems to exert physical pressure on the ground.

### The Terrain
The land is a vast, sculpted labyrinth of white calcified ridges and high, wind-swept mesas. Because erosion has been working these stones for eons, there are no jagged edges. Every cliff is buffed to a silk-smooth finish by the passage of dry gales. The scales are gargantuan—a single "valley" is wide enough to contain its own atmospheric circulation, creating localized high-pressure cells that hum against the stone. 

The ground is not soil, but a fine, crystalline dust that behaves like flour. It does not clump. If you were to pour it from your hand, it would fall in a perfect, silent stream, unaffected by humidity, layering into dunes that have stood for ten thousand years.

### The Vegetation
In this desiccated brilliance, life has found a non-aqueous path. The landscape is lush, but not green. The flora consists of "Lithobolides"—massive, slow-growing structures that resemble a cross between giant puffballs and intricate glass lace. They do not drink; they breathe. They harvest energy directly from the relentless light, converting radiation into a slow-moving, silicon-based sap.

They are spaced widely apart, respecting the scarcity of mineral nutrients. Each organism might be the size of a cathedral, a translucent violet or pale amber dome that shimmers in the glare. They are hollow, their interiors acting as bellows that expand and contract over the course of weeks, inhaling the dry dust and exhaling pure oxygen and fine, needle-like spores. These "forests" do not rustle; they vibrate with a low-frequency thrum that can be felt in the soles of the feet but barely heard by the ear.

### The Dynamics of the World
The world is active, but its pace is geological. The primary dynamic is the "Dust Cycle." Massive plumes of white silicate are lifted from the lowlands by rising heat and carried across the high plateaus. This dust acts as both food and abrasive. As it scours the Lithobolides, they grow thicker shells to compensate, creating a constant state of biological tension between growth and erosion.

There is no "weather" in the sense of storms. Instead, there are "Light Tides." Every few days, the luminosity intensifies until the shadows vanish entirely. During these peaks, the vegetation glows from within, a bioluminescent response to the solar feast. The air grows cold—a dry, brittle cold that would shatter a wet lung—but the light makes the eyes ache with the illusion of heat.

### The Fauna
Animals are ghosts here. They are rare, spindly, and largely transparent. Long-legged "Striders" move between the violet domes, their limbs as thin as glass rods. They do not have mouths; they have porous pads on their feet that absorb the chemical secretions left on the mineral soil by the Lithobolides. They move with an agonizing, fluid grace, leaping miles in the low gravity, silent as falling feathers. There is no predation as we understand it, only a slow, competitive grazing of the mineral-residue.

### The Rules and Atmosphere
This is a realm of profound, ringing silence. The lack of liquid water means there is no splashing, no gurgling, no rhythmic crashing of waves. Sound travels poorly in the thin, dry air, muffled by the vast distances and the soft dust. If a spire of rock were to collapse, it would fall with a muted thirr, like a curtain closing.

The law of this place is Clarity. Without a haze of water vapor, your vision extends for hundreds of miles. You can see the curvature of the world at the horizon, where the white stone meets the velvet black of the upper atmosphere. There is no rot here, no decay. When a creature or a plant dies, it does not crumble; it is slowly sanded away by the wind, becoming the very dust that feeds the next generation. It is a world of pure, sterile persistence—bright, high, and hauntingly empty.
