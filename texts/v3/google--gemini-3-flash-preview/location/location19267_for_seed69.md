The world is a vertical ocean of iron and chlorophyll, suspended in a perpetual, warm midnight. 

At this elevation, the atmosphere is so thick it behaves like a pressurized fluid. Here, miles above any bedrock, the "terrain" is a continental scaffold of oxidized girders and massive, interlocking gears that have been grinding for so long they have become a geography unto themselves. This is a realm of **High Industry**, where the machine and the jungle have fused into a single, breathing metabolism.

The light is nonexistent. There is no sun, no moon, no stars—only the absolute, velvety black of a world-sized interior. 

### The Terrain of Rust and Sap
The scale is staggering. You are standing on the rim of a cooling vent three miles wide. Below you, the abyss; above you, the underside of a sister-continent of piston-driven steel. Because the erosion is so established (2.3/3), the original sharp edges of the machines have been softened. Thousands of years of hyper-oxygenated moisture have coated the iron in a thick, velvety patina of rust, which in turn has been colonised by the lushness.

Water is everywhere—not as standing pools, but as a constant, heavy perspiration. The air is 2.0/3 "wet," meaning it is a warm, suspended mist that clings to the skin. It weeps from the undersides of the great gears, dripping in rhythmic heavy splashes into the dark. It smells of hot oil, crushed ferns, and ozone.

### The Vegetation: The Great Weeping Canopy
In the total darkness, the plants (1.8/3) have solved the problem of energy through heat and vibration. Massive, fleshy bromeliads the size of houses grip the rusted struts. They are deep purple, almost black, their leaves ribbed with conductive filaments that drink the static electricity generated by the friction of the moving landscape. 

There are "Clockwork Mangroves" whose roots are not wood, but braided copper wire encrusted in lichen. They grow in the joints of the great machines, their pulsing vascular systems synchronized with the thrum of the spinning flywheels. Everything is dense. There is no empty space; if a surface is not moving too fast to be touched, it is carpeted in bioluminescent moss that flickers with a dim, sickly green phosphor when the ground shakes.

### The Sound: The Great Resonance
The world is 2.1/3 loud—a cacophony that is both mechanical and biological. 
There is the **Deep Bass**: the tectonic groan of planetary-scale hydraulics shifting miles below. 
There is the **Rhythmic Clatter**: the sound of millions of metallic valves opening and closing in the dark, sounding like the chattering of giant teeth.
There is the **Biological Cry**: out in the dark, the fauna (2.0/3) are screaming. Huge, sightless moths with wings of gossamer-thin foil beat against the steam pipes. Things with too many legs scuttle over the weeping moss, their carapaces clicking like castanets. Apex predators—massive, soft-bodied cephalopods that "fly" through the thick air—emit ultrasonic pings that ripple the hanging moisture.

### The Built and the Tech
The "human" or "sentient" presence (1.1/3) is swallowed by this scale. Occasionally, you find a gantry or a control booth bolted to the side of a thousand-foot piston. These structures are industrial (2.5/3), made of riveted brass and wired with glowing vacuum tubes, but they are weathered and weary. They feel like pilot houses on a ship that has been sailing without a crew for a millennium. 

Steam whistles scream from hidden vents, signaling a pressure release that shifted a thousand tons of foliage three levels up. The technology isn't "high-tech" in a digital sense; it is heavy, greasy, and mechanical. It is a world of boilers the size of cathedrals and drive-belts made of woven vine and leather that span valleys of darkness.

### The Dynamics of the Dark
This is a high-motion world. It is active (2.1/3). Nothing stays still. The "weather" here is a cycle of pressure. When the great boilers vent, a "Warm Gale" rushes through the lattice, carrying scorched spores and droplets of lubricant. This triggers a feeding frenzy among the aerial fauna, who rise from the lower vents to catch the nutrient-rich soot.

Rules of the world:
1.  **Gravity is Negotiable:** Because the density of the air and the complexity of the lattice are so high, "falling" often just means landing on a softer layer of vegetation fifty feet down.
2.  **Sound is Sight:** In the 0.0 light, the inhabitants navigate by the vibrations of the machines. To be silent is to be blind; to be loud is to be seen.
3.  **The Machine is the Mother:** The plants do not grow toward light; they grow toward heat. The closer a vine is to a friction-hot bearing, the more lushly it blooms.

It is a warm, wet, thundering cathedral of rot and iron, where the heartbeat of the forest is the rhythmic thud of a ten-thousand-ton hammer hitting an anvil in the dark.
