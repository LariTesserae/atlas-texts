The world is not a planet, but an endless, vertical loom of woven green. It is a continent-sized lattice of living fiber, suspended at an altitude where the air is thin but perpetually warm, heated by the friction of its own immense biological processes.

There is no ground. If you were to drop a stone, it would fall for miles through a hollow, green-tinted cathedral of void, passing through successive layers of woven "shelves" that stretch toward horizon-lines hidden by mist. This is the **Weave-State**: a fully constructed, sentient landscape made of bio-engineered vines and gargantuan, calcified stalks that have been guided, braided, and knotted by ancient, simple hands over millennia.

### The Material and the Build
The substrate is entirely vegetable, yet it is not "wild." Every square inch of the terrain bears the mark of primitive, obsessive craftsmanship. The floor beneath your feet is a tightly plaited mat of living seagrass-analogs, soft and yielding, yet reinforced with hand-lashed bamboo-like struts the size of ocean liners. Because the world is "Built" but "Primitive," there are no machines, only the results of billions of man-hours of knotting. Great hanging bridges of fibrous rope, some three miles wide, span the gaps between the main vertical columns. 

Everything is damp. The elevation brings the Weave into the path of wandering, slow-moving clouds that snag on the thorns and leaves. Water does not fall as rain so much as it "weeps" from the architecture. The woven walls are constantly sweating, silver droplets clinging to the undersides of every leaf, feeding the mosses that serve as the world’s skin.

### The Atmosphere and Scale
The scale is terrifying. The hollows between the woven continents are so vast they possess their own internal weather systems. You can stand on the edge of a woven pier and watch a localized thunderstorm brew three miles below you, fueled by the humidity of the transpiring leaves, while the sun—a dim, orange smudge seen through the upper canopy—remains motionless. 

The light is a constant, bruised twilight. The density of the vegetation above filters out the harshness of the sky, leaving a gloom the color of a hemlock forest at dusk. In this dimness, distances are impossible to judge. A flickering light a mile away might be a predatory insect, or it might be a distant village fireside suspended in a basket of wicker.

### The Ecology of the Void
Because the density is so low (mostly empty space between the massive structural weaves), life has evolved to be either tethered or airborne. 
*   **The Tethers:** Abundant fauna, mostly hexapedal and soft-bodied, crawl along the underside of the weave. They move with a slow, rhythmic suction, grazing on the bioluminescent algae that grows in the damp shadows.
*   **The Drifters:** Great, translucent bladders—beings like aerial jellyfish—float in the voids between the structures. They have no eyes, only long, sensory ribbons that trail hundreds of feet, tasting the humidity for the scent of ripening fruit-pods.

### The Reality Breakdown
Here, the laws of physics are beginning to lose their grip to the "Built" will of the forest. The gravity is inconsistent; in the deeper, more densely woven "wells," you might find yourself walking on the walls or finding that the water from the weeping rocks is falling *upward* toward a more massive cluster of vegetation. 

Time is tied to the growth of the Weave. There are "areas of speed" where the vines grow so fast you can hear them crackling like a slow-burning fire, and "areas of stasis" where a fallen leaf takes a generation to reach the bottom. The "Reality Breakdown" manifests as a blurring of the line between the observer and the observed—the environment is so heavily "Made" that the plants seem to respond to the intentions of those walking upon them. If you seek a path, the vines may begin to lacing themselves together before your feet.

### Sound and Silence
For all its biological activity, the world is eerily quiet. There is no wind-whine against stone, only the muffled *thrum* of sap moving through trillion-gallon vascular systems. The sound is a low-frequency vibration, more felt in the soles of the feet than heard in the ears—the heartbeat of a continent made of wood. Occasionally, the silence is punctuated by the distant, wet *splat* of a massive seed pod bursting or the mournful, flute-like call of a drifter miles away in the mist.

### The Inhabitants
Humanoid or otherwise, the builders move through this world with pre-industrial grace. They carry no metal. Their tools are teeth, bone, and sharpened obsidian. They are the gardeners of the abyss, spending their quiet lives ensuring the knots hold, grafting new shoots to the old, and living in the warm, humid hollows of the living architecture. They do not fight nature; they are weaving it into a shape they can inhabit, one slow, wet knot at a time.
