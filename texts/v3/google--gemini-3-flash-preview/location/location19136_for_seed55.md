This is a world of the **Cold Drown**. 

It is a singular, planetary structure of infinite wicker and joinery, submerged entirely within a lightless, freezing ocean. There is no sky, no shore, and no stone. Every inch of the environment has been woven, lashed, or carved by hands that have long since departed, leaving behind a "total build" that spans the diameter of a world.

### The Materiality of the Deep
The "ground" here is not earth, but a compacted, pressurized mesh of ironwood pilings and woven kelp-ropes, some the thickness of cathedral pillars. Because the erosion is so advanced, the sharp edges of the original construction have been buffeted into smooth, rounded nubs by eons of slow current. The surfaces are slick, not with slime, but with the velvet texture of water-logged fibers. 

Everything is a result of primitive, monumental labor. You can trace the thumbprints in the fired clay seals and the uneven cross-hatching of the Great Weave. It is a technological paradox: a structure of planetary scale built with the tools of an apprentice carpenter. There are no machines here, only the trillion-fold repetition of the knot and the dowel.

### The Lush Darkness
Despite the near-total absence of light, the world is suffocatingly lush. In the 0.2 light—a faint, bioluminescent ghost-blue that leaks from the pores of the wood—vegetation thrives in cold-adapted, tactile forms. 

Gigantic, translucent ferns with fronds as heavy as leather cling to the latticework. They do not need the sun; they feed on the mineral-rich cold of the deep currents. Sponges like porous lung-tissue have overgrown the corridors, bridging the gaps between the massive support beams. The density is such that a traveler never moves through open water, but rather through a series of narrowing and widening capillaries of flora. To move is to push aside soft, freezing veils of hanging moss that have spent ten thousand years lengthening by a single inch.

### The Temperature and the Quiet
The water is stilled by the density of the construction. It is a heavy, viscous cold that leeches heat from anything it touches. It does not freeze, but it holds the memory of ice. 

The sound is a muted, rhythmic thrum—the "Slow Pulse." Because the entire world is a single connected object, a tremor on the far side of the planet travels through the wood as a low-frequency vibration. It is the sound of a billion tons of woven fiber settling under the weight of an ocean. Occasionally, a wooden joint the size of a city will groan, a sound that takes minutes to resolve, echoing like a dying whale’s song through the saturated darkness.

### The Absence of Breath
There are no fish here. No glittering schools, no predators in the dark. The "fauna" is a vacuum. The only thing that breathes is the flora, and it breathes with a glacial slowness. The ecology is closed, perfect, and lonely. It is a garden without a gardener, a city without citizens, a machine made of wood that has forgotten its purpose.

### The Rules of the World
In the Cold Drown, the primary law is **stasis**. Because the erosion has already reached its "final form," nothing breaks, but nothing is new. The world is a masterpiece of equilibrium. 

1.  **The Law of the Knot:** Everything is connected. If you pull a loose vine in one chamber, a bell-shaped flower may shiver in a hall a thousand miles away.
2.  **The Law of the Sinking:** Without the "up" of the sun or the "down" of a seabed, gravity is felt only as a faint pull toward the center of the planetary mass. The further "in" one goes, the tighter the weave becomes, until the water is squeezed out and there is only the crushing density of ancient, frozen wood.
3.  **The Law of the Slow:** To live here—if one could—would be to adopt the pace of the lichen. A conversation would take decades. A footstep would be an event for the next generation.

It is a monument to the primitive. A vast, intricate, hand-made cage for an ocean, where the only thing that changes is the gradual, infinitesimal thickening of the moss on the beams. It is a quiet, cold, and beautiful end to the concept of the "natural" world.
