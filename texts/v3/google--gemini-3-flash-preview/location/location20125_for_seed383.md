The world is a vast, subterranean hollow, an inverted city etched into the belly of a continent. It is not a cave of jagged rocks or claustrophobic tunnels, but an ancient, open-air vault kilometers wide, ceilinged by a luminous phosphor-vein that glows with the unwavering intensity of a midday sun. This light is white and merciless, casting no soft edges, reflecting off the pale, pulverized dust of a world that has been eroded into its final, most stable geometry.

The terrain is a basin of near-perfect flatness, broken only by the **Built**.

Rising from the chalky floor are the remains of a city that was never grown, but carved. The structures are massive, blocky monoliths of pitted limestone and grit-blasted basalt. They resemble a cross between a necropolis and a salt-mine settlement—low-slung, thick-walled, and interconnected by wide, paved plazas that have been worn down until the stones are as smooth as glass. There is no wood here, no metal, no plastic. Everything is heavy stone, stacked with a primitive but obsessive precision. The architecture suggests a civilization that understood gravity and time better than it understood comfort.

It is a world of **mineral stillness** and **chaotic motion**.

There are no plants. Not a single blade of moss or lick of lichen softens the hard angles of the stone. Instead, the biological niche is filled by an abundance of **lithic fauna**. Small, beetle-like creatures with shells of translucent mica scuttle in massive, flowing drifts across the plazas. They move like liquid, millions of them forming shimmering ribbons that change direction in a sudden, panicked synchronicity. In the shadows of the monoliths, slow-moving, soft-bodied slugs, the color of wet slate, leave trails of phosphorescent slime that provide the only moisture in the environment.

**The Water** is a rare, rhythmic haunting. Every few hours, the atmosphere—which is cool and smells of ozone and crushed rock—reaches a tipping point. The ceiling "perspires." Great, heavy droplets of condensation detach from the glowing phosphor-veins and fall hundreds of meters. They do not fall as rain, but as individual, heavy missiles. When they hit the stone floor, they shatter into fine mist, creating localized pockets of dampness. For a few minutes, the thirsty stone darkens to a deep charcoal, and the fauna erupts in a feeding frenzy, licking the moisture from the pores of the rock before it evaporates back into the thirsty air.

**The Dynamic** is one of constant, "anthill" turbulence. While the stones are ancient and the light is frozen, the inhabitants—both the swarming fauna and the silent, robed figures that move among the monoliths—are in a state of high-energy agitation. The "people" here use tools of sharpened flint and heavy bone. They do not speak; the only sound is the dry *chitinous* click of the mica-beetles and the occasional, distant *thud* of a water droplet hitting the earth. Despite the silence, the city is a bazaar of frantic, primitive industry. Figures are constantly dragging heavy stone sleds, rearranging piles of salt, or carving grooves into the floor to direct the falling water. It is a masterpiece of busy-work, a settlement functioning at the speed of a storm but with the technology of the stone age.

The erosion here is the most profound law of the land. This place is at the end of its history. Every corner has been rounded by the passage of billions of feet; every pillar is tapering toward a needle-point from the constant scouring of dust-laden drafts. It is a world that has settled into its final form—a bright, cold, busy warehouse of stone, where life persists not by growing, but by recycling the ancient dust of its own foundations. 

You stand in the center of a plaza that stretches toward a horizon of hazy, white light. Around you, the air is thick with the frantic, silent movement of a million living things, all scraping out an existence in a city that was old before the first sun ever rose above the surface. It is bright, it is cold, and it is endlessly, restlessy alive.
