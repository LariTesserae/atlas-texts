Beneath the basalt crust of a dying world lies the **Sub-Atrium of Oura-Kahn**, a city-sized machine-hollow that feels less like a dwelling and more like the inside of a gargantuan, warm lung. 

### The Terrain and Architecture
The geography is a "sunken urbanism." The floor of this massive cavern is a flat, dark expanse of obsidian-slick composite, but the city does not grow upward from the ground. Instead, it hangs—a forest of inverted skyscrapers, suspended from the cavern ceiling by gossamer-thin filaments of high-tensile vibration-glass. These structures are millions of years old, weathered by a subterranean atmosphere until their hard edges have become rounded, tactile, and bone-like. The erosion here is not from wind or water, but from the relentless friction of sound and the slow molecular migration of the building materials themselves.

The scale is paradoxical: the cavern is vast and empty, a near-vacuum of physical matter, yet where the "buildings" cluster, the density of ancient, post-physical engineering is overwhelming. These structures do not use wires or pipes; they are composed of programmable matter that shimmers with a dull, internal warmth.

### Atmosphere and Water
It is hot—a thick, humid 35°C (95°F) generated by the city’s core scavengers. Water is a precious, haunting presence. It does not fall as rain but manifests as a heavy, pervasive "bloom" of condensation that clings to the undersides of the hanging towers. Every few hours, the thermal gradients shift, and these heavy droplets release all at once, creating a "vertical dew" that streaks through the darkness like falling mercury before vanishing into porous drains in the floor. The air smells of ozone, wet copper, and old, recycled yeast.

### Light and Sound
The world is perpetually dark, a 0.3 on the scale of visibility. There is no sun, and no artificial luminescence beyond the dim, rhythmic pulse of amber navigation nodes. Residents and machines see via sonoluminescence and haptic resonance. 

Because light is absent, sound is the sovereign ruler. The Sub-Atrium is *loud*. It is a roar composed of a thousand mechanical processes: the deep, subsonic thrum of the ceiling-anchors, the shrieking whistles of heat-exchange vents, and the rhythmic *clack-hiss* of the condensation cycle. The city is an orchestra of heavy industry that has forgotten its purpose, a cacophony that travelers feel in their marrow rather than hear with their ears.

### Ecology and Fauna
Vegetation is a desperate, sparse afterthought. Pale, translucent mosses—engineered to thrive on vibrational energy—cling to the "roots" of the hanging buildings. They don't have leaves; they have fine, hair-like cilia that twitch in rhythm with the city’s hum. 

Fauna is rare and unsettling. The most common residents are the *Loom-Strider*—delicate, multi-legged maintenance drones the size of greyhounds, made of the same weathered composite as the buildings. They move with a biological fluidity, scavenging stray minerals from the dew-fall. Occasionally, one might spot a *Vapor-Ray*, a leathery, flat creature that glides on the thermal updrafts created by the city’s heat, filtering microscopic bio-matter from the humid air. They are silent, ghostly witnesses to the mechanical roar.

### The Rules of the World
In Oura-Kahn, gravity is a suggestion, and sound is the only ground. The inhabitants (if they still exist in a form recognizable as human) likely communicate through direct neural-link or tactile vibration, as the air is too thick with noise for speech. 

The "Beyond Modern" tech manifests in the city’s self-repair. If a tower is damaged, it does not fall; it simply reconfigures its molecular density, "healing" the crack over centuries of slow, agonizing movement. The city is active—alive in a way that suggests it has outlived its creators and is now simply maintaining its own heartbeat, waiting for a command that was sent from the surface ten thousand years ago and has yet to arrive through the miles of rock above. 

It is a place of heavy, warm emptiness—a cathedral of industry vibrating in the dark.
