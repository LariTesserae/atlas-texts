This is the **Latticework of the Ascended High-Plains**.

The world here does not sit upon stone or earth. It is a total construction, an infinite, interconnected scaffold of galvanized trusses and riveted plating that spans the horizon at a dizzying elevation. There is no "ground," only layers of floor-grating and catwalks that descend into a hazy, industrial abyss far below. 

The air is cool and thin, tasting of ozone and wet iron.

### The Material and the Build
Everything is made. Even the weather is an industrial byproduct. The landscape is a dense, high-saturation thicket of vertical pipes, cooling towers that do not smoke, and massive, immobile piston-arms frozen in mid-stroke. The architecture is "young"—not because it was recently built, but because the materials refuse to age. The steel is sharp, the edges of the girders are crisp as glass, and the rivets are polished to a mirror sheen. There is no rust, only a faint, persistent dampness. 

Because the density is so high, one cannot see the sun, yet the world is blindingly bright. Light emanates from the structural members themselves—luminescent phosphors embedded in the white-painted beams. The light is steady, sterile, and pervasive, casting almost no shadows because it comes from every direction at once.

### The Intermittent Damp
Water is a rare, curated commodity. It does not fall as rain from a sky; instead, it bleeds from the joints of massive overhead conduits. Every few miles, a "leak" has been sanctioned. In these pockets, a fine, cool mist saturates the air, slicking the metal floors and collecting in shallow, rectangular basins etched into the plates. These are the damp-zones, the riverine arteries of an otherwise dry, metallic desert.

### The Sparse Green and the Abundant Life
Vegetation is an afterthought of the machine, or perhaps a stubborn intruder. Sparse, pale-blue moss clings to the shaded undersides of the walk-ways, and spindly, translucent ferns sprout from the seams of the pressure-valves. There are no trees, only "vent-vines" that mimic the shape of weeping willows, their silver-leafed fronds shivering in the updrafts from the lower levels.

Despite the lack of greenery, the world teems with fauna. These are creatures of the gear and the wire. Thousands of small, multi-legged brass "scuttlers" move across the trusses with the fluid grace of crabs, their carapaces reflecting the ambient glare. Large, slow-moving avian constructs with wings of gossamer mesh glide between the towers, their migrations dictated by the slow cooling cycles of the massive engines. They do not sing. They click, a rhythmic, metronomic sound that punctuate the quiet.

### The Quiet and the Slow
The soundscape is hushed. The great industrial engines of this world operate with a terrifying, frictionless silence. There is no roar of combustion, only a deep, sub-audible thrum that one feels in the marrow of their bones rather than hears. The dynamic of the place is glacial. A massive crane may take a century to rotate forty-five degrees; a piston may take a decade to complete a single compression. To an observer, the world appears static, a photograph of an industrial revolution caught in amber.

### The Breakdown of Reality
As one moves deeper into the High-Plains, the "weirdness" of the construction begins to manifest. The laws of Euclidean space serve only as suggestions. You may walk a straight line across a bridge only to find yourself standing on the ceiling of the walkway you just departed, your internal gravity adjusting so slowly you barely feel the shift. 

In some sectors, the "total build" begins to fold in on itself. A row of identical chimneys might stretch upward, only to terminate in a horizontal floor that belongs to a different district. You might look through a gap in the floorboards and see not a lower level, but a distant, miniature version of the very plaza you are standing on. The world is a recursive, industrial fractal. 

Time, too, behaves strangely here. Because the light never changes and the machines move so slowly, the passage of hours is measured only by the accumulation of condensation on a particular bolt. If you sit still long enough, you might see a "glitch"—a piece of the architecture flickering out of existence for a microsecond, revealing a white void beneath, before the reality-engine reasserts itself and the steel snaps back into place.

This is a world that is finished, yet dreaming; a silent, bright laboratory of metal and mist where the only rule is the integrity of the Lattice.
