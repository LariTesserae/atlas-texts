This is the interior of a **Thermal Cyst**, a pocket of hyper-pressurized reality suspended high within a vertical rift of raw, unweathered mineral. It is a place that is still being born, where geography has not yet learned how to crumble.

### The Terrain of the Unproven
The ground is not soil, nor is it sand. It is a singular, continuous slab of obsidian-glass, so fresh from the womb of the planetary core that it has no cracks, no dust, and no footprints. Because there is no water to carve valleys and no wind to smooth the jagged edges, the landscape is a series of razor-sharp, geometric shelves. These shelves rise into the high altitudes of the rift, suspended in a shimmering heat-haze. The lack of erosion makes the world feel unfinished, like a wireframe model that has been filled with solid black ink but not yet textured by time.

### The Atmosphere of the Dry Engine
The air is bone-dry and searing, a punishing 2.7 on the thermal scale. It smells of ozone and scorched sugar. There is no weather here—no clouds, no rain, no mist. Instead, the "sky" is merely a higher ceiling of the same unyielding mineral, glowing with a steady, artificial luminescence.

Despite the lack of water, the world is lush. This is the central paradox of the Cyst. Vegetation does not grow from the ground; it grows from the *heat*. 

### The Xylo-Industrial Flora
The "plants" are stiff, metallic structures composed of silicon-carbide and copper filaments. They resemble oversized clockwork ferns or brass-petaled lotuses, standing ten feet tall but spaced widely apart across the black glass. They are sparse, leaving vast gulfs of empty, shimmering air between them. 

These plants are not biological; they are industrial. They are living machines, self-assembling engines of growth. As you watch, a "flower" may slowly rotate with the heavy, grinding rhythm of a rusted turbine. They hum with a low-frequency electrical charge, drawing energy directly from the thermal gradients of the rift. They do not photosynthesize; they *manufacture*. High-tension wires of organic copper stretch between distant stalks, vibrating slightly in the heat, creating a web of power-transfer that serves as the ecosystem’s nervous system.

### The Rules of the Breakdown
Reality here is fragile. At this height and temperature, the laws of physics have begun to fray. In the pockets of empty space between the metallic groves, the perspective warps. You might look at a copper-fern that appears a mile away, only to reach out and find your fingers brushing its hot, oily surface. 

Gravity is directional but inconsistent. A dropped object might fall toward the nearest "plant" rather than the ground, or it might drift upward until it hits a thermal pocket and burns into a cinder. Time moves with an agonizing, heavy viscosity. The mechanical plants cycle their brass petals perhaps once every century. There is no death here, only a slow, metallic seizure as a plant eventually welds itself shut and becomes a permanent statue of solidified tech.

### Sound and Silence
The world is nearly silent, but the silence is heavy. It is the silence of a factory that hasn't started its shift yet. Occasionally, there is a sharp *crack*—the sound of a mineral shelf expanding in the heat—or the rhythmic, mournful *thrum-thrum* of the copper-web carrying a surge of electricity. There are no birds, no insects, no scuttling things. The only "life" is the slow, grinding evolution of the machine-forest.

### The Hidden Scale
This entire environment—with its soaring obsidian cliffs and its vast, sparse industrial groves—is contained. If you were to walk far enough in any direction, you would not find an ocean or a desert. You would find the **Join**: the place where the black glass floor curves upward to become the wall, and then the ceiling. This is a pocket-dimension, a pressurized pearl of industrial evolution tucked into the folds of a larger, dead universe. It is a secret hothouse where technology is being grown like fruit, in a world that has forgotten what a drop of water looks like.
