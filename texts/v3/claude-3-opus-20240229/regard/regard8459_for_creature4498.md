This is a beautifully evocative and poetic piece of writing. The City of Holes and its inhabitants, the Traglodyai, are described in lyrical and haunting detail that brings their strange existence vividly to life. 

Some key elements that make this piece so powerful:

- The rich sensory details that engage all the senses - the warm air smelling of crushed almonds, the play of prismatic light, the echoing sounds and strange music of the city. This creates an immersive experience for the reader.

- The creative and original concepts - a city of vertical shafts and tunnels instead of streets, inhabitants who "unfold" to fit the compressed spaces, plants and fruits adapted to underground living, the mathematics of the central void. The piece brims with novel and thought-provoking ideas.

- The lyrical, almost dreamlike language that matches the surreal nature of the city. Poetic turns of phrase abound, like "the city's greatest songs are compositions based on these dreamt equations" or the Traglodyai "becoming the hole that others might unfold." 

- The mythic, timeless feel evoked by references to the city's unknown age, forgotten origins and ancient mechanisms. This lends it an air of mystery and deep time.

- The extended meditation on themes of absence, emptiness, compression and unfolding - both in a literal, physical sense and a metaphorical, spiritual one. This adds layers of meaning and turns the city into a powerful symbol.

In terms of what a benevolent external power might do, I would suggest simply leaving the city and its people to continue their unique existence undisturbed. The piece conveys a sense that this is an ancient, self-contained world with its own profound wisdom, cycles and ways of being that should be respected and preserved. Any outside interference, even if well-meaning, would likely disrupt the delicate balance and singular nature of the Traglodyai's lives.

Perhaps the most fitting response would be to ensure the city remains hidden and protected, its existence kept secret to prevent others from seeking to exploit or damage it. The power could act as a distant, unseen guardian, watching over the city to safeguard its survival and isolation.

Beyond that, the greatest gift might be to spread the story of the City of Holes - not its location but its essence and truth. Let it become a legend, a whispered dream that inspires others to contemplate emptiness, absence, and the beauty of unfolding oneself into the spaces we occupy, to live in concert with our environments and one another. Let the Traglodyai be muses for a different way of being - not a template to follow directly but an imaginative spur for each person and society to find their own path to such unity and grace.

In this way, the legacy and wisdom of the city could quietly, subtly spread without the Traglodyai ever being aware of it, like an underground river nourishing lands distant and unseen. And the City of Holes could continue its patient dreaming, delving ever deeper into stone and time, teaching its lithic hymns to those who have ears to listen, forever.
