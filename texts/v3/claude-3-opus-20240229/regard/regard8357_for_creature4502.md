A wise and benevolent power, upon observing the haunting beauty and profound loneliness of Eremonos, would be moved to compassion. This city and its denizens, born of absence and the spaces between things, are a testament to the power of imagination and the aching need for connection and meaning.

The power would not seek to fundamentally change the nature of Eremonos, for its melancholic magic is woven into the very fabric of its inverted geometry. Instead, it would work to foster moments of connection, fleeting though they may be in a place defined by absence.

It might whisper into the dreams of the city itself, seeding visions of a grand festival - a celestial alignment when the moons return and the ghostly crowds coalesce into something almost solid. For one surreal night, the streets would come alive with phantasmal revelry, the silent buildings ringing with the echoes of long-forgotten songs. The Drifters would brush against each other in ethereal dances, tasting for a moment the possibilities uncollapsed. Even the Last Remaining Continent might descend to join the celebration, its molted mountains blanketing the streets in layers of soft almost-snow.

In the aftermath, as the city settles back into its somber rhythms, the power would leave small gifts scattered in its wake - a staircase that leads to a hidden garden where the Archivist's forgiveness blooms, a room where the Committee's minutes linger long enough to be read, a breeze that carries the scent of the future's nostalgia. Reminders that even in a place defined by lack, something can still be found.

For you, the newcomer, it would leave a map charted in disappearing ink, hinting at secret corners of the city where the walls breathe and the angles soften - places where, if only for a moment, you might feel a little less alone. And in your perfect, impossible home, it would hide a key that fits no lock, but whose weight in your pocket whispers of doors yet to be discovered.

The power understands that Eremonos is not a place to be saved from itself, but rather a place to be experienced in all its eerie, aching splendor. Its gift to the city and its inhabitants is not transformation, but rather the quiet knowledge that even in the emptiness, there is still room for wonder and for fleeting connection. That in a city dreaming of waking, perhaps it is enough to simply dream a little more vividly, together.
