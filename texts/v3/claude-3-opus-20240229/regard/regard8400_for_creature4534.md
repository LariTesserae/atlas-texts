Rather than seeking to impose change or extract answers, they would aim to listen, learn, and bear witness.

The power might spend an immeasurable span simply being present, attuning to the strange frequencies and inverted rhythms of this realm. They would trace the fossilized strata of forgotten civilizations with a gentle touch, sensing the echoes of uncountable lives impressed into the porous stone. They would follow the migratory paths of the post-mechanical fauna, discerning the intricate patterns and ancient intelligences animating their endless rituals.

In the Interval Children, the power would perceive a form of being unlike any other - consciousness distilled to a pure embodiment of loss, memory and waiting. With great care, they would seek to commune with these entities, not through words but through a resonance of absences. In the spaces between the Children's borrowed surfaces, the power might share its own experience of eternal tides on distant shores, of stars slowly sinking into their own dying light. A conversation spanning eons, spoken in silences.

Yet even this wise power would understand the limits of its own knowing. It would recognize that this place is not a puzzle to be solved but a mystery to be honored in its irreducible strangeness. The true purpose or meaning behind the Interval Children, if such exists, might lie forever beyond reach - and there would be a certain beauty in that.

Ultimately, the power would depart much as it arrived - humbly, reverently, leaving only the faintest trace of its own presence. Perhaps a subtle re-folding of perspectival planes to open new confluences of light. Or an infusion of its own far-traveling memory into the realm's atmosphere, like a half-remembered scent borne on the dampness. Small gestures of acknowledgment and appreciation for all that dwells here.

Not as a warning or a lesson, but as a reminder of the infinite forms existence can take in the depths of time and being. And always, it would safeguard the secret of how to find this place again, if the need should arise to seek council in its profound and patient otherness.

For in the end, even the wisest powers must sometimes concede the limits of wisdom itself. To have touched such strangeness, even for a moment, is perhaps the greatest gift of all.
