What a hauntingly beautiful and profound vision. The Old Layer feels like a liminal space for wandering souls, a purgatory of sorts where time, memory, and identity blur. Those who linger there are suspended between worlds and states of being, navigating an alien landscape that seems to reflect the intangible essence of their journeys more than any physical reality.

If I were a benevolent and wise power observing this place and its "Continued" inhabitants, I believe my instinct would be to provide some form of succor or guidance, but in a way that respects the metaphysical nature of their experience. Rather than dramatic intervention, I would aim to work within the dreamlike logic of the Old Layer itself.

Perhaps I would manifest as a new kind of structure that occasionally appears on the plateau - something akin to a temporary shelter or way station. It could take the form of a shimmering dome or pavilion woven from strands of starlight, with archways always facing the prevailing wind. The Continued would be gently drawn to this place when they feel lost or in need of rest.

Inside, they would find ethereal comforts - pools of pure cleansing radiance to bathe their weary forms, orbs of soothing music that revive faded memories of love and belonging, flowing banners of poetry in forgotten tongues that still communicate wordless understanding and peace. Shadows would regain their strength here, re-merging with their owners. Windflowers would bloom in symphonies of color revealing hints of ultimate enlightenment.

Most importantly, I would imbue this space with a subtle beacon of true direction amidst the disorientation - not a blaring signpost to the final exit, but a faint lifeline for those ready to move on from their long limbo. Somehow, the light and music and poetry would weave together to whisper the way onward for each soul in their own private metaphor - the final note in a half-remembered song, a secret phrase that unlocks the heart, a shimmer at the edge of sight leading to a door only they can see.

Not all would perceive or heed this calling, and that would be their choice. This sanctuary would be a momentary grace, not a cage or demand. But my hope would be that over uncounted ages, the Continued would find their way here in ones and twos and small groups, taking comfort and counsel with each other, until they each in their own time recognize the road and step forward into the unknown with newfound wholeness and purpose.

Thus the Old Layer would remain both journey and destination, dream and lucidity, exile and exodus. And those who finally pass beyond might carry some of its copper melancholy and weathered wisdom with them, an echo of eternity to light their way through whatever worlds await. Though in truth, perhaps the Object and its plateaus and all who walk there are simply thoughts in the mind of a vaster dreaming, and to awaken is to arrive where we have always been, knowing it for the first time.
