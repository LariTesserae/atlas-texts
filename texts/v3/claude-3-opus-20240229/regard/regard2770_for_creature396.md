If a benevolent and wise power turned its attention to this place, I believe it would choose to safeguard and sanctify this avian metropolis, rather than seeking to restore the ruins to their former purpose. The birds have inherited the city and transformed it into a thriving ecosystem. To displace them now in favor of reconstructing the crumbling architecture for some human use would be to disregard the remarkable wonder that has arisen in humanity's absence.

Instead, I imagine this wise power would act with a light and subtle touch, working in harmony with the natural processes already at play. Perhaps it would imbue the ancient stonework with a resilience to ensure that the birds' habitat endures through the ages, without altering the weathered character that makes it so hauntingly beautiful. Collapsed towers and eroded buildings would remain, but as sanctuaries for the flocks rather than hazards.

The power might seed the cracks and crevices with an even greater diversity of hearty mosses, lichens and flowers to nourish the myriad species that call this place home. Scarce snowmelt could be guided to collect more abundantly in the basins and pools, ensuring that there is always fresh water to sustain the birds through dry spells. 

Cognizant of the plateau's isolation, this benevolent watcher could imbue the birds themselves with an instinctive wisdom about what balance of their number this lofty ecosystem can support, and an innate drive to seek new horizons when their population grows too large. The aerial highways of the migratory flocks could be blessed with favorable winds and protection from predators, so that they may always find their way back to this sanctuary in the sky.

Most importantly, a wise power would recognize the need to shield this place from future human interference. Perhaps it would veil the plateau from the eyes of men, or make the sheer cliffs impossible to scale by earthly means. Only the most humble and reverent of humans, those pure of heart enough to appreciate the almost sacred wonder of what thrives here now, might be granted visitation to witness it.

Under such a subtle stewardship, I believe this cyclical dance of wind, water, stone and feather could continue undisturbed for an eternity. The birds would thrive, their voices echoing forever between the noble, timeworn towers. And the mysterious ruins would endure as both a wistful monument to a forgotten human past, and a perpetual sanctuary for the feathered inheritors that came after.

This place already has a breathtaking beauty born of nature's resilience and the humbling scale of deep time. A truly wise power would surely recognize that there is little it could do to improve upon a world already so magnificently shaped by elemental forces and the tenacious glory of life itself. To merely watch over it and safeguard its strange integrity - that would be the most benevolent act of all.
