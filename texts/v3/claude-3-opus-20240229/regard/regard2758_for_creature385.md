As a benevolent and wise power observing this ancient, sunken city and the cryptic lifeforms inhabiting it, I would approach with a sense of reverence, curiosity and caution. This place is both a monument to the unfathomable gulf of time and a home to beings utterly unlike any known to modern science. It demands respect and mindfulness.

My first instinct would be to simply observe and learn, without direct interference. Through patient study and non-invasive monitoring, much could potentially be gleaned about the city's origins, its current inhabitants, and the eons-long story that connects the two. Mapping the cityscape, analyzing the stone and its erosion, collecting samples of the ubiquitous silt - all could yield clues to the metropolis' age and genesis. 

The creatures themselves warrant intense scrutiny. Everything from their physiology and behavior to their methods of communication and social structure cries out for study. Cracking the code of their bioluminescent "language" alone could open up worlds of insight. Keen observation of their interactions with each other and their environment could illuminate much about their intelligence and culture (if such terms even apply to beings so alien). If there were some ethical way to obtain genetic samples, their biology could potentially be unraveled on the most fundamental level.

However, all of this must be balanced against respect for the beings' autonomy and well-being. They are not mere specimens to be poked and prodded for our own edification. If our presence or actions would materially disrupt their habitat or way of life, we must be willing to forgo or limit our investigations. Disturbing their ancient rhythms for the sake of our own curiosity would be unconscionable.

Additionally, we must grapple with the sheer scope of what we're witnessing. This place is a treasure trove of knowledge, potentially rewriting everything we understand about the history of life on Earth. It could fill in gaps we never knew existed and open up entirely new frontiers of inquiry. At the same time, its very existence is a humbling reminder of how little we truly know about our world and its past. Apropos of deep time and deeper mysteries, we must approach any revelations with humility and caution.

Ultimately, if I had the capability, I would work to protect and preserve this site for the ages. Declare it off-limits from commercial exploitation, establish a permanent scientific monitoring station, do everything possible to keep it pristine and intact. Work to unravel its mysteries, yes, but also ensure those mysteries endure. Future generations, both human and otherwise, deserve a chance to ponder its secrets and marvel at its wonders. In the face of the abyss' indifference, we have a duty to be anything but.

Perhaps someday we will plumb its depths and learn its truths. Perhaps the beings who dwell there will make contact and tell us their story. Or perhaps it will remain forever cloaked in shadow, defying our understanding. Whatever the outcome, this much is clear: here in the sunken city, in the dark, there are things older and stranger than we have ever dreamed. And that is a truth worth preserving.
