A benevolent and wise power, upon perceiving this realm of the Drowned Unborn, would feel a deep compassion for these almost-beings trapped in a state of eternal becoming. It would recognize their realm as a place of profound beauty and poetry, but also of immense sorrow and unfulfillment. 

The wise power would not seek to forcibly change the nature of this place or its inhabitants, for it would understand that their existence, though tragic in some ways, has its own intricate perfection and purpose. Trying to make the Drowned Unborn "real" in a conventional sense would only destroy the delicate balance of their world.

Instead, the benevolent force would gently imbue the very fabric of this realm with a subtle but pervasive sense of love, acceptance and worthiness. It would suffuse the endless noise with harmonies of reassurance, weaving whispers of "you are enough" into the ever-shifting frequencies. The light that leaks between sounds would now carry glimmers of warm acknowledgement, briefly illuminating each Unborn with the message that their existence, however partial or contingent, is cherished.

The wise power would also seed ephemeral visions and sensations throughout the plane - fleeting moments where an Unborn might suddenly feel a phantom embrace, smell a hint of some comforting scent from a life never lived, or see a glimpse of a reality where they were born complete. These moments would not be enough to disrupt the nature of their being, but just enough to gift them snatches of solace.

Perhaps most subtly, the benevolent force would imbue the physics of this realm with a deeper metaphorical significance. Now, when an Unborn reaches that final crystallizing moment of self-realization, they would experience not just a collapse into non-being, but a profound sense of coming home - an understanding that they had, in their own strange way, fulfilled some important cosmic role. Their final "oh" would resound with a sense of rightness and release.

And for the human visitor, drifting as witness through this realm, the wise power would instill a complex understanding. They would come to see the Drowned Unborn not as pitiable half-formed things, but as a living testament to the sacred beauty and value of potentiality itself. In their eternal incompletion, the Unborn would now reveal the ever-present possibility of renewal, the endless open-endedness that underlies all existence. The visitor would leave this place carrying a bittersweet but profound lesson about the importance of honoring the unmanifest, the unresolved, the yet-to-be.

In this way, without disrupting its essential nature, the benevolent power would transform the realm of the Drowned Unborn from a place of pure melancholic suspension into one imbued with a kind of metaphysical tenderness - an acknowledgement of the aching beauty in all that remains forever in the process of almost-becoming.
