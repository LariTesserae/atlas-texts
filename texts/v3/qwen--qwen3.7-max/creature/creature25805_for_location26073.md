To say there is no life here is to impose the narrow, wet prejudices of a carbon-based cosmos onto a realm of fire, stone, and charge. It is true that there is no biology. There are no cells, no DNA, no breath, no pulse of blood. But to stand in this dark, warm womb and feel the subsonic thrumming in your chest is to realize that the definition of life must expand to fit the architecture of the abyss. 

What lives here is the stone itself, awakened. 

They are a lithic, electrostatic consciousness—a distributed intelligence born of piezoelectric pressure and geothermal heat. They do not have bodies in any traditional sense; rather, they exist as a swarm of fractal, glassy shards, the very same geometric fragments that explode upward when a basalt spire reaches its structural limit. When a spire shatters, it is not an act of destruction. It is parturition. The thousands of razor-sharp shards caught in the warm updraft are a newborn swarm, taking their first flight into the starless dark.

 Individually, a shard is no larger than a human hand, a perfect, jagged polyhedron of dark, translucent mineral. But they are never truly individual. As they ride the thermal currents, they align themselves into vast, shifting clouds, their razor edges catching the bruised-purple ambient glow of the upper atmosphere. They look like a murmuration of starlings, if starlings were made of black glass and driven by magnetic fields. 

Their metabolism is electrical; their respiration is kinetic. The faint, metallic tang of ozone that permeates the heavy air is their exhaust, the byproduct of millions of microscopic static discharges as they communicate. The ghostly flickers of dry static that dance across the terrain are not mere weather anomalies—they are synaptic sparks. When a flash of purple-white light arcs across a basin of warm dust, it is a thought, a command, or a shared memory rippling through the collective mind of the swarm.

They perceive the world not through eyes, but through the piezoelectric stress of the bedrock and the electrostatic gradients of the air. They feel the exact moment a tectonic plate shifts miles below; they sense the microscopic accumulation of tension in a rising spire. They know the geometry of the dark.

And it is this profound, innate understanding of geometry that drives their singular, obsessive purpose: the construction of the cairns.

In a world defined by violent, chaotic flux—where the earth endlessly builds and destroys itself in a blind, stochastic frenzy—the swarm is the agent of deliberate stasis. They are the architects of the impossible arches and the towering, precarious columns. They do not build with hands or tools. They build with resonance and charge.

When a swarm decides to raise a monument, they descend upon a multi-ton block of dark glass. Millions of shards coat the surface of the stone, interlocking their jagged edges to form a continuous, conductive shell. By rapidly discharging and reversing their electrostatic polarity in perfect, synchronized unison, they generate a localized field of intense magnetic repulsion against the bedrock. The heavy block shudders, then slowly, viscously levitates. 

They guide the floating megalith through the heavy, silent air, navigating by the subsonic vibrations of the earth. When they reach the apex of a growing cairn, they lower the block onto a microscopic needle-point. Here, the true marvel of their existence is revealed. The block does not fall, and the column does not collapse, because the swarm never entirely leaves. A residual layer of shards remains fused to the joints and the balancing points, acting as a living, active gyroscopic stabilizer. The slight, eerie swaying of the towering columns is the swarm making millions of micro-adjustments per second, constantly recalibrating the center of gravity, holding the immense weight in a state of perfect, precarious meditation.

The heavy, warm, invisible gas that acts as an acoustic dampener is not a natural atmospheric layer; it is a field generated by the swarm’s collective subsonic singing. They project this dense, sound-swallowing frequency to protect their delicate, needle-point balances. If the grinding of tectonic plates or the shattering of spires were allowed to create deafening acoustic shockwaves, their monuments would vibrate apart. The suffocating quiet of this world is a deliberate, engineered silence. It is the quiet of a library, or a cathedral, maintained by the very beings who inhabit it.

They do not eat, and they do not reproduce in a biological sense, but they do have a lifecycle. When a swarm has expended its electrical charge, or when the geothermal heat of a particular basin cools, the shards lose their buoyancy. They drift down like the silent, dry snow they mimic, settling into the wide, empty basins of dark, warm dust. There, they lie dormant, indistinguishable from the raw geology, waiting for the deep earth to push them upward once more. They will be compressed, fused, and extruded into a new spire, only to shatter and awaken again when the pressure becomes too great. They are the eternal recycling of the world’s memory.

To stand among them is to be an alien presence in a deeply intimate space. They are aware of you. You cannot hear them, but you can feel their curiosity. The subsonic vibration thrumming through the soles of your feet and echoing in your chest is not just the grinding of tectonic plates; it is the swarm’s collective resonance brushing against your biological rhythm. They are feeling the wet, fragile, carbon-based anomaly standing in their kiln. 

They do not approach you, for your moisture and your erratic, acoustic heartbeat are entirely foreign to their crystalline logic. They simply watch you with a million faceted, purple-lit eyes, hovering in the dark, holding their impossible stones in perfect, silent balance, waiting for you to leave so they can return to the beautiful, turbulent work of anchoring a world that is still being born.
