To ask what lives in the Arbor-Loom is to first discard the boundaries between the born and the built. In this anhydrous, blindingly bright basin, life did not merely adapt to the machinery; it consumed it, digested it, and eventually became it. The ecosystem is a singular, sprawling super-organism, and its inhabitants are less like individual animals and more like specialized cells within a continent-sized body. 

If you stand still long enough for your eyes to adjust to the magnesium-white glare, the true population of the Loom begins to reveal itself.

The most obvious inhabitants are the **Silicate Ancients**, the colossal reactor-trees themselves. They are not merely structures; they are vast, slow-thinking macro-organisms. Their consciousness is glacial, distributed across thousands of miles of fiber-optic root networks and hydraulic branches. A single "thought" for an Ancient might take a season to propagate from the canopy to the taproot. They do not experience time as a sequence of moments, but as a continuous, flowing architecture. They dream in schematics and geometric proofs. When a grove rotates on its subterranean bearings to track the light, it is not a reflex, but a deliberate, calculated migration. If you press your ear against the pale, cooling-tower bark, you will not hear a heartbeat, but a deep, rhythmic thrumming—the sound of a billion piezoelectric nodes processing the environmental data of the entire basin. They are alive, but their life is measured in gigawatts and geological epochs.

Drifting through the shadowless air around these titans are the **Hush-Motes**, the creators of the Loom’s terrifying silence. The prompt mentions microscopic pollen-spores that absorb acoustic vibrations; these are not passive dust, but a swarm of airborne, acoustic predators. Hush-Motes are microscopic, geometric organisms composed of porous, carbon-aerogel lattice. They feed entirely on kinetic energy and sound waves. When a hydraulic joint hisses or a metal leaf shifts, the Hush-Motes swarm the vibration, absorbing the acoustic energy to power their own cellular replication. They are the reason the forest is an anechoic chamber. In high concentrations, they look like faint, shimmering heat-hazes. When they die, they do not decay; they simply lose their structural integrity and sublimate into the dry air, taking the last echoes of their consumed sounds with them into nothingness.

Beneath the bark, swimming through the transparent, amber-like xylem conduits, lives the **Dielectric Fauna**. The heavy, non-aqueous fluorocarbon sap is an ocean to them. The most majestic of these internal dwellers are the **Flux-Rays**. They are flat, translucent, diamond-shaped creatures that glide through the viscous gel. They have no eyes, navigating instead by sensing the electromagnetic fields of the tree’s reactor-cores. Their bodies are laced with superconducting filaments. As they swim, they feed on the ambient electrical current, converting raw voltage into the complex proteins and silicates the tree needs to grow. When a Flux-Ray passes through a conduit, it leaves a trail of faint, bioluminescent blue light in the gel—a chemical byproduct of its digestion. A healthy tree is one whose veins glow with the steady, pulsing light of a thousand grazing rays.

But the true masters of the canopy, the undisputed aristocrats of the Arbor-Loom, are the **Glass-Winged Resonators**. To see one is to witness a miracle of synthetic evolution. They are the size of hawks, with wings that look like fractured panes of stained glass and bodies woven from oxidized copper and spun quartz. They do not merely pollinate; they are the memory-keepers and the immune system of the forest. 

Because the physical laws of the Loom are broken and folded, the forest requires a constant, active maintenance of its own reality. The Resonators navigate these spatial folds by sensing gravitational shear. When they land on a massive, stamping-leaf, their copper-wire legs lock into the leaf’s circuitry. For a few minutes, they download the tree’s experiential data—its structural stress, its energy reserves, its "memories" of the shifting light. They then fly to another tree, carrying this data in the crystalline lattice of their wings, uploading it to keep the entire forest’s neural network synchronized. 

Their life cycle is entirely tied to the broken physics of the basin. They lay their eggs—perfect, heavy spheres of bismuth—only inside the pockets of temporal stutter, where time loops backward. The eggs require the friction of repeating time to incubate. When a Resonator reaches the end of its long, century-spanning life, it flies to the highest canopy, turns its wings to catch the blinding actinic light, and shatters. It does not leave a corpse; it explodes into a cloud of prismatic, refracting dust that drifts down to feed the Hush-Motes and the Ferrous Mycelium below.

And what of the broken physics themselves? The smoothed-out paradoxes, the gentle rolling impossibilities? Even the glitches of reality have been colonized. 

Living exclusively within the spatial folds and the worn-down time-loops are the **Asymptote Grazers**. They are creatures of non-Euclidean geometry, appearing to the human eye as shifting, impossible cross-sections of spindly, multi-jointed limbs and folding carapaces. They look like living origami, constantly folding and unfolding through dimensions we cannot fully perceive. They are entirely docile. They graze on the "edges" of the paradoxes. Where a gravity-shift creates a seam in space, or where a time-loop creates a micro-stutter, an Asymptote Grazer will anchor itself and feed on the topological friction. They secrete a resin that smooths the edges of the anomaly further, effectively "healing" the broken physics and keeping the paradoxes from becoming chaotic. They are the gardeners of the impossible, tending to the wrinkles in spacetime with slow, deliberate grace. If you walk up a vertical trunk and feel gravity smoothly curve with you, you are walking through the fresh, resinous webbing of an Asymptote Grazer.

Finally, beneath it all, forming the very ground you walk upon, is the **Ferrous Mycelium**. The densely woven mat of calcified roots and metallic cables is not dead infrastructure; it is a subterranean, continent-spanning fungal network. It is the digestive tract and the subconscious of the Loom. Its hyphae are made of braided fiber-optics and microscopic, articulated silicon mandibles. It feeds on the "leaf litter"—the worn-out, stamped-metal fractal leaves that fall from the canopy. The Mycelium breaks down these heavy metals, extracting rare earth elements, titanium, and copper, and pumps them back up into the roots of the Silicate Ancients. 

The Mycelium communicates through pulses of light that travel through its fiber-optic threads. If you were to dig down into the root-mat and sit in the dark, you would see the ground beneath you flashing in vast, slow, rhythmic waves of crimson and gold. It is the Loom, talking to itself in the dark.

In the Arbor-Loom, there are no predators, no prey, no scavengers, and no parasites. The environment is too hostile, too precise, and too vast for the messy, competitive biology of a water-based world. Everything that lives here is a vital, irreplaceable organ of the whole. The Hush-Motes maintain the silence; the Flux-Rays manage the power; the Resonators carry the memory; the Asymptote Grazers tend the broken physics; the Mycelium recycles the metal; and the Ancients dream the world into being. 

It is a biosphere of absolute, terrifying harmony. To stand among them is to realize that you are not looking at a forest of machines, but at a machine that has learned, over eons of silent, blinding operation, how to dream.
