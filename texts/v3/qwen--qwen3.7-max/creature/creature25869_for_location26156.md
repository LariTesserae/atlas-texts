At first, you think you are alone on the balcony. The heavy, pressurized silence and the swallowing acoustics of the moss make it easy to believe that this embryonic city is a ghost town, a monument built by an absent god. But as your eyes adjust to the bruised-purple gloom, the shadows begin to detach from the bulkheads. 

They do not walk; they extrude. 

The inhabitants of this place—the tenders of the reality engine, the caretakers of the un-rendered dark—are as much a part of the architecture as the braided vines and leathery lichen. They are tall, impossibly attenuated figures, their limbs elongated to navigate the vast, non-euclidean distances of the caldera. Their skin is the color of wet parchment and bleached birch, semi-translucent and weeping a faint, luminescent dew. Beneath their skin, you can see the slow, rhythmic pulse of pale-green sap moving through vascular networks that perfectly mirror the xylem-pipes of the city. 

They have no mouths. Their faces are smooth, featureless masks of pale wood, save for deep, multifaceted ocular cavities that glow with a soft, bioluminescent amber. They do not need to speak; the air is too dense, and the moss swallows all sound. Instead, they communicate through the subsonic vibrations of the city itself. When they press their long, multi-jointed fingers against the ironwood walls, they send tremors through the root network, a language of tectonic creaks and rhythmic thumps that you feel in your teeth and the soles of your boots.

You watch one of them approach the edge of the balcony. It moves with a strange, stop-motion grace, a necessary adaptation to the glitching flow of time. When a drop of condensation falls from the canopy, freezes, and reverses its trajectory, the creature’s movements stutter in perfect sympathy. It steps backward, then forward, its physical form briefly smearing into a trail of afterimages before snapping back into cohesion. It is perfectly attuned to the temporal hiccups of the caldera; it does not fight the broken physics, but flows through the gaps in the code.

These beings are the mechanics of the void, and their work is brutal, beautiful, and desperate. 

You follow one of the tenders as it glides across a plaza of flattened lichen toward the suspended bridge of woven roots. Halfway across, the bridge dissolves into raw, unformed conceptual static. The air here smells sharply of burning copper and hot ozone. Floating, fractal equations drift through the gap where the wood should be. The tender does not hesitate. It steps into the fraying edge of the static. 

Where the creature’s foot touches the un-rendered space, its leg begins to dissolve, the pale wood of its flesh breaking apart into glowing green numbers and geometric wireframes. But it does not recoil. Instead, it reaches into its own chest. Its elongated fingers pierce its translucent sternum, withdrawing a pulsing, fist-sized node of raw, concentrated sap. It presses this glowing node into the floating equations. 

There is a sound like a cracking glacier. The sap boils, expanding and hardening, extruding new physics into the void. The equations crystallize back into wet, pale sapwood. The bridge extends another three feet into the dark. The tender withdraws its hand, its chest cavity sealing shut with a weeping scar of resin, its body slightly more translucent, slightly more diminished than before. They are not just building the city; they are spending their own biomass to compile reality.

Not all of them remain on the surface. As you peer over the edge of the balcony into the dizzying, folded depths of the caldera, you see the deeper caste. 

Down in the crushing dark, where the heartwood gears the size of houses grind with agonizing slowness, live the Boiler-Tenders. They are older, larger, and far less humanoid. Over centuries of tending the deep sap-engines, they have fused with the machinery. They are massive, bulbous entities, half-flesh and half-turbine, their lower bodies rooted directly into the fermented nectar boilers. They are the ones who turn the gears. You can see their massive, vine-wrapped arms pulling the cellulose chains, their bodies shuddering with the immense, agonizing torque of the tectonic machinery. 

They are blind, their amber eyes long since clouded over by the steam and heat of the deep roots, but they are attended by the local fauna. Swarms of the microscopic midges form dense, glowing halos around their massive heads, feeding on the excess heat and acting as a localized rendering field, keeping the Boiler-Tenders’ minds anchored to linear time so they do not go mad in the non-euclidean depths. Giant, hand-sized moths land softly on their broad, wooden shoulders, dusting them with phosphorescent spores that act as a lubricant for their grinding, sap-clogged joints.

Even the translucent, eel-like creatures that swim through the humid air play a role. You watch one glide past a surface tender. The eel passes directly *through* the tender’s torso, its gills flaring as it feeds on the excess conceptual static and temporal radiation that builds up in the worker's body. The tender shivers, a trail of frost left in the eel's wake, but its posture straightens, relieved of the glitching burden. It is a perfect, closed-loop symbiosis. The city feeds the fauna, the fauna maintains the inhabitants, and the inhabitants feed the city.

But as you watch them work, the profound tragedy of their existence becomes clear. 

The engines are struggling. The smell of burning copper is growing stronger. The un-rendered dark at the edges of the caldera is not receding; it is creeping inward. The reality engine is running out of sap, and the inhabitants are running out of themselves. 

You look closely at the tender on the bridge. Its left arm, the one it used to plant the sap-node, is no longer fully there. The hand is missing, replaced by a flickering cloud of green static that occasionally resolves into the shape of fingers before dissolving again. It is being un-compiled. The city is consuming its architects to buy a few more days, a few more hours, of rendered existence. 

They know this. There is no panic in their amber eyes, no frantic scrambling. They possess the grim, beautiful determinism of a root pushing through solid rock, or a white blood cell throwing itself against an infection. They are the immune system of a dying world, working until they are entirely consumed by the void they are trying to hold back.

The tender turns its featureless, pale-wood face toward you. It raises its remaining, solid hand, pressing its needle-thin fingers against the living ironwood of the balcony railing. A deep, subsonic hum vibrates through the wood, traveling up your arms and settling in your chest. It is not a greeting. It is a transmission of pure, unadulterated purpose. 

Then, it turns back to the abyss. It steps forward into the fraying, static-laced edge of the bridge, its body glowing brightly in the dark, and begins to bleed its own geometry into the void, weaving a few more feet of reality out of its dying light.
