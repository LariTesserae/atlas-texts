To understand what lives in this sunken, roaring world, you must first understand that the monumental negative space of the architecture is not empty. It is an acoustic chamber. The vast, hollowed-out geodes of living space, the wide, uncluttered plazas, the sweeping, spiraling staircases—they were not designed merely to be walked through. They were shaped to be *sung* through. 

The inhabitants of this anhydrous city do not possess flesh, blood, or sap. They are entirely devoid of moisture, born of the same deep-time geology and non-aqueous chemistry as the canyon itself. They are litho-acoustic entities, living intersections of polished stone, oxidized copper, and resonant frequency. 

When you first see them, they do not step out of the corbelled doorways so much as they detach from the walls. Their bodies are tall, elongated, and multi-limbed, perfectly proportioned to the sweeping, monumental scale of the staircases. They are constructed of interlocking plates of the same smooth, undulating stone as the canyon walls, polished to a glass-like finish by millennia of friction. Where their limbs articulate, there are no biological joints; instead, there are complex, interlocking hinges of simple, heavy copper, green with ancient verdigris and glowing with geothermal heat. 

They have no faces. In a world of dim amber light and deafening noise, eyes and ears are useless. Instead, their heads and torsos are defined by deep, hollowed cavities and fluted ridges, resembling the intricate interiors of nautilus shells or the soundholes of colossal, petrified instruments. They are, in essence, walking, thinking cymatic resonators. 

Because they contain no water, they do not move with the fluid grace of biological animals. Their movement is staccato, rhythmic, and deeply deliberate, driven by thermal expansion and acoustic levitation. As the hot, dry winds howl down through the fissure, the entities absorb the kinetic energy of the sound. They vibrate. The copper joints hum, and the stone plates shift and glide over one another with the sound of grinding tectonic plates, moving in perfect synchronization with the chaotic noise of the canyon. They do not walk; they are propelled by the standing waves of the roaring wind, gliding across the empty plazas like vessels on a sea of compressed air.

They are the gardeners of this violent, loud ecosystem. 

You watch as one of the towering entities approaches a grove of glass-trees. The tree is groaning, its thick, translucent trunk swelling with pressurized, non-aqueous solvents, on the verge of a violent, cracking expansion. The entity reaches out with a multi-jointed, copper-tipped appendage and gently presses it against the stone pillar beside the tree. It begins to vibrate its own hollow chest cavity, emitting a deep, sub-bass frequency that you feel in your teeth and marrow. 

The counter-frequency hits the tree. The violent, unpredictable cracking of the trunk suddenly aligns, snapping and reforming in a clean, controlled fracture. The entity is tuning the flora. It moves down the line of copper-colored vines, plucking the braided fibers to release trapped chemical gases, and adjusting the pneumatic bladders so they vent their ozone-scented hisses in a synchronized rhythm. They do not eat the plants, nor do they harvest them. They cultivate the *noise*. They prune the chaos into harmony.

It becomes clear, as you watch them tend to the shattering leaves and detonating seed-pods, who these beings are. They are the descendants of the primitive hands that carved the city. Over uncounted millennia, isolated in this baking, desiccant crucible, the original builders did not die out; they adapted. They shed their water, shed their fragile biology, and slowly replaced their failing flesh with the enduring stone and copper of their environment. They became the architecture. They became the sound. The city is not an abandoned sanctuary; it is a monastery of resonance, and they are its eternal, post-biological monks.

When the thermal heat reaches its absolute peak, the canyon’s cacophony reaches a crescendo. The wind shrieks, the glass-trees crack like cannon fire, the seed-pods detonate in rapid succession, and the brittle leaves rattle like a torrential downpour of gravel. 

This is when the inhabitants gather in the center of the largest, emptiest plaza. Dozens of them arrange themselves in concentric circles, their copper joints glowing cherry-red in the twilight. They open their hollow, fluted chests to the roaring wind. 

They begin to sing. 

It is not a song of melody, but of pure, overwhelming physics. They use their stone bodies to catch the chaotic, deafening roar of the canyon and filter it. The wind rushes into their hollow cavities and is expelled as a massive, coordinated, low-frequency chord. The sound is so profound, so heavy, that it temporarily cancels out the chaotic noise of the flora. The cracking trees, the hissing bladders, the clacking seeds—all of it is swallowed by the deep, vibrating hum of the lithic choir. The air itself seems to solidify around them, shimmering with cymatic patterns, the dust and mineral spores hanging in perfect, geometric mandalas in the amber light.

For a few breathtaking minutes, the oppressive, violent noise of the canyon is transformed into a single, earth-shaking note of absolute, perfect order. 

Then, the thermal peak passes. The wind drops a fraction. The entities close their resonant chambers. The chord fades, and the chaotic, roaring, cracking, hissing symphony of the dry, lush jungle rushes back in to fill the void. 

The inhabitants do not linger. They turn and glide back toward the sweeping staircases and the corbelled doorways. As they press themselves against the smooth, undulating walls, their stone plates align perfectly with the grooves of the architecture. Their copper joints cool and darken. They fold their limbs and become still, blending seamlessly back into the polished rock. 

They return to the stone, waiting in the heavy, baking stillness for the wind to rise, for the trees to crack, and for the deep, dark heat of the earth to call them out to sing the canyon into balance once again.
