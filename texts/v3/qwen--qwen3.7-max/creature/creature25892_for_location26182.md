To look for the inhabitants of the Bathyal Ward is to fundamentally misunderstand the nature of the place. You do not spot them; rather, you slowly realize that what you thought was a shadowed cornice of oxidized copper is, in fact, breathing. 

They are the Tenders of the Deep, though they have no spoken name for themselves, as their language does not permit the rapid exchange of consonants. To exist in the Bathyal Ward is to be shaped by it, and the Tenders are the absolute, biological expression of the cavern’s heavy, thixotropic physics. 

At first glance, their silhouette suggests something akin to a deep-sea sloth crossed with an eel, but this is a clumsy terrestrial comparison. Their bodies are elongated and sinuous, built without a single sharp angle or abrupt joint. Every contour is smoothed, tapered, and rounded to ensure that their movement never creates a sudden shear in the viscous water. They possess a central, barrel-like torso that tapers into a long, muscular prehensile tail, used not for swimming, but for anchoring to the dense lattice of pipes and catwalks. 

Their skin is a marvel of evolutionary adaptation to the heavy fluid. It is thick, poreless, and the color of bruised plums and weathered verdigris. It feels like wet, polished velvet, secreting a thin layer of lubricating mucus that prevents the thixotropic water from gripping them too tightly. Along their ribs, gill-flutes pulse with an imperceptible, agonizing slowness, drawing oxygen from the warm gel. But they are not solely water-breathers. 

From their shoulders extend four primary limbs, multi-jointed and ending in broad, soft, paddle-like manipulators. These hands have no sharp fingers; instead, they feature wide, spongy pads that distribute pressure evenly. This is a vital survival trait. If a Tender were to punch, strike, or grab quickly, the water around their fist would instantly flash-harden into sandstone, shattering their bones and entombing their arm. Therefore, every gesture is a slow, deliberate extrusion. To reach for a valve, a Tender extends their arm over the course of two or three minutes, allowing the heavy fluid to part and flow around their limb like warm honey. If a tool slips from their grasp, they do not lunge to catch it. They simply watch it drift downward for a minute or more, their faces masks of serene acceptance. To rush is to invite the stone. To be hasty is to die.

Their faces are serene, almost sculptural, dominated by wide, visor-like eyes that gather the dim amber light of the arc-lamps. These eyes are entirely black, lacking a visible iris or pupil, protected by a thick, translucent nictitating membrane that sweeps across them once every few hours. They have no external ears, for sound travels too strangely in the heavy gel. Instead, they perceive the world through a highly developed lateral line system running down their flanks, feeling the subsonic thrum of the great turbines and the minute displacements of water caused by their peers.

Yet, they do communicate. 

Rising from the center of a Tender’s chest is a pale, translucent biological bellows—a specialized lung-sac. This is why they harvest the silvery domes of trapped air from the cavern ceilings. Once a day, a Tender will slowly ascend a kelp-wrapped pylon, press their mouth-slit against an air dome, and spend an hour gently sipping the precious gas. This air is not used for respiration, but for resonance. 

When they wish to speak, they slowly compress this internal bellows. The air is forced through a series of dense, cartilaginous reeds in their throat, producing a subsonic, cello-like hum. A single "word" or concept in their language might take ten minutes to vocalize, a deep vibration that travels through the heavy water and is felt in the chest cavities of those nearby. Their conversations are slow, overlapping symphonies of low-frequency thrums, discussing the structural integrity of a rusted beam, the growth rate of the jade moss, or the philosophical implications of a gear’s fractional turn. To listen to a Tender speak is to feel your own heartbeat synchronize with theirs.

Their culture, if it can be called that, is entirely centered around the Liturgy of the Millimeter. They are the custodians of the slow industry, though they do not view it as labor. It is a meditative, existential practice. 

Watch a Tender at work, and you will witness a profound, hypnotic devotion. A single Tender might be assigned to the anointing of a colossal brass flywheel. Armed with a wide, soft brush and a vat of viscous, geothermal lipid, they will spend a three-day shift applying a single, continuous coat of oil to a ten-foot section of the gear. They move in perfect tandem with the machine, their bodies swaying in a micro-millimeter dance, anticipating the imperceptible rotation of the metal. They do not force the machinery; they accompany it. They know the exact thermal expansion rates of the copper pipes, the precise moment a calcified rind of rust needs to be gently scraped away, and the exact tension required to keep a drive-shaft humming in harmony with the deep-earth currents. 

They share their dense, interlocking habitat not just with the flora, but in a state of total symbiosis with it. The pale jade moss that coats the handrails also grows in soft, velvety patches on the Tenders' shoulders and backs. The aquatic ferns that filter the water are gently pruned by the Tenders, who weave the gossamer fronds into loose, decorative mantles during the long, quiet cycles of rest. When they sleep, they anchor themselves by the tail to a sturdy pipe, wrap themselves in the oxygen-exhaling ferns, and enter a state of torpor that can last for a week. In this state, their heart beats once every four minutes.

Fauna is rare in the Ward, but the Tenders share a gentle, reverent relationship with the glass-rays. When one of the translucent, blind creatures drifts into a tight alleyway, a Tender will spend hours gently guiding it with the slow, sweeping motions of their broad hands, creating a gentle current to coax the ray away from the grinding, slow-moving gears. They view the glass-rays as the purest expression of the Ward’s philosophy: beings that have entirely surrendered to the slow, moving through the world without demanding anything of it.

There is no concept of "progress" in the Bathyal Ward, no desire to build outward or upward, no ambition to increase the speed of the turbines. The cavern is perfectly packed, perfectly balanced, and perfectly sustained. The Tenders are not trapped in the slow; they are liberated by it. The agonizing pace of the surface world—the frantic rushing, the sudden impacts, the chaotic noise—is to them a terrifying myth, a chaotic hell where water does not hold you, where time slips through your fingers, and where things break because they move too fast.

Down here, in the warm, heavy dark, the Tenders exist in a state of profound, heavy grace. They are the flesh-and-blood embodiment of the cavern’s equilibrium. When you finally leave the Bathyal Ward, ascending back to the thin, cold, fast-moving waters above, you will carry with you the phantom weight of their world. You will feel the ghost of their subsonic hum in your ribs, and for a long time, you will find the surface world unbearably, violently fast, longing for the day you can return to the deep, sink into the warm gel, and spend an hour just watching a single bubble rise.
