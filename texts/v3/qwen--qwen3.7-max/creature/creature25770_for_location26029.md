To understand what lives on the Summit Peneplain, you must first abandon the idea of a food chain. There is no simple pyramid of predator and prey here. Instead, life exists as a chaotic, multi-dimensional web of thermodynamic scavengers, temporal parasites, and geometric apex predators, all evolving in the crucible of a broken, screaming world.

If you kneel upon the mirror-smooth bedrock and press your ear near the grinding, copper-colored stolons, you will first encounter the **Friction Mites**. 
They are the microscopic foundation of this roaring ecosystem. They do not eat the plant matter; they eat the *kinetic energy* of the bio-factory. Looking like slivers of liquid bismuth, these nematodes live in the micro-gaps between the lignified resin gears. As the heavy wooden cogs grind together with their rhythmic *thud-thud-thud*, the mites absorb the friction-heat and the mechanical vibration, their bodies glowing white-hot for a fraction of a second before they vent the excess energy as microscopic sparks. When a root-boiler ruptures, millions of these mites are flash-boiled into the air, riding the shrieking steam vents like a glittering, metallic pollen. They are the spark-plugs of the plain, ensuring the mechanical flora never seizes up, lubricating the grinding joints with their own crushed, silica-rich corpses.

Moving up the scale, living within the violent, churning skin of the creeping moss, are the **Crucible Weavers**. 
Because the bio-factories are in a constant state of explosive rupture and rapid regrowth, the Weavers have evolved to live entirely within the "echoes" of these explosions. They are arachnid-analogues, but their bodies are spun from cooling slag and flash-frozen rime. They do not build webs of silk; they build webs of *probability*. When a giant, swollen tuber blows its exhaust-pores and violently ruptures, sending chunks of iron-wood and boiling sap into the air, the Weavers instantly swarm the crater. They spin geometric, crystalline lattices across the exploding shrapnel, catching the flash-sublimating steam and weaving it into solid, glass-like threads. They use these temporary, hyper-tensioned webs to snare the temporal afterimages of the glass-winged birds. When a bird shatters into fractal dust and flies backward in time, it leaves behind a "ghost" of its flight path. The Weavers eat these ghosts, digesting the residual chronal energy to fuel their own rapid, stuttering life cycles. They exist in a state of perpetual half-life, dying and reforming with every explosion of the flora beneath them.

Above the ground, swarming in the bruised-purple twilight, the fauna of the empty air hosts its own terrifying symbiotes. 
The massive, floating manta-beasts that graze on the steam are plagued by **Zenith-Ticks**. Because the mantas’ shadows fall *upward*, pooling on the dim, cloudy sky above, the Zenith-Ticks have evolved to live entirely in two dimensions. They are flat, impossibly wide, and completely black, resembling floating oil slicks against the clouds. They cling to the sky, waiting for the upward-falling shadows of the mantas to reach them. When a shadow pools on the cloud layer, the Zenith-Ticks latch onto the 2D projection, drinking the darkness and the cold. If a manta-beast dies and its physical body falls to the grinding earth below, its shadow detaches completely. The freed shadow becomes a permanent, free-floating 2D predator, sliding across the cloudy sky, wrapping around the glass-winged birds and suffocating them in absolute, freezing darkness before absorbing their mass into the cloud layer.

But the true masters of the Summit Peneplain—the entities that tie the ancient stone, the screaming bio-industry, and the fractured physics together—do not swarm, and they do not scurry. 

They are the **Aeon-Striders**.

You do not see them approach, because they do not move through space in a linear fashion. You simply realize that the horizon has changed, and that what you thought was a distant, jagged outcropping of polished bedrock is actually a leg. 

The Aeon-Striders are colossal, impossibly tall, multi-jointed entities. They are the apex gardeners of the broken physics, and their biology is an argument against geometry. Their legs are extruded directly from the ancient, mirror-smooth bedrock of the peneplain itself, animated by thick, pulsing cables of the copper-colored stolons that wrap around their joints like muscular tendons. They stand hundreds of feet tall, their bodies resembling massive, walking geodes. The outer shells of their torsos are made of the primordial, billion-year-old stone, cracked open to reveal spinning, bio-mechanical hearts of pure, blinding violet light and liquid chroniton. 

They move in agonizing slow motion, yet because of the spatial distortions they generate, they appear to teleport. One moment, a Strider is a mile away, its heavy, metallic hooves crunching the frost-moss; the next, the sound arrives from directly behind your ear, and the Strider is towering over you, its massive stone body eclipsing the bruised sun. 

The Aeon-Striders do not eat the mechanical flora, nor do they hunt the fractal birds. They feed on the *paradoxes*. 

Their "heads" are not faces, but open, howling geodes lined with thousands of spinning, turbine-bladed fronds. As they walk, they inhale the turbulent, chaotic storm-state of the plain. They draw in the shrieking steam, the heavy wet snow, and the spatial distortions. When a flock of glass-winged birds folds themselves into a single point of light and vanishes, an Aeon-Strider will lower its massive, geode-head and swallow the residual singularity. You can hear the paradox digesting inside them—a deep, bass frequency that rattles your teeth and vibrates up through the soles of your boots, harmonizing with the grinding of the plant-gears below. 

They are the reason the local physics are fractured, and they are the only things keeping the reality breakdown from consuming the entire world. By consuming the temporal anomalies and the spatial folds, they act as the pressure-valves for the peneplain’s hyperactive, bio-industrial engine. As they digest the broken geometry, they excrete pure, refined heavy metals and perfectly stable, mirror-smooth bedrock from their rear exhaust-pores, continually polishing and renewing the ancient floor of the world.

To stand among them is to feel entirely insignificant. You shiver in the weeping cold, watching an Aeon-Strider lift a massive, stone-hewn leg and step *between* seconds, its footfall landing three minutes in the past. The air ripples around it, smearing its colossal form into a delayed, ghostly afterimage. It lets out a low, grinding moan that sounds like tectonic plates shifting, a sound that perfectly matches the rhythmic *thud-thud-thud* of the million spinning turbines beneath your feet. 

Here, on the highest, oldest, flattest place in the world, life has not just survived the freezing, thin air and the eons of wind. It has weaponized the very fabric of reality, turning the breakdown of time and space into a thriving, deafening, beautiful, and terrifying industry. And the Aeon-Striders walk through it all, silent and immense, endlessly shepherding the storm.
