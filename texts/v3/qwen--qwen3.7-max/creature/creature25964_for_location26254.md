To look closely at the shadows moving along the iron catwalks is to realize that the inhabitants of this hollow are not merely living within the leviathan; they are its nervous system. 

They do not have a single, easily defined shape, for their bodies are in a constant state of negotiation with their environment. At a glance, they appear humanoid, but stretched and warped by the heavy gravity, the intense heat, and the relentless acoustic pressure of the cavern. They are tall and impossibly lean, with dense, heavy bone structures required to withstand the subsonic *THOOM* of the drop-forges. Their skin is a mosaic of calloused, ash-grey flesh and ingrown, polished bark. Where the cavern’s weeping moisture touches them, their dermis thickens into a leathery, water-resistant hide; where the furnace heat bakes them, it calcifies into pale, heat-shielded scales. 

They are the Arc-Born, the caretakers of the closed loop, and they wear the hollow’s industry like a second skin.

Their clothing is spun from the fibrous, fungal mycelium that blooms in the damp shadows of the tin shanties, woven tightly with strands of stripped copper wire. This wire is not merely decorative; it is functional. The Arc-Born do not carry tools in the traditional sense. Their forearms are heavily scarred, laced with subdermal brass filaments that terminate in their fingertips. When an Arc-Born needs to interface with a sputtering dynamo or a jammed stamping piston, they do not use a wrench. They press their bare, wire-threaded hands against the iron casing, sliding their fingers into the service ports. Their nervous systems, flooded with the same conductive, electrified sap that fuels the boilers, bridge the gap between flesh and machine. They feel the voltage. They sense the friction. They diagnose the engine by the taste of ozone on their tongues and the phantom heat in their bones.

Because the cavern is swallowed in deafening, mechanical roaring, the Arc-Born do not speak with their vocal cords. To shout over the drop-forges would be to tear one's own throat. Instead, they communicate through *thrumming* and *sparking*. 

When two of them meet on a swaying, condensation-slicked catwalk, they press their palms flat against the iron grating or the ancient bark of the root-pillars. They vibrate their heavy bones, sending localized, subsonic rhythmic pulses through the substrate. A conversation is a shared tremor, felt in the soles of the feet and the base of the spine. For longer distances, or to command the swarming fauna, they use their hands. By consciously flexing the copper weaves in their garments and the filaments in their skin, they generate localized electromagnetic fields. They spark. A sharp, blue arc leaping from thumb to forefinger means *danger* or *halt*; a slow, pulsing, bioluminescent glow radiating from their chests—a byproduct of the sap in their blood—means *all is well*. 

Their society is vertically stratified, mirroring the architecture of the suspended shanties and terraced foundries. 

Down in the deepest, hottest tiers, near the unseen depths where the massive taproots plunge, live the **Stokers and Tappers**. These are the largest and most heavily modified of the Arc-Born. Their eyes have entirely sealed over, replaced by thick, translucent plates of keratin, as the glare of the violet sap-fires would blind them. They navigate purely by the electromagnetic hum of the pipes and the vibrations of the wood. They wield heavy, iron-shod hooks, harvesting the glowing, electrified sap and feeding the crushing maws of the boilers. Their skin is permanently blackened by soot and scorched by violet light, and they move with a slow, deliberate grace, entirely unbothered by the blistering heat.

Higher up, in the dense, corrugated tin slums clinging to the mid-level bark, live the **Swarm-Herders and Weavers**. They are the most agile, their limbs elongated, their fingers ending in delicate, conductive taps. They live in intimate proximity to the swarming, chitinous capacitor-beasts. The Herders do not fear the swarm; they wear it. A master Herder is often cloaked in a shifting, buzzing mantle of the pale, blind insects. The beasts are drawn to the electromagnetic aura of the Herders, clustering on their shoulders and arms. With tuning forks made of polished bone and brass, the Herders strike specific frequencies, directing the swirling rivers of insects to discharge their electrical payloads into the hanging root-pods, or to swarm a cooling boiler to absorb its excess heat. The Herders smell intensely of crushed chitin, ozone, and sweet, rotting resin.

At the very top, suspended in the highest, darkest vaults where the massive iron flywheels spin in the gloom, are the **Grafters**. They are the priests and surgeons of the hollow. It is their sacred duty to manage the boundary where the biological and the mechanical dissolve. When the ancient wood begins to digest an iron boiler casing, creeping over it and incorporating the rust into its grain, it is the Grafters who guide the process. They use delicate, heated blades to prune the encroaching bark, ensuring the wood does not crush the vital pistons, while simultaneously carving channels into the cambium to allow the metal to feed the roots. They are the mediators of the closed loop, their hands permanently stained with the violet sap and the black blood of the machines. 

Life in the shanties is intensely communal, driven by the absolute necessity of the ecosystem. The interiors of the tin huts are not squalid, but highly organized, resembling the inside of a wasp’s nest. They sleep in hammocks woven from root-hair, suspended directly over the heat vents to stay warm in the brief, artificial "nights" when the dynamos are cycled down for maintenance. They eat a diet of nutrient-dense, pale fungi that they cultivate on the exhaust pipes, supplemented by a rich, protein-heavy paste rendered from the larger, hound-sized swarm-beasts that die of old age or overcharge. Nothing is wasted. Every drop of moisture, every scrap of organic matter, every joule of electricity is accounted for.

But the true, deep weirdness of the Arc-Born is only revealed when one of them dies. 

There are no graves in the hollow. There is no soil to dig, and the space is too precious. When an Arc-Born’s heart finally stops—when the sap in their veins cools and the copper in their skin tarnishes—they are carried by the Grafters to the heart of the greatest, oldest root-pillar. 

Here, the body is stripped of its reusable brass and iron. The remaining flesh, bone, and ingrown bark are pressed directly into a deep, weeping fissure in the wood. The leviathan accepts its child back. Over the course of a few weeks, the calcified wood digests the body, breaking down the proteins and the residual electrical charge. The deceased Arc-Born is drawn down into the taproots, their biological matter converted into the very sap that will fuel the boilers, their electrical charge dispersed into the canopy to feed the swarm. 

In this way, the inhabitants do not just maintain the hollow; they *are* the hollow. They are born from the resin, they live as the white blood cells of the great engine, and in death, they become the fuel. 

If you stand quietly on a catwalk, ignoring the shriek of the dynamos and the heavy *THOOM* of the forges, and simply watch them move through the glowing steam, you will realize the ultimate truth of the subterranean cathedral. The leviathan is buried, and it is breathing, but it is not asleep. It is dreaming. And the Arc-Born, with their sparking hands and their subsonic voices, are the architecture of its dream, endlessly walking the iron veins, keeping the great, dark heart beating in the warm, damp dark.
