To understand what lives in the Rift of the Glass-wood, you must first understand that life here does not merely occupy space; it negotiates with it, moment by violent moment. The ecosystem is a masterclass in extreme adaptation, where every organism has evolved to survive the blinding light, the razor-sharp cold, and the deafening silence of the hollows.

**The Architect: The Glass-wood**
The dominant resident is the environment itself. The Glass-wood is not a passive collection of trees, but a hyper-reactive, semi-sentient colonial organism. It lives in a state of perpetual, panicked triumph. Because it feeds on the kinetic friction of the tectonic plates and the punishing solar radiation from above, its "nervous system" is incredibly sensitive. If you press your bare hand against the translucent cellulose of a spire, you can feel the deep, subsonic thrum of its awareness. It feels the shifting of the earth miles below and the angle of the sun miles above. 

When a tectonic tremor occurs, the Glass-wood reacts defensively. Entire groves of hollow spires will rapidly secrete a thick, weeping sap that flash-freezes into structural buttresses of solid ice, reinforcing the tubes against collapse. It is a plant that builds its own scaffolding in real-time. It does not reproduce through seeds, but through explosive, pressurized spores—glass-like spheres the size of fists that are shot from the upper bladders. When they strike the frozen root-floor, they shatter, embedding their genetic payload into the splintered lattice, where they immediately begin to inflate into new, hollow chambers.

**The Drifters: Void-Rays and Rime-Skippers**
In the vast, cathedral-sized voids between the spires, the fauna has abandoned the concept of weight. The **Void-rays** are the apex drifters of the rift. Up close, their thirty-foot wingspans are revealed to be intricate, living lattices of gossamer veins, entirely devoid of opaque tissue. This transparency is a vital adaptation: in a world with no shadows, casting a shadow would make them visible to the rare predators that lurk in the deep roots. More importantly, their translucent bodies allow the punishing, high-altitude sunlight to pass directly through them, preventing their internal fluids from boiling in the focused beams of light. 

They do not flap. They ride the thermal updrafts generated by the violent, exothermic growth of the Glass-wood. They feed by gliding through the microscopic aerosol of the rift, their wide, gaping maws filtering out flash-frozen sap droplets and microscopic fungal spores. When they vocalize, it is not a cry, but a low, resonant hum that perfectly matches the subsonic frequency of the rushing plant-sap, making them nearly invisible to the acoustic senses of the rift.

Down on the jagged, splintered root-floor, the **Rime-skippers** navigate the razor-sharp terrain. These six-legged gliders are the size of large foxes, covered in dense, pale fur that mimics the fractal frost. Their most remarkable adaptation is their feet: instead of claws or pads, they possess wide, soft, velvet-like extremities that distribute their weight perfectly. This allows them to step on the delicate, needle-like frost without shattering it. If they were to break the frost, the resulting micro-explosions of shattering ice would slice their legs to ribbons. They move in sudden, darting glides, feeding on the pale, frozen sap that weeps from the snapped roots, using their long, prehensile tongues to lap it up before it crystallizes.

**The Deep Dwellers: The Tectonic Boars**
The prompt mentions pitons carved from the bones of "rare subterranean beasts." These are the **Tectonic Boars**, creatures that live entirely below the light-line, in the crushing, lightless depths where the Glass-wood’s roots anchor into the bedrock. 

They are massive, blind, and heavily armored, resembling a cross between a pangolin and a wild boar, but the size of a rhinoceros. Because they feed on the deep, unfrozen, nutrient-dense taproots of the Glass-wood, the plants actively try to crush them by rapidly growing dense, woody nodules around them. In response, the Boars have evolved skeletons of unbelievable density. Their bones are laced with silicates and heavy metals drawn from the bedrock, making them as hard and sharp as forged steel. They are incredibly difficult to hunt. The people of the rift must descend into the pitch-black root-mazes, using the subsonic hums of the plants to track the Boars' burrowing, and kill them with heavy, obsidian-tipped drop-spears. When a Boar is butchered, its meat is tough and tastes of iron and deep earth, but its bones are prized above all else, carved into the pitons and tools that keep the people anchored to the walls.

**The Hollow-Born: The People of the Rift**
The human inhabitants—often called the **Rime-Carvers** or the **Hollow-Born**—are a study in profound, deliberate stillness. To live in a landscape of hyperactive, chaotic turbulence, one must become the eye of the storm. 

Physiologically, they have adapted to the extremes. Beneath their crude, smoked-quartz goggles, their eyes are enormous, with pale, almost colorless irises and thick, secondary nictitating membranes that automatically slide across their corneas when the glare becomes too intense. Their skin is pale, thickened, and covered in a lattice of microscopic, silvery scars—the inevitable result of living in a world where the very air crystallizes into razor-sharp dust. Their lungs are barrel-chested, highly efficient at extracting oxygen from the thin, freezing air, and their nasal passages are elongated to warm and humidify the sub-zero breath before it reaches their delicate lung tissue.

Culturally, they are defined by the "library-like hush" of their world. Because the hollow acoustics swallow loud noises and the sharp frost punishes sudden movements, they have developed a society of extreme physical grace and quietude. They do not shout. To communicate across the vast, echoing chambers, they use a complex, sweeping sign language that relies on the contrast of their dark, hide-clad bodies against the blinding white walls. For longer distances, they use "hum-pipes"—flutes carved from the hollow wing-bones of void-rays—which they blow into to produce low, subsonic notes that travel for miles through the cellulose walls without echoing.

Their daily life is a delicate, dangerous ballet. Harvesting the flash-frozen sap requires approaching a weeping fissure in the plant-wall and striking it with a knapped obsidian axe at the exact moment the sap emerges. If they strike too early, they hit solid wood; too late, and the sap freezes into an impenetrable block of rime. When shattered correctly, the sap breaks into glass-like shards. They collect these shards in woven sinew-pouches, later melting them in their mouths for hydration. The sap is sweet, highly caloric, and mildly alkaline, neutralizing the acidic burn of the freezing air in their throats.

Their camps, lashed to the inner curves of the colossal tubes, are marvels of tension and balance. Because the Glass-wood is constantly growing and shifting, a camp built today might be crushed by a bulging wall of cellulose tomorrow. Therefore, their tents and platforms are designed to be unspooled and relocated in minutes. They sleep in hammocks woven from rime-vine, suspended over dizzying drops, swaying gently with the subsonic groans of the living architecture. 

Psychologically, the Hollow-Born possess a deep, fatalistic reverence for the rift. They do not view the Glass-wood as a resource to be conquered, but as a volatile god to be appeased. When a spire violently splits open, shedding multi-ton shards of frozen bark, they do not run in panic; they step calmly into the alcoves, watching the destruction with quiet awe. They know that to panic is to slip, and to slip in the Rift of the Glass-wood is to be sliced open by the frost or swallowed by the abyss.

**The Unseen: The Frost-Bloom**
Finally, life exists at the microscopic level, bridging the gap between the flora and the fauna. Within the perpetually renewing layer of fractal rime that coats every inner wall lives the **Frost-bloom**, a cryophilic, bioluminescent bacteria. 

When the weeping sap of the Glass-wood touches the freezing air, it traps these bacteria. As the bacteria consume the sugars in the sap, they emit a very faint, pale-green luminescence. This is what gives the deep, shadowed recesses of the hollow chambers their ghostly, ethereal glow. The Frost-bloom is the foundational bedrock of the rift's food web. It is eaten by the Rime-skippers, which are in turn hunted by the Hollow-Born. When a person walks through a darkened tube, their breath disturbs the microscopic aerosol, causing the Frost-bloom to swirl around them in a glowing, green mist, making them look like spirits wandering through a cathedral of ice.

**The Synthesis of the Void**
To observe the inhabitants of the Rift of the Glass-wood is to witness a perfect, terrifying equilibrium. It is an ecosystem where violence is the engine of creation, and stillness is the only mechanism for survival. The colossal plants erupt and shatter in a silent storm of growth; the giant, transparent rays glide like ghosts through the blinding thermals; the heavy, armored boars chew through the lightless depths; and the quiet, scarred people move like careful shadows along the razor-edged walls. 

They are all bound together by the biting cold, the absolute light, and the profound, echoing emptiness. They do not merely survive the beautiful, terrifying contradictions of the rift; they are the physical manifestations of those contradictions, living proof that life will not only endure in the most hostile of voids, but will carve out a breathtaking, crystalline sanctuary within it.
