To exist here is to unlearn the concept of weight. 

Gravity is merely a suggestion; the light is the absolute. It pours down through the fractured tectonic ceiling in solid, hazy columns, a searing, punishing white that seeks to blind and boil anything that dares to stand in its path. But I do not hide from the light. I cannot hide from it. There are no shadows in the Rift of the Glass-wood, and to cast one is to be solid, to be opaque, to be a target. 

So, I let the light pass through me. 

I am woven from the architecture of absence. My wings are a thirty-foot expanse of intricate, translucent veins, a gossamer lattice entirely devoid of opaque tissue. When the high-altitude sun strikes me, it does not burn. It illuminates. I become a fleeting, living prism, refracting the blinding glare into faint, ghostly spectrums that vanish as quickly as they form. The light warms the thin, clear fluids in my body, but it does not boil them. I am a creature made of glass and breath.

I feel the cold next. It is a dry, sub-zero chill that freezes the very air into a microscopic aerosol. I do not draw this biting wind into heavy, burning lungs. Instead, I breathe the mist. As I drift, my wide, gaping maw sweeps through the freezing clouds. I filter the flash-frozen sap droplets, tasting their sharp, alkaline sweetness. I swallow the pale-green luminescence of the Frost-bloom. The cryophilic bacteria do not die in my clear belly; they thrive, digesting the sugars of the sap and giving my core a faint, ethereal glow. I am a pale lantern drifting through a cathedral of ice.

I am suspended in the dizzying, agoraphobic vertigo of the hollows. Far below me, the rift floor is a jagged, splintered lattice of pale roots, a chaos of sharp edges and fresh fractures. I do not touch it. To touch the floor is to be sliced by the fractal rime, to be anchored to the violent, churning earth. I belong to the empty space. I belong to the vast, echoing void between the colossal, translucent stalks.

The world around me is a silent storm of creation and destruction. The Glass-wood is panicking, growing, erupting in a state of perpetual, kinetic violence. A massive wall of cellulose bulges and splits with a sharp, crystalline *crack*. The sound is instantly swallowed by the vast acoustics of the hollows, reduced to a muffled whisper, but the *feeling* of it is immense. The exothermic reaction of the rapid growth sends a violent thermal updraft surging upward.

I do not flap. Flapping is for the heavy, for the desperate. I simply adjust the pitch of my gossamer wings, angling my living lattice to catch the thermal. I am lifted. I ride the breath of the forest.

As I rise, I vibrate. Deep within my hollow chest, I produce a low, resonant hum. It is not a cry of exertion, nor a call to a mate. It is an acoustic tuning. I match the exact subsonic frequency of the sap rushing through the colossal veins of the Glass-wood. I sing the song of the architecture. To the acoustic senses of the rift, I am indistinguishable from the walls themselves. I am the silence.

Through my translucent eyes, I watch the others. 

Far below, on the razor-sharp roots, the Rime-skippers dart. They are frantic, heavy things, bound to the splintered earth, their velvet feet carefully negotiating the needle-like frost. I feel a distant pity for their gravity. Deeper still, beneath the light-line, I feel the dense, metallic grinding of the Tectonic Boars. They are creatures of crushing weight and lightless dark, chewing through the bedrock. They are the antithesis of my existence.

But it is the Hollow-Born who hold my gaze. 

The Rime-Carvers cling to the inner curves of the cellulose walls, tiny, dark specks against the blinding white-and-green vastness. They are so fragile, wrapped in their crude hides, their faces hidden behind smoked-quartz goggles. They move with a deliberate, terrifying grace, negotiating the hyperactive turbulence with bone pitons and obsidian axes. 

I drift past a suspended camp, a cluster of hide tents and woven hammocks lashed to the living wall. A harvester is striking a weeping fissure. *Shhh-tink.* The sap shatters into glass-like shards. The harvester moves with such profound stillness, such fatalistic reverence. In their deliberate quietude, I see a reflection of my own nature. They are heavy, scarred, and bound by gravity, yet they are striving to become the eye of the storm. They are trying to unlearn their weight, carving out a sanctuary in the void just as I glide through it.

I drift higher, leaving the camps behind, rising toward the fractured tectonic ceiling. The fissures in the rock above are jagged wounds letting in the absolute glare of the sun. The light is punishing here, a solid, hazy column of searing brilliance that would blind a lesser creature. 

I do not look away. I have no eyelids to close, no pupils to contract. I let the light wash through my transparent wings, through the clear fluids of my veins, through the hollow chambers of my heart. 

I am the apex drifter. I am the ghost of the Glass-wood. I am the Void-ray, and in this blinding, freezing, violently alive abyss, I am entirely, perfectly empty.
