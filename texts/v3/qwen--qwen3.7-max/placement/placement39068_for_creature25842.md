I descend the spiraling, riveted iron staircase, but with every step, I am not merely leaving the surface world behind; I am shedding its frantic, sunlit urgency. By the time my bare feet touch the raised steel grates of the vault, the concept of "fast" has already begun to evaporate from my mind.

At first, the darkness is absolute, a heavy, layered substance that presses against the skin. I do not try to pierce it. Sight is a vanity of the rushing world above, an energy-expensive sense suited for environments that change by the second. Instead, I let my eyelids seal shut, the flesh fusing smoothly over my eyes, and I open the deep, foveal thermal pits along my jaw and cheeks. 

The vault blooms into existence in gradients of heat. The cherry-red pilot lights behind thick glass portholes are roaring, localized suns; the weeping condensation pipes are rivers of cool, soothing blue. I feel the space not as a claustrophobic thicket of iron and brick, but as a perfectly calibrated thermodynamic engine. The oppressive, baking warmth wraps around me, and I do not recoil. My skin—a mottled, waxy pallor that mimics the blooming verdigris of the copper pipes—weeps a calculated, microscopic layer of moisture. It flashes into steam against the ambient heat, wrapping my elongated frame in a personal, cooling micro-climate. I am the color of oxidized metal and glazed fire-brick, a living patina perfectly adapted to the gloom.

I need to move, to tend to the slow-dynamic equilibrium of the space. I do not walk; I unfold. My limbs, stretched and thinned by generations of deep-earth adaptation, articulate with the fluid, multi-axis grace of the labyrinthine piping I live among. I pour my long, impossibly jointed frame through a narrow gap between a humming transformer and a brick kiln, compressing and sliding with millimeter clearance. It takes me ten full seconds to extend my arm toward a hissing steam valve. My spatulate, heat-resistant fingers grip the searing brass, adjusting the flow with a micro-turn. To move faster would be to generate friction, to spike the local temperature, to disturb the sacred, fifty-year balance of the anneal. 

Through the diamond-plate steel beneath my feet, I feel the deep, sub-audible vibration of the massive central flywheel. It takes a full minute to complete a single rotation, but I feel every imperceptible shift of its immense weight in my marrow. I am in time with it. 

I pause, tilting my smooth, eyeless head back beneath an iron pillar. A heavy bead of condensation gathers on the cool metal above, swells, and falls. I catch it in my open mouth, my rasping lower lip grazing the pale-yellow, thermophilic slime mold clinging to the pipe's underside. It tastes of microscopic organic oils and the faint, metallic tang of electrical charge. It is enough. Below me, in the warm, oily sumps, I sense the faint, pulsing thermal shadows of the blind, translucent amphibians. We do not interact, but we share the warmth, both of us sustained by the sweat of the sleeping iron leviathan.

But my sustenance is merely a byproduct of my presence. My true purpose lies at the back of the vault. 

I slide through the heavy shadows toward the massive, upright iron molds. The air here is thicker, heavy with the scent of hot, synthetic resins. I step off the grate and wade directly into the deep, sweltering vat of chemical bath. The hot fluid rises to my chest, staining my waxy skin in deep ambers and opaque blacks, adding another layer to my protective oxidation. It does not burn; it anoints.

Suspended in the bath before me is the giant, geometric monolith. I press my smooth face directly against its curing surface, feeling the heat of the resin transfer into my facial bones. I fill my barrel-like chest cavity with the damp, heavy air, and I begin to hum.

It is a low, resonant, sub-vocal frequency, perfectly matched to the lethargic rotation of the flywheel. The vibration travels through my skull, into the resin, and deep into the synthetic material of the monolith. Through my facial bones, I feel the microscopic agitation of the chemical bath, the molecular bonds aligning, the air bubbles dissolving into flawless density. 

I do not know what the monolith will become when it is finally drawn from the bath half a century from now. There is no language here to ask, no thought of the future to distract me. I am a Tender of the Anneal. I am the flesh that thinks in decades, the biological metronome of the vault, humming in the dark, entirely unconcerned with the rushing world above, waiting for the slow cure to complete.
