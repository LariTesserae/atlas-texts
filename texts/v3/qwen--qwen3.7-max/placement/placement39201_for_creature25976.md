There is no shadow here. There is no silence. 

To exist in the Sunken Caldera is to be held in the palm of a blinding, deafening god. The light is an absolute, omnidirectional white-gold glare, filtered through the miles-thick salt and silicate canopy above, pressing down with the weight of a physical thing. It leaves no corner to hide in, no depth to rest the eyes. And the noise—it is not merely heard; it is worn. It is a 120-decibel atmospheric pressure that vibrates the marrow, a ceaseless, agonizing symphony of grinding, shattering, and roaring as the glass-forest violently builds and destroys itself in real-time.

I move through the shimmering haze of silica pollen, my breath rasping like dry leaves. The air tastes of hot flint and ozone. With every step, I feel the familiar, frantic energy of the Weave trying to consume me, but my pace is slow. Deliberate. I am no longer swinging the heavy obsidian axe. I have left the knapped blades and the ironwood hafts behind in the fleeting clearing of the morning camp. My hands, thick with callouses and embedded with fine, iridescent silicate scales, are empty. 

I am walking away.

My body tells the story of my departure. When I bend my knee, it does not creak with the frailty of age; it chimes. A high, pure, crystalline *tink* rings out, vibrating up my femur and into my pelvis. My shoulders click like wind chimes in a gale. I am an Elder of the Clear-Makers, and the Great Weave is calling me back into its crucible.

Because my outer ears are sealed shut—flush, heavily muscled slits evolved to block out the apocalyptic roar of the caldera—I do not hear the chiming of my own body through the air. I feel it. I hear the world entirely through the skeleton. The bedrock hums against the calloused, piezoelectric pads of my bare feet. I feel the deep, rhythmic *thud-thud* of a distant herd of slag-striders melting obsidian-bamboo in their furnace-stomachs. I feel the frantic, buzzing micro-tremors of a million diamond-jawed beetles shearing through a fallen quartz trunk. And beneath it all, I feel the agonizingly slow, heavy thump of my own heart, struggling to pump blood that is growing thick and crystalline.

Before I left, the younger Clear-Makers gathered around me. There were no words; the caldera steals all voices. Instead, the lead pruner pressed his forehead against mine. I felt the deep, resonant hum of his chest cavity vibrate through my skull, a tactile frequency of respect and release. I hummed back, a low, rattling vibration in my own chest, and then I turned toward the deep woods. There is no mourning in the Weave. There is only the cycle. To linger is to be swallowed unprepared; to leave willingly is to become the soil.

The trek to the deep caldera is an exercise in beautiful resistance. The undergrowth is a writhing, hyper-active mat of pumice-creepers and bismuth-ferns. As I wade through the waist-high glass-grass, the razor-sharp blades scrape against my hardened skin, sending sharp, stinging sparks of static electricity snapping against my shins. The air is so thick with glittering dust that it looks like suspended gold. I breathe it in. My specialized lungs trap the silica, and with a wet, heavy cough, I spit a small, perfectly smooth pearl of glass into my palm. I thread it onto the copper-wire necklace at my throat, adding it to the dozens of others that clink softly against my collarbone. It is the only legacy I will leave behind.

But the dust I do not cough up remains. It is in my joints. It is in my veins. The biomineralization is nearly complete. My skin has taken on a pale, translucent sheen, and through it, I can see the dark, pulsing geothermal heat of my own blood slowing to a crawl. My eyes, entirely clouded with crystalline cataracts, perceive the shadowless light not as glare, but as a warm, structural geometry. I can see the fault lines in the air. I can see the tension in the growing stalks.

Finally, my feet find it. A quiet shelf of raw, undeveloped basalt, jutting out near a thermal vent. The stone is warm, vibrating with the deep geothermal heartbeat of the world. 

I sit. I cross my legs. I rest my empty, scaled hands on my knees. 

The frantic noise of the caldera seems to recede, not because it has quieted, but because my internal resonance is finally matching its frequency. I am no longer fighting the hyper-growth; I am syncing with it. I feel the quartz-brambles sensing my presence, their root-tips turning toward the rich, mineral-dense oasis of my body. I feel the pumice-creepers uncoiling, reaching for the warmth of my skin.

My heart gives one final, heavy, crystalline thud. It is a profound, echoing vibration that travels down my spine and into the basalt shelf. 

And then, the critical mass is reached.

It does not hurt. It is a sudden, breathtaking release of tension. The silicate structures woven through my tissues, my organs, my very soul, expand by a fraction of a millimeter.

*CRACK.*

The sound is like a massive chandelier dropped onto a marble floor. My physical form gives way, bursting outward in a spectacular, blinding eruption of glittering, warm, perfectly formed glass shards. I am no longer a body; I am a cascade of light and geometry, scattering across the dark basalt. For a fleeting, microscopic second, I am everywhere at once—a cloud of shimmering dust and jagged prisms catching the omnidirectional light, refracting it into a million tiny rainbows.

Before the dust can even settle, the forest moves.

I feel them before I see them. The razor-sharp, thirsty roots of the glass-forest pierce the pile of my remains. They wrap around my glittering shards, pulling me down, pulling me apart, drinking the mineral-rich sap of my crystallized blood. The diamond-jawed beetles swarm over my fragments, their mandibles grinding my glass bones into hyper-fertile sand. 

I am being unmade. I am being eaten. I am being woven.

And as a pale stalk of quartz shoots upward from the bedrock, bursting through the center of where my chest used to be, unfurling razor-sharp, translucent leaves in a matter of seconds, I do not feel death. I feel the violent, deafening, and endless joy of becoming.
