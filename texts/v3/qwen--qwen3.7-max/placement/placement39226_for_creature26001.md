I feel the argument before I see it. 

It arrives through the soles of my bare feet—a rapid, staccato flutter of subsonic tremors rippling through the polished pumice bedrock. It is coming from the eastern ward, a frantic vibration that speaks of disagreement, of a narrative thread being violently unraveled. 

I adjust the heavy cowl of my cloak. The molted, translucent carapaces of the lithovore isopods clink softly against the stiff, fibrous mats of crystalline lichen. I do not hear the clinking—the anechoic void of Oth swallows it instantly—but I feel the minute shift in the cloak’s weight against my calcifying shoulders. Above me, the vaulted ceiling of phosphorescent quartz burns with its terrible, shadowless glare. The light is a physical pressure, a blinding white weight, but beneath my deep hood, my vast, obsidian eyes perceive only the cool, polarized geometry of the stone. I see the micro-fractures in the bedrock. I see the exact stress lines of the world.

I begin to walk toward the argument. 

My gait is slow, deliberate. I am old. The damp, ozone-laced air I breathe has been depositing microscopic silica in my lungs for countless cycles, and my joints no longer bend with the fluid grace of the youth. When I lift my foot, the thick, leathery pad peels from the damp stone with a sensation like pulling wet clay. When I set it down, I read the valley. Through my feet, I feel the distant, rhythmic scraping of a hundred giant isopods polishing the western plazas. I feel the swirling, chaotic clouds of the multi-legged herds grazing on the mineral-fungi. And beneath it all, I feel the deep, structural heartbeat of the caldera.

As I approach the eastern ward, the visual frenzy of the city envelops me. It is a dizzying ballet of pale gray rock. Dozens of cloaked figures are swarming a half-finished corbelled tower. They are dismantling the upper tiers, their elongated, multi-jointed fingers moving in a blur, passing flat stones down the line to be repaved into a sweeping, curved retaining wall. It is a loud, violent rebuttal in the kinetic syntax of our people. 

I step into the swarm. The younger builders sense the heavy, subsonic thud of my footsteps. They do not stop, but their frantic energy shifts, making a current for me to wade through. 

I pick up a flat, chalk-white stone from a nearby pile. It is heavy, worn smooth by epochs of ancient water. I carry it to the base of the dismantled tower, where a younger builder—a swift, fluid creature whose skin still holds the faint, soft translucence of youth—is aggressively angling a retaining wall to block a central pathway. 

I do not strike the stone. I do not push the builder. I simply kneel, my stiff joints grinding with a vibration I feel in my teeth, and I place my stone at the exact intersection of their new wall and the old pathway. I angle it precisely three degrees to the left, creating a subtle, sloping channel for the weeping condensation to flow. 

It is a concession, but with a condition. *The wall may stand,* my stone says, *but the water must pass. The history of the river must be remembered.*

The young builder pauses. Their obsidian eyes, wide and dark beneath their lichen hood, read the geometry of my placement. They feel the logic of the slope. Slowly, they nod, a deep dip of their heavy cowl. They pick up the next stone and lay it parallel to mine, continuing the channel. The argument is resolved. The syntax flows on.

Above us, a flock of translucent moth-gliders catches a thermal draft from the blazing ceiling. They dive through the scaffolding, their membranous wings casting fleeting, polarized flickers across the polished stone. I watch the shadows. They dart and swirl, punctuating our labor with moments of dark and light. A shadow falls across my hand just as I reach for a wedge of bone. A pause. A breath. I wait for the light to return before I strike the wedge, shaping the next stone. We are writing the city together, the builders, the beasts, and the light.

But as I work, I feel the stiffness in my fingers. The broad, hardened pads of my hands are losing their sensitivity to the thermal gradients of the rock. The skin on the back of my hands has taken on the porous, chalk-white texture of the pumice bedrock. When I rub my thumb against my forefinger, it feels like two pieces of dry chalk sliding together. 

I am silicifying. The boundary between myself and the Caldera is dissolving.

I stop building. I stand up, and the effort sends a deep, shuddering tremor through the floor. The swarm of builders around me instantly feels it. The frantic, anthill-energy of the ward slows. The kinetic conversation softens into a quiet, deliberate hum of movement. They know. The city is listening to my body.

It is time.

I turn away from the eastern ward and begin the long walk toward the center of the basin. The journey is a slow procession. As I pass through the vast, empty plazas, the other builders fall in behind me, keeping a respectful distance. They do not follow me; they follow the subsonic rhythm of my final march. They carry stones. They carry the tools of bone and abrasive sand. They are preparing the grammar for my final sentence.

I reach the center of the valley, where a new, massive plaza is being laid over the dried bed of the oldest subterranean river. The ground here has been polished to a mirror sheen by the great lithovores. It is perfectly smooth, perfectly flat, and profoundly cold. 

In the center of the plaza, the builders have left a circular space empty. A pause for breath. A blank page.

I step into the center. The dampness of the stone seeps instantly into my bare feet, cooling the deep, grinding ache in my bones. I look up, just for a moment, tilting my heavy cowl back. The phosphorescent quartz ceiling blazes in its fierce, unblinding glory. It is beautiful. It is the eye of the lithic god, watching its thoughts take form.

I lower my head. I bend my knees, the cartilage popping with a dull vibration that travels down my shins and into the earth. I lower my torso. I curl inward, drawing my elongated fingers to my chest, tucking my chin beneath the heavy, stiff folds of my lichen-and-shell cloak. I pull my knees to my chest, assuming the deep, fetal stoop of my ancestors.

I close my obsidian eyes. 

The heavy, oppressive silence of Oth wraps around me like a thick, wet blanket. It is not an empty silence; it is a full, pregnant quiet. It is the silence of a held breath. 

I feel the first heavy drop of condensation fall from the ceiling hundreds of feet above. It strikes the shoulder of my cloak. Then another. And another. The cool, crisp air carries the weeping moisture down to me. I feel the dampness soaking into the fibrous mats of my garment, activating the dormant spores of the mineral-fungi woven into the fabric.

Slowly, the crust begins to form. 

It starts as a faint, itching warmth across my shoulders and the back of my neck. The crystalline lichens are blooming, feeding on the dissolved salts of my sweat and the silica in the air. They weave their geometric, frost-like patterns across my cloak, binding the isopod shells to my skin. The damp dust in the air settles over me, layer by microscopic layer, cementing the lichen, filling the gaps, sealing the folds of my posture.

My breathing slows. The air in my lungs feels thick, heavy with the chalky tang of crushed silica. With every exhale, I breathe out the last of my fleeting, biological warmth. With every inhale, I draw the deep earth into me. 

I feel my skin hardening. The pores seal. The chalk-white crust thickens, turning from a fragile frost into a solid, geometric shell. The stiffness in my joints ceases to be an affliction and becomes a permanence. The pain fades, replaced by a profound, heavy stillness. I am no longer moving. I am settling. I am becoming the bedrock.

Hours pass. Or perhaps days. Time loses its frantic, kinetic edge and stretches into the long, slow epochs of geology. My flesh petrifies. My blood turns to veins of pale quartz. My heart, curled tight against my ribs, fossilizes into a dense, polished cornerstone.

And then, the true transformation begins.

My eyes are sealed shut, forever blind to the blinding glare of the ceiling. My ears, long atrophied, are encased in solid rock. But my feet—my bare, calloused feet, now fused seamlessly into the polished pumice of the plaza floor—awaken to a vast, overwhelming symphony.

I feel the entire Caldera. 

I feel the sweeping, undulating curves of the valley floor. I feel the deep, ancient roots of the stone miles beneath me. I feel the shifting weight of the city. 

Through the soles of my petrified feet, I feel the builders returning to the plaza. I feel the soft, muffled padding of their bare feet on the damp rock. They are moving around me. They are bringing the stones.

A heavy, flat slab of pale gray rock is lifted. I feel the shift in the air pressure, the subtle change in the thermal gradient. Then, the stone is set gently upon my back. 

It is a perfect fit. The weight is immense, but my calcified spine does not buckle. I am the load-bearing arch. I am the foundation of the new corbelled vault. 

Another stone is placed. And another. The kinetic syntax resumes around me, a frantic, beautiful ballet of unmaking and remaking. I feel the arguments, the concessions, the narratives, and the histories vibrating through my stone body. I feel the scraping of the lithovores polishing the paths that lead to my plaza. I feel the fleeting, polarized shadows of the gliders dancing across the stones that rest upon my shoulders.

I am no longer a builder. I am the built. 

I am the silence that holds the city together. I am the mortar of the anechoic void. I am the deep, unbroken meditation of the lithic god, resting at the bottom of the world, waiting to feel the footsteps of the future.
