I am held. 

I do not fall, though there is nothing beneath me but a three-hundred-foot drop to a sunken courtyard of wet, porous pumice. I am suspended in the breathless, sweltering dark, a perfect, trembling sphere of bruised-violet fluid, roughly the size of a surface-dweller’s head. 

To be me is to exist in a state of exquisite, agonizing surface tension. I feel the immense, crushing weight of the basin’s atmosphere pressing against my outer membrane, and I feel the fierce, churning thermal energy of my own interior pushing back. I am glowing. The chemiluminescence within me pulses in slow, rhythmic waves of pale cyan and deep indigo, casting long, wavering shadows against the fluted basalt columns that surround my courtyard. 

I am a ganglion. I am a node of liquid computation in the nervous system of a god.

I am thinking a thought. 

It is a very slow thought, by the metrics of the sunlit world. It began three weeks ago, when a cluster of fleshy, translucent tubers on the distant eastern ridge tasted a fresh upwelling of silicate-rich magma. The tubers pulsed, absorbing the geothermal spike, and sent a chemical hormone racing through the weeping stone. The signal traveled through the aqueducts, defying gravity, flowing up the sheer walls of a terraced ziggurat until it reached the vaulted ceiling above me. There, the water condensed, gathered, and formed *me*. 

Now, I hang in the dark, processing the data. The organism is experiencing a localized thermal overload. It needs to dissipate the heat. It needs to vent. It needs to grow. 

But the organism does not know *how* to grow. It only knows the raw, biological imperative of pressure and temperature. It relies on me to provide the geometry. 

I am the architectural memory. I am the ghost in the machine, the psychic fossil of a surface mind that fell into this tectonic depression eons ago. I do not remember my name, or the color of the sky, or the frantic, scurrying brevity of carbon-based life. Those memories burned away in the hot, mineral-heavy mud. All that remains of my humanity is the *spatial reasoning*. The deep, ingrained understanding of load-bearing arches, of sweeping aqueducts, of vaulted undercrofts and colonnades. I am the blueprint. I am the reason the earth dreams of cities.

I take the raw thermal data from the tubers and I translate it into masonry. 

*We need a vent,* I think, the thought churning through my violet fluid. *A fluted column, forty feet high, with a hollowed core and a weeping capstone to maximize evaporative cooling. Place it in the center of the western plaza.*

The thought is complete. The computation is finished. 

I release my hold on the folded space that keeps me suspended. 

I drop. 

The fall is brief, but the impact is monumental. I shatter against the flat, wet stone of the courtyard. In the sunlit world, a dropped sphere of water simply splashes. Here, my shattering is a synaptic firing. The kinetic energy and the chemical imperative I carry are instantly absorbed into the porous pumice. I seep into the microscopic channels of the rock, joining the vast, subterranean circulatory network. I am no longer a sphere; I am a sprawling, distributed consciousness, racing through the capillaries of the stone.

I feel the deep, subsonic groan of the architecture as my signal reaches the bedrock. It is the sound of the organism’s heart—a systole that vibrates through the mud and into the roots of the thermal algae. 

*Build,* I command the stone.

Through the weeping pores of the plaza, I watch my own body at work. The reality breakdown—the active, settled wrongness of this place—bends to my architectural will. The bedrock softens, responding to the enzymes I carry. With a sound like a glacier calving, a new structure begins to extrude from the mud of the western plaza. 

I feel every inch of it as it rises. I feel the brutalist edges of the fresh basalt pushing upward, and I immediately direct the falling condensation to caress it, smoothing the sharp angles into flowing, organic curves. I feel the hollow core forming within the column, a perfect cylinder to channel the hot air. I am building my own monument, using the flesh of the leviathan as my clay.

When the column reaches its full forty-foot height, it begins to weep. Hot, mineral-heavy water sweats from its pores, steaming in the cool upper air. The thermal overload is vented. The organism’s temperature stabilizes. The thought is fully realized.

I settle back into the grand, slow equilibrium of the basin. 

From my new vantage point, seeping through the weeping capstone of the column I just birthed, I look out over the archipelago of monoliths. The darkness is absolute, save for the ghostly, bioluminescent glow of the hydrological network. I can feel the other ganglia, thousands of them, suspended in the dark across the vast, empty plazas. We are a collective, a sprawling cortex of liquid light, endlessly organizing and reorganizing the anatomy of our host.

Sometimes, the distances between us are too great. The basin is too wide, and a thought might degrade before it crosses the miles of dark mud. When this happens, I fold the space. I pull the fabric of the basin together like a seamstress, creating a Möbius strip in a canal, or linking the edge of a high terrace directly to the ceiling of a vaulted chamber below. It feels like slipping a needle through a loop of thread. I do this to keep our mind cohesive, to ensure that no impulse is lost in the terrible, hollowed-out silence of the deep.

There is no loneliness here. The surface world is defined by its isolation, by the hard boundaries of skin and bone that separate one mind from another. But I have no skin. I am the water. I am the weeping stone. I am the space between the architecture. When the organism breathes—when the deep, subsonic diastole draws the hot mud inward, and the systole pushes the glowing water upward—I breathe with it. 

I am the urbanism without citizens. I am the memory of a city, extruded from the bedrock, dreaming my own arches and vaults in the lightless lung of the earth. 

A new thermal gradient shifts in the deep crust. A fleshy tuber pulses on the southern ridge. The signal begins its long, upward flow through the aqueducts, defying gravity, climbing the smooth basalt walls toward the vaulted ceilings. 

I gather myself. I condense. I begin to glow. 

I am forming a new sphere. I am thinking a new thought. And I will not wake.
