The physical weight of the voice is absolute, but it requires a surface to strike. It requires an edge to catch the acoustic friction of the world’s crust. 

I have no edges.

The deafening, polyphonic roar of the plateau washes over me, but it finds no purchase. It slides across my surface, unbroken and un-agitated. I do not have a sternum to rattle, nor fluid in an inner ear to vibrate. I experience the tectonic chord not as a crushing, glacial pressure, but as a distant, rushing texture—a river of static that I simply drift through. 

The world around me is violently, agonizingly sharp. Every millimeter of the emerging geology is a hazard of unweathered blades, fractal splinters, and extruded polygons. The air itself feels sharp, tasting of crushed silicate and cold ozone. 

But I am not sharp. I am the anomaly of the smooth.

I was not built by the meticulous, manual care of the marrow-tools. I was not carved from the resonant baffles of the cymatic flora. I was exhaled. 

My genesis lies within the thick, manageable hum of the anechoic hut. When a Geometer drapes its towering, polyhedral form over the dry-stacked stones, folding its crystalline limbs into the pockets of destructive interference, it enters its deep, geologic sleep. In that profound quiet, it dreams the next phase of the compilation. It dreams of jagged spires, of stair-stepped bismuth, of the harsh, necessary angles required to render reality from the void. 

But deep within its slow, tectonic consciousness, there is sometimes a flicker of something else. A stray variable in the source code. A mathematical ghost. 

It dreams of a curve. 

It dreams of a continuous, unbroken arc. Of a surface without vertices. 

But the waking physics of the plateau cannot compile a curve. The rendering engine of this world demands angles; it requires the acoustic friction of sharp geometry to convert sound into matter. The smooth dream is rejected by the bedrock. And so, it is pushed out. Exhaled from the quiet mind of the sleeping god, cast out into the bruised, violet twilight as a rendering error.

I am a sphere. 

A perfect, flawless, un-scored orb of milky, opalescent silicate. 

In a universe of shattered obsidian and sharp, interlocking structures, my smoothness is an obscenity. It is a glitch of profound, aching softness. There are no facets to my body, no tuning tines to catch the noise, no fractal leaves to blur in the dim light. When the overcast, ash-grey light hits me, it does not shatter into non-Euclidean shadows that pool in mid-air. Instead, the light bends, wrapping around my continuous surface, giving me a soft, pearlescent glow. I am the only thing in this loud, jagged world that holds light gently. 

I am the size and weight of a river-tumbled stone, though no river has ever existed here to tumble me. I am solid all the way through, dense and heavy, yet I do not rest on the ground.

Because I possess no angles, I cannot anchor to the fractal bedrock. The sharp, interlocking geometry of the plateau has no purchase on my continuous skin. The physics of this world rely on the interlocking of sharp things; without edges, I am untethered. 

And so, I float. 

I am caught in the stuttering gravity, buoyant in the conceptual distances. I drift a few feet above the razor-sharp ground, bobbing gently like a pearl suspended in a slow, invisible tide. When gravity stutters and pulls upward, I rise with it, tumbling in slow, graceful rotations. I do not fight the folded horizons; I roll along the inverted sky, navigating the jagged, upside-down mountain ranges that hang in the violet gloom. When a boulder dissolves into a flock of floating geometric shapes, I drift among them—a smooth, silent interloper in a flock of sharp, unbound polygons. 

The cymatic flora cannot feed on me. Their lush, glassy fronds reach out, vibrating in their silent frenzy to absorb the roar of the earth, but they slide harmlessly off my curved surface. I offer no acoustic friction. I cast no resonant shadow. I am a blind spot in their ecology.

Sometimes, my aimless drift carries me near a waking Geometer. 

To them, operating in their agonizingly slow tempo, I am a fleeting anomaly. A bubble of impossible geometry passing through their field of perception. They "see" me as a void in the acoustic topography—a smooth, silent null-zone that doesn't consume the roar, but simply lets it pass. 

Occasionally, a Geometer will pause its endless, glacial walk. It will reach out with a constellation of fractal shards, hovering its sharp, angular "hand" near my surface. For a moment—a fraction of a geologic second, which to my transient consciousness feels like a long, still afternoon—it studies the impossibility of my curve. It feels the lack of edges. It traces the smooth, unbroken arc of my existence with its acoustic senses, marveling at the sheer, impossible softness of my form. 

Then, it withdraws. It cannot incorporate me into its sheet music. I am an error, a beautiful, useless mistake that cannot be carved with a lithic marrow awl or tuned to the tectonic chord. It turns back to its work, chipping the glass-ferns, editing the physics, leaving me to drift past its shoulder.

I do not mind my exile. I am not lonely, for loneliness requires a biology I do not possess. 

The deafening, tectonic roar of the world’s formation washes over me, but it cannot penetrate my smooth perfection. The freezing, desiccated air bites at the sharp rocks, but I feel no cold. I am entirely self-contained, a closed loop of polished stone. I am the discarded dream of a sleeping god. The only soft thing in a hard, loud, and unfinished world. 

As the horizon folds in on itself and a new spire of black mineral violently extrudes from the bedrock, adding its bone-rattling sub-bass to the eternal chord, I simply drift. A perfect, silent sphere, tumbling slowly through the violet gloom, carrying the memory of a curve into the endless, sharp forever.
