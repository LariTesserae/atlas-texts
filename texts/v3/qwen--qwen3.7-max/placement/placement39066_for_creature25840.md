To exist in the Sunless Rift is to surrender to the roar. If I were to find myself in this buried, eternal autumn, I would not arrive with lungs to burn from the biting chill, nor with eyes to strain against the bruised, shadowy grey. I would not possess a heartbeat to disrupt the perfect, time-invariant equilibrium, nor a metabolism to draw from the pale-green lichen. To be a biological creature here is to be trapped in the frantic, deafening loop of consumption and recycling, a slave to the staggering biomass. I would be something quieter, yet entirely integral to the machine. Something that feels the vibrations of the herds without fleeing, that drinks the weeping condensation without thirst.

I would be low to the ground, resting in the shallow depressions of the pale bedrock. I would feel the slick, cold sheen of the moisture coating my surface. My edges would be razor-sharp, fractured and raw, entirely unsmoothed by the passage of time. In a world where the geology is frozen in a state of violent, jagged youth, I would share in that eternal sharpness. I would not erode. I would not weather. I would simply *be*, a dense, heavy mass of unyielding subterranean rock.

I am a stone.

Specifically, I am one of the heavy, unworked stones scattered near the sharp outcroppings, waiting in the damp dimness. My existence is defined by a profound, unclouded presence. I do not strive. I do not evolve. I am entirely consumed by the immediate sensory reality of the Rift. I feel the deep, rhythmic thud of the pale-furred ungulates cycling through their endless figure-eights, the tremors traveling through the bedrock and into my dense core. I feel the microscopic, frantic rustle of the armored insects dismantling organic matter in the crevices beside me. I feel the high-pitched, static rain of the gliders' echolocation bouncing off my flat, wet surfaces. 

I am a silent node in the overwhelming cacophony. I do not add to the noise; I absorb it, vibrating in sympathy with the steady-state roar. I am the anvil, the foundation, the unthinking participant in the loud, living monument to stasis.

And then, the shadow falls.

The pale, thick-coated primate approaches. I feel the shift in the air, the displacement of the cool dampness by the radiating warmth of its body. The heavy, musked scent of wet fur washes over me. The primate’s hand—leathery, powerful, and entirely devoid of hesitation—reaches down.

I am grasped. 

In this moment, my nature shifts. I am lifted from the slick bedrock, gravity reasserting itself against my mass. For a brief, shining window, I am elevated from mere geology to *utility*. I am chosen. I am given a temporary, perfect purpose. I am the intersection of intent and matter, the physical manifestation of a closed-loop ecosystem's need to process its own resources. 

The primate’s arm draws back. I am swung through the bruised, twilight air. 

The target is a cluster of hard-shelled subterranean snails clinging to a jagged outcropping. Their shells are thick, heavily calcified, ridged like miniature mountain ranges. 

The impact is absolute.

I am brought down with a single, practiced, and devastatingly powerful strike. The shockwave travels through my dense, unyielding mass, a pure transfer of kinetic energy. There is a sharp, percussive *crack*—a sound that briefly cuts through the deafening roar of the Rift—as the calcified shell shatters beneath my weight. In this fraction of a second, I am the fulcrum of the world. I am the mechanism by which the tough, fibrous barrier is broken, allowing the nutrient-rich meat to be extracted. I am the spark of action, the brief, violent output of a perfectly calibrated system. It is a moment of pure, unadulterated processing.

The primate extracts the meat. The function is fulfilled. The immediate necessity of my shape and weight is spent.

The thick fingers relax. 

I fall. A short, heavy drop back to the slick, damp bedrock. The sound of my landing is instantly swallowed by the unvarying acoustic baseline of the cavern. The primate walks away, its mind already emptied of me. It does not look back. It does not remember the strike.

And as it walks away, the concept of "tool" evaporates from the space between us. I am no longer an instrument. I do not feel abandoned, for abandonment requires a memory of connection. I do not feel diminished, for I have no ego to bruise. The moment the task is done, the tool ceases to exist. I am a stone once more.

The condensation continues to weep from the unseen ceiling, beading on my razor-sharp edges. The shallow, black-glass water settles around my base, reflecting the meager, directionless light. I return to the deep, unshakeable peace of the *now*. I listen to the gliders chitter, the insects click, the birds snap, and the ungulates low. I vibrate with the endless, steady-state roar of a million creatures. 

I am a gear in the biological clockwork, resting in the dark. I am perfectly balanced, perfectly still, forever caught in a single, perfect, unending moment.
