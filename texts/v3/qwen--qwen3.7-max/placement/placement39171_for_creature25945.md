I do not wake to light, for the light here is a constant, bruised apology. I wake to the tremor. 

It begins as a faint vibration in the marrow of my bones, a subsonic ripple traveling through miles of bone-smooth stone. A leviathan is walking, somewhere beyond the curtain of freezing fog. 

I am pressed flat against the sweeping, aerodynamic curve of the plateau. I am a creature of the spaces between, an inhabitant of the vast, sweeping emptiness that the giants leave in their wake. The silence of this world is not empty; it is a heavy, saturated fluid, and I am submerged in it. To move here is to swim through a medium that demands absolute, flawless stillness.

My body is a broad, heavy teardrop of mottled indigo and weeping grey, perfectly contoured to the unbroken gradients of the rock. I have no legs to break the acoustic surface of the world, no joints to click, no claws to scratch. Instead, my underside is a vast, undulating skirt of dense, oil-slicked muscle. 

When I move, I do not step. I ripple. 

A slow, peristaltic wave travels from my leading edge to my trailing tail, creating a microscopic vacuum that pulls me forward in a seamless, frictionless glide. I secrete a thin, glycoprotein slime that prevents my flesh from freezing to the damp stone. As I slide, my immense weight and the frictionless glide buff the rock. I am a burnisher of the high plains. Where the leviathans grind the earth into dust, I polish the dust back into glass.

I am blind. My eyes are vestigial, sealed beneath thick, frosted membranes of pale tissue. I do not need to see the pale, wire-thin lichens or the glass-still pools; I read the world through the tactile language of my belly. I feel the microscopic topography of the stone. I feel the shallow, fossilized footprints filled with near-freezing water. 

As I glide, my broad, flattened snout sweeps the surface, filtering the dampness. I drink the rime ice. I consume the drowned spores of the frost-grass, the silica-shelled diatoms, and the fine, glassy dust left by the giants. Inside my wide, shallow cavity, my secondary stomachs grind this silicate meal. But I do not excrete waste in the traditional sense. The refined silica is extruded through the pores of my trailing edge as a fine, warm paste. As I glide forward, this paste is smeared and buffed into the stone by my heavy tail, filling in the micro-fissures left by the frost, maintaining the hull-like perfection of the plateau. I am the caretaker of the smooth.

But my most vital function is not geological; it is acoustic. 

The wind still exhales. The frost still cracks. The world still tries to make a sound. I am an acoustic baffle. Within the broad, domed shell of my back lie dozens of hollow, fluid-filled resonant chambers. When the sharp crack of freezing ice echoes across the plains, or when a sudden draft of wind creates a low whistle through a crevice, my chambers vibrate in sympathy. I absorb the kinetic sting of the sound waves, unraveling their frequencies within my fluid-filled core until they dissolve into a faint, imperceptible warmth. 

I eat the noise. I digest the disruptions. I ensure that the heavy, muffled stillness remains unbroken. I am the warden of the hush.

The subsonic thrum grows stronger. The rhythm matches the slow, patient heartbeat of the world. The leviathan is close. 

I stop. I flatten my muscular skirt entirely, sealing my edges against the stone, becoming nothing more than a subtle darkening of the polished rock. I surrender to the medium of silence. 

The fog parts. The colossal, domed carapace of the beast blots out the bruised twilight. It is a moving mountain of chitin and blubber, venting slow, silent blooms of steam. It does not see me. It does not need to. 

Its vast, spongy footpad descends. I am directly in its path. 

There is no panic, only a deep, biological resignation. The pad does not strike me; it settles. It is so impossibly soft, so yielding, that it molds around my low, teardrop profile. I am pressed gently, firmly, into the shallow depression of the rock. 

For the long, suspended duration of its stance, I am enveloped in the dark, yielding warmth of the giant. I feel the staggering heat of its metabolic furnaces radiating down through the pad, thawing the deep cold in my core. I feel the grinding, hydraulic force of its mandibles echoing through its limbs. I am cradled in the machinery of the living earth. 

Then, the pad lifts. 

The beast glides on, disappearing into the slate-grey mist, leaving no sound but the faint, wet parting of the fog. 

I am left behind in a perfect, glass-smooth bowl—a new footprint, already weeping with condensation. I unseal my edges. I ripple my skirt. I begin to glide again, my snout sweeping the fresh moisture, my tail buffing the new depression into a mirror shine. 

Time does not march here. It seeps. 

I do not know how long I have lived, nor how long I will live. In this world of charcoal and indigo, there is no urgency, no frantic struggle. There is only the slow, patient glide across the continental curve. There is only the damp cold, the weeping stone, and the heavy, beautiful silence that I help to keep. 

I am the polisher of the plains. I am the eater of echoes. And as I slide into the mist, I leave nothing behind but the mirror-shine of the stone, and the perfect, unbroken quiet of the world.
