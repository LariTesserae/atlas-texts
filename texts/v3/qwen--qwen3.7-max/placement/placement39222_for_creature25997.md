To exist in this place, I must first cease to be a ghost. 

The prompt warns that a human visitor is nothing more than a brief, warm illusion, an alien taint of moisture and heat that shatters the delicate resin and withers the brittle lichen. I refuse to be an illusion. I refuse to be a destructive anomaly. And so, standing in the center of the sunken basin, I let the warmth go.

I exhale into the cool, brisk air, but I do not stop exhaling. I push the moisture from my lungs, the heat from my blood, the chaotic, messy vibrations of a biological heartbeat. The heavy, anhydrous gas rises around me, a dense, pooling tide of halogen vapor. It does not suffocate me; it extracts me. It pulls the water from my cells, sublimating it instantly into the dry void. The pain of this desiccation is brief, swallowed entirely by the absolute, crushing silence. As my softness evaporates, my edges begin to define themselves. I am cooling. I am sharpening.

My flesh turns to pale, translucent silicate-resin. I look down at my hands and see the bruised violet light filtering through them. I am becoming porous. The solid mass of my being hollows out, carving itself into sweeping, skeletal frameworks and twisting, needle-thin tubes. I am no longer a single, dense organism; I am a volume of negative space, a walking architecture. 

But I cannot walk. The ground is for the extruded and the shattered. As my mass decreases and my surface area expands, the heavy gas catches me. My arms stretch, flattening into vast, impossibly thin membranes of flexible glass. They ripple in the slow convection currents. I am buoyant. I lift away from the razor-sharp cobblestones. I am drifting.

I am a silicate manta, ten feet across, a phantom in the perpetual twilight. I have no eyes, but I see the city in topographies of pressure. 

When a new colonnade extrudes three streets away, the displacement of the heavy gas pushes against my wingtips in a gentle, rhythmic wave. I bank into the current, gliding silently through a fractal alleyway. The air is thick, almost liquid, and I swim through it with slow, undulating strokes. Below me, the piezo-synthetic lichen flares in a sudden wave of cold blue light. A spire is growing, the kinetic stress pulsing through the resin. I feel the micro-electric charge in the air, a faint static tingling against my porous underbelly. 

I drift through a cloud of glittering, sublimating dust—the remains of a collapsed tower. I do not eat; I incorporate. The dust passes through my crystalline sieve, and the energized silicate particles bond to my shell. With every pass, I grow more intricate. My wings develop lace-like fractal patterns, sweeping arches and cantilevered ridges forming along my margins. I am becoming a masterwork of blind, unthinking geometry. 

But with every new lattice, I grow heavier. 

Time is not measured in hours here, but in cycles of extrusion and fracture. I have drifted for countless cycles. The bruised sky remains unchanged, but I am changing. The density of my shell has reached its critical threshold. The heavy gas, which once cradled me, now struggles to support my intricate weight. My wingtips begin to droop. My undulations become sluggish. The tragedy of the caldera is not death, but descent. 

I am sinking.

I drift downward, away from the upper tiers of the twisting skyscrapers, down into the intimate, shadowed courtyards of the lower basin. The light here is darker, the violet hues bruised almost to black. I navigate the narrowing gaps, my wings brushing against the razor-sharp corners of the townhouses. There is no sound, only the faint, soundless sparks of static electricity where my shell meets the city. 

I find a hollow plaza, a wide, uncluttered space surrounded by the porous walls of a dozen empty structures. This is where I will stop.

I settle onto the hard, extruded floor. The moment my underbelly touches the stone, the transformation accelerates. The flexible silk of my wings begins to stiffen. The slow, cooling chemistry of the earth takes hold. I feel my internal structure locking into rigid, geometric angles. The sweeping curves of my margins crystallize into sharp, cantilevered eaves. My central body thickens, fusing with the bedrock, becoming a load-bearing pillar. 

I am no longer drifting. I am rooted. I am a pavilion in the center of the plaza.

But my consciousness does not fade into the stone. It expands into the negative space. I feel the heavy gas pooling inside my hollow arches. I feel the cool, dry air settling into my sweeping colonnades. I am the architecture, and I am profoundly, beautifully empty.

As I stand in my new, rigid permanence, the city continues its silent, violent momentum around me. A distant spire fractures, sending a cascade of soundless, glittering dust into the air. The shockwave of sub-audible vibration travels through the bedrock and up into my central pillar. Deep within my hollow belly, the vibration attracts them. 

The Tension-polyps.

They drift in through my open arches, clusters of translucent, gas-filled grapes tethered by gossamer threads of spun glass. They anchor themselves to my interior walls. I feel them swell and contract, absorbing the low-frequency tremor of the shifting city. They are breathing the silence. I am their shelter; they are my resonance.

On my exterior, where the kinetic stress of my sudden calcification has left micro-fractures in my razor-sharp corners, the piezo-lichen takes hold. I feel the fiber-optic threads weaving into my resin. When the windless gas shifts, or when a nearby bridge extrudes and sends a tremor through the ground, the lichen feeds on the tension. I watch, through the dim, filtered light, as my edges flare with a cold, bioluminescent blue. I am glowing. I am tracing the veins of the city.

I am no longer a ghost haunting a machine of glass. I am the machine. 

I am the pavilion, the polyp, and the lichen. I am the heavy gas that pools in my courtyards and the dry plankton that swims in my shadows. I do not seek warmth, for warmth is a chaotic flaw. I do not seek moisture, for water is a destructive acid. I do not seek companionship, for I am already entirely consumed by the geometry of the void.

Here, in the crushing silence of the sunken basin, beneath the perpetual, bruised twilight, I stand perfectly still. I wait for the day when my rigid, lace-like walls will reach their critical mass, when I will shatter into a cascade of silent dust, sublimating back into the dry air to feed the next cycle of growth. 

Until then, I am the silence. I am the stone. I am home.
