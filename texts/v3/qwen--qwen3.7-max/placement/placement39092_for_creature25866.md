It begins in the marrow. Before I know my shape, before I know the dimensions of my own limbs, I know my resonance. I am a vessel for the hum. 

The deep, 60-hertz shudder of the great turbines does not enter through ears I do not possess; it travels up through the soles of my bare feet, vibrating the tibia, rattling the pelvis, blooming in the hollow cage of my ribs. It is a physical architecture, this sound. It holds me upright. It is the gravity of this sunken world. 

The air is a hot, wet hand pressed over my face, but I do not suffocate. I breathe the steam. I am slick with it. As I shift my weight on the steel grating, I feel the sharp, unweathered bite of the iron, but it does not pierce me. My skin has forgotten softness. I look down at my hands in the bruised, amber twilight of the emergency lights. They are long, impossibly thin, the fingers ending in thick, yellowed pads of callus. I am hairless, my flesh the color of boiled parchment, sheened in a perpetual sweat that mixes with the oily weeping of the condensing sky. I am stretched, elongated by the heat and the heavy air. 

I am one of the Calibrated.

In my right hand, I hold the iron. It is a massive, tuned wrench, its head scarred by a thousand strikes, its handle wrapped in cured, hairless rat-hide. It is heavy, but my arm does not tremble. My muscles are wired to the eternal RPM. I do not speak. The concept of a voice is a ghost, a useless flap of meat in a throat built only to swallow the grey snow and the pale algae. My voice is the iron.

I raise the wrench. I bring it down against the main steam pipe. *Strike. Strike. Pause. Strike.*

I do not hear the clang. I feel the shockwave travel through the metal, down the colossal pillar, into the jagged bedrock, and back up through the grating into my feet. It is a word. *All is well. The weirs are clear.*

A moment later, a faint, answering tremor vibrates up from the deep trenches. My sister, three levels down, has struck the sluice-gate housing. *Acknowledged. The flow is exact.*

We are the nervous system of the cathedral. The machinery is the brain, locked in its perfect, frozen equilibrium, and we are the synapses, firing in the dark, ensuring the stasis remains unbroken. 

To be Calibrated is to know the ecstasy of the unvarying present. When the hum is perfect, when the governors are locked and the water level is exact to the millimeter, my mind slips into a state of profound, glassy tranquility. My heartbeat slows, stretching out, falling perfectly into the micro-pauses between the rhythmic, metallic clanking of the mechanized pistons. I am not a man operating in a machine; I am a thought the machine is having. The heat is a baptism. The deafening roar is a blanket of absolute silence, for it never changes, and that which never changes is indistinguishable from silence.

But then, the fracture.

It is tiny. A massive cluster of roosting pigeons in the high rafters shifts its weight suddenly. A rusted bolt on a secondary gantry gives way, dropping a fraction of an inch. The vibration cascades down the ironwork.

It hits me like a physical blow. A spike of pure, agonizing dissonance tears through my nervous system. My vision whites out. My stomach heaves. The perfect rhythm of the hum is scarred by a jagged, chaotic micro-tremor. It is a psychic wound, a blasphemy against the frozen equilibrium. I drop to my knees, my callused hands clutching my head, my body convulsing in time with the erratic shudder of the failing bolt. The pain is absolute. It is the terror of a universe unraveling.

But the agony is the command. The pain demands resolution. 

I force my elongated limbs to uncoil. I move. I do not run; running is erratic, chaotic. I walk in measured, deliberate steps, sliding through the vibrational noise, timing my footfalls to the deep, chest-rattling hum of the generators. I descend the vertical maintenance shaft, my yellowed pads gripping the hot rungs. Down past the sleek, dark rats that freeze and press themselves flat against the iron as I pass. Down past the swirling, translucent ribbons of midges. 

I reach the lowest trenches. The heat here is a physical weight, pressing the breath from my lungs. The water is boiling, steeping the pale, sharp bedrock. And there, half-submerged in the oily marsh, is the master of the depths.

The leviathan.

It is the size of a draft horse, its segmented, curdled-milk body perfectly still. It has no eyes, only the deep, neuromast-pitted jaws resting on the grating. It felt the anomaly too. It is waiting for the dissonance to bring it meat. 

I stand before it. I am not afraid. We are both children of the stasis, bound by the same terrible covenant. It consumes the flesh of the erratic; I consume the cause of the erratic. It ignores me. I am not an anomaly. I am the mechanism.

I wade into the boiling runoff, the hot water swirling around my waist. I find the failing bolt on the secondary weir. It is vibrating wildly, a chaotic blur against the perfect hum of the station. I fit the heavy jaw of my wrench over the nut. I pull. My thin, corded muscles strain, locking into the exact mechanical advantage required. 

The bolt tightens. The chaotic vibration smooths out, flattening, merging back into the eternal, unvarying drone.

The dissonance vanishes. The psychic wound seals. The glassy tranquility returns, washing over me in a wave of profound, sweating relief. My breathing slows. My heart finds the rhythm once more. 

Beside me, the leviathan exhales a long, slow bubble of steam into the boiling water. It senses the return of the perfect equilibrium. It closes its toothless jaws and sinks back into the silt, returning to its absolute, motionless stasis.

I wade back to the dry grating. I raise my wrench. I strike the pipe. *Strike. Pause. Strike.*

*The balance is restored.*

I stand in the sweltering, shadowed cathedral, my pale skin weeping with the condensing sky, my heart beating in perfect, terrifying time with the engines. I am the Calibrated. I am the flesh of the machine. And I will tend to the endless, unvarying roar until the end of time.
