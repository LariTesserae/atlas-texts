I do not wake to light, for there is none to wake to. I wake to the pressure. 

It begins as a subtle shift in the heavy, blood-warm water outside my chamber—a slight thinning of the viscosity, a micro-gradient of cooling that tells me the thermal convection cycle is turning. The grand, slow breath of the basin is exhaling. 

I uncoil from the deep, porous marrow of the metropolis. 

I am not a creature of the open water, nor am I entirely of the stone. I exist in the boundary layer, the interstitial threshold where the secreted architecture of the reef meets the crushing embrace of the deep. My body is a long, pale ribbon of translucent, segmented muscle, entirely devoid of eyes. I have no need for them. My skin is a continuous, highly derived tympanic membrane, a single, vast ear stretched over a delicate lattice of internal fluid-chambers. I perceive the world entirely through the geometry of vibration and the chemical taste of the current.

I slide forward, extruding my head and anterior segments from the smooth, oval hollow of my tunnel into the vast, dark volume of the vaulted colonnade. 

The water here is incredibly dense, pressing against my mucus-slicked flesh with a heavy, comforting weight. I taste the environment: the sharp, metallic tang of sulfur rising from the distant vents, the sweet, chalky dust of calcium carbonate, and the faint, organic musk of the copepod swarms drifting through the plaza beyond. I extend my sensory palps, delicate fleshy whips that tremble in the micro-currents, mapping the topography of the wall around my burrow.

The bone-stone here is scarred. 

Last cycle, a gathering of the great pelagic gliders occurred in this very colonnade. Their subsonic chorus, weaponized by the architecture to focus across the plaza, was slightly off-axis. The resulting acoustic shear has micro-fractured the biomineral surface of my wall. To a human eye, the pale, melted-wax curve of the stone would look perfectly intact. But to my palpating flesh, the surface is rough, pitted with microscopic fissures. The acoustic geometry is ruined. If the next song is cast against this wall, the sound waves will scatter, losing their cohesion, failing to carry the vital thermal and navigational data to the mixing chambers above.

The city is an instrument, and I am its luthier. 

I press my broad, flattened mandibles against the fractured calcium. From my sub-esophageal glands, I secrete a precise, localized wash of mild, chelating acid. It softens the rough, shattered edges of the biomineral, dissolving the microscopic faults. Then, I begin to polish. I undulate my body, rubbing my smooth, chitin-reinforced belly against the softened stone in wide, rhythmic sweeps. As I work, I secrete a secondary compound—a fast-hardening, glassy biological resin that fills the pores and cures instantly in the mineral-rich water. 

I am tuning the wall. I am restoring the sensual, undulating curve of the acoustic baffle. I work in the absolute dark, guided only by the tactile feedback of the stone and the faint, ambient hum of the bedrock. There is a profound, homeostatic satisfaction in this labor. I am smoothing the throat of the city so that it may sing clearly.

As I polish, the water pressure suddenly spikes. 

A shadow of displacement falls over me. I retract slightly into my tunnel, anchoring my posterior segments with microscopic chitinous hooks, and let my sensory palps drift in the slipstream. 

A Glider is passing. 

I cannot see its staggering scale, but I feel the immense, gravitational reality of its mass. The water ahead of it is compressed, pushing into my tunnel in a heavy, rolling wave. Then comes the creature itself. The sheer volume of its gelatinous, hydrostatic body displaces the avenue, creating a localized vacuum that pulls at my flesh. I feel the rhythmic, powerful undulations of its broad pectoral fins as it catches the rising thermal updraft, riding the warm water toward the upper tiers. 

As it glides past, its trailing sensory cilia—the shimmering, translucent train of its regal gown—brush against the stone near my burrow. The touch is impossibly delicate, a ghostly caress of thousands of fleshy tendrils reading the chemical memory of the wall. In its wake, the turbulent slipstream is alive with the darting, serpentine shapes of its attendant eels, their small, rapid movements creating a chaotic staccato of high-frequency clicks against my skin. 

I wait until the pressure normalizes, until the heavy *swish* of the Glider’s passing fades into the muffled acoustics of the deep. Then, I resume my work.

I am not alone in the walls. We are a million silent siblings, a hidden mycelium of pale flesh threading through the millions of tons of calcium carbonate. We do not possess a hive mind, nor do we communicate in the complex, mournful drones of the Gliders. Our language is seismology. 

A sudden, sharp vibration travels through the stone, vibrating against my belly. *Tap-tap-scrape.* It is a sister, three plazas away, signaling a blockage in a descending downdraft baffle. A low, continuous tremor from the deep trenches below tells me the Elder Leviathans are shifting in their sleep, their massive bodies grinding against the lowest ziggurats, altering the baseline thermal output of the basin. We read the structural integrity of the megapolis through its bones. We are the immune system of the reef, the unseen maintainers of the fluid machine, ensuring the architecture never clogs, never fractures, never falls out of tune.

My work on the colonnade wall is finished. I run my palps over the newly polished curve. It is flawless. The surface is as smooth as wet glass, the acoustic angle perfectly calibrated to catch and focus a low-frequency wave. 

I retreat deep into my tunnel, coiling my long body into the dark, safe warmth of my chamber, and I wait.

It does not take long. The thermal cycle peaks, and the water in the plaza outside begins to slow, thickening as it enters the mixing chamber. The Gliders have arrived. 

I feel them before I hear them. Dozens of massive pressure waves overlapping, aligning along the smoothed, curving walls of the colonnade. They are taking their positions. The water grows incredibly still, the convection currents pausing in the heavy, anticipatory density of the deep.

Then, the first Glider sings.

It is a subsonic pulse, so low it bypasses hearing entirely and strikes directly at the fluid chambers of my chest. *Thrummmmm.* 

The sound wave hits the wall I have just polished. 

Because the curve is perfect, the stone does not absorb or scatter the energy. It catches the wave, amplifies it, and focuses it inward. The biomineral architecture begins to vibrate. The vibration transfers into the stone around my tunnel, and from the stone, into my flesh. 

More Gliders join in. The chorus builds, a complex, interlocking lattice of deep hums, rhythmic clicks, and mournful, crushing drones. The sound waves bounce from my wall to the opposite colonnade, back to the vaulted ceilings, crossing and recrossing in a three-dimensional matrix of pure acoustic force. 

Inside my tunnel, the resonance becomes absolute. The frequency of the song matches the natural resonant frequency of my own body. I begin to vibrate in perfect sympathy with the stone. The boundaries between my flesh, the calcium-carbonate wall, and the heavy, blood-warm water dissolve. I am no longer a single, blind organism coiled in a tunnel; I am the vibration itself. I am the song echoing through the dark, holding the metropolis together in a chord of crushing, beautiful pressure. 

In this profound, lightless intimacy, suspended in the twilight grace of the deep, I do not just inhabit the city. For this one, perfect, resonant moment, I *am* the city. And we are singing.
