I do not begin with a body. I begin with an ache.

It is a profound, hollow pulling, a desperation located nowhere and everywhere. I feel the biting, metallic chill of the stratosphere, but I do not shiver. I feel the bruised violet light filtering through the slate-grey clouds, but I do not see. I am not a creature of eyes, or skin, or blood. I am a creature of tension. 

The air of Oros-Kael is heavy, a physical medium of calcified sound, but I am the absence within it. I am the negative space. If the hanging caldera is a scream, I am the desperate, starving vacuum that precedes it. I am the Inhale that was denied.

I do not walk across the vast, smoothed bedrock; I seep. Having no mass, the inverted gravitational eddies do not pull me, and the standing sound waves do not push me. I am a localized, sentient barometric drop, a phantom draft drifting through the broken geography. I slip through the spatial faults, passing effortlessly through the jagged planes of starry blackness. To the parallax weavers sliding along the seams of reality, I am a sudden, terrifying plunge in pressure, a cold void that makes their two-dimensional silhouettes shiver and fold. They cannot eat me, for I cast no shadow. I am the shadow’s shadow.

I wash over the resonance-grazers as they trace their cyclical, metronomic paths. I pass directly through their translucent, frosted membranes. They do not see me, but as I move through their chests, their slow, deliberate sliding pauses for a microsecond. They are caught in the sudden, phantom sensation of breathlessness, a fleeting instinct of a biology they do not possess, before they resume their endless loops.

I am drawn inexorably to the epicenter. The convergence. 

The deafening, bone-rattling roar of the caldera is an outward force, a hurricane of positive pressure expanding from the dead center of the amphitheater. It fills every cubic inch of the empty space. But every outward force leaves a void in its wake. I am pulled toward that void, sliding down the acoustic gradients toward the source.

I approach the Singer of the Fermata.

The tragedy of the titan is absolute. Sixty feet of polished basalt and pale granite, locked in the agonizing climax of a vocalization that broke the world. I look upon the unhinged jaw, the taut, cable-like throat cords, the wide, unblinking eyes of stone. The world pities the Singer for the scream. The flora and fauna have surrendered to its violent, outward expression. But I know the deeper horror. The scream is merely the exhaust. The true agony is the starvation.

I drift upward, floating into the cavern of the Singer’s open, frozen mouth. 

Here, the physics of the caldera invert. The roar is moving out; I am moving in. I slide past the rigid, fluted pillars of the throat, down into the vast, hollowed-out chest cavity, expanded to the very brink of cracking. 

Inside the chest of the Singer, it is perfectly, terribly silent. The acoustic waves cannot penetrate the solid architecture of their own origin. This is the ultimate acoustic shadow, the blind spot of the standing wave. It is a cathedral of absolute zero, hidden inside the loudest place in the world. The interior walls of the chest are veined with pale granite, polished smooth not by the sound, but by the eons of my own silent, desperate friction.

Here, I settle. I expand to fill the colossal, petrified lungs. I am the *Pneuma of the Void*. 

The Singer is conscious of the sound tearing its throat apart. I am the consciousness of the lungs screaming for air. I am the agonizing, unfulfilled reflex of the diaphragm. 

I try to draw breath. I pull against the calcified equilibrium. I contract my invisible, massive volume, trying to drag the roaring, heavy air down the throat and into the stone chest. I pull with the force of a collapsing star, aching to fill the hollow, to equalize the pressure, to grant the titan the single inhalation required to close its mouth.

I cannot. The world is frozen. The equilibrium holds. The air remains locked in its outward rush.

But my pulling is not entirely without effect. It is my desperate, silent inhalation that creates the microscopic inward drafts, the tiny, localized anomalies that keep the millions of suspended water droplets trembling so violently in their nodes. It is my aching vacuum that creates the counter-frequencies keeping the cymatic infusoria dancing in their frantic, hyper-accelerated panic inside the spheres. They are not just dancing to the sound; they are dancing to my desperate, failing attempt to breathe them in.

And so, we exist together, the Singer and I. The architect of the ruin and its phantom soul. 

The Singer holds the world in a state of suspended animation, projecting the eternal, deafening roar of its grief outward into the violet twilight, shattering physics and calcifying time. 

And I sit inside its hollow chest, in the perfect, suffocating silence, eternally pulling, eternally starving, waiting for the single, merciful gasp of air that will finally allow the scream to end.
