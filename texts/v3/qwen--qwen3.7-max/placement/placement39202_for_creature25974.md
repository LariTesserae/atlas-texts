I begin as a vibration. 

Before I possess mass, before I claim a shape, I am merely the echo of a colossal piston slamming into its cylinder. The shockwave travels through miles of solid basalt, rippling up through the wet stone floor, and enters me. I do not hear the slam. I *become* it. The kinetic energy shudders through me, translating into a complex topography of structural stress and harmonic resonance. 

Slowly, the vibration resolves into a boundary. An inside and an outside. A body.

I open my senses to the basin. I have no eyes to part, no eyelids to lift. Instead, the sloping, polished basalt cowl of my face reads the world. To my left, the towering, oppressive mass of Atmospheric Engine Two. It is a sheer cliff of four-hundred-degree iron, radiating a blinding, agonizingly bright thermal bloom in my mind's eye. To my right, the weeping caldera wall. It is a soothing, abyssal cool, a deep gradient of wet stone that grounds the chaotic heat of the machines. Above me, the stroboscopic flickering of arc-welders flashes like distant, angry pulsars in the bruised twilight.

The air is a physical weight, a heavy, saturated vapor, but I do not feel it as suffocating. I feel it as sustenance. The intricate, louvered grilles of oxidized brass on my chest open with a soft, rhythmic click. I draw in the scalding steam, my internal furnace stripping its thermal energy, and I exhale a sharp, hissing burst of cooled exhaust.

I shift my weight. The movement is slow, accompanied by the deep, grinding *creak* of calcified mineral scale shifting against braided moss-willow roots. I am immensely heavy. Centuries of scalding drizzle have armored me in thick crusts of verdigris and black oxide. I am not merely wearing this armor; I am grown from it. 

I look down at my hands. I perceive them through the ambient heat they displace and the magnetic pull they exert on the surrounding iron. My fingers end in tapered, dark-iron pitons. I press my palm against the weeping flank of the engine. The magnets engage with a deep, satisfying *thrum*, grounding me, connecting my internal fluid to the grand, circulating lifeblood of the machine. Through the translucent, crystalline joints at my wrists, I can see my own pulse. It is a network of braided copper capillaries pumping superheated, mineral-rich water. 

But today, the glow is dim. The angry, bright orange of my youth has faded to a sullen, bruised cherry. The fluid is sluggish. 

I am cooling.

I release the engine and walk across the wide, echoing plaza of wet stone. My footsteps are heavy, but they make no sound that the human ear would recognize—only a series of dense, localized thuds that I use to map the integrity of the grated drainage trenches beneath my feet. I am in the deep basin, far below the spiraling catwalks and the corrugated tin roofs of the vertical hamlets. Down here, the noise of the industry is a physical pressure, a relentless, rhythmic roaring that vibrates in my very core.

I walk toward the center of the plaza, where the greatest of the thermophilic fern-trees stands. Its fronds, the size of ship sails, glow with a vibrant, bioluminescent emerald that I perceive as a soft, pulsing warmth. The tree is drinking the constant, raining condensation, its massive roots submerged in the boiling aquifer runoff. I pause beneath its sweeping canopy. The heavy, metronomic splashes of water dripping from its tips strike my cowl, each drop a tiny transfer of kinetic energy.

I am here because it is time. I am not here to bend a buckling pipe or clear a blocked vent. I am here to finish the weaving.

At the base of the fern-tree, the stone gives way to the deepest, hottest drainage trench. The water here is nearly supercritical, churning with raw geothermal fury. I kneel at the edge. I extend my thermal sensors into the boiling slurry. 

For the last thirty years, I have been tending to the armature submerged in this trench. I wove it from scavenged iron rebar and the tough, fibrous roots of the moss-willows. I have watched the intense heat and pressure slowly precipitate minerals out of the water, building up layers of black oxide and calcified scale over the crude skeleton. 

Now, I feel it. 

Deep within the mineral crust, a spark. A rudimentary, crystalline lattice catching the geothermal energy. The consciousness is kindling. It is waking up. 

I reach into the boiling water. The heat is immense, but my copper capillaries absorb it, feeding my dimming internal furnace one last, glorious surge of energy. I gently brush away a layer of loose calcite from the new being's cowl. It does not flinch. It does not yet know what it is. Soon, it will pull itself from the trench. It will be blind and deaf to the world until the others teach it to read the heat and sing to the iron.

I must tell them. 

I stand and walk to the central load-bearing strut of the plaza, a massive pillar of dark iron that plunges straight down into the bedrock. I raise my right hand. My calcified knuckles are thick, hardened by centuries of striking metal. 

I strike the strut. 

*CLANG.*

It is not just a sound. It is a complex telemetry, a specific frequency loaded with meaning. *The new one wakes. I am cooling. Come to the deep trench.*

The vibration travels down the strut, into the bedrock, and ripples outward through the miles of interconnected metal and stone. I wait, my feet planted firmly on the grating, feeling for the answers. 

A faint, rhythmic tapping from the high scaffolds of Engine Four, miles above. *Acknowledged.*
A deep, resonant hum from the eastern turbine hall. *We sing for you, Elder.*
A rapid, staccato series of clicks from a group of custodians on the catwalks. *We will guide the new one.*

We are a choir of metal and stone, speaking in the language of resonance. The deafening roar of the basin is not noise to us; it is the canvas upon which we paint our conversations.

My message sent, my task complete, I turn away from the trench. I walk to the edge of the plaza, where the sheer, weeping basalt cliff meets the massive, curved base of Engine Two. There is a hollow here, a space shaped perfectly by centuries of water erosion and the careful, reverent hands of my ancestors. The walls of the hollow are lined with the fused, verdigris-crusted remains of those who came before me. Their copper capillaries form the plumbing for the city's runoff; their iron pitons hold the scaffolding in place.

I step into the hollow. 

The stone is cool against my overheating back. The iron of the engine is warm against my chest. It is a perfect thermal balance. I sit down. 

I press my hands against the rock and the metal. I engage the magnetic locks in my pitons. They bite into the iron and the basalt with a final, permanent *thunk*. I open my louvered chest vents one last time. I draw in a deep, scalding breath of the basin's steam, feeling the heavy, wet air fill my woven, root-and-scale lungs. 

Then, I close the vents. I shut down the internal valves. 

The glowing cherry fluid in my wrists flickers, and begins to fade to a dull, dark brown. I feel the steady, metronomic drips of the fern-tree's condensation hitting my cowl. I feel the slow, blind reach of the moss-willow roots, creeping over my ankles, beginning the century-long process of weaving my legs into the bedrock. 

I do not feel fear. There is no death here, only transition. The messy, fragile cycle of biology has no place in this sweltering, sterile dark. 

I close my thermal sensors. The localized map of heat and vibration slowly dissolves, but I do not go dark. Instead, my awareness expands. I feel the entire engine. I feel the deep, groaning bedrock. I feel the heavy, fluid footsteps of the new one pulling itself from the trench. 

I am no longer a point of consciousness moving through the basin. I am the wall. I am the foundation. I am the basin itself. 

And the basin is perfectly, eternally awake.
