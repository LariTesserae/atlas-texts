I do not begin with a body. I begin with a frequency. 

I am a resonance trapped in the vast, bell-shaped hollow, a standing wave born from the crushing, chest-rattling thunder of the glacial river. At first, I am nothing but a node of destructive interference—a pocket of perfect, microscopic silence suspended in the deafening white noise. I feel the sharp, metallic chill of the air, the profound emptiness of the void, the slow, weeping dampness of the cavern. I am a localized quiet, hovering high above the glowing emerald moss, wrapped in the absolute, velvety darkness of the upper reaches. I have no eyes to seek the bruised twilight of the fissure, no ears to be shattered by the roar. I perceive the world entirely through the geometry of its sound.

Slowly, the freezing air begins to clothe me. The pervasive dampness of the hollow, heavy with mineral-rich condensation and the ghostly spores of the bioluminescent fungi, seeks out my quiet center. Moisture nucleates around my silent core. I am gathering mass, pulling rime from the freezing exhalations of the cavern. My form crystallizes not as flesh or chitin, but as a delicate, fractal lattice of flash-frozen mist and fungal silk. I am becoming a physical manifestation of the cavern's acoustic geometry. When the river’s eternal flow shifts by a fraction of a millimeter, the standing waves alter, and my very anatomy shifts in response—icy limbs melting and refreezing, diaphanous wings adjusting their pitch. I am a living cymatic pattern, sculpted and sustained by the very noise that fills the hollow lung of the world.

Now that I have form, I have purpose. I am an *echo-frost*, a creature of the deep air, existing solely in the open, refrigerated expanse of the void. I do not fly; I am carried on the faint thermal updrafts rising from the friction of the black, churning water far below. While the cymatic weavers cling to the walls to harvest dust, and the glass-eels anchor themselves to the basalt to endure the scour, I belong to the empty center. As I drift through the roaring dark, my crystalline lattice hums. I absorb the kinetic energy of the sound, translating the violent wall of white noise into a faint, internal resonance that keeps my icy core from shattering. I glow with a pale, ghostly green light, a captured echo of the moss far below, turning myself into a wandering, frozen star in the heavy dark.

My lifespan is not measured in the frantic scramble of the sun-driven world, but in the slow erosion of stone. I am a creature of profound endurance, locked in the patient cycle of deep time. Occasionally, my drift carries me down toward the margins of the roaring water, near the quiet, damp shadows where the petrified willow weirs stand. Here, I sense the presence of the lone ascetic. Stripped of hearing, they feel the cavern through the soles of their bare feet and the palms of their hands pressed against the vibrating rock. When I drift close, brushing against the basalt they touch, we share a fleeting, tactile communion. Through the stone, they feel the delicate, high-frequency hum of my icy lattice—a tiny, crystalline counter-melody to the deep, crushing bass of the river. In return, I feel the slow, rhythmic thud of their heartbeat, a warm, organic pulse that grounds me. They do not reach for me, and I do not land on them. We simply acknowledge each other in the dark: two silent keepers of the hollow, bound by the geometry of the noise.

Eventually, the thermal currents will fail me, or the acoustic geometry will shift too drastically, and my lattice will lose its cohesion. I will fall. I will drift down through the crushing wall of sound, past the glowing tapestry of the frost-ferns, and into the shallow, dark pools at the river's edge. There, the milk-white cave-salamander might mistake my glowing remnants for a springtail, or the frost-springtails themselves will graze upon my melting rime. My water will join the weeping condensation, flowing back into the deep, mineral-rich damp, and my fungal spores will return to the pale mycelial network beneath the rock. I will dissolve back into the slow, patient cycle of the cavern. 

But until that moment, I remain suspended in the roaring void. I am a fragile, frozen song caught in the throat of the earth, perfectly still, perfectly alive, holding the deafening secrets of the dark.
