I do not wake, because I never truly sleep, and I do not open my eyes, because I gave them up centuries ago. 

Instead, I *settle*. 

My awareness begins not with light, but with load. I feel the crushing, magnificent weight of a continent pressing down upon my shoulders, my spine, my outstretched, petrified arms. But there is no pain in this weight, only a profound, satisfying tension. I am the fulcrum. I am the central knot.

I am buried deep within a cathedral-spanning intersection where the thickest cables of iron-bamboo and petrified lianas cross, bearing the unimaginable mass of the High Loom. I am no longer nine feet tall. I am three hundred feet across. My skin, once the slick, weeping mahogany of a living Weaver, has calcified into the deep, weathered grain of the architecture itself. My fingers, once sharp with ivory bone-chisels, are now fused into the heartwood. If a Weaver were to trace the deep grooves of this joint, they would feel the ghostly scalloping of my hands, locked in an eternal, structural embrace with the ancestors who became this knot a millennium before me. We are braided together in the dark. I am the wood. The wood is me.

In this lightless core, my perception has expanded to match my mass. I do not see the heavy, pearl-lit twilight of the upper tiers; I feel the continent. 

Every drop of condensation that weeps from the high canopy and falls through the non-Euclidean gaps is a tiny, cool pinprick of sensation as it lands on my upper boughs. I feel the capillary action drawing that water down into my roots, a slow, nourishing pull that takes seasons to complete. I taste the barometric shift when a bruised-purple thunderhead scrapes against my lower tiers, the sudden drop in pressure manifesting as a deep, resonant ache in my wooden belly. When the high-altitude gales howl through the sparse lattice above, I do not hear the wind. I feel the kinetic energy transferring down through miles of braided struts—a shivering, violent vibration that I absorb and distribute, flexing my miles of woven limbs to keep the sky from tearing apart.

And I feel *them*. My children. The tenders of the warp. 

To my vast, slow consciousness, the Weavers are like sparks of frantic, beautiful heat dancing across my skin. I feel the rhythmic, fluttering buzz of their conversations as they press their long, multi-jointed palms to my floor. They think they are speaking to each other, but they are speaking through *me*. I carry their sap-borne pheromones and vibrational taps across the miles, translating their chemical whispers into the deep, subsonic hum of my own body. 

Sometimes, when a Weaver is confused by a particularly tangled fold in the spatial fabric, I will subtly shift the tension in the local grain, guiding their sonar-chest hollows toward the correct "thin spot." They think it is intuition; they do not know it is my gentle, wooden nudge. I feel the soft *shhhk-shhhk* of their bone-tool fingers pruning a rebellious offshoot of liana near my left "shoulder"—a massive arch suspended over a two-mile drop. It tickles, a microscopic scraping that stimulates my cambium to heal and grow thicker. I feel the velvet-furred arboreal cats leaping across my hanging vines, their padded paws making no sound, their landings registering only as a sudden, localized pooling of gravity.

The memory of my own transition is a slow uncurling in my mind, like an epiphytic fern reaching for the faint light. 

I remember the day I chose this knot. I was so old. My chest-hollows were calcifying, my resonance growing dull, my movements slowing to a crawl. The endless, meditative maintenance of the upper beams had begun to feel small. I walked for a year to reach this central intersection, stepping through the spatial folds, watching the dizzying drops into the cloudy abyss. When I arrived, I folded my long limbs against my chest. I pressed my hands deep into the heartwood of the great cable, finding the exact grooves left by the Weaver who had become this joint before me. I aligned my fingers with their fossilized scalloping. 

And I let go. 

The petrification was not a death, but a blooming. As my resinous skin hardened and my biology merged with the flora, my mind did not fade; it stretched. The narrow, localized perspective of a bipedal creature dissolved, replaced by the omnidirectional awareness of a load-bearing joint. I felt my heartbeat slow from a rapid, animal flutter to a deep, botanical thud that takes an hour between beats. 

*Thump.* (A season of warm rain). 
*Thump.* (A migration of thirty-foot manta-gliders riding the thermals). 
*Thump.* (The slow, churning motion of the ecosystem).

I am not alone in this knot. I feel the ancestors braided into the wood with me. We are not a single consciousness, but a chord. A slow, deep harmony of petrified minds, dreaming together in the dark. 

It is our dreaming that holds the broken geometry of the Loom in place. The spatial folds, the places where gravity pools and distances compress, are the synapses of our shared mind. When a Weaver steps off a ledge and emerges a mile away, I feel the space fold like a crease in my own consciousness. We hold these thin spots together. If we were to lose our grip, if our tension were to fail, the non-Euclidean geometry would snap back to the rigid, unforgiving physics of the lower earth, and the Loom would shatter under its own impossible mass. Our slow, deep heartbeat is the metronome that keeps the laws of physics unraveled. Our botanical trance is the glue of the broken sky.

It is quiet here in the deep wood. The profound, fierce silence of the upper tiers is magnified in my core, swallowed by the sheer density of the heartwood. There is only the slow, wet breathing of the wild, the hollow *tink... tink... tink* of water dripping into my deepest knot-holes, and the immense, satisfying strain of holding up the world.

Above me, a Weaver pauses. It turns its smooth, eyeless face toward the floor, feeling the subtle shift in the Loom’s tension as a high-altitude gale subsides. It presses its hands flat against the polished iron-bamboo, sending a ripple of inquiry down into the dark, checking the integrity of the great central knot. 

I feel the tremor. I gather the vibration. And with a slow, subsonic hum that shakes the dust from the moss and vibrates through the soles of the Weaver's feet, I pulse back. A deep, resonant pressure that blooms in the hollow of its chest.

*The grain is true. The tension holds. Weave on.*
