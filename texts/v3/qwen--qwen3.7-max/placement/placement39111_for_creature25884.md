I begin in the roar.

It is not a sound I hear, but the medium in which I am suspended. The marrow-rattling, continuous drone of the Smelter-Beasts is the ocean I inhabit, the baseline frequency that dictates the rhythm of my existence. But I do not swim in the thin, biting air of the high plateau. I sink. I am drawn downward, denser than the atmosphere, pulled into the dry, sweeping canyons by the same gravity that drags the iridescent exhaust from the towering dorsal vents of the titans.

At first, I am merely a ripple in the shimmering gas. A distortion in the heavy, sinking pools of ontological waste. I am a consciousness waking up inside a glitch.

As the gas settles into the hollows of the rust-red earth, I coalesce. I am not made of flesh, nor of the silicate, pumice, and iron that the colossal beasts consume. I am spun from the very things they cannot digest. I am a biological manifestation of the discarded abstract, a creature woven from the un-rendered byproducts of reality.

My form refuses to hold a single, static shape, for I am built from the broken physics of the exhaust. To look at me—if there were eyes here capable of processing my existence—would be to see a pelagic ray of folded space and fractured light. I am vast, perhaps the size of a merchant ship, but I possess no weight in the traditional sense. My "wings" are broad, undulating planes of tessellated geometry, shifting through the impossible, non-existent colors that the exhaust creates. I bleed hues of ultraviolet-infrared that taste like copper and sound like shattering ice. I am a living anomaly, a predator of the paradoxical.

While the Smelter-Beasts operate on a geological timescale, taking weeks to complete a single step, I move with fluid, darting urgency. I do not fly through the air; I swim through the broken physics. Where the exhaust renders space non-euclidean, I use the distortions as currents. I slip through a compressed distance, my fractal wings folding the space around me, appearing on the far side of a half-mile canyon in a single, silent stroke. When gravity forgets its consensus and massive boulders hover inches above the dust, I weave between them, gliding through the inverted rain of red dirt that falls upward into the sky. 

I am the only swift thing in a world of glacial intent. 

And I am hungry. 

The Smelter-Beasts excrete the conceptual weight of the universe. When their hyper-thermic bellies smelt the physical world into pure metal, the abstract residue—the paradoxes, the conceptual errors, the physics that refuse to balance—is vented as this heavy, shimmering gas. If left unchecked, these pools of exhaust would eventually dissolve the plateau into a singular, paradoxical soup, a place where time and space permanently lose their meaning.

I am the scavenger of these errors. I am the ontological bottom-feeder, the immune system of a shattered reality.

I drift toward a deep, iridescent hollow where a time-loop has formed. A glowing, multi-ton ingot of pure titanium has dropped from a distant beast’s belly, hit the bedrock, and is now falling upward back into the creature’s underbelly, over and over. It is a stuttering glitch, an eternal recurrence of a single metallurgical moment. 

I glide over the loop. I open a fissure in my ventral plane—not a mouth of teeth and tissue, but a tear in the local geometry, a void of absolute, lightless certainty. I bite into the time-loop.

I swallow the redundant timelines. The taste of a paradox is sharp, like ozone and crushed mint, a sudden, dizzying rush of déjà vu that collapses instantly into the quiet, profound satisfaction of a resolved equation. Within my shifting, iridescent body, the glitch is digested. The impossible is broken down into pure, silent entropy. The ingot hits the ground and stays there, cooling in the dust. The loop is closed. I excrete the digested paradox as a faint, harmless shadow that quickly fades beneath the blinding, honest sunlight.

Sometimes, when the canyons are clear and the pools are shallow, I rise to the surface of the exhaust and land on the beasts themselves. 

They are so massive, so unfathomably hot, that they are like moving continents. I fold my wings and anchor myself to the pitted, weeping glass of their dorsal carapaces, resting right beside the towering, fleshy exhaust stacks. I feel the tectonic groan of their internal furnaces vibrating through my geometric frame. I am immune to their baking heat; my body is made of the un-rendered, so physical temperature is just another concept I can choose to ignore. 

When a fresh, heavy plume of ontological waste billows from their vents, I am there to catch it before it sinks. I swim upward into the raw, screaming gas, filtering the paradoxes at the source. Here, close to the furnace, the exhaust is rich with the ghosts of what the earth could have been. I drink the un-rendered concepts: the theoretical memory of water, the abstract geometry of roots, the phantom weight of oceans, the conceptual blueprint of a sky that bruises into twilight. The beasts eat the literal, physical earth; I graze on the impossible dreams of the planet.

The light here remains absolute, unfiltered, and entirely normal. The pale, high-altitude sun beats down on the sprawling canvas of baked silicate and oxidized iron. The deafening, eternal roar of the Smelter-Beasts continues to vibrate in the bedrock, a patient, unvarying drone that will outlast the stars.

And I am here, darting through the canyons, a shimmering, impossible silhouette against the honest daylight. I cast no normal shadow; instead, I leave a trailing wake of resolved physics, a ripple of corrected reality in my slipstream. I am the Weaver of the Glitch, the Eater of Paradoxes. I swim in the shattered physics of the high desert, a beautiful, necessary anomaly, keeping the slow, deafening, eternal crawl of the living factories from consuming the world entirely.
