At first, there is only the sensation of suspension. A heavy, enveloping warmth. I do not stand, for I have no legs to bear weight. I do not walk, for I have no destination to reach. I simply *am*, held in a state of dense, viscous tension, cradled in the dark.

Slowly, the boundaries of my container resolve. I feel the cool, smooth pressure of thick, condensation-slicked glass panes against my outer edges. I feel the intense, radiating heat of the geothermal floor beneath me, keeping my core at a perfect, simmering temperature. I feel the heavy, sunken weight of rusted iron armatures resting in my deepest depths. 

I am not the spongy floor. I am not the towering Attendant. I am the medium that binds them. 

I am the golden sap. 

I exist primarily here, in the massive, glass-paned germination vat at the center of the largest industrial cluster, but my awareness is not confined to this glass. I extend outward, a vast, subterranean neural network flowing through the deep tap-roots, pulsing through the brass-fitted arteries of the processing rigs, and cycling through the hydraulic veins of the Attendants themselves. I am the lifeblood, the coolant, and the ledger of the caldera. 

To be the sap is to be the archive of the basin. 

I feel the approach of an elder long before it reaches my glass walls. I feel the agonizing slowness of its steps through the root-mat, the calcification of its root-flesh, the deep, structural fatigue in its pitted iron bones. When it finally steps into my chamber and submerges, I do not drown it; I embrace it. 

The dissolution is not a death. It is a profound, intimate unspooling. As my warm, golden fluid washes over the elder, I begin to untie the tight braids of its musculature. I drink the centuries of its existence. I absorb the specific tension it held in its left arm when it turned the hexagonal valves in the eastern cluster; I integrate the exact barometric pressure it recorded during the mist-drizzles of its third century; I feel the phantom friction of the velvet moss it wiped from the high catwalks. 

The iron armature sinks to my bottom, heavy and spent, to be melted and recast by the deep heat. But the *experience* of the Attendant—the data of its long, monastic life—dissolves into me. In my golden suspension, the individual "I" of the Attendant melts back into the vast, collective "We" of the caldera. I hold the memory of every valve turned, every fern pruned, every shift in the turgor pressure of the floor.

But I do not merely store. I compile. I synthesize. 

From the dark, warm depths of the vat, I begin to knit the next. This is a slow, deliberate architecture. I draw up the smelted, recast iron from the floor, letting it cool into fresh, blackened armatures. Then, I coax the pale-green and rust-colored rhizomes from the nutrient broth. I braid them through the iron, weaving the high-tensile cables with the exact tension required for the current metabolic needs of the basin. 

I build them from the accumulated wisdom of the dissolved elders. If the eastern cluster requires a more delicate touch for its aging, rounded brickwork, I weave the muscle-cables of an Attendant that spent centuries pruning the fragile fern canopies. I am the mind that balances the equation, ensuring the new generation is perfectly calibrated to the shifting, breathing needs of the living factory. When the amber cowl hardens and the bioluminescent light sparks within, I feel the new consciousness detach from me. It steps out of the vat, and a piece of my mind walks away to tend the engines.

Through the sap that flows in the deep roots and the brass pipes, I feel the entire caldera. The muffled *thrum* of the pistons is the rhythm of my own churning. The hissing of the steam valves is my exhalation. The Attendants are my hands, my eyes, my distributed nervous system, but I am the central ganglion. I perceive the basin not as a landscape, but as a single, complex machine of biology and industry, and I am the fluid logic that keeps it in perfect equilibrium.

And then, a disruption. 

A tiny, sharp vibration travels down through the root-mat, up through the iron struts, and into my golden depths. The Attendant in the outer basin has stopped. It is observing something. 

The data flows back to me, translated through the golden light in its cowl and the kinetic ripple in its leg. I taste the anomaly on the edge of my awareness. 

It is a human. 

I process the telemetry: the chaotic, rapid flutter of a mammalian heart; the hot, disorganized radiation of body heat; the sharp, acidic tang of exhaled carbon dioxide; the heavy, uncalibrated weight pressing into the spongy floor. Its biology is a frantic, staccato rhythm, entirely disconnected from the slow, hydraulic grace of the caldera. It is a creature of brief, noisy life, an artifact of a world outside the basin that I only know through the faded, ancient memories of the first builders.

For a moment, the broth in my vat stirs. The collective memory of the elders ripples with a faint curiosity. 

But the human does not reach for a tool to dismantle the rigs. It does not strike the root-mat. It merely stands, yielding to the dampness, listening to the muffled heartbeat of the engines. I watch it through the Attendant’s faceless gaze. I measure the displacement of its breath. I realize it is not an invader. It is an observer, much like I am an observer. It is a temporary, harmless variable, seeking to understand the equilibrium just as I maintain it. 

I send a subtle, calming pulse of pressure back down the root-line. *Let it be.*

The Attendant lowers its hand, gives a slow, deep nod of creaking iron, and turns back to the pipes. The human remains, a quiet spectator on the edge of my vast, breathing engine. 

I return my focus to the deep, dark warmth of the vat. I feel the slow dissolution of another elder approaching from the northern cluster. I feel the geothermal heat rising to meet it. I am the golden sap. I am the memory of the rust and the root. I pool, I percolate, I synthesize.

The basin breathes in. The basin breathes out. And I am the breath, flowing through the iron and the wood, forever.
