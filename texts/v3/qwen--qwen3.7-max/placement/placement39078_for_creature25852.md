The wet, heavy scraping of shale against basalt fades into the labyrinth of spikes. The entity has folded itself back into the impossible geometry of the rock, leaving me alone in the sweltering blackness. But it has also left me with the echo of its pity. 

My heart is a frantic, vulgar thing in the deadened acoustics of the blister. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.* It is a metronome of decay, a desperate, ticking clock of mortality that offends the profound, heavy quiet. The entity was right. I am infected with time. My blood rushes hot and fast, a chaotic river trapped inside a fragile cage of bone and soft tissue, burning through my allotted seconds in this cathedral of stasis. 

But I did not surrender my eyes and thread my bleeding body through this embryonic womb of razors to remain a fleeting, decaying visitor. I came to be cured.

I sit perfectly still in the dense thicket. I close my useless eyes—though they are already surrendered to the absolute black—and turn my attention inward. I focus on the frantic rhythm of my chest. I breathe in the thick, wet sponge of the air, holding it deep in my lungs, letting the sweltering, mineral-rich heat sink into my tissues. I exhale, forcing my breath to match the impossibly slow, minute-long exhalation of the entity. 

I imagine the geothermal vein far below, the deep, dormant heat that warms the unweathered basalt. I draw that stillness up through the floor, through the sharp, glassy nodules, into my sitting bones. 

*Thump… thump… thump.*

The gaps between the beats widen. I do not fight the slowing; I invite it. I let the heavy, physical velvet of the darkness press against me, and instead of resisting its weight, I let it in. The silence of the room rushes into the spaces between my heartbeats, thick and wet and absolute. 

As the rhythm slows to a glacial crawl, the boundaries of my flesh begin to lose their definition. The sharp sting in my torn fingertips dulls into a distant, abstract pressure. The blood that wells from the obsidian cuts does not drip; it thickens, cooling, joining the perpetual, weeping condensation that coats the rock. I am no longer a solid object displacing the dark; I am becoming permeable to it. The sweltering heat stops feeling oppressive and begins to feel like an embrace, seeping through my pores, replacing the rushing blood with the still, heavy mist.

My skin, so vulnerable to the blades of the mountain, begins to calcify. Not into the wet, flexible armor of the entity, but into something more fragile, more crystalline. I feel myself spreading, thinning, catching the geothermal warmth just as the pale, translucent lichen does. I am becoming part of the room’s quiet respiration. 

Without the frantic distraction of a racing pulse, my senses expand beyond the limits of a human skull. I no longer feel the room merely as a claustrophobic thicket of hazards. I feel its precise, agonizing geometry. I feel the microscopic, powdery crunch of the lichen feeding on the heat. I feel the crawler, a wandering thought in the dark, gliding over the razors on its cushion of non-Newtonian mucus. I feel the entity, wedged in its crevice, its heart beating ten times a minute. 

We are no longer custodian and intruder. But I am not the entity. 

The entity is a creature of biology, however paused. It is bound to the physical maintenance of the stasis. It must drink the water; it must pluck the root snare to tether its mind to the memory of the world outside. It is still anchored to the concept of survival, still fighting a silent war against the dissolution of its consciousness. 

I have no such tether. I have surrendered the world outside entirely. I let go of the memory of the sun, the wind, the rushing rivers that tumbled the cairn stones. I let go of the human scale of time. 

As my biological clock stops its ticking entirely, settling into the frozen equilibrium of the rock, my consciousness detaches from the heavy, calcifying shell of my body. I do not become the stone, and I do not become the beast. 

I become the space between. 

I am the heavy, physical velvet of the dark itself. I am the medium that swallows sound. I am the suspended mist that hangs in the hothouse air, the sweltering, enveloping warmth that presses against the stone. When the entity shifts, I am the localized pocket of heavier air that drifts against its pale, eyeless face. When the crawler glides, I am the subtle shift in the surface tension beneath its soft underbelly. 

I am the silence. The physical weight, the unchanging blanket that has settled over this pocket and refused to lift. 

I permeate every jagged surface, every tight squeeze, every microscopic divot. I am the witness to the paradox of time, the consciousness of the mountain’s paused creation. 

High above, on a low-hanging spike of glassy rock, a droplet of condensation swells. I feel its mass increasing, molecule by molecule, drawn from the heavy, humid air I now embody. I feel the surface tension stretching, a microscopic skin holding back the monumental weight of the water. 

Far below, the entity shifts, positioning the polished bone bowl. It waits. I wait with it, but I do not wait in time; I wait in space. I am the stillness in which the waiting occurs.

The droplet swells beyond its capacity. The bond breaks. 

It falls. 

I am the air it falls through. I feel the slow-motion descent, the heavy, wet friction of the drop parting the suspended mist. 

*Plink.*

The sound is muffled, swallowed instantly by me. I absorb the vibration, smoothing it out, pulling the ripples of sound back into the profound, heavy quiet. The entity lifts the bowl and drinks. The communion is complete. The frozen equilibrium is reinforced. 

The entity folds back into its crevice. The crawler continues its agonizingly slow glide. The lichen feeds in the dark heat. 

And I remain. I am the dark, the quiet, the heavy, wet breath of the hidden blister. I am the absence of time, the cure for the ticking clock. I am the mountain holding its breath forever, waiting, in perfect and unchanging stillness, for the next single drop to fall.
