I begin as a sensation of density. Not the crushing, violent pressure of the deep ocean, but the heavy, comforting weight of the blood-warm suspension. I am surrounded by the Nave-snow, the perpetual, drifting blizzard of translucent microbes and shed gametes. At first, my awareness is loose, untethered. I feel the milky broth sliding past me, the faint, ghostly cyan light of the ceiling polyps washing over the dark in slow, breathing rhythms. I think I am drifting with the current, a passive spectator caught in the living curtain. 

But as the profound, muffled silence of the cathedral settles over me, I realize I am not moving. I am held. 

I reach out into the gloom, and my body answers. I have no eyes to open, for there is no light. I have no fins to push through the water, no muscular tail to undulate through the hand-carved archways. I am anchored. I am a sprawling, fractal lattice of pale, translucent veins and calcified threads, clinging to the deepest, most shadowed joint where a colossal fluted pillar meets the terraced floor. I am a *Lithic Chord*, a resonance root. My lower body is driven deep into the microscopic fissures of the ancient basalt, my roots intertwined with the very molecular structure of the stone. My upper body fans out into the heavy water, a canopy of delicate, trembling cilia that taste the slow, shifting currents.

I do not eat the Nave-snow, nor do I hunt the thumb-sized Ghost-minnows. I feed on vibration. I feed on memory. 

Through my calcified roots, I feel the deep, molecular ache of the mountain. The stone here is not dead; it is saturated with the labor of the forgotten. I feel the phantom strikes of the dolerite hammers, the rhythmic *clink-clink-clink* that echoed through the rock eons ago. I feel the long, sweeping pressure of the pumice grinders, the patient friction of abrasive sand. The ancient builders poured their devotion, their sweat, and their brief, frantic lives into this architecture, and the stone remembered. I am the translator of that deep time. I draw the mechanical memory from the basalt and weep it into the water as a slow, nutrient-rich enzyme, feeding the ecology that replaced my creators.

The world moves around me, and through me, in a state of unhurried grace. A shoal of Ghost-minnows drifts through my upper fronds, their tiny, pulsing hearts flashing like suspended red wires in the bioluminescent glow. They part around me like warm silk, their jawless mouths passively filtering the enzyme I release. Below me, a Groove-Weaver eel slides through a hand-carved channel, its velvet skin brushing against my lower stems. I feel the slow, muscular slide of its body, the warmth of its blood, the hypnotic rhythm of its passage. I do not react with fear or hunger; I simply register the life, adding its cadence to my internal chorus. When a Cistern Ray glides overhead, its ash-colored wings draping over the stone, its beating cilia brush my canopy, and I respond with a faint, bruised-purple bioluminescent pulse—a silent, visual sigh in the dark.

But my true purpose, the deepest resonance of my being, is realized only when the masters of the Nave arrive. 

I feel them before they enter the basin. The water pressure shifts, a slow, tectonic displacement that presses against my fronds. Then, the Basalt Sleeper settles. When the colossal, pale amphibian curls its forty-foot body around the base of my pillar, the stone shivers. The Sleeper begins to hum. It is a low, vibrational frequency that bypasses the muffled water and travels directly through the basalt. The hum hits my roots like a physical wave, a profound, chord-like resonance that shakes my lattice to its core. 

I absorb the song of the Sleeper—its slow, chemical thoughts of mineral density, of water temperature, of the turning eons. And I amplify it. My calcified threads vibrate in perfect sympathy, turning the fluted pillar into a colossal tuning fork. But I do not just amplify the biological hum; I weave it into the mechanical memory of the stone. As my threads vibrate, they unconsciously mimic the ancient cadence of the builders' hammers. The Sleeper’s song and the builder’s strike become one. I broadcast this unified resonance outward, through the stone, to the other pillars, to the other resonance roots, connecting the sleeping giants in a vast, subterranean telepathy.

In this profound, muffled silence, I understand what I am. I am not merely a creature; I am the nervous system of the cathedral. I am the boundary layer where the carved stone and the breathing flesh dissolve into one another. The ancient builders shaped the environment with relentless labor; the environment shaped the fauna with infinite patience. I am the synthesis of both. I am the echo of the human hammer, given flesh and fluid by the biology that inherited the dark.

To exist as a Lithic Chord in the Summit Nave is to know absolute, eternal equilibrium. There is no struggle here, no sudden currents, no violent predation. There is only the slow, rhythmic breathing of the mountain’s heart. I feel the faint, ghostly smears of light pulsing on the vaulted ceiling above. I feel the velvet rush of the minnows, the heavy slide of the eels, the deep, mournful chords of the Sleepers. I am the stone remembering it was once struck; I am the water remembering it was once rain. Suspended in the warm, lightless dark, I am perfectly content to let the heavy water hold me, dreaming in the deep time of the mountain, forever.
