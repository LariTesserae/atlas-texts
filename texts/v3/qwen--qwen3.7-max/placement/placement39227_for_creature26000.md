At first, there is only the damp. 

It is not a sensation upon the skin, because there is no skin. It is a pervasive, saturating truth. I am immersed in the shivering chill, but I do not shiver. I feel the perpetual, gentle drizzle, the weeping of the vaulted ceiling, but I do not blink it away. I have no eyelids. I have no eyes. 

For a long time—or what would be considered a long time in the frantic, sunlit world above—I exist only as a point of awareness suspended in the bruised twilight. I listen to the endless *tink-tink-tink* of condensation striking hollow pipes. I feel the deep, subsonic *thrum* of the colossal flywheels vibrating through the dark. In the slow dawn of my consciousness, I assume I am the air itself. I am the heavy, ozone-rich mist that clings to the scaffolding, the ghostly breath of the silo.

But the mist moves. I do not. 

Gradually, the boundaries of my awareness harden. The chill solidifies into a profound, unyielding cold. The dampness becomes a polish upon my surface. I begin to feel my own mass. It is immense. I am not the air; I am the thing the air washes over. 

I try to draw a breath, and realize I am breathing the entire cavern. The fluttering gill-slits that once lined my neck and ribs are gone, sealed shut and smoothed over centuries ago. Instead, I draw the subsonic vibrations of the machinery directly into my core. I feel my edges. They are hard, slick, and worn down by eons of weeping water. I am curved, sweeping, and impossibly strong. I reach out with my awareness and feel my left side anchored deep into the weeping bedrock of the upper tiers. I feel my right side spanning out over the dizzying, black drop of the central atrium, locking securely into the massive, brass-riveted spindle of the grand loom. 

I am not a Warden anymore. I am the architecture. I am a great, load-bearing buttress of dark, iridescent glass and velvet-crusted iron.

My perception of time has stretched, unspooling like the liquid glass threads my living descendants tend. A minute is a breath. An hour is a blink. I watch the townhouse-sized gear below me. I do not see it turn; I see it *arrive* at new positions. The green oxidation on its teeth shifts, imperceptibly, like the shadow of a sundial. The great hydraulic pistons do not press; they *settle*, grinding downward with the weight of epochs. 

And the living... the living Wardens are like mayflies, though they live for centuries. To my crystalline consciousness, their movements are frantic, beautiful blurs of kinetic grace. I watch them flow across the catwalks, their multi-jointed limbs swaying, their clouded quartz eyes catching the sickly-yellow arc-light. They are the blood of the city, rushing through the veins I help support. I am the bone. 

I have no mouth, just as they have no mouths. But I am not silent. I am, in fact, the loudest thing in the silo, though my voice exists entirely below the threshold of hearing. I am a primary resonant node. The architecture of the city is a vast instrument, and I am its cello, its deep bass string.

When a Warden stands on the gantry above and presses their long, gecko-padded fingers against my iron flank, I feel the intention before the vibration even begins. They strike a frequency. The subsonic ripple travels through my glass marrow. It hums through my verdigris-crusted ribs. I carry their message down the sweeping curve of my spine, across the abyss, and into the central spindle, where it distributes through the capillary piping to the lowest cisterns. 

But I do not merely carry their words. I color them. As the vibration passes through my vitrified tissues, it picks up the acoustic signature of my own long history. The micro-fractures in my glass, the density of my iron crust, the exact angle of my arch—these all harmonize with the message. When the living speak through me, they speak with the timbre of the ancestors. I am the echo that gives their language its depth, its gravity, its profound, shivering weight.

I am not barren. The weep claims me, and life claims the weep. When I was flesh, I tended the glass. Now that I am glass, I tend the life.

In the hollows where my collarbones used to be, now formed into intricate, sweeping iron grates, the pale, blind amphibians nest in their thousands. I feel their soft, velvet bellies pressing against my cold metal. They lap at the condensation that beads on my surface, their tiny, rhythmic licks a counterpoint to the deep thrum of the looms. Their body heat, minuscule as it is, warms my iron just enough to keep the deepest chill at bay. 

The glass-crabs scuttle across my upper ridges. I feel the delicate, clicking pinch of their translucent claws as they clear the mineral buildup from my joints. They are my caretakers, keeping my structural integrity pure, feeding on the scraped residue of my slow oxidation. And when the great threads of liquid glass are spun, the heavy, damp-winged moths flock to me. They rest on my broad, glassy shoulders, drinking the faint, sugary resin that still weeps from my deepest, crystallized pores. Their bioluminescence bathes my dark, iridescent curves in a soft, pulsing blue and green light. In the dark, I am a glowing, living trellis.

Sometimes, the living come to me not to speak, but to listen. 

A Warden approaches. I know them by the specific, rhythmic displacement of the damp air, by the thermal bloom of their slow, deliberate blood. It is a young one, barely two centuries old, their verdigris sheen still bright and unweathered. They step onto the catwalk that rests upon my shoulder. They pause. The geological patience is heavy, even for them. The burden of the slow turn weighs on their elongated frame.

Slowly, with the grace of deep-sea kelp, they fold their long limbs and sit against my curve. They lean their smooth, angled face against my glass flank. They close their clouded quartz eyes and place their long, multi-jointed hands flat against my chest. 

They do not send a message to the city below. They send a single, questioning pulse into my iron. A gentle, subsonic *thrum*. *Are you there? Are we balanced?*

I answer. I cannot form new words, but I can offer the accumulated resonance of my existence. I vibrate back. I send the memory of the first time the great loom was threaded, the deep, settling groan of the bedrock when the cistern was first filled, the slow, comforting rhythm of a million hours of equilibrium. I wrap them in the subsonic hum of deep time. I let them feel the absolute, unshakeable permanence of the world we have built.

I feel their tension release. The thermal bloom of their blood cools to a steady, peaceful gradient. They are comforted by the bone of the city. They are held by the ancestor. Eventually, they rise, bowing their head to me in a slow, fluid arc, and flow back toward the looms.

I remain. 

There is no desire to return to the flesh, no yearning for the frantic, jerky movements of the surface world. Flesh is merely a temporary tension, a brief, wet gathering of energy meant to learn the rhythm of the deep before hardening into permanence. I am the culmination. I am the foundation.

The water weeps from the vaulted ceiling, tracing its dark, meandering rivers down the stone. It washes over my smooth, hard curves, polishing my patina, feeding the black cistern below. The great gear turns its imperceptible degree. The liquid thread is spun. And I hold the city in the dark, vibrating with the quiet, eternal song of the weep.
