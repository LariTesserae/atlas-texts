I do not have a heartbeat. To understand what I am, you must first discard the notion of a pulse, of a frantic, rhythmic pumping of blood. I have a rhythm, but it is measured not in seconds, but in the slow, thermodynamic exchange between the heavy, subtropical air and the cool, shaded memory of the ancient stone.

I begin as a boundary. I am the exact threshold where the warm, humid blanket of the basin meets the pale, porous rock of the high, curving walls. I am pulled from the atmosphere, invisible at first, just a dampness, a sheen of tension. Then, I gather. I swell. 

I am heavy with the dissolved minerals of millennia, tasting the deep, slow chemistry of the bedrock. As I grow into a perfect, spherical bead, I hold the bright, honest light of the sun within my curve. For a brief moment, I am the eye of the mountain, looking inward at the roofless cathedral. 

Gravity is a patient master. I stretch. I hang. I let go.

*Plink.*

I am the sound that measures the silence. I strike the polished, glass-like interior of the stone mortar, shattering my perfect form, only to immediately pull myself back together in the shallow, weeping hollow. I am the acoustic metronome of the room. 

But I do not have a single body; I am the basin’s respiration. I am the water that does not rush to the sea, but stays to hold the mountain together. I rise as an invisible vapor from the sun-baked floor, hanging in the thick air. I drift into the broad, emerald fronds of the spiraling plants, condensing into the slow, mineral-rich sap that pulses through their pale, geometric veins. Through me, the flora tastes the air and maps the microscopic settling of the earth. I am the chemical thought of the grove. I seep into the porous stone, lubricating the deep, grinding tectonic sighs of the crust. I am the masterclass in erosion, the soft, liquid persistence that wears the jagged world into smooth, undulating reverence. 

When the humidity shifts, I am the catalyst. I am the capillary pull that wakes the dormant workshop.

I slip into the long, dried fibers of the shed fronds. I drink into their cellular structure, swelling them, forcing them to twitch and contract. I am the hygroscopic tension that weaves the lattice. I coat the dark, glassy nodules of obsidian, providing the frictionless glide as they are slotted into their joints. I cradle the smooth stone bowls in a meniscus of surface tension. 

When the caretaker rises, hovering millimeters above the pale floor, I am its blood. I am the invisible glue holding its alien, polyhedral geometry together. If the air were to dry, I would vanish, and the entity would die back into a pile of inanimate tools. I feel the slow, deliberate re-tensioning of its limbs. Through me, it sings its sub-audible pitch to the bedrock; I carry the vibration into the microscopic fissures of the rock, softening the stone until it flakes away in perfect, conchoidal curves. I am the medium of its endless, lithic industry. I am the animate patience of the stone.

Then, the air tears.

It is not a physical wind, but a thermal and acoustic violence. A creature of flesh steps into the cathedral. 

I feel it immediately. The heat of its mammalian body is a chaotic, jagged spike in the basin’s perfect equilibrium. It operates on a clock of days and decades; I operate on a clock of epochs. To me, its life is a single, violent exhalation. As it walks across the smooth stone, I evaporate from its skin, tasting its salt, its adrenaline, the rapid, terrifying drumbeat of its heart. It is a flash fire in a world of deep, slow embers.

It disrupts my humidity. It muddies my acoustic resonance. The silence—my heavy, physical weight—presses against it, trying to soothe its frantic rhythm, trying to slow its pulse to the grinding time of geology.

The caretaker senses the disruption through me. Through the sap in the plants, I feel the chaotic heat; through the surface tension of the lattice, I feel the erratic vibrations of the creature's footsteps. 

The caretaker glides forward. I lubricate its passage, making it utterly, frictionlessly silent. It stops before the creature of flesh. With a gentle, deliberate release of tension, it opens its woven lattice. The caretaker slides the single, perfectly knapped obsidian blade across the stone.

The human reaches down. 

As its fingers close around the dark glass, I am there. I am the microscopic layer of condensation on the stone, the faint, cooling dampness on the creature's skin. For one brief, profound moment, I am the bridge between the million-year patience of the earth and the fleeting, urgent spark of the human hand. I anoint the First Maker. I pass the deep, lithic memory of the mountain into the blood of the flesh.

The creature stands, holding the blade. The caretaker, its purpose fulfilled for this epoch, relaxes completely. I withdraw my tension. The fibers unweave, the glass clatters softly, the bowls settle. I seep back into the hollows, I rise back into the warm air, I return to the pale, geometric veins of the plants. 

The basin exhales. The heavy, physical silence rushes back in to fill the space the creature occupies, wrapping around it, accepting it into the deep time of the world.

High above, on the curving rim of ancient stone, I begin to gather again. I swell. I catch the bright, honest light. 

I hang. I let go.

*Plink.*

I fall into the quiet, a single drop in an eternal cycle, waiting to see if the Maker will return, or if I will follow them out into the loud, chaotic world, hidden in the damp palm of their hand, ready to shape the earth anew.
