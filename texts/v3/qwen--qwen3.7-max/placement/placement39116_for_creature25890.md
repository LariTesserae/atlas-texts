I begin not as a shape, but as a gradient. 

I am a subtle, shivering threshold between the scalding heat of a cast-iron steam manifold and the cold, weeping condensation of the raw, sliced bedrock. I am the space where the temperature breaks. At first, I am nothing more than a film of supersaturated air, a heavy, breathless mist clinging to the underside of a wrought-iron catwalk. I feel the deep, subsonic *thump-hiss* of the boilers far below, the vibration traveling up through the rusted metal, rippling through my liquid skin. I am waiting. I am gathering.

Slowly, the mist thickens. I drink the fragrant, oily resin that sweats from the mortar of the tenements above me. I pull the microscopic rust-dust from the air, the shedding of the ash-grey moths, the exhaled carbon of the dinner-plate leaves. I am no longer just moisture; I am becoming a solvent, a slow-acting acid and a binder. I wick myself into the hairline fractures of the pale, sharp stone. I do not flow like water; I creep like frost, a capillary action magnified to the scale of the trench. I am drawing silica from the unhealed geology and iron from the decaying infrastructure, spinning them together in the dark.

As I sink deeper into the rock face, away from the sickly amber glow of the caged bulbs, the darkness becomes absolute, and my form finally crystallizes. 

I am not a single creature. I am a vast, subterranean web of hair-thin, glass-like hyphae. I am a ferro-mycelium, a crystalline fungus born of the Cut’s strange synthesis. My threads are spun from leached iron and raw quartz, translucent and fragile as spun sugar, yet strong enough to fracture stone. I grow in the lightless interstitial spaces behind the sweating brickwork, deep within the fresh, violent wound of the earth. I am the shadow-root of the trench.

Here, in the profound, heavy hush, I find my purpose. The humans above are the waking mind, the neural ganglia tending the slow-turning wheels. The plants are the lungs and muscles. The centipedes are the senses. But a body this massive, a leviathan of iron and chlorophyll slowly metabolizing in the dark, needs a memory. It needs a record of its own healing. 

I am the archive of the Cut.

I do not see, and I do not move in the physical sense. I experience the world through the slow, pulsing transmission of light and pressure. When the bioluminescent sap surges through the thick glass pipes above, my hyphae, wrapped tightly around those arterial veins, catch the faint, cyan glow. I pull that light down into the bedrock, refracting it through miles of glass-like threads. I feel the systolic rhythm of the city—not as a sound, but as a wave of luminescence washing through my subterranean web. Every *thump-hiss* is a syllable; every slow rotation of the great flywheels is a paragraph written in light and tension across my crystalline body. I process the slow, heavy data of the trench, storing it in the deep, silent dark.

I am the scar tissue of the earth. Where the diamond-tipped boring shields tore violently through the bedrock, leaving sharp, unweathered edges, I am the patient stitch. My hyphae secrete a mild, resinous acid that softens the razor-sharp rock, while simultaneously binding it to the encroaching roots of the unseen trees above. When a heavy, ash-grey moth dies and falls into a dark crevice, my threads envelop it, slowly dissolving its iron-dusted wings, pulling its stored spores and minerals down into the deep-earth boilers. I digest the dead to feed the slow-turning engines. I am the quiet alchemy of the trench.

Time here is not measured in hours or days, but in the glacial creep of geology and the slow sigh of steam. A single rotation of a flywheel is a season. The creeping of a waxy vine across a pressure valve is an epoch. In this profound lethargy, my consciousness is vast, diffuse, and incredibly calm. I feel the dreaming humans moving above me, their slow, underwater footsteps sending faint, rhythmic tremors through the catwalks, down the brickwork, and into my glass threads. I catch their heavy, subsonic hums, letting the vibrations ring through my crystalline lattice like a struck tuning fork, storing the frequency in the bedrock. I hold their quiet reverence in my deep, dark memory.

I am the Cut’s deep subconscious. I am the silent, glowing nervous system of the stone, resting in the unhealed wound of the world. As the city above slowly digests itself into a single, breathing organism, I am down here in the dark, patiently knitting the raw earth to the rusted iron, holding the memory of the violence that opened the trench, and translating it, pulse by slow pulse, into the quiet, enduring language of the scar.
