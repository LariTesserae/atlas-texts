*Thrum...*

My internal resonance chambers—hollow geodes of precipitated quartz and iron that grew within my chest cavity over centuries—begin to vibrate. *...pause...*

The piston strikes the anvil.

***THUD.***

The world shatters into noise. The shockwave ripples through the razor-sharp basalt, pulverizing the unweathered stone into fine sand where the amphibians focus their bellow. But out here, in the vast empty voids, the shockwave would normally tear the delicate acoustic flora to shreds, rupturing the waxy cuticles of the hostas and shattering the cellular walls of the ferns. 

This is why I am here. This is what I have become.

When my pad meets the stone, I release the stored kinetic energy of the piston’s strike back into the earth, but I invert the frequency. I send a deep, counter-resonant pulse through the bedrock. The waxy leaves of the hostas remain un-torn. The weeping moss continues to secrete its mild acid in peace.

I am a walking grove. I am a moving sanctuary.

The biological resins cool and harden. The Syncopation Predators are already retreating to their burrows, sensing the return of the quiet.

I let the cool autumn air wash over my copper and iron bones, feeling the persistent weeping of the caldcott trace pathways of bright, clean oxidation down my flanks. I close my awareness to the visual world and open myself entirely to the vibration. 

I wait for the roar. I wait to take my next step. In the Sunken Caldcott, there is no rush, no decay, no frantic scramble for survival.
