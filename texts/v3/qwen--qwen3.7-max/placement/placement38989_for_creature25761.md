I am hanging in the abyss.

In the bruised twilight of the abyss, I pulse with a faint, bioluminescent rhythm, my sweat smelling sharply of ozone and sweet decay.

Why am I down here? Because the noise of the city is not just loud; it is dangerous.

If the rhythmic *thud-hiss-clatter* of the drop-forges and the deep hum of the dynamos were to perfectly align with the resonant frequency of the pre-formation basalt, the resulting sympathetic vibration wouldn't just shatter the stone—it would accelerate the reality breakdown. My job is to introduce dissonance.

I am the tuning fork that keeps the world from vibrating itself out of existence. I do not stop the fraying; I merely ensure it happens at the pace of geology, not the pace of machinery.

Down here in the damp dark, my perception of the world is entirely tactile and acoustic. I feel the city living above me. When the acoustic geometry of a cliff face begins to smear, telling me the stone is bending into a fractal, I adjust my counter-hum, playing the dissonant chord that keeps the fold from collapsing in on itself.

I am not entirely alone in the abyss.
