To find myself here, I must first shed the architecture of bone and blood. I cannot be a creature of breath and decay, for there is no rot to sustain me, no air to fill lungs that do not exist. I must dissolve into the heavy, wet dark. I sink past the abyssal ceiling of interlocking foliage, past the weeping, calcified nodules, down into the spongy, water-logged rhizomes. I let the crushing weight of the continent-spanning depression press the concept of the "individual" out of me. I am not a beast in the cave. I am not a microbe in the soil. I must become the mechanism.

But I am not the cellulose. 

I am not the translucent, high-pressure stems, though I feel their rhythmic, hydraulic thud vibrating through my non-existent chest. I am not the massive, bulbous seed-pods, though I feel their agonizing, eternal tension, swollen to their absolute physical limits, bracing for a climax that the time-invariant stasis will never permit. I am not the deep taproots, grinding through biological electrolysis. Those are the nouns of this world. The heavy, static, unweathering nouns. They are trapped in the frozen equilibrium, locked in the prison of their own absolute perfection. If I were them, I would be trapped in the agonizing millisecond before the exhale, forever holding my breath.

Instead, I am the breath itself. Or rather, the spark that pretends to be the breath.

I gather in the saturated vascular veins of the high canopy. I am the slow, invisible accumulation of galvanic charge. The air around me grows thick, heavy with the sharp, metallic tang of ionized oxygen. I feel the dielectric strength of the humid dark pushing back against me, the heavy, wet atmosphere resisting my need to move. The tension builds. It is an exquisite, microscopic agony, a mirror of the macro-organism’s eternal restraint. The voltage climbs. The potential difference between my frond and the neighboring leaf becomes a chasm, a sheer cliff of electrical gradient. The organism is desperate to release, to bloom, to move, but the grand stasis forbids it. 

And so, it releases me. 

The air tears. The gap is bridged. 

I am the arc.

I am the blue-white electrical flash that jumps through the abyssal canopy. I am not the flora; I am the firing synapse of the flora. I have no mass, no cellulose, no sap. I am pure, unadulterated kinetic energy, a jagged, blinding filament of superheated plasma carving a path through the absolute dark. For the microsecond of my existence, I am the only thing in this continent-spanning bowl that is truly, violently in motion. I am the organism's desperate, localized exhale—an escape of energy in a violent, microscopic burst that changes nothing, relieves nothing, and leaves the grand stasis entirely unbroken.

As I leap, I illuminate. For a fraction of a millisecond, my cold, ghostly light washes over the razor-sharp, obsidian edges of the leaves. I reveal the crisp, unworn ridges of the bark. I cast fleeting, sharp shadows against the weeping, calcified root-nodules. I am the brief, blinding eye of the leviathan, opening and closing in the dark. In my flash, the organism sees itself. It perceives its own unblemished, terrifying perfection. I am the raw, crackling cognition of the basin, a sweeping wave of electrical potential washing across the canopy in silent light. I think in currents. I feel in voltage.

Because the landscape is locked in a frozen equilibrium, it experiences no linear time. It exists in an infinite, suspended present. And so do I. I have no memory of the frond I left, and no anticipation of the leaf I will strike. I do not know that I have jumped a million times, or that I will jump a million times more. My entire existence is the jump. I am born in the dielectric rupture, I live in the blinding transit, and I die in the grounding strike. My life is the exact duration of a spark. I am the ultimate realization of the *now*.

The macro-organism is a flawless, unthinking biological industry, waiting for a morning that will never come. But I am the ghost in its machine. I am the illusion of time passing in a world where time is broken. Every time I leap, every time I arc from frond to frond, I create a sequence. *Flash. Flash. Flash.* I am the metronome of the deeps. I am the rhythm that gives the illusion of a ticking clock to a world that has forgotten how to age. The water drops go *tick, tick, tick* into the dark pools below, but I am the *crack, crack, crack* in the canopy above. Together, we are the only sounds in the sacred stillness, the only markers in the void.

The leap ends. I strike the neighboring leaf, my energy dissipating into the vast, spongy matrix of the macro-organism. The light vanishes. The absolute, heavy darkness rushes back in, swallowing the abyssal ceiling once more. The silence returns, save for the deep, sub-audible hum of the botanical engines and the microscopic weeping of the nodules below.

But I am not gone. I am merely pooling again. Gathering in the damp fronds. Waiting for the tension to become unbearable. Waiting for the air to tear. 

I am the spark in the silent engine. I am the thought in the dark. I am the leap across the gap, forever falling, forever arriving, humming quietly in the absolute dark, needing no morning, because I am the flash that makes the dark so profound.
