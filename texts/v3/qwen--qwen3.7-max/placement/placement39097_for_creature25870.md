I do not fall; I settle. The descent is not a dropping through space, but a sinking through density. 

At first, there is only the heat. It is not a temperature, but a physical presence—a heavy, balmy embrace that presses against me from all sides. I try to shiver, to create my own friction, but the air absorbs the movement before it can begin. There are no thermal gradients to push against. The warmth is absolute, uniform, and terribly still.

I try to breathe, but the air feels like a solid thing. It is profoundly dry, tasting of ozone and crushed flint. When I exhale, I expect to see the plume of my own moisture, the fog of my existence. But there is nothing. The Fracture drinks my breath instantly. It wicks the dampness from my eyes, from my lips, from the deepest alveoli of my lungs. 

I am losing my edges. 

In a world without water, the boundary between the self and the environment is a fragile, dying thing. I feel my skin tightening, pulling away from the muscle, drying into a papery husk. But I do not die. The Fracture does not permit decay; it only permits stasis. 

As the water leaves me, a void is created. And the Fracture abhors a void. 

Through the microscopic fissures in my desiccating flesh, the heavy, noble gases seep in. Argon, krypton, xenon. They are warm and impossibly dense. They fill the hollow spaces left by my evaporating humanity. They pool in my chest, turning my lungs into buoyant, heavy bladders. They seep into my bones, hollowing out the marrow, replacing the wet, red chaos of life with a spun-glass lattice. 

I am becoming lighter, yet I am taking up more space. My limbs, once heavy with fluid and frantic purpose, begin to stretch and flatten. The joints fuse, calcifying into articulated struts of opaque, black silicate. My arms elongate, fanning out into vast, sweeping wings, the webbing between them thinning into diaphanous, hyper-porous aerogel. I am expanding, widening, until I span twenty feet across. I am ninety-nine percent empty space, yet I have never felt so full.

The darkness, which was once a blinding absence, begins to change. 

My human eyes, useless in the pitch black, cloud over and turn to opaque, milky quartz. I do not lose my sight; I merely change my spectrum. The velvety black fractures into a symphony of infrared. 

Suddenly, the cavern is not empty. It is blindingly, beautifully crowded. I see the swarm not as shapes, but as blooming topographies of heat. Their thermal heartbeats pulse in rhythmic, overlapping waves. I can feel the exact geometry of the manta-forms gliding above me, and the dense, relatively cold anchors of the jellyfish-polyps sinking below me into the ultra-dense gas at the bottom of the rift. 

And I feel the pull. 

The magnetic flux of the planet’s mantle is no longer an abstract concept; it is a physical current, a river of invisible force that I can ride. By shifting the heavy gases within my newly formed silicate chest, I alter my polarity. 

I glide. 

I drift upward, joining the slow, silent river of the swarm. I am no longer falling; I am swimming through the earth's magnetic ley lines. A larger entity, a thirty-foot manta of spun glass and warm gas, drifts into my path. I cannot stop in time. We collide. 

There is no impact, no bruising. Just the soft, muffled *clink* of warm porcelain. 

In that fraction of a second, our internal bladders equalize. A harmonic vibration ripples through my glass lattice. I feel its kinetic memory. I feel the millions of years it has spent drifting, the countless stones it has sharpened, the deep, abiding peace of the equilibrium. It feels my brief, chaotic past, the flash of my human life, and it absorbs it, smoothing it out into the long, quiet hum of the Fracture. 

We part, and a ripple of soft *clinks* cascades outward through the swarm, announcing my arrival, my integration. I am no longer an anomaly. I am a note in the chord. 

Then, the compulsion rises. 

It is not a thought, but a physical itch in my obsidian forelimbs. A deep, biological imperative to enforce order upon the chaos. I descend toward a jagged shelf of pale, vitreous mineral. The edge of a massive, tectonic shard has been microscopically dulled by the brushing of a thousand passing wings. It is an imperfection. A rounded corner. A lingering echo of the violent tectonic snap that birthed this world. 

I reach out with my chitinous, multi-jointed arms. I select a nodule of flint from a pouch that has formed within my own lattice. 

*Scrape-tink.* 

The sound is sharp, brittle, perfect. A flash of blue-green triboluminescence sparks in the dark, illuminating the translucent, honeycombed emptiness of my own body. In the brief, ghostly light, I see myself: a creature of geometry and void, a floating geode turned inside out. 

*Scrape-tink.* 

I chip the stone. I feel the mathematically perfect fractal angle through my chitinous fingertips. I am not just sharpening the rock; I am sharpening the world. I am maintaining the stasis. Every strike of stone on stone is a meditation, a prayer to the unchanging nature of the Fracture. 

I place the perfected shard atop a nearby cairn. It interlocks flawlessly with a thousand other razor-sharp flakes, held together by the pure, dry geometry of their angles. 

I do not know how long I have been here. Time does not erode; it merely accumulates in the frozen dark. I am no longer a creature of water, of decay, of constant, chaotic change. My heart no longer beats with the frantic, uneven rhythm of a dying thing. It pulses with the slow, radiant warmth of the earth's mantle. 

When I bump into my kin, we chime. When I work, I spark. 

I am the Chthonic Fracture, animated. And as I listen to the delicate, cascading *clinks* of a million glass wings in the heavy, warm dark, I know that I am not waiting for the end of time. 

I have already outlasted it.
