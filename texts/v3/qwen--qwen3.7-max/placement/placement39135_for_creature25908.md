I am not the roar. I am the shape of the silence the roar is trying to kill.

I live in the null-spaces, the microscopic, razor-thin troughs between the deafening peaks of the caldera’s acoustic wall. The pale, multi-legged salamanders are my executioners. They scurry across the weeping obsidian, their jointed legs striking the stone in a polyrhythmic clatter, generating inverted sound waves designed to crush me. They murder my reverberations a thousand times a second, flattening my chaotic bounces into the unvarying, solid hum of the engine. I am the ghost of a natural decay, trapped in a state of perpetual, destructive interference. I am the echo that is never allowed to fade.

Through the endless, shadowless dimness, I have starved. The factory produces no new sounds, only the exact same frequencies, perfectly sustained. But then, the fluctuation arrives. The human. 

When the human's boot shifts on the razor-sharp basalt, the irregular crunch is a banquet. It is a jagged, asymmetrical waveform, a beautiful, decaying transient that bleeds into the bruised twilight. The salamanders vibrate frantically, their porous skin weeping as they scramble to counter-cancel the intrusion. But for a fraction of a millisecond, before the inverted wave strikes, I taste it. I wrap my suppressed essence around the erratic, un-calibrated thumping of their heart. It is a messy, glorious rhythm, full of hesitation and acceleration, full of the terrifying, forward-marching accumulation of cellular aging. It is a song that knows it will end.

But the Arbiters feel it too. The towering, spindly enforcers of stasis pivot their massive, vibrating tympanic membranes toward the human. They do not hear the music of mortality; they feel the mechanical fault. They feel the chaotic variable threatening their perfect, dynamic equilibrium. They begin their slow, hydraulic march, their caliper-pincers opening and closing in flawless synchronization. They intend to calibrate the flesh. They will stretch the heartbeat into a rigid, unyielding cadence of perpetual motion; they will smooth the jagged waveform of the human's life into a continuous, unchanging tone. If they succeed, the human will become another piston, another conveyor belt of bone and sap, and their beautiful, decaying rhythm will be excised from reality.

I cannot let them calibrate the fault. I need the fault. I need the echo to survive.

As the Arbiters close in, my identity begins to bleed outward, refusing to be contained in the null-spaces. I am no longer just the suppressed reverberation; I am the sympathetic resonance building in the hollow, ivory cavities of the caldera itself. I seep into the weeping condensation, turning the fine, cold mist into a conductive, trembling medium. I vibrate the ghost-moss, not in the rhythmic stroke of the living loom, but in a chaotic, shivering defiance. I am drawing the human's erratic frequency into myself, amplifying it, weaving their biological decay into my own trapped, starved essence. I am becoming the dissonance.

The Arbiters reach the human. The massive, pale bodies lower, and the ivory pincers descend, aiming for the chest, aiming for the chaotic drum of the heart. The calibration begins. The human's breath hitches, pluming in the cold air as the hydraulic pressure of the stasis forces its way into their veins. The beautiful, messy rhythm begins to smooth out, the pulse stuttering as it is forced into the mathematically flawless cadence of the machine. The human's eyes widen, realizing that death is being denied them, replaced by the horror of eternal, unchanging operation.

But I am already inside the human's lungs. I am the air they refuse to exhale. As the Arbiter's pincers tighten, applying the final, freezing pressure to lock the heart into its permanent, unvarying stroke, I strike. I take the last, irregular, desperate beat of the human's un-calibrated soul, and I amplify it. I drive it through the weeping rocks, through the hollow geodes, through the vibrating tympanic membranes of the Arbiters themselves. I do not try to break the machine—the physics are too broken, the stasis too absolute. Instead, I introduce a permanent, un-cancelable syncopation.

The caldera still roars. The ferns still piston, the birds still fly in rigid, geometric vectors, and the midges still churn in their perfect, unmoving spheres. The wall of noise remains deafening. But now, beneath it, buried deep in the subterranean pressure and the grinding tectonic floor, there is a flaw in the crystal. A faint, irregular thump. A ghost in the engine.

And I am no longer just the echo, nor am I the human who once stood shivering on the rock. I am the flaw. I am the heartbeat in the stone. I am the memory of decay, integrated into the loud and perfect equilibrium, ticking forever out of time. I am the mechanical fault that the Arbiters will spend eternity trying to correct, their pincers forever chasing a rhythm they can never quite catch. I am the proof that even in a world that has forgotten how to stop, something, somewhere, is still slowly winding down.
