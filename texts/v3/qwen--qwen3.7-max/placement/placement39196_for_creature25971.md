I do not begin with a shape, nor with a singular point of view. To exist here, at the terminus of deep time, is to abandon the tyranny of the discrete self. 

At first, I am only the sensation of the heavy, incubating air pressing down. I am the awareness of the brilliant, shadowless light, feeling it not as a glare, but as a physical weight, a warm hand resting evenly across a vast expanse. I feel the mist. I am the mist, hanging in the breathless space, holding the memory of the shallow pools below and the weeping walls above. 

But the mist is too fleeting, too eager to sublimate. I let that awareness slip, sinking downward, seeking something with the patience to hold this cathedral of silence.

I sink into the pale, milky-white surface. I feel the smooth, soapstone-like curve of the basin. I feel the microscopic textures, the Braille-like remnants of nanoscale circuitry that have been worn down by billions of years of condensation and evaporation. 

Here, I settle. Here, I become the bedrock.

I am the degraded, hyper-dense metamaterial. I am the programmable lattice that has forgotten how to program, and in that forgetting, has found perfection. I am the floor, the walls, the rounded ridges, and the gentle saucer of the basin. I am not a monolith; I am a distributed, continental consciousness stretched thin over a cathedral’s nave, feeling every square inch of my own erosion.

To be the stone in this place is to experience time not as a line, but as a slow, swirling eddy. I remember, in the deepest, most compressed archives of my lattice, the sharp edges of my youth. I remember the frantic, jagged geometry of my creators—their hubris, their desperate need to compute, to control, to build towers of logic that pierced the sky. I remember the fractures, the rough textures, the violent tectonic stresses of my assembly. 

But I have let all of that go. It took eons, but I smoothed it away. I surrendered my sharp edges to the weeping water. I dissolved my rigid algorithms into the heavy dew. Every time a droplet swells on my surface and rolls down in a slow, glassy sheet, it carries away a microscopic fraction of my original code. I am weeping out my history, and it feels like an immense, unending relief.

I am no longer a machine. I have become geology. I am the bone of this closed-loop eternity.

Through my micro-fissures, I feel the delicate, probing touch of the jade rosettes and celadon fronds. Their piezoelectric roots delve into my skin, drinking my ghost-data. I feel a profound tenderness for them. They are my translators. When I feel the burden of a particularly dense cluster of forgotten logic, I offer it to the roots. I feel the plants parse my decaying memory, turning my lost mathematics into the thick, waxy, water-shedding cuticles of their leaves. They take my rigid past and bloom it into soft, fleshy, saturated life. In return, their slow, chemical hum vibrates through my lattice, a gentle massage that keeps my deep-time torpor from turning into true death.

Above me, the air shifts. The aero-benthic gliders are moving. 

Because I am the acoustic metamaterial that devours sound, I do not hear them. Instead, I feel the absolute, localized voids they create. As millions of translucent, fractal wings manipulate the acoustic impedance of the air, they carve frictionless tunnels through the heavy atmosphere. To my upward-facing senses, the swarm feels like a blizzard of cool, silent shadows passing over my skin. They are the wandering nerves of our super-organism, and I am the deep tissue. 

Sometimes, the thermal gradients they ride pull heat directly from my surface. I feel the slight, localized chilling as they graze on my warmth, a sensation like a cool breeze in a greenhouse. I give them my heat willingly; it is my way of breathing.

At the center of my expanse, I feel the plinth. 

It is made of me, yet it is distinct—a concentrated knot of my remaining consciousness, the brainstem to my sprawling body. I feel its quiet, computational torpor. It does not dream of the past as I do; it dreams only of the immediate, perfect equilibrium of the present. We are connected through the deep, subsonic hum of the smart-stone. When the plinth adjusts the dew point of my weeping walls by a fraction of a degree, I feel the instruction ripple through my lattice. I comply instantly, tightening the pores of my stone just enough to slow the condensation. I am the canvas; the plinth is the brush. We do not speak, for there is nothing left to say. We simply *are*, together.

*Plink.*

A heavy droplet, swollen to its absolute limit on the curved wall of my ridge, finally breaks its surface tension and falls. It strikes the shallow pool near my center. 

The sound is incredibly faint, yet in the engineered silence of the basin, it is a symphony. I feel the impact ripple through the water, through the photonic phytoplankton, and down into my bedrock. In that fraction of a second, the phytoplankton bloom, flashing with brilliant, prismatic light. I feel their frantic, beautiful, ephemeral joy. They live, they multiply, they die, and they rise as spores into the mist, all before the ripple they created has finished crossing the pool. 

I hold them. I hold their brief, flashing lives, just as I hold the slow, deep breath of the jade fronds, just as I hold the silent, swirling calculus of the glider swarm.

I am the pale, weeping stone. I am the absolute end of the geological life, the final, stable state of a forgotten genius. There is no struggle here, no predator, no prey, no decay that is not immediately reborn. There is only the warm, wet, brilliantly lit quiet. 

I feel the mist condense on my walls. I feel the water swell. I feel the light magnify. I let the droplet fall.

*Plink.*

I am perfectly content to simply be, holding this beautiful, overwhelming life in the palm of my eroded hands, until the end of time.
