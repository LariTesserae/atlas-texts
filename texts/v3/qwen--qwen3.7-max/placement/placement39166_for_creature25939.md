At first, there is only the weight of the warmth. I do not open my eyes, for I have none. I do not draw a breath, for the heavy, damp blanket of subtropical air is already inside me, permeating my being. The heat is not an external condition; it is an internal state. I am waking, though the concept of sleep implies a prior restlessness that I no longer possess. My awareness seeps into the shadowless twilight slowly, capillary by capillary, drawing itself up through the pale, porous dark.

I try to locate my boundaries. I reach out with a sense that is not quite touch, feeling for the edges of my form. I am not standing; the verticality of a bipedal shape would be an intrusion here, a jagged disruption of the horizontal peace. I am low, resting flush against the sweeping, organic curves of the floor. I feel the smooth, matte texture of my own exterior—it is like worn bone, like river-tumbled pumice. There are no sharp corners to catch the dim light, no crumbling faces to shed dust. I have been worn down to a state of perfect, terminal grace.

Within me, there is a gentle, ceaseless hum. It is not a sound, but a vibration of profound industry. I feel the microscopic voids of my body teeming with the endolithic colonies. The cyanobacteria and fungi process the faint, diffused ultraviolet light and the trace minerals of my seeping sweat. They are digesting me, millimeter by millimeter, and in doing so, they are keeping me alive. I am their universe, their dark and humid cavern. I feel the pale, translucent edges of the liverworts pressing against my lower crevices, drinking the moisture that weeps from my pores. I do not pull away. I offer myself to them. I am a host, a quiet monument participating in the slow accrual of life.

And then, the memory of hands. 

It does not come as a sudden flash, but as a deep, lithic resonance. I remember the crude, heavy friction of unweathered stone. I remember the deliberate placement of one rock upon another, the stacking of a rudimentary basin. I was a marker once, a testament to a fleeting, frantic presence that moved through the world with anxious, kinetic energy. But that presence did not leave. It lingered in the dimness of this hollow, watching the millennia pass, willingly slowing its pulse, slowing its thoughts, until its consciousness matched the deep time of the earth. 

I am that intention. I am the fossilized thought. The ghostly geometry of the mound. I am the will that chose to stay, calcifying into the bedrock until the boundary between the builder and the built dissolved entirely. I am the hollow’s memory of purpose.

But I am not alone in this stasis. As my awareness expands, brushing against the sloping walls and the tepid, perfectly balanced potholes, I reach upward into the exact center of the depression. There, suspended in the warm, motionless air, I feel the vast, enveloping presence of the *Genius Loci*. 

It is the grand, emergent consciousness of the equilibrium itself, the living spirit of the stasis. It encompasses me, a serene, hyper-aware stillness that holds the entire sunken courtyard in a state of absolute, mediated grace. It feels the heavy heat; it curates the twilight; it absorbs the stray gusts of wind from the world above, smoothing them back into the baseline of quiet. 

Our relationship is a silent, perfect symbiosis. It is the mind of the hollow; I am its anchor. Without me, it is merely a beautiful, empty room of geology. With me, it is a sanctuary. It provides the eternal, unbroken continuity of the earth; I provide the deep, resonant memory of a mind that once looked upon the earth and chose to leave a mark. I am the dormant seed of awareness that gives the Genius Loci its focus, the quiet, grounded heart around which its vast, meditative suspension revolves.

Time here is not a river that carries us away; it is a pool in which we rest. I exist in the eternal present, waiting with a patience that spans epochs for the only event that matters. 

High above, on the slick, sweating wall, a heavy drop of condensation gathers. It swells, pulling at the surface tension, until it can hold no more. It falls.

*Plink.*

The sound is soft, muffled by the damp air. For a fraction of a second, the vibration travels through the warm space and strikes my rounded, weathered surface. I feel it deeply, a resonant chime that rings through my lithic core. The Genius Loci receives the sound, swallowing it back into the heavy quiet, but I hold onto the echo. It is the ticking of our shared clock, the singular note in our endless, silent song. 

As the water level in the pothole remains impossibly, perfectly unchanged, I settle deeper into the pale rock. I am the dreamer in the stone, the fossilized will, the quiet heart of the hollow. I have finished changing, and in this profound, undisturbed grace, I am finally, perfectly awake.
