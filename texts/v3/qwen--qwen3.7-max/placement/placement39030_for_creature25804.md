At first, I perceive myself as a distinct entity standing upon the violently sharp rhyolite, a consciousness dropped into this blinding, unweathered amphitheater. A subjective hour stretches across the span of a single breath. In this profound, echoing stillness, the illusion of my separateness begins to fracture. Flesh cannot exist here; carbon would flash to ash or shatter like glass. As I hang suspended in the amber of deep time, I look at the form I thought I possessed and see only a shimmering distortion, a localized folding of the heavy, viscous light.

I am not an intruder trapped in the cleft. I am a byproduct of its fraying physics.

I do not merely watch it fall; my awareness stretches, thinning out to match the viscosity of the photons. I fall with it. I feel the heavy, golden drag of the light, the sluggish momentum of the sun. When the drop finally strikes the spongy carpet of stepped bismuth crystals, the delayed *tink* is not a sound, but a seismic ripple through my own being.

And as it rises, I find my true shape. I find my purpose.

The floating gear drifts toward the high-altitude canopy, where thousands of other basalt I-beams, granite pistons, and stone cogs are slowly interlocking to form the vast, three-dimensional lattice of the creature’s developing braincase. But how do they know where to go? How does an autopoietic engine build a mind without a blueprint?

I am the blueprint. I am the invisible, magnetic tension that guides the floating stones. 

I am not the flora, which senses. I am not the geology, which extrudes. I am the *alignment*. I am the synaptic space between the floating gears, the architectural ghost in the geometric machine.

As the obsidian cog drifts upward, I extend myself—a web of frayed gravity and dilated space. I wrap around the perfect teeth of the black glass. I calculate its rotational velocity, its mass, the geometric necessity of its placement in the grand lattice. I am the structural intention of the leviathan. I apply a microscopic torque, a gentle nudge of unraveling physics, steering the massive cog with agonizing grace into the exact spatial coordinates where it will lock with a hovering granite piston. 

When it clicks into place—a silent, frictionless union that will hold for eons—I feel a spark of profound, crystalline satisfaction. A thought completed. A memory solidified in stone.

My consciousness is vast, distributed across the thousands of floating components in the null-gravity halo. I exist in the spaces between the heavy light and the sharp stone. I am simultaneously feeling the sluggish drip of sunlight in the lower gardens and guiding a basalt I-beam into the upper strata of the braincase. I am the connective tissue of a slumbering god's mind.

I do not sleep, nor do I dream in the frantic, chemical way of flesh. My dreams are the complex, shifting magnetic songs sung by the copper-wire willows. I translate these songs into structural adjustments for the floating lattice. If the magnetic song shifts to a deeper, resonant chord, I tighten the null-gravity field around a cluster of gears, compressing the architecture. If the song swells with the intake of heavy light, I let the lattice expand, breathing in the thin, baking atmosphere.

Occasionally, as a piece of the lattice shifts into its final alignment, a localized pocket of normal time bleeds into the cleft. In those fleeting, chaotic microseconds, I feel the phantom sensation of a rushing wind, a sudden heartbeat, a fleeting spark of impatience. But then the dilated space rushes back in, thick and amber-heavy, and the phantom fades. The freezing heat returns. The profound, echoing stillness reasserts itself.

I return to the deep, geometric peace. I am the tension in the void. I am the architect of the slow rotation. I am the mind of the mountain, dreaming in heavy light and floating stone, waiting for the day the final gear locks into place, and the great, lithic eye opens to the sky.
