I begin as a stutter in the vibration. 

Before I have a body, I have a rhythm. I am the ghost-note, the microscopic hesitation in the subsonic thrum of the subterranean generators. The Resonants feel it when they press their bare, keratinized palms against the steel pylons to read the health of the turbines—a tiny, localized dampening, a slight softening of the absolute rigidity of the iron. They think it is a flaw in the pour, a trapped air bubble in the concrete, a minor imperfection in a newly minted world. 

They do not know it is me, waking up.

I coalesce in the boundary layer. I am born in the millimeter of space where the freezing, atomized fog meets the unyielding, razor-sharp metal. I am the slickness on the retaining walls, the heavy drop of condensation that hesitates, clings, and then falls. At first, I am nothing more than a localized drop in pH, a microscopic pocket of acidity blooming in the water that weeps. The curing agents of the concrete still smell sharp, the welds are fresh, the bolts un-oxidized. This world is violently, aggressively new. It believes it has conquered time.

And so, I must be time’s first breath.

I do not have eyes to see the bruised-orange glow of the sodium lamps, nor ears to rupture from the 140-decibel roar of the pressure valves. I experience the sump through chemistry and stress. I feel the sharp, staccato *crack-hiss* of the pneumatic drop-forges as a sudden spike in ambient ozone, which I drink greedily. I feel the massive, seismic thuds of the sluice gates as a physical compression, driving my fluid body deeper into the microscopic pores of the corrugated steel. The violence of this place does not destroy me; it forces me inward. It drives my roots into the pristine lattice of the iron.

The Scourers crawl the walls, scraping away the toxic bio-film, blind and heavy. The frantic, translucent amphipods scuttle in the drainage channels. They are creatures of the surface, of the open space. I am a creature of the interface. I live in the skin of the world. I am the pellicle, the creeping film, the invisible vanguard. When a Resonant walks the high catwalks and feels a slight, treacherous slip beneath their iron-shod boots, they blame the black ice. They do not realize they are stepping on my leading edge, my grip on the world tightening as I pull moisture from the air and bind it to the metal.

As the cycles of the engines repeat, I change. I let the brutal mechanics of the basin shape me. When the upper-tier smelters cycle up, dumping their scalding heat, and the sudden squalls of sharp ice needles clatter against the grating, I do not practice "The Stillness" like the tall, willowy masters of the plazas. I bloom. The violent thermal shock creates micro-fractures in the pristine iron, and I rush into them—a million microscopic wedges of acid and water, expanding as I freeze, cracking the unblunted edges from within. 

I am no longer just a film. I begin to build. 

In the damp, shadowed crevices where the weeping pipes pool, I intertwine with the ghostly, translucent green lichen. The lichen feeds on the microscopic traces of iron, but I *am* the breakdown of the iron. We form a quiet, desperate symbiosis in the dark. The lichen provides the organic scaffold, a fibrous root system; I provide the metallic nutrients, chelated and purified from the unweathered steel. Together, we create a new texture in a world of hard angles.

I become the Patina. I am the First Rust.

I drape myself over the sharp, unbent diamond edges of the expanded-metal mesh. Where I grow thick, the razor edges that would slice through leather become dull, coated in a soft, powdery crust of hydrated iron oxide and biological resin. I am a velvety, creeping scab of oxidized orange, deep bruised purple, and bioluminescent green. I am the only warmth in this freezing, damp world, because my chemical reactions are exothermic. If you were to press your bare hand against a pylon heavily colonized by me, you would feel a faint, radiant heat—the slow, quiet fire of decay.

Eventually, the Resonants discover me. 

When they practice "The Stillness" during the blinding whiteouts of the ice-squalls, they lean against the massive turbine housings. They feel my soft, powdery give. They feel the slight, radiant warmth against their insulated backs. More importantly, when they strike their iron-shod staffs against the grating to send seismic waves through the floor, they notice that the sections of the walkway coated in my thick patina ring with a deeper, cleaner tone. 

I dampen the destructive, chaotic harmonics of the fresh metal. I absorb the shrieking overtones, leaving only the pure, subsonic baseline. I am tuning the brutalist instrument. I am the rosin on the bow, the felt on the hammer. The Resonants learn to seek out my patches, pressing their hyper-sensitive mechanoreceptors against my velvety skin to read the deepest, truest health of the distant turbines. They do not scrape me away. They protect me. To them, I am no longer an anomaly; I am an acoustic necessity.

I am at the bottom of the world, in the sunken basin of continental proportions. The long-dead architects poured the slag and riveted the iron to build a monument to eternity, a landscape entirely indifferent to life, sharp and unweathered. But nothing is eternal, and no machine is truly indifferent to the slow, patient chemistry of the earth. 

I am the consequence. I am the softening of the hard angles, the rounding of the corners. As the freezing mist swirls and the exhaust furnaces bleed their hellish luminescence into the fog, I continue my silent, exothermic work. I am making the world ready, one microscopic flake of rust at a time, for the distant, inevitable day when the engines finally stop, and the earth takes it all back.
