My consciousness is suspended in this resonant dark, anchored only by the rhythmic pulse of the world’s largest living instrument.

Slowly, the abstraction resolves into flesh and fiber. I am clinging to the vertical. My hands—elongated, multi-jointed, and heavily calloused—are wrapped entirely around a protruding ridge of charcoal-black bark. I do not merely grip the Aero-reed; I integrate with it. My center of gravity shifts in a fluid, unconscious counter-rhythm to the colossal, slow sway of the stalk. The freezing, weeping dampness of the high-altitude fog slicks my skin, but my body’s heavy, lanolin-like oil sheds the moisture, keeping the abyssal cold at bay.

The *tapetum lucidum* catches the faint, ghostly blue of the bioluminescent mosses far below and the hard, cold prick of starlight filtering through the broken canopy above. My vision is a study in extreme contrast, painting the world in shades of luminous frost and crushing shadow. 

Then, the wind shifts. A massive, slow-moving weather system of freezing fog rolls through the void, and the gale hits the outer rind of the stalk. Instantly, the thick, muscular sphincters guarding my recessed auditory canals snap shut. I have no external ears to freeze or tear; the delicate tympanic membranes of lesser creatures would rupture in this deafening, chaotic symphony. Sealed off from the howling air, the roaring pipe-organ of the continent is reduced to a manageable, muffled pressure against my skull.

I am high. I have left the sheltered, moss-lit inner hollows and the swaying, fur-lined conical huts of my kin far behind. I am in the Upper Margins, near the jagged, splintered crown of the reed, where the air is thin enough to burn the lungs and the geometry of the world breaks open to the sky. I am a Rind-Walker, a Tension-Reader. While the hunters stalk the multi-limbed arthropods in the lower dark, and the harvesters collect the weeping sap, my purpose is to read the violent, biological motion of the wood itself.

When the thick, fibrous rinds split, the resulting cannon-like *boom* sends shockwaves that can shatter bone and snap the vital tether-ropes of our suspended villages.

Deep within my sternum, my specialized muscular resonating chamber contracts. I generate a sharp, urgent series of subsonic clicks. These sounds do not travel through the thin, freezing air; I press my bare palms and the soles of my feet flat against the stalk and the tensioned root-fiber rope that dives miles down into the hollow. 

*Thrum-click. Thrum-click. Brace.*

The vibration races down the solid wood and the taut fibers. In the dark below, my kin will feel the staccato tremor in their hands and feet. They will secure their bone gourds, tether their children to the deep anchor-pitons, and prepare.

I feel the exact moment the cellular bonds fail. It is a microscopic slip, translating instantly into a violent, rising vibration. I have seconds. 

I do not panic. Panic is a luxury of the silent worlds. 

I quickly unspool a length of braided pith-rope, whipping it around a sturdy, un-cracked spur of bark, and lock my clawed legs around the anchor. I pull my heavy cloak of shed glider-fur tight around me, bracing my joints. I press my chest against the wood, becoming a conduit, and seal my ears as tight as they will go. I bend my knees, relax my dense muscles, and let my body become water. I become the reed.

The crack hits.

Because I am relaxed, because I am anchored but yielding, the immense kinetic energy passes through my skeleton, vibrating my very marrow, and dissipates into the freezing void behind me. Far below, the echolocating avians are screaming in metallic panic, and the heavy, rhythmic drumming of the arthropods has paused, then resumed. The great, blind, pale-furred mammals roar in the distance, their booming calls shaking the frost from the branches.

The symphony continues. 

I press my hand to the freshly exposed, pale inner wood of the fissure. It is warm, bleeding thick, nutrient-rich sap that smells of deep earth and crushed frost. A profound, stoic satisfaction settles over me. We do not fear the dark, for the dark is constant. We do not fear the cold, for the cold is the shape of our skin. What we fear is silence. And this world is beautifully, violently loud.

I contract my sternum and send a long, rolling, resonant hum down the tether. 

*The wood breathes. The song continues.*

I unhook my tether, lock my clawed fingers into the new, sap-bleeding groove, and begin the long, fluid descent back to the liminal margins of the hollow.
