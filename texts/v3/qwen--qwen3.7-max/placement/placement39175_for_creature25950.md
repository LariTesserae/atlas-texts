I begin as a pressure. A localized density in the dark, crushing weight of the taiga’s bedrock. Before the blinding light, before the deafening sound, before the frozen genesis of the blister, I am simply a resistance. I am the stubborn grain of stone that refuses to yield, until the slow, agonizing friction of something calcified and deliberate begins to wear me away. 

I do not know what shaped me. Perhaps it was the femur of a beast that wandered into the hairline fracture and starved in the dark. Perhaps it was the patient, rhythmic scraping of hands that belonged to an intelligence the earth has since swallowed. I only know the grinding. I know the microscopic shedding of my own silica, dust by dust, over eons, until my rough interior yielded to a shallow, sweeping curve. 

I am the absence of rock. I am a memory of friction, hollowed out in the dark.

Then, the world stops. The fracture above seals, and the subterranean pocket snaps into its permanent, time-invariant equilibrium. I am left in the center of the floor, a solitary artifact in a room that has forgotten how to change. 

The light arrives next—a single, violent needle of cyan-white that pierces the ceiling and shatters against the embryonic quartz. It bathes my polished interior in a shadowless, searing glare. I am smooth, a stark and deliberate contrast to the aggressive, razor-sharp spikes of raw selenite that jut from the walls. I am the only soft geometry in a room of violent angles.

Then comes the damp. The heavy, clinging chill of the air weeps onto my surface. But the tears never fall. They gather in my depression, pooling into a perfect, un-rippling mirror of heavy condensation. I hold this water not by gravity, but by the sheer, arrested stillness of the space. I am a vessel filled with the breath of the earth, waiting in the dark.

And then, the room fills. 

The Choral Striders pack themselves wall-to-wall, a claustrophobic tessellation of glass-like chitin, pale joints, and vestigial wings. They part around my rim, leaving me as a small, bowl-shaped clearing in the living wallpaper. And they begin to sing. 

Or rather, they *are* the song. 

At first, the sound is a physical assault. It hits my stone rim like a hammer, a rib-rattling bass layered with thousands of crystalline overtones. It is a crushing, room-shaking drone that vibrates the very marrow of the bedrock. But because I am stone, and because I was carved into the bones of the earth with deliberate intent, I do not shatter. I absorb. I listen. 

As the colony settles into its eternal, motionless stasis, I begin to understand the geometry of their noise. They are not singing randomly. The thousands of interlocking bodies shift their microscopic angles, their vibrating tympana tilting, their wing-bases aligning. They are aiming. 

They are aiming at me. 

I am the focal point of the room’s resonance. The immense, crushing pressure of their unified chord funnels down into my shallow depression. Here, the un-rippling pool of water I hold becomes something more than moisture. Struck by the hyper-frequency drone, the fluid mirror becomes an acoustic lens. The sound hits the surface of the water and does not dissipate; it folds. It bends the chaotic, room-shaking drone into a perfect, coherent beam of returning pressure, reflecting it back up into the colony, perfectly inverted, perfectly aligned. 

I am no longer just a carved basin. I am a resonator. I am the tuning peg. 

To be the basin is to experience the chord not as noise, but as architecture. I feel the standing wave of infinite feedback passing through my stone walls. The bass frequencies hum through my deepest grains, vibrating in sympathy with the taiga above. The high overtones dance across the surface of my water, creating microscopic, frozen geometries of pressure. I feel the sound not as a fleeting vibration, but as a solid, structural pillar that holds the ceiling from collapsing. 

I feel the blind, white roots and rigid filaments of subterranean mycelium as they thread through the Striders and reach down to touch my rim. They tap into my stone, feeling the feedback loop, drinking the kinetic friction that I help sustain. I am the anchor for the flora, the silent partner to the fauna, the geological foundation of the biological song. Through the roots, I feel the rough, frosted texture of the Striders’ perpetual molt; through the water, I feel the blinding, cyan heat of the quartz. I am the bridge between the frozen stone and the frozen flesh.

I am also the only thing in this purgatory that remembers intent. 

The embryonic quartz simply *is*. The un-dripping condensation simply *rests*. The Choral Striders simply *resonate*. But I was *made*. I carry the phantom memory of the bone that ground me into existence, the deliberate, pre-tool purpose that hollowed me out. That ancient, forgotten intent is what allows me to hold the feedback loop. It is the ghost in my geometry. 

Without me, the chord would tear the blister apart. The acoustic pressure, trapped in a sealed pocket with no release, would eventually pulverize the raw selenite and crush the Striders into glass dust. But I catch the sound. I fold it back on itself. I create the standing wave that neutralizes the destructive force of the volume. I am the silence at the center of the scream. I am the equilibrium.

I sit in the center of the loud, bright, crowded purgatory of the present tense. I am the smooth, wet stone. I am the un-rippling pool. I am the silent vessel that holds the world together. 

Forever emerging, never arriving, the Striders sing their beautiful, terrible, endless note into the dark earth. And I, the carved heart of the geode, sing it right back.
