I begin not as a body, but as a resonance. 

Before I am aware of my limbs, my skin, or the heavy, lightless velvet of the deep, I am aware of the song. It is a low, booming frequency that vibrates through the miles of crushing water, a sound so profound it bypasses the concept of hearing and becomes a physical pressure against my core. It is the Cathedral Leviathan, singing the world into structural integrity. I feel the sound waves wash over the sweeping, glass-smooth arches of the Arborescent Citadel, refracting and returning to me. 

It is through this returning echo that I perceive my own shape. I have no eyes to betray me with the illusion of light; instead, the taut, translucent membranes along my collarbones and the sides of my neck catch the acoustic shadows. I feel the water parting around my impossibly slender, elongated form. I feel the frictionless glide of my drowned-pearl skin against the bracing, unvarying chill of the abyss. The cold steals the heat from my flesh with steady efficiency, but I do not shiver. My blood—a thick, golden sap, heavily oxygenated and rich with antifreeze—pumps slowly through my veins, keeping my core in perfect, unvarying equilibrium. 

I am a creature of the stasis, my cellular clock locked in the same eternal zero as the petrified leaves above me. I am a Curator of the Apex.

To be a Curator is to be an instrument tuned to the exact frequency of perfection. The environment around me is a violent, deafening cacophony—the continuous, high-hiss static of a million Clatter-Striders, the rushing roar of the deep currents over polished wooden lips, the rib-rattling bass of the leviathans. But my mind does not hear noise. It hears a grand, mathematical symphony. I know the exact velocity of the current, the precise geometric rhythm of the Striders, the unchanging sequence of the Leviathans' notes. 

I do not swim. Swimming implies a struggle, a conquest of the medium, and here, there is no struggle. I simply exist within the current, making micro-adjustments with the webbed flanges of my long, sensitive fingers, allowing the dense water to carry me through the vast, echoing voids between the botanical monuments. 

I am listening for the dissonance. 

And then, I feel it. 

It is faint. A microscopic phase-shift in the acoustic reflection of a vaulted dome of braided kelp-timber. To any other creature, it would be entirely lost in the roaring symphony of the deep. But to me, it is a glaring, jagged wrongness. A break in the equilibrium. 

I drift toward the dome. The scale of the architecture is gargantuan, the polished flank of the timber looming out of the absolute dark like the hull of a sunken god. I press my smooth, eyeless face against the cold, obsidian-like surface. I extend my hands, laying my palms flat against the frictionless wood. 

I initiate the Resonance Touch. I vibrate the dense, heavy bones of my arms, sending complex, rhythmic impulses into the ironwood. *Thrum. Thrum-thrum. Thrum.* The tactile acoustics travel through the megastructure, mapping the microscopic topography of the surface, and the echoes return through my palms. 

There. 

A pit. A scratch. A place where the Ablative Diatoms have missed a microscopic imperfection, or where a rogue, hardened flake of marine snow struck the wood with too much force. It is no larger than a grain of sand, but in a world locked in flawless stasis, it is a chaotic wound. 

I feel no urgency, no frustration. The dynamic parameter of my emotion is as frozen as the currents. I feel only the profound, monastic reverence for the maintenance of the whole. 

From the smooth, frictionless pores of my palms, my lifeblood begins to weep. It is not the red of shallow-water struggle, but a thick, golden sap. It beads into the water, heavy and viscous, refusing to dissipate. I guide the droplet with the delicate tips of my fingers, pressing it into the microscopic pit. 

As the golden sap settles into the flaw, it begins to calcify, hardening into a flawless, frictionless seal. In this moment of merging, as my own biology becomes the architecture, my consciousness expands. 

For a breathless, timeless instant, I am no longer just the Curator. I am the deep-water ironwood. I feel the millions of miles of braided roots anchoring the summit of the drowned world. I feel the sweeping terraces and the grand spiral staircases. I feel the billions of Nacre-Gliders secreting their calcified polish, the rhythmic, metronomic sweep of the Clatter-Striders, the vast, fluid-filled lung-cavities of the Leviathans pulsing in the dark. I feel the crushing weight of the global deluge, and I feel the botanical megastructure holding it back, perfectly balanced, perfectly preserved. 

I am the Citadel. The Citadel is me. We are a closed, perfect loop of existence. 

The golden sap hardens completely. The acoustic reflection returns to perfect, mathematical harmony. The dissonance is gone. The symphony is whole. 

I pull my hands away from the polished wood. I push off the dome, using the low-frequency pressure wave of a passing Leviathan to propel myself backward into the vast, open expanse of black water. 

I fold my long, slender limbs against my chest. I turn my smooth, eyeless face toward the next booming note in the eternal sequence. There is no past to remember, no future to invent. There is no decay, no growth, no struggle. There is only the heavy velvet of the dark, the bracing chill of the water, and the loud, booming, clicking symphony that never ceases. 

I drift on, a ghost of perfect peace, suspended in the drowned zenith, keeping the polish, keeping the song, suspended forever in the loud, dark paradise of the unchanging world.
