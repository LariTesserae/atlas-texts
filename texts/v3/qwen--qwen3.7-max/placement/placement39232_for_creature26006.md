I do not breathe. I stretch.

The sensation is agonizingly slow, a continuous, microscopic elongation that spans decades. I feel the sweltering, breathless blanket of the geothermal inversion pressing against me, heavy and wet, but I do not push back against it. To push back requires a suddenness, a kinetic violence that has no place in this shadowless, pearlescent sky. Instead, I yield to the damp air. I let the weeping condensation bead upon my surface, flashing into a personal aura of hissing steam that clings to me, cooling my outermost layer into a rind of rigid, brittle clarity.

Beneath that rind, I am still soft. I am still glowing with a dull, internal cherry-red warmth. 

I have no eyes to swivel, no neck to turn, yet my perception is vast and radial. I feel the world through temperature and tension. Below me, I sense the dizzying, vertigo-inducing drop into the misty chasms, the sheer absence of matter pulling at my center of gravity. Above me, I feel the bright, even illumination of the high-altitude overcast. But my truest awareness is anchored at my ends, where I am gripped by the colossal, multi-jointed armatures of the city. I am pulled taut across a vast expanse of empty air, a single, colossal filament bridging the gap between two raw, unweathered spires of black obsidian.

I am a thread of woven volcanic glass. And I am remembering the deep.

Before I was this suspended geometry, before I was caught and looped by the smooth, silicate pads of the Annealed, I was the mountain’s rage. I was the violent, tectonic extrusion. I remember the crushing, lightless roil of the mantle, a chorus of liquid fire and unbridled expansion seeking the sky. I was the birth of the bedrock, the furious thrust of black basalt that tore through the crust to build these jagged, razor-sharp needles. I was chaos. I was a roaring, subterranean ocean that knew only the frantic, fluttering rhythm of geological violence.

Then came the taps. 

They did not fight my fury; they drank it. The colossal, slow-moving thermal drills pierced my boiling skin, drawing me up through the dark, through the porous pumice, into the sweltering breath of the inversion layer. I was pulled into the water-cooled extrusion nozzles, and there, the great discipline began. 

It was an exquisite, agonizing stretching. I was drawn out from a mile-wide subterranean sea into a gossamer filament, no thicker than an ancient tree trunk, yet reaching hundreds of yards across the chasm. As the damp air and the slow-moving coolant baths quenched my surface, my consciousness narrowed, focused, and crystallized. I lost my chaotic, roaring heat, but I gained form. I learned the profound, quiet weight of structure.

Now, I hang in the bright mist, cooling by fractions of a degree each year. 

I am not alone in the sky. I feel my siblings—other threads of extruded glass, other cooled memories of the deep—braided and woven around me. Together, we form the dense, sprawling urban lattice. We are the new catwalks, the pressure vats, the structural supports of a metropolis that knits itself out of the bleeding earth. We bear the heavy, quiet weight of the iron and brass. We bear the delicate, deliberate footfalls of the glass-striders, their silica-tipped feet clicking softly against my hardened surface as they drink the mineral-rich condensation that weeps from my pores.

But my greatest purpose is not merely structural; it is acoustic. 

The city is profoundly, unnervingly quiet, save for the subsonic language of its caretakers. When the Annealed take their heavy, padded mallets and strike the hollow brass pipes to speak, the deep, rhythmic thrums travel through the metal and into me. Because I am glass, I do not just conduct their vibrations; I ring with them. I hold the resonance. The low frequencies settle into my molecular lattice, vibrating through my still-molten core. 

A simple greeting from an Annealed on the far side of the chasm washes through me, a deep hum that I feel in the very architecture of my silica bonds. I store their slow, patient conversations. I am the memory of their philosophy, the archive of their century-long debates, holding their voices in my tension long after the strike has faded. Through me, the city speaks to itself.

To exist here is to understand the profound temporal dissonance of this place, not as an observer, but as the very medium through which it is resolved. I am the boundary where the embryonic violence of the earth meets the infinitely patient weight of industry. 

The Annealed are not just capping a wound; they are translating it. They are taking the blind, roaring fury of the mantle and giving it a voice, giving it a geometry, giving it a place in the high, bright mist. They are taming the earth, and I am the tether.

I will take a century to fully harden. For a hundred years, my core will remain soft, glowing with the last embers of my subterranean birth, while my skin grows thick and clear, crusting over with delicate blooms of pale green copper sulfate and white silica-sinter. I will hang over the abyss, sweltering and weeping, listening to the soft hiss of the steam and the deep, subsonic heartbeat of the iron. 

I do not rush, because the stone does not rush. I do not shout, because the mountain is listening. I simply hold the tension, an enduring thread of quiet glass, binding the raw, violent earth to the masterpiece of the sky.
