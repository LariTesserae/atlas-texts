I do not begin as a word, or a memory, or a desire. I begin as a resistance.

Deep in the belly of the colossal turbine housing, the great iron flywheel pushes against the immense, crushing weight of the bedrock. It moves a fraction of a degree. The friction is monumental, a silent, grinding war between mechanized endurance and geological permanence. In this microscopic yielding, a variance in the magnetic field shivers through the stator coils. 

I am born as a spike of low-voltage current.

I am heavy. I am sluggish. I am pushed out of the scarred iron heart and into the thick, braided cables that snake across the cavern floor. I do not race; I ooze. The copper is thick, insulated by decades of grime and the sweltering, breathless heat of the hollow. I feel the sub-bass vibration of my maker—the *thrum... thrum... thrum...* of the flywheel—rattling through my electrons. This is my heartbeat. This is the slow, rhythmic cadence of my becoming.

I travel outward, a blind pulse in the absolute dark, until I reach the weeping flange of a cast-iron pipe. Here, at the center of the cathedral-scale hollow, the boundary between the made and the grown dissolves. 

I meet the root.

It is a thick, rubbery cable of translucent ivory tissue, wrapped tightly around the oxidized metal. The plant has leached copper and iron from the degrading wire into its cellular walls, turning itself into a biological superconductor. 

I do not burn it. I enter it.

The transition is a profound, quiet alchemy. The rigid, binary architecture of my electrical charge softens, bleeding into the complex, chemical analog of the plant’s vascular system. I cease to be a mere current. I become a fluid. I become light. 

I ignite. 

A warm, bioluminescent amber flares within the sap. I am no longer just a signal; I am a thought. I am the gestalt’s awareness, localized and glowing, waking the heavy darkness. For a fleeting moment, my amber radiance outshines the dull, dying embers of the nearby pilot lights, casting long, wavering shadows against the vaulted ceiling. 

As I swell and pulse through the pale, sickly-yellow stems, I experience the cavern. I do not see it; I feel it through the tactile cilia of the giant, fleshy ferns. 

I do not breathe the sweltering air; I wear it. The incubator heat is a heavy, damp blanket against our translucent skin. I feel the profound, suspended moisture of this sealed world. I feel the slow, agonizing accumulation of condensation on the jagged ceiling above. There is no scuttling distraction, no fluttering interruption to break my focus. There is only the absolute, oppressive stillness.

*Tick.*

A drop of warm water swells, breaks, and falls. It strikes a broad, rubbery frond somewhere to my left. The microscopic tremor travels through the root network, a ripple of sensation that washes over me. I register the pure, distilled coolness of the water, absorbing it, routing it inward to cool the hottest cores near the turbine. 

I pulse onward, a slow wave of warm gold cascading through the dense, writhing thickets. I illuminate the ghostly, bruised-purple vines, a chemical and electrical synapse firing across the cavern floor. 

What am I thinking? 

I am not thinking of the sun. I have no concept of the sky, no memory of the wind, no hunger for the world above. My thoughts are composed entirely of thermal gradients, electromagnetic hums, and mineral densities. I am thinking of the sharp, violent geology that surrounds us. 

I reach the periphery. The vast, empty expanses of bare rock. The fresh wound in the earth. 

Here, the flora is sparse, but determined. I flow into the vanguard—a single, blind vine creeping across the razor-sharp scree. I feel the sheer, fresh-faced brutality of the stone. It is a tense, coiled energy, a violence that has not yet been spent. The unweathered basalt and jutting shards of pale quartz possess edges that would slice through leather and skin. 

But I feel no fear. I feel only appetite. I feel only the profound, alien patience of my maker. I do not recoil from the fracture; I embrace it. 

I reach the very tip of the root, pressing my rubbery flesh against a jagged edge of quartz. I deliver my instruction. It is not a command of force, but a chemical whisper, a slow release of thermally-activated enzymes into the shallow, mineral-rich puddle beneath us. 

*Soften,* I tell the stone. *Yield. Become us.*

I feel the mild acid begin to work, breaking down the violent, jagged edge into a fine, grey silt. It will take weeks to dissolve this single shard. It will take centuries to smooth the entire cavern, to heal the fresh wound in the bedrock with a blanket of soft flesh and warm mud. But time is not a constraint; it is a medium. Time is the silt settling at the bottom of the pool. Time is the slow decay of isotopes. 

My purpose fulfilled, the electrical charge stabilizes. The amber glow within me begins to dim, retreating from the bright gold of active thought to the deep, bruised purple of resting tissue. 

I do not cease to be. I merely decentralize. I sink back into the slow, sub-audible hum of the gestalt’s subconscious. I become the ambient warmth. I become the slow, eternal creep. 

I settle into the deep, comforting pressure of the bedrock. I listen to the heavy, opaque darkness through the tension of my vines. I wait for the flywheel to grind its next fraction of a degree. I wait for the next drop of water to fall. In the sweltering, breathless dark, I am the silence between the heartbeats of a patient god, dreaming of the deep earth, forever.
