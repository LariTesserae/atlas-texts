The air is thin, but my chest is deep. I draw it in, a slow, expansive pull that fills the massive caverns of my lungs. I hold it. The highland mist curls around my face, cool and heavy, carrying the scent of wet dust and crushed lichen. I exhale.

*Thud.*

My heart beats. A heavy, resonant strike against my ribs. Then, a long, luxurious pause. I wait in the quiet. The rock weeps. A drop of condensation swells on the ceiling above, detaches, and shatters against the stone floor. 

*Thud.*

Forty beats a minute. The metronome of deep time. 

I do not feel the biting chill of the upper atmosphere. Beneath my pale, ash-mottled skin, a thick mantle of lipid tissue holds my warmth, giving my limbs a smooth, rounded contour. The perpetual dampness of the metropolis cannot reach my bones. As I stand on the bowed, water-polished steps of the lower canyon, a bead of freezing condensation rolls down my shoulder. It does not soak in; it slides off my hydrophobic skin, leaving a dry trail in its wake before joining the thin rill of water at my feet.

Through the thick, sensitive calluses of my splayed feet, I am tethered to the nervous system of the mountain. The stone beneath me is not dead; it is vibrating with the flow. I can feel the heavy, rushing pulse of the primary aqueduct three terraces below. I can feel the delicate, capillary trickle of a weeping fissure to my left. I shift my weight, and my gripping pads seal against the wet rock with a soft, leathery *scuff*—the only sound I make.

My hands hang at my sides, heavy and purposeful. My fingers are long, multi-jointed, ending not in thin nails, but in thick, flat spades of dense keratin. They are worn smooth at the edges, dulled by centuries of scraping frost-fractured granite. I am a Revealer. It is the only identity our language holds. 

I approach the cliff face. The rock here is tight-grained, stubborn. I reach into the woven grass pouch at my hip and pull out a handful of wet, mineral-heavy earth. With precise, deliberate movements, I pack the soil deep into a natural fissure. I do not force it; I coax it. I smooth the surface with my keratin spades, ensuring the seal is perfect. Tonight, the highland temperature will plummet. The moisture in the earth will freeze, expand, and fracture the stone. Tomorrow, we will lever it away with wooden wedges. We are not building a city; we are unearthing it. Where an outsider might look upon this staggering density of terraces and plazas and see a constructed metropolis, I see only the mountain, finally shedding its excess weight. We are removing the unnecessary to let the stone breathe.

A shadow shifts in the peripheral mist. One of my kin moves beside me. Their skin is a slightly deeper shade of wet-stone grey, their chest rising and falling in the same slow, glacial rhythm as mine. There are no words here. The acoustics of the porous stone would swallow them anyway. 

They step close, and we press our palms flat against each other’s chests. 

I feel their heart. *Thud.* Perfectly synchronized with my own. Through the contact, a subtle pheromonal shift reaches me—a scent like bruised alpine grass and the sharp tang of cold ozone. It carries a complex, layered intent: *The frost will be heavy tonight. The upper terraces are weeping too fast. We must clear the secondary runnels before we sleep.*

I reply with a micro-expression, a slight softening of my eyes, and a gentle tapering of my own pheromones to signal agreement. We release each other, our hands sliding apart with a soft whisper of wet skin. We move together in a fluid, synchronized choreography, anticipating each other's steps, bound by a hive-like empathy forged in the quiet dark.

Before we retreat, I pause at a shallow, terraced pool. The water is freezing, clear as glass. I wade in, the icy liquid parting around my hydrophobic skin. With a finely woven net of tough alpine grass, I sift the shallows, catching a few of the pale, translucent amphipods that dart through the shadows. I consume them raw, a small, efficient burst of calories to sustain my slow metabolism. They taste of cold stone and pure water.

As the pale daylight begins to fail, bruising into a deep violet through the fog, we retreat from the exposed escarpment. We move deeper into the honeycombed dwellings, navigating the corridors not by sight, but by touch. 

My long fingers trail along the walls, reading the subtle grooves, the polished curves, the deliberate pits left by generations of my ancestors. Here, a sweeping, continuous arc denotes a great flood from three millennia ago. There, a series of sharp, staccato depressions records a season of bitter, unyielding cold. This is our history, our literature, written in the subtracted rock. It is meant only for the dark, to be read by the fingertips when the eyes are useless. I trace a particularly deep groove, feeling the phantom pressure of the hands that shaped it, a silent conversation across the centuries.

I enter my private alcove. It is small, perfectly contoured to my body, worn smooth by centuries of my resting. I curl into the dry, shadowed space, pulling my knees to my chest. Outside, the wind begins its low, distant sigh, and the rhythmic dripping of the cisterns accelerates as the evening squall arrives.

I am old. I can feel it in the thinning of my lipid layer, in the brittle edges of my keratin spades, in the way my joints ache when the barometric pressure drops. My time in the deep alcoves is drawing to a close. 

I know what comes next. When my heart finally settles into its last, long pause and does not strike again, my kin will not bury me in the hard rock. They will carry me up the spiraling staircases, higher and higher, to the crown of the plateau. They will lay me in the rushing, freezing water of the highest aqueduct. 

I do not fear this. I look forward to it. I look forward to the moment the relentless flow and the microscopic aquatic life break me down. I will become the sediment that packs the fissures to crack the stone. I will become the nutrients that feed the pale green lichens on the sunlit terraces. I will return to the hydrological cycle. I will become the very mechanism of the Great Subtraction.

As sleep pulls me under, my heartbeat slows even further, blending with the rhythm of the dripping water. 

*Thud.* The mountain breathes in the mist. 

*Thud.* The water carves the dark. 

I am a Revealer of the stone, a caretaker of the flow, a silent note in the eternal, wet chord of the high world. And in the morning, when the fog clears and the pale light touches the sweeping terraces, I will wake. We will step out into the damp air, and together, we will remove the world again.
