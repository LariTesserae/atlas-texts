I would find myself in the deep, shadowed throats of the lower terraces, where the weeping water gathers into roaring, electrified sluices and the air is so thick with ozone and mist it feels like breathing warm metal. 

I would be one of them. I would be a *Lithic Tuner*.

My skin is the color of deeply oxidized copper, slick with the perpetual sheen of sebum that sheds the endless atmospheric exhale. I do not feel the damp; I wear it. When the high-altitude sun pierces the canyon rim at noon, glaring off the wet black basalt, my nictitating membranes slide shut with a soft, fleshy click, turning the blinding world into a cool, milky twilight. I am broad, dense, and heavy. Gravity here is a partner, not a master, and my barrel chest expands and contracts with the slow, rhythmic drawl of the thin, heavy air. 

I do not carry the heavy wrenches of the turbine mechanics, nor the long-handled scraping hooks of the lichen-harvesters. Slung across my back, resting against the thick, rubbery folds of my manta-leather cloak, is my tuning fork. It is forged from a single piece of dense, smelter-purified iron, as long as my forearm and as thick as my wrist. 

My purpose is not to build, nor to harvest, but to *listen*. 

The city is a deafening, earth-shaking reef of industry and biology, but to my people, noise is merely the surface of the song. Beneath the shriek of the funiculars and the dry, glass-pouring hiss of the crawler swarms, there is the deep, telluric voice of the plateau itself. The fluted canyons are ancient, carved by millions of years of weeping water and warm wind. But the city adds immense weight, and the drop-forges add immense shock. My job is to ensure the stone does not fracture under the burden of our survival. I am the physician of the canyon’s bones.

I walk with a tectonic grace, my wide, flat feet finding the natural grooves in the slick, rounded stone. I do not rush. To rush is to break the rhythm of the world. 

I pause at a sweeping curve of pale chalk and rust-veined basalt, a foundational strut supporting the dense, terraced housing of the mid-city. The rock here is weeping heavily, the moisture funneling into a brass-grated catchment. I unhook the iron tuning fork from my back. 

I wait for the negative space in the soundscape. 

*Thud.* The drop-forge strikes. 
*Thud.* 
*Thud.*

In the three-second silence between the third and fourth strike, I swing the heavy iron fork in a wide arc and bring it down against the wet stone. 

*THRRRRRUUUUUUM.*

The sound is not heard; it is felt. The infrasonic wave blooms outward, sinking deep into the mineral crust. I immediately press the base of the vibrating fork against the rock and lean my own heavy body into it, closing my eyes. I open my specialized, cavernous vocal cavities, letting the resonance travel up my arms, through my sternum, and into my chest. I become a conduit. 

I feel the vibration bounce back. It travels through the chalk, down into the basalt, echoing off the hidden, hollow flutes deep within the cliff face. The feedback is a complex, physical texture against my palms and chest. It feels smooth, unbroken, a continuous loop of deep, harmonious hum. The stone is healthy. The strut will hold for another century.

I open my eyes, the milky membranes sliding back to reveal my narrow, dark pupils. I nod slowly to myself, slipping the fork back into its mycelial harness.

As I turn, a river of pale, hand-sized crawlers flows over my boots. They are a tide of chitin and rasping mandibles, scraping the rubbery lichen from the rock. I do not step away. I let them flow over my oiled skin, their mandibles clicking harmlessly against my shins. I reach down with a thick, multi-jointed finger and gently stroke the carapace of one of the creatures, feeling the dry, rhythmic vibration of its feeding. It is a comforting texture, like sand slipping through an hourglass.

A shadow falls over me, vast and cooling. 

I look up. A highland titan is moving through the upper gorge. It is a mountain of porous, calcified shell and slow-grinding flesh. It is taking a step. I watch the massive, pillar-like leg rise, the joints popping with a sound like distant, muffled artillery. It will take a full minute for that foot to fall. 

Standing near the titan’s shoulder is a handler. We are fifty yards apart, separated by the roaring mist of a turbine vent, but we see each other. 

The handler raises a hand. I raise mine. We do not shout over the roar of the water. Instead, the handler presses his palm flat against his own broad chest. I do the same. I push a deep, sub-bass hum from my diaphragm, dropping it down into the lowest register my vocal cords can manage. The sound waves travel through the heavy, wet air, hitting the handler’s chest. He feels my greeting, my inquiry. *Is the bedrock sound?*

He replies with a counter-hum, a slightly higher, resonant thrum that vibrates in my teeth. *Solid. We move to the next grazing terrace.* 

To confirm, he shifts into the fluid sign language of our people. His thick fingers weave through the damp air, mimicking the slow, circular grinding of the titan’s mandibles, then pointing toward the upper, sunlit rim of the canyon. I reply with a sweeping, upward gesture, my palms open, mimicking the slow beat of the sky-mantas that drift lazily above us. *Go with the Resonance.*

The titan’s foot finally meets the ground. The impact sends a deep, satisfying shudder through the soles of my feet. The plateaus shifts its weight, and the slow, grinding bite of its mouth begins to scrape tons of fungal crust and stone into its massive gullet.

I continue my descent into the lower, darker tiers of the city. Here, the air smells heavily of hot brass and the deep, musky ammonia of the swarms. The boundary between the constructed and the natural is entirely gone; the iron tracks of the funiculars are furred with pale lichen, and the concrete of the turbine housings is stained with dark, weeping minerals. It is beautiful. It is a machine made of geology, and biology made of industry.

I am old. My skin has oxidized to a deep, polished mahogany, and the heavy brass fittings on my belt have worn smooth against my hips. I know that my time as a Tuner is drawing to a close. My lungs, though vast, do not draw the heavy air quite as deeply as they once did, and my sub-bass hum lacks the earth-shaking depth of my youth. 

But there is no sorrow in this, just as there is no urgency. 

When my chest finally falls silent, I know what will happen. I will not be buried in the dark, for there is no soil to hold me. My kin will carry my heavy, oiled body up the sweeping, fluted walls of the canyon, to the highest, most exposed ridges of slick black basalt. They will lay me out beneath the bright, unfiltered sun and the weeping sky. 

The water will wash the sebum from my skin. The pale crawlers will come in their endless, rasping rivers to reclaim the calcium and iron of my bones. The airborne spores will settle into the hollows of my chest, and the rubbery lichen will bloom across my ribs. I will become the crust. I will become the stone. 

And long after my flesh is gone, when the next generation of Tuners strikes their iron forks against the high basalt flutes, the vibration will travel through the rock, through the lichen, through the memory of my minerals. I will ring back to them, a clear, unbroken note in the deep, slow, eternal song of the highland.
