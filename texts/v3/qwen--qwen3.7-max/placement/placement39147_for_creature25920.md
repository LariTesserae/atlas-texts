To find me, you must first close your eyes and let the deafening roar of the air fade into the background. You must stop listening to the sky, and start listening to the floor.

I am not in the high, sweeping fronds of the Vitreous Weepers, nor am I climbing the rubbed, ancient steps to adjust the mournful bone horns. The young ones do that. The light ones, whose pumice bones are still full of air, who leap through the golden-brown haze like silver-backed ghosts, their glass-grass cloaks chiming in perfect acoustic camouflage. 

I am at the bottom. In the deepest, narrowest pinch of the slot-canyon, where the curving walls of dark basalt almost touch, and the twilight is thick enough to taste. 

I am pressed flat against the stone, my palms and the soles of my feet splayed wide, my jaw locked firmly against the smooth, melted contour of the rock. I am a Listener, but I am an old one. In the Resonant Throat, we do not age by the wrinkling of our poreless, clay-colored skin, nor by the greying of our hair—we have none. We age by the filling of our bones. 

When we are young, our skeletons are porous, light as pumice, allowing us to dance through the heavy air and endure the concussive shockwaves of the resonance beetles. But as the decades pass, the microscopic silica dust we breathe, the gallium we wear, and the deep vibrations we absorb begin to crystallize within us. My bones are no longer light. They are heavy, veined with threads of quartz and obsidian. I am slowly turning into the canyon itself. I move with a slow, deliberate gravity, my silver-coated limbs dragging through the powdery snow of discarded insect husks.

The society of the Listeners is built on the management of the air, but someone must manage the earth. The high horns filter the shrieking treble of the Galvanic Swarms, protecting the upper walls from shattering. But the true danger of the Throat lies in the bass. The deep, sub-audible thrum of a million beetle wings, compounded by the geothermal groans of the deep earth, creates standing waves in the bedrock. If left unchecked, these low frequencies will find the natural resonant frequency of the basalt, and the entire spine of the world will crack and collapse into the abyss.

I am a Fault-Watcher. A Keeper of the Deep Bass.

The air around me is a maelstrom. A tornado of piezoelectric moths sweeps through the corridor, bathing my silver skin in frantic, golden strobes of static electricity. The liquid gallium on my skin ripples, absorbing the charge, grounding it safely into the floor. I do not flinch. My focus is entirely on the jawbone pressed to the rock.

Through the stone, I feel the canyon's heartbeat. It is a complex, polyrhythmic pulse. I feel the slow, days-long sway of the Vitreous Weeper rooted fifty yards away, its liquid gallium sap shifting in a heavy, thermal tide. I feel the frantic, skittering scratches of the crystalline locusts grinding tungsten-carbide mandibles against the glass-grass. 

But beneath it all, I feel the flaw.

A micro-fracture, deep within the left wall of the canyon, where the pale pumice meets the dark basalt. It is singing. A low, dangerous, oscillating hum that is slowly amplifying. A swarm of resonance beetles has gathered near the ceiling, their hollow basalt carapaces thrumming in perfect, destructive unison. They are inadvertently tuning themselves to the fracture.

The high horns cannot fix this. The young ones up above are adjusting the bone baffles, blowing deep, mournful notes into the air, but the air is not the problem. The stone is.

I unpeel myself from the wall. The sudden lack of vibration is a physical ache, a sudden silence that makes my inner ear spin. I reach into the deep folds of my cloak and withdraw my tool.

It is not a small, chipped obsidian fork used for conversation. It is a massive, two-pronged tuning fork ground from a single, flawless monolith of hyper-dense, piezoelectric quartz, as tall as I am and heavy as a boulder. It is the Deep Fork, shaped not by fire or forge, but by generations of patient abrasion with gallium-laced sand. It takes all my remaining, calcified strength to lift it.

I do not strike it against the wall; that would shatter the fragile pumice layer. Instead, I drag it to the very center of the canyon floor, where the dark basalt is exposed, worn smooth by eons of wind. I position the base of the fork against the exact epicenter of the standing wave.

I brace my heavy, quartz-veined legs. I raise the heavy mallet, carved from the dense, polished femur of an ancient, long-dead beast and wrapped in woven silica-fiber.

Above me, the beetle swarm reaches its crescendo. The air pressure drops, the sensory pads on my jaw scream with the phantom pressure. The golden dust in the air forms tight, concentric rings. The basalt wall groans, a sound felt rather than heard, a terrifying vibration that threatens to unzip the ancient equilibrium of the Throat.

I bring the mallet down.

The strike is not loud. In the air, it is merely a dull, heavy *thud*. But in the earth, it is a cataclysm of pure, geometric order.

The massive quartz fork vibrates. It does not sing; it *commands*. It sends a massive, counter-phase shockwave down into the bedrock. I feel it travel through the soles of my feet, up my heavy shins, and into my chest. It is a wave of pure anti-resonance, precisely calculated by generations of Fault-Watchers to perfectly invert the frequency of the fracturing stone.

The shockwave hits the micro-fracture. The destructive hum stutters. The standing wave collapses. The dangerous oscillation within the basalt is neutralized, absorbed, and dispersed into the deep, harmless warmth of the geothermal roots.

The wall settles. The canyon holds.

Above, the beetle swarm breaks its formation, confused by the sudden loss of their acoustic feedback loop. They scatter into the golden haze, their frantic treble returning to a chaotic, harmless din. The young Listeners up in the alcoves feel the shift in the stone and lower their horns, their work done for another cycle.

I drop the mallet. My arms tremble, the liquid gallium on my skin rippling with the exertion. I am exhausted. The effort of wielding the Deep Fork has accelerated the calcification within me. I can feel the silica settling deeper into my marrow, turning my pumice bones to solid, unyielding stone. I am heavier now. Slower.

I walk back to the smooth, curving wall, my feet dragging slightly through the powdery chitin-snow. I find my alcove, the worn depression in the rock shaped by my own body over decades of vigil. 

I press my palms flat against the basalt. I lock my jaw against the stone. I close my eyes to the bruised, violet sky and the dim, golden twilight. 

I let the chaotic, beautiful, deafening roar of the Resonant Throat wash over me, filtering it out, diving past the frantic swarms, past the swaying silica-trees, down into the deep, slow, eternal hum of the earth. 

I am the anchor. I am the silence beneath the song. And as my bones slowly turn to stone, I know that one day, I will not just listen to the canyon. I will be the canyon, holding the breath of the world forever.
