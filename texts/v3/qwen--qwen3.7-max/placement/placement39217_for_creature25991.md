My palms are pressed flat against the pale, undulating wall, feeling for the memory of the ice. 

High up on the western curve of the cylinder, just fifty feet below the blinding rim, the stone retains the fierce heat of the high-altitude sun. It radiates through the thick, waxy calluses of my hands. I do not sweat; my skin, taut and sheened like the jade-green rosettes far below, merely flushes with a deep, vascular warmth, venting the excess into the rising thermal draft. 

I am a Shaper of Echoes. A Tuner of the Bowl.

In my right hand, I hold a mallet of dense, taproot hardwood, polished smooth by the oils of my grip. In my left, a wedge carved from the mandible of an elder hooked-beak bird. The rock beneath my hands is unfathomably old, ground down by eons of wind into this perfect, polished sinkhole. But it is not dead. It holds the compressed, geological tension of the primordial mountains it once was. To carve a dwelling is to hollow out space; to carve a *resonant node* is to give that space a voice.

I tap the wall lightly with the mallet. *Tink.*

Too shallow. The grain here is tight, compressed by a million years of aeolian scouring. I slide my hand down, feeling the micro-undulations, searching for the softer matrix, the fossilized breath of the ancient earth. 

There. A slight give.

I set the bone wedge. I strike. 

*Thrummm.*

A deep, resonant vibration blossoms from the impact, traveling through the stone, up my arms, and into my chest. It is a perfect, low note, the foundational pitch of the upper canopy. 

The pit is screaming around me. It is the hour of the late afternoon zenith, and the air is thick with the heavy, droning buzz of the sparrow-sized pollinators. Their iridescent shells clatter against the waxy leaves of the rubbery trees below. A flock of darting birds trills sharply, the sound bouncing off the acoustic-bowl walls in a dizzying, overlapping cacophony. The sheer volume of life is a physical weight pressing against my eardrums.

I pause, close my eyes, and flex the muscles at the base of my skull. 

With a practiced, muscular contraction, my outer ears collapse. The sleek, pointed pinnae fold tight, sealing the canals with a soft, fleshy finality. The roaring ecology vanishes, replaced by a profound, muffled peace. I am plunged into the tactile world. I no longer hear the pit; I *feel* it. I feel the rhythmic clicking of the sleek climbing mammals scrambling up the vines. I feel the subtle sway of the braided-vine ladder beneath my bare feet. I feel the deep, slow pulse of the thermal inversion holding the warm air in place. 

In this silence, I can feel the exact decay of the stone's resonance beneath my palm. It lasts for four heartbeats. Perfect.

I open my ears just a slit, allowing the pure tone of the rock to filter in while dampening the biological roar. Three hundred feet across the cylinder, and two hundred feet down near the basin floor, my apprentice, Kael, is working the lower nodes. The air down there is heavier, thick with the dry mineral dust and the sharp, pine-like scent of crushed leaves. The acoustics are entirely different.

I raise my mallet and strike the new node in a specific, staccato rhythm: *Clack... clack-clack... thrum.* 

*Are you catching the shift? The updraft is slowing.*

I watch the far wall. The polished stone acts as a mirror for sound. A moment later, the sharp, melodic reply bounces back to me, perfectly focused by the parabolic curve of the pit. 

*Clack-tink... clack.*

*The dust is high today. The lower notes are dampened. I am carving deeper.*

I smile, my large, amber eyes catching the fading light, the tapetum lucidum flaring briefly in the dimness. We are tuning the twilight shift. As the temperature differential between the high plains and the pit floor equalizes, the air density changes. The pit’s voice drops half an octave. If we do not adjust the nodes, the evening messages—the calls of mothers, the signals of the water-harvesters, the quiet rhythms of lovers—will scatter and die in the mid-air turbulence. We are the custodians of the city's long-distance intimacy.

Above me, the blinding white aperture begins to bruise. The high-altitude sun dips below the ancient roof of the world, and the sky turns a deep, velvety purple, then absolute, starry black. 

The temperature in the pit stabilizes. The giant insects slow their frantic foraging, their droning dropping to a low, sleepy hum as they tuck themselves into the waxy folds of the canopy. The climbing mammals curl into the hollows of the rock. 

Down in the layered web of the city, the resin lamps are lit. 

From my vantage point, the pit transforms. Hundreds of carved doorways spill warm, amber light into the twilight. The glow reflects off the polished walls, the silver undersides of the hanging vines, and the snapping, earth-toned banners. It is a glowing honeycomb suspended in the dark. The smell of warm dust and sweet, smokeless sap rises on the final, dying breath of the updraft.

My work is done. I pack my bone wedges into the woven pouch at my hip and begin the descent. I do not use the ladders for the first few drops; I slide down the smooth, carved staircases that follow the natural undulations of the wall, my center of gravity low, my movements flowing with the deliberate grace of the mammals. My waxy skin sheds the fine layer of pale stone dust, it beading and rolling off me like water.

I reach my dwelling—a protruding barnacle of stone and thatched fronds near the mid-canopy. Inside, the air is deeply warm, heated by the thermal mass of the rock. I light my own small lamp, the amber glow illuminating the intricate, spiraling patterns I have etched into the lintel, inlaid with crushed malachite and lapis bound in clear resin. 

I sit by the open archway, looking out over the contained, fiercely loved microcosm. 

The vast, empty void of the high plains above is terrifying in its openness, a place where the wind steals your moisture and the sun burns your skin. But here, in this claustrophobic density, in this screaming, lush, dry jungle, there is no waste. Every drop of moisture is hoarded, every husk is woven, every dead ancestor resting in the crunchy basin is drawn up through the deep taproots to shade us. We are held.

A mason on the eastern wall taps a rhythmic, resonant beat on a stone wall. The day is done. 

Across the cylinder, hands flash in the dim light, signing the quiet satisfaction of survival in the fluid, rustling gestures of The Weave. The pit hums with the residual energy of a million living things. 

I close my ear-slits, sealing out the noise. I close my golden eyes. I breathe in the scent of pine-resin and warm dust, and I sleep in the warm, beating heart of the earth.
