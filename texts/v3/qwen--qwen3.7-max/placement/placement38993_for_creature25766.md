The heavy, wet wool of my coat slides from my shoulders, pulled away by hands that feel like polished river stones. Without it, the aggressive cold does not bite; it embraces. It is a solvent, just as the blinding light is a solvent, washing away the frantic, noisy residue of the surface world. I sit on the bowed threshold, the stone dipped by millennia of passing feet, and I wait for the end of my time.

The Custodian before me tilts its head. Its milky quartz eyes catch the funneled noon, flashing a brief, complex sequence of light against the soapstone wall behind me. *Acceptance. Integration. Balance.*

Then, the collar of living emerald moss is draped over my neck.

At first, it is merely cold and damp. But as my heartbeat begins to stutter and slow—the silence between the pulses stretching, widening, until the rhythm of my blood matches the imperceptible shifting of the bedrock—the moss wakes. It recognizes the chaotic, roaring furnace of my mammalian biology, and it feeds. The microscopic roots do not just press against my skin; they slip beneath it. They thread through the pores of my collarbones, winding around my carotid artery, diving deep into the frantic musculature of my chest. There is no pain, only a profound, heavy lethargy, a sensation of being gently pulled downward into the deep, quiet earth.

I close my eyes, but the shadowless, milky glare burns through my eyelids. So, I simply stop using them.

As the last of my body heat leaches into the freezing damp, my form begins to surrender to the equation of the pocket. I realize, with a distant and fading human logic, that I am too dense with time, too heavy with the entropy of the surface world, to simply become another pale, walking Custodian. A single body could not balance the sheer volume of chaotic years I brought into the hollow. The environment requires a broader distribution. 

The roots thicken, splitting my skin, replacing my veins with pale, fibrous sap. My bones calcify, not into soapstone, but into the dense, unyielding grain of shaped petrified wood. I am not standing up to join their glacial choreography. I am sinking. I am melting into the masonry.

I find myself no longer sitting upon the threshold, but woven entirely through it.

My consciousness does not fade; it shatters and expands, rushing outward through the microscopic fissures of the micro-city. I am no longer a singular entity occupying a point in space. I am the frost-cured root system, the vast, hidden, tensile lattice that binds the sunken city together. I am in the carved wooden lintels polishing under the touch of passing hands; I am in the tiny, bridging aqueducts spanning the narrow alleys; I am deep within the mortar-less joints of the pale soapstone walls. I am the unseen, subterranean nervous system of the pocket.

In this dispersed, woody state, the profound silence of the hollow transforms. It is no longer an absence of sound pressing against my eardrums; it is a physical medium, a dense ocean through which I feel the entire ecosystem.

I feel the Custodians. When they press their long, multi-jointed fingers against the stone to speak, I do not just feel the vibration—I *am* the vibration. Their subsonic, rhythmic language travels through my petrified fibers like a warm current. I feel the bright, high-speed flares of their quartz eyes as a subtle shifting of weight in my uppermost branches. I know their thoughts, their meticulous calculations of balance, not as words, but as structural stresses and releases within my own body.

I feel the fauna. The glass-winged beetles and the long-legged spiders do not just walk upon the city; they walk upon *me*. I feel the microscopic, feather-light tap of a pale weasel’s paws on a mossy roof, a sensation as delicate as a dusting of snow. I feel the translucent moths resting on the ferns, their tiny weights registering as slight, comforting pressures on my leaves.

And I feel the water. The heavy, swollen beads of condensation weeping from the selenite walls are held in place by my tension. I am the surface tension. I am the grip that keeps the water from falling, the invisible force locking the droplets in their trembling, eternal suspension. When a Custodian gently guides a droplet into a patch of moss, I feel the release of that tension, followed instantly by the swelling of the moss against my roots, and the simultaneous, balancing formation of a new droplet on the quartz above.

I am the counterweight to the dynamic stasis. The Custodians are the active agents, the gears moving to maintain the picture, but I am the canvas, the frame, and the nails holding it to the wall. I am the structural memory of the fissure. The chaotic, eroding time I brought with me from the surface has been absorbed, distributed, and locked away within my petrified grain, providing the ballast needed to keep this hidden pocket frozen for another millennium.

Here, in the blinding, shadowless light, I am the dark, quiet tension beneath the stone. I do not move, yet I hold everything in place. I am the held breath of the earth, the silent, unyielding grip of the roots, existing in a brilliant, eternal quiet, asking for nothing, changing nothing, holding the world perfectly, beautifully still.
