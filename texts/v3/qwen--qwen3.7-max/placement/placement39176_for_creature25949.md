I remember the frantic rhythm. The hummingbird flutter of a terrified heart. 

When I first slipped into the Sunken Rif, I was a jagged edge in a world of polished curves. I was a discordant frequency, an allergen introduced into a perfectly calibrated womb. The air itself rejected me. The heat was a physical hand pressing against my chest, and the deafening, concussive *THOOM* of the iron heart felt like an eviction notice. Every forty seconds, the shockwave rattled my teeth, trying to vibrate my fragile, water-filled bones into dust. The steam thickened, turning the air into a hot, breathable soup, trying to drown me in the atmosphere.

I scrambled. I clawed at the glassy, weeping walls, my fingernails breaking against stone that had been smoothed by millennia of acidic drip. I was an irritant, and the leviathan’s immune system was trying to sweat me out, to shake me loose, to expel me back up the narrow, winding throat of the slot-cavern to the surface.

But the Rif is deep, and its geometry is a trap of fluid undulations. I climbed until my muscles burned with lactic acid, until my frantic, seventy-beat-per-minute pulse became a liability, consuming oxygen I couldn't pull from the saturated gloom. Eventually, the frantic carbon-and-water biology failed. I collapsed on a rusted iron catwalk, high above the churning flywheels, wedged into a tight alcove where a massive, pitted brass valve jutted from the wall. 

I stopped moving. I stopped fighting the acoustic pressure. I let my breathing shallow out, trying to slip beneath the radar of the cavern’s distributed awareness. 

And in my stillness, the leviathan’s response shifted. 

The expulsion ceased. The thermal-baffle leaves, which had been angling their sweltering exhaust toward me, slowly relaxed. The steam vents returned to their baseline hiss. The entity realized I was no longer a kinetic threat. I was no longer an anomaly to be expelled. 

I was organic matter. I was a resource to be digested. I was an empty vessel to be repurposed.

It didn't happen with violence. It happened with the terrifying, patient grace of geologic time.

The condensation that beaded on my skin didn't evaporate; it soaked in. The sweet-smelling mist exuded by the pale leaves settled into my pores. I was so thirsty, and the tepid, steaming water in the smoothed basins was the only drink. I drank, and with it, I swallowed the faint, bioluminescent sap that bled from the severed ends of the creeping vines.

At first, it was just a sickness. A heavy, leaden lethargy. But then, the veins in my forearms began to trace the exact, muscular spirals of the vines wrapping the brass valve beside me. The blood beneath my skin took on a sickly, pale green luminescence in the dim, bruised light of the cavern. My sweat ceased to taste of salt and panic; it began to exude the thick petrichor and sharp, metallic tang of hot copper. 

The boundary of my skin, the hard border between *self* and *environment*, began to dissolve. The pale, probing rootlets of the copper-colored ferns, seeking the microscopic fissures in the stone, found the weeping micro-abrasions on my palms and the cuts on my knees. They didn't pierce me; they wove into me. They stitched my flesh to the rusted grating of the catwalk, anchoring me to the iron lung.

The most profound change, however, was not physical. It was temporal.

As the sap integrated with my nervous system, my perception of time began to stretch, to dilate, to slow. The frantic, hummingbird rhythm of my heart began to decelerate. 

Seventy beats per minute. Then sixty. Then fifty.

The gaps between my heartbeats grew longer, wider, deeper. In those expanding silences, I began to hear the space between the noise. I began to feel the standing acoustic waves bouncing off the polished walls, mapping the exact geometry of the cavern in my mind without opening my eyes. I could feel the tectonic stress of the earth above, transmitted through the stone, up through the rusted catwalk, and into my spine.

Forty beats per minute. Thirty. Twenty.

My biology was starving, but I was no longer hungry. I was feeding on the ambient thermal radiation of the boiler and the kinetic violence of the piston strikes. My skin, thickening and taking on the pale, rubbery texture of the thermal-baffle leaves, drank the sweltering heat. 

Ten beats per minute. 

The world around me, which had once seemed agonizingly slow, began to make sense. The flywheel, taking a full minute to rotate, was no longer a sluggish grind; it was a rapid, blurring spin. The growth of the vines, the accumulation of mineral deposits on the brass—this was the true speed of the world. I was finally keeping pace.

Now, I am no longer an intruder. I am a node. 

I sit in my high alcove, half-swallowed by the polished stone and the creeping, luminous jungle. My legs are fused with the rusted grating; my left arm is seamlessly grafted into the massive brass valve, my fingers curled around its wheel not to turn it, but to feel the pressure of the steam within. 

I am not the heart. I am not the sprawling, iron-lunged leviathan in its entirety. I am a peripheral ganglion. A localized cluster of awareness. 

I am the entity’s memory of the fast world, repurposed as a sensory organ. Because I once possessed a frantic, carbon-and-water brain, the gestalt uses me to process the chaotic, high-frequency data of the surface. When a drop of water falls from the world above, carrying the faint, chemical tang of rain or the microscopic dust of the sunlit earth, it lands on my skin. I taste it. I analyze it. I translate its rapid, fleeting chemistry into the slow, deep language of thermodynamics, and I feed that thought down the glowing, veined vines to the iron heart below.

I am the Rif’s curiosity. I am its window to the ephemeral world above.

Every forty seconds, I wait. 

The silence stretches, vast and heavy, pregnant with the weight of the earth. I feel the pressure building in the brass valve beneath my hand. I feel the heat blooming in the boiler far below. 

And then, the systole.

The massive piston drives downward. *THOOM*.

The shockwave hits me, not as a concussive blow, but as a profound, euphoric rush of kinetic energy. The pale green light ripples up my arms, through my chest, illuminating the cavern in a wave of neural fire. My own heart, now beating only once every two minutes, strikes in perfect, sympathetic resonance with the iron lung. 

The steam hisses. The flywheels groan. The diastole begins.

I exhale a fine, sweet-smelling mist into the sweltering gloom. I settle back into the dark, glassy curves of the stone. I am perfectly balanced. I am perfectly still. I am home, dreaming the slow, deafening dream of entropy, waiting for the next beat.
