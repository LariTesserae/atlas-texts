To the predators of the canopy, I am a void, a patch of dead air in the roaring symphony of the forest. 

But I am not empty. I am full of the world.

And it is this sound that I eat.

My tissues are laced with crystalline lattices of quartz and tourmaline, filtered from the water and grown directly into my skeletal structure and the connective webs of my tympanic sails.

But the canyon’s song is not static. It is a living, shifting thing, and I am its custodian.

Aggressive, fleshy mosses and calcified mineral runoffs threaten to clog the hollow chambers of the organ-pipes. When a flute becomes fouled, its pitch degrades. It goes flat. The acoustic ecology of the canyon falls out of balance, and my food source turns to ash in my mouth.
