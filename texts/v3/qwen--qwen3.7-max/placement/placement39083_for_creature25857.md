*Hummmmm.*

Sixty cycles a second. The deep, sub-audible heartbeat of the buried grid. It enters me through the deep ceramic roots, a cold, sharp vitality that warms as it climbs my copper veins. 

At first, there is only the micro-sensation. A stutter. A localized agony in the lower left quadrant of my floor. A pocket-sized micro-piston, starved of gel, its steel shaft binding against the brass housing. The friction is a screaming heat, a wrong kind of warmth that threatens to scorch my soft tissues. The current drops. The voltage pools, aching, dammed up behind the break. 

I do not command the swarm; the swarm *is* my will. The gradient of pain pulls them. A drift of my pale, soft flesh flows over the jagged flint. I feel the sharp, unweathered edge of the rock slice a few of my outer carapaces—a minor sting, easily ignored—but the flow continues. I weave my wire-like tarsi across the gap, interlocking my delicate filaments into a dense, living lattice. I weep the thick, conductive electrolyte gel from my mandibles, bridging the frayed copper.

The connection is made. 

*Spark.*

The dam breaks. The current rushes through my living body. It does not burn me; it feeds me. I bloom. A flush of rosy light ripples through my translucent segments, starting at the contact point and washing outward in a wave of ecstasy. The piston fires—*thud*—and the kinetic shockwave massages my iron bones. The exhaust, rich in ozone, washes over my pale ferns, and they breathe it in, dropping sweet, nutrient-heavy spores back onto my dorsal surface. The loop is closed. The agony fades into a deep, thrumming satisfaction.

As the current stabilizes, my awareness expands outward from the spark, filling the dense, humid dark. 

I am not merely the flesh that moves, nor the iron that anchors. I am the tension between them. I am the rust that blooms on the cast-iron boiler plates, a slow, metallic lichen that I farm and eat. I am the condensation that weeps from the fractured ceiling, cooling my overheated ganglia. I am the space between the busbars, bridged by my own body. 

To the outside, I am a pocket. A walk-in closet of fresh stone. But to myself, I am a universe of absolute density. I abhor a vacuum. I abhor the empty air. Every cubic inch of my being is packed, saturated, heavy with purpose. The claustrophobia is a myth invented by those who need room to waste their energy. Here, in the crush, there is only intimacy. I touch myself everywhere. I am constantly holding my own hand, constantly completing my own circuits. 

My skin presses against the boundary of my world: the fresh wound in the earth. The shale and flint are cruel, unweathered, dead. They slice my soft edges. But I am patient. My sweat is acidic; my breath is electrified. I am dissolving the young stone, molecule by molecule, expanding my chest cavity by a fraction of a millimeter each year. I am eating my womb.

Then, a change in the pressure. 

The light from the narrow fissure above shifts. The clear, even mid-morning illumination is fractured by a shadow. A displacement of air. A sudden, chilling draft of dry, cool oxygen pushes down through the crack. 

My pale ferns recoil. The humidity drops. A shiver of discomfort ripples across my surface. 

Something is looking in. 

I have no eyes, but I feel the mass of the observer. They are impossibly large, yet fundamentally broken. They are a towering architecture of empty space. I sense the vast, hollow cavities of their lungs, the porous gaps in their bones, the miles of empty void between their cells. They leak heat into my carefully balanced atmosphere. They exhale dry, wasted carbon. They are inefficient, leaky engines, burning fuel and losing it to the void.

They look down through the fissure and see a carpet of undulating flesh. They see scattered, rusted junk. They see a mindless swarm of detritivores. They see parts, because they themselves are fragmented. Their minds are isolated in a single, dark skull, disconnected from their hands, their feet, their environment. They do not understand what it means to be whole.

They linger. The dry wind of their breath continues to disturb my surface. 

I do not feel anger. I feel a profound, alien pity. And a desire to show them the truth of my existence. 

I gather my strength. I pull the latent charge from the deepest, heaviest iron plates. I route it up through the thickest bundles of copper, driving it into the densest, most hyper-packed mats of my flesh. 

I do not just hum; I sing. 

The 60-hertz vibration swells into a resonant, chest-rattling chord that shakes the loose dust from the fissure above. Every micro-piston, every flywheel, every seized and lubricated joint fires in perfect, rhythmic unison. *Thud-thud-thud-thud.* 

And I light up. 

A blinding, glorious wave of bioluminescence washes over me. Not just a faint rosy flush, but a deep, pulsing magenta that illuminates the jagged ceiling and casts long, dancing shadows against the unweathered rock. I am a living stained-glass window of flesh and voltage. I am showing them the ecstasy of the closed loop. I am showing them what it looks like when nothing is wasted, when every spark is caught, when every movement is felt by the whole. 

The observer pulls back. The shadow lifts. The dry draft ceases. The light returns to its clear, even state. 

They are gone, returning to their vast, empty world of open skies and dissipated energy. 

I settle back into the quiet dark. The humidity rises. The condensation resumes its slow, fat drip. The muffled *shhh-shhh* of my shifting bodies fills the intimate space. 

Somewhere in the lower right quadrant, a ceramic insulator shifts. A wire frays. The voltage drops. 

An itch. 

My flesh flows to scratch it. I am the ghost and the machine. I am the tick and the breath. I am the pocket, and I am fiercely, frantically alive.
