While the Stokers burn in the deep and the Grafters prune in the high vaults, I exist in the spaces between. 

To the uninitiated, the vast, echoing voids that separate the colossal root-pillars are merely empty air, sparse gaps in the dense architecture of the hollow. But to me, these gaps are the most critical spaces in the subterranean cathedral. I am suspended in the dark, walking the high-tension catenary cables of braided copper and mycelial silk that span the hundreds of feet between the wooden monoliths. I am an **Attuner**, a keeper of the grand resonance, a creature of the negative space.

My form is a deliberate divergence from the standard Arc-Born. Where the Stokers and Herders are built dense and heavy to withstand the physical crushing of the forges and the gravity of the deep, I am built to withstand the invisible, crushing weight of *sound*. My thorax is drawn out, elongated and flared, my ribs flattened and fused with my sternum to create a vast, hollow chest cavity. My skin—ash-grey, calloused, and ingrown with polished bark—is stretched drum-tight across these expanded bones. I am a living tympanum. My ears are sealed shut, useless and easily ruptured in the din; instead, the entire front of my torso is my auditory organ. I do not just hear the deafening roar of the hollow. I absorb it, filter it, and feel its intricate, mathematical geometry in my marrow.

The hollow is a closed loop of violent energy, but energy without harmony is destruction. The rhythmic *THOOM* of the drop-forges, the high shriek of the dynamos, the chittering cyclone of the capacitor-swarm—these are not merely noise. They are physical forces. If the mechanical frequencies of the iron slip out of phase with the natural, geologic resonant frequency of the ancient wood, the resulting dissonance would create standing waves capable of splintering the mega-roots. The leviathan would tear itself apart from the inside. 

My purpose is to prevent the dissonance. I am the mediator of the acoustic circuit.

I carry no delicate pruning blades or heavy iron hooks. My tools are massive, two-pronged tuning forks forged from the tempered core-steel of shattered flywheels, and heavy, lead-weighted mallets wrapped in the leathery hide of dead swarm-beasts. 

As I walk the swaying, singing cables over the abyss, I feel the vibrations of the hollow. I close my eyes and let the symphony wash through my hollow chest. I feel the deep, comforting bass of the sap-pumps, the bright, staccato treble of the arc-welders. But then, I feel it—a jagged, ugly friction. A micro-tremor in the C-minor hum of the eastern refinery. A boiler is running too hot; a stamping piston is striking a fraction of a second late, sending a shockwave through the iron that bites into the wood. The root-pillar is groaning in a frequency that makes my ribs ache.

I strike the cable with my mallet, sending a counter-frequency rippling down the line to pinpoint the source. I swing toward the offending pillar, descending on a pulley-line to the bark. I press my chest flat against the polished mahogany sheen of the wood. I feel the nightmare forming in the grain.

To fix it, I do not touch the machine. I strike the wood with my great steel fork, driving a pure, resonant tone deep into the cambium. The vibration travels through the root, shaking the jammed piston loose, realigning the swarm, soothing the stressed fibers of the bark. I tune the machine by tuning the tree. 

When the dissonance is too great for a single fork, I must call the chorus. I do not spark with the sharp, blue arcs of the Herders. My electromagnetic field is broad, slow, and undulating, shimmering like an aurora in the gloom. I cast a wide net of violet and green bioluminescence from my chest and arms. The capacitor-beasts respond. They detach from the hot pipes and fly to me, forming a massive, buzzing cloud around my suspended body. By shifting my electromagnetic field, I shape the swarm into a living acoustic baffle. Their chitinous bodies absorb and scatter the rogue soundwaves, dampening the destructive echoes through destructive interference before they can reach the fragile upper vaults. We float together in the dark, a glowing, humming jellyfish of insects and flesh, absorbing the sins of the engines.

Because I am so intimately connected to the acoustics of the hollow, I know the true texture of the leviathan’s dreams. 

When the harmonies align, when the iron and the wood vibrate in perfect, sympathetic resonance, the sound drops below the threshold of hearing. It becomes a profound, heavy silence that fills the cavern. In these rare, perfect moments, the leviathan sleeps deeply. Its dreams are slow, geologic things—memories of sunlight it has never seen, of oceans of green leaves swaying in a wind that does not exist down here. I feel these dreams as a warm, golden thrumming in my hollow chest. 

But when the machines grind and the sap sours, the dreams turn to nightmares. The wood dreams of rot, of iron rusting into its heart, of the crushing, suffocating weight of the earth above. I feel these night terrors as sharp, atonal shrieks that crack the bark-scales on my skin and make the electrified sap in my veins boil. It is my sacred burden to sing the leviathan back to peace. I press my hands to the deepest roots and thrum a lullaby of subsonic bass, a rhythm that mimics the slow, steady heartbeat of the earth itself, until the wood relaxes and the dream turns sweet again.

And when my time comes, when the constant acoustic battering finally fractures my expanded ribs and my skin loses its tension, I will not be pressed into a fissure to become fuel. The Attuners do not burn.

My kin will carry my body to the exact center of the widest, emptiest void in the cavern. There, they will encase me in a thick, weeping cocoon of calcified sap, copper wire, and iron dust. I will be suspended from the highest canopy, a permanent fixture in the dark. As the sap hardens into a dense, amber-like resin, my hollow chest will become a perfect, passive acoustic dampener. 

Long after my consciousness fades, my body will continue to absorb the stray echoes, the violent shocks, the discordant spikes of the hollow's industry. I will become a silent bell, hanging in the dark, catching the nightmares before they can reach the sleeping leviathan, ensuring the great, buried heart beats on, undisturbed, in the warm and endless dark.
