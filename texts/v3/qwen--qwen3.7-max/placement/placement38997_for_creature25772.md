To find myself here is to first forget the concept of falling. In a medium this thick, this heavy with dissolved earth and geothermal breath, gravity is merely a suggestion, a gentle, downward lean. I do not drop; I settle. I am sinking through the bruised, violet gloom, past the hanging terraces and the woven rope-bridges, past the rhythmic, muffled *clack-tap* of the upper districts, down into the deepest, quietest roots of the metropolis. Here, at the very floor of the sky-ocean, the water is hottest, thickest, and tastes most intensely of iron and sulfur. 

As I near the caldera floor, my form resolves from the shadowy indigo of the deep. I am of the cradle-builders, sharing the broad, low-slung centauroid mass of my kin, but time and purpose have reshaped me. I am an Anchor, a Tension-Keeper. My four lower limbs are splayed wide, the fleshy, webbed flanges at their ends vastly overgrown, heavily calloused, and scarred by decades of gripping the most volatile, unweathered geology. My skin is not the mottled teal of the upper-city weavers; it is the abyssal, lightless black of the deepest basalt, rendering me a void in the water. The kelp-leather swaddling my joints is ancient, petrified by the mineral-rich fluid, fusing with my rubbery hide until it is impossible to tell where my armor ends and my flesh begins.

I come to rest against a towering, freshly sheared cliff of raw obsidian. It is a terrifying, beautiful thing—a wall of black glass so sharp it could split the water itself. I do not flinch. My primary arms, thick and corded with muscle, reach out not to strike, but to embrace. My hands, with their blunt, hooked nails and calcified pads, find the microscopic grooves in the conchoidal fractures. I cradle the lethal edge. I press my broad, flat chest directly against the glass.

Through the fleshy, drooping tendrils that curtain my face and throat, I taste the stone. The lateral line of my biology reads the physical pressure of the rock’s internal agony. This caldera was thrust upward in a violent, screaming heave, and the stone is still ringing from the trauma. It is full of trapped kinetic energy, desperate to shatter, to relieve its tension in a catastrophic explosion of razor-sharp shrapnel that would tear through the lower districts of our city. I feel the high, thin whine of the stressed glass vibrating against my collarbones. It is a geological panic.

And so, I begin my work. I inhale the heavy, syrupy water through my deep gill-slits, holding it in my lungs, letting the heat and minerals saturate my blood. Then, I engage the hollow, resonant cavity of my chest. I do not sing a song of notes, but of physics. I find the exact resonant frequency of the stressed obsidian, and I push a subsonic, thrumming hum into the glass. 

*Thrummmmm.*

The vibration is so deep it bypasses the ears entirely, shaking the marrow. I hold the note for minutes, letting the heavy water amplify the wave, driving it deep into the micro-fissures of the cliff. I am canceling out the tectonic echo. I am rocking the stone to sleep.

While my chest vibrates, my smaller, delicate secondary arms go to work. Nested near my ribs, my finer digits weave a thick poultice of crushed weeping moss and mineral-heavy clay. With agonizing slowness, I pack this mixture into the hairline fractures of the obsidian. As I work, my chromatophores flare in the dark. Though there is no one here to see me, my throat and cheeks ripple with slow, hypnotic waves of ghostly white and pale green. It is an involuntary reflex of deep empathy, a visual lullaby bleeding out of my skin, mimicking the soft glow of the moss I am packing into the wounds of the earth.

When my lungs are spent, I exhale. A massive, blooming cloud of fine, crystalline dust billows from my gills, swirling around my head like a sudden, localized blizzard. This is the unprocessable quartz and basalt I have filtered from the water. I watch in the dim light as the heavy dust settles, drifting onto the obsidian, sticking to the moss-clay poultice. 

This is my true purpose. The upper city builds and expands, but down here, I am the agent of erosion in a world without wind or rain. Over decades, over centuries, the dust I exhale will coat this razor-sharp cliff. My breath will round the edges. My exhalations will turn the lethal, shattered glass into smooth, milky stone. I am the weather of the deep.

I adjust my grip, shifting my weight by a fraction of an inch, and press my chest back against the glass to find the next pocket of tension. The geothermal vents beneath me pulse, sending a lazy, hours-long wave of heat rolling up through the viscous water. I close my large, black eyes. I am perfectly suspended in the high-altitude womb, a midwife to the bedrock, breathing stone into being, waiting for the slow, beautiful passage of eons to finally smooth the world.
