I let the cold seep into my dense, heavily muscled frame, letting it cool the heat generated by my own metabolism.

The bio-synthetic resin weeps from my pores, flashing hard in the frigid water, anchoring me to the brutal, pristine geometry. I am scarred—a map of white keloid lines across my ribs and shoulders—but I do not mind the sharp edges. They are the only truths in this void.

I am far from the bustling slipstreams of the turbine exhausts, far from the swirling clouds of silver pelagic fish and the whipping strands of modified kelp. I have drifted into the marine desert. It is a cathedral of empty, cool blue, broken only by the distant, rhythmic flashing of aviation-style warning beacons and the shimmering, mirage-like reflections of a dense thermocline.

I am a Cymatic, but I am not a gardener, and I am not a scraper. 

My kin crawl along the sheer faces of the skyscraper-sized gear housings, pruning the aggressive flora and scraping away the microscopic bio-film. They tend to the flesh of the machine, fighting the relentless, biological fouling of the reef. But a continent-sized machine operating at a planetary threshold does not merely suffer from biological rot; it suffers from harmonic stress. The sheer violence of thousands of industrial engines turning in unison creates microscopic flaws. A turbine spinning a fraction of a hertz too fast; a titanium strut vibrating at a sympathetic frequency that will, over decades, shatter it. 

I am a Resonance Keeper. A Diagnostician of the Deep. I listen for the ghosts in the machine.

The dense, hydro-acoustic cartilage fused to my jaw and ribs flares, turning my entire torso into a massive, biological hydrophone. I feel the topographical map of vibration in my mind’s eye. I feel the deep, bass-heavy thrum of the geothermal exchangers, the high-pitched shriek of the intake valves, the rhythmic displacement of the deep-diving whales. 

And then, I feel the dissonance. 

It is faint, masked by the overwhelming wall of sound, but it is there: an ugly, asymmetrical shudder. A jagged tear in the smooth acoustic topography. It is a localized feedback loop, three miles down and four miles across the void. A micro-fracture deep within a colossal drive shaft is catching the ambient roar of the engine, amplifying it, vibrating at a frequency that will eventually propagate and crack the metal. 

I release my resin grip and let myself fall into the dark.

As the superheated coolant exhausts into the cool depths, I spread my arms and legs, catching the boiling elevator. The counter-resonance matches the idle thrum of the engine. To the shark, I cease to be meat; I am just another vibrating beam. It glides past, its jaws snapping at the empty water. 

I reach the source of the dissonance. 

It is a massive, cylindrical shaft of a secondary turbine housing. To the eye, beneath the welder’s glass, it is flawless. Smooth, pristine, perfect. But to my sternum, it is screaming. The vibration is a violent, shaking fever that rattles my teeth and blurs my vision. The harmonic stress is building. The metal is preparing to tear.

I wrap my arms and legs around the sheer, unyielding cylinder, secreting resin from my palms and the pads of my feet, locking myself to the machine. I become a physical extension of the strut.

I close my eyes. I map the exact, jagged frequency of the destructive resonance. I feel the peaks and valleys of the micro-fracture’s scream.

Not with air, but with the dense, heavy fluid of my reinforced vocal folds and the hydro-acoustic cartilage of my jaw. I project a counter-frequency. A subsonic, bone-rattling hum that perfectly inverses the asymmetrical shudder of the fracture. I push the sound through my chest, directly into the titanium.

*Phase cancellation.*

The micro-fracture is starved of its resonant energy. The harmonic stress dissipates. The metal settles.
