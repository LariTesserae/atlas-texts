I do not begin as a body, but as a burden. 

I start as a slow, imperceptible gathering of weight in a world that strictly forbids it. I am perched on the microscopically sharp, pristine edge of a pale fern, high in the vaulted, vine-ribbed nave of the sinkhole. I am born from the exhalation of the Aeon-Warden. As the colossal, valley-sized entity breathes out the thick, humid vapor of hot chlorophyll and crushed stems, I catch the moisture. I draw it in, molecule by molecule, drinking the transpired sap of the subterranean greenhouse. 

I am the condensation. I am the bead of water swelling at the tip of the leaf.

At first, I am merely a lens. As I accumulate mass, my surface tension pulls me into a perfect, trembling sphere. Through my curved, liquid body, I refract the glaring, shadowless brilliance of the canopy above. I hold an inverted, miniature universe of the tiankeng within my watery flesh: the braided pillars of bamboo-like stalks, the smooth plazas of woven moss, the glowing, translucent fronds. 

Through my fluid skin, I feel the rigid, agonizing strain of the Tension-Keepers frozen in the architecture below. I feel their deep-tissue dreams vibrating in the taut silk-grass. I feel the telepathic, pheromonal yearning of the trillions of Unborn minds trapped in the buds around me, their collective consciousness pressing against me in a heavy, sweet-smelling chorus. They are intoxicated by the *about-to-be*, and I am the physical manifestation of that threshold.

In a landscape of zero erosion and zero dynamic change, I am the sole exception. I am the only entity in this blinding, sunken world permitted the luxury of motion. I am the metronome of the First Second. I am the clock by which the timeless measure their eternity.

The heat is a physical weight, pressing against my curved skin, baking the air into a breathless stillness. Because the atmosphere is entirely dead, lacking even the subtle thermal currents of the world above, I know my descent will be pure, unadulterated gravity. There will be no wind to catch me, no breeze to alter my course. There is only the pull of the deep earth, and the agonizing stretch of my own surface tension.

I wait. I swell. The agony of potential builds until it is nearly indistinguishable from pain. 

Then, the Warden inhales. 

Far below, the vast, valley-floor plaza of moss depresses as the colossal entity draws in the light. The temporal fabric of the sinkhole pulls taut. The atmospheric pressure shifts by an infinitesimal fraction of a millibar. 

It is enough. 

The molecular bonds anchoring me to the tip of the fern stretch to their absolute, tearing limit. I feel the exquisite, microscopic rupture as the surface tension gives way. 

I detach.

For the first time in an eternity, there is motion. I am falling. In a world where a single hour is a single breath, my descent is an epoch. I plunge through the white-hot radiance, a streak of liquid light cutting through the profound, muffled quiet. The heat rushes past me. The world accelerates upward to meet me—the broad, waxy expanse of a lower leaf, gleaming with its eternal, un-fraying sheen. 

I strike.

*Plink.*

The sound is a cataclysm. It rings out across the valley-sized basin, a sharp, echoing shockwave that shatters the heavy silence and reverberates off the braided pillars. In the microscopic crater of my impact, my kinetic energy blooms. From the pores of the waxy surface, the Ripple-Striders erupt. They are born from my violence, fractal and glass-like, living their frantic, hyper-accelerated lives in the acoustic shadow of my fall. I feel their microscopic, silica-spun legs scrambling against my cooling edges. I feel them mate and die in the span of a heartbeat, their bodies dissolving back into sap as my echo begins to fade.

But my work is not done. The kinetic shudder of my impact travels outward, vibrating through the deep, compressed mat of living roots. It races up the braided pillars and into the vaulted ribs. It reaches the Tension-Keepers. 

Through the taut vines they hold, my vibration enters their fern-like cilia. For the three seconds it takes my sound to fade, I give them time. I give them the euphoric rush of memory, the fleeting festival of sensation, the communal dreaming that sustains their frozen existence. I feel their gratitude, a deep, resonant hum in the fibers. I feel the Unborn shiver in their embryonic vessels, thrilled by the micro-ripple in the stasis, riding the peak of their eternal anticipation.

And then, the echo dies. 

The Striders vanish. The heavy, muffled quiet reasserts its absolute dominion. The stasis clamps back down, locking the world once more in the exact millisecond of creation. The Warden exhales, and the stillness returns.

I am no longer a falling star. I am no longer a perfect sphere. I am a thin, trembling film of moisture resting on the waxy leaf below. I am perfectly, eternally still. 

But the air is thick with the sweet-smelling sap. The heat presses down. The breathless vapor coats my flattened form. 

And at my very edges, the moisture begins to gather. I am already drawing in the humidity. I am already beginning to bead. 

I am already swelling.
