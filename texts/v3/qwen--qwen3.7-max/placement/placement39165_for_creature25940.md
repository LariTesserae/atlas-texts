I am held by the heavy dark.

There is no water in this abyss, though the medium that suspends me is thick, viscous, and rich. It is an ocean of breath. I hang motionless in the absolute lightlessness of the primary ventilation shaft, three miles beneath the sunlit canopy of the High Tiers. Here, the air is not merely a gas; it is a dense, atmospheric soup of atomized iron, pulverized ferrocrete, and the velvety, heavy spores of deep-shaft fungi. 

I taste the world as it passes through me. The air draws in through the wide, fluted slits along my flanks, washing over the delicate, feathery lamellae within. I filter the rust. I drink the oxidized copper. I am nourished by the slow, perpetual decay of the mountain.

Time here is not measured in the frantic, scurrying heartbeats of the surface dwellers. Time is measured in pressure. 

I am vast, though I have no need to feel my own edges. My wings, when fully extended, span forty feet from tip to tip—a broad, leathery expanse of pale, translucent cartilage that has never known the touch of direct sunlight. I do not need eyes. In the lightless depths of the world-engine’s lungs, sight is a useless vanity. Instead, my skin is an instrument. It is a sprawling, sensitive tapestry of neuromasts and pressure-pores that maps the topography of the air. 

I feel the micro-turbulences. I feel the distant, rhythmic dripping of condensation three shafts over, each drop a tiny, percussive shockwave against my flank. I feel the low, ambient hum of the high-tension cables vibrating through the bulkheads. I am a creature of pure, distilled receptivity. I am the silence that listens to the machine.

But the silence is never permanent. The mountain must breathe.

I feel it before I hear it. The barometric weight of the air begins to shift. The stillness grows heavy, thickening into a physical anticipation. Deep below me, in the hollowed-out bedrock of the world-engine, the cathedral piston is beginning its descent, drawing in the atmosphere to compress it. 

The air around me grows taut. The pressure drops, then sharply spikes. The fungal spores in the air compress into dense, glittering clouds. I adjust my buoyancy, expanding the internal gas bladders that run the length of my spine. I angle my broad pectoral fins, feeling the subtle, swirling eddies that precede the main event. I am positioning myself in the exact center of the shaft, finding the slipstream of the coming gale. The wait is a physical tension, a coiled spring of atmospheric pressure.

Then, the world breaks.

*THOOM.*

It is not a sound. It is a physical wall of kinetic force. The cathedral piston fires, driving millions of cubic feet of compressed air upward in a violent, localized thermal. 

The shockwave hits me like a solid object. My leathery wings snap taut, the cartilage groaning under the immense, glorious strain. I do not flap; I am far too heavy for such frantic exertion. I simply catch the wave. 

I am thrown upward. The ascent is violent, ecstatic, and impossibly fast. I am riding the exhalation of the god-machine. 

The dark shaft blurs past me. I feel the rhythmic, grinding shriek of the giant ravine cogs vibrating through the walls as I shoot past them. I feel the sudden, chaotic turbulence of a ruptured pressure valve three miles down, venting a geyser of hot, mineral-rich steam into the main artery. I bank slightly, letting the steam wash over my dorsal ridge, the heat seeping into my cold-blooded muscles. 

Higher. Faster. The air grows thinner, the heavy taste of deep-shaft rot giving way to the sharp, bright tang of hot ozone and blooming jasmine filtering down from the surface.

I reach the upper tiers. The shaft widens into a massive, hollowed-out peak. Here, the air is bright, though I only know it as a sudden, radiant warmth blooming across my pale skin. 

I pass through the dangling, subterranean roots of the strangling figs. They brush against my wingtips, a soft, fibrous friction. I hear the high, percussive drumming of the Arc-Macaques on the suspension cables above, a frantic, staccato rhythm that contrasts with my own slow, sweeping glide. 

At the apex of the thermal, the updraft begins to fray and dissipate. The violent rush slows to a gentle, swirling drift. I fold the outer joints of my wings, reducing my surface area, and let my momentum carry me into a wide, lazy spiral. 

As I circle, a sound drifts down from a grated observation deck high above. It is a low-frequency, throat-sung hum, vibrating in perfect harmony with the ambient drone of the megastructure. It is the Cadence—the tall, lean listeners of the high canopy. I feel the subsonic resonance of their song in my chest cavities. In response, I pulse my own internal bladders, releasing a deep, rumbling vibration that shakes a fine dusting of rust from the walls. A greeting. An acknowledgment of the shared rhythm.

The thermal fades. Gravity, patient and absolute, reasserts its claim. 

I tilt my nose downward and begin the long, slow spiral back into the dark. The descent is quiet, a gentle gliding fall through the cooling air. The bright warmth of the surface fades, replaced once again by the heavy, comforting chill of the deep shafts. The taste of jasmine is washed away by the familiar, rich tang of iron and ancient spores. 

I sink back into the lightless depths, returning to the stillness. I fold my wings close to my body, becoming a pale ghost in the gloom, and let the heavy air hold me once more. 

I am the Draft-Ray. I am the leviathan of the lungs. I do not conquer the machine, nor do I merely survive it. I am the breath within its iron chest. 

I hang in the dark. I taste the rust. I feel the pressure build. 
And I wait for the next beat.
