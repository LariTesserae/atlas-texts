It begins with the sound. Before you can perceive the space, the noise obliterates your capacity to think. It is a 3.0 on the scale of acoustic violence—a deafening, physical roar that does not merely enter the ears but vibrates the marrow, rattles the teeth, and turns the fluid in your eyes to tremors. It is the sound of a thousand waterfalls trapped in a bottle, the shrieking grind of tectonic plates, and the deep, bass-heavy thunder of a sky tearing open, all compressed into a single, unbroken, continuous scream. 

When your vision finally clears through the sheer physical pressure of the noise, the scale of the place reveals itself to be surprisingly intimate. It is a cathedral-sized geode, a hollowed-out chamber no larger than a grand nave, suspended impossibly high in the thin, freezing altosphere of a jagged peak. Yet, inside this high-altitude fissure, the air is entirely displaced by water. You are submerged in a flooded, room-sized apocalypse.

The water is warm. It holds the heavy, blood-temperature heat of a living thing, wrapping around you in a muggy, oppressive embrace. But it is not normal water. The turbulence here is hyperactive, a storm-state of chaotic change confined within walls too close together. The liquid churns, boils, and spirals with an anthill-energy, yet it produces no bubbles. Instead, it folds in on itself. Because the rules of physics are actively breaking down in this space, the water behaves like a glitching simulation. Columns of warm liquid shoot upward, freeze into temporary, glass-like staircases, and then shatter into mist that immediately re-condenses into heavy, falling blocks. Droplets drift sideways. Currents flow in impossible, non-Euclidean loops, passing through themselves without mixing. 

The walls of this flooded cathedral speak of extreme, violent youth. This is not a cavern smoothed by millennia of gentle erosion; it is a fresh wound in the world. The rock is razor-sharp, composed of newly fractured, obsidian-like stone that snapped open only moments—or perhaps hours—ago in geological time. There are no curves, no soft edges, only aggressive, jagged shards and acute angles that cut through the dim, murky light. The stone looks like shattered black glass, thrust upward by a sudden, violent tectonic spasm that threw this mountain peak into the sky and cracked its crown wide open.

Light struggles to penetrate the churning, heavy water. The space is steeped in a bruised, dim twilight, illuminated only by the faint, sickly-sweet bioluminescence of the fracturing liquid itself. As the water breaks reality, it bleeds a deep, shadowy violet light, casting long, erratic, strobing shadows across the sharp black walls. 

Amidst this hyperactive, deafening chaos, the physical presences are strikingly sparse. The density of objects is low, leaving vast, empty volumes of churning water between them. Clinging to the sharpest, most precarious ledges of the fresh rock are the faint, scattered remains of something built. They are not technological—there are no metals, no wires, no tools. Instead, they are biological artifacts: massive, calcified arches and rib-like structures grown from a pale, bone-white coral that could only thrive in high-pressure depths. How they came to be at the peak of a newly formed mountain is a question the reality breakdown refuses to answer. These structures are broken, scattered in pieces across the floor and walls, their smooth, worn curves contrasting violently with the razor-sharp youth of the surrounding stone. They stand as faint, ghostly markers of a previous state of the world, now partially dissolved by the warm, chaotic water.

Life here is an afterthought, rare and struggling against the sheer kinetic violence of the environment. There is no green, no leaves. The sparse vegetation consists only of a few translucent, kelp-like ribbons anchored to the shattered coral. They do not sway; the turbulence is too violent for swaying. Instead, they vibrate rigidly, acting as tuning forks for the deafening roar of the chamber. 

Fauna is almost non-existent. Once, you might glimpse a single, blind, gelatinous polyp the size of a fist, drifting through a localized eddy of slow-moving water. It has no eyes, no mouth, only a translucent bell that pulses in time with the grinding noise of the room, feeding purely on the acoustic and kinetic energy of the reality breakdown. It is a creature born of the glitch, perfectly adapted to a world that is actively unmaking itself.

To exist in this place is to be caught in a localized paradox. You are high in the sky, yet submerged in a deep-sea pressure. You are in a small, confined room, yet the spatial distortions make the far wall seem miles away one second, and inches from your face the next. The warmth of the water is comforting, but the razor-sharp rocks and the chaotic, shattering currents threaten to tear you apart. 

The deafening sound never stops. It never lessens. It is the audio of the universe screaming as it is stretched too thin and snapped. The water continues its turbulent, storm-state dance, building liquid arches that collapse into heavy rain, creating a wall of water that falls upward, churning and boiling in the dim, violet light. It is a small, fresh, violently loud pocket of broken reality, suspended in the high, cold air, keeping its warm, chaotic secrets entirely to itself.
