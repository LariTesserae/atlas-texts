The sound hits you before your eyes adjust to the gloom. It is a deafening, physical force—a roaring, rhythmic clatter that vibrates through the soles of your feet and rattles in your chest. *Clack. Scrape. Thud. Grind.* It is the noise of a million stones shifting, striking, and settling, echoing through a vast, lightless expanse. 

When your vision finally pierces the heavy, cool darkness, you realize there is no natural ground beneath you. There is no bedrock, no soil, no dust. You are standing on a tightly packed, meticulously stacked mound of hand-knapped basalt cobbles. Every single inch of this world is built. 

This is a low, sprawling basin, a regional-sized depression swallowed by perpetual twilight, yet it is choked to the brim with structures. The density is claustrophobic. Towering, windowless ziggurats of dry-stacked shale press against labyrinthine walls of molded ash-clay. Spiraling ramps of fractured flint weave through dense thickets of interlocking, mortise-and-tenon pillars carved from fossilized leviathan bone. There are no gaps, no empty plazas, no streets. The architecture is packed so tightly that the structures lean into one another, a high-saturation forest of masonry extending out into the open, shadowy distance.

The air is crisp and chilling, the kind of cool that makes your breath bloom in faint, ghostly plumes. It is deeply damp. Without rain, the moisture manifests as a heavy, weeping condensation. The cold air pulls water from the deep, unseen subterranean pockets below, forcing it upward to bead on the millions of sharp, unweathered stone faces. Intermittent drips fall from overhanging lintels, splashing into perfectly spherical, hand-carved stone bowls that pool the black water. The dampness smells of wet flint, crushed minerals, and cold, undisturbed clay.

There is no plant life here. No moss softens the sharp corners; no roots crack the mortarless joints. The world is entirely mineral and post-biological. Where bindings are needed, thick ropes of woven glass-silk and braided sinew hold massive stone counterweights. Where levers are required, they are fashioned from splintered, petrified marrow-wood, polished smooth by endless friction. 

And it is this friction, this relentless mechanical motion, that drives the overwhelming noise. The landscape is fiercely active, a loud and churning engine of primitive technology. It is an environment under construction, but there are no builders to be seen. Instead, the world builds itself through a dizzying, Rube Goldberg-esque network of rudimentary physics. 

Water drips into carved stone buckets until they are heavy enough to tip. *Thwack.* A bone lever drops. *Clack.* A woven sinew rope pulls taut, dragging a massive slab of slate up a greased ramp of polished quartz. *Griiiind.* The slab reaches the apex, tips over, and crashes into place atop a growing wall. *THUD.* 

Everywhere in the dark, these primitive, automated traps and counterweights are cycling. Waterwheels made of bound, radial shale paddles turn slowly in the weeping condensation, lifting baskets of fist-sized rocks that dump their contents onto growing cairns. The landscape is embryonic, raw, and emerging. The stones are not smoothed by time or erosion; they are violently fresh, bearing the deep, striated scars of bronze-less chisels and the thumbprints of whoever, or whatever, shaped the clay bricks millennia ago. The geometry is deeply strange—fractal, obsessive, and utterly purposeless. Stairs lead into solid walls; archways support nothing but the dark air; millions of stone shelves hold nothing but pooling water.

Amidst the roaring din of shifting rock, movement catches your eye. Fauna here is rare, struggling to survive in a world of crushing stone and deafening noise. A pale, eyeless creature, resembling a massive, soft-shelled isopod but composed of translucent, cartilaginous plates, scuttles rapidly across a wet ledge. It is a scavenger of the construction, its mandibles scraping up the fine, mineral friction-dust created by the grinding rocks. It pauses, vibrating in the loud air, before slipping into a narrow, dark crevice between two leaning megaliths. 

To stand here is to be swallowed by an unfinished, alien monument. The scale is immense, stretching out into the misty, open dark of the basin, yet the immediate surroundings are overwhelmingly dense, pressing in on you with the weight of a billion hand-placed stones. The cool, damp air clings to your skin, the sharp, un-eroded edges of the raw geology threaten to cut you with every step, and the loud, relentless clatter of the primitive mechanisms never, ever stops. It is a world entirely made, yet perpetually becoming, trapped in a loud, dark, and endless infancy.
