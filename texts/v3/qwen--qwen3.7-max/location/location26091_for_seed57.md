To find it, you must descend into the crushing, lightless lowlands of the abyssal plain and locate a freshly shattered shelf of pale basalt. The fissure is narrow, the rock edges razor-sharp and entirely unweathered. This fracture happened only yesterday in the deep earth’s time; there is no silt, no smoothing, no softening of the stone. You slip through the jagged, biting wound into a submerged pocket, an intimate, hidden room no larger than a modest parlor, entirely sealed off from the ocean beyond.

Here, the water is absolute. It fills the pocket to the very ceiling, cool and heavy. It lacks the biting, numbing freeze of the open trench, carrying instead the crisp, chilling clarity of a shaded subterranean spring. Light does not penetrate this deep, and the pocket generates none of its own. The darkness is total, a thick and velvety blackness, relieved only by a phantom, bruised-violet luminescence that seems to exist solely in your peripheral vision—a trick of the mind, just bright enough to trace the faintest outlines of the dark.

Because you cannot see, you must feel the space, and the space is utterly saturated. There is no open water to swim through; the pocket is packed shoulder-to-shoulder with lush, submerged vegetation. Thick, weeping ribbons of pale kelp, dense cushions of aquatic moss, and broad, waxy fronds choke the hollow. It is a suffocatingly soft embrace. The flora is so densely packed that it forms a solid, yielding mass, pressing against you from all sides. You must push and weave through the foliage, feeling the cool water slide over the soft, slick leaves, your hands navigating a labyrinth of stems and bulbs.

But as you move through the dark, the geometry of the room begins to unravel, and the rules of the world quietly snap. 

The water in this tiny pocket is not a uniform fluid. It is fractured into distinct, unmixed planes of varying density, intersecting at impossible, non-Euclidean angles. Your hand passes through a layer of cool, heavy liquid and enters a layer that feels like liquid glass, then another that feels like a dense, chilled mist, though all are entirely submerged. The water forms solid-looking geometric shelves and sharp, invisible staircases in the dark. 

Stranger still, the lush vegetation is not rooted in the sharp, young stone of the walls. The plants grow directly out of the *intersections* of these water planes. Their pale roots anchor into the mathematical angles where two distinct fluids meet, drawing sustenance from the physical tension of the reality breakdown. When you brush against a frond, the water-plane it is rooted in ripples, and the plant shifts its geometry, folding through a fourth dimension before settling back into the dark. Furthermore, the pocket is vastly larger on the inside. You push through ten feet of dense ferns, yet the sharp, unweathered walls of the basalt never seem to get closer. The space folds inward, a submerged tesseract hidden inside a closet-sized geode.

Deep within this folded, saturated thicket, your fingers brush against something that is not plant, nor sharp rock, nor weird water. It is a single, scattered remnant of design. It is a small, perfectly smooth basin carved from a dark, porous bone or stone, no taller than your waist. It stands alone, half-swallowed by the dense, velvety moss, a silent and solitary monument in the lightless deep. There are no tools here, no mechanisms, no other ruins—just this single, haunting mark of intention, perfectly preserved in the cool dark.

The silence of the pocket is profound. The dense packing of the heavy water and the lush flora absorbs all vibration. There is no current, no rustle of leaves, no distant roar of the ocean. The only sound is a sub-audible, glacial hum—the sound of the water’s geometric planes shifting against one another, a movement of perhaps one degree per century. 

Life, beyond the endless flora, is incredibly rare. Only once, in the bruised-violet periphery of your vision, do you perceive a single, translucent, eyeless shrimp. It is suspended perfectly still in a seam between two intersecting water planes, its tiny legs moving with a slowness that makes it indistinguishable from the plants. It is the sole keeper of this flooded room.

It is a place of patient, eternal stillness. A hidden, saturated pocket where physics has quietly fractured, leaving behind a cool, dark, and lush sanctuary. It will remain perfectly, silently folded in the deep, changing by mere millimeters and fractions of degrees, waiting out the eons in the quiet dark.
