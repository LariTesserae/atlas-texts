To enter the Hollow of the Anhydrous Loom is to step inside a running engine that has forgotten how to age. 

It lies deep below the surface, a subterranean vault roughly the size and proportion of a grand, vaulted cathedral. The boundaries of this hamlet-sized space are immediately visible: walls of dark, jagged stone curving inward to meet a ribbed ceiling fifty feet above. There is no water here. Not a drop, not a seep, not a whisper of moisture. The air is bone-dry, carrying the desiccated, ozone-sharp scent of hot dust and crushed flint. It is a profoundly anhydrous chemistry, a sealed pocket of the deep crust where liquid is a theoretical concept. Yet, it is distinctly warm. The ambient temperature rests at a comfortable, enveloping heat, like the residual warmth of a kiln long after the fire has been banked, or the breath of a sleeping animal.

The geology of the cavern is violently young. The rock formations have not been smoothed by time, wind, or water. Every surface is a hazard of razor-sharp edges, freshly fractured shelves, and abrupt, jagged spires of dark bismuth and dry obsidian. It looks as though the tectonic plates cracked this space open only yesterday, leaving the stone in a state of raw, unweathered shock. Because the space is relatively small, this sharp, young terrain creates a profoundly dense environment. There is no open floor; the cavern is choked with a layered, near-touching clutter of crystalline spikes, shelving ledges, and deep, dry fissures. You must navigate it by weaving through a claustrophobic forest of stone.

Wedged into this dense, jagged geology are the scattered remnants of an industrial endeavor. Heavy, mechanized structures are bolted directly into the sharp rock. There are no sprawling complexes, just single, isolated artifacts of scaled production: a massive, riveted boiler housing, a cluster of thick exhaust pipes that vent nothing, a suspended iron catwalk that bridges two towering stalagmites, and a row of dormant, electrified generator housings. The technology is distinctly industrial—heavy iron, grease-stained brass, thick cabling—but it is integrated into the cavern with a haphazard, scattered grace, as if dropped into the geode and left to settle.

And the machines are running. 

This is where the profound weirdness of the Hollow begins to reveal itself. The cavern is incredibly loud. A deafening, physical roar fills the space, a cacophony of clanking pistons, grinding gears, hissing steam-valves that release dry gas, and the deep, rhythmic thrum of heavy engines. The sound pressure is immense, vibrating through the soles of your boots and rattling your teeth. 

Yet, the sound *never changes*. 

If you stand in the center of the cathedral-space and listen, you realize the roar does not fluctuate. There is no crescendo, no decrescendo, no random clatter of a loose bolt, no variation in the pitch of the grinding gears. It is a solid, unvarying wall of noise, as static and unchanging as a painted backdrop. 

This is the rule of the Hollow: it is locked in a state of absolute, frozen equilibrium. It is a dynamic stasis. The massive iron pistons pump up and down, the gears turn, the flywheels spin, but they produce no wear. The brass does not tarnish; the iron does not rust. The friction of the moving parts generates no excess heat, and the dry gas venting from the pipes dissipates into the warm air without altering the room's humidity or temperature by a fraction of a percent. The machinery is caught in a perfect, entropy-less loop. It is an industrial heartbeat that will neither wind down nor tear itself apart.

The light in the cavern is dim, cast in deep, shadowy amber. It comes from the faint, eternal glow of the warm rocks, the dull orange embers visible through the grates of the scattered boilers, and the occasional, unvarying spark that leaps from an electrified cable. The sparks always leap at the exact same interval, tracing the exact same parabolic arc before vanishing. The shadows they cast do not dance; they pulse in a rigid, metronomic rhythm.

Life, strangely, has adapted to this unchanging, loud, and dry purgatory. Vegetation is sparse, limited to thin, copper-colored vines that resemble braided wire. They cling to the sharpest edges of the young rock, drawing trace minerals from the stone. They do not grow, nor do they wither; they simply exist, holding their rigid shapes against the dark stone.

But the fauna is abundant. The air and the jagged walls are thick with swarms of vitreous, multi-winged creatures. They resemble cicadas, but their carapaces are made of something akin to spun glass and dry silica, and their wings are translucent membranes that catch the dim amber light. There are millions of them. They coat the walls, flutter through the dense air, and crawl over the spinning flywheels of the eternal engines. The noise of their buzzing layers over the mechanical roar, adding a high, static frequency to the din.

Like the machines, the swarm is caught in the frozen equilibrium. They fly in complex, swirling patterns, but the *shape* of the swarm never alters. It is a fluid dynamic locked in amber. If a glass-winged creature falls into the dry, hot fissure of a thermal vent, another instantly drops from the ceiling to take its exact place in the swarm's geometry. They do not eat the sparse copper-vines; they seem to feed on the ambient vibrations of the loud, unchanging noise, converting the kinetic energy of the soundwaves into their own static life force. They do not age. They do not evolve. They are a perfect, unchanging population, maintaining an exact, high-saturation density in the cramped space.

To spend time in the Hollow of the Anhydrous Loom is to experience a profound, creeping vertigo. Your eyes and ears are overwhelmed by the dense, layered clutter, the sharp and violent geology, the blinding sparks, the swarming glass-insects, and the roaring, heavy industry. Every sense tells you that you are in a place of intense, chaotic action. 

But your mind slowly registers the terrifying stillness beneath it all. The dust kicked up by your boots settles at a perfectly predictable rate. The sweat on your skin evaporates without cooling you, sublimating into the dry air to maintain the exact atmospheric balance. Nothing is being consumed. Nothing is being created. The loud, warm, crowded, and mechanized dark is entirely, perfectly, and eternally still. It is a monument to a moment that refuses to pass, a loud and bustling purgatory where time itself has been engineered into a closed, unbreakable loop.
