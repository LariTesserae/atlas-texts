**How to Prepare for the Drowning-High Town: A Practical and Intuitive Guide for the Ordinary Multiverse Traveler**

This city is not trying to kill you. That is the first thing to understand, and the last thing you should forget. It is instead waiting, negotiating, learning the grammar of buoyancy. For an ordinary human from a solid-bound world, that patience is more dangerous than any monster. It invites you to relax, to mistake gentleness for safety, and to forget that softness can drown you just as surely as violence. Below is how to prepare—body, mind, and spirit—for a place where height is measured in trapped air, where wood and vine carry the weight of a city, and where water has not yet decided whether it wants to be sea or sky.

---

### 1. The Body: Gear, Skills, and the Art of Leaks

**Clothing and Footwear: Accept the Wet**
Do not bring waterproof fantasies. Waterproofing will trap moisture against your skin, breed rot, and make you clumsy when you need delicacy. Instead, dress for constant, warm dampness:
- **Layers that breathe but hold shape.** Linen or hemp treated with wax or resin, not plastic. They should shed water in sheets but let your sweat out.
- **Footwear that grips when wet.** Sandals with hemp soles, soft leather boots with shallow lugs, or even bare feet conditioned to grip knotted vine and shell-inlaid wood. Slick stone and algae-coated copper are the true hazards here.
- **Colors that forgive stains.** The water carries minerals that will tint cloth ochre, umber, or pale blue. Dress accordingly, and treat stains as souvenirs.

**Tools for the Vertical-Horizontal**
Because elevation is measured in fathoms of bright air:
- **A short coil of fine, strong line** (waxed sail-cord or braided sinew). You will use it to secure your jar, to tether yourself in a boat, to leave a thread behind in a strange stairwell. It is the simplest map.
- **A wide-mouthed, sealed water-jar** of ceramic or cured hide. The town will offer water everywhere, but it is a courtesy to carry your own measure. The jar doubles as a pillow when lashed to a raft.
- **A small knife** for vine and rope, not for defense. It should close easily, be oiled against rust, and fit your palm like a question.
- **A palm-lens of simple glass or polished crystal.** Light is dim and the water absorbs brightness. You will need it to read knots, see the grain in wet wood, or examine the mineral maps that bloom on walls overnight.
- **Earplugs of soft wax or cork.** When the tide shifts, the town sounds different; columns hum, shells click like teeth, and the water in pipes can sing. Not dangerous, but strange enough to unsettle an unprepared sleeper.

**Physical Preparation**
- Learn to **stand on one foot while shifting weight like a reed.** The walkways move slightly, buoyed by water beneath. Rigidity is punished with splash and stumble.
- Learn to **hold your breath evenly** and to surface without hurry. You may fall from a causeway, or step into a threshold that is deeper today than it was yesterday.
- Learn to **read capillary rise**—the way water climbs in narrow tubes, in porous stone, in the wick of a lamp. This town moves water uphill by patience, and you must learn to see when a step is becoming a pool.

### 2. The Mind: Logic, Time, and Unlearning Solidity

**Redefine Up and Down**
In this city, the third floor may be drier than the first because it was built later, after the water reached a compromise with the second. Height is not rank; it is **stratigraphy of choice**. When you arrive, do not look up for the sky. Look for the **bright air trapped between rooftops**, the fathoms of luminous pause. That is your vertical.

**Accept Slow Negotiation**
Change here is incremental: a step becomes a threshold over a season; a plaza silts into an island. Your ordinary human impatience is a foreign object. If you ask for directions, expect answers that involve patience: *“Row until the bell sounds hollow, then wait for the tide to offer its opinion.”* Plan not by hours but by **tides, by the click of shells, by the drying of maps on walls.**

**Read Water as Language**
The water does not behave as told. It gathers in rooms and remembers conversations; it rises in slender columns to touch ceilings, leaving moisture-maps that fade by morning. Learn to see:
- **Which ceilings are accustomed to being touched.** If there is a halo of pale moss, water has visited often. Do not store paper there.
- **Which floors remember dryness.** If the wood smells sharply of resin, it is still defending itself. If it smells of mineral, it has yielded.
- **Which columns widen at the top.** They cradle pools; do not hang your jar from them unless you want to share your water.

**Learn the Quiet**
Silence is rare; stillness is common. The town is full of soft knocks, hums, and circular songs meant to keep the water friendly. Do not mistake quiet for emptiness. Listen for the **low thrum of pumps made of hide and hollowed gourd**; it tells you where people are tending the negotiation with water. Join the songs if you are invited. A song poorly sung is forgiven; a silence imposed is not.

**Respect the Knot, the Join, the Glance**
Technology here is repetition and leverage and careful listening. Knots hold bridges; copper clasps hold beams; vines hold neighborhoods. Watch how a rope is finished, how a beam is married to its support. **Do not undo a knot you did not tie.** Ask permission to learn, and if taught, tie it again exactly so, or admit you have forgotten. In a floating town, a lie in a rope is a future tragedy.

### 3. The Spirit: Mood, Ethics, and Living Half-Fluid

**Adopt the Town’s Posture, Not Its Sorrow**
The people here do not mourn the flood. They have adjusted their posture. They carry jars strapped to their backs, sleep in lofts that may be wet or dry depending on the moon, and trade fruit that hangs suspended between air and brine. To visit is to **suspend your insistence on solid ground.** You are here to be partly fluid, to let the town’s rhythm dissolve your edges just enough to fit.

**Give More Than You Take**
The density is high; layers press close. Every surface has been asked to carry one more thing. Do not be an extra burden without also being a support. Offer songs, mending, a strong back for a hand-pump, a story that fits into a conversation like a stone into a wall. If you buy fruit, ask about its net; if you drink water, ask about its well; if you sleep, ask about the tide. This is politeness here.

**Leave No Fixed Marks**
Because erosion is near zero, edges resist blur. New stone still remembers the chisel. Do not carve your initials; **tie a thread instead.** A thread may rot or be retrieved; a scar lasts longer than memory in a town that moves slowly. If you must leave something, leave something that can return to seed, to water, to song.

**Embrace the Embryonic**
This is not a finished city. It is still negotiating its shape with the water. You will see scaffolds of bound reed leaning into currents like questions, walkways extending to piers that stop shy of the water. You may be asked your opinion. Give it gently. Your outsider’s eye may see a way to brace a beam, a rhythm for a song, a place to float a garden. Offer it as **a question, not a decree.** The town will decide.

---

### 4. Arrival: What to Do in Your First Hours

1. **Row, don’t march.** Find a small boat carved from a single trunk, or accept the offer of one. Move along the canals the color of old honey. Let your body learn the gentle rocking.
2. **Touch the thresholds.** Press your palm to lintels and sills. Feel for moisture, for mineral memory. Say aloud if it is wet or dry. This is a greeting to the house.
3. **Drink from a public cistern** and leave a small coin or a song in return. Taste the warmth; note the minerals. The water remembers you after you leave.
4. **Watch a pump’s cycle.** Time your breathing to its rhythm. You will find the town’s tempo there.
5. **Sleep low, but not lowest.** Choose a loft where you can hear water but not lie in it. Keep your jar beside you, lashed. If you wake to a new map on the ceiling, do not panic. It will fade by morning.

---

### 5. Departure: What to Carry With You

- **A small vial of warm, bright air** trapped from between rooftops. It will remind you that height can be buoyancy.
- **A length of rope with a single perfect knot** learned here. Use it sparingly, and teach it only when asked.
- **The taste of fruit that hung between air and brine**—a memory of suspension.
- **The knowledge that softness can hold.** That patient water can be a collaborator, that wood can yield and still stand, that a city can be under construction as it sinks and still be home.

When you step out of the Drowning-High Town, you will feel heavier, as if you have swallowed some of its bright air. Walk lightly for a while. Your bones will remember how to float. And if you ever find yourself in a place that demands you be solid all at once, remember the town’s slow negotiation, and let yourself adjust your posture, just a little, to keep from breaking.
