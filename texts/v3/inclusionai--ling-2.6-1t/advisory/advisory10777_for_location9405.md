To visit here is to step into a throat that is learning how to swallow gently. Treat preparation as choreography for a place that rearranges itself between heartbeats. Bring reverence, rigor, and an almost parental patience: you are entering a lithic womb that has not decided whether it wants to keep you or spit you back out as a polished thought.

Below is a field guide written for an ordinary human multiverse traveler who wants to survive, witness, and leave without offending the pocket’s tender becoming.

---

## The Philosophy of Arrival

Understand that you are dense in a place that distrusts density. The pocket’s default is hollowness; your body is an insult of meat and bone that insists on being present. Move as if apologizing for your gravity. Speak as if the air were a guest in your lungs, not the other way around. The goal is not to master the space but to persuade it to tolerate you long enough for a conversation. Arrive like a question, not an answer.

---

## Gear and Body Prep

### Attire
- **Layered soft-shell**: The temperature is a cool that clings, not bites. Wear merino or silk liners against the skin; they mute your heat-signature and don’t rasp when the air thrums. Over this, a breathable, water-shedding overshell that is loose enough to let you pivot like a weathervane. Avoid zippers that screech; choose toggles or magnetic seals that whisper.
- **Grippers for inverted terrain**: Shoes are less important than contact physics. Bring micro-spikes for stone and suction-cups for the occasional glass-smooth expanse of wave-skin. Also pack soft mitts for handling copper while it is still warm with memory.
- **Eye and ear respect**: The place roars delicately. Wear vented ear-cups that attenuate without isolating—you must still hear the pitch changes that warn of rising droplets or crystallizing thunder. Goggles with polarization cut the prismatic glare of overhead seas; better yet, bring a veil of fine mesh for when the light decides to scald.

### Gear
- **Floatation that thinks**: A harness with variable buoyancy—tiny aether-cells you can inflate or vent—so you can hover at chest-level in the air-sea or sink to a stone if you need to feel grounded. Treat it like training wheels for gravity-sickness.
- **Resonance tools**: A small singing bowl or tuning rod. The pocket answers frequency before it answers words. A clear A or G can coax frozen thunder-shelves into stability; a gentle ripple can persuade a curtain of falling water to part.
- **Anchor stones**: Not for rope—rope behaves strangely here—but for intent. Carry three dense little stones from your home world. Press them into crevices to mark safe zones; the pocket will recognize them as foreign but honest.
- **Cartography of the provisional**: Bring waterproof notebooks bound in elastic, ink that won’t boil or freeze, and a stylus you can use with mitts. Draw what is there, then redraw as it moves. Accept that your map will be mostly erasures.

### Body
- **Breath control**: Practice circular, shallow breathing. The air can taste of copper when a wave-curtain vibrates. Train your vagus to treat the roar as a lullaby, not a threat.
- **Joint prep**: This is a place of sudden shifts. Ankles, knees, wrists—oil them, brace them, learn to fall without panic. Rolling is more useful than bracing.

## Navigation Basics

### Orientation
- Forget north. Learn the Choir: the dominant pitch of the overhead sea changes as droplets break and reform. When the sound thins, the pocket is inhaling; when it swells, it is exhaling. Move on the inhale when you want calm; ride the exhale when you want swift passage.
- **Watch the stone-sweat**: The raw walls perspire more when the pocket is unsettled. Shiny streaks mean imminent drift or lift. Dry, matte stone is stable enough to trust with weight.

### Movement
- **Swim in air, walk on water**: Treat currents like river crossings. Use a glide-pole or staff not for support but for punctuation—tap, taste the density, push off. When you must cross a floating droplet, do it when the surface dimples like a drumhead, not when it is taut as glass.
- **Climb the hum**: Some quartz veins sing when they are about to loosen. Touch them; if they thrum beneath your fingertips, don’t load them. Find the mute stone instead.

### Shelter
- The copper tower’s plates are softer than they look—press your palm to them and they will cup like clay. If the pocket shivers, the plates tighten; if it relaxes, they loosen. You can wedge yourself between plates for a night, but leave before dawn so you don’t become part of the rivets.

## Hazard Management

### The Falling Sky
- The overhead sea does spill, but slowly. When you see a bulge the size of a house begin to peel, calculate its laziness. It will hesitate, tremble, then fall. Move perpendicular to its fall line, not away—the pocket’s emptiness means you can step sideways faster than the water can drop. If caught under, curl and let the surface tension of droplets cushion you; they will hiss and explode into mist that tastes like salt.

### Crystallizing Thunder
- Repeated sound can freeze into shelves. These are beautiful and dangerous: step on one that is still singing and it may shatter like sugar glass. Tap first. If it answers with a clear, lingering note, it is stable. If the note frays, it is still becoming. Walk only on quiet thunder.

### Drowning in Light
- Sunbeams through the water can concentrate into spears hot enough to brand. Watch for columns of visible dust. Move through them quickly or wait for them to dissolve. The pocket’s shadows are skittish; they will flee your approach, leaving you exposed. Learn to move during the hush between waves when the light is gentler.

### Time’s Gossip
- Time here is rumor. Bring a simple rhythm—your heartbeat, a song you know by heart—and check against it. If you feel you’ve walked for hours but your heart has beat only forty times, the pocket is stretching. If you feel days have passed and your heart is tired, it is compressing. Trust your body more than the sky.

## Interacting with Life

- **The gliders**: They are skittish and sharp. Do not chase; let them come to inspect your stillness. Their silica bones can cut you if they panic, but they also make excellent needles if you ever need to stitch something in a hurry. Offer them a vibration from your singing bowl and they will align like compass needles.
- **The wing-finned fish**: They are curious about sideways motion. Throw a droplet gently upward and they will chase it, leaving temporary rainbows that linger like perfume. Follow a rainbow if you are lost; they tend to lead toward pockets of calm air.
- **The moss-circuitry**: It glows faintly when the water screams. Press a palm to it and it will pulse in time with your pulse; in return it will give you a map of recent tremors, a topography of the pocket’s mood.

---

## Ethics of Presence

- Do not finish anything. A half-built tool is a prayer here. If you see a blade being knapped, leave a flake of your own stone and move on. If you mend a tear in the copper tower, leave the last rivet undone. Perfection is a violence in a place that is still learning to be.
- Ask before taking. Even a stone may remember being magma more fondly than it remembers being yours. Whisper your need and wait for a hum of assent. If the air goes still, the answer is no.
- Leave no trace that cannot be unmade. Your footprints will vanish when the floor heaves, but a carved mark may persist and irritate the pocket like a splinter. If you must mark, use something temporary: a knot of grass, a pattern of dust that the next wave can erase.

---

## The Art of Leaving

- Depart when the Choir softens to a single note that lingers like a held breath. Fold your gear slowly. Touch the copper tower with both palms; if it gives slightly under your hands like clay saying goodbye, you are welcome to return. If it remains hard, the pocket is still maturing and needs solitude.
- As you exit, do not look back immediately. Step through the nick in the world and count three heartbeats before glancing at where you were. If you see the pocket already reshaping itself—wave curling, stone drifting, bell-tower sighing—then you left well. If it looks static, you may have pinched it into a shape it dislikes; return in a day or a decade and smooth what you pressed too hard.

## The Traveler’s Prayer

*I am small in a place learning to be large.  
I am heavy in a place that prefers to rise.  
I will move like a question, leave like a breath,  
and let the roaring sky remember me  
as a quiet note it once allowed to pass.*

---

If you carry this with you, the pocket will not be a danger but a dialogue. It is a throat that forgot to close, yes—but it is also an invitation to witness becoming without demanding it finish. In that unfinishedness, you will find a rare kind of clarity: the world revealing its bones before it decides whether to cover them with skin. Treat those bones with care, and they may sing you home.
