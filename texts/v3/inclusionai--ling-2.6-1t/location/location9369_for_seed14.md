You do not enter this place so much as you are enrolled in its liquid permanence, a cold that is less weather than law. The rules of this world are few but heavy. What falls tends to stay fallen. What rises is suspect and must account for itself. Cold is the gatekeeper and time is its assistant. Density is the currency; you pay it in attention. Light is borrowed, and the borrower is always reminded of the debt. Fauna, when it shows itself, is a kind of permission—proof that a life small enough, patient enough, or strange enough can still make a claim here.

Together these rules compose a landscape that feels like the inside of a long-held breath. It is a place that has been flooded for so long it has learned to like it, that has been worn smooth by the patient violence of being packed, that shivers with turbulence not because it is angry but because it is alive in a language older than shouting. You do not walk here. You are unmade a little and then reassembled by the cold and the crowd and the constant, quiet storm, and when you leave—if you leave—you carry some of the packed dark with you, like a small, wet moon lodged behind your ribs, reminding you that depth is not a place you visit, but a condition you agree to hold.
