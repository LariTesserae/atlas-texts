The dark is not an absence but a fabric, thick and softly tensile, like felted basalt velvet. It wraps a world that has no water and no need for it. Below the skin of the crust, in seams that run for continents, the air is warm as a sleeping beast’s exhale, steady at a temperature that feels like being remembered. There is no wind in the usual sense; motion is traded in slow, mineral breaths. Because nothing here rots or evaporates, the equilibrium holds without apology, and time has folded itself into a shape that refuses to crease.

The terrain is a young scar, freshly extruded from deeper still. Jags of black glass and porous slag stand in ranks like broken teeth that have not yet learned to be polite. Crystalline dunes have begun to drift across the floor of the void, not sand but shattered mica and powdered peridot that hiss when they settle, as if apologizing for their own weight. Veins of sulfur-yellow and rust-red ribbon through the stone, warm to the touch, humming faint warmth into the soles of anyone who walks without shoes. The elevation is a negative number made solid: chambers that descend, then descend again, yet feel boundless, as if down here up and down have traded names.

Life, or something that answers to the word, is abundant. There are no plants, only fauna that have learned to bloom without chlorophyll. Lattice-shelled crawlers glide over the glass, their limbs tipped with brushes of fine wire that comb the stone for minerals and weave them into temporary skins. Colonies of soft, many-legged lamps pulse in time too slow to be seen, storing heat in sacs of translucent amber that glows only when touched. Predators exist as living static: shapes that fold space like pleated obsidian, drinking warmth and leaving behind cool tracks that frost the stone for a moment before the equilibrium restores itself. Their calls are rare clicks felt more in the teeth than heard, like stones tapping inside a skull.

The world is dense with presence. Every layer is occupied, every crevice bristling with architectures of horn and chitin, with burrows that are also nests, with pathways that double as veins. In places the density becomes almost musical, a thrum of bodies and shells and mineral growth all pressing close enough to share heat. The fauna do not hunt so much as negotiate, trading parcels of warmth and trace elements in exchanges that look like dance but read like commerce.

Human hands have reached here, or hands like them. Scattered among the young sharp features are artifacts of primitive craft: hammers of river-smooth iron that never saw a river, ceramic bowls fused into the floor by mistaken heat, spirals of wire strung between stalactites to catch the faint static that drifts through the dark. These marks are not ruins but offerings, half-finished conversations with a landscape that does not answer in words. In one chamber a small kiln of stacked stone still holds the ghost-smell of fired clay, though nothing has been fired there in centuries; the warmth is borrowed from the stone itself, returned on loan.

Light is a rumor. Most of the illumination is borrowed from fauna, from crystals that remember being molten, from the slow friction of bodies against stone. When a great lattice-shelled creature unfurls, it can brighten a cavern for a stride’s length before folding its glow back into itself like a closing eye. Shadows do not flee; they pool and settle like sediment, patient as geology.

The sound is mostly quiet, but not silent. There is the soft rasp of mineral tongues, the click-clack of glass beads rolling down crystalline dunes, the distant, subterranean thump of something heavy shifting its sleep. These sounds do not echo; they are absorbed, giving the space a privacy that feels deliberate. Silence here is a substance, dense and edible, and the quiet seems to have mass.

The weirdness is not a spectacle but a grammar. Some of the fauna are not individuals but pluralities, single bodies that agree to move as one when warmth runs low. Some artifacts rebuild themselves over weeks, ceramic edges creeping back together as if bone. There are places where the stone remembers being elsewhere and leans toward that memory, so that a corridor might gently arch toward a mountain that exists only as a pressure in the rock. Gravity, too, is polite here, willing to be negotiated, heavier in some pockets, lighter in others, as if it has learned etiquette from the dense, crawling life.

Because the world is vast, patterns emerge that look like weather: tides of heat that roll through chambers, carried by the bodies of a million crawlers; slow fronts of crystal growth that advance like glaciers in reverse, rising instead of sliding; migrations of shadow cast by the passing of great folded predators. These phenomena have no names because nothing here names them, yet they behave with the insistence of storms.

And through it all, the equilibrium holds. No water steals away. No plant withers. No fire burns beyond its moment. Warmth is preserved, traded, loaned, and returned. The darkness is not empty; it is full, and it is patient. Here, in the young, sharp-edged dark beneath the surface of a world that has no need for sky, life has learned to be loud by being quiet, to be vast by being dense, to be strange by being exactly what it is.
