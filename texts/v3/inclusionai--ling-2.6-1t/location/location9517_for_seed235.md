Cliffs step down in terraces, not like stairs for people but like the slowed breath of a giant who has long since stopped caring about up or down. Density, one and eight-tenths, crowds the senses. Every handspan holds a handful of lives, a press of textures: bark against bark, frond against frond, stone against lichen against water. The place is high-saturation, almost to the point of collision, but it never crashes; it vibrates. There is no tech, only biology pushed to its loudest, most elaborate whisper. No tools except the shape of a root that has learned to cup water, the arch of a branch that braids itself into a bridge, the hollow in a rock that catches sound and gives it back changed.

It is a city without citizens, built not by hands but by continuance, by the decision of water to stay near the surface and of stone to remember its voice. In this loud, high, humid altitude, you are the only transient thing, and the landscape does not mind you, exactly; it simply goes on being itself, dense and extreme and unconcerned with change.
