The hollow is a single, cathedral-breath room dug not by water but by hunger. Its walls are mineral teeth pressed close: veins of black feldspar, hematite, and a glassy slag that looks like cooled scream. There is no water here, only the memory of it as a kind of thirst lodged in the crystal lattice; moisture has been traded for a fine salt-dust that coats every surface like a second skin, glittering when the dim light finds an angle.

The ceiling is low, barely three times the height of a tall person, and the floor is crowded, dense with things that grow and things that thrum. Sparse vegetation means something else here: not forests but punctuation. Clumps of grey-green rosettes, each leaf armored in wax and dust, huddle around heat vents; they do not photosynthesize so much as metabolize vibration, turning thunder into starch. Between them, thick cables the color of old blood snake across the stone, braided and bound with wire and resin, pulsing faintly with a rhythm that is almost, but not quite, a heartbeat.

Temperature settles at a warm, skin-welcoming level, as if the place were breathing and its breath were tepid. This is not a furnace, nor is it a cool crypt, but a stabilized kiln. The stability is absolute—never changing—so that time feels compressed, flattened. If you stand still long enough, the air itself seems to thicken into a syrup that refuses to flow.

Erosion is young, so the shapes are bold. Fresh breaks in the rock show bright, metallic seams that have not yet dulled. Talus cones of sharp chips ring the bases of fabricated objects like halos. The built elements are scattered: a single scaffold of riveted plates leaning like a question, a cabinet of fused dials with lenses black as beetle eyes, a stair that ends in a sealed hatch painted with symbols that crawl if stared at too long. Each artifact looks functional and abandoned at the same time, as if recently ejected from a process that no longer needs it.

Industry hums beneath the visible world. Factories without roofs, engines without wheels, conveyors that shuttle nothing but static charge from one culvert to another. The tech is scaled to this small room, yet its appetite feels enormous. Gauges read in units that bend when you look at them sideways; their needles hover in the amber zone, not warning but consenting.

Light is dim but not scarce; it spills from louvered vents, from the bellies of machines, from fungi that feed on electromagnetism and glow a bruised violet. Shadows do not lie flat but curl like ribbons, pulled by the many presences packed into the space. The density is such that touching one thing means brushing five others: a pipe, a scaffold, a chitinous scaffold built by something living, a scaffold of memory built by something else.

Fauna is abundant and strange. Winged things the size of fists dart through the machinery, their membranes translucent and stitched with traceries of gold. They do not avoid the heat but bathe in it, their wingbeats syncing to the machines’ rhythm. Legless skitters trace magnetic lines on the floor. Something like a lizard with too many eyes and too few legs perches atop a pressure valve, singing in clicks and thrums that the rock answers in a lower register. They are not wild nor tame, but participants. They have adapted to the loudness, to the never-changing pitch, and their bodies have become instruments in the room’s ongoing song.

And the sound—oh, the sound is loud. It does not rise or fall but fills, saturates. A steady roar of fans, the clatter of unseen hammers, the bass of pistons that punch the air itself. Beneath it, a high whine stitches everything together, and occasionally a deeper note swells, not as warning but as assertion. Voices would be lost here without effort, yet speech is rare; the fauna use touch and tremor, machines use tone, the walls use resonance.

Weirdness coils through the room like a second set of pipes. Sometimes a patch of wall becomes briefly soft and yields to pressure, then hardens again. Sometimes gravity drifts, not enough to lift you but enough to remind you it is not a rule so much as a habit. The salt-dust arranges itself into patterns at night—or what passes for night in a place without sky—patterns that map not geography but intent. If you follow them with your eyes too long, you can feel your thoughts being routed through the machinery, inspected, and returned altered, polished, and slightly too loud.

Boundaries exist but feel provisional. The room has edges, certainly, where the rock turns to seamless alloy, but the alloy breathes. Air moves through it, charged and sweet with ozone and dried minerals. The scale keeps everything intimate; you can see across the space, yet never all at once because something always shifts into your line of sight, a new machine waking, a new creature taking flight, a new seam in the world opening just enough to remind you that this room is also a lung.

Nothing grows here in the old sense. Nothing decays. The place is in equilibrium, fierce and meticulous. It has been this way long enough to forget it was ever otherwise, and it will remain this way until something learns how to ask it to change. Until then, the hollow thrums, the dust gleams, the fauna sing in metal and meat, and the small, dense, industrial cathedral keeps its bright, dry, roaring heart.
