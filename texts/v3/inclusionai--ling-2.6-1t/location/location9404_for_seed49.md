The world does not begin with horizon; it begins with pressure.  
You are already inside the city, inside the weather, inside the engine. Elevation is a rumor here—the ground has been pressed into a low, continental saucer so vast it wears its own sky. Clouds do not drift; they are bolted into circulation by towers that look like storm fronts petrified mid-gesture. Light is ordinary, as if the sun were merely a contractor who finished on schedule, leaving behind a brilliance that is bright without being generous. Warmth seeps from every seam, the kind that beads sweat before it is born.

Water is everywhere but never wild. At 1.7, wetness behaves like a civic utility: it beads on non-wettable skins of mineral skin, weeps from porous rock that remembers rain, gathers in capillary streets that run between buildings not as decoration but as load-bearing breath. Canals have been convinced to flow uphill where the city’s logic says they should. Roofs sweat condensation that feeds gutters of glassy basalt, which in turn feed cisterns humming with pump-songs. The air tastes of ozone and wet stone, a coastal tongue in a landlocked bulk.

There are no plants. What looks like moss is a mineral scab; what looks like vine is conduit. Vegetation at 0.4 means biology bowed out early, leaving a kingdom of glass, slag, ceramic, and something stranger—crystalline lattices grown like coral from waste heat, forests of cooled lightning trapped in prismatic columns. The ground is not soil but a laminate: sedimentary pages of compressed industry, volcanic glass smoothed into mosaic, and a substrate that remembers every footfall as data.

The city is built, but building here is less architecture than sedimentation. At 2.4, urban means layers are palimpsests. Streets stack like strata; a boulevard may be a bridge made of yesterday’s overpass. Infrastructure is dense enough that a single block contains circulation, waste, production, and residence in a single breath. Pipes are thoroughfares; ducts are boulevards. Nothing is buried so much as conserved. Density at 3.0 means there is no gap—not even vacancy is empty. Even silence is crowded.

Tech at 2.9 is beyond modern the way a lung is beyond bellows. Machines do not have wires; they have gradients. Magnetic tattoos on surfaces steer water, light, and heat through geometries that refuse Euclidean hospitality. Nanoscale swarms polish the air into something thick enough to lean against. Cosmic inputs—solar-wind harvests, orbital tides, neutrino rains—are metabolized like sugar. The weirdness at 1.1 is small, but potent: clocks run faster when admired; corridors lengthen if you apologize too often; doors open into yesterday’s weather.

Scale at 2.9 means you can walk for days and never rise above the city-roof, which is also the cloud-base. Weather is manufactured down here, then released to drift upward like civic gossip. Micro-storms are scheduled. A squall might be the city shedding excess charge through spirals of aluminum and prayer. The turbulence at 2.9 makes this place restless. Streets rearrange when no one is looking. Markets bloom overnight like mold, then calcify into monuments by noon. Rivers change allegiance; pavements bloom with temporary tributaries that race toward drains chanting numbers.

Erosion at 1.5 has given everything a settled face. Corners are rounded not by wind but by consensus—by bodies passing, by water polishing, by time agreeing to be polite. The place is in balance, but balance here is not stillness; it is a practiced wobble, like a spinning plate on a stick that has learned applause.

Fauna at 1.5 makes this a living place, but life wears borrowed shapes. Beetles with ceramic shells scuttle along conduits, harvesting static. Winged things that are half-kite, half-nerve glide between towers, reading magnetic graffiti. There are things that feed on noise and excrete light, turning thunder into lanterns. You feel watched not by predators but by citizens.

Sound is deafening at 2.7. It is a physical wall composed of pump-thrum, turbine-murmurs, footfalls that set off sympathetic vibrations in grates, and the city’s own voice—a polyphonic drone that rises from every grate, every pane, every wet stone. Speech must be shaped to cut through it; people talk in angles, in stabs of clarity. Silence is rare and expensive, kept in pockets behind acoustic seals, sold by weight.

Dynamic at 2.9 makes the city a creature of impulse. A plaza can become a lake if the mood is right; a bridge might fold into a kiln. Markets move like shoals. Information moves faster than water; rumor is infrastructure. The place is never settled, only resettling.

And yet, for all its noise and motion, the place holds you in a kind of intimacy. The packed density means you are never alone; the wet warmth means you are never cold; the established erosion means you are walking on a surface that has already forgiven your coming. The city is vast, loud, alive, and unrelenting, and it breathes—slow, heavy, mineral, warm—with the unblinking patience of something that has outgrown the difference between being built and being born.
