The world here is an altitude that refuses to end, a spine of air so high that the sky has forgotten how to be bright. Darkness is not an absence here but a material, thick and slow, pooling in the hollows of the heights like ink in a tilted bowl. Yet the coolness keeps everything from going numb; it is a living cool, the kind that settles against skin like a second membrane, carrying the memory of glaciers that learned to sleep long ago.

This is a place built rather than born. The ground—if it can be called ground—is an architecture of grown things, a scaffold woven from vegetal matter so old and so total that it has become masonry. Stems the girth of towers arch into vaults, their surfaces lacquered with ages of secretions, mosses turned to felt, resins turned to glass. The structure extends outward, region after region, a lattice that holds up the weight of the elevation and, at the same time, displaces whatever might have been soil. There is no natural substrate left, only this all-living mass engineered by time into a stable, never-changing state. It is the final form of a forest that learned to build, and having built, decided to remain.

Water is everywhere, a nearly full tide of it, but not the water of lakes or seas as they are commonly known. It saturates the walls, sheets, and air itself, an atmospheric ocean pressed into the canopy and the corridors between. The liquid is not falling; it is standing, or flowing with a patience that mocks motion, threading through the plant-built halls like a slow thought through a mind. Rain, when it occurs, is less a storm than a thickening of what is already there, a brief insistence that the world is, in fact, submerged. To move is to swim through altitude, lungs learning new economies, footsteps replaced by drifts and glides.

Fauna thrives in the spaces that refuse to be full. The density is low, a scattering of bodies across a vast built wild, each creature a punctuation mark in a sentence that spans valleys. They are many, but not crowded—wings that know how to read eddies in the water-heavy air, limbs that grip the vegetal lattice without bruising its long patience. Their sounds meet the two-point mark of loudness not as noise but as pressure, calls that travel like currents, mapping the place by bouncing off the living walls. A kind of sonar made of voices, stitching the darkness into patterns the fauna can wear like skins.

Erosion has reached its endgame. The oldest-stable-state shows in the rounding of joints, the smoothing of corridors, the way every angle has been asked so many times whether it might soften that it finally has. There is no urgency left in the wearing away, only the quiet certainty of shapes that have agreed to remain. This stability extends through everything: temperature, water, light, life. The rules of the world here are a single rule: hold. Maintain. Do not invent change. Even the weirdness, faint at 0.8, feels like a hush rather than a shout—veins that carry something other than sap, fruits that hum in frequencies older than hearing, a sense that the architecture is dreaming in wavelengths it has never needed to name.

Because there is no tech, nothing extraneous to the organism of the place, perception expands to fill the gaps. Eyes grow attuned to the difference between one kind of dark and another; skin learns to taste the mineral signature of the water in the air. The vastness, mid-range but unbounded in feeling, encourages a kind of regional thinking, a belonging measured not in meters but in the number of chambers one can recognize by the particular coolness of their breath.

Above it all, the elevation insists on itself, a pressure that makes every action deliberate, every exchange precious. The combined weight of water and plant and built wild presses downward gently, insistently, as if to ask whether anything present is necessary. The answer comes back in the steady, loud voices of fauna, in the way the darkness pools and then flows, in the refusal of the lattice to splinter or bow. The place holds, and in holding becomes itself—an extreme height that is also a depth, a submerged forest that is also a city, a final form that breathes without inhaling, alive without needing to grow.
