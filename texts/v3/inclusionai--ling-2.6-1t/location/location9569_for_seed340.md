The air tastes of uncured leaf and warm loam, thick with sugars that have not yet learned to crystallize. Light is not from a single sun but from millions of pinprick bioluminescences woven into the canopy—though “canopy” is misleading here, for there is no sky in the usual sense, only vaults of root and stem that arch into more root, a continuous green ceiling still soft with youth, its edges fraying into new shoots like threads pulled from unfinished cloth. We are below ground, yet elevation feels like a rumor; the place breathes upward, swelling with the stubborn insistence of sap refusing to stop.

Water is almost absent—rare dew gathered in cup-leaves evaporates before it can pool, leaving salts that glint like crushed stars. Without rivers, the land moves by other circulations: pulsing veins of syrup-thick resin, slow and warm, that rise and fall through the flesh of trunks as if the planet were a lung learning to breathe. The temperature is a persistent embrace, warm enough to coax growth but not so hot as to scorch; it accelerates decay and birth with equal hunger.

Erosion is embryonic. Walls of cellulose and lignin are still naming themselves: ridges soften into valleys of moss; bark splits to reveal rooms inside that did not exist a moment ago. Nothing is finished. Stems thicken and fuse midair, forming buttresses and arches that collapse as soon as they are named. You can watch a doorway being born, its edges curling like new fern fronds, and then watch it heal, seal, and bloom into something else.

The scale opens wider the longer you look. Valleys of vine braid into plains of leaf, which rise into ranges of trunk, each ridge threaded with bridges woven from living cord. The horizon is not a line but a texture of green-on-green, always receding, always generating depth. Density is high; life crowds life. Ferns perch on fronds; moss writes sentences on bark; beetles carry gardens on their backs. There is no empty step, no neutral floor, only degrees of occupation.

Fauna is overwhelming. Not herds, but swarms—winged, crawling, burrowing—each individual a small storm, together a roaring weather of chitin and wing, a tide that lifts and drops, floods through corridors, pauses to taste, then parts again. Some animals are architecture: mobile pillars that sprout flowers as they walk; some are tools, their bones picked clean and reshaped into hooks and needles by patient hands. Their sounds make the sky ring: clicks, scrapes, thrums, harmonics that vibrate in the teeth, a deafening chorus in which meaning drifts like smoke, half-warning, half-ritual.

Tech is primitive and scattered. Shelters are half-finished platforms woven into forks, tools are sharpened thorns bound with sinew, pottery is baked in hollow trunks where heat concentrates like fever. There are no cities, only knots of activity—clearings where sap is tapped and stories are traded, altars of polished seed and bone, signal-fires of resin-saturated moss that smoke without flame. Artifacts appear and vanish: a loom strung with spider-silk, a ladder of interlocked vines, a drum made from a cured gourd that sounds like a falling stone.

Weirdness saturates everything. Plants behave like animals when you aren’t looking—roots reaching for footsteps, leaves cupping sound, flowers that close to hold secrets. Gravity loosens near certain blooms, which drift and hover, pollen trailing like question marks. Time does not pass uniformly; some corridors age fast, others linger in bud for days, refusing to open. Logic here is a suggestion, not a law.

Dynamic turbulence is the norm. Growth is violent: a trunk can thicken a handspan in a breath, pushing aside walls, tilting walkways. Storms arrive without warning—downbursts of seed, gales of wing, tides of pollen that rewrite the map. Yet this chaos is also creative: after each upheaval, new symmetries emerge, brief and beautiful, then undone again. The place is always under construction, always practicing itself.

Sound is the texture of being here. It does not fade; it accumulates. The rustle of ten thousand leaves writing and erasing their edges; the creak of living hinges; the wet snap of branches deciding to become stairs; the constant undercurrent of animal voices braided into a single roaring hymn that fills the body more than the ears. Silence is not absence but a rare, dense material, gathered in pockets where growth hesitates, held like breath before a plunge.

Rules of this world are few and mutable. What lives may become structure; what structure exists may wake. Pathways appear where they are needed and vanish when forgotten. Exchange is the currency—sap, scent, sound, labor—and nothing is wasted; even collapse is gathered back into the weave. To stand still is to be remade; to move is to negotiate. The place is alive, and it is learning how to hold us, how to let us be part of its unfinished, roaring body.

In all of it, warmth persists, a steady hum beneath the noise, the feeling of something enormous and green, below the world and rising, holding its breath and then letting it out, again and again, in a voice of leaf and wing and thunderous, tender becoming.
