The world is a flooded manufactory sunk below its own skin.  

Water is the floor, the ceiling, the atmosphere: a brine-thick medium that presses in from all sides at nearly three-quarters of its possible violence. It is not ocean in the old sense but something younger, louder, engineered—currents routed like belts, tides scheduled like shifts. The liquid carries the chill of deep metal, just under cool, so that heat is something you borrow from machines and leak back into the flow. Light behaves normally here only because it has been assigned to: broad bands of white-gold luminescence stitched into the vault, rippling as pressure flexes the water, so that brightness moves like shoals.  

There is almost nothing natural left to stand on. The substrate has been replaced wholesale—foundations grown from fused slag, ceramics, and compacted polymer that look geological but are entirely made. Erosion is young, so edges are fresh, nicks still bright, seams still proud. The built realm is total: pipes braid into canyons, conduits arch like bridges, and scaffold reefs rise in geometries that reject softness. Vegetation is sparse and stubborn, a few mineral-coated vines or lichen-threads that survive in the thin air pockets trapped in maintenance alcoves, more ornament than ecosystem.  

Elevation is nonexistent; you are below the surface of everything, inside the planet’s ribcage. Scale is continental and yet condensed, a single vast facility that feels like its own world because distances carry weather. Density is nearly a void—great gulfs of open water between structures, hollow chambers tall enough to hold storms, silences that seem emptied of matter until something passes through and wakes them.  

The sound is loud and multivocal: pumps thumping arrhythmias, turbines chanting in overlapping keys, the slap of bulkheads against current, and beneath it all the continuous white-hiss of water moving through engineered channels. The acoustics are thick, bent by temperature layers, so that noise arrives from directions it should not be able to reach.  

Life is abundant despite the sparseness of land. Fauna swims in disciplined shoals—engineered things with metallized scales, articulated fins, and sensor clusters where eyes should be. Some tend the machines, polishing intake grates with pads of living fiber; others hunt the slower currents, darting through scaffold forests in flashes of calculated pattern. There are no predators in the wild sense, only scheduled culls and maintenance cycles, but movement still carries risk: intake vortices, shear layers at valve gates, sudden releases of compressed gas that bloom like translucent flowers and scatter everything light enough to ride them.  

Tech is industrial without being crude: belts of electrified grids float midwater, humming at safe-but-present potentials; factories open like ribbed lungs and exhale particulate clouds that drift until they settle on condenser fields; heat exchangers bloom along canyon walls, glowing faintly, turning the chill into gradients that drive circulation. The dynamic is feverish—multiple processes interlocked, each adjusting to the others, a weather of pressures, temperatures, and flows that shifts twice a day as the great pumps change phase.  

Weirdness is low, almost absent, but that only makes the place stranger: when everything is made and nothing is natural, and yet the system feels alive, the ordinary becomes uncanny. You can watch a school of fauna thread a turbine array, timing their passage to the millisecond, and feel as though you are witnessing ritual.  

There are rules here, unstated but enforced by physics that have been tuned: currents do not forgive hesitation; pressure doors close with the certainty of night; light is promised but not guaranteed in the deep ducts; heat is earned by labor and lost by stillness. The world is active, almost impatient, always rearranging itself, and the water keeps the memory of every change in its temperature layers and sediment ghosts.  

In this vast, hollow, built ocean below the surface, life and industry swim together through corridors the size of nations, each carrying the other forward, loud and cool and endlessly in motion.
