The pocket holds itself like a clenched fist. It is a vertical city compressed into a seam no wider than a canyon but plunging far enough that the sky, if it can be called that, is only a bruised stripe overhead. The elevation is extreme not in the sense of proud summits but of sheer drop: walls press inward from all sides, scarred by youth, each ledge a fresh fault line that has not yet learned to lie. Erosion here is an event still being invented—brittle mineral skins shear off in razor-edged sheets, raining down as glittering chaff that hisses when it lands, for there is no water to soften it, only a cool, metallic breath that pools in hollows and makes the air taste like old coins.

The mineral palette is austere and many-layered. Basalts the color of extinguished forge alternate with veins of salt-white and glass-black that spiderweb across every surface. Where compression has forced materials together, densities crowd like elbows in a hurry—strata abut strata, ore knots press against ore knots, pipes thread through rock like hungry roots. The city that has grown here does not stand on the land so much as it stands inside it, built into the walls, hung from the ceiling, tucked into crevices, a settlement that refuses to admit where geology ends and architecture begins. Towers of riveted alloy rise in crooked stacks, their legs braced against living stone; scaffold-bridges stitch gaps that keep opening as the place settles with adolescent violence.

This is an industrial lung. Factories exhale in rhythmic wheezes, their vents crusted with condensed minerals. Electrified rails hiss along ledges, carts shuttling ingots and half-formed parts through tunnels that reroute themselves when no one is looking. Engines thrum behind bulkheads, their pulses felt in the floor more than heard. Light is a sparse currency: sodium-glow stripes paint the walkways in jaundiced bands, while work-lamps carve cones of visibility that tremble in the cool air. In the dimness, the city flickers like a film stuck between frames.

Fauna is abundant and adapted to the clenching dark. Creatures with too many joints and too little patience scuttle along magnetic ledges, their bodies lacquered in dust to ward off the bite of raw ore. Others hang from ceilings in chitinous clusters, feeding on the city’s leavings, metabolizing metal into thin, piping songs. Where industry bleeds heat, they gather like lichen on a grave, their numbers so dense that movement becomes a texture—swarms that flow around ankles, tides of chitin and wire that pause only to taste a new seam. Their abundance is not pastoral; it is logistical, a pressure of lives packed into every cubic hand, each breath shared, contested, borrowed.

Vegetation as the old books know it is absent. Instead, the place grows by other rules. There are mineral accretions that behave like mosses—slow, crystalline films that creep across rails and cables, thickening until they must be scraped away or worked into the next batch of slurry. In corners where condensation collects, fungal analogues rise in spindly columns, pale and porous, drinking static and vibration. They are simpler than plants, hungrier, and when disturbed they collapse into powder that smells of ozone and chalk. The ecology is post-life, busy with substitutions, trading chlorophyll for conductivity, cellulose for ceramic.

The atmosphere is cool and thin but heavy with particulate, as if the air itself has been ground fine. It carries the tang of hot iron, the bittersweet ghost of solvents, and something sharper, stranger—like the scent of a memory being erased. Light bounces too many times off angled faces, losing brightness but gaining density, so that shadows feel substantial enough to lean against.

Sounds arrive sparingly, then retreat. A distant press stamping sheet-metal into shape; a ratchet tightening somewhere unseen; the skitter of a thousand claws pausing in unison; the occasional whine of a bearing on its last tolerance. Mostly, it is the city’s own shifting that speaks—settlement cracks like thin ice, the groan of a girder accepting a new burden, the soft clatter of mineral skins falling from above.

Weirdness here is modest but exact. Gravity drifts in small domains; a stair may run upward into a ceiling that, for three steps, behaves like a floor. Magnetic anomalies tug at fillings and tools, and certain corridors remember how wide they are, narrowing or expanding with the pace of traffic. Time, too, seems to accumulate in pockets; workers speak of days that tasted longer than others, of shifts that seemed to loop at the edges.

Above all, the place is turbulent. Stability is a rumor. The city reorganizes itself with frantic energy, streets rerouting overnight, whole levels shearing away to be rebuilt in a harsher configuration. The mineral world, still young and sharp, offers no forgiveness; the built world answers with a feverish industry that tries to outrun collapse. Fauna and mechanism alike feed the chaos, each innovation sparking three failures, each failure birthing two new patterns. It is a bazaar of forces, a microcosm of creation and destruction locked in a small, contained space where every surface is provisional and every breath is loaned.

In this hidden, elevated pocket, life and industry and stone press together, cool and crowded and quietly roaring, building a city that knows it may have to invent itself again before morning.
