The place does not remember ever having been unbuilt. 

Its ground is a low, continental tray of pressed chloroplast-glass and lignin-ceramic, enameled by ages into a matte, cool skin that feels like slate touched with mint. This is not a floor you stand on; it is a floor that breathes. Every square league is ribbed with vascular highways, pale channels that thrum just below the surface, carrying slow, refrigerated sap instead of water. There is no water here in the usual sense—only the ghost of it, bound into crystalline polymers and capillary walls, a sealed, non-aqueous chemistry that hums like a held breath. The elevation is so low that the horizon leans in, as if the world were cupping its hands to whisper.

And yet the place is overrun with life, so much life it has congealed into architecture. Forests have not been planted here; they have been poured. Trunks of engineered cypress alloy rise in colonnades that are not quite trees and not quite pillars, their rings inscribed with circuitry that glows faintly when the temperature dips. Canopies knit themselves into tensile domes, leaves broad as scoops, veined with fiber-optic filaments, overlapping until the sky is a rumor. The vegetation is total, a substrate that thinks it is a city. Branches braid into bridges; roots swell into buttresses; fruits harden into fixtures. Even the air is thick with pollen that glitters like milled brass, a density of presence that crowds the lungs without choking them, a saturation that feels almost solid.

Light is generous, almost imperial, yet it arrives without heat. It pours from a pale sky patched with synthetic cerulean, reflected and re-reflected by every polished frond and enameled ridge. The brightness has a sound, or rather a lack: a near-silence that settles in the ears, occasionally pricked by the soft click of stomata sealing, the papery sigh of a frond adjusting to thermal stress. The fauna are few, spectral, glimpsed as distant silhouettes gliding along the vascular highways—beings more like maintenance thoughts than animals, their bodies half-machine, half-moth, keeping to the margins.

This is an industrial garden. Engines grow where roots would be, piston-ferns cycling coolant instead of sap, their rhythms so deep they register as tectonic patience. Factories are not separate from the forest; they are its oldest trees, hollowed into reaction chambers, their chimneys blooming not with smoke but with bioluminescent spores that drift like slow snow. Belts of woven vine convey minerals mined from the continent’s own bones, carrying them toward fruiting towers that extrude new growths, new rooms, new seasons. The place produces itself, endlessly, and its weather is the byproduct: thermal tides that roll across the canopy, pressure fronts made of fragrance and static, cold downdrafts that taste of copper and chlorophyll.

Erosion has had its way here, but gently. Surfaces have softened into curves, edges rounded by wind that carries not sand but fine, glassy pollen that polishes as it abrades. Cracks are caulked with resin that hardens into stained glass. Ruins are absorbed; a collapsed spire becomes a mound for orchids of spun alloy, their petals inscribed with the serial numbers of the beams they climb. The balance is long-established, a contract between making and unmaking that has learned to breathe.

And then there is the weirdness, which is not an intrusion but a seasoning. At scale this vast, the laws of physics grow thin and elastic. Gravity loosens its grip in the afternoons, letting leaves float free for minutes at a time before settling back into place. Directions forget themselves; a path that led north in the morning may lead inward by dusk. Time dilates in the heart of the engineered groves, so that one can step from a chamber whose walls remember yesterday into a glade whose air remembers last century, all without moving far. Light sometimes refracts into colors that have no names, illuminating corridors that were not there a moment before. The plant-built world dreams in geometries that cannot hold for long, and the rare traveler who moves through it must negotiate not just terrain but temperament.

The dynamic of the place is a slow-motion festival. Canopies retract to let chilled air through; panels of living bark slide open to reveal chambers where photosynthesis has been tuned to manufacture, where glucose is distilled into glass and glass into seed. There is a rhythm to it, an industrial tide: production pulses like a heartbeat, cooling cycles like breaths, maintenance migrations like blood. Predator and prey have been replaced by harvester and harvest, by pruner and pruned, by the caretaker and the cared-for, and the line between them is porous, overgrown.

To stand here is to be inside a lung the size of a continent, cool and bright and dense with consequences. The silence is not empty; it is attentive. The vastness is not empty either; it is full of attention, of mechanisms that have learned to soften, of life that has learned to last, of a quiet, shining world that makes itself anew each day without ever admitting it has begun.
