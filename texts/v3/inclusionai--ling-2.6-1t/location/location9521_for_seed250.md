The sky is mostly water, and the water has forgotten it ever needed a bottom.

Here, at an altitude that would strangle most atmospheres, the ocean does not fall; it thickens. It hangs in shelves and cataracts that span horizons, held aloft less by air than by a hush in gravity’s grammar. The elevation is a kind of boast: the world has been crumpled upward into a ridge of wind and wet, a crest where coolness pools like spilled mercury. Temperature is a persistent shiver, not cold enough to bite but cool enough to etch clarity into every surface—metal-blue mists, glass-still veneers on stone that has barely decided to be stone.

The land, to call it that, is embryonic. Rock is less a floor than a proposal. Basalt and sedimentary rumor form plates that still remember they were liquid, still wrinkle into place with audible hesitation. Erosion has hardly begun; edges are raw, mineral-fresh, as if the planet only just this morning laid them down and has not yet decided whether to keep them. The ground, where it can be distinguished from the air, feels like a scaffold rather than a home.

And it is vast. Not merely open but roomy with its own physics. Distances here do not behave like measurements; they behave like invitations. A valley can widen into sky, a cloud can condense into a plateau, and the horizon is a negotiable thing, stitched and restitched by currents that have no map. Weather is continental in ambition, self-contained systems wheeling like slow, wet galaxies caught in the throat of altitude.

Yet for all this mass, there is almost nothing here. Density is a rumor. The space between things—between one droplet and the next, between one stone and the next—feels luxurious, near-void, as if being mostly empty is the condition of being able to hold so much water aloft. Vegetation is sparse not from lack of light but from lack of patience; what grows does so in the negative, as lichen on doubt, as moss on hesitation. No fauna. No voices but those lent by motion.

The light is normal in brightness but strange in texture, filtered through a permanent iridescence of spray. Sunlight does not strike; it diffuses, becomes a soft, widespread luminescence that keeps shadows shallow and anxious. In that glow, the wet shines like circuitry, and indeed the technology of this place is beyond modern—not tools but textures, not machines but manners of matter. Surfaces thrum with nanoscale intention, atomic-scale choreography that stitches water to air to stone in arrangements that current physics can annotate but not explain. Gravity loosens its tie. Coherence flickers.

The sound is loud without being noisy. A steady, tectonic susurrus, the roar of suspended seas rubbing against each other, of droplets colliding like cymbals in a cathedral. Thunder, when it comes, is less a crack than a thickening. The air itself seems to have volume, to be a membrane that can be bowed.

All of this is in motion. The dynamic is turbulent not like a storm at sea but like a place that has not yet chosen its mind. Water climbs into spirals that unspool into new columns. Channels appear, vanish, reappear elsewhere, as if the landscape is rehearsing its own geography. Ridges migrate. Lakes invert into waterfalls and then into clouds that become lakes again, each transformation louder than the last. This is a bazaar of states, a place where solid, liquid, and airy trade currencies without exchange rates.

There is a mild, persistent weirdness, not grotesque but off-kilter—an echo in angles that do not quite reflect, a taste like copper and ozone in the back of the mouth, a sense that altitude here is not only up but sideways, like a coordinate that has learned to doubt itself.

And through it all, you can feel the world still deciding what to keep. Every stone is a draft. Every curtain of water is a sentence that rewrites itself before it lands. This is a place under construction, unfinished and unowned, vast enough to be lonely, dense enough to sing, cool enough to remember that it is alive with change. The flood is not an end here; it is the beginning, and it is still arriving.
