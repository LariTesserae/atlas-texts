The pocket is not a room so much as a throat that forgot to close.

From the outside it is a nick in the world, a thumbprint pressed into altitude, but inside it has ballooned into a sky-lake that refuses to spill. The walls are newborn: raw stone still sweating its birth heat, striations like fresh brushstrokes in ochre and tarnished silver, veins of quartz that snap and spark when the place breathes. Erosion is a rumor here; the rock remembers being magma more clearly than it remembers being stone. It is less landscape than lithic embryo, ribs of chert and flint only just deciding to be a cage.

The water does not pool; it is a ceiling more than a floor. Two-point-eight out of three means almost all is liquid, but gravity has been persuaded to drowse, so the lake sits above you like a bowl turned over, a skin of mercury-streaked ocean hung between tower-shards of pale basalt. To look up is to see waves cresting overhead, plunging in slow hush, then rising again. Droplets break away and float sideways, pearls the size of pumpkins drifting through the cool air, condensing and exploding into smaller droplets that hiss like thrown salt. The temperature is a persistent cool that clings to forearms, not cold enough to bite but enough to make breath feel borrowed.

There is almost nothing here. Density is a polite word for absence. Between the floating stones and the suspended sea there are miles of nearly empty air, a hollow big enough to lose a city in, though the whole pocket would fit inside a barn from the old world. What exists is scattered: a single bell-tower made of riveted copper plates that have softened like wet clay; a stair that climbs into nothing and ends in a platform holding one rusted chair; a cairn of flint tools someone knapped yesterday or ten thousand years ago — time behaves like a rumor here too. Built things are timid, apologetic visitors.

The water roars with a deafening delicacy. It is the sound of being inside a struck bell that is also flooding. Every collision of wave to wave is amplified by the emptiness, bounced back and forth until noise becomes texture, a pressure against the teeth. Occasionally a sheet of liquid peels away from the overhead sea and hangs like a curtain, vibrating, singing in a pitch that smells of copper. The sound is not just heard; it is walked on, slept inside, tasted on the back of the tongue.

Life is a guest that forgot to leave. Fauna are few but extravagant: jelly-skinned gliders with bones of silica that ride the air currents like knives on glass; eyeless fish with wings that are not wings but fins that learned to catch wind instead of water, darting through the floating droplets, each flight leaving a wake of tiny rainbows. Vegetation is sparse and bold — blue mosses that thrive on vibration alone, lining crevices like circuitry, pulsing faintly whenever the water screams.

Tech is of the simple, confident kind. A forge of stone and bellows made from stitched bladder sits beside a loom strung with sinew and copper wire. Tools are half-finished, as if their making is a ritual that must be restarted each time the place shakes. Someone — or something — still hammers at dawn, though dawn here is just a period of slightly brighter roar.

Weirdness seeps from the seams. Two-point-two means the place obeys laws it has not yet named. The water sometimes runs uphill to keep from being lonely. Stones untether and drift, orbiting one another like bashful moons before settling back into place with a sound like a struck bell. The high elevation is not just altitude but attitude: everything here is trying to rise, even the water, which heaves in bulges that crest and freeze for a breath before collapsing into glitter.

The turbulence is constant, a hyperactive dance of creation and unmaking. One moment the pocket is a cathedral of quiet droplets; the next it is a riot of geysers punching through the floor, of waterfalls that pour sideways into themselves, of wind that unspools sound into visible braids. Nothing holds shape for long. A bridge of ice forms, rings like glass, and dissolves before you can test it. The copper tower groans, shedding plates that immediately grow fins and swim away.

Light behaves normally but feels intrusive, as if it knows better than to be here and still came. Sunbeams lance through the overhead water, refracted into spears that scorch the air into visible columns, illuminating dust that is actually powdered quartz and bone. Shadows are quick, as if afraid to linger.

Rules of the world, as much as they can be caught:
- Water prefers to be above.
- Sound hardens into shapes if it repeats long enough; you can find shelves made of frozen thunder.
- What is broken may reassemble if you leave it alone and speak to it gently.
- Rising is easier than staying.
- Nothing is finished, and finishing is offensive here.

If you stand very still, the pocket leans in, curious. The water overhead hesitates in its fall. The flint blades in the cairn hum in sympathy with your pulse. The chair at the top of the nowhere-stair waits like a question you haven’t learned to ask. Somewhere in the roar there is a softer note, a seam of calm that might be a doorway, might be a lie, might be the place’s heart beating once before it decides what to become next.

This is a world still chewing its own name, drowning in sky, roaring itself into being one deafening, floating, fragile stone at a time.
