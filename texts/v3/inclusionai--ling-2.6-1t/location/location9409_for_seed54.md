Cold does not fall here so much as it is manufactured. At two thousand strides up, the air carries a metallic taste of chilled condensate that coats every surface in a film like breath on glass, only slower, thicker, self-repairing. The darkness is not an absence but a material: a matte, heavy curtain woven from soot-black nanofibers that absorb stray photons and convert them to faint, downward-bowing pressure. Yet within this gloom the place is loud—not wind, not water, but a dense, overlapping chorus of clicks, scrapes, whines, and low electric thrums that propagate through the wet stone as through a drumskin.

Water is everywhere, not as rivers or lakes but as a capillary ghost. It weeps from the undersides of overhangs, beads on walls without beading, gathers in films thick as oil, and occasionally drops in precise, metronomic intervals. The cold keeps it from freezing, or perhaps something else keeps it from freezing—an additive in the stone, a charge in the water, a rule of the world. The ground is a high, embryonic plateau still stitching itself together. Ridges are half-formed, canyons are half-erased, and faults have been plated over with seamless, urban skins that rise like stacks of dark trays. The elevation gives a wide, open sense of distances that seem to fold back on themselves—terraces, balconies, bridges, and causeways extending into fog, their edges blurred by condensation.

There are no plants, never have been, or not in any sense recognized beyond this place. What grows here are mineral accretions and structures that behave like organisms without being alive: glass-bark lattices, black-ice buttresses, porous spires that ring like bells when struck by droplets. The city is built into the raw geology as much as upon it—towers fused to embryonic cliffs, roads that have been polished by slow flows of wet stone, chambers that exhale cold vapor in long, patient sighs. Infrastructure is dense and layered: conduits like rootless veins, shutters that slide without sound, collectors that drink the weeping water and shunt it through transparent tubes that glow with a dim, sub-visible light. The technology is beyond modern, a quiet mastery of small forces and tight constraints—gravity teased into gradients, time bent into loops for maintenance cycles, matter held in states that should not be stable at this temperature.

The weirdness is moderate but potent: sometimes a section of wall will remember being elsewhere and shift its texture accordingly; sometimes footpaths rearrange themselves overnight, not to confuse but to optimize, as if the city dreams in efficiency. The wetness helps this—water acts as a memory, a conductor, a slow glue. The cold helps too, keeping motion sluggish enough to be deliberate.

Fauna is overwhelming in number, not size. They are small, many-legged, chitinous, and wet, pouring through the city in tides that rise and fall with the weeping of the rocks. Some resemble insects, others resemble machines, others resemble neither. Their shells click and scrape, their limbs tap in rhythms that harmonize with the hum of the city. They drink the condensate, harvest minerals from the walls, build temporary nests that dissolve into the stone. They are tolerated, even cultivated—there are ledges designed for them, channels that guide them, lights that herd them like livestock. In return they polish surfaces, distribute trace elements, and sing. Their song is part of the loudness, a dense sonic texture that makes the darkness feel crowded.

Movement through this place is slow. Everything is patient. The erosion that has not yet finished its work continues at a geologic creep; the city adjusts itself by millimeters over days. Water travels in thin, persistent threads. Heat, such as there is, leaks from vents in long, invisible plumes. Time feels braided: the short cycles of the fauna, the mid cycles of the city’s maintenance, the long cycles of the stone still becoming itself.

At the center of this high, wet, dark city rises a structure like a frozen geyser turned inside out—a hollow column where water ascends in a glassy skin, refracting the city’s hidden lights into faint, shifting glyphs. The column is silent, but the fauna cluster around it, and their clicking rises to a pitch that seems to answer something deeper in the stone. The air thrums. The cold bites gently. The city waits, not sleeping, but thinking in slow, wet, metallic dreams, and all around it, the unfinished world continues to learn how to hold itself together.
