The ceiling is low enough to remember your own height.  
Stone hangs in shelves and ribs, blackened by years of breath and the slow sweat of walls. This is not a cavern so much as a folded room deep enough to have forgotten the sky, yet shallow enough that a shout could scrape the stone above. The air tastes of iron and wet loam, carrying a heat that settles on the skin like a second hide: twenty-six degrees at rest, climbing higher in pockets where no draft dares. Light does not enter; it pools, thick and sulfurous, from seams that weep a pale jelly, giving the place a dimness like the inside of a closed fruit.

Water moves in small ambitions. A vein of seepage weeps from a fault line, braiding across flagstones that have learned to lean, then pooling in saucers of stone where mosses keep tiny calendars of flood. These ponds are no larger than a table, rimmed with crusts of salt and the ghost rings of last season’s overflow. When the drip quickens—afternoons when the rock itself seems to inhale—the pools shiver and spill, then settle into mirrors that hold the heat like coins. The damp is insistent but never drowns; it suggests rivers rather than becoming them, a grammar of moisture that speaks in commas and colons rather than paragraphs.

Vegetation is lush but knows its borders. Fronds unfurl from cracks with a deliberateness that looks planned, each leaf darker at the base, lighter at the tip, as if lit from within. Vines braid around ribs of stone the way rope braids around a memory, and bulbous mosses swell after each seep, translucent as lungs, then contract into velvet scars. There is a carnivorous patience here: traps disguised as petals, cups that hold not rain but enzymes, stems that coil when brushed by wing or claw. The air is dense with spores that taste faintly of cinnamon and rust, drifting in clouds when a lizard’s foot or a falling seed parts the green.

Fauna moves like a rumor that outlives its source. Beetles armored in iridescent plates patrol the rims of pools, their treads leaving trails that harden into lacquer by dawn. Skinks with crests like combs dart along stone shelves, pausing to taste the wall as if reading braille written in salt. Larger shapes negotiate the low ceiling by flattening themselves, slipping between layers of air and rock with a sound like breath drawn through grass. At dusk—or what passes for it when the jelly-light dims—hunters with too many joints descend from the hanging gardens, leaving silken maps that hum. Predation here is intimate: one creature’s gain is another’s geometry rearranged.

The few built things do not impose. A fallen lintel, half-swallowed by roots, serves as a bridge for a procession of crabs with shells like cracked enamel. A ring of stones, laid long ago and since learned by moss, marks a hearth that no longer holds fire but holds memory of it: a circle of warmth that lingers in the stone like a bruise. Scattered pottery, fired from local clay and glazed by mineral seep, rests in nests of fern, each vessel a question without an answer. Nothing stands tall; everything settles as if gravity were a story these fragments have agreed to finish quietly.

Erosion has been a collaborator, not a thief. Water has polished shelves into basins; wind, when it finds its way down, has thumbed edges into curves. The floor is a map of decisions made by drip and slide: stone worn concave where generations of feet and claws have paused, grooves that remember the direction of flow even when the water does not. In this equilibrium, decay gives back as much as it takes: cracks become nurseries, splits become song tunnels, what falls becomes soil that lifts again toward the light it will never see.

The world operates by rules that feel older than names. Heat rises, but damp clings, and the two negotiate in a daily hush that sounds like paper shifting. Light from the seams dims and brightens without a sun’s schedule, as if something in the rock itself breathes. Movement is permitted, but speed is suspect: those who rush find their edges softened, their intentions absorbed. To linger is to be remade; to leave is to carry the place as a weight behind the eyes.

Sounds, when they come, are small. A bead of water striking tin. A foot on stone that has never known frost. The papery whisper of a leaf detaching, not falling but deciding. Silence is not empty here; it is dense, like heat trapped in wool, and it settles in the throat when you try to speak.

There is a mild, useful strangeness that hums along the borders. Flowers that close when stared at for too long. Stones that warm when touched by certain feet and remain cool to others. A pool that reflects not the ceiling but something older: a sky with unfamiliar constellations, visible only if you kneel close enough to drink. This weirdness is not chaotic; it is polite, almost bureaucratic, as if the place keeps a ledger of exceptions.

And through it all, activity continues: spores sail, roots probe, jaws click in the green gloom. The heat rises, the damp answers, the light dims further, and the small, crowded world keeps its balance by constant, careful motion—not toward an end, but toward the next moment, held in a room that remembers how to hold.
