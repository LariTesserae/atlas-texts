Erosion has not yet learned patience; edges are abrupt, clay cliffs striped with fresh fault-lines that glisten like sliced fruit.

Two-tenths past lush: mats of fern-analogs unfurl in iridescent whorls, vines that look like braided circuitry drape from scaffold-like trunks, and mosses bloom in sudden violet clouds wherever moisture lingers. Weather is a public utility—rain scheduled like buses, sunshine parceled out in measured lumens.  

At the center of the region—a center that moves—lies a plaza paved with porous ceramic that remembers every footprint as a negative image in faint bioluminescence. Around it, sparse towers hold up a ceiling of braided vines threaded with fiber-optic nerves. The air tastes metallic and sweet, as if the city itself is slowly dissolving into something drinkable.  

And if you walk far enough, past the last sparse blocks, you find the edge where the city is still being born: raw, warm seams in the earth, young canyons of fresh-cut stone, water gathering in them not from rain but from the ground’s own exhalation, and along the banks, gardens that grow upward in spirals, reaching for a dim sky that offers almost nothing, and yet is enough.
