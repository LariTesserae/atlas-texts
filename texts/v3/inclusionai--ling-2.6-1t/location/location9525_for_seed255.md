The world is a lung that refuses to exhale. It is low and yet it crowds you, a dense compression of things that have been put here instead of grown. Water is everywhere, not as river or sea but as a patient thief: it condenses on the undersides of vaults, beads along ribbed seams, drips from false eaves into catch-basins that chime like cracked bells. The damp is a language. It translates coolness into a skin-prick, a breath-cloud, a rust that blooms like ink on paper even though nothing here is paper, and nothing here should rust.

This is a totally built place, and it shows its making too openly. The erosion is emerging: edges still remember the tool that gouged them. Surfaces bear hatch-marks, chisel ghosts, poured joints that have not yet decided whether to fuse or to separate. It is pre-formation wearing a mask of completion, embryonic stone and fired clay and braided mineral threads that stiffen into corridors, pylons, buttresses, staircases that lead to other staircases. Nothing is natural substrate; even the ground is a deck, a skin of interlocking tiles whose gaps gather moisture and the occasional, rare footfall of fauna.

The fauna are few and strange, as if they have wandered in from elsewhere and are not sure whether they are allowed to stay. You glimpse them by their double-shadows: a soft-bodied quadruped with too many knees, a drifting sack that hums to keep from settling, something like a scaffold-crab made of stolen hinges. They move with careful economy, because here density is a law. There are many presences per volume, layers pressed close, high-saturation colors of enamel and oxide and wet mica. Things brush against one another: a pipe nests in a niche that is also a shrine; a bell-shaped hood covers a vent that breathes steam; a lattice sags with condensation and is also a map. Almost everything touches almost everything else, and each touch sings a little.

Which is to say the place is loud. Not with voices but with surfaces working: metal coughing as it cools, water clocking into copper bowls, stone shifting in its fresh joints, low throats of air being forced through thin chimneys. The tech is primitive in method but grandiose in application: hand-cut grooves, hammered plates, rope-lashed spars, simple alloys that gleam like wet teeth. There are no motors, only windlasses, counterweights, drip-clocks, tension-snares. The making is manual, proud, and ongoing; you can see tool marks that are still fresh, still damp with the maker’s patience.

Vegetation is absent or else has been replaced by a simpler, stranger analogue. Fungal pads dust the undersides of arches, pale and cool, digesting not wood but the slow leach of minerals from mortar. There are no leaves, only filigree of frost, mineral whiskers that sprout where breath meets wall, and something like lichen that tastes of iron and listens. Ecology, here, is a negotiation between damp and density, between sound and coolness. Fauna drink from catchments shaped like mouths; the mouths open and close with the day’s condensation; the fauna blink and move on.

Light is scarce, as if the place hoards it. Darkness pools in the low places, but it is not empty: it is a thick, textured medium, threaded with the glow of phosphor-stains, wet metal, a soft blue seam where two kinds of tile almost meet. Shadows do not lie flat; they bunch up like cloth, tugged by the many corners crammed into each view. You feel you could wring them out and get more damp, more cool, more faint bell-noise.

The scale is open enough that you can sense it continuing past the next corner, the next rise of almost-hill, but density keeps insisting that all of it is near. This valley-sized place is a cluster, a hive, a layered thing folded into itself, so that moving through it feels like turning the pages of a book whose leaves are damp and stuck along the edges. Every step is a negotiation with what has been made and what is still becoming, with water that does not flow so much as linger, with noise that is not random so much as purposeful, a loud conversation in a tongue built of clink and seep and cool air.

Strangeness rises from the way all these parameters hold one another. Because nothing is natural, the weirdness is not an exception but a texture: a corridor that slopes slightly inward, as if learning to cradle its own breath; a basin that fills not from above but from the sides, condensing inward; a bridge whose rails are spaced to let through the rare fauna but not the light; a set of bells that ring not from wind but from the passage of water through tuned pipes buried in walls. The active dynamism of the place is quiet in its parts and roaring in its sum: a cool, damp, crowded, handmade underworld that is still being built while it is already in use, still learning how to be itself while it speaks in loud, mineral voices into the dark.
