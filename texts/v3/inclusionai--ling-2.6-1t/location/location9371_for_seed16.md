The pocket sits like a closed lung inside a world that forgot how to breathe. Low, yes, but not sunk—cradled. A caldera of no volcano, a bowl scooped by slow sighs rather than violence, its rim a fretwork of blackened spines that lean in as if eavesdropping on the interior. The ground is a skin of salt-crust and powdered glass, pale and blinding when it chooses to be, but now it is dim, as if the light were rationed, meted out in thin sheets that dare not pool.

Vegetation here is lush by local measure—a riot of forms that have never seen water and do not miss it. Frond-thickets of glass-fiber weave themselves into archways, each leaf a hollow prism that stores not moisture but pressure, a tension that hums. Vines of braided ceramic ribbon the walls, flowering with knots that puff silver pollen at intervals, the grains drifting like snow upward because buoyancy here is perverse, or at least disobedient. Root-systems map the bowl in braille, dense tangles of calcified thread that knit the floor into a single, breathing panel. The plants drink heat, or so it seems, sipping from the ambient scald until their tissues gleam like wet enamel.

The temperature settles at a threshold just shy of intolerable, high enough to keep the air viscous, as if the atmosphere itself were a syrup that has forgotten how to thin. At this warmth, materials behave with a sluggish appetite: metal yawns, stone sweats oils that do not evaporate but pool and retract like slow tongues. Surfaces are never still. Every afternoon—or what stands in for one—the bowl undergoes a shift, a cycle of expansion that buckles the floor in gentle waves. Cracks open, breathe out drafts that smell of baked clay and bruised mint, then seal themselves with a click like teeth finding a new bite.

It is an urban place, though no hand you could recognize as human built it. A city for things that are neither plant nor animal nor machine, but something that learned to congregate. Towers of compacted ash and shell rise in spindly clusters, their walls porous enough to whisper through. Pathways are not laid but grown, ribbons of fused grit that conduct heat like nerves. There are plazas that are really only hollows where density thins, spaces so emptied they feel loud simply by contrast. In these hollows, the wind does not so much blow as accumulate, then release in percussive bursts that rattle the fronds and set the ceramic vines to chiming. It is a loud landscape: the susurrus of expanding stone, the crackle of glass-leaf adjusting to the day’s weight, the clatter of spines on spines when the bowl shifts. Noise here is fabric, worn close.

Fauna is absent, and this absence shapes everything. Without predation or flight, the ecology is a parliament of negotiations: which root may drink first, which bloom may unfurl its silver, which tower may lean. The plants compete by generosity, gifting shade, gifting sound, gifting hollows that catch and recycle warmth. Waste is a concept they metabolize immediately, folding expended chemicals back into skin and strut. Even decay is a construction: when a frond topples, it is claimed within hours by threads that spin it into lint, then felt, then mortar.

Technology is primitive, not for lack of cleverness but for lack of need. Tools appear where hands would be—hooks of sharpened obsidian bound with fiber, scoops of bone-white ceramic, ladders of braided vine that stiffen when stepped on. These are kept in niches that open only when a certain temperature is reached, as if the city itself decides who may work and when. There is no writing, only scoring: patterns etched into the bases of towers that map heat-flow rather than history, archives of sensation instead of event.

Erosion is established—not ruin, but a way of being settled. Corners have rounded themselves into continuities; edges have grown lips to soften collisions. What wears away is not lost but redistributed. Dust motes drift in the dim light, each speck a minuscule archive of prior forms, circulating like breath. Scale is hidden; you could stand in the center and see the far rim, yet feel as though you are inside a pocket that has turned itself inside out to make room for you. The city is intimate, its architecture cupping you like a palm.

The weirdness is mild but persistent. Shadows do not always follow the light-source, sometimes lagging or arriving early, as if they have their own errands. At certain moments, the heat thins just enough to let sound carry across the bowl in clean arcs, and you can hear the city talking to itself in a language of creak and chime. Density is near-void in the upper bowl, where the air thins into a ceiling that is almost a fiction, but down here, among the towers and fronds, closeness is absolute, every surface within reach of every other, pressed into companionship by the heat.

This is a place that exists because it can, because the parameters of no water, high heat, and settled form have conspired to make a city of the unwatered garden, loud with its own maintenance, lush with life that never learned to thirst, dim with a light that is not refused but held in reserve, waiting for the bowl to shift once more and let everything settle into a new, temporary balance.
