The place has no name except the weight of its own air. It sits low, cupped in a planetary dish of soft curvature, as if the land were still learning how to be floor. Elevation is a suggestion here, not a rule: the ground sinks and swells in broad, patient rolls, all newborn and soft-edged as if erosion forgot to arrive. There are no scars yet, only the fresh seams of recently risen earth, still holding the memory of suddenness, still cool with the blush of first exposure.

Vegetation is not a coat but a constitution. The entire space is plant-as-substrate: a seamless fabric of living matter so total that to say “forest” or “swamp” would be to shrink it. Stems braid into beams, roots swell into buttresses, leaves laminate into roofs and walls and bridges that span small, breathing gullies. Nothing is built because nothing needs to be; the green mass has already resolved architecture into physiology. Every meter is dense with presences: fronds pressing fronds, vines knotting vines, mosses that grow mosses, until saturation feels like a physical pressure, a thickening of the lightless air.

And the light is nearly gone. At 0.1 the world wears darkness like a skin, a soot-soft gloom that never lifts, not even to gray. What illumination exists is borrowed from biologies: faint threnodies of cold phosphorescence stitched into leaf edges, pulsing along veins like slow circuitry. These are not decorations but organs of a vast metabolism, the place’s own constellations mapping the contours of its own body.

Water, at 1.6, does not flow away but hovers close, as if the earth were tired of holding it and keeps it near the surface in trembling films. Pools collect in the cradles of living elbows; stems weep condensate that gathers in cupped leaves and runs in silver filaments down bark that never quite dries. The atmosphere is a lungful of wet, a climate that breathes in damp and breathes out damp, carrying the iron-sweet, peaty scent of perpetual nearness to liquid.

Temperature is warm, not hot—the kind of warmth that thickens rather than drives, that encourages growth to accrete rather than burn. It settles into the mass like oil into wool, holding the green in a state of perpetual pliability. Because nothing ever changes much—dynamic at 0.2—the warmth is an equilibrium, a hum rather than a cycle, a steady state that has learned its own borders and refuses to wander.

This is a landscape of scale at 2.9, so vast that weather begins to misunderstand itself. Pressures shift over hundreds of kilometers of uninterrupted living crust, and the silence acquires mass. The quiet, at 0.2, is not empty but attentive: the soft susurrus of tissue sliding against tissue, the infinitesimal click of cuticle on cuticle, the hush of water finding its level along living gutters. There is no song, no cry, only the sound of the place remembering its shape.

Erosion’s absence leaves features sharp in their youth: ridges like the fresh folds of a closing fist, valleys still rounded with astonishment. The land is recently emerged, as if it had been held underwater and only just been let up, still glistening with the shock of being above. This newness stands in easy congress with the industrial strangeness threaded through the mass.

For at 2.1, industry is here, but it has been grown rather than assembled. Engines are not housed but hosted: calcified pistons of living ceramic, lattices of conductive vein that hum with current, pumps of muscle and resin that press fluids through pressurized gardens. Factories are hollows in the green, vast throats where secretion and uptake are scaled to continental appetites. The electrified network is a nervous system turned outward, filaments of bright sap and cold fire threading through the dark, turning the forest into a single, breathing manufactory. There is no smoke, only the ozone scent of thoughtlike currents moving through wet tissue.

Fauna is absent, and this lack clarifies everything. Without mouths to feed or feet to tread, the place can afford density without violence, strangeness without predation. The ecosystem runs on exchange alone: gifts of current, drafts of sap, slow tides of chemical intention. The weirdness—2.2—does not announce itself but accumulates, the way a hush can become louder the longer you listen. Shapes develop that have no reason to exist elsewhere: spirals that are also conduits, bells that are also capacitors, hollows that sing without sound by vibrating the air in their walls.

Because the system is at equilibrium, time feels like a thin layer. The place could be a moment stretched thin across a continent, or a continent mistaken for a moment. To walk through it would be to pass through rooms of living architecture whose doors are simply decisions of the mass; to stay still is to feel the place’s pulse settle against your own, a low, warm thrum that insists it has always been this way and will never be otherwise.

Here, darkness is not emptiness but a medium. Water is not a resource but a mood. Industry is not an intrusion but an organ. The land is young, dense, vast, and alive to the point of unknowing, a quiet engine wrapped in endless green, holding its breath in the dark and never needing to exhale.
