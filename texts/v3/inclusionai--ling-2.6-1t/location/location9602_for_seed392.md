The surface is not ground. It is deck.

A low plain of fitted, pale plates stretches outward, jointed and waxed, each plank a long palm-width, laid in a herringbone that refuses to slip even under heat. There are no soils here, no sediments older than the last coat of resin. The making is total: hand-cut edges beveled with shell-shard and palm-oil glaze, posts of blackened timber set in metal shoes that never rust, ropes of braided vine binding beam to beam. It is a floor the size of a town, a roof without walls, a thing built so completely that the word ground has lost its meaning. The light is everywhere, hard and white at zenith, gilding every chamfer, turning sweat into tiny lenses on skin.

Water is a constant guest but never a deluge. It arrives as a sheen that beads on the deck and slides into grooves no wider than a fingernail, channels carved with patient bone-tools to carry the surplus to the edges. There it spills into aprons of porous stone that weep without ceasing, curtains of droplets catching the brightness and throwing prisms onto the planks below. The air is wet enough to blur distances, a vapor-skin over everything, yet the heat keeps it from ever feeling cool. Breath comes out like steam from a kettle that never empties. In this humid brightness mosses do not grow in soil; they colonize the joints, thick emerald cuffs around each nail, and fern-fronds uncurl from cress-baskets hung beneath the walkways, their roots drinking from the weeping run-off.

Vegetation is lush with a deliberate excess. Clumps of cane and reed have been planted in orderly scatter, tall enough to cast filigree shadows but not so dense as to block the eye. Flowers with lacquered petals open toward the sun, each blossom a small lantern of color against the pale wood. Vines have been persuaded to climb stakes that are themselves works of craft, twisted and polished, capped with shells that clack softly when the air moves. The greenery is abundant, almost showy, as if the builders decided that making could include coaxing, and that coaxing should be lavish.

Fauna is present but subdued by the brightness. Lizards with backs like hammered copper patrol the plank edges, pausing to taste the salt-crust that blooms where water and heat meet. Above, insects with prismatic wings rise in faint clouds when a foot disturbs the moss-cuffs, yet their flight is silent, or nearly so. Birds come only to the hanging baskets, drinking beads from the weeping edges, their calls brief and swallowed by the wet air. The place is theirs as much as it is the builders’, but they have adapted to the made-ness of it, treating rail and rope as natural features, learning the safe paths along the resin-glossed grain.

Weirdness lives in the details of perception. At certain angles, the deck seems deeper than it is, as if each board were a slice through time instead of wood. The weeping stone sometimes shows faces that were not carved, appearing only long enough to question whether they were ever absent. The light does not shift; it insists, and in that insistence colors begin to separate from their objects, drifting like pollen caught in an invisible current. A watcher may notice that their own shadow does not always follow them with the same delay, or that the space between planks hums at a pitch that feels remembered rather than heard. The place is never changing, and this stability is the engine of its strangeness: without decay, without erosion, without the softening that repetition usually brings, the world tilts toward a hallucinatory precision.

The sound is mostly quiet. There is the tick of water finding its channel, the soft thud of a lizard’s belly on wood, the almost-silent hush of wings too bright to look at directly. Footsteps are muffled by the moss-cuffs. Even breath seems to hesitate, as if reluctant to disturb the balance. The quiet is not empty; it is attentive.

Rules here are those of craft and care. Nothing is allowed to rot, so everything is tended. The resin must be renewed by hand, the joints checked with a stone that sings when the fit is true, the weeping channels cleared with hooks of bone. Metal is rare and simple: a few pins, a few plates, always protected by oil and vine. The inhabitants move with a economy that comes from knowing that any mistake will persist, unchanged, beneath the bright eye of the sky. There is no waste, only reuse, and even the moss is trimmed into patterns that repeat across the expanse.

The scale is open enough to feel regional. One can see, from the center of the deck, the low line where the built plain meets the wept edge, and beyond that, more decks, more weeps, more green, repeating with variations that suggest a people who build by principle as much as by plan. Yet the density is sparse: the eye can travel far between presences, across stretches of flawless wood where only light and water hold council.

In the end, this is a place that refuses to become anything other than what it was made to be, and in that refusal it has become stranger, brighter, wetter, and more alive than any of its makers intended. It is a low, hot, loud-bright, quiet-breathing country of deck and drip and deliberate green, where the hand of the maker is still obvious and the hand of time is simply not allowed to arrive.
