The world does not remember oceans. What it keeps instead are basins that learned to be bottom without ever having been wet—great, cupped plains of calcined salt and fused silica that stretch as if the sky had pressed down and, finding no give, left a dent. From the edges of the plateau the land steps upward in faulted terraces, each riser a kilometer high, its face banded with the slow chemistry of aeons: ochre rinds of oxidized iron, veils of gypsum like dried lace, strata of slag that fell from early, hotter skies. This is a high world, the air thinned and sharpened so that distances look cut from glass, and the horizon bends with a patient, deliberate curve, as though the planet is exhaling across epochs.

Warmth pools in the hollows and bleeds across the slopes without hurry. Heat shimmers stand sentinel over playas that glint like beaten pewter. At midday, the light is ample but not harsh; it leans across slopes, polishing mineral faces until they glow from within. As the long day tilts, shadows pool like spilled ink in the creases of the world, turning ridges into blades and valleys into corridors of cooled stone. The cold does not arrive so much as settle, a patient guest that thickens the air until breath plumes in pale threads and the rocks sing with a deeper tone.

There is no water to shape this place, only wind and time. Erosion here is a scholar, not a thief: it rounds edges by gentle abrasion, polishes tunnels through uplifted blocks, and gathers dust into dunes that crawl inches per decade. The ground is scoured into tessellations—hexagons and fracturing fans—that map the tug of slow expansions and contractions. In sheltered basins, powder-fine ash has accumulated into drifts as soft as sleep, through which pathways of compacted grit run like calligraphy. The air tastes of iron and ozone, of baked salt and something sharper, metallic, like the memory of engines.

This is also a city of sorts, or what a city becomes when it learns geology. Settlements cling to the terraces as if mortared there by gravity itself. Towers of riveted basalt and heat-blackened alloy rise on spindly legs, their feet braced against thermal cracks that wander across the plain like old lightning. Conduits snake along cliff faces, painted in the colors of warning and function, their surfaces mottled with bloom and scorch. Platforms stack above platforms, each level a deck, a factory floor, a roost, a terrace garden of silica-lichen and captured dew. Bridges of cable and spar stitch across canyons, swaying with a rhythm that matches the deep pulse of the machines below.

Industry hums in the marrow of the city. Foundries set in the lee of scarps vent pale flames that rise straight and thin, like needles stitching sky to earth. Grinding plants, vast as districts, chew ore into glittering rivers of sand that are sorted by magnetic song and color. Cooling fins radiate heat in waves that make the air waver; at night, they glow like embers banked for tomorrow. Pneumatic tubes hiss and sigh along their runs, carrying powders and pellets through a circulatory system that knits the city to the plain. Waste heat melts the margins of ice-analogue veins that thread the deep rock, turning them into slow, glittering gutters that trace maps of exhaustion.

Fauna moves through this place as if it were a tide that learned to walk. Beasts with limbs like jointed cranes pace the rims, their hides plated with ceramic and scar tissue, their eyes lidless and bright. They graze on mineral accretions and lick the salt-crusts that sweat from warm seams. In the city’s undercrofts, smaller multitudes scuttle—chitinous, many-limbed, their shells polished by passage—hauling loads, harvesting the fallout of industry, threading through ducts with a patience that mirrors the city itself. A few kinds have learned to read the city’s rhythms, appearing at gates when shifts change, vanishing when storms of static climb the slopes. Their calls are soft, more vibration than sound, a language of knocks and hums that settles in the chest.

Strangeness seeps from the seams of things. Along certain faults, light congeals into curtains that drift without wind, refracting images of places that may or may not be behind you. Machines sometimes finish tasks before they are begun, their records showing a smear of cause and effect tilted like a slope. Patterns appear in the dust overnight, geometries too perfect for wind or footfall, and in the morning they are gone, abraded into sense by the slow turning of the world. The fauna understand this, in their way; they avoid certain plazas at certain hours, and no one trains them to do it.

Sound is a luxury here. The dominant note is the low thrum of the city’s heart, felt more than heard, a vibration that travels up through boots and bedrock alike. Beyond that, there is the whisper of grit over grit, the crisp snap of cooling metal, the occasional long groan of a beam settling into its own weight. When the wind finds a canyon tuned to its pitch, it produces a note that hangs in the air for minutes, bending other sounds around it. At night, the quiet deepens into a kind of clarity, as if the world has lowered its voice to tell itself a secret.

Rules here are the rules of patience and balance. Heat rises, cold descends, and between them lies a band where life and machines conspire to linger. What is built must be maintained, because the land yields nothing easily and reclaims slowly but thoroughly. Energy is currency, drawn from geothermal taps and the planet’s own faint magnetic tides. Time is measured in the thickness of a patina, in the slow migration of a dune across a threshold, in the number of cycles a bearing can turn before it asks for rest.

At the farthest edge of the plateau, where the steps finally end and the world drops into a haze of distance, a line of pylons marches into the unmarked expanse. Beyond them, the plain continues, paler and simpler, its features erased by scale into suggestion. If you stand there long enough, the horizon seems to lean in, as if offering a trade: give up your urgency, it says, and I will show you how to last.

The city breathes. The plain remembers. The warm, thin air carries both forward, grain by grain, beat by beat, into a future that is not so much ahead as it is already underfoot, waiting for its turn to be worn into being.
