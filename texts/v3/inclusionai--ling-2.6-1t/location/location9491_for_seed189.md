The hollow is a lung no larger than a chapel, cupped beneath rock that still remembers being mud. It breathes by swelling: every few minutes the walls soften, the floor heaves like a mattress waking, and the whole cavity tightens its grip on the damp air. What looks like stone is a living rind—veined, corky, salt-crusted bark that has learned to imitate geology. Vegetation here is not decoration but masonry and weather both. It arches overhead in interlocked vaults of petiole and tendon, fibrous beams that creak when they shift, exuding a resin that smells like bruised mint and old copper. Water is a guest, not a resident: it beads on waxy leaves, condenses overnight into trembling lenses, and is promptly drunk. Yet moisture lingers in the form of sound—the place is loud with it.

The roar is hydraulic and vegetal. When the chamber contracts, sap forced through helical capillaries whistles like tea-kettles tuned to different keys. When it expands, cell walls pop and hiss, releasing clouds of warm vapor that catch the normal, sourceless light and turn it opalescent. The air tastes of warm rain about to happen, though none falls. The temperature hovers at that border where skin flushes without sweating, a constant low fever that keeps every surface tacky. Because erosion is young, nothing has yet surrendered to roundness; edges are crisp as snapped celery, ridges fresh as torn ligament, and the floor is a carpet of bristling undergrowth that parts reluctantly underfoot, then slowly, visibly, closes behind you.

Fauna are rare and skittish, as if borrowed from somewhere else and still deciding whether to stay. Beetles with shells like flint click across hollow stalks. Something that might be a lizard, but whose scales are arranged like overlapping petals, darts along a vein the width of a forearm, then vanishes into a sphincter of folded leaf that seals itself with a sound like a cork. Most life here is vegetative predation disguised as architecture: pillars that thicken toward the top to bear their own weight, arches that sway to pump water upward, ceilings that sweat and then drink themselves dry in cycles. The ecosystem is less a web than a single organism learning how to be many.

Scale is hidden in the way distances fold. From the center you can touch both walls with outstretched arms, yet the space feels deeper than it is because the vegetation crowds inward, layering itself like a throat. Density is near-void not in matter but in interruption—long clearings between stalks that make the eye feel untethered, spaces where light pools like spilled milk. Nothing is built, yet everything is arranged with intent: handholds braided from liana, shelves grown to hold cups of resin, ledges that warm in rhythm with the chamber’s pulse. Tech is primitive not because it is simple, but because it is bodily: tools are grown and shed, blades of silica honed on bark, cords woven from veins that tighten when damp.

Weirdness accumulates at the edges of perception. Sometimes the light doubles, as if a second sun flickers behind the membrane of the world. Stalks repeat themselves in echoes that are not visual but tactile—you feel a leaf brushing your shoulder a second before it happens. Water runs uphill for seconds at a time, obeying gradients of thirst rather than gravity. The sound rises to a pitch that loosens teeth, then drops into a bass thrum that vibrates in the marrow. Rules here are provisional: mass is negotiable, direction is persuasive, time is a texture. The hollow can decide, for a while, to be several places at once, stacking like cards that refuse to stay flat.

To stand still is to be changed. The bristles underfoot learn the shape of your sole and send up pale shoots that taste your sweat. The walls remember your passage by thickening where you leaned, by leaving a scar of sweeter sap. When you leave—if you leave—the chamber sighs and settles into a new geometry, already forgetting you, already preparing for the next damp inhalation, the next loud unfurling, the next brief, brilliant insistence that it is not a place at all but a process wearing a room like a temporary skin.
