The place begins as a sky that forgets it ever needed rain.

It is a high plain lifted into thinner air, an open bowl of mineral skin that stretches far enough to make the horizon feel like a decision rather than a limit. There is no water, not even in memory: no dew, no underground pulse, no salting tide. Instead, the ground exhales a dry chemistry that glints like cooled slag. The surface looks as though it is deciding what kind of stone it wants to be, erosion so young that edges still remember being liquid. Ridges are half-whittled, valleys half-uttered, as if the land were rehearsing its own geology.

Vegetation has arrived anyway, lush in a way that feels imported, almost out of place. Towering stalks and broad-leaved crowns grow not from soil but from mineral veneers and crusts that behave like slow-setting plaster. Their roots do not seek moisture; they seek structure, threading through newborn fractures and holding the raw earth in a gentle, green grip. The plants hum with internal pressure, warm to the touch, their surfaces furred with industrial dust that settles like a second skin. The warmth is steady, a thermal plateau that never spikes or falls, as if the air has agreed to pause its moods.

Silence is a thin but sturdy thing here. It does not press; it waits. When the rare sound comes, it is small and precise: the click of expanding plates, the soft rasp of mineral skin stretching, the distant, regulated sigh of engines. Industry is woven into the land without being built upon it. Factories are less structures than habits of motion—conveyor belts that are also roots, stacks that exhale refined light, turbines that spin like weather patterns given metal purpose. Electrified veins run under the surface, luminous and calm, pulsing on a rhythm that is less mechanical than geological. The machines do not impose; they acclimate.

The scale opens up without crowding. Density is low; space stretches between presences like a held breath. You can walk for long minutes and meet only your own shadow, or a scaffold of green and iron that is more process than thing. Fauna have not arrived, and in their absence, motion is taken up by other actors: pumps that breathe, lifts that climb like slow vines, lights that bloom and retract as if they were organs.

Weirdness is ordinary here. Light is normal, but it behaves politely, as if it knows the rules of the place and follows them with interest. Nothing decays because nothing has finished forming; nothing rots because there is no water to betray. Time is flat, a table without tilt, so that change occurs without accumulation, like a conversation that repeats but deepens. The land is in equilibrium, but it is a living sort of stillness, the pause between syllables in a word that is still being coined.

If you stand long enough, the place begins to feel like a room that is also a machine that is also a garden. The high air tastes metallic and sweet, as if it has been scrubbed by industry and rinsed by warmth. The plants seem to know they are the only softness in a world that is still hardening, and they grow with a kind of generosity, offering shade where there is no sun to avoid, offering color where there is no eye required.

This is a landscape that is building itself while pretending it has always been this way, a quiet, open, industrial garden suspended in thin air, waiting for the next instruction that will never come, and thriving anyway.
