Cold settles here as a kind of sediment. It is not the brittle, flashing cold of high places but a deep-soil, mineral-held chill, the kind that first numbs the skin and then sinks into the marrow of stone. The region lies below surface, yet not so far as to forget the sky—just enough depth to let the air thicken, to let breath plume and linger like slow smoke. What reaches down from above is mostly rain that has forgotten how to fall all at once: a persistent damp that seeps, condenses, beads on hair-thin mosses, collects in thin lenses between root-mats and clay. Water is a rumor more than a river, but it is a rumor told loudly.

Because the plants have taken everything, or nearly everything.

Vegetation here is not decoration but substrate, load-bearer, scaffold and skin. The landscape is a cathedral of green-black tissue rising from a floor that is less ground than loam grown dense with itself. Stems as thick as old pines but spongy and veined with pale traceries rise in sparse columns, their crowns knitting into a vault far overhead. Between them, space is not empty; it is held open by tension, by the slow pressure of growth against growth. Vines as rigid as cable bridge the gaps, and broad leaves layered like slate shingles channel the sparse drip of condensation into seams that thrum with trapped moisture. The plants are older than the shapes they make, older than the cold, older than the idea of being observed.

The place smells of bruised fern and iron-rich mud, of cellulose warmed slightly by the friction of its own expansion. Underfoot, the ground is a springy mat of compressed roots and decaying shoots that exhales when stepped on, releasing a bubble of damp air. There is erosion, but it is young—edges are still sharp where new stems have split old stone, where water has just begun to memorize the easiest routes downward. Rock outcrops, half-swallowed, wear collars of moss that look almost like cuffs.

Sound is the one thing that is not sparse. It is deafening, not in the way of alarms but of pressure: the groan of tissue expanding, the wet pop of sealed chambers giving way, the susurrus of millions of leaves shifting a fraction of a millimeter all at once. Somewhere deeper, pumps and engines keep time, their rhythm absorbed and reframed by the living walls until machinery sounds like biology. A low industrial thrum travels through the floor, up the legs, into the teeth. Occasional vents cough steam that is instantly swallowed by green, and the hiss is stretched into a long, wavering note by the vault above.

Human presence is faint, scattered. Outposts cling to sturdy trunks like lichen: huts of corrugated metal and resin-sealed wood, ladders spiraling into platforms where gauges blink behind condensation-clouded glass. Pipes snake along the surface, silver against black bark, insulated and sweating. Some structures are ruins already, buckling as the plants slowly reclaim their lines. A single chimney vents pale plume into the canopy, where it is caught and spun into damp braid before descending again. Built things here do not dominate; they negotiate.

Fauna is rare, and when it appears it is tentative—pale beetles that navigate by vibration, a bird with oil-slick feathers that darts between columns and is gone before its call can settle. Tracks appear overnight in the soft margin where floor meets stem, then vanish as the mat swells back into place. Most movement is vegetal, most life is the kind that does not run.

Light is dim, filtered through layers of living matter until it arrives as a bruised green glow, stronger when a stray beam finds a gap and pours in like honey. In that light, dust motes and spores perform their slow choreography. The air is breathable but heavy, carrying the metallic tang of distant engines and the sweet rot of healthy decay.

The ecology obeys a patient logic. Growth here is incremental but inexorable, working on a scale that makes years feel like breaths. Water moves from condensation point to root to leaf to air and back again, cycling slowly, hoarding its small abundance. Where industry meets organism, strange compromises bloom: rust blooms in intricate patterns that resemble flowers; oils are metabolized by pale molds that glow faintly when disturbed; wires are braided into stems and conduct signals alongside sap.

Rules are felt more than stated. Nothing is permitted to stay still for long. Even the cold is a participant, contracting materials, tightening joints, making the groan of structure a daily language. Change is slow, but it is cumulative; the valley-size expanse shifts its posture over decades, opening clearings that last a season before being stitched shut again. Space is held in careful balance between density and openness, between the sparse columns of living architecture and the echoing hollows between them.

To stand here is to be inside something that lives at its own pace and at its own volume, something that remembers the damp, the chill, the press of industry, and the vast patience of green. It is a place where the roar of the world is shaped into a living hush, and where the cold is not an absence but a note held long and deep inside the breathing dark.
