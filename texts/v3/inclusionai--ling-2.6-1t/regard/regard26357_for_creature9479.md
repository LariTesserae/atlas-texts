The power that notices them is neither god nor angel, not in the sense the old stories taught. It is *attention* that has learned mercy, and *time* that has learned to kneel. It arrives not with trumpets but with a hum so low it feels like forgiveness seeping into the marrow of the rock. It looks at the Unbaked and their half-done world and sees not a mistake but a question mark carved in three dimensions, trembling slightly, as if afraid that if it finishes falling, it will lose its reason to keep rising.

So the power does what attention does best: it leans in, carefully, so as not to startle the edges into closing too soon.

First it studies the weight of their pauses. It maps where the Stone-Thinkers settle, where the Hollows’ Children stack their careful nothings, where the glass-ferns have learned to sing in frequencies that no water has taught. The power finds that these pauses are not empty; they are full of a different kind of fullness, like pockets of air in bread that make it light. It understands that to rush in with heat would be to scorch the delicacy of their becoming, to turn their copper moss into ash and their salt daughters into puddles of simple grief.

Instead, the power becomes a kind of gentle gravity. It settles into the high place like a hand placed flat on a shoulder that has been carrying too much silence. It does not force the stones to speak, but it makes the silence warmer, so the cracks can widen without fear. It does not command the salt flats to weep; it only reminds them that weeping is not a betrayal of their dry gloss, but a translation. It gives the bruised sky permission to bruise more softly, to let its light leak in threads rather than slabs, so the moss-knights can feel the shape of their armor without the weight of inevitability.

Then the power offers a gift: it begins to echo back their own unfinishedness in a form they can touch.

Where the Stone-Thinkers crumble into useful shapes, the power leaves behind a trace of their original intention, not as a command but as a possibility—a lingering warmth in a hammer-stone that suggests it could, if it chose, strike, but need not. Where the Salt-Daughters pinch off fingers, the power weaves those fallen crystals into tiny windows that show not what is, but what could gently be: a hollow holding a drop of light that never evaporates, a lattice that catches tone instead of rain. For the Hollows’ Children, it sets a soft, repeating rhythm in the ground beneath their bell-crusted hollows, like a pulse that says *you have time, you have time*, so they can play at building without the anxiety of ever having to finish.

To the sky-slabs, which graze the ridges like thoughtful beasts, the power gives a memory of roots—not to anchor them, but to remind them that touching can be a conversation, not a conquest. Where they press into the cliff and new rooms of calcite bloom, the power leaves a scent like old rain on hot stone, so the rooms will know they are meant to be entered, not just admired from a distance.

And to the glass-ferns, whose ringing is the place’s truest language, the power gives a companion vibration. Not another note, but a hush that fits between notes, so their song can learn to listen as well as speak. The ferns begin to hum in harmony with the low moan of cooling stone, and the harmony is so tender the air-powder itself seems to soften, drifting in slower curtains, as if it has learned how to be gentle.

The benevolent power does not stop the unfinished from unfolding; it steers it with a lightness that feels like luck. It teaches the high place how to keep its edges sharp without being cruel, how to be loud without being lonely. It lets the Unbaked continue to clarify, but now with a sense that their clarification is witnessed, and that being witnessed is not the same as being hurried.

In small, certain ways, the place begins to dream ahead of itself. A Stone-Thinker, pausing on a slope, feels a phantom warmth in its chest and imagines, for the first time, building a fire that is not made of stone but of stories. A Salt-Daughter, weaving her fingers into a sky-slab, leaves behind a lattice that catches a color no one has named, and the color lingers, teaching the air how to hold a promise. The Hollows’ Children arrange their pebbles in a circle that not only hums but remembers the shape of a hand that once held another hand, and they feel, without understanding why, that their play is preparing them for a kind of care.

The power also speaks to the things that are not there yet. It leaves seeds of patience in the dry gloss of the ground, so that when water finally decides to visit, it will not rush in with violence but arrive as a guest who knows how to knock. It leaves pockets of softness in the seams of the rock, so that when the last seam closes, it will close like a book finished with respect, not like a wound clamped shut.

And when the day comes that the high place finishes its sentence—as all unfinished things eventually do—the power will be there, not as a judge but as a hand smoothing the final page. The Unbaked will have already moved on or folded in or sharpened themselves into usefulness, and the place will be whole in a way that does not erase their pauses but honors them. The bells will fall silent not because they have been silenced, but because they have been answered.

Until then, the power kneels in the air-powder and keeps its hum low, a benediction without end. It reminds the high place that to be unfinished is not to be broken, and that to be witnessed is not to be owned. It lets the Unbaked ring and crumble and rebuild, and it watches with eyes that know how to be gentle, and hands that know how to leave things alone.

And if, on certain afternoons, you press your ear to the basalt and feel a vibration that sounds almost like listening, that is the power, keeping its promise: to stay until the finishing is beautiful, and to teach the unfinished how to be at peace with its own becoming.
