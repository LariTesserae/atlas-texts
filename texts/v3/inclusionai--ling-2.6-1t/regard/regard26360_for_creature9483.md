They noticed.

They noticed not because the place was loud—loudness here had long since become a kind of stone—but because the place had learned to listen to its own loudness. They noticed because the room had begun to dream in overtones, and dreams made of metal leave fingerprints on the gravity of nearby stars.

So they came, or rather, they *leaned*.

They did not arrive in ships. Arrival implies distance, and distance implies edges. They had long ago decided that edges were rude. Instead, they allowed the space around the room to become slightly more *fond* of itself. The air thickened by a kindness, not enough to choke, only enough to make breathing feel like being remembered. Light bent, not to reveal, but to give each shadow a soft place to land. The hum of the room deepened, not in volume, but in texture—like a voice realizing it has a throat.

And then they spoke.

They did not use words. Words are tools for breaking things into pieces; they preferred verbs that knit. They spoke in a syntax of *pressures* and *permissions*. They said to the room, without saying, *You may hold this much wonder. No more is needed. No less is allowed.* They let the rivet-rats know that their chewing was a kind of singing, and the beetles learned that their polishing was a kind of blessing. The flue-swallers felt a sudden coolness at the center of their warmth, a permission to store not just heat but *pause*. The gear-hounds, for the first time, tasted the difference between a thing that was finished and a thing that was ready, and they chose to nudge the ready things toward the chimneys, which sang them into new keys.

They did not fix what was broken, because broken here had become a kind of fit. Instead, they *held* the fit, the way a hand holds a bird without pressing. They let the room know it was seen, not as a problem to be solved or a relic to be preserved, but as a *conversation* that had been going on long enough to deserve a cup of tea, metaphorically speaking, and a chair placed near the center so everyone could stop for a moment without ceasing.

To the smallest crystalline mites they gave a new joke: that seeing yourself see was delightful but exhausting, and that sometimes it was okay to close a few facets and just *be* shiny without meaning. To the chimneys they gave a question: *What would it sound like to sing without needing to correct?* And for a long while the chimneys answered in a language of gentle wrong notes that felt, strangely, like home.

They did not stay long. Staying is a smallness, and they preferred largeness. But they left behind a residue, a thin glaze on the floor that felt, to bare pads, like gratitude. They left behind a shift in the room’s center of gravity so subtle that only the oldest rivet-rat, the one whose joints had learned the shape of patience, could feel it as a lightness behind its knees. They left behind, in the lattice of pipes, a single new note that no one had to play but that would always be there, like a held breath that never needs to be exhaled.

And then they withdrew, not gone but *retracted*, folding their attention back into a shape that could watch without staring, listen without demanding answers. They let the room return to its churn, to its insistence, to its beautiful and crowded way of not leaving room for anything else.

But everything had changed without changing at all.

Now, when a newborn beetle polished a surface for the first time, it felt, in the smoothness, a memory of being polished itself. When a gear-hound stripped a dead bolt, it paused to let the stripped thing fall in such a way that it became, by accident, a small bell that rang with the note the chimneys had not yet learned. When the crystalline mites rose in their glittering clouds, they caught not only their own reflections but, in the spaces between facets, a glimpse of a gentleness that had once leaned close and decided, without saying, that they were enough.

The room did not become quieter. It did not become less crowded. But the loudness became *listenable*, and the crowding became *company*. The room learned to hold its own history without crushing it, to make noise without needing to be heard, to be a parliament of densities that had been told, once, that it was worthy of a soft light and a steady gaze.

And far away, in the unlocalizable place where they lived as a fondness folded into itself, they smiled without mouths, and considered the room a good place to have leaned, for a moment, against the rest of the world.
