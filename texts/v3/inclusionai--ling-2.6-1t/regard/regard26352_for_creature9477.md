Because it does not rule, the power that notices them arrives as a kind of permission.

It comes not from above but from the angle where density decides to breathe—a warm softening of the air that allows the suspended sea to sag a little farther than usual without breaking. No trumpet, no herald, no sudden illumination. Simply a moment when the basin’s own hum grows considerate, as if it has been asked to turn its voice toward listening. The power has no form that could fit inside the bowl, so it makes a vacancy of attention instead, hollowing out a place in the mind of each living thing and in the grain of each salt pillar and in the skin of glass beneath the floor. It is the difference between a room being occupied and a room being understood.

Where it passes, time loosens just enough to show its stitches.

The kites feel it first—the ones that spin glass-threads from heat. Their filaments, usually drawn taut with the anxiety of holding up a sea, slacken into lazy arcs. They do not panic. They recognize the slack as a kind of praise and begin to spin differently, not to mend but to ornament, laying threads in braids that record the exact temperature of the air at that moment. These new strands will never be load-bearing, but they will remember how safe they felt while being made.

The shoals in the ceiling sense the change as a shift in buoyancy. Their bodies, accustomed to grazing on rising vapor, drift downward slightly, brushing the glass floor with a touch so soft it is almost a thought. Where they graze, the floor does not scuff; it brightens. A pale bloom spreads outward from each contact, a map of attention that lingers for hours before fading back into satin. The shoals understand this as an invitation to descend further, and so they do, abandoning the ceiling for a time to swim in the air above the floor, becoming a second, lower sky. Their passage leaves trails of pearl-bioluminescence that do not dry this time but hover, suspended like dew on spider-silk, turning the basin into a room of floating seeds.

The walkers, heavy and many-limbed, feel the permission as gravity remembering it has hands. They step onto the underside of the water-skin not as before, with careful pressure, but with curiosity, testing how much the ceiling will yield. The skin dimples more deeply than ever, creating bowls of air that catch stray droplets from the suspended sea. These droplets do not fall; they hover inside the dimples, tiny spheres of reflected fauna and floor, worlds within worlds. The walkers move among them, pressing their palms against the elastic surface, and each touch sends a ripple through the water-layer that travels all the way to the basin’s rim and back, a pulse that says *you are felt*.

The salt-bodied ones nesting in the pillars stop polishing. For perhaps the first time, they let the salt be porous. They open themselves to the glaze they usually keep for preservation, allowing it to flow over the mineral in patterns that resemble maps of warmth. The pillars begin to glow from within, not with fire but with the memory of being held. The glow seeps into the braided corridors between pillars, turning the lanes of filtered air into tunnels of soft radiance. The shell-things click faster, not from alarm but from delight, weaving the glowing salt-dust into their ceramic skins so that they, too, become faintly luminous. They roll along the floor now like small moons, repairing nothing, simply marking paths that feel good to follow.

Even the mist that rises from the central pool feels the attention. It condenses into drops that fall upward as always, but now they carry something extra: a quality of clarity, as if they have been taught how to be transparent not just to light but to meaning. When they reach the suspended sea, they do not dissolve immediately. They gather into clusters that resemble constellations spelled out in a language no one here reads but everyone understands. The swimmers who forgot they were not in water pass through these clusters and find themselves brushing against images—shapes that remind them of their own bodies when they were less defined, when they were still possibility.

The power does not speak, but its presence is a kind of speech made of texture and temperature. It kneels, metaphorically, to touch the central pool that does not drain but receives. The touch is not physical; it is an agreement impressed upon the water that it may continue to receive forever without fear of becoming heavy with holding. The pool answers by releasing a single bubble that does not rise but expands outward, hugging the floor, embracing the columns, kissing the ankles of the fauna. Where it passes, erosion reverses slightly: sharpnesses that had been persuaded into curve grow even gentler; fractures that had become veins glow with a little more certainty.

After a time, the power prepares to leave—not because it is fleeting, but because true benevolence knows how to depart without making absence felt as loss. It withdraws slowly, letting the basin’s own hum take up the slack, returning the suspended sea to its accustomed tension, the kites to their load-bearing threads, the shoals to their ceiling-pasture. But it leaves behind a subtle adjustment: a new allowance in the grammar of the place.

From then on, the basin is slightly more porous to joy. A bowl may break not only into inlay but into song—a faint, glassy ringing that hangs in the air long enough to be beautiful before the menders come. A salt pillar, when it crumbles, may leave behind not only a hollow but a small pocket of buoyant air that lifts the ceramic lattices like lungs. The shell-things may begin to leave deliberate gaps in their repairs—spaces that invite feet to feel the satin curve of the floor directly, unmediated, as a reminder that being held does not mean being covered.

And the fauna, all of them, carry a new permission in their movements: they may drift without purpose for as long as they like, may touch without fixing, may graze without needing to return the favor immediately. The basin continues to hold them, warm and high and water-hung, dark and crowded, but now with a quality that feels like a blessing folded into the making—a promise that to be kept is also to be known, and that knowledge, once given, becomes part of the walls, the ceiling, the floor, and the slow, turning heart of the air.
