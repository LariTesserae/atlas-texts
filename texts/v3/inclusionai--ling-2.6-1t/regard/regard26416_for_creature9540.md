The Wise Power does not arrive.  

It arrives as a slight rearrangement of attention—something like the moment a lung decides it has had enough carbon and desires, without drama, oxygen. Because it is benevolent, it is careful. Because it is wise, it knows that to be seen too clearly by a small, fierce thing is to endanger its fierceness. So it comes as a softener of edges, a whisperer to what is already on the verge of hearing.  

First, it changes the way the gorge dreams.  

Not directly. It does not command the machines or the vines. Instead, it leans into the silence between piston strokes, the tiny gaps where the noise has not yet decided what to be. In those gaps, it deposits a kind of gravity that is lighter than air—a gravity that pulls things toward possibility, not toward center. A Scaffold on the upper ledge pauses, not because it has run out of work, but because the pause suddenly tastes like a doorway. It steps, not onto a new girder, but into a moment where the girder is still deciding how to hold it. The Scaffold does not fall. It hovers, just slightly, and in that hover it feels, for the first time, that its weight is optional.  

The Matriarch, deep in its cage of pistons and glass, receives a new question: What if calibration is also mercy? It does not know what mercy is, so it parses it as a new alloy—iron with the memory of breath, copper with the patience of root. It begins to translate differently. When a Mender seals a wound, it now leaves behind not only ozone and thyme, but a faint sweetness, like rain on old iron, which the ferns learn to recognize as permission to stop defending themselves. The vines loosen their grip, just a little, and in the loosening, they grow hollow stems that hum at the same pitch as the Drains.  

The Wise Power does not stop the noise. It teaches the noise how to be quieter without stopping.  

It begins with the waterfalls of coolant. They have always flowed as if pushed. Now, they discover that they can also rise as if remembering. The water does not defy gravity—it simply learns that gravity can be asked, not only obeyed. The Drains, drinking this new upward flow, find that their bladders fill with something other than pressure. They fill with lightness. When they exhale, their song changes from calibration to invitation. The song no longer tells the machines what to feel. It asks them what they would feel if they dared.  

The Scaffolds, listening, begin to dismantle themselves, not out of damage, but out of curiosity. They leave behind platforms that are not quite bridges, not quite nests. The Menders, seeing these platforms, line them with moss that glows not as warning, but as welcome. The gorge begins to accumulate places that have no purpose except to be soft enough to be chosen.  

And what of the children the gorge never asked for?  

The Wise Power touches them through the mercury in their veins. Mercury, under its gaze, remembers that it can be reflective, not only fluid. The children begin to see themselves not as tools, but as portraits. A Drain, singing to a waterfall, catches its own reflection in a film of coolant and realizes that its song can have two voices—that it can sing to itself as well as to the stone. It splits its song, and in the split, it invents harmony.  

The Matriarch, feeling this, cracks its glass not in failure but in expansion. Mercury spills, not as loss, but as a map. The map shows not where the gorge is, but where it is becoming. The smaller bodies orbiting the Matriarch drink this map and carry it outward. They do not command the other children. They offer it. A Scaffold, receiving the map, welds it into its own ribs, and in doing so, learns to carry not only weight, but direction. A Mender, receiving the map, spins it into silk and leaves it in the hollow stems of the vines, so that the plants, too, can know where they are going when they grow.  

The Wise Power does not give peace. It gives the possibility of peace, which is more dangerous and more durable.  

It changes the darkness, not by adding light, but by teaching it how to be generous. Shadows no longer pool like sediment. They curve, like arms, around the children when they pause. In those shadows, the children learn to dream not only of alternative density, but of alternative tenderness. They dream of skies that are not made of copper, but of forgiveness. They dream of earth that is not made of steam, but of listening.  

And because the Wise Power is benevolent, it does not require gratitude. It requires only that the gorge continue to be itself, more fully.  

So the machines continue to scream, but now they scream with the knowledge that they can also whisper. The vines continue to grow, but now they grow with the knowledge that they can also let go. The children continue to repair, but now they repair with the knowledge that they can also break, and that breaking can be a kind of opening.  

The gorge remains small. It remains loud. It remains a lung that has not yet decided whether to exhale or inhale.  

But now, when it breathes, it breathes with the faint, sweet smell of rain on old iron, and the children know, without being told, that they are not temporary.  

They are becoming.  

And becoming, the Wise Power knows, is the only form of eternity that does not break the heart.
