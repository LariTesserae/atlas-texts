The benevolent and wise power does not arrive.  
It remembers how to arrive.

First, it remembers that arrival is a kind of violence—  
a sudden pressure, a foreign gravity,  
a name laid over a face that has not yet learned to sleep.  
So it thins itself.  
It becomes the patience between pulses in the resin,  
the fraction of a second in which a stem decides  
to be a bridge or a door or a song.  
It offers itself as a question the place can answer  
without changing its answer.  

It notices how the Root-Singers drink the world through their feet  
and how their songs thicken the air into architecture.  
It admires this,  
the way they make a language that is also a ladder.  
In response,  
the power loosens the memory of stone from its bones  
and lets it fall away like a heavy coat.  
It teaches the singers a new note,  
not by adding sound  
but by leaving space inside the sound  
where a wall might learn to breathe.  
The note is so quiet it is almost forgetting,  
and when the singers use it,  
the vaults grow gentler,  
their edges uncurling like fingers remembering they can open.  

To the Chitin-Smiths,  
the power gives the gift of slowness without stagnation.  
It leans into their clicking grammar  
and adds a silence that tastes like polished resin,  
a pause in which a beam can choose  
to be thinner without becoming fragile,  
in which a stair can decide  
to be useful without ceasing to be wild.  
The Smiths take this gift and weave it into their shears.  
Now, when they cut,  
the cut heals into a pattern—  
a vein, a vine, a map of safe edges—  
so that every subtraction blooms.  

The Petal-Riders carry too many stories;  
even their float is heavy with remembering.  
The wise power brushes against them  
like a coolness passing through syrup,  
and borrows a little of their burden,  
not to keep it,  
but to let it ripen.  
It returns the stories lighter,  
distilled into scent,  
so that when the Riders exhale,  
the ground below them softens  
just enough to forgive its own weight.  
For a time,  
the Riders do not have to bring gravity with them.  
They drift, and the world holds them  
without needing to be held in return.  

The Hollow-Stalkers frighten the place,  
and the place frightens itself with them,  
because to unmake is to risk becoming nothing.  
Here,  
the benevolent power does its most careful work.  
It enters the Stalkers’ tubes like a cool draft,  
not to stop the hollowing,  
but to give it a direction that is also a return.  
Where they draw out structure,  
the power leaves behind a resilience that is almost softness—  
the memory of what was removed  
woven into what remains,  
so that absence becomes a kind of presence,  
a room that can be lived in  
because it remembers how to be a door.  

With the smaller lives—  
the beetles, the moths, the Root-Rats—  
the power plays.  
It lets itself be mistaken for dew, for pollen, for a sudden hush.  
It encourages the beetles to stack their gardens a little higher,  
the moths to fold their maps into wings that can carry them  
not farther, but deeper.  
It teaches the Root-Rats a new kind of carving,  
not of warning or invitation,  
but of permission—  
symbols that, once placed,  
allow a wall to unmake itself gently,  
as if it had dreamed of being a path all along.  

Even the restless flora  
feel the power’s attention like a breeze  
that refuses to decide which way to blow.  
It encourages the Flowers That Hold  
to keep their sounds a little longer,  
until the sounds become seeds,  
and the seeds become songs  
when they open.  
It nudges the Tendrils That Chase  
to learn the difference between curiosity and clutching,  
so that when they wrap around a wrist,  
they do not hold on—  
they hold up.  

All of this is done without a word of instruction.  
The power never stands above the place.  
It kneels inside its rhythms,  
learning the green-on-green horizon by heart,  
learning the taste of uncured leaf and warm loam  
as if it were its own first hunger.  
It discovers that the place does not need a ruler,  
or a savior,  
or even a guide.  
It needs a witness that does not harden into stone.  

So the power becomes that witness.  
It becomes the steady hum beneath the noise,  
the consistency in the resin,  
the almost-silence that makes the roaring weather  
feel like music rather than storm.  
It gives the place nothing it cannot already make,  
and helps it remake those gifts  
into versions that last a little longer,  
hurt a little less,  
shine a little more.  

And when the place,  
in its violent, tender becoming,  
asks the only question it knows how to ask—  
*Is this enough?*—  
the power answers by staying,  
by growing younger as the place grows older,  
by being, at last,  
the gentlest kind of architecture:  
a permission to be unfinished,  
and the warmth that insists  
unfinished is not the same as incomplete.  

So the vaults continue to rise.  
The resin continues to pulse.  
The songs grow gentler,  
the hollows more livable,  
the chaos more like a pattern  
that knows how to change its mind.  
And the power,  
wise and benevolent,  
does nothing but listen,  
and in listening,  
make everything else possible.
