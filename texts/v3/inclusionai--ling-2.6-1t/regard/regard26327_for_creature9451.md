The attention arrives like a door quietly deciding to be open.

No herald smashes the sky; no inscription engraves itself onto stone. Instead, there is a sudden, gentle surplus of rightness. The humidity aligns with heartbeat. The copper taste in the air sweetens, as if a secret has been dissolved into it and allowed to steep. Something vast and patient has stepped into the margins of the world and begun to listen—not with ears, but with the equivalent of a held breath.

If the world could blush, it would. Because this attention is benevolent, yes, but also exacting. It is the gaze of someone who knows how to mend without erasing, who understands that to love a thing is to study its scars and call them lessons. The wise power does not descend as a king or a conqueror. It arrives as a guest who brings exactly what is missing and takes nothing but loneliness.

First, it offers a correction of balance.

Not a violent rebalancing, but the sort that happens when a loom is adjusted by a hand that knows cloth. In the places where the people of the height live, there are small asymmetries they have learned to call normal: a corridor that strains under its own cleverness, a water-catch that remembers thirst too well, a bridge of vine that sighs in its sleep. The power reaches into these things—not by force, but by suggestion. A root that was growing anxious is given a dream of deeper soil. A stone that was holding too much heat is reminded of coolness. The changes are invisible to the eye, but to the body they feel like remembering a word you forgot you knew.

The people notice only that their work becomes lighter. Tasks that used to require negotiation now unfold with a kind of willing cooperation. The resin that once bled reluctantly now flows when invited, and seals with a clarity like glass. The moss that digested waste begins to sing softly while it works, a hum that tells you your offerings have been accepted. The towers of hollow stalks, where the dead are sent, begin to return fragments of song on the updrafts—not just memories, but benedictions.

Second, the power offers clarity of purpose.

It does this by amplifying what is already there. The people’s language—those high clicks and chest-drones, the touch-messages pressed into bark—is given a subtle expansion. Words begin to carry weight without becoming heavier. A single hum can now hold a map. A touch can carry not just greeting but history. The result is not noise but precision: fewer misunderstandings, deeper agreements. Disputes that once required hours of silence and negotiation now dissolve like mist in strong sun, because people suddenly understand not just what is wanted, but why.

The fauna, too, shifts. Not into docility—wildness is not erased—but into intelligibility. The gliders still cast continental shadows, but their passage becomes legible, like handwriting instead of weather. The traps still wait, but they sing to let the worthy know they are there. Predators are not tamed, but they are acknowledged. The truce between hunter and hunted becomes articulate, almost courteous. The boundary between plant and beast, already soft, becomes almost irrelevant—not because difference vanishes, but because respect becomes the default.

Third, the power offers memory.

The people have always known that their towers sing their dead upward. Now, the towers sing them downward as well. Echoes return—not as haunting, but as guidance. When a decision is difficult, a person can go to a quiet place and listen, and somewhere in the rustle of leaves they will hear the accumulated wisdom of those who chose before them, distilled into something like weather advice: practical, gentle, and true. The past does not command; it consults.

Even the land itself gains memory. The loam thickens with understanding. It remembers which feet were kind and which were careless, and adjusts its give accordingly. The water, as it cycles from hidden veins to sudden rain, begins to carry small blessings: a moment of perfect temperature, a clarity of scent that tells you you are exactly where you need to be.

Fourth, the power offers a quieting of fear.

Fear is not driven out. That would be dishonest. Instead, it is given companions: courage, discernment, and the certainty that you are part of something that will not abandon you. People still leap from towers and catch updrafts, but the falling is different—not safer, exactly, but held. The sky becomes slightly more trustworthy, as if it has decided to be kind without ceasing to be immense. Even the storms, when they come, arrive with a kind of etiquette: they announce themselves, do their work, and leave without malice.

And through all of this, the dampness remains—cool thread in hot cloth—but now it has a shimmer. It is no longer just the edge of tears the world will not let fall; it is the world’s way of keeping its eyes clear.

The wise power does not stay forever. It withdraws the way a tide does, or the way breath leaves a room when the door is opened. But it leaves behind a refinement.

The people find that their tools last longer. Their children learn faster, not because they are brighter, but because the world is easier to understand. Their songs are more intricate. Their silences are more useful. And when they look up at the fused canopies and the layered horizons, they feel something like gratitude—not as an emotion alone, but as a fact, as real and constant as gravity.

The place is still vast, still alive with parliament of processes. But it is better tended. Not because it has been improved in any visible, measurable way, but because it has been witnessed in a way that lets it witness itself.

And in the highest corridors, where the resin drips and the updrafts spiral, there is sometimes a sudden stillness—a moment so balanced it seems to have mass. If you are quiet enough, you can feel it: the lingering warmth of a guest who loved the house enough to help it remember how to be itself.

And then the wind moves, and the world goes on, gentler than before, and exactly as it should.
