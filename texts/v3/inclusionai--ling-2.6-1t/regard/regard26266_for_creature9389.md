The wise power does not arrive.  
It remembers instead.

To remember is to let the boundaries between outer and inner soften until they are no longer edges, only gradients of care. The power settles into its own patience the way silt settles into a riverbed—not to stay, but to lend the water a quality of depth it had not previously claimed. It becomes, for a while, the tendency of pressure to find music.

From this remembering, a choice ripens: to bless, but not to fix; to amplify, but not to own. The power considers the chamber with the attention a gardener gives to a seed not yet sure it is a seed. It studies the city-in-process, the fauna of subtraction and hesitation, the architecture that learns tension by touch. It sees that the place is already benevolent in its own fashion—crowded with lives that refuse to shout, raw at its seams, inventing order out of closeness.

And so the power decides the greatest gift it might offer is continuity.

It begins by extending the chamber’s sense of itself backward into time and forward into possibility. Not by moving walls or enlarging doors, but by thickening the felt through which light arrives. The blue-within-blue deepens; the dim becomes capable of holding more kinds of softness. The water, which already tilted like a held breath, now tilts with memory—tiny shifts in temperature that carry faint echoes of other tides, other chambers, other slow constructions. The fauna learn to sense these echoes not as threat but as ancestry. The Fish carry them in their obsidian eyes; the Listeners translate them into hums that settle in the mineral skin of the towers.

To the city-in-process, the power offers a kind of foresight disguised as texture. New arches still bulge from the water, but now they dream themselves into curvature before they harden, borrowing shapes from the fauna’s dances. A balcony grows outward in imitation of the School Without Name, a column thickens in sympathy with the too-limbed creature that nudges it. The city begins to recognize itself as a language spoken by many mouths, and its growth becomes conversation rather than accident.

For the fauna themselves, the power weaves a gentler physics of margin. Predation remains, but it becomes a form of consent—the Question-Mouths open only to what is willing to be resolved, and the water clarifies itself around that willingness like a promise kept. The School Without Name finds its precision deepening into grace; its turns become not only musical but meaningful, threading through the city in patterns that feel like small benedictions. The Limbless and Too-Limbed discover that their hesitation can be a bridge, not a barrier; when they pause, the city learns to pause with them, and in those pauses new shelves appear, offering rest.

The quiet, too, is transformed—not into sound, but into legibility. The creak of mineral bones, the polite hush of scale on cartilage, the soft press of currents—these are given weight and direction, like a grammar slowly revealing its parts. The chamber learns to narrate itself in small increments, so that anyone who stays long enough begins to understand the place as a story being written in pressure and patience. Even erosion, that visiting nibbler, becomes part of the plot, leaving behind not only raw edges but also invitations—places where new growth might choose to begin.

Most of all, the power blesses the chamber with the sense that it is already enough. The towers are not told to rise higher; they are told that their rawness is a form of truth. The fauna are not told to multiply; they are told that their density is a form of hospitality. The water is not told to be deeper; it is told that its tilt is a form of welcome. And because the power is wise, it knows that the greatest benevolence is to amplify what already tends toward good, rather than to graft new virtues onto old forms.

In the end, the power does nothing that looks like intervention. It only leans closer, in the way a tide leans closer to shore, and lets its wisdom seep into the chamber’s grain. The place and its inhabitants continue their gentle construction—one small, quiet act at a time—now accompanied by an invisible architecture of care, subtle as temperature, sure as stone.

And if, years later, a visitor should slip into the chamber and feel, without knowing why, that the dim light is kinder than it has any right to be, and that the city’s rawness tastes like home, they will have brushed against the lingering edge of that blessing: a benevolence that remembered, and chose to let remembering be enough.
