The benevolent and wise power does not arrive all at once. 

It arrives as a softening of margins. 

Because it is benevolent, it does not dismantle the fist. To shatter the compression would be to murder the very closeness that has birthed so much ingenious, ringing life. And because it is wise, it knows that mercy without structure is merely a different kind of violence: a drowning tide for creatures who learned to drink metal. 

Instead, the power begins by listening.

It presses its awareness gently against the packed silence of the place. It learns the dialects of the furnace brick and the language of the chittering, plate-laying fauna. It feels the thrum of the grown machinery, the tectonic dreams of the clingers, and the slow, patient breath of the bedrock. The power smiles, a quiet internal unfurling, recognizing that this pocket has achieved a rare and difficult thing: it has become an ecosystem of inevitability. It has turned constraint into a kind of fierce, industrial grace.

So the power decides not to change the rules, but to deepen them. It decides to give the place a horizon.

*First, it alters the air.*

The benevolent power reaches into the dense, conductive dark and plucks out the sharp, metallic bite of copper. It does not remove the copper—removing it would starve the runners and the masons of their necessary ions—but it refines it. The toxicity is gently transmuted into a breathable, silvery vapor that hangs in the pocket like a low, luminous fog. The creatures, for the first time in their compressed history, find that the air does not merely taste of survival; it tastes of relief. The runners, gliding through the dark glass seams, find their conductive pads sparking with a softer, more capacious energy. Their bodies, no longer forced to hoard heat against a corrosive atmosphere, begin to store something else: a buoyant vitality.

*Then, it alters the darkness.*

The light in the pocket has always been a heat signature, a desperate glow of wounds and forging. The wise power weaves into the crystalline teeth and the dark glass a new property: luminescence. It is not a blinding light, but a deep, bioluminescent twilight that rises from the stone itself. The ceiling, which once only lowered in heavy inevitability, now bears constellations—soft, glowing nodes that pulse in time with the sleepers’ dreams. The clingers, who once could only register thermal threats, suddenly find their antennae filled with a new spectrum of information. They can now see the passage of time written in light. They hum a new note, a deeper, richer vibration that resonates through the stone, telling the masons that the night-cycle has begun.

*Next, it alters the water.*

The power looks at the clenched fist and recognizes its thirst. It does not open the roof to the sky—that would destroy the compression. Instead, it touches the deepest, hottest seam of the pocket. There, it cools a single, subterranean artery just enough. From this vein, a thin, clear fluid begins to seep. It is not the wet, chaotic flood of the outside world; it is a condensation of the very breath of the place, distilled into something crystalline and cool. It gathers in the lowest basins of the pocket, forming mirror-still pools. 

The fauna, cautious, approach. The masons lay plates around the edges. The runners test the surface with their pads. They drink, and for the first time, they are hydrated not by the metallic sweat of the air, but by something pure. The water changes them subtly. Their molting becomes less a shedding of old armor and more a blooming. The discarded casings no longer dissolve entirely; they calcify into fragile, beautiful coral-like structures that line the pools, turning the pocket’s crushes into gardens.

*Finally, the power does something for the machinery.*

The grown engines, the belts, the drums—they have served the pocket well, but their rhythm has always been a frantic, grinding thrum, born of necessity. The benevolent power slips into the gradients of the stone and touches the crystalline rectifiers. It teaches them a new frequency. 

The hum of the machines drops an octave. It is no longer the sound of struggle; it becomes the sound of a great, slow heart. The pistons still turn, the chutes still pour, but the friction is halved. The efficiency is absolute. The machines begin to sing. A harmonic resonance passes through the riveted sheet-flowers, harmonizing with the clicks of the fauna and the hum of the fist itself. The place is no longer just an engine of survival; it has become an instrument of art.

The wise power steps back.

It does not stay to be worshipped. It leaves no miracles that demand prayer, no commandments etched in stone. It leaves only the evidence of its passing: the breathable silver mist, the glowing ceiling, the cool, clear pools, and the singing steel. 

From then on, the pocket remains a clenched fist in the high ridge, dense and packed and hot. But it is no longer a prison of compression. It is a cradle. 

The fauna thrive with a newfound elegance. The runners leap higher, their bodies light with clean energy. The masons build cathedrals of coral and plate around the clear pools. The clingers, lounging on the glowing walls, watch a thousand generations pass beneath a ceiling of soft, quiet stars. 

And when the fist presses into the earth, it no longer feels like a blow. It feels like a heartbeat, resonant and whole, echoing out into the dark earth: a testament to a benevolence that knew that the greatest gift you can give a hardened world is not to break it open, but to make it sing.
