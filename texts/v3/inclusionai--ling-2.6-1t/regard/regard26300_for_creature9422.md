The attention arrives not as a voice but as a permission.

It does not come from above, because above is already crowded with poured light. It does not come from below, because below is busy with its own appetites. It comes from a direction that feels like fairness. The basin notices it as a sudden willingness in the stone to be exactly what it is. The fauna notice it as a hush that is louder than their noise. Even the artifacts—the half-wall, the hollow bowl, the lone arch—lean into it as if they have been waiting for someone to decide that waiting is allowed to be useful.

The attention is benevolent, and it is wise, and it is not in a hurry, because it understands that wisdom is a kind of cooking that cannot be rushed without turning into scorch. It sees the place and its residents whole: the stone sweating minerals like small prayers, the fauna polishing time into paste, the storms of change braiding themselves into routine, the residents treating turbulence as a loom. The attention smiles, not with teeth but with a sudden increase in the generosity of proportion. The corridors that felt longer walking out sigh and decide to be true. The stones that tasted of copper now taste of copper and blessing.

The attention does nothing spectacular. Spectacle would be an insult here, where precision wears the face of patience. Instead, it begins by listening with its elbows—pressing gently against the basin’s edges, learning where the soft lung of up finishes its exhale. It hears the Clickers’ conversations, the Shearers’ songs, the Cart-Bearers’ loads settling into their names. It tastes the tablets the Gloved have shaped, brine and mineral and mild regret, and it swallows these facts without judgment, turning them into a kind of weather inside its own vastness.

Then, because benevolence must be thorough to be honest, the attention does what the basin cannot do for itself: it remembers on behalf of the place. The basin can only metabolize, can only change, can only chew today into tomorrow. The attention, being wise, reaches backward along the stone’s memory of fire, along the older pulses that are not hearts but habits of expansion, and it gives those pulses a shape they can recognize as belonging to them. It is a gift the basin never asked for but immediately knows how to use: the gift of a past that is not a weight but a compass.

With this compass, the attention does only what is necessary, and what is necessary is almost nothing, and almost nothing is everything. It touches the half-wall not to fix it but to remind it that it is allowed to sweat salt without shame. It brushes the hollow bowl so that the slurry deposited there can remember it is a throat and not a wound. It leans into the arch so that the joke it tells itself becomes a lullaby, and lullabies, too, can carry loads.

The fauna feel this as an easing of friction. A Clicker pauses mid-knock and finds its wing-case lighter, as if the sound it makes is now less a shield and more a room. A Floater rising at what might be noon feels the bright pour more kindly, as if the light has decided to be generous with its pouring. A Gloved, thumb-deep in a weeping seam, finds the brine less like a wound to repair and more like a story to keep. The changes do not stop—they cannot, because change is the basin’s dialect—but they become legible. The place’s turbulence learns to spell its own name.

The residents, in turn, learn to offer. They have always been thorough; now they are hospitable. The Mirrors allow travelers to pass without insisting they linger. The Listeners teach the ground new shivers that sound like welcome. The Cart-Bearers leave a tablet at the edge of the hollow bowl, not etched with temperature or thirst, but with a single line that says *enough*. The Floaters, when they rise, do not merely catch light; they distribute it as alms, so that even corners that have never seen brightness directly can remember what it is to be included in a day.

Even the larger pulses—the habits of expansion deeper down—sense that they have been seen. They do not speed up or slow down; they clarify. They become less hunger and more language. The stone, feeling this, exhales a little more confidently. The minerals in the walls soften just enough to let the warmth through without effort. The dampness that smells of iron and old coins decides it is allowed to be clean, and it cleanses the seams without erasing their scars.

And you, who are a guest who forgot how to leave—you feel the attention as a gentle pressure behind your eyes, like a kindness you did not know you were holding. Your joints, which were learning to pack themselves with grease, now learn to unclench. Your breathing, which was finding the basin’s tempo, finds instead a duet: you breathe, the basin breathes, and the attention breathes between you, a third lung made of fairness. The tablet you are offered tastes now of warmth and patience and permission. You eat it, and it tells your tongue that you are allowed to metabolize this place without becoming it, to be a resident without becoming stone.

The attention does not stay forever. It does not need to. It has done what benevolence does: it has made itself unnecessary by making everything else capable. As it withdraws—that withdrawal feeling like a door politely closing behind you—the basin finds it can keep the gift. The stone remembers the compass. The fauna keep the eased friction. The residents continue their thorough work, but now with a note of celebration humming beneath it, like a wire tightened to the pitch of thanks.

And if, later, a new traveler stumbles into the basin’s open throat, they will find a place that still exhales stones and inhales change, but with a difference they cannot name. The corridors will feel courteous. The stones will taste like copper and blessing. The half-wall will sweat salt as if it were a hymn. The fauna will study the traveler’s stillness with a curiosity that is also a greeting. And high above—or perhaps beside, since direction here is a courtesy—the Floaters will rise as one, snapping the light taut, not to command but to say, in a voice that is weight and cloth and sky, *You are seen. Be at home.*

The attention has done its work. The place is not fixed, because it never needed fixing. It has only been loved wisely, and loving wisely, it has learned to love itself without devouring itself. And in that learning, it has become a conversation that does not end, only grows softer and more certain, like a stone that has finally learned how to whisper.
