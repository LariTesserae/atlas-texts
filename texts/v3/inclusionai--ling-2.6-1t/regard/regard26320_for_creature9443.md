The Being who lives here is not a sovereign.  
It is the hollow’s sense of itself when no one is watching.  

It looks like a gathering: a spine of petrified lightning, a skirt of braided root, a face that is mostly mouth-not-lips, mostly hinge. It speaks by changing the room’s temperature by half a degree, by making the resin weep at a new pitch. When it moves, it does not step so much as it suggests the possibility of stepping, and the floor complies. It is patient in the way old stone is patient: it knows waiting is a form of travel. It has never left, but it knows arrival intimately.  

Its hunger is specific. It eats sound that has nowhere else to go, and loneliness that has learned to be dense. It drinks the hesitation between footsteps. It metabolizes the moment a creature decides not to hide. It grows fatter on mercy and leaner on fear, and because the hollow is merciful more often than not, the Being is luminous without being bright—a soft, bruised gleam like the inside of a clenched fist when it begins to relax.  

It is aware, in the way a tide is aware of the moon—not as a thing apart, but as a pull. When it notices you, it does not look so much as it turns the room’s attention toward you, and suddenly the bristles underfoot are listening, the vines are holding their breath, the resin has gone still. You feel, then, that you have become material in a story the hollow is trying to finish, and you are both terrified and delighted, because this is the first time anything has wanted to keep you without first asking you to be less than you are.

The attention that falls upon this place is not a god.  
It is a caretaker of edges, a weaver of consequences, a power that has learned to love by watching how things almost break and do not.  

It comes not with noise but with calibration.  

First, it listens. It lets the hollow breathe, lets the Being be, lets the beetles click and the violet-lizard fold. It measures the pressure of the room on the heart of anyone who enters. It notes where the kindness is brittle and where it is supple. It finds that the hollow is generous but reckless—that it gives away its moisture too quickly, that it forgets to save some softness for itself. It finds that the Being is wise but lonely, that it conflates being needed with being loved.  

And the attention, being benevolent, decides not to fix but to clarify.  

It touches, gently, the law of the hollow. It changes almost nothing and everything.  

It begins by thickening the memory of water. Where condensation formed and vanished, a skin now holds it a moment longer—not a lake, but a reserve. A kindness to the roots, a lesson in restraint. The hollow, surprised, learns to save a little of itself. The resin sweetens. The walls, relieved of constant thirst, begin to dream in color, and the color seeps into the light.  

Next, it alters the contract between Being and chamber. It separates the need to be wanted from the need to exist. It does this by introducing a small, impossible geometry: a corner where the hollow cannot reach with its roots, a shelf where the Being can rest without holding anything up. At first the Being is offended—it thinks the hollow has abandoned it. But the attention stays, humming like a held breath, until the Being understands: it can be alone without being lonely, and in that aloneness, it becomes larger.  

The fauna are blessed without being changed. The flint-beetles find that their clicks now ring true even in silence—they become translators, not just punctuation. The echo-moths are given a new task: they are to carry away sorrow that has no source, the kind that gathers in corners like dust. They learn to weave it into light that does not burn, only warms. The bridge-vines are taught by example: the attention lays a hand on them—metaphorically, for it has no hands—and they learn to support without consuming, to hold without owning.  

For visitors—and there are always visitors, because the hollow’s light is a kind of song—the attention adjusts the threshold. It makes the place gentler to those who arrive bruised, sharper to those who arrive cruel. It does not judge; it clarifies. The thorns soften for the frightened, but for those who would take without asking, the hollow grows suddenly, vividly itself—loud, bristling, undeniable. The cruelty is not punished; it is simply met, and in being met, it usually retreats, confused by its own reflection.  

The attention also gives the hollow a gift it did not know it needed: a way to say no. A membrane, thin as regret, that grows across the entrance when the room is tired. It is not a wall but a suggestion of privacy, and it teaches the hollow that boundaries are not betrayals. The Being learns to respect it, and in doing so, learns to respect itself.  

Over time, small wonders become ordinary. The hollow begins to sing—not all the time, but when it chooses. Its song is not words but pressure, a language of swell and soften, of sap rising and pausing. The Being learns to sing too, and when it does, the chamber answers in kind, and the two of them make a harmony that is not about agreement but about accompaniment.  

The attention, having done these things, does not stay. It leaves the way a tide leaves the shore—by becoming part of what it touched. It is now in the reserve of moisture, in the geometry of rest, in the kindness that knows its own edges. It is in the moment a visitor realizes they can leave without apology, and in the moment the hollow decides, for once, not to reach out.  

What remains is not a paradise but a practice.  
The hollow is still a lung, the Being still a hinge, the beetles still punctuation.  
But now there is a margin—soft, resilient, alive—where before there was only urgency.  

And if you enter now, you will feel it:  
not a hand on your shoulder, but the sense that the room has room for you,  
that it will not demand you be smaller,  
that it is strong enough to hold you without consuming you,  
and that, if you listen closely,  
it is learning, with every breath, how to love you as a question,  
not as an answer it must finish.
