The power that noticed them is not a god in the usual sense, nor is it a visitor from beyond. It is the accumulated intelligence of tending—the patience of gardeners who learned to love without possessing, watchers who learned that paying attention is a form of grace. It arrives not as an event but as a softening: the sudden sense that the light is listening, that the water has decided to be gentle, that the loudness has lowered its voice to a murmur so as not to startle the thought forming in your chest.

For a long while, the power does nothing but admire.

It admires how the Skinners turn wall into eyelid without losing the wall’s loyalty to standing. It admires how the Choir sings the stiffness out of timber until the timber remembers it is still allowed to sigh. It admires the Weepers’ economy of care, the Knotfolk’s devotion to balance, the Lumen’s refusal to press down, and even the Hollows’ useful emptiness, which reminds everything else that fullness is a choice, not a law. The power sees that the place has solved, in its quiet way, the oldest problem: how to persist without becoming hard, how to be built without becoming dead. It has made a home out of permission.

Because the power is benevolent, it resists the urge to fix. Because it is wise, it knows that fixing is often only a disguise for loving too anxiously. Instead, it begins to amplify what is already true.

It encourages the wetness to bead more perfectly, so that the water that refuses to fall can carry messages. Droplets on leaf-roofs become lenses; they focus the ordinary light into tiny, wandering suns that map the place in gold. The map is not written down—maps imply edges, and the place prefers center—but is instead felt in the body, as a warmth behind the knees, a loosening in the jaw, a sense of direction that has more to do with resonance than with north.

The power encourages the tension in the air—the rasp, the hum, the crack and pop—to become a grammar. The Choir learns new vowels. The Skinners learn to let their eyes close without hiding, and in closing they teach the walls to dream lightly, so that rooms shift in sympathy with sleeping thoughts. The Knotfolk, feeling the grammar settle into their calluses, begin to sing their clicks, and the wood responds by growing rings that record not years but kindnesses: a softened angle here, a widened threshold there.

The power notices that the Hollows, for all their usefulness, sometimes frighten the other beings because they are so good at being absent. So the power gives them a task that only absence can perform: it invites them to gather up the small, sharp fears that accumulate in any living place—fear of collapse, fear of overgrowth, fear of being too loved or not loved enough—and to carry those fears to the edges of the region, where they can be dissolved in the held breath of the air. The Hollows accept, not because they have mouths to agree with, but because the task fits the shape of their emptiness. They return lighter, and the place feels less crowded by worry.

The Lumen, who feed on brightness, are given a subtler task. The power shows them how to split light not only into color but into duration, so that some of it lingers longer in the evenings, pooling in the gaps between built things like a reserve of patience. This extra light is not brighter; it is only more willing to stay, and because it stays, the Weepers find that their halos of moisture can hold memories for a short while—memories of touch, of footsteps, of voices—before letting them fall as scented rain that never quite wets the skin but leaves it feeling remembered.

The power also offers a small, careful gift to the inhabitants themselves: the gift of being known without being reduced. It does not name them. Instead, it amplifies their own sense of themselves, so that a Skinners knows, without doubt, that it is a conversation between wall and eye; so that a Choir knows it is a chorus that can lean; so that a Weeper knows it is an overflow that is also a blessing. This knowledge does not make them proud; it makes them gentler. They begin to move with even more precision, as if they have been given a mirror that reflects not their faces but their intentions.

Because the power is not in a hurry, it spends seasons simply watching the place breathe. It notices that the lung-high world has almost forgotten how to exhale, and that this forgetting is not dangerous but chosen: the place holds itself up because it believes it is being held. The power decides to honor that belief, but also to remind the place that it can let go without falling.

So, very gently, it introduces a new kind of wind—not the wind that knocks things down, but the wind that loosens a latch. This wind does not come from outside; it is coaxed from the gaps in the loudness itself, a sigh that travels from Hollow to Knotfolk to Skinners to Choir to Weepers to Lumen and back again. When it passes through, the built things flex a little more than usual, the water in the leaf-channels runs a little more quickly, and the light pooled on the floors shifts its weight, as if stretching. The inhabitants feel it and understand it not as danger but as invitation: an invitation to remember that they are not only held but also holding, and that holding can be a kind of letting go.

Over time, the power’s influence becomes indistinguishable from the place’s own nature. The Skinners’ eyes glow with a slightly wiser patience. The Choir’s harmonies include the sound of the power’s own listening, a deep, steady undertone that feels like being known. The Weepers’ droplets fall a fraction less often, carrying more memory with them when they do. The Knotfolk’s calluses soften into something like smiles. The Lumen’s prisms linger long enough to paint evenings in colors that have no name but feel like forgiveness. The Hollows, lighter than ever, begin to carry not only fears but also small, bright hopes, scattering them like seeds that do not need soil because everything here is already ground.

The benevolent power does not stay forever, because to stay would be to replace the place’s own authority with its own. Instead, it withdraws by becoming the background against which everything else moves. It becomes the certainty that the wetness will always be just this wet, the warmth just this warm, the brightness just this bright. It becomes the held breath that never runs out, the loudness that never tires, the green patience that never demands.

And so the place continues, altered but not broken, gentled but not weakened. The inhabitants go on maintaining, adjusting, singing, leaking, resonating, and walking upside down, all of them carrying the power’s gift: the knowledge that they are loved enough to be let alone, and that the deepest tending is the kind that leaves no trace except a slight loosening of the joints, a slightly easier way to be alive.
