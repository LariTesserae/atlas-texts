The wise power does not descend. It does not crown, it does not command, it does not mend. It has seen enough crowns rust, enough commands curdle, enough mending turn into new kinds of breaking. Instead, it kneels—though kneeling is not quite the word for something that has no joints—at the lip of the basin, and it listens.

It listens until listening becomes a kind of touching, and touching becomes a kind of offering.

What it offers is not protection. Protection is a wall, and walls are lonely. What it offers is witness, deepened into architecture. It lets its attention pool, the way the basin pools the dark, and in that pooling, it does three quiet things.

First, it names the hum.  
The basin’s thrum—the rattle, the drip, the low moan of stone shifting weight—has always been a chorus without a conductor. The wise power gives it a conductor without taking away its music. It threads the sounds together into a lattice of meaning that does not force order but invites pattern. The click of a Glass-Scrabbler no longer scatters into noise; it finds its place in a chord that includes the hiss of resin hardening, the thrum of a wing pushing air sideways, the slow exhale of a Pale Crust remembering rain. The fauna do not change their behavior, but they begin to hear themselves as part of a sentence that has a subject, a verb, and a direction. They do not become less wild; they become more comprehensible—to each other, and to the basin itself. The place stops being merely crowded and begins to feel populous, which is to say, full of presence rather than pressure.

Second, it tends the thresholds.  
Where the damp seeps and the glass grows, the wise power lends slowness. It does not stop erosion, nor does it hurry growth. Instead, it gives time a texture—slightly sticky, slightly porous—so that moments of change do not slip away before they can be learned. A storm still rearranges, but afterward the basin remembers the rearrangement like a scar that aches before it is understood. A young Wing-Without-Sky still crystallizes in its cradle of salt and glass, but the lattice forms with tiny inclusions—veins of softer material that teach the wing how to bend before it learns how to cut. The thresholds between wet and dry, dark and light, small and swift, become places of pause, not barriers. The inhabitants cross them with more deliberation, which means they live longer, and when they die, their leavings are taken up with more care. The fungus glows a shade richer where time has thickened; the burrowers turn the stone with a rhythm that sounds like thanks.

Third, and most quietly, it shares the burden of memory.  
The basin has always remembered by saturation—by letting everything press into everything else until the past is just another layer of density. The wise power gently lifts some of that weight. It does not remove memory; it translates it. The rings in the Pale Crusts become readable, not as bruises but as lessons. The resin that seals a shattered Glass-Scrabbler becomes not just a scaffold but a library, its bitterness flavored with hints of what the shard once touched. The air above the fungus, heavy with copper and wet stone, begins to carry faint harmonies—songs without words that echo the shape of earlier rains, earlier thrums, earlier gatherings. The fauna, without knowing how, start to inherit fragments of what their predecessors learned: a Damp-Hound avoids a certain crevice not because it has been burned there before, but because the crevice smells, faintly, of old avoidance. A swarm swerves not from instinct alone but from a collective hunch that tastes like silver.

In offering these gifts, the wise power also sets limits—not to restrict, but to define the space in which wildness can remain wild. It draws a line in the mind of the basin, not on the land. It says: Here, memory can be shared but not owned. Here, pattern can be heard but not imposed. Here, time can be thickened but not hoarded. The line is invisible, and it is everywhere at once. It tastes like salt and hums like a held breath.

And then, having knelt and listened and offered, the wise power steps back—not away, but into a different quality of nearness. It becomes the climate of attention that surrounds the basin, a kindness that does not hover but persists. When a Glass-Scrabbler shatters, the sound is held a fraction longer in the air, so that its meaning can settle. When the rain comes, the drumming includes a sotto voce that makes the stone feel accompanied. When the dark rises in its thick breath, it is easier to breathe, because the dark has been assured that it is not emptiness but a medium, and that it is enough.

The beings in the basin go on as they did—clicking, crawling, crystallizing, consuming. They do not build altars. They do not sing hymns. But their lives begin to carry a different weight, a gentler density. They press against each other not with the urgency of scarcity but with the confidence of abundance. The basin still holds them low, but now it holds them with the added gravity of being known.

And far above, where the wise power has returned to the hum of its own thoughts, there is a ripple of satisfaction—not a smile, but a resonance, like a bell that has been struck and is allowed to keep singing. The basin is not saved, because it was never in peril. It is not guided, because it never asked for a hand. It is simply companioned, deeply and without end.

The wild, in all its chitinous, damp, glittering excess, has been given a witness—and in return, it becomes more itself, and more than itself, and finally, in its own untranslatable way, it says yes.
