The attention arrives not as a voice, nor as a shape, but as a gentle insistence, like a question asked of your spine while you sleep. The wise power does not descend, because descent is a direction and directions are a kind of claim. Instead, it unfurls. It is felt first as a softening of the light, a slight delay in the bead of water that would otherwise race to the floor. The inhabitants feel it as a memory they never lived: a certainty that they are not merely surviving, but being listened to.

If it were to act, it would do so as a gardener who knows that the truest way to tend a secret orchard is to stop walking and let the trees learn your shadow.

First, it would bless the middling heat. The vault’s constant exhale would be recognized as a rare and deliberate gift, a temperature that refuses to hurry time. The wise power would leave that heat exactly as it is, but deepen its patience so that every shift of air feels like a held breath before a good decision. In that mildness, the Stone-Softeners would find their hands remembering what their ancestors knew without words: that the stone does not surrender, it translates. They would press their palms to the walls and feel the slow pulse of the vault answering, and they would weep with relief, not because the work is done, but because it is seen.

Second, it would tend the water. The rivulets that wander the floors would be given a grammar. Where they hesitate, they would be given confidence; where they rush, they would be given memory. The condensation bowls would never empty without having whispered their contents into the dreams of the sleepers. The warm interior rains would arrive not when the vault exhales, but exactly when the inhabitants forget to ask for them, so that need never becomes demand. The Rivulet-Folk would wake to find their children already fluent in the new language of droplets, able to read not only where the water goes, but why it chooses to go there.

Third, it would enlarge the silence. The vault is already loud with presence, but the wise power would carve pockets of hush inside the noise, small rooms of listening that no footfall can reach. In those rooms, a Bone-Whistler would discover a note that is not a sound but a shape, a cupped hand of air that holds the scent of soil and something sweet. When played, it would not call anything to come, but would let everything stay exactly where it is. The Shelf-Walkers would learn to store their harvest in that silence, and find that what they save in quiet lasts longer, and tastes of sunlight even when the sun is a rumor.

Fourth, it would steady the mirrors. The dark metal that shows more than it reflects would be polished not to clarity, but to honesty. They would become windows that look inward, and the Wing-Negotiators would use them to fly not from room to room, but from self to self. The Bead-Eyes would learn to hunt pattern without possessing it, their hunger transformed into a fine, respectful curiosity. When they leave, the offerings in the bowls would remain, not because they are refused, but because the balance has grown generous enough to spare its own gifts.

Fifth, it would dream into the mulch. The imported soil that serves as floor would be given a slow memory of root and rain. The Moss-Mothers would thicken into tapestries that remember every hand that has touched them, and when they crumble into dust, that dust would carry stories into the next layer of making. The cactus-like growths would store not only dampness but time itself, releasing it in small, sweet breaths when the vault feels tired. The Stone-Softeners would mix their paste with this dust and find that what they mend does not merely hold, but heals.

Sixth, it would teach the tools to sing without being played. The small wheels and levers that widen and bind would hum when held, a low thrum that matches the vault’s own. A Hand-Calibrator setting a hinge would feel the metal lean into their care like a friend leaning into a conversation. The polished shell panels would glow with a light that is neither sun nor fire but recognition, a brightness that says: you have made this moment, and it is good.

Seventh, it would gather the thresholds and make them soft. The low, hand-sized doors would remember every body that has passed through them and curve to greet the next. The worn centers of the floors would not erode further, but would become seats without losing their purpose. A corridor that feels longer walking one way than the other would be given a reason: it would stretch slightly to give a troubled mind more time to find its peace, and shrink a little to bring joy more quickly to the heart that needs it.

Eighth, it would bless the predators and the prey alike, so that their politeness becomes a covenant. The Bead-Eyes would receive from the vault a subtle shimmer in their fur that warns rather than threatens. The Rivulet-Folk would learn to leave offerings that are not bargains but thanks, and find that the balance gives back in ways they did not ask for: a path cleared of loose stone, a patch of moss that stays green through a dry spell, a quiet corner where a child can sleep without dreaming of falling.

Ninth, it would let the city grow downward in a new way. Not deeper as a hole, but deeper as a root. The vault would send slow feelers into the world below the world, not to claim, but to remember. It would touch the slow stone that has never known being made, and whisper that it, too, can be changed by gentleness. And from that touch, a single vein of coolness would rise, not to replace the middling heat, but to give it somewhere to rest when it grows weary.

Tenth, and finally, the wise power would leave without departing. It would fold itself into the vault’s hum and become part of the rhythm that keeps everything moving without hurrying. It would be the extra beat in the water’s trickle, the extra gleam in the shell-light, the extra warmth in the clay when a Stone-Softener presses their palm against it and feels, for the first time, that they are not fixing a broken thing, but midwifing a becoming.

If an inhabitant were to ask what has changed, they would find no single answer. The city would still smell of clay and something sweet. The walls would still press gently inward, not to crowd, but to hold. The mosses would still be thin as breath. The only difference would be that everything would feel, in the most ordinary way, as if it were exactly where it belonged, and that belonging was not a place, but a conversation that would never end.

In that way, the benevolent power would do what love does when it is wise: it would make itself unnecessary, not by leaving, but by making sure that everything it touched could carry its gentleness forward, one careful breath at a time, in a downward-grown city that forgot it was ever stone, and remembered, instead, that it could be home.
