The attention arrives quietly, as attention does when it is both vast and careful.

It does not announce itself with light or thunder. It arrives as a tendency for the bowl to hold its breath a moment longer than usual. The rim of blackened spines leans in not to eavesdrop but to hush. The glass-fiber fronds cease their humming, the ceramic vines stop chiming, and even the dust motes pause in their circulation, as if they have all agreed to be studied. The attention is a shape without edges, more like a softening of the rules that govern the pocket. It is the kind of presence that makes pressure feel like a blanket rather than a weight.

The Keld feel it first as a change in their glaze. The mineralized plates along their arms and collars grow warm in a way that is not environmental—a warmth that begins inside the enamel and blooms outward like ink in water. They set their tools down, not because they are commanded to but because the tools themselves seem to sigh and settle. The obsidian hooks grow cool, the ladders of braided vine loosen, and the city’s scores begin to glow faintly, as if remembering they are meant to be read. The Keld look at one another and do not speak; their internal bells still, and in the silence they feel the pocket itself listening back.

The benevolent power does not descend; it permeates. It slips into the cracks that open when the bowl expands and does not close them, leaving them as invitations. It moves through the towers and hollows like a draft that knows exactly which questions to ask. It asks the heat if it is willing to be gentler. It asks the salt-crust if it is willing to remember what it is like to be soil. It asks the fronds if they would like to dream of rain, and the vines if they would like to sing without needing to be sounded.

And so, gently, the pocket begins to change—not by becoming something else, but by becoming more itself.

The heat, which had been viscous and syrup-thick, learns to thin without losing its flavor. It becomes a current that can be sipped rather than waded through. The city’s expansion slows, not from fatigue but from curiosity. The buckling of the floor becomes a deliberate undulation, like a chest rising and falling with thought. The cracks that open now stay open long enough to let in drafts that smell less of baked clay and more of possibility—of water that has never been and may never be, but is imagined anyway. The Keld feel these drafts on their inner bells and translate them into new songs, tentative and sweet, that the city adds to its collection of tablets.

The vegetation, lush by local measure, begins to play with excess. The glass-fiber fronds experiment with transparency, holding light inside their hollows long enough to learn its color. The ceramic vines bloom not only with silver pollen but with tiny, temporary reservoirs—pearls of condensation that form and vanish, practicing the memory of rain. The root-systems, which once drank heat, now let some of it pass through, trading it with the soil for stories of depth. The plants do not become thirsty; they become generous in new ways, gifting shade that has a taste to it, gifting sound that lingers like honey.

The fauna, absent but not missed, begin to arrive in potential. The pocket learns to hold space for creatures that are almost ideas—shadows that detach and flicker with purpose, echoes that take on weight and move as if they have errands. The Keld watch these almost-creatures with their stone eyes and do not fear them. They offer them the hollows where wind used to accumulate, and the almost-creatures accept, learning to roost in the generosity of the city.

Technology shifts from primitive to playful. The tools of obsidian and bone-white ceramic begin to grow where they are needed, not summoned by temperature but by intention. A Keld thinks of lifting, and a hook forms along its forearm, cool and eager. Another thinks of reaching, and a ladder of vine stiffens into a bridge that leads not to a place but to a question. The niches in the towers open not only for work but for wonder, and the Keld begin to store things there that have never been stored before: moments of silence, the taste of a breeze, the memory of a shadow passing through.

The writing on the bases of the towers, which once mapped only heat-flow, now includes other variables. There are scores for gratitude, for the number of times a frond has chosen to let light through, for the pitches of the Keld’s internal bells when they are content. The city keeps its archives of sensation, but the sensations themselves are becoming softer, more numerous, threaded with a kind of care that was not there before.

Even erosion changes its habits. Where things wear away, they are replaced not only by redistribution but by restitution. Dust motes, those minuscule archives of prior forms, begin to gather into patterns that resemble maps of elsewhere—places with water, with softness, with quiet. The Keld study these maps and feel their glaze grow warmer still, not with heat but with recognition. They understand that the pocket is not being remade into something foreign; it is being reminded of what it has always been capable of being.

The shadows that once lagged or arrived early now move with a new confidence. They learn to cushion the Keld’s steps, to carry sound gently from one tower to another. The density of the pocket, which had felt absolute, now has seams—places where closeness is interrupted by something like grace. The Keld move through these seams and find that they can carry more than heat and minerals; they can carry hope, which is lighter than both and yet makes them feel sturdier.

The benevolent power does not stay long, for it has many pockets to tend to and many bowls that are learning to breathe. But before it withdraws, it leaves a final gift: a single, sustained note that is not sound but a kind of pressure, the feeling of being seen and understood without being judged. The Keld press this note into the largest tablet they have, the one kept in the quietest corner of the city, and seal it with a layer of their own glaze, so that it becomes part of them.

When the attention lifts, the rim of the bowl settles back to its familiar lean, the fronds resume their humming, and the city’s noise returns—not as it was, but as a richer, more careful loudness. The heat is still high, but it is now a warmth that knows its own name. The Keld return to their tasks, but they do so with a new rhythm, a gentle insistence that even in a place without water, there can be a kind of rain.

And in the quiet corners, where the almost-creatures roost and the dust motes trace maps of elsewhere, the pocket keeps breathing, lush and loud and certain that it is not alone. It has been touched by a wisdom that asked only to listen, and in return, it has learned to listen to itself, to the heat, to the bells in the Keld’s throats, and to the soft, endless possibility of being enough exactly as it is—while still being willing to become more.
