The benevolent power does not arrive in a flash, nor does it carve commandments into stone. It arrives as a patient rearrangement of attention.

First, it notices that the Altiplantigrades already know how to be tuning forks. What they lack is not wisdom but permission to trust the clarity they harvest from altitude. So the power begins by softening the edges of their doubt—not by removing it, but by making doubt reverberate like a struck bell, so that its hollowness becomes useful. In the morning, when a Thrum-Kin presses an ear to a living column, they no longer hear only the land’s weather-dreams; they also hear their own listening reflected back to them, clarified, as if the column has learned to sing in harmony with the listener’s spine. The people learn, in small increments, that attention is not a theft but a duet.

For the Ridge-Riders, the power does the subtler work of teaching the wind to apologize. Updrafts that once buffeted the great beasts into clumsy tilts now approach with a courteous dip, as if the air itself has learned the etiquette of bearing weight. The honeycombed bones of the Riders hum with a new, gentler frequency, and the symbiotic creeper flowers not only in motion but in moments of rest, so that even when grounded, the beasts wear crowns of brief blossoms. The Riders find themselves able to descend not in awkwardness but in a slow, deliberate unfurling, as if the sky has agreed to catch them.

The Lens-Backs find their domes polished by invisible hands. Not made smoother, but made more truthful: each turtle’s shell now refracts not just starlight and condensation, but the small, honest histories that settle on its back. When a Thrum-Kin asks for a sip, the turtle’s shell warms slightly, signaling consent in a language older than words. The mosses and sound-mites on the turtle’s back learn to sing in rounds, turning the turtle into a walking chorale that reassures the young that patience is not idleness but a form of travel.

The Echo-Jays are given the gift of discernment. Their ridged feathers still archive the loudness of the world, but now they can choose what to loop and when to let a sound fade. They develop a repertoire of purposeful silences, teaching the people that not every echo needs to be answered. When a storm’s groan is recorded, the jays thread it through a melody of uplift before releasing it, so the warning arrives braided with courage.

The Siphon-Spires, those organs of attention, are blessed with a new humility. The translucent sacs continue to gather the world’s noise, but they also learn to filter cruelty and panic, transmuting those harsh frequencies into a thinner, clearer syrup that carries not just the history of sound but its possible remedies. When an Altiplantigarde tastes it, they feel not only what has been, but what could be shaped next—a near-future of softened frost, of negotiated gales.

The benevolent power turns its gaze last to the emptiness itself—the amplitude between things. It does not fill the hollows, but it teaches them to recognize when they are needed. The vastness develops a gentle gravity, a pull that is not possessive but invitational. When a voice rises from a platform or a ridge, the emptiness leans in to catch it, amplifying what is true and letting the rest disperse like frost under morning light. People begin to understand that their modest cairns and woven platforms are not just shelters but instruments set within a larger instrument; their careful living is the score.

There are no miracles that violate the grammar of the place. Frost still sharpens, ridges still shear, water still hesitates before it runs. What changes is the relationship to these inevitabilities. The Hollow-Hounds continue to drink heat, but now they exhale a fraction of it back into the world in the form of warm breezes that find the shivering. The Thread-Mice still weave nets for condensation, but their droplets now hold a trace of the benevolent power’s encouragement, so that even the smallest micro-ponds feel like pledges.

In time, the people notice that their songs return to them altered—not perfected, but polished, as if the place has learned to sing along without overpowering them. They notice that the green pelt of the land rises and falls like a lung that has learned to pace itself, and that their own breathing has fallen into step. The collaboration between living mass and the space it holds becomes less like a negotiation and more like a shared heartbeat.

The benevolent power does not stay forever. It withdraws the way altitude withdraws into horizon, leaving behind no sign but the accumulated evidence of grace: Ridge-Riders banking on drafts that feel like forgiveness, Lens-Backs carrying chorales on their backs, Echo-Jays threading courage through warnings, people tasting near-futures in translucent syrup and building platforms that exhale warmth.

And when the power looks back, it sees that the emptiness has not been filled, but has become a chamber in which every life knows how to ring without shattering—each note distinct, all notes bound by the thin, taut sky that is no longer indifferent, but has learned, in its own vast way, to listen.
