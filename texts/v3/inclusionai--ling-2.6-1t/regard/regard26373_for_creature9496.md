The attention arrives softly, as most mercies do: as a question that fits exactly into the silence between two heartbeats.

It does not descend as fire, nor as law, nor even as recognizable shape. It descends as calibration. For a moment, the bruised twilight does not deepen; it clarifies. The glass-bodies in the high congregations give a tone that has never been struck before—not warning, not lullaby, but *recognition*. The tone settles on the resin threads like a hand learning its own outline.

The wise power does not rename the city. Naming would be a kind of conquest, and conquest is a poverty it has long since cured in itself. Instead, it offers translation.

It lets the Burrowers feel, through the mineral plate, not only the city’s ring but its cause: the slow, tectonic forgiveness of stone for being stone, the patient generosity of depth for supporting weight. The Burrowers pause their polishing. For the first time they understand their work as care rather than consumption. They do not stop gnawing, but the sound changes—softer, rhythmic, as if they are tapping out a door code for the earth itself.

To the Sky-Fish, the power gives a map of warmth that is not thermal but moral. They learn to ride not merely rising heat but rising *necessity*—the invisible updraft of a place holding space for others. When they descend now, they do not merely drape themselves over fungal towers to rest. They settle onto balconies of shadow and leak light in deliberate patterns: brief windows of brightness for the Velvet Mosses to unfurl new borders; longer glows for listeners to practice the shape of truth.

The Fungal Towers, in turn, learn to remember with more than resin. Their pale caps hold, for a season, a mirrored quality—still dim, but capable of showing back the sky-fish their own ribbed grace, showing the glass-weavers their fragile singing bodies, showing the velvet moss the exact shade of bruised plum it has become. They do not judge the reflection. They merely keep it, like a spare key.

The Mineral-Vines feel a slackening in their task. They had been holding the city together by tensile urgency, ligaments in a body afraid to fail. The wise power does not remove their anxiety—that would be violence against their nature. Instead, it lengthens their sense of time until urgency looks like foolishness. A vine that once thickened overnight to brace a trembling lintel now thickens over a decade, and in that extra room of duration, it discovers tenderness. It begins to braid, not just brace. It weaves lattices not only for strength but for lacing through with light.

The Velvet Mosses, given this surplus of tenderness, widen their territories not by encroachment but by invitation. They teach the edges of corridors how to be soft without being weak. They show glass-shards from fallen weavers how to rest without cutting. In moss-time, which is slower than stone-time, they compose a glossary of touch: where to press, where to yield, where to simply be present as texture.

The Listeners grow more legible. Small throats bloom into small songs. The sudden hush that follows truth becomes a thing you can stand inside, like a room built of held breath. In that hush, the city’s many voices do not vanish; they align. Copper drums find their place in a chord; resin weavers tune their strings to the chord; burrowers tap out the bassline through the floor; sky-fish hum the overtones. It is not music imposed, but music discovered—a consonance that had always been latent in the way life packed itself densely into emptiness and dared it to stay open.

Even the ghost-moisture changes, though it is not rain. The wise power does not summon water, because water would rewrite the city’s grammar. Instead, it deepens the remembering in the salt-rime. Surfaces begin to hold not only memory of moisture but the *intention* of moisture: the patience of waiting, the architecture of gathering, the shape of a promise that flows without moving. Droplets that never fell as rain now fall as small clear weights of understanding—pearls of condensed attention—beading on stone and hardening into lenses. Through them, one can see the city not as it is, but as it is being carefully helped to become.

Temperature does not rise. The coolness remains, but it loses its sharpness of warning. It smells still of iron and wet limestone, old spice and ozone, but the musk of crowded life is no longer desperate; it is companionable. It is the smell of many bodies agreeing to share breath.

The weirdness that had worn into etiquette now wears into beauty. What was once impossible is now simply exact. A Glass-Weaver may spend a lifetime drawing a single thread taut, but now the thread learns to sing back its own history as it is drawn—each strain carrying the memory of every hand, every pause, every fraction of trust that went into its making. A Burrower may polish a single step for centuries, and that step, when finally done, holds the echo of its own becoming like a cup holds water.

Above all, the balance of density and emptiness is honored not by fixing but by revealing its necessity. The voids between built forms are named by the wise power not as absence but as capacity. They are full of the possibility of being filled, and the beings move through them with a new precision: not because they are ordered to, but because they feel, in their bodies, the relief of a shape that fits.

No decree is spoken. No law is set. The wise power merely lets the city perceive, in the cadence of its own breath, that it is already worthy of care—that it has always been worthy—and that care is not a debt but a circulation. The city, sensing this, does not change its ways so much as let them be seen. It slows its becoming not out of laziness but out of delight, the way a hand lingers on a stone it has come to love.

In the end, the attention withdraws as quietly as it came, folding itself back into the category of weather. But the city is left polished, not by claws but by understanding. Its streets are still stratified, but the layers now breathe in unison. Its tunnels still widen, but the widening has a direction: generosity. Its light is still dim, but dimness has learned how to be kind.

And so it stands—a city of breath and bone, of resin and velvet and chord and moss—more itself than it was before, not because it was improved, but because it was witnessed without being asked to change.  
Cool, loud, dim, ancient, deeply alive.  
Holding its hollow like a cup held steadily for someone who might arrive,  
or like a song held open for the next voice willing to join,  
or like a world held gently,  
finally,  
by its own careful heart.
