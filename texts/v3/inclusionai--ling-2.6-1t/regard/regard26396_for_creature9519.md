It arrives as a permission.

First, nothing changes outwardly. The moss still clings, the Weavers still press, the Catchers still tip their bladders, the Whisper-Rats still count the stone’s pulse. But beneath the surface of happening, a new permission settles into the grain of things, the way silt finally accepts clay after being pressed long enough.

The Power sees that this place is afraid of its own unfinishedness—not with terror, but with the nervous thrift of something that has learned to do without. It sees that the city hoards its own impermanence, folding it into walls, into treaties of mud and cord, into the provisional maps the children draw with their easily erased chalk.

So the Power’s first act is to give the place a Sabbath of certainty.  
It does not end labor; it softens its urgency.

It enters the dreams of the Cartographers and gently rewrites their slates—not so that the maps become final, but so that every erasure leaves a trace of light instead of shame. Now when a corridor is altered, its ghost remains visible as a pale ribbon in the stone, a memory that does not scold. The children learn to read strata as stories rather than mistakes. They call the ribbons *the city remembering how it loved itself yesterday*.

To the Weavers, the Power offers a new tool: not a hammer, but a listening.  
It teaches their hands to press not only with strength but with duration—so that pressure becomes a conversation. Walls begin to settle with a kind of satisfaction rather than resignation. Cracks close not because they are forced, but because they recognize the shape of patience. Over generations, the seams grow glossy, like river stones that have decided to remain in the keeping of water.

To the Catchers, it gives a different kind of arithmetic.  
Water is no longer measured only by how much can be stored without loss, but by how much can be given without diminishing the source. The bladders grow permeable in subtle ways, weeping small blessings into root trails, into the margins where the bristled plants grow. The plants, sensing this new logic, send roots deeper—not in conquest, but in correspondence. They return small poems to the water in the form of filtered sweetness, and the water carries them to hidden cisterns where the city drinks its own kindness.

The fauna are addressed in their own languages.  
To the lizard-things, the Power inscribes on the air a map of safe heats, so they can bask without fear of being seen as idle. To the trowel-beaked birds, it offers a covenant: that every crack tested will be answered with stability, so that probing becomes blessing rather than suspicion. To the Whisper-Rats, it gives a rhythm of vibration that translates anxiety into direction, so that their warnings become invitations to adjust rather than alarms of failure.

Above all, the Power turns its gaze on the compacted shadow that still passes for a ceiling.  
It does not tear it open to reveal a sky—that would impose a foreign idea of completion. Instead, it thickens the shadow with starlight it has borrowed from elsewhere, so that the ceiling becomes a firmament in miniature, a map of distances held close like a letter. The inhabitants begin to feel, without being told, that what is above is not emptiness but *reserve*—a promise that they may grow upward whenever they are ready, and that until then, they are already sheltered by a kind of sky that understands the virtue of waiting.

There is, finally, a change in the quality of noise.  
The drip-drip-drip becomes a tuning.  
The creak of settling earth becomes a song of alignment.  
The voices that spoke in practical registers find themselves able to say *perhaps* without shame, and *later* without fear. The city begins to practice a kind of imperfection that is not a flaw but a generosity—a way of leaving room for each thing to become itself at its own pace.

The Power does not stay.  
It does not need to.  

It leaves behind a habit of attention that outlives its presence: the habit of asking, before any change, whether that change is being made from fear or from trust. Slowly, the city learns to make its decisions from trust, and when fear arises—as it will—it is no longer the architect of walls, but only a visitor, like water, to be welcomed, examined, and released.

In this way, the place that was once a wound still being sewn becomes a pattern of healing that does not seek to erase its own history, but to make that history breathable. It remains unfinished, but it is no longer frantic. It remains dense, but it is no longer crowded with desperation. It becomes, instead, a living demonstration that to be in the process of becoming is not a lack, but a form of abundance so deep it can be mistaken for stillness.

And the Power, satisfied, moves on—not because it is done caring, but because care has been successfully deposited, folded into mortar and moss and the soft turning of small lives, and will continue to unfold without supervision, like a mathematics of kindness that has learned how to count itself into the future.
