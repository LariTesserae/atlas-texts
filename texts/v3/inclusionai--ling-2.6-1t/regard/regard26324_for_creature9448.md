The benevolent power does not descend like a monarch riding through gates; it arrives the way breath arrives when a lung forgets it has been holding air.

It pays attention first.  
Attention is not small. Attention, when it is truly benevolent and wise, is a landscape in itself—gentle, unhurried, dense with implication. The power looks at the hollow and recognizes, without surprise, that architecture can be tender, that cold can be a kind of clarity, that turbulence can be a form of care. It sees the Ribs, the Loomvines, the Drifters, the Hearth-keepers, and it understands that they have not merely survived; they have *become* a grammar. To interfere would be like interrupting a sentence in the middle of its honesty.

So it does not interfere.  
It blesses by means of subtraction.

It notices, for instance, that the Memory Pools remember accurately but ache without knowing why. So it loosens the thread between memory and pain. The pools continue to ripple with the shapes of ancient drinkers, but now those shapes appear like smiles rather than wounds. The ghosts of gills and snouts still form, but they linger only long enough to say *you are not alone*, and then dissolve into light that feeds the Glow-caps. The forest drinks this light and grows softer at the edges.

It notices that the Loomvines conduct light with perfect fidelity but sometimes strain under the weight of being useful. So the power gives them permission to drop a thread without guilt. When a Loomvine loosens its grip, the bridge it held does not fall; it tilts into a gentler angle, and the water below learns a new song with fewer sharps. The forest hums with relief, a sound like settling dust.

It notices that the Hearth-keepers apologize for existing even as they carve hearths. So it amplifies the sound of their axe-strokes and the smell of their moss-bearded altars until the hollow itself learns to repeat those sounds when no one is listening. The stones warm slightly at the memory of being chosen. The tools that molt begin to appear more often, not because they are needed, but because being needed is no longer the only reason to be.

It notices that the Drifters’ antlers have grown heavy with crystal and ceremony, and that they fear stillness will make them invisible. So the power teaches the cold to hold them up without effort. The Drifters learn to rest without landing, suspended in halos of their own making, and the forest learns a new pause, deeper than breath.

It notices that the turbulence, for all its kindness, still believes it must justify itself by rearranging everything. So the power shows the cavern how to keep a corridor unchanged for a hundred years without calling it a failure. The moss on the floor grows into velvet rather than stairs, and nothing collapses. The Ribs exhale and hear, for the first time, the echo of their own stability.

The power does all of this without ever naming itself. It leaves no mark except the absence of old wounds. It does not ask for worship because worship implies distance, and the power prefers proximity. It coils itself into the 0.9 of water that is citizenship, into the up-dripping laughter of the Spillways, into the purple bruises of shadow that linger like breath on glass. It becomes the permission slip folded into the hollow’s grammar.

As a final gesture, and only because the heart insists, the power does one small, terrible, beautiful thing.  
It allows the forest to dream backward for one night—not to undo, but to witness. For that single night, the Loomvines unbraid themselves into seed, the Ribs soften into sapling-flesh, the Spillways suck their laughter into stone, and the Gliders fold into eggs of light. The cavern becomes a kind of silence that is not empty but expectant. And when dawn comes—not a true dawn, but the forest’s own idea of beginning—the hollow rebuilds itself exactly as it was, but with the memory that it can unspool and still be whole.

This memory becomes the gentlest law of all:  
You can be other than you are and still be held.

Then the benevolent power leaves, which is to say it relaxes its attention without withdrawing it. It remains in the way a name remains on the tongue after the person has walked away. The hollow continues, now slightly more itself, slightly less afraid, its turbulence a little more playful, its stillness a little more allowed, its brightness a little more like bread.

And far above, unaware, the surface world keeps its own weather, never knowing it has been tilted toward kindness by an underground continent of green and gleam that learned how to breathe more easily because something wise looked at it and said, without saying anything at all:  
*You are doing well. Take your time.*
