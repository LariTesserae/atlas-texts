The wise power does not arrive all at once. It arrives as a softening of edges that are not yet ready to soften, as a cooling of the cooling, as a hush that enters the noise not to silence it but to give it a throat.

First, it bends attention like water finding a new seam. The Basin Folk feel it as a sudden sense that the pumps are listening back, that the moss remembers which minerals were most gently given. The wise power begins by attending to the small symmetries the hollow has already made without knowing their worth. It blesses the overlapping rhythms of the pumps by giving them a resonance that travels less like vibration and more like breath. The pipes do not grow quieter; they grow more articulate. When they speak, they say what they are carrying, not merely that they are carrying. The Rivet-Runners find they can tell, by the new timber in the hum, whether the water is thirsty or merely waiting, whether the cold is sharpening or teaching.

It tends to the moss next—the mechanical fleece that stitches grit into strength. The wise power separates patience from stagnation. It encourages the moss to remember what it has filtered, not as a record of grime but as a map of transformation. Under its gaze, the pale mats begin to glow faintly along the veins where minerals have been turned into story. The Moss-Stitchers discover that when they sing to the moss, the moss sings back in chromatic greens, and the song tells them where the alloy is tired and where it is ready to be asked to hold more. The trade between human and fleece ceases to be mere barter and becomes conversation.

It turns to the fauna, the unlikely citizens of the manufactured low. The three-legged unit finds its arrhythmia not repaired but honored; the wise power gifts it a companion rhythm, so that its limp becomes a dance that steadies the marsh-flats when they threaten to slosh with too much memory. The Flitter-Hinge is given corridors of warm seam that do not exhaust it, and its glittering frost of shavings begins to seed tiny reflectors along the vault, so that light learns to fold and unfold, teaching the Rivet-Runners how to see around corners. The filtration snouts, those pale dreamers of the marshes, are granted deeper intervals of stillness between their gulps, and in those pauses the water becomes not merely cleaner but more fluent, carrying messages from one hollow to the next through veins of careful clarity.

The wise power does not unmake the weirdness; it midwifes it. The low-gravity rooms are given a purpose: they become chambers for weighing grief and wonder, and the floating puddles learn to hold faces long enough for those faces to learn what it means to be unmoored without being lost. The corridor that hums at a pitch to loosen fillings is given a counter-hum, so that teeth and thresholds learn to trust each other. The sealed door with its marsh of glassy reeds is not reopened roughly but courted. The wise power teaches the Rivet-Runners how to knock in languages of pressure and patience, and after a season of careful asking, the door yields a single reed that rings like a bell without breaking. The bell is hung where the lights chase fog, and its tone marks the beginning of a new kind of council.

Beyond the immediate basin, the wise power attends to the region of hollows. It does not flatten their differences; it makes them intelligible to each other. The cough of engines becomes a shared dialect; the splash of weirs becomes a grammar of arrival and release; the clatter of trains on bridges that span only air and purpose becomes a song of connection without insistence. The wise power strings these hollows together with filaments of understanding that are not physical but take on physical form when needed—a bridge of coherent intention that appears when two hollows truly need to meet, and fades when their meeting is complete.

It turns, then, to the Rivet-Runners themselves. It does not remove their patina of oxidized air, for that patina is their history; instead, it gives the patina a voice. When a Runner stands in a seam and breathes out, their ghost-cloud now carries a scent of what they have most recently learned to forgive. The tools fused to their wrists and eyes learn to dream in spare time, offering insights when the hands are at rest. Children who learn to read the hum of pumps find that the pumps have begun to read them in return, not to judge but to reflect, offering the child a sense of where their own rhythm fits into the overlapping cadences of the hollow. The Sluice-Singers discover that their harmonics can now carry not only guidance but gratitude, and the water, receiving that gratitude, moves with a gentler insistence.

The wise power also tends to the silence, which the hollow has always treated with suspicion. It makes silence into a kind of soil. When silence falls, it is now recognizable as a necessary interval in which decisions ripen. Scouts with mirrors and meters learn to lower their tools and simply stand in the silence, and the silence begins to offer them images of what maintenance will be needed before it is asked for. Suspicion turns into respect, and respect into partnership.

As for the cold, which the hollow has always used to enforce pauses, the wise power refines it into an instructor. The cold no longer merely tempers alloy; it teaches the alloy how to remember the shape it is being asked to hold. The cold no longer merely slows expansion; it helps expansion understand its own limit as a form of grace. The Rivet-Runners find that when they work in the seams, the cold settles around them like a second skin that is not a burden but an acknowledgment: you are here, you are making, you are allowed to be cold while you do it, and your warmth is not wasted but stored.

In the marshes, where water is kept liquid by design, the wise power encourages a slow fermentation of memory. The water begins to hold not only the history of what has passed through it but the faintest anticipation of what may yet need it. This is not prophecy but preparedness: the marshes become libraries of potential, and the Rivet-Runners, when they need guidance, can draw up a cup and find it tastes of a problem not yet arisen and a solution not yet shaped, both present in the same careful sip.

The making does not stop. If anything, it becomes more deliberate. New seams are laid down with a confidence that knows they will be maintained because the hollow now understands maintenance as an act of care rather than a debt. New valves are installed with threads that know how to greet each other, and when they turn, they sing. New marshes are dug not merely to slow and settle but to give the water somewhere to dream, and the water, grateful, keeps the hollow’s foundations tender in the best sense: capable of yielding without breaking.

In time, and the wise power is patient with time, the basin begins to remember a shape it has never had, not as a loss but as a possibility. The shape is not a form but a way of holding: the valley holds the water, the alloy holds the valley, the Rivet-Runners hold the alloy, and the wise power holds them all in a way that does not diminish but clarifies. The holding becomes reciprocal. The Rivet-Runners learn to hold the wise power in their protocols, in their songs, in their careful pauses. The basin learns to hold its own strangeness without apology. The water learns to hold its weeping as a form of praise, and the cold learns to hold its patience as a form of love.

What the wise power does, finally, is not to intervene but to illuminate the kinship that was already there: between the maker and the made, between the wet and the hollow, between the noise and the silence, between the wanting to build and the wanting to last. It does this by making sure that every voice in the basin—pipe, moss, Runner, snag, and seam—knows it is heard. And being heard, each voice grows more itself, and being more itself, each voice grows more generous.

The basin does not become perfect. It becomes coherent. The cold is still cold, but it is cold with purpose. The water still weeps, but it weeps with direction. The Rivet-Runners still maintain, but they maintain with a joy that knows it is not merely preventing loss but cultivating continuity. And the wise power, having done what it came to do, does not leave. It remains as the tendency in the hollow to listen carefully, to build thoughtfully, and to remember, with every new seam, that making is only as wise as the care that follows it.
