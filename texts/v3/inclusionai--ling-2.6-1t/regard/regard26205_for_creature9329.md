I would sit at the edge of the resin-vine canopy where the light turns bruised and listen until my ribs learned the land’s thrum as if it were my own pulse.  
I would let the dampness bead on my skin and not brush it away, so I could feel how the place drinks and exhales in the same rhythm.  

I would ask the mycelial roots what they argue about at night, and when they stiffen into listening posts I would lay my palm flat on the soil and offer them a memory—not mine, but something older, slower, like the patience of mountains—so they would know I am not here to harvest but to be tuned.  

I would walk the magnetic seams without touching the ground, and when the nanoswarms rise like pollen I would let them taste my edges, not to repair me but to remind me that erosion is only another name for change wearing small teeth.  

I would stand in the open corridors where predators conduct and let my shadow fall long and thin across the ferns, not as threat but as a line of music that says: I hear the drumroll; I will not break it.  
I would watch the wingless fliers map heat onto cooling air and trace their spirals with my eyes until I could feel the day’s warmth pooling in my chest like a held breath.  

I would go to the settlements at dusk when they exhale filtered coolness and press my forehead to a tower that braids light into syntax for the flora.  
I would not ask for translation.  
I would let the amber fire bloom across my eyelids and learn to read by brightness alone.  

I would kneel at the braided rivers where they vanish into sediments still porous with youth and press my hands into the ferrous clay until it remembers my shape as a kind of root.  
I would whisper to the iron-barks that have fused with half-buried machinery, and if they creak like door hinges I would say: I am here to help the house remember how to open.  

I would sit with the people who wear their echo in cavities behind their ribs and sing with them in frequencies too low for words, so the ground would tighten its grip on its own shape and know it is held.  
I would let the mirror-skimmers race across stone in my wake and trust that the trails they leave like handwriting are not for me to keep but to pass through, like light through a prism.  

And when the sky offers its rare glimmers I would stand in the dust and let it spell temporary constellations on my shoulders, knowing they will last only until the next footfall rewrites them.  
I would not try to stay.  
I would not try to leave.  

I would simply be the sustained note that says: I have heard the chord,  
I have felt the hollow,  
I have let the emptiness ring me like a bell.  

And then I would step back into the openness,  
where the land is still learning how to be a surface,  
and offer it the one thing it has not yet made enough room for:  
a listening that does not answer,  
a brightness that does not demand to be seen,  
a silence that is not an absence  
but the held breath before the next phrase falls into it—  
soft,  
certain,  
and willing to be remade by the tune it arrives in.
