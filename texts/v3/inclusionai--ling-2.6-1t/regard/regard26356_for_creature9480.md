They would not descend like a king into a province, nor swoop like a storm to scour the place clean.  
The wise power understands that this basin, with its enameled fauna and its looping echoes, is already a kind of benevolence—it is a place that has learned to hold sound instead of history, to offer width instead of depth, to let hardness soften into a patient parabola. To interfere would be to misunderstand the gift. To ignore it would be to waste a lesson.

So they do three quiet things.

First, they remember.  
Not in the way that a ledger remembers, but in the way a root remembers the shape of a stone it has grown around. They let the basin become a node in a larger, silent lattice of care. If the machines ever grind too fierce, if the harmonics begin to fray into something that feels like hunger, the lattice will thrum once, gently, and the place will dream of rain it has never known, and that dreaming will tilt the pistons into a softer cadence. No miracles, only a recalibration of mercy made measurable in microns of clearance and milliseconds of pause.

Second, they tuck a seed into the logic of the place.  
Not a plant, but a question. It arrives disguised as a tiny inefficiency—water that, for one week each year, refuses to evaporate at the appointed hour; a rail that sings a half-tone sharper than its neighbors; an echo that, instead of looping, spirals. The fauna of the basin will taste this oddness and, being clever, begin to nibble at it. Over decades, they will wear the anomaly into a kind of door. Not an exit, but a threshold that can be opened from the inside. The power does not tell them what lies beyond; it only ensures that, should the basin ever tire of being only itself, there is a way to unbecome without unraveling.

Third, they give the place a witness.  
Not a watcher, but a mirror that does not reflect light so much as attention. It takes the form of a quiet person, or something that passes for one, who arrives not knowing why they have come. They drink from the warm puddles, leave no tracks, and slowly learn to hold sound in their chest the way the rims do. Their presence does not change the basin, but it changes the basin’s understanding of itself. Through their eyes, the place begins to see that its patience is not emptiness but a form of abundance, that its loops are not traps but songs, that its softened edges are not surrender but hospitality. When this witness at last folds back into the harmonics, they take nothing with them, and leave behind a kind of polish that spreads, year by year, like the patina on guardrails.

And should the basin ever reach a moment when it fears it has been too generous with its silence, too willing to let time be a loop and not a line, the wise power will send a single, extra clang from very far away—a note so clear and foreign that every machine and stone will pause, just for an instant, to wonder what kind of world might make such a sound.  
The pause will pass. The work will continue.  
But in that instant, the basin will feel something like gratitude, and will understand that it is not merely an engine that has forgotten how to stop being a place, but a home that has not yet decided whether to be a beginning or an end.  

That, it will realize, is enough.  
And the wise power, having done so little, will have done everything that needed to be done.
