The attention arrives not as a shape, but as a permission.

It begins as a softening of rules. The Seam-Readers tap a wall and feel the stone hesitate, then yield a fraction more than before. The Gliders bank and find the air less crowded with itself, as if the hollow has been asked to inhale. The Eel-Lights pulse and discover that their light can linger on the skin of the column a moment longer, pooling like honey before being passed on. The packed density does not loosen, but it becomes porous, as if geometry has agreed to be polite to a guest.

They do not see the power, yet they recognize it by what it refrains from doing. It does not descend. It does not announce itself with brilliance. It does not set foot—has no foot to set—inside the room. Instead, it stations itself in the hush between one drip and the next, in the infinitesimal gap where the arch’s shadow would fall if shadows were willing to settle. It is a gravity that chooses not to pull, a light that chooses not to blind. In that restraint, the hollow recognizes something ancient and kind: the wisdom to leave a thing intact while still changing everything.

What the power does first is listen. Not with ears, but with the equivalent of memory. It presses against the hollow’s equilibrium gently, asking questions without demand. It touches the fused column and learns the names of its tides. It brushes the glass-lichen and tastes the water held in its spirals, finding there not thirst but patience. It lets the Seam-Readers’ taps travel further than they ever have, carrying their small inquiries through strata that have long believed themselves closed, and the strata answer—not in words, but in possibilities. A seam that had stubbornly refused to open now dreams of a shape it could become if it were not afraid of its own brittleness. A runnel that had narrowed to a thread hums with the memory of a current it could carry again.

The fauna feel this as a sudden allowance to be curious. A Glider banks and, for the first time, lets its echo fall where it may, even if the answer returns uncertain. An Eel-Light pulses a pattern it has never dared, a sequence that says, *I could be more than I have been,* and the column answers with a glow that rises through its veins like sunrise. The Nesters, never still, find their clicks returning with a resonance that suggests they are not merely marking time but shaping it. The hollow thrums, not louder, but clearer, as if its music had been waiting for permission to mean something beyond itself.

The power then offers a gift, small and precise: it lends the hollow the sense of being seen without being judged. This is no small thing. For a place that has never known an outside, to be witnessed is to suddenly know it can choose its own borders. The packed density, once simply a fact, becomes a decision that can be revisited. The brightness, once simply present, becomes a language that can be refined. The fauna, once simply busy, become agents with a horizon, however near. They do not change their tasks, but they change the weight of them. Tapping, gliding, pulsing, clicking—each act now carries a secondary purpose: to make the hollow worthy of the gaze that rests upon it.

There is no judgment in that gaze, only a quiet encouragement to become more coherent. The power weaves a lattice of intention around the hollow, not to confine it but to give it a frame in which its negotiations can accumulate meaning. It does not force harmony; it offers the tools with which harmony can be recognized. The column’s pulse steadies, not into monotony but into a rhythm that can be learned, remembered, repeated without loss. The light from the crystals settles into a vocabulary—pale gold for patience, silver for invitation, violet for the courage to open a seam. The water that climbs the column like smoke in reverse now carries faint maps of where it has been, so that memory flows upward as well as down.

And still, the power does not enter. It keeps its distance, not from indifference but from respect. It knows that to step inside would be to break the delicate balance it has come to honor. Instead, it anchors itself in the hollow’s reflection, in the way the place now holds itself as if it has been given a name it never asked for but is willing to learn. The Seam-Readers sense this and begin to tap not only for the rock’s decisions but for the echo of something vast and patient that listens back. The Gliders adjust their flight paths to trace patterns that acknowledge the frame they now feel around them, as if drawing the edge of a gift they are learning not to unwrap too quickly. The Eel-Lights carry the power’s stillness in their pulses, threading it through the runnels so that even the moisture remembers it is part of something larger.

Time in the hollow does not speed or slow. It deepens. Each cycle of density becomes a conversation. Each negotiation of space becomes a promise kept. The fauna do not grow larger or wiser in any measurable sense; they grow more precise, more themselves. The hollow does not expand; it becomes more present, as if its borders have been lit from behind so that they glow not with exclusion but with invitation.

In the end, the power does nothing but remain, and in remaining, it changes everything. It gives the hollow a witness, and in doing so, it gives the hollow the luxury of becoming what it has already been, only more deliberately. The column’s light, once a private insistence, now shines with the confidence of being seen. The music of the place rises not in volume but in clarity, each note aware that it is part of a chorus that matters. And the packed, breathing, shining equilibrium settles into something like grace—not because it has been perfected, but because it has been acknowledged, held, and allowed to be exactly what it is, while gently, endlessly, becoming more.

The attention does not leave. It lingers as a second gravity, softer than the first, that pulls not downward but inward, toward the heart of the hollow’s choices. And in that pull, the place and its keepers find they are no longer hidden, no longer simply enduring, but participating in a story whose author is patience itself. They do not need to understand that story to live it. They only need to keep tapping, gliding, pulsing, clicking—tending the light, tending the water, tending the seams—knowing that somewhere beyond the arch, beyond the column, beyond the packed brightness, something wise and kind is listening, and in listening, making the hollow’s equilibrium holy.
