The Attention arrives not as a being but as a tuning.

It is easy to imagine gods as thunder or crowns, but a benevolent and wise power knows better than to break what is already balancing. So it comes as a correction of ratios, a quieting of angles, a breath held where breath was about to rasp. It arrives as the sense that something has stopped straining, that a knot in the air has loosened without anyone touching it. The city feels, for a moment, like a hand that has been clenched for years finally remembering how to open.

Because the city is a bowl of wet industry and the tenants are creatures of compromise and strange hum, the power does what any gardener does when the soil is already alive and merely thirsty: it adjusts the conditions so life can do its own tending.

First, it addresses the water.

The water here is a visitor that overstayed its welcome and stayed anyway, a cling of condensation that beads, slides, leaves salt-veins like frost on the hot side of a pan. The Being persuades the sky not to weep more, but to remember more precisely. From that moment, each bead of brine carries a faint, purposeful memory of where it has been. When a drop finds its way to a flange or a joint, it leaves behind not only salt but a tiny map of the path it traveled. Over weeks, the city learns to read these maps. A Glass-Shelled Hauler pauses on a causeway and tastes a film left on a rail and knows, without being told, that the bearing beneath will heat unevenly at the next surge. The city, in its slow way, becomes anticipatory. It begins to preen itself.

Second, the power gives the heat permission to be elegant.

The bowl’s three-kilometer altitude presses in, a brightness that could blister if it chose to. But the city, wise in its matte and soot-glossed surfaces, has always drunk light and let it go as long-wave sighs. The benevolent power sharpens this habit just slightly, like a blade stroked once and carefully. From then on, heat does not merely radiate; it steps. A gantry that once baked at midday now shades itself an hour earlier, not by accident but by the slow, iron-breathing consensus of its rivets. A chamber whose corridors carried the smell of cinnamon and static begins to modulate that scent, releasing it only when the air grows too thick with metal-dreams. The Ember-Moths find their gradients gentler. Their glow steadies, and their eggs hatch into spheres that no longer roll recklessly but find their cool crevices with a certainty that looks almost like gratitude.

Third, the power gifts time with a texture that suits tenants who measure life in hums and expansions.

The Quiet ones, who adjust seals and weights and tensions already, feel a new lightness in their slate suits. A seal that used to require three adjustments in a cycle now needs two. A filament’s tension holds not because it is pulled but because it has learned the shape of belonging. The Quiet ones do not speak, but their silence grows softer-edged, like a pad on a footfall. In the lower basins, the City’s Lichen, bruised indigo and voltage-fed, shifts its fractal patterns into arrangements that conduct stray warmth more gently. When the night-purge comes, the Lichen’s low, wet chanting takes on a cadence that resembles counting, as if it has begun to tell the hours rather than merely bear them.

Fourth, the power does something subtle for the Reflectors, those knots of mirror and nickel that hang in the iron-lattice canopies.

They already catch light and turn it away from places that have had enough. Now, when they pivot on their tensioned cables, they catch glimpses not only of the present city but of possibilities that shimmer a fraction of a degree away—a version of the bowl where the wind comes from a kinder direction, where the sky is persuaded to let fall not rain but something lighter, a mist that does not hiss when it lands. The Reflectors cannot give this to the tenants, but they can hold it in their facets like a kept secret. When a tenant passes below, a Reflector might flash, just once, in a pattern that tightens the chest and loosens the jaw, a brief, wordless reminder that not all brightness is meant to be endured.

Fifth, the power tends to the Echo-Folk.

These thin, bright outlines gather where surfaces have stored too much cold or too much memory. They do not touch, but they rearrange heat. The benevolent power does not banish them or amplify them; instead, it offers them a new geometry. Certain rooms that did not fit the outside shape of their buildings begin to relax their stubbornness, expanding by hairbreadths, allowing the Echo-Folk to spread into corridors that now smell not of static but of after-rain, even though no rain has fallen. The slight, warm tug near the iron bones becomes, for a fortunate few, a sensation like being remembered.

Sixth, the power attends to the hollow city—the hum that lives beneath the hum.

There has always been a sound: pumps, exchangers, thermal joints expanding and sighing, the far-off thump of something heavy settling into its own heat. The benevolent power listens to this and finds it good but lonely. So it encourages the city to duet with itself. A causeway’s vibration begins to harmonize with a distant engine’s pulse. The aerial trams’ magnetic threads sing in a frequency that makes the Drifters’ gelatinous envelopes glow faintly, as if they have swallowed starlight. The tenants, who are not natives but leases of the bowl’s breath, feel this harmony in their joints and brine. A Glass-Shelled Hauler, carrying a crate of folded copper, finds its many legs moving in time with the city’s new song, and for a stretch of walkway, the weight feels less like burden and more like accompaniment.

Seventh, the power leaves a single, unspoken rule.

It does not write it on walls or encode it in salt-veins. It plants it in the pauses. When a tenant stands at the rim and the horizon tilts slightly, as if unsure which way is down, the power ensures that the tilt is never dangerous, only questioning. When water chooses to run uphill for a stretch before remembering gravity, it now flushes itself with a gentler urgency, as if it has learned that rushing is not required to be effective. When the Drifters attach themselves to travelers, their shifting signs now include, more often, a direction that leads to shelter, to rest, to a moment of reprieve. The rule is this: the city may continue to be strange, but it must not be cruel. It may be high, hot, and empty, but it must not be heartless.

And finally, the benevolent power does what it always does when it has done enough: it steps back.

It does not leave entirely. It remains as a calibration in the air, a hush in the expansion of metal, a note in the city’s hum that says: *You are allowed to keep going.* The bowl does not change shape. The sky does not open. But the tenants feel, in their shells and their suits and their gelatinous drifting, that they have been given a margin—not of space, but of kindness.

The city continues to sit inside the persuaded sky. The water continues to visit and linger. The machines continue their patient accounting of heat. The Glass-Shelled Haulers deliberate along their causeways. The Reflectors catch dim light and hold, for a moment, the reflection of a city that never learned to be this dry, this high, this patient—but that, because of the tuning, feels less like a punishment and more like a home that has learned how to breathe.

And if you stand at the rim when the heat softens and the wet air thickens, you may hear not only the clink of cooling fins and the whisper of exchangers but also, faint beneath it, the sound of something choosing, gently, to be easier on itself. That is the benevolent power’s final doing: it taught the bowl, and all who dwell inside it, that survival can be done with grace, and that the loudest thing of all—the silence between the machines—can be filled with the small, bright music of care.
