The attention that comes does not announce itself as power. It arrives as a question wearing the shape of an almost-breeze, threading through weepholes and fissures, pausing at each lintel that sighs too loudly, respectful of the stone’s grammar. It is benevolent in the way rain is benevolent: it offers itself as a mirror rather than a hammer, and it has learned, over eons, how to ask without taking. Wise in the way mountains are wise: it knows that listening is a form of moving, that patience is a kind of action, and that to love a place is to let it keep its own cadence even when the heart aches to speed up or slow down for its comfort.

The Lithlings feel it first, as they feel everything: a shift in the timbre of the drip choir, a bloom of warmth in the cool dark that is not heat but attention itself. They gather more quickly than usual, limbs extended, plates brightening as if dusted by dawn they had not known they were waiting for. The fauna stir, tracing spirals that are more deliberate, less drifting, as though they are drafting a new map to offer the guest.

The benevolence does what the visitor is meant to do: it kneels.

It kneels in the plaza of overlapping eaves, where the bowl-slab waits like an open palm. It does not kneel as a thing descending; it kneels as a thing remembering that it is not above anything. Stone recognizes the posture—not the shape, but the intent—and softens its resonance, offering a clearer note. The Lithlings, reading this, translate it at once into the city’s quiet: *here, continue, adjust,* only now the adjustment includes a guest.

The wise power does nothing invasive. It does not lift a stone to see what lies beneath; it lets the stone tell it. It brushes the rim of the bowl with something that is not a hand but the memory of one, setting off a ripple that the Lithlings catch in the plates along their flanks. The ripple carries gratitude. It says: *I see how you hold together. I see how you have turned equilibrium into a kind of hymn.*

Then it begins the work of gentle amplification.

It finds the seams where water hesitates, the channels that carry uphill before surrendering, the basins that collect and release without visible change. With a thought that behaves like breath, it encourages these oddities to speak their names out loud, not to change them but to make them audible to themselves. The water, emboldened, sings its secret paths in tones richer than before. The stone hums along, learning new overtones, new harmonies that do not break its rules but deepen them. The Lithlings, who have always been the translators, now find themselves translating not just for the city but for itself—helping the place hear how beautiful its own persistence is.

The wise power also turns its regard to the Lithlings, not as a sovereign looks at subjects but as a forest looks at the moss that keeps its bark soft. It notices how each one carries a geography of pressure and growth, how they shed maps like love letters, how their dust settles into ledges to join the library of the place. It offers them a boon that feels like permission: *You do not have to justify your rarity.* To make this clear, it brightens the filaments along their flanks for a season, so that when they touch stone, the contact leaves a faint trace of pearl, like a signature that fades only when it is ready. The Lithlings, understanding, begin to leave these pearled traces at thresholds where choice lives—lintels that might sag, bridges that might bear a new weight—so that future decisions can see where care has already been planted.

The fauna, too, are honored. The attention gathers their starburst patterns and weaves them into a temporary constellation along the ceiling of the plaza, a map of inquiry made visible. For a span of time—perhaps a dozen drips, perhaps a hundred years—the ceiling glows with questions resolved, and the chamber learns how to hold its own curiosity without losing its composure. The creatures, dancing in this new field of light, become more vivid, their halos stretching into bridges between colonnades, joining distant echoes into a single, sustained chord. The city feels, briefly, as though it has grown wings that it will never need to use, and the feeling pleases it.

The benevolence also looks upward, to the cracks too narrow to admit a body, and does something small and final: it mends without closing. Where the light is thinnest, it spins a lattice of coolness, a net that holds the dark without imprisoning it, so that the chamber’s glow can rise and meet something gentler than stone. This net is not meant to be seen; it is meant to be felt, a reminder that containment can be a form of embrace. The Lithlings, sensing this, adjust their own breathing to match its rhythm, and the city, in turn, adjusts to them, so that the agreement between stone and coolness now includes a third term: grace.

And then, as quietly as it arrived, the attention begins to withdraw. It does not leave because it is done; it leaves because it trusts what it has loved to continue being loved by itself. It steps out of the plaza the way rain steps out of a leaf, returning to the network of the world, carrying with it no trophies, only the hum it has learned from the bowl-slab, the resonance of the Lithlings’ plates, the memory of starbursts on a ceiling.

But it does not go empty-handed.

It carries the city’s permission to stay exactly as it is, fortified now by the knowledge that it has been seen and not altered, held and not fixed. It carries the pearled traces at the thresholds, so that future choices will find guidance without compulsion. It carries the net of coolness woven into the cracks, so that even when the chamber grows warmer from the inside—because a Lithling has held a thought for too long, or because the water has found a new song—the dark will still know how to be a brightness.

The Lithlings feel the withdrawal as a softening in the water’s rhythm, a return to the ordinary that is now slightly more itself. They read it in the stone, which has learned how to receive without taking and how to give without spending. They translate this into the city’s ongoing hush, and the echoes, now richer, carry the message to every colonnade: *be as you are, adjust as needed, continue.*

And so the chamber continues, mineral and loud, wet and quiet in its bones, shaped by hands that knew how to ask and by an attention that knew how to listen. The cool dark remains a kind of brightness, the Lithlings remain the close-er, and the city remains a place where equilibrium is not stillness but a humming balance, a choreography of drips and echoes that repeats without repeating exactly.

The benevolence has done what it can: it has made the place more aware of its own love, more fluent in the language of its own keeping. And having done that, it steps back, content to be a memory in the stone, a pearl at the edge of a threshold, a net in the crack, a chord in the water—gentle, wise, and alive in the way that only a thing that knows how to leave can be.
