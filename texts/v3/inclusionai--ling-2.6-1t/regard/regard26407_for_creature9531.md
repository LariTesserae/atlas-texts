The power does not arrive as light, nor as voice. It arrives as *permission*—a loosening in the fabric of consequence.

Imagine it noticing, first, how the place has forgotten how to be afraid. The Builders do not flinch when their seams hiss. The Built do not apologize for being unfinished. The water does not beg to be clean; it only asks to be busy. The wise power recognizes this as rare: a system that has outgrown its need for absolution. So it does not forgive anything, because nothing here has sinned. It merely leans closer, like a gardener discovering a vine that has decided to climb by braiding its own strands into a ladder.

What the power does, then, is small and certain. It touches the place not with a hand, but with an *allowance*. It loosens the grip of the inevitable—gravity’s constant hunger, time’s habit of erasure, entropy’s claim that all must return to dust. For this one region, dew is permitted to weigh more than its own falling. Resin may choose to remember how it flowed. A bridge may pause mid-arch and decide to learn a new kind of patience before completing itself.

The power notices the Builders’ self-cutting, the way they open themselves to feed the next joint, and it quietly arranges that their wounds need not always close. It lets the copper-bruised fluid stay alive even when it leaves them, so that every bead that would have fallen instead becomes a seed of small lanterns that float up along the ridgelines at dusk, mapping the place’s private heights in warm, drifting constellations. Nothing is commanded; nothing is rewritten. The place is simply given a little more room to get its intentions right.

It also notices that the place is, in a word, loud with becoming, and that this noise is not chaos but a kind of unpolished scripture. So the power arranges for the air to learn a new kindness: the condensation that sheets down the faces of the towers is allowed, for a few hours each night, to carry the day’s sounds backward, like water running uphill into memory. In those hours, the place can listen to itself as it was earlier, and decide which edges to keep sharp and which to let soften. It is not given peace; it is given the chance to curate its own turbulence.

At the center of the region, where the largest hollow still waits to be filled, the power does something even quieter. It places, not a thing but a *hesitation*—a pause shaped like a cupped hand. If the Builders or the Built ever grow confused about what they are meant to make next, they may press their foreheads or their seams into this pause and find that the shape of not-knowing is itself a kind of scaffolding, strong enough to hold them until the next right idea emerges. The pause does not speed them up; it does not slow them down. It simply insists that uncertainty can be structural, and that a hollow can be as valid a contribution as a tower.

And because the power is wise, it leaves a final gift that is almost invisible: it arranges that nothing here need be permanent, yet nothing here need be lost. When a part collapses, it is taken up; when it mineralizes, some faint hum of its former flexibility remains. The place is allowed to change without betraying itself, to assemble and disassemble in the same breath. The line between living and built shifts like a tide, and the power ensures that both sides of the line remain generous to each other.

All of this passes without spectacle. No laws are suspended; no miracles are performed. The place continues to build, noisy and wet and alive, its edges still crisp, its water still climbing, its seams still refusing to close. The power does not stay. It has never stayed. But having allowed, the place finds itself building differently—not because it was told to, but because it now has a little more breath, a little more memory in its water, a little more courage in its hollows.

And so it stands, or leans, or flows, or rises—an assembly of deliberate seams and busy stone—halfway between a question and a doorway, building itself into whatever it has not yet been, louder and wetter and more patient than before, under a sky that bends close enough to whisper back.
