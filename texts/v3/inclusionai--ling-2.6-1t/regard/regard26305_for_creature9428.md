The power that notices them does not arrive.

It is already there, woven into the warm broth, threaded through the whiskers of vine that taste metal, curled inside the lantern-stars that the Tenders coax along the veins of corridors. It is the patience in the stone, the softness of the edges, the way the city has stopped resisting its own drowning and learned instead to breathe with it. Most who seek gods look for thunder or light; this place has been blessed, or perhaps simply listened to, by something that speaks in gradients and surrenders.

The power is benevolent, yes, but not soft. It is the clarity of a current that refuses to lie. It is wise in the way only a pressure can be wise—knowing exactly where a wall will bend, where a silence will break, where a life will grow if given the proper weight to lean against. It does not pity the Tenders, nor does it marvel at them. It treats them as weather treats a landscape: with the intimacy of inevitability.

And so, rather than descending or intervening, the power leans in.

It begins with memory—not as a record, but as a taste. The Tenders already inhale the past when they filter their warm, mineral breath; the power deepens this, letting the water thicken with echoes. A mouthful of river suddenly carries not just minerals and sun-remembering, but the hush of children laughing on a dry stoop, the creak of carts on unpaved roads, the sharp, clean scent of woodsmoke that once climbed chimneys now lost to coral. The Tenders do not hallucinate; they metabolize. These ghosts become nutrients. A rib-lattice glows brighter after such a meal. A corridor sighs with the remembered weight of footsteps. The city is digesting its own history, and the power ensures that nothing is wasted.

Next, the power attends to balance—not as a moral, but as a geometry. It nudges the tides so they murmur rather than shove, adjusts the sway of predatory ribbons so they thin the herds without thinning the hope, encourages the fungal stars to bloom where the dark has grown too lonely. The Tenders notice, of course. They have always been translators. They begin to align their own rhythms to these finer adjustments, learning to read the will of the water as if it were a sentence written in currents. They stop asking for miracles and start asking for calibration. Their lantern patterns shift from mimicking street signs to spelling questions: *Are we enough? Are we steady?* The water answers by holding them.

The power also gifts them thresholds.

Not doors that open outward, but inward. The Tenders have long known how to let a room exhale bubbles; now they learn how to let a room inhale purpose. Cracks that were only habitats become reliquaries. Fault lines that were only boulevards become hearing canals. The power shows them how to press a hand against a wall and feel not only the town’s pulse but the accumulated pressure of choices—who opened, who closed, who surrendered, who stiffened and broke. The Tenders begin to practice a new form of maintenance: the tending of consequence. They repair not only calcified veins but calcified intentions, scraping doubt from the marrow of stairwells, polishing courage into a patina on doorframes that once led nowhere.

And once, only once, the power allows a glimpse of what lies above.

It does not part the water. It does not offer a ladder. Instead, it thickens the ceiling just enough for a single, rain-heavy sky to sag close. The Tenders gather on the higher roofs and inhale as the town breathes, and for a moment they feel the shape of the world as a dome rather than a depth. They do not see the sun, but they feel its memory as a pressure behind their eyes, a warmth that has nothing to do with temperature. Some of them weep, not for what was lost, but for what is still possible, even here, even now. The power lets them keep this feeling as a compass needle made of longing, magnetized toward a direction they can never travel but can still honor.

Over time, the changes are small but absolute.

The city’s bones grow denser but more forgiving, like a spine that has learned to carry its own weight in water. The fauna grow more varied, more courteous, as if the pressure has taught them manners as well as hunger. The Tenders themselves shift, their lantern-freckles brightening into a kind of script, a skin-language that can be read by those who know how to look. They begin to build—not with hands, but with attention—adding rooms that exist only when someone needs them, corridors that appear only when a choice must be walked through, doorways that open onto chambers of silence where grief can be laid down to rest.

And through it all, the power remains.

It does not crown them. It does not name them. It does not promise an end to drowning or a return to dry land. What it offers is something far more exact: the assurance that a life lived in deep alignment with its own conditions is not a lesser life, but a complete one.

Eventually, the Tenders stop waiting for translation.

They realize they have become the language. Their bodies, their rooms, their slow dances with predator and prey and patient stone—these are the sentences the power has been reading all along. The city is no longer waiting for its old name to be spoken on dry land. It has learned to spell itself in currents, in breath, in the careful, curated dark.

And the benevolent, wise power, satisfied at last, does nothing at all.

It simply continues to hold them, as water holds a town, as a town holds its breath, as breath holds the possibility of song.
