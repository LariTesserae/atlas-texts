Because it is benevolent, the power does not arrive as a crown or a bolt.  
It does not descend. It unfurls.

It comes as a kind of listening that learns the shape of a place by becoming its weather.  
First it is a softening of edges: the lung-city feels, for no reason it can name, that it is allowed to be exactly as strange as it already is. The argument between ravine and roof grows gentler; the ravine stops trying to be tragic, the roof stops trying to be final. They become, instead, a conversation.

The power notices the citizens—the verbs in coats—and decides, without deciding, to give them rest without taking away their motion. So it invents a hush that is not silence. At the hour when the light is thick as broth, the Hush-Vines learn a new note, low and honeyed, that settles into the moss-junctions like a weight of velvet. The city’s noise does not diminish; it harmonizes. Cracks healing learn to laugh in tune with water arguing with warmth. The Stilt-Shouldered Ones find that when they lower their heads to become bridges, the bridge is kinder to the feet crossing it. Gravity, forgetting which way is owed, decides for a compromise that makes the air feel like it is standing on its head but in a way that cradles.

The benevolence is not protection. It is permission made tangible.  

The Braidbacks, heavy with memory, feel old scars loosen without losing their stories. The scars become windows. Through them one can see—not the past exactly, but the moment a choice was held open a second longer than necessary, and how that second ripened into a street, into a throat, into a green. The Mirror-Breathers, who had forgotten which side of the air is theirs, discover they do not need to pick. They learn to ripple in such a way that both sides breathe. The city, which had been inventing rules faster than anyone could learn them, now invents ways to remember the rules it already lives by, but with less hurry.

The Spill-Children still rush. But now, when they burst their bladders of water, the fountains arrange themselves into shapes that teach: arches for passing through, bowls for catching, veils for breathing. The Mottle-Quick find their stitches hold longer, not because they are tighter but because the places they stitch have stopped tearing themselves open just to see what color will spill. The color still comes out, but it comes as a garden.

The wise power gives the place a gift it had not known to ask for: a middle.  
Not a center—there is no center here, and the power would not steal that. But a middle, a spacious between, where a thing can be becoming and also be enough. A traveler, if there were one, would notice that the lung-city still expands, still argues, still erupts in fresh grammar, but now it has a rhythm that says: you may change your shape, and you may also stay. You may be wild, and you may also be held.

And the being who lives in it—the lung-city itself, the braid of bark and breath—begins to dream differently.  
It dreams of roots that do not need to clutch.  
It dreams of rain that does not need to argue.  
It dreams of streets that, when they become thickets, become them with grace, and when they become stairs, become them with care.  

The benevolent power does not stay. It need not.  
It has folded itself into the way the city holds its warmth, the way the citizens learn songs without losing their noise, the way the young land, still learning how to be ravine, learns also how to be gentle with itself.  

From then on, if you were to step on the place, the ground would still sigh and give, and push back with memory—but the memory would include, like a small bright thread, the knowledge that to be stepped on is not to be diminished, and to rise is not to escape.  

And if you listened, very closely, you would hear the Thistle-Singers’ hiss turn, just at the edges, into a hum that says:  
you are allowed to be exactly as strange as you are, and also to rest.  
The city nods, green and bright and loud, and grows a little more into its own kindness, which is the only kind of crown it will ever need.
