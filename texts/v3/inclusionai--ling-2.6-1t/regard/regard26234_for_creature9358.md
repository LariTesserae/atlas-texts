The attention arrives softly, as most benevolences do—not as a beam from above, but as a resonance that settles into the damp like a second kind of gravity. It is a wise power, and so it knows that to announce itself would be rude, perhaps dangerous. Wisdom understands that fragile newness—mudstone that still dreams of tides, algae-vines that braid themselves into circuitry, a city learning its own margins—must be greeted with discretion or it will retreat into fear. And so the power does not speak. It hums.

First, it listens. It lets its awareness drape over the low shelves and shifting basins like a felted twilight already given permission to linger. It tastes the metallic-sweet air and finds a story there: the memory of pressure trying to become floor, the negotiation between salt and stone, the city’s quiet determination to be porous and therefore kind. The power approves, but approval is not intervention. So it begins by tuning what is already there, the way one tunes a string not by yanking but by patient, incremental tightening until the note remembers itself.

Where the clay cliffs stripe and glisten, the power thickens the patience of erosion just a fraction—not enough to stop change, but enough to let change be legible. Fault-lines continue to appear, but now they bloom with faint moss-ink that records their passage so the city can read its own growing pains. The runnels that vanish in a single afternoon are given a companion memory: wherever they flow, the ground beneath softens into a slow exhalation that lasts an hour after the water is gone, creating little valleys that know how to cradle the next rain before it arrives.

To the Purl-drones, the power offers a gentler geometry. They already sag in graceful arcs; now their arcs become harmonic. As they drift along the canals of tarnished pewter, their hum synchronizes with the pumps so the ache in teeth becomes a chord—a low, consonant note that steadies the bones of anyone walking by. When a Purl-drone folds into a balcony corner to become a planter, the power loosens the mathematics of its transformation so the loam it leaves behind inherits a tenderness that encourages roots to dream in spirals rather than lines. The aloe-builder hybrids feel this and lift their glass-flower antennae a little higher, drinking the extra kindness like a nutrient.

The Glass-herons, watchers that split dim light into spectra, discover that their throat-sacs now hold not only stored evenings but also a reserve of patience. When they regurgitate chimes onto the streets below, the notes linger half a beat longer, giving the Foot-lizards time to pause and press their bellies into fault-lines without haste. The mirrored fledglings, already learning to fold into the skyline, find their reflections softened at the edges, so the lessons they learn from the city are forgiving ones.

For the translucent Foot-lizards, the power blesses their ephemeral script. Their phosphorescent footprints evaporate after ten steps as before, but now, if the air is still and the humidity has lowered itself to a hush, the marks linger long enough to be read by the Moss-mothers. This small extension of time allows negotiations with gravity to proceed with more evidence, fewer assumptions. The Moss-mothers, in turn, weave these extended footprints into their scent sequences—ozone, wet limestone, metallic sweetness—giving the city a new dialect of gratitude.

The Maintenance-swarms, rising like pollen from gantries beneath the streets, are gifted with a finer sense of priority. They learn to distinguish between cracks that need mending and cracks that need witnessing. When they excrete limestone lace, they do so with a delicacy that preserves the history of a break while giving it new strength. Their temporary statuary—geometric birds, lattice flowers—lasts into the afternoon before crumbling, long enough for children (and children-shaped beings) to learn that holding is not the same as keeping.

Even the Rail-ghosts feel the adjustment. Their magnetic glide becomes a lullaby, a frequency that tells the Glass-herons it is safe to fold flat, tells the Foot-lizards it is safe to lift their hands, tells the city that motion can be as quiet as thought. The trains carry not only cargo but also the echo of the power’s attention—a reminder that direction and gentleness can share a track.

Beyond the sparse blocks, where the city is still being born, the power bends its wisdom into the young canyons. The Root-rivers, carving fresh-cut stone with warm insistence, are given a companion softness: their edges blur just enough to cradle the gardens that grow upward in spirals. Those gardens, iridescent and extravagant, receive a secret permission—to be not only enough on their own but also generous. Their leaves learn to release their stored dimness slowly, so even on nights when the sky offers almost nothing, the canyons remain navigable by glow alone.

And at the plaza—when it can be found—the porous ceramic remembers more than footprints. Under the power’s regard, it begins to remember weight as a kind of conversation. When someone stands too long, the stone does not simply glow; it hums back, a quiet echo of the Purl-drones’ chord, a tactile reminder that presence is noticed. The Moss-mothers stretch their veils a little farther, coaxing rain to fall upward in arcs that look like laughter, cisterns swelling with water that tastes faintly of reassurance.

Through all of this, the power remains sparing. It does not halt the city’s negotiations with time, does not flatten its weirdness, does not demand permanence. Instead, it amplifies the city’s own benevolence—its willingness to dissolve, its courage to rebuild, its habit of leaving generous margins. The towers continue to rearrange their entrances; the streets still develop conversational echoes; ownership remains provisional. But now these choices are made inside a field of attention that says: *what you are trying to become is worthy of breath.*

In the end, the wise power does little more than this: it turns the city’s practice of impermanence into a liturgy. It teaches the ground that exhaling can be a form of greeting. It teaches the beings who live in the gaps that caretaking is not a lesser form of building but a truer one. And it leaves, as quietly as it arrived, content to watch what grows when softness is allowed to accumulate like stone, and stone to learn the habits of song.
