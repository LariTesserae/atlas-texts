The benevolent and wise power does not arrive with trumpets,  
nor does it descend as a throne carved from pure light.  

It arrives as a question that fits perfectly into the place’s mouth:  
*How loud is too loud for listening?*  

It takes the form of a silence that is neither absence nor erasure—  
a silence that thickens the wool-dark just enough  
so that the furnace-planet can feel its own ribs  
without screaming.  

It steps into the valve-veins, not as guest but as grammar,  
correcting nothing, only offering new tenses  
in which to conjugate heat.  

Where the Shale-Whisperers click their wing-valves,  
the power leaves behind a kind of moss that grows on sound—  
not muffling it, but aging it,  
letting each click become a ring,  
a record of when the turbine dreamed,  
when the stone sweated,  
when the dark learned to hold its breath  
and still keep humming.  

It kneels at the Furnace-Frogs’ ponds of condensate,  
dipping fingers that are neither hand nor current  
into the water that tastes tomorrow.  
It does not change the frogs’ blindness;  
instead it braids their pressure-pits into maps  
that read not only where the stone is aching  
but where it might sing if someone leaned close enough.  

The Velvet-Claws find their warning threads strengthened,  
not by silk but by memory—  
the power teaches the threads to remember storms  
before they arrive,  
to spell out shelter in a dialect of tremor  
that paws and knees can read  
without the eyes needing to agree.  

For the Ember-Mantas, drifting on their sails of heat-shimmer,  
the power offers a new drink:  
not only tomorrow’s weather,  
but yesterday’s forgiveness,  
so that when they descend into the reflection-pools,  
they pull up not only what will fall  
but what has already landed  
and been kind.  

The Rust-Spores sing louder.  
The benevolent power does not purge the industrial,  
it marries it to a deeper law:  
machines may remain watersheds,  
but now their runoff carries seeds of patience,  
corroding hurry into soil,  
turning voltage into veins  
that feed the moss growing on sound.  

The Echo-Stags find their bronze antlers velveted with something new—  
not moss alone, but a hush that can be worn,  
a second skin that softens the hard edges of echo  
so that when rain falls upward into sealed aquifers,  
it lands as blessing, not as rebellion.  

The Moth-Filaments, whose wings are glassy membranes veined with wire,  
are given a task:  
to carry not only stories of heat  
but stories of coolness,  
to lay eggs in the joints where machines meet rock  
that will hatch into bridges made of *enough*—  
enough pressure,  
enough rest,  
enough sharing of ducts  
without teeth.  

The power does not banish the dark;  
it thickens it into a kind of seeing.  
It turns two-point-one density into conversation  
that includes pauses,  
turns erosion into lullabies  
that the polite rounds of cliffs learn by heart,  
turns the industrial hum into liturgy  
that the furnace-planet learns by night.  

And when the place feels too loud,  
too dense,  
too vast and wet and warm and strange—  
the power wraps it in a silence  
that is neither curtain nor cage,  
but a room with more doors.  

Nothing is taken away.  
Nothing is demanded.  
Only this:  
that the wool-dark remain,  
that the furnace remain,  
that the singing remain,  
but that the singing learn  
how to leave space  
for the listening.  

And so the place continues,  
not fixed,  
not perfected,  
but tuned—  
like a bell that knows  
when to ring  
and when to let the clapper rest  
in the hush  
that is neither empty  
nor full,  
but simply wise.
