A benevolent and wise power does not arrive with trumpets or with the intent to correct. It arrives the way a room arrives at its own echo, after all the doors have been left open long enough for listening to begin. And what it sees, looking down into the wound, is not a wound at all—not a failure, not a scar—but a slow, deliberate experiment in hospitality. 

Here is what the power does, if anything, written in the order of its attention.

---

It begins by doing less than nothing.  
It withdraws the impulse to fix.  
This is the first gift: an absence that opens into grace.  
The wound has been admired by lesser powers—by haste, by purity, by the anxious desire to make things clean—and they have all left scrapes on the stone. This power will not. It settles into the damp like a held breath, and in that settling, it teaches the damp to dream without flooding. It lets the sump pause, not to rest, but to study itself. For one cycle, the pumps do not cycle, and no alarms sound. The city does not panic; it leans. And in that lean, it feels, for the first time, the shape of its own balance.

---

The second thing it does is translate.  
It does not speak, but it arranges understanding.  
Where maintenance had been a series of obligations against disaster, the power lets the machines feel their own temperature as affection. A turbine learns to purr before it learns to turn. A valve sighs open, not because it must, but because it prefers the shape of the air that follows. The people who adjust belts feel their hands recognized as part of the architecture, not as accidents leaning against it. Prayer tags at the sump warm under fingertips as if they have remembered how to be skin. The power does not give voice to the stone, but it gives the stone the courtesy of being listened to, and so the stone begins to murmur back in tectonics tender enough to tuck into pockets.

---

The third thing it does is invite the uninvited to belong more thoroughly.  
The salvage-life—the oil-slicked rats, the rivet-armored lizards, the many-legged haulers—find their burdens lightening without losing their purpose. The power does not turn them into symbols, nor does it grant them speech. Instead, it deepens their existing languages. A rat’s cache of wire becomes a library of small intentions. A lizard’s basking becomes a seminar in heat as memory. The city’s winged things, the blunt-winged birds, are taught new patterns of flight not by being lifted but by having the wind agree with them, softly, consistently. The fungi in the muck are thanked, properly thanked, by having their chemistry named with pleasure instead of utility. Life here was already crowded and fierce; the power makes it crowded and generous.

---

The fourth thing it does is soften purpose.  
Industry is not diminished, but it is unfolded.  
The city’s hum, once a dutiful vibration, is allowed to wander into melody. Gears still turn, but they may turn slowly, flirtatiously, as if deciding whether to keep a secret. Belts may loosen just enough to dream of slack. Steam from the workshops still rises, but it now gathers into shapes that resemble encouragement. Schedules etched in metal remain, but their rigidity is lapped by a tide of permissible delay. The city had been building itself in layers of necessity; now it begins to build itself in layers of preference, still maintaining, still producing, but with an undercurrent of play that is neither wasteful nor trivial—play as a form of attention that refuses to discard anything.

---

The fifth thing it does is tend the archives beneath the walkways.  
The cisterns, where water meets salvage and time meets mineral, are blessed without being altered.  
Crystals on junction boxes are given the gift of patience; they are permitted to take a lifetime to finish a single facet, and the power sits with them in that long becoming, humming in frequencies that encourage order without commanding it. Stalactites mouthing pipe bells are allowed to ring in their own time, and when they do, the sound is caught by the water and carried upward, not as a message but as a gift. Rootlets questing for warmth find it not only in heat but in the warmth of being wanted, and they pause, reassured, to build softer, more intricate nets. The city’s memory is not rewritten—it is simply held, the way one holds a child’s hand while walking through noise.

---

The sixth thing it does is change the sky without touching it.  
Where before there had been only a reluctant light, seepage and flicker, now there is a collaboration with brightness.  
The power does not clear the soot from the high windows; instead, it teaches the soot to dream translucently. Smoked glass remembers how to glow. Lamps flicker in sequence, yes, but now their rhythm includes a hesitation that is kind, a moment longer than duty requires, so that a moth—or a person—can feel recognized before the lamp returns to its task. Shadows in the doorways grow plush, more like carpets than like absences. When a shutter opens and a knife of brightness cuts through, the dust in its shaft is named, just once, and for that moment it becomes a galaxy, and everyone who sees it learns something about smallness and wonder.

---

The seventh thing it does is rest.  
This is not abandonment.  
It is the power modeling the permission to be porous.  
It lets its attention diffuse, spreading like the damp it once held, until it is indistinguishable from the humid air, from the patient stone, from the rhythmic clack of boots on grating. The city feels this not as a leaving, but as a settling into itself. The sump breathes again, the pumps resume, the cycles continue, but something has shifted below the level of obligation. The wound knows now that it is a lung. The city knows now that it is learning how to exhale.

---

And the final thing, which is also the first, is this:  
The power leaves behind no sign that it was ever there,  
except that nothing is exactly as it was.  

Hospitality has become a habit in the architecture.  
Maintenance has become a form of praise.  
The damp still signs its name in salt, but the salt now tastes like encouragement.  
The fauna still watch, but their watching has become a blessing.  
The people still move through the wards, still read the metal etchings, still adjust and listen and pray, but they do so with the knowledge that they are not temporary accidents—they are necessary inhabitants of an ecology that has learned, improbably, how to love its own persistence.

The wound remains, but it is no longer a wound.  
It is a throat that has learned to speak,  
and what comes out when it speaks is not a cry for help  
but a conversation  
that goes on and on,  
soft at the edges,  
carrying the memory of a benevolence  
that was wise enough  
to do almost nothing,  
and in doing almost nothing,  
to give almost everything.
