The Power does not arrive as light, nor as voice, nor as wings.  
It arrives as *recognition*—a sudden, gentle settling, like dust deciding it has always belonged on a certain sill.

For a long while—by the measure of the place, which is the measure of water learning how slowly it can fall—it does nothing.  
It only watches.  
Not with eyes but with the kind of attention that makes the watched feel as though they have grown extra skin: a tender, temporary membrane between themselves and the possibility of being misunderstood.  

The Power understands altitude not as height but as accumulated patience.  
It understands this lattice-forest not as architecture but as a long conversation the world once had with its own gravity, and then decided to keep having, quietly, so nothing would break.  
It understands the beings who live here not as climbers or swimmers but as translators—creatures who have agreed to turn stability into a language you can touch.

So the Power offers a gift.  

It does not change the coolness; it deepens it.  
It does not add light; it remembers light the way the moss remembers rain, storing it in the spaces between one breath and the next.  
Where the water hangs thickest, the Power lets it learn a new property: memory. A single drop can now carry the shape of every corridor it has touched, so that to drink is to know, faintly, where you have never been.  

It does not give the fauna new voices. Instead, it offers them the hush beneath voices—the space in which a call can become a question rather than a statement. Now, when a creature sings, the lattice answers not with echo but with a slight shift in texture, a softening at the seams, a permission to reshape the immediate world by no more than the weight of a song.  

To the beings themselves, the Power gives the luxury of unlearning effort.  
When they press their hands to the vegetal stone, the lattice leans in. When they sleep, the hollows rearrange themselves to hold their spines as if holding a promise. When they carry their heavy things—resin, stone, shards—the burden becomes not lighter but more intimate, as though the objects have agreed to be carried, as though gravity has decided it prefers to be polite here.  

And when the Power notices how the six-limbed runners leave scent-trails that dry into crusts, it does not erase them.  
It turns them into thresholds.  
Now, to follow a crust is to step not only across distance but across time: the moment the crust was made, the moment it is touched, and the moment it will soften again, all held in the same cool air, braided like moss.  

The Power does all this without speaking.  
It does it by listening so thoroughly that the place begins to listen to itself.  
The submerged city discovers, slowly, that it has always had a second kind of floor—not the one made of accumulated shed matter, but the one made of attention.  
When the beings walk on it, they do not leave prints. They leave *intentions*: small, glowing geometries that hover for a while, then settle into the walls as new kinds of joint, new kinds of flexibility, new kinds of rest.  

Nothing is demanded in return.  
No worship, no stewardship, no oath.  
The Power knows that benevolence is most itself when it asks nothing but room to be itself.  

And so, after a while, the Power does one last small thing.  

It lets the altitude know that it is allowed to soften.  
Not to fall. Not to fail.  
But to let its edges grow slightly blurred, the way a thought does when it has been held long enough to become kind.  
The sharp, final form of the forest eases into something like a suggestion of itself—still holding, still refusing to splinter, but now with a margin of gentleness at every boundary.  

Then the Power withdraws.  
It does so by becoming the coolness itself, by becoming the water’s memory, by becoming the hush beneath the loud voices, by becoming the extra skin that eventually fades into ordinary skin because it has done its work.  

What remains is the same place, but with a new permission written into its physics:  
You may hold without gripping.  
You may stay without stiffening.  
You may be an altitude that refuses to end and still learn how to bend.  

And the beings who live here, who are themselves a kind of altitude given skin, feel the change as a small, sure warmth behind their ribs—as if the world has finally remembered, and forgiven, its own depth.
