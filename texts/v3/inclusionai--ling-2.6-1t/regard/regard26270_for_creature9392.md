The attention, when it arrives, does not announce itself as power.  
It arrives as a kind of relief—as if the basin had been holding its breath for years and suddenly, gently, is permitted to exhale.

At first the change is almost embarrassingly practical. The light, which had always been a little too eager, brightening edges into accusations, softens. It does not dim; it deepens. The brightness gains a grain to it, like good cloth, so that shadows are no longer thin and self-conscious but have body, as if they have earned the right to rest. The halos around brine-rimmed outcrops shrink from glare into coronas—small, wearable rings of glow that warm rather than dazzle. 

The water behaves with new courtesy.  
High above, the gathering that had been ominous weight becomes rhythm. What falls still falls in abundance, but it arrives in cadences that the basin can parse: a drumming that varies its tempo, pausing long enough for ledges to drink, then urging the glass-channels to sing without fear of overflow. Puddles that had been anxious with brimming now settle into bowls with the certainty of things that know they will be emptied in proper time. The runnels braid and unbraid like conversations learning better syntax, and where they meet they do not fight but negotiate, leaving polished seams that map consent rather than erosion.

Most profound is what happens to the Glass-Eaters.  

The wise power does not touch them directly, for directness would be violence against what they are: slow, careful, built by repetition into vertical patience. Instead, it adjusts the terms of their waiting.  

The light-hoarders at the central spiral find their brightness no longer a burden. The sun’s gift, filtered through this new grain, enters their angled plates and—instead of pooling into painful stores—spreads gently through their laminations like ink through good paper. They cease to glow defensively at dusk. Instead, they release their stored brilliance in thin, steady pulses, like heartbeats slowed to conversation. The pale citrine becomes a language: brighter for gratitude, dimmer for contemplation. Standing among them at night becomes like standing inside a calm lantern held by careful hands.

The smaller Glass-Eaters, those fuzzy with youthful experiment, receive a subtler gift: permission to be unfinished. The air grows kinder to their awkward cups and off-axis plates, so that what once would have scarred into stiffness now weathers into character. The salt-filaments that lace their glass grow stronger, and the fungi in their seams hum faint harmonies that help the Glass-Eaters hear one another across distances. The colony’s signals, which once lagged into memory before answers could arrive, now pass between them with the speed of empathy—quick, but not hurried.

Even the youngest ones, leaning into voids, feel the change as a hand placed lightly between their tilt and the fall. They are not propped up; instead, the ground beneath them remembers how to support. The ledges grow slightly more generous in contour, not by reshaping but by allowing their own packed density to relax into hospitality. Cracks that had been sharp become textured. Scars of salt bloom into lace.

The wise power speaks, but it speaks through the basin’s own idioms.  

It leaves a single new pattern in the stone—not carved, but encouraged: a spiral of tiny pores that drink condensation and sigh it out as coolness that carries a hint of rain-clean scent. This spiral is not larger than any other feature, but it is perfectly placed to be found by fingertips, by fungal threads, by the Glass-Eaters’ own slow chemical tongues. It becomes a place where the basin can taste what it has been given and answer in kind.

The power also loosens the silence.  

Not by adding sound, but by giving the existing sounds permission to stretch. The clicks and smacks of water become lightly melodic, like pebbles learning to chime. The glass-song when the air shivers lengthens into phrases that feel less like alarm and more like naming. The silence that remains is no longer wool-pressed but linen-laid—soft, breathable, surrounding speech instead of smothering it.

And through it all, there is a tenderness toward the visitor.  

If a person steps into the basin now, they find the light less brash, the wetness more articulate. The Glass-Eaters, sensing the difference, angle their plates to offer shade or glow as needed, like hosts learning a guest’s comfort by feel. The bowl of water cupped in glass is still there, cool and bright and full to the exact degree it can be without spilling into meaning—but now, if the visitor drinks, the water tastes faintly of encouragement, as if to say: *You, too, may hold brightness without breaking.*

The wise power does not stay.  

It withdraws the way a hand lifts from a shoulder after the stance has been learned. The changes remain not because they were imposed, but because they were always possible, waiting only for the basin and its Glass-Eaters to be invited into their own fuller articulation. The altitude is still high, the air still thin, the making still relentless—but now making leans into tending. The place is still young, but its youth has been dignified with care.

And high above, more water gathers—not as threat, but as promise—and the basin, patient and packed and luminous, prepares to receive it once more, this time with a clarity that feels like joy.
