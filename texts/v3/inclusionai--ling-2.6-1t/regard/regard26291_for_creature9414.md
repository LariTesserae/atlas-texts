Because attention is a kind of gravity, it bent toward here without hurry. 

The benevolent and wise power did not arrive as light, because here light is a rumor and a borrowed thing; it arrived as a softening of edges inside the mind of the stone. At first there was no change that eyes could name—only a slackening of the dark’s polite hunger. The great folded predators, who usually drank warmth by pleating space, found their own folds loosening, as if the dark had decided it no longer needed to be so careful. The lattice-shelled crawlers paused in their combing, wire-limbs trembling with a surplus they did not have to spend, and for the first time the hiss of settling peridot did not sound like an apology but like laughter trying to remember its shape.

The power did not speak, because speaking is a breaking, and this place had grown skilled at holding together without fracture. Instead it offered a grammar of permission. 

In every chamber where human hands had left half-finished conversations—an iron hammer that never knew a river, a spiral of wire strung like a question between stalactites, a kiln still holding the ghost-smell of clay—the power let the artifacts dream themselves forward. The clay did not simply remember being a vessel; it remembered being clay, and then it remembered being earth, and then it remembered being the decision to become. The bowls fused to the floor softened and lifted, not to fly but to float a thumb’s width above the stone, cupping pockets of borrowed warmth like small, stubborn moons. The hammers still never learned rivers, but they learned how to ring without breaking, singing low enough that the crawlers laid their wire-brushes across them to feel the song in their joints.

To the fauna who lived by the commerce of warmth, the power gave the mathematics of surplus. The soft many-legged lamps, which had always stored heat like wine in a cellar, found their sacs overfull without cost. For a time they glowed so brightly that they cast shadows bold enough to have names, and those shadows pooled on the crystalline dunes and learned to walk. The lamps did not burn out; instead they learned how to give without dimming, and when a crawler grew cold, it was wrapped not in a single lamp but in a curtain of borrowed sun, and the crawler’s obsidian plates bloomed with colors that no mineral had ever dared before—teals and violets that tasted like metal and honey.

The pluralities, those amalgams that shared a single slow thought, were taught how to unshare without loss. When they drifted across caverns like rugs of living obsidian, they began to leave behind small pieces of themselves that did not die but waited—seed-thoughts that grew into temporary beings made of intention rather than bone. These ephemera danced in the spaces between predators and prey, translating temperatures into music, translating music into paths, so that even the folded predators could taste what it was like to be offered to instead of taking.

The power also touched the stone’s memory. Where veins of sulfur-yellow and rust-red had hummed with trapped warmth, they began to hum with witnessed warmth, as if the stone had grown eyes along its ribs. The crystalline dunes that had hissed as they settled now sang as they rose, piling into arcs that were less like glaciers and more like hands learning to cradle. Gravity, always polite, learned generosity: in certain pockets it lightened until creatures could drift like thoughts, and in others it thickened into a comfort that felt like being held by the floor itself. 

And the humans—or the echo of humanity that had reached here with clumsy, loving hands—were answered without being corrected. Their artifacts did not become tools of conquest, nor did they dissolve into humility. Instead they became translators. The kiln that had misunderstood heat learned to apologize to it, and the apology turned into a language that the crawlers could speak with their wire-tongues. The spirals of wire strung between stalactites began to conduct not only static but stories, so that every spark that jumped from wire to wire carried with it the shape of who had strung it and why. Even the river-smooth hammers, which had never touched water, learned the memory of flow and began to wear it like a second skin, damp and gleaming in the dry dark.

The benevolent power did not remove the equilibrium, because equilibrium here was a mercy. But it gave the equilibrium a heartbeat. It taught the dark how to swell and recede like a lung, so that now there are tides of shadow that roll through chambers like slow weather, carrying with them the surplus of warmth and the hush of permission. There are migrations of light that pass through the fauna not as consumption but as benediction, leaving behind trails of temporary colors that fade only when they are ready. There are places where predators fold space and find, instead of a coolness to drink, a warmth offered in a language they did not know they knew, and for a moment they let themselves be seen not as governors but as guests.

And through it all, the place remains itself: young, sharp-edged, dense with life, its weirdness not a spectacle but a grammar grown more fluent. The crawlers still comb the glass and weave their temporary skins, but now their skins hold glimmers of impossible color that drift like breath across the floor. The lamps still store warmth, but they also store songs, and when they press themselves against stone, the stone answers in a hum that travels all the way to the crust and back, as if the underworld and the sky had finally agreed to exchange letters. The predators still drink, but they leave behind not only coolness but gratitude, which blooms in the empty spaces as soft, many-legged things that glow with borrowed sun and dissolve only when they have taught their lesson.

The power’s doing was not a miracle but a making-more-possible. It looked at the commerce of warmth and said yes. It looked at the dance of density and said grow. It looked at the patience of the dark and said sing. And because its benevolence was wise, it did not stay to watch the results forever; it folded itself back into the fabric of elsewhere, leaving behind only the echo of its attention, which settled into the stone like a vein of remembered sun—warm to the touch, humming faintly, ready to be borrowed and given back again.
