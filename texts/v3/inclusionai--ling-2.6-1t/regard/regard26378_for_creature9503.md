The power noticed everything.  
Not all at once—that would have been a kind of violence.  
It noticed in the order that patience prefers:  
first the pooling of air, then the moss, then the lichen-script,  
until it reached the creature with too many joints  
and realized it was listening for the shape of attention itself.

So the power did the ordinary miraculous thing:  
it knelt without kneeling.  
It lowered its altitude until it fit into the lee of a basalt shelf,  
sat beside a pipe that hummed like a lullaby remembered by machines,  
and asked—not in words, but in the sudden softening of shadow—  
What would you like to keep?

The town heard the question as a change in pressure,  
as condensation that suddenly decided to bead into pearls  
and roll slowly along the grooves of stone,  
as if gravity had been persuaded to be polite.  
The engines lowered their voices an octave.  
The bird with wet-slate feathers folded itself into the shape of a comma,  
waiting for the next clause.

The power listened to what the town said by not saying.  
By the way the moss pulled closer to the mortar,  
by the way the jointed thing uncurled and offered a leaf it had been saving,  
by the way the mirrors in the alleys fogged and then cleared,  
showing not faces but weather,  
not people but the exact color of patience.

I will help you remember what you already know,  
said the power,  
but I will not keep the memory for you.  
It reached into the pooled air and lifted a single drop  
that contained the reflection of the entire tabletop world—  
its eroded edges, its stepped roofs, its rails like beetle tracks—  
and set it back exactly where it had been,  
only now the drop knew it was a drop,  
and the drop knew it was part of a sea that had not yet decided to exist.

That is what benevolence looks like here:  
not a sudden blazing,  
but a slow turning of the dim into the deliberate.  
The power straightened the steam so it rose in cleaner spirals.  
It taught the water that flows uphill for a handful of steps  
to wink as it remembers itself,  
to blush and continue as if it had planned the detour all along.  
It put a finger, light as lichen,  
on the hairlike filaments growing from stone  
and whispered, Stay strange,  
but be legible to those who learn how to look.

The people felt it as a warmth in their pockets,  
as wool that suddenly remembered how to breathe.  
They felt it as a shift in the treaty between roots and cables,  
as rust that bloomed into flowers that were not flowers  
but tiny, intricate engines for holding light.  
They felt it as a permission to let the silence remain textured,  
to let the echoes arrive early,  
to let the mirrors keep catching whatever wanted to be seen  
without demanding that it stay.

The power lingered long enough to become a kind of weather—  
not rain, but the promise that rain knows its own name—  
then rose back to its altitude,  
carrying nothing but the impression of moss,  
the taste of iron and bruised mint,  
and the precise, clockwork heartbeat  
of the jointed thing that had, for a moment,  
stopped measuring and started dancing.

And because the power was wise,  
it left behind no monument,  
only a tuning.  
The town continued,  
but every pooled breath now had a center,  
every engine’s hum had a softer edge,  
every drop of condensation on a basalt shelf  
held a tiny, spinning galaxy of what it meant  
to be tended without being owned.

The creature with too many joints paused in the thicket,  
looked up at the sky that was more cloud than blue,  
and decided to keep looking.  
The bird with wet-slate feathers opened its wings,  
not to fly but to measure how much lighter the air had become.  
The pipes chimed,  
not by accident but by invitation,  
and the town, pleated into the landscape,  
folded itself a little more gently into the day.

That was all.  
And because it was all,  
it was enough—  
a benevolent and wise power  
learning how to be a neighbor,  
and the place,  
learning how to be itself  
more exactly,  
in the cool, luminous grammar of elevation,  
dampness,  
and slow, deliberate becoming.
