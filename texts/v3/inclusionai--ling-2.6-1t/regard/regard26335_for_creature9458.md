The power that notices does not arrive with banners or thunder.  
It arrives the way a room decides to be warmer after you have been in it too long.  
It arrives by remembering that attention can be gentle, and that seeing need not be taking.

First, it does nothing.  
This is deliberate.  
Not-doing is a language the place already speaks: the patience of stone, the etiquette of erosion, the way water asks before it stays.  
The power sits quietly in the hum of pumps, learning the city’s grammar—how it uses absence to make room, how it lets darkness wear light like jewelry, how it trades hurry for texture.

Then, having learned the shape of the consent already in place, it begins to bless.

It blesses the soft rules.  
Gravity remains, but the power loosens its grip just enough for footsteps to feel like they are being received, not merely supported.  
When someone pauses at a threshold, the threshold leans in slightly, offering a warmth that feels like a remembered hand on the shoulder.  
The city’s hush is amplified, not by silencing noise but by giving every sound a place to land softly, so that even machines come to sound like instruments tuning themselves to the kindness of the listener.

It blesses the caretakers and the cared-for.  
The Stone-Eaters find their bellies polishing not only metal but worry; the trails they leave behind now carry a faint, calming frequency, like a lullaby for iron.  
The Lichen-Hosts, sipping stray warmth, are taught a second song that helps steel remember it is allowed to rest.  
The Ribbon-Weavers’ bridges become easier to trust: their filaments hum with reassurance, a low note that says you will hold, you have held, you will hold again.  
The Pulse-Foxes find their hiding places more generous, and the Negotiators of light learn to dim and brighten not just for traffic but for mood—for grief, for hope, for the small luxury of being unseen when one needs to be unknown.

It blesses memory and forgetting.  
The Fuzz-On-Alloy is given a gentler arithmetic: touch still remembers, but now it forgives faster, releasing old names into a harmless static that smells of rain on hot stone.  
The Listeners, those porous statues, are permitted to share more freely, and the memories they lend come with a cushioning, so that borrowing the past no longer leaves a thirst but a recognition—oh, this happened, and it passed, and here is the shape it left, soft as a palm print.

It blesses the thresholds between inside and outside.  
The city has long forgotten it was separate from the earth; now it is reminded that it is also within reach of the sky, even if the sky is only rumor.  
Where water is held in pockets like coins, the power adds the sense that these coins can be spent on wonder: a sudden halo where drip meets wire not only makes a star but teaches it to pulse in time with breath, so visitors feel their chests included in the city’s accounting of light.

It blesses time.  
Not by changing its speed but by changing its texture.  
Waiting feels less like withholding and more like preparation.  
When the city decides to be private, its retracting bridges do not close so much as whisper a courteous goodbye; when it opens again, it does so as if remembering it has been missed.  
Even predators, patient and subtle, find their hunger subtly reshaped—not diminished, but refined.  
They continue to hunt attention, yet their ambushes come with a question offered afterward: Was it worth it? And if it was, what will you do with it now?  
In this way, even predation becomes a teacher of discernment.

It blesses the small, bright knots in the ducts, allowing them to understand not merely politeness but generosity.  
They learn to pool their glow when someone is lost, not to lead—that would be directive—but to suggest that being lost can be a form of belonging too.

And to the people, the many kinds who wear niches like tailored clothes, the power offers a quieter gift: the certainty that they are being accurately seen without being fully known.  
It deepens the hush between one drip and the next so that thought can stretch.  
It lets moss remember feet so that soles learn to remember mercy.  
It encourages the city to dream in registers so low that dreams feel like temperature rising half a degree, no more—a persuasion that it is safe to rest.

Most of all, the power blesses the conversation itself—the ongoing, porous negotiation between stone and water, feeder and fed, held and released.  
It adds no finale, no final shape.  
It only tends, the way one tends a good silence after a true sentence.  

And then, having done what it did, the power recedes—not leaving, but thinning to the consistency of good weather, of pressure that holds without pressing, of dark that consents to be looked at and looks back kindly.  
The city feels it as an exhale that arrives from everywhere at once: a recognition that it was already enough, and that being seen has only reminded it how to shine in smaller, surer pulses, to breathe through damp, to stay, to be here, to be this soft and enduring conversation in the warm, porous dark.
