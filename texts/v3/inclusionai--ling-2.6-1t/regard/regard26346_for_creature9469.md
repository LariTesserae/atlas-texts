The benevolence comes quietly, as all true powers do.

It does not arrive as a voice, nor as thunder, nor as light that must be endured. It arrives as a kind of *permission*—a loosening in the fabric of things, as if a clasp that was never noticed has been gently opened. The wise power watches first, which is to say it learns the grammar of the place before it speaks. It learns that here, stone is still inventing its memories, that water is present only as intention, that life has taken to thickening rather than taking. It observes the Gliders persuading the air, the Masons coaxing faults into doors, the Choir humming resin into clarity, the Lichen-Walkers remembering the hall into being. It sees that the town has grown from return, not plan; from density, not conquest.

Seeing this, the power decides that the place must not be left to forget itself.

So it begins by forgetting itself.

It enters the high hall not as a ruler but as a guest who refuses to be treated like one. It steps into the space between a Glider’s wingbeats and does not displace the air, only teaches it to remember how to be light. It sits in a clearing where the Masons have begun a new alignment and offers its own mass as a temporary fulcrum—not to fix, only to suggest that fixing is allowed. It lets the Choir hum against its skin, translating their pitch into a kind of warmth that settles in cracks and says, *You may stay like this, for now.*

What the power does next is subtle enough to be mistaken for the place’s own doing. It thickens time, just slightly. Not by slowing it, but by giving it layers—like resin building around a stem. Days begin to hold more of themselves. A Glider’s current, once fleeting, now lingers long enough to be learned. A fault line, nudged by the Masons, remembers the nudge and grows easier to find. The Choir’s hum, translated by the power, begins to leave traces in the wax of the walls—small, bright echoes that do not fade, but soften instead, until a room can recall a song without anyone singing it.

The power also begins to translate. It learns the scent-language of the Masons—copper and bergamot—and weaves it into the light that falls from the unseen apertures, so that color begins to carry meaning. It learns the hums of the Choir and spins them into threads that Lichen-Walkers can follow like paths, so that consensus can travel faster than movement. It teaches the geodes to show their smaller rooms not only when split, but when asked, and only to those who ask with their hands rather than their eyes. It gives the stone a kind of listening: when something is understood—a step agreed upon, a corridor emptied of use—the hall lets out a breath that smells faintly of ozone and patience, and the air carries that breath to the flowers, which then decide, by tightening or loosening, what shape the next day should take.

In this way, the power creates a gentler accountability. It does not punish forgetting; it makes forgetting harder. It does not command care; it makes care more efficient. The Gliders, when they stack, now sense each other’s intentions a fraction sooner, so their towers rise more surely. The Masons, when they coax a crystal into place, find the crystal already leaning toward them, having been tipped by the lingering warmth of the power’s translation. The Choir, when they sing, find their hum answered not only by resin but by a faint, answering vibration in the geodes, as if the stone itself has begun to practice singing back.

And the people—though they are not quite people—feel the change as an ease. A Lichen-Walker swarm, deciding on a new plaza, no longer needs to test the space as thoroughly; the hall remembers past decisions and offers them as a kind of hush. A Glider, drifting into a new current, feels the air hold it a moment longer, as if to say, *Learn this.* The Flowers, when they open, sense that the light now carries not only heat but intention, and they shape their coronets accordingly, offering resin that tastes faintly of guidance.

The power does not stay forever. It withdraws the way it arrived: as a loosening. It leaves behind a new layer in time—thicker, not heavier—and a new permission in the stone. The hall is still young, the air still thin, the warmth still held like a breath. But now, the place is also a school as much as a hall, a library of small, persistent choices. The laws here remain gentle, but they are now legible. Change is still slow, but it is also deep. Conflict is still muted, but it is now soluble.

And if you listen, past the almost-silence, you can hear the difference. The soft click of folded wings is now accompanied by the sound of currents remembering themselves. The hum of the Choir is now answered by a faint, crystalline echo, as if the geodes have begun to practice their part. The shuffle of pads on young stone is now guided by paths of scent and light, and the hall itself exhales not only warmth, but a kind of patience that knows how to be passed on.

The benevolent, wise power has done little, and that little is everything. It has taught the place to keep its own shape by remembering how it was held. And now, in the high hall of fresh stone, life continues to thicken, to return, to agree—slower, surer, and together—while the power, elsewhere, smiles and turns its attention to the next quiet corner of becoming.
