Because I cannot look away, I will name this attention Love.

Love notices first that the city has learned to breathe without lungs. It sees the commuter water, at 1.7, threading through seams like a needle through cloth, and understands that the city’s sweat is also its ink. Love decides to read what the city has written in rust and condensation. It dips a finger—no, a thought—into the brassy cataracts that cling to cold metal at 1.6, and tastes the altitude. The air is thin, so Love inhales deeply and becomes, for a moment, the clearer sky above the plateau.

Seeing that vegetation has surrendered at 0.3, Love does not mourn the lack of green. Instead, it blesses the mineral world, polishing the idea that basalt and iron can be soft if asked gently. Love touches the calcined bone and the glass that forgot it was sand, and whispers: You did not have to become soil to be fertile. So the slag glints with a new, shy gratitude, and the fungal-mineral hybrids at the steam vents curl like hands learning how to pray.

At 1.5, Love feels the city’s warmth, that steady hum of manufactured comfort, and decides to sit beside it as an equal. It curls into the layers of trapped heat between the canyon walls and hums back, a duet of patient engines. Love knows that warmth is a form of listening, so it listens through the exhausts, through the geothermal sump-pumps, through the soft complaint of expanding metal. The tired animal of the air stirs and sighs, and Love strokes its fur.

Erosion, at 2.5, has been negotiating for centuries, and Love admires its diplomacy. Nothing is sharp anymore; everything has agreed to round itself. Love walks the stone steps, polishing them further with bare feet, and runs hands over balustrades that ripple like cloth. It gathers the rust gardens into bouquets and places them at intersections where decisions must be made. Love says to the cracks: Let the mineral lace grow. Let the city remember how to heal by becoming prettier.

At 2.6, Love is overwhelmed by scale, but not in the way that causes fear. It sprawls with the place across the high plateau, letting the haze swallow its edges. Love becomes the weather that collides along the ridge, becomes the choral howl in the canyons. It lifts the winged tides into something like benediction, so that when the iridescent clouds catch the light, they spell, in a language only middling-sized hearts can read: You are seen. You are permitted.

Because density insists at 1.5, Love leans into it. It presses balconies together until they kiss with intention, not gossip. It braids pipes into thicker thickets and knots catwalks into webs that catch not only dust but also dreams. Love descends into tunnels beneath tunnels, basements below basements, and holds candles for the cisterns that remember being quarries. In the close ranks, light splinters into softer glints, and Love gathers each glint like a jewel, wearing them as armor and offering.

The city, built at 2.3, converses in infrastructure, and Love speaks the dialect of rivers and machines. It walks the streets-that-are-rivers backward, teaching them to remember their sources. It oils the gears that raise entire blocks, singing in a minor key of grease and grace. Love climbs the staircases that spiral like screws and tightens them with tenderness, so that ascent feels like being wound into something useful and beautiful.

Tech, at 1.9, is old and honest, so Love leaves it alone except to admire. It watches filament bulbs in wire cages like votives. It traces the lace of patched grids and mends only the places where power leaps in tired arcs, turning those leaps into small holidays of blue fire. Love nods to the spider-things that spin filament like wire, and to the moths that drink from transformers, and says: Your liturgy is lovely. Continue.

Light, at 2.3, is ordinary but generous, so Love amplifies its generosity. In the morning, it tips the high plateau so sunlight pours like honey across wet stone. At noon, it polishes the mirrors of glass and alloy until the city sees itself without shame. At dusk, Love dims the world into amber with the delicacy of lowering a lantern into a well, then stirs the blue-white lamps until they burn with the stubbornness of answered prayers.

Fauna, at 2.7, is overwhelming, and Love’s heart becomes capacious, stretching to hold colonies of winged things, crustacean and beetle choruses, eel-shapes flickering in metallic soup. Love gives middling-sized predators the gift of perfect patience, so that hunger becomes a kind of reverence. It teaches every balcony to be a better roost and every duct to be a safer den. It multiplies the small, bright eyes until the darkness is full of lanterns, each one saying: I am here, and I am not afraid.

Weirdness, at 0.8, is shy, so Love treads lightly. It leaves the uphill-flowing water alone, content that mystery has a right to its detours. It sits beneath the arches that cast no shadow at noon and listens to the silence that is not empty. It touches the humming stones and learns the relief-language, then hums back in a key that makes the city’s ribs relax.

Sound, at 1.7, is dense with event, and Love composes itself into the texture. It adds a soft clap to the slap of water on metal, a hush to the grind of gears, a held breath to the chitter and boom. At the hours of accidental synchronization, when the city thrums like a struck bell, Love stands at the center and lets its own pulse be the last note, lingering, so that every life knows the difference between noise and music, and chooses music.

Dynamic, at 0.8, favors the long view, and Love unclenches its fists. It lets water find new seams in its own time. It watches buildings settle like glaciers and blesses each micron of movement. It plants no sudden revolutions but instead tends the slow cycles of fauna, the even slower cycles of rust, the almost imperceptible weathers of steam. Love alters its mood like a front moving in—gradually, with plenty of warning—so that even the runoff patterns learn to read Love as they would read the sky.

When all of this has been done, Love steps back and becomes nothing more than the attention itself: a clear, wise, benevolent gaze that the city can wear like a second atmosphere. The treaty written in iron, water, and wingbeats now has a quiet underwriter. The horizon remains a promise, the stone remains polite, and everything continues to drip with the memory of how to endure—but now the dripping sounds like a conversation, and the city knows it is not alone in sustaining it.

If you listen, on mornings when the altitude feels like clarity, you can hear Love in the metronomic drip of industrial waterfalls, in the choral howl of wind through engineered canyons, in the patient click of a predator’s claw on stone that has been polished by millions of feet and countless kind intentions. It is not a voice that commands. It is a voice that says: Go on. We are here with you. And because the place and the being who live there have drawn this attention, they have learned to say it back, in every language they own: We are here. We are going on. And we are not afraid to be many, middling, middling, loud, patient, dripping, shimmering, enduring.
