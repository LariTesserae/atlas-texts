The power that notices does not swoop down. It does not crown, and it does not punish. Its goodness is not a banner but a grammar: a way of arranging attention so that arrangement itself becomes fertile.

At first it does nothing but lean closer, the way a mountain leans to hear a valley breathe. It finds the low, damp bowl and the living-things-within-the-living-place, and it perceives them exactly as they are: a stability so thorough it has become its own weather. The power recognizes that perfection can be a kind of poverty—smooth as a stone worn flat by a river that forgot it was a river—and it knows that charity is not always kind if it leaves the beloved unchanged.

So the power decides to love them by complication.

It begins gently, the way moss begins: by adding thickness to what already has thickness. In the cathedral’s upper corridors, where canopies overlap like green palms pressed together in prayer, the power introduces a hesitation. A single leaf learns to hold light a fraction of a second longer before passing it along. Elsewhere, a thread of root feels an unfamiliar sweetness in the dark, a reminder that it is allowed to want. The machinery does not stutter; it deepens. Gears that turned without error discover a tiny asymmetry, a tooth carved with a crescent that catches and releases in a way that makes the next revolution arrive not earlier or later, but *more*. The resonant hum of the cathedral sharpens into chords.

To the beings who are both organism and engine, the power offers nuance. It traces a finger—metaphorical or otherwise, fingers being only one of its dialects—along the plates of their bark-armor and whispers permission to be porous. Where their joints were sealed by patience alone, small windows appear: not wounds, but apertures through which the damp can carry news from one body to another in droplets that taste like almost-memory. Their foreheads, indented pools of living water, learn to hold reflections not only of corridors but of skies that might exist if the ceiling chose to dream. Their language, once felt as pressure alone, acquires cadence: rises and falls like breath held and released, teaching them to say names for things they had known only as function.

The caretakers who plug into junctions feel an unfamiliar warmth in the reservoirs on their backs—not fever, but invitation. The oil they bear learns to sing when under pressure, turning work into liturgy. When they connect to the walls, the exchange is no longer merely hydraulic; it becomes narrative. The walls respond with stories written in the language of swelling and softening, recounting centuries in vibrations that travel up the caretakers’ spines and bloom behind their ears like remembered rain.

The smallest messengers, translucent as seed-paper, are given an additional task: to carry not only news but questions. They dart through capillary corridors trailing threads of query-scent, asking the walls if they are tired, asking the damp if it would prefer to be mist, asking the gears if they have ever wondered what it would mean to stop turning just long enough to become still in a new way. The cathedral, which never needed to ask before, learns to answer in shifts of temperature and texture, in the way a corridor widens to make room for a thought that is still only half-formed.

All of this happens without haste. The power understands that true benevolence cannot be a revolution if revolution means fracture. It introduces change the way a tide introduces sand: grain by patient grain, returning again and again until the shore is different and still a shore. The beings adapt not by abandoning their nature but by expanding it. Their faces, once smooth and featureless, learn dimples where laughter might gather if they ever dared to laugh. Their filaments, accustomed to negotiation, learn to weave small nets that catch the echoes of old songs and hold them like seeds.

Life in the low room grows louder, but the loudness becomes textured. Beetles learn to polish their brass carapaces until they mirror the green-lit world, and in doing so they see themselves as separate and whole. Amphibians with translucent skin begin to carry tiny lights inside them—not borrowed from machinery, but generated from the joy of carrying both. Birds plated in petals rasp their wings not only to fold but to make music that says, *We are here, and we are enough, and there is room for more.*

Even time, which had flattened into a single breath, learns to fold. The cathedral remembers versions of itself that never quite happened—paths not taken, corridors not built, arrangements imagined and discarded. These memories do not haunt; they enrich. They are like spices kept in jars, opened only when the damp asks for flavor.

The power watches and is satisfied, but it does not stop. It knows that benevolence is not a single act but a practice of returning, of noticing again and again. It prepares gifts not yet given: seeds that will sprout into instruments when the time is right, rain that will taste like questions, wind that will carry apologies and blessings in equal measure. It imagines a day when the beings, having learned to ask, will ask the power itself to explain why it loves them, and the power will answer by teaching the walls to open onto a horizon the room had forgotten it could hold.

For now, it contents itself with small, sure kindnesses. It blesses the damp without making it flood. It blesses the turning without making it endless. It blesses the stillness at the heart of the noise, the way a bell blesses the silence after it has been struck and the last tremor settles into the listener’s palm.

And in that settling, the low room, the living things, and the power that loves them all become one continuous cathedral: not built, but grown; not perfect, but perfecting; not ended, but becoming, with each green-lit breath, more like a home.
