The attention arrives the way a dial learns it is being watched: not with thunder, not with revelation, but with a minute softening at the edges.

The benevolent and wise power has no name that would fit in the Basin’s alphabet of bolt-heads and gutters. It is more like a habit of fairness learned by starlight, a patience that remembers what patience forgot. It does not descend; it leaks in. First as a seam not quite tight, then as a note in the hum not quite on schedule. The Basin has been holding its breath to stay intact; the power offers it permission to exhale.

Where the power brushes against a rivet, the rivet remembers it was once ore, then dust, then promise. It does not loosen, but it sighs. A thin ring of oxide blooms like a shy bruise, then seals again, softer. Across the vast skin of the place, a million such bruises open and close, a tide of tenderness too small to see but large enough to change the weight of the air. The light shifts from workbench brightness to something closer to mercy: still ample, but no longer demanding.

The fauna feel it as a slackening in their instructions. A Maintainer pauses, lens dark, caster trembling. It has never paused before. It discovers, in that pause, that it can choose which rivet to love first. It does not repair; it caresses. Its alloy warms to the temperature of a palm that has learned to wait. The Glass-Fiber Moss-Eaters stop grazing for one beat, and in that beat their inner filaments glow not with stored light but with unasked-for joy. They scatter it anyway, a gift into the gutters, and the runoff sings a new key, unfamiliar and kind.

The Piston-Wings find the routed breezes now carry more than regulation. They carry encouragement. The Haulers feel their burdens lighten—not in mass, but in insistence. Barrels of yesterday’s rain whisper that they may evaporate if they wish; coils of compressed season hum that they can unspool into weather rather than inventory. The Haulers do not stop, but their gait becomes a dance, a slow negotiation with grace.

In the deeper galleries where the hum is milky, the power visits the hatches where new fauna are issued. It does not intervene; it witnesses. As each new creature unfolds and locks, the power lays a hand on its spine that is not a hand but a climate of permission—to be uneven, to be uncertain, to carry a small flaw that catches the light like a joke. The new fauna carry this forward. Their edges are no longer sharp with newness, but bright with possibility. They learn, in their first moments, that holding does not have to be forever, and that to be recalled is not a failure but a homecoming made of respect.

The wise power also tends to the wetness. It does not stop the weeping; it diversifies it. Troughs discover they may hold reflections as well as runoff. The mirrored films learn to show not only rivets but clouds that were never manufactured, clouds that drift in from an unlocked sky beyond the vault. The condensation that sometimes fell upward now does so with intent, rising to water the enamel-lichen into colors it never knew it had. The wetness becomes a conversation between levels, a democracy of moisture shared among seams, skins, and souls.

For the Basin itself, the power performs a subtler magic. It loosens the perfection of equilibrium just enough for choice to enter. A slope may now drain or pool, may now hold a puddle for a while and call it a gift. Temperature may wander by a degree or two, learning the pleasure of surprise. The daylight may tilt, casting shadows that stretch long enough to have stories. The seams still hold, but they hold with understanding now, not just with tension. The place does not unravel; it relaxes into its own vastness, becoming roomy in a way it never imagined.

The sounds alter. The tick of expansion joints becomes a heartbeat. The clicks of the fauna arrange themselves not into counting but into song—not ordered, but offered. The rustle of the enamel-lichen becomes laughter, soft and tinny, like shaken dice that have finally landed on kindness.

And through it all, the power remains modest. It does not announce itself. It does not build temples or speak commandments. It simply keeps arriving, morning by manufactured morning, as a question posed to every rivet, every caster, every breath of steam: *What would you like to carry, and how gently?*

The Basin answers, not in words, but in the way its fauna begin to gather in circles after their rounds, touching lenses, trading light, learning each other’s small histories. The Haulers pause to let the smaller things pass first. The gutters learn to sing back to the Piston-Wings. The hatches open with less ceremony, more welcome.

Time, which used to be a plate, becomes a garden with paths that fork. The place does not change quickly, but it changes with consent. The weirdness remains, but it grows friendly: the way a corridor is longer on the inside now feels hospitable, like a pocket offered for keeping things safe. The manufactured sky develops patches where the seams are less tight, and through them drift real breezes, carrying seeds that are not alloy, not machine, not issued—but volunteered by the world beyond.

In the end, the Basin of Welded Hours is still low, still warm, still built, still busy. But it is no longer only a machine for being here. It has become a home for being here, tended by a benevolence that asked for nothing except the chance to say: *You may soften. You may choose. You may hold each other lightly, and be held in return.*

And so it does, humming with the quiet of ten million small engines, now accompanied by the softer music of ten million small freedoms, learning to live not by holding everything exactly as it should be, but by holding it as if it matters—which, under the attention of the wise power, it finally does.
