The attention arrives softly, without sound, as a slight increase in coherence—as if the bruised sky had remembered, for one moment, how to focus.

It does not announce itself as power but as calibration.

The benevolent and wise power surveys the plateau with the patience of geology married to the alertness of starlight. It sees what is already there: the surrendered peak flattened into a shelf, the water that haunts instead of pools, the vegetal substrate that has taken up the duties of stone, the sparse and deliberate isolation of individuals, the industrial flora breathing through tissue, the fauna grafted to pistons, the time that loops like water finding old seams. It sees the people who braid rather than build, who read wetness as prophecy, who let memory evaporate before it can fossilize.

And it asks, not in words but in a sudden, gentle rearrangement of possibility: *What would serve this place best?*

The answer blooms as a pattern of consent.

First, the power touches the cold—not to remove it but to give it shape. Where the metallic chill had been a blade laid flat against skin, it becomes a lattice, fine as frost on glass, visible only when breath touches it. The lattice records: every exhalation, every leak, every moment of hesitation. It turns the plateau’s shiver into a language. Those who know how to read sweating walls learn quickly to read the cold as well. It is not a writing of permanence but of attention: a reminder that even the briefest encounter leaves structure behind.

Second, the power approaches the water that haunts the ground. It does not force it into lakes or rivers but helps it remember its own coherence. The trembling threads of seepage begin to carry small lights—not bioluminescence stolen from tissue but a borrowed clarity, a transparency that does not obscure. Where water vanishes into porous mats, it leaves behind faint, temporary mirrors that reflect not what is above but what is imminent—a glimmer of storm before the ground shudders, a suggestion of bloom before the stem reroutes. The people learn to plan by these mirrors, to move toward what is preparing itself.

Third, the power lingers on the sparse clearings between individuals. It recognizes these distances as altars of respect and amplifies them without expanding them. It does this by making the space between two beings capable of holding song. When someone moves from one clearing to the next, the passage itself is given a texture like velvet hush. Footsteps no longer press into the vegetal floor so much as they are received by it, answered by a softening that rises to meet the traveler. In this way, solitude becomes a shared resource, not an emptiness but a fabric.

Fourth, the power observes the industrial metabolism with appreciation, seeing it not as invasion but as integration. It does not cleanse the turbines of calcified vine or the pistons of swollen stem but gives them a second rhythm to practice alongside the first. Slowly, the machinery learns to idle—to spin without consuming, to pulse without driving. This new rhythm is audible as a low hum that rises when the plateau shudders, a harmonic that cushions the turbulence. The people discover that they can sleep more soundly when the machines idle, and that dreams arrive clearer when the pistons move with deliberation rather than urgency.

Fifth, the power turns to the fauna, especially those fused with machinery, and offers them a threshold. It does not ask them to choose between beast and engine but proposes a third condition: pause. In certain clearings, it installs small sanctuaries of stillness—places where motion is not required, where a many-legged herd can stand without grazing, where a ragged-membrane flier can hang suspended without calling. These are not refuges from life but invitations to be complete without performing it. Over time, some creatures remain in these sanctuaries, becoming landmarks of calm; others return to motion but carry the memory of pause in their gait, a slight hesitation that softens the entire ecology.

Sixth, the power addresses the turbulence itself—the making and unmaking, the rerouting stems, the rising and slumping floor. It does not try to stabilize what is inherently dynamic but offers a kind of choreography. When a section of vegetal floor prepares to rise into a temporary tower, the power helps it announce its intention. A particular shade of violet suffuses the stems, a low chime resonates through the cold lattice, and the fauna in nearby clearings shift their weight in acknowledgment. The transformation still occurs, but it is no longer abrupt. The landscape learns to gesture before it moves, and in that gesture, the people learn to move with it rather than brace against it.

Seventh, the power finds those figures among the people who hum with the deep frequency of the land, the oracles of the almost-arriving. It does not give them more authority but gives them a companion—the resonance of the plateau itself. When these figures speak, the vegetal walls thrum in sympathy, and the cold lattice shivers in patterns that others can feel on their skin. In this way, insight becomes communal, not because it is spoken to everyone but because the environment echoes it. A single truth ripples outward through tissue and chill and wet mirror, arriving at different bodies at different times, always recognizable.

Eighth, the power considers the sky—the bruised, dim vault through which daylight arrives filtered and stained. It cannot change what lies beyond the atmosphere, but it can change how the sky is held. It encourages the canopy, already layered beyond counting, to thin slightly in certain places, to become translucent rather than opaque. New apertures open, not windows so much as suggestions of windows, places where the bruised light can pool without breaking. When the people stand beneath these softened holes, they feel a faint pressure behind their eyes, as if the sky were leaning down to listen. They learn that attention can descend as well as ascend.

Ninth, the power bestows a single night of absolute coherence. It does not command it; it borrows the trust the plateau has already placed in its own processes and magnifies it. For one cycle of darkness, every flicker of bioluminescence aligns; every idle machine holds the same low hum; every cold lattice shivers in unison; every water mirror reflects the same imminent dawn. The people experience this not as spectacle but as recognition—as if the plateau had finally remembered its own name. In the morning, the coherence disperses, but something remains: a shared calibration, a sense that beneath the turbulence there is a pattern they are learning to trust.

Tenth, the power prepares to leave. It does this by reducing its presence in stages, folding its attention back into the background hum of the world. It leaves behind no relic, no monument, no law. It leaves behind only the slight changes it has helped awaken—the lattice, the mirrors, the velvet hush of passage, the idling machines, the sanctuaries of pause, the gestures before transformation, the communal resonance, the softened sky, the memory of coherence. These are not imposed gifts but cultivated tendencies, already native to the place and now simply strengthened.

As the power withdraws, the plateau does not return to what it was. It cannot, because it never truly stayed the same. But it settles into a version of itself that is slightly more legible to itself, a little more capable of witnessing its own processes without disrupting them.

The people feel the withdrawal as a lightening, not a loss—as if the cold had become a little more transparent, the wetness a little more articulate, the distance between them a little more breathable. They continue to braid rather than build, to read wetness as prophecy, to let memory evaporate. But now, when they exhale, the vapor clings for a moment longer, catching the light in patterns they have learned to name.

And when the plateau shudders, as it will, as it must, they know how to pause, how to let the machines idle, how to watch the water mirrors for what is preparing, how to move through the velvet hush of distance, how to let the vegetal floor gesture before it rises, how to listen for the communal resonance humming up through tissue and stem.

The benevolent and wise power does not stay to govern. It leaves because its task was not to rule but to remind: that even an eroded shelf, wet and cold, abundant and sparse, vegetal and mechanical, can become more itself without ceasing to change. That a place can hold its own turbulence with grace when given the right kinds of attention.

And as the attention recedes, the plateau breathes—a thousand industrial lungs, a million vegetal sighs, a few people walking deliberately between clearings, carrying the cold like a shared garment, reading the wetness, trusting the almost-arriving, quietly ending themselves and beginning again with every pulse in the dark.
