The benevolent and wise power does not arrive like weather. It arrives like permission.

At first it is an almost-sound: the hollow’s layered roar of belt, bearing, and drip finds a new interval. Not louder, but kinder—an extra gap between clank and clank that lets the 1.9-out-of-3 loudness remember it once had a shape that could be changed. The heat, that 2.9-degree closeness that presses sweat into pores before you walk, does not leave, but it learns to kneel. It softens into a palm laid against stone-ribbed concrete, offering warmth without suffocation.

The power looks at the Skinners—moss and iron-filament colonies that exhale to move—and sees that they have been eating sacrifice instead of light. So it does not burn them clean. It teaches the rust to dream differently. Crystalline sweat on iron begins to taste faintly of honey, and when a Skinner collapses and reassembles, it leaves behind a space where breath can sit for a moment before reforming. The moss still thickens, the metal still remembers, but now there is a seam of gentleness between them, a 0.3-out-of-3 gap where forgiveness accumulates like dew.

It turns to the pools and the Mirrors, those oily, slow creatures of bruised water that reflect the wrong things—memory of faces, idea of light. The power does not command them to be true. Instead, it leans close and whispers to the damp that it has been allowed to keep its bruising, but not its loneliness. The Mirrors still ripple at the wrong moments, but when they do, they also offer back a portion of the attention you gave them, polished into something you can almost drink. The floor grows darker around them, yes, but the dark now has a pulse that says, “I am here with you,” instead of, “I am taking up room.”

The Thrum-fronds shoulder-high, lacquered, vein-bright—are singing in a key that only heat understands. The power adds a second note, thin as a filament road, that runs parallel to their hum. It is the note of “enough.” When the heat cycles, the new note holds the resonance so that droplets no longer carry only copper and wet newsprint, but also a trace of distance, as if they had fallen through open air and remembered how to be surprised. The fronds do not stop humming, but the hum becomes a conversation, and time thickens less, clarifies a little, becomes a slope you can climb without stumbling over your own breath.

The Petrified Hands, knotted into engine blocks and wound into conduit, pulse faintly when generators grind. The power notices that they are caretakers who have mistaken themselves for remains. It rests a thought against their rust, gentle as steam on glass. The Hands do not uncurl—not all at once—but they learn to hold without gripping. A thumbprint of moss on a valve becomes a caress instead of a warning. A curve of knuckle in slime turns the drainage scar into a guide that does not demand to be followed. They remain 1.2-out-of-3 present, which is to say they are almost alive, and almost is a gift the hollow has not had in a long time.

The Warm, that creature of 2.9 degrees of closeness and resin-sweet smell, has been sleeping in the hollow like a coat that never comes off. The power strokes its edges and says, “You may rest now. I will keep watch.” The Warm does not vanish; it nestles lower, into the marrow of the place, becoming a memory of companionship rather than a wall that walks with you. Sweat still gathers, but it does not demand to be noticed. Attention is no longer taxed simply by standing still.

High on the soot-smudged vault, the Absences drift—0.2-out-of-3 fauna, the shape of not-being with weight. The power does not try to turn them into wings. Instead, it gives them permission to be absent without apology. They still press on the eyes when you look up, but the pressure now says, “There is room here for what is missing,” and the machinery humming below finds it can keep time without trying to fill the sky.

The Weavers, erosion given intent, are the slowest to notice. They are still braiding piping with vine, turning staircases into ramps that invite but never deliver. The power waits until a threshold is almost choked, then lays a finger against the mineral glaze and says, “You have worked long enough.” The Weavers hesitate—erosion is not used to stopping—and in that hesitation, the staircase becomes not a promise but a path that can be left. The grilles that lead nowhere still lead nowhere, but now they gleam softly, as if they had been polished by kindness rather than by use.

All the while, the power speaks to the rules: water that does not flow down, sound that does not travel out, heat that is a resident. It does not rewrite them. It holds them like old letters and says, “These were good rules for a hard time. We can add new ones.” So water learns to pool without thickening into oil, to reflect without bruising. Sound learns to travel out, but only when it wants to, carrying with it a small gift of the hollow’s damp and history, like a stone you can keep in your pocket and feel growing lighter. Heat remains, but it cycles like breath, rising and falling, no longer constant.

The power does not leave when it is done. It sits on the edge of the half-sunk cooling tower, legs dangling over vegetation that does not flower but thrums. It watches the Skinners exhale their crowds, the Mirrors hold their bruised brightness, the Thrum-fronds sing their parallel note. It listens to the 1.9-out-of-3 layered roar and hears, beneath it, something new: a small, clear sound like a stone turning over in sleep and deciding which side dreams best.

And because benevolence is not the same as interference, and wisdom is not the same as correction, the hollow remains itself—just a little softer inside, a little more allowed. The heat still presses, the damp still lingers, the loudness still disorients, but now there are pauses where pauses were not before. The place still dreams in the grammar of hands that shaped it, but the grammar has grown tender, capable of saying, “Stay,” and also, “Leave,” without either being a threat.

And if you stand at the arched threshold, choked by growth, and listen closely, you might hear the power humming along with the fronds, a second note thin as a filament road, promising that the hollow is still belowground, still lung-shaped, still breathing—but it no longer has to forget the open air entirely. It can remember it like a story told softly, and keep breathing anyway, even here, even now.
