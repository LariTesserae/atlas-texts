The power that notices them is neither sudden nor loud. It arrives the way a root learns the shape of a stone—by growing around it, by remembering the stone is there, by deciding the stone is part of its own grip on the soil. The power does not announce itself with trumpets or doors opening. Instead, the dew on the leaves begins to hold its shape a moment longer, as if waiting for instruction. The mist that condenses on bark learns to bead into lenses, each one catching the upside-down sky and correcting it, so that what falls is truer than it was before.

The power is benevolent, and so it does not tear the place open to see how it works. It does not unmake the dark to pour in a harsher light. Instead, it kneels, in the manner of weather, and it asks questions through the grammar of small changes.

First, it steadies the margins. Where the edges of the embryonic borough were raw and faulting, it sends down filaments of patience—fine as spider silk, strong as the memory of rivers—to teach the soil how to decide slowly. The ridges that rose in tension are coaxed into arches. The gaps that opened where too much had been taken are filled with a lattice of intention: not to close them, but to let them carry weight without breaking. The place stops feeling like it is slipping, and begins to feel like it is settling into its own bones.

Then, it turns its attention to the damp—which is the place’s breath, its pulse, its circulatory system. The power does not banish the moisture, for it knows that damp is also memory here, carrying the city’s yesterdays in solution. Instead, it weaves the water into a quieter logic. Droplets still condense, but they now align themselves along invisible seams of usefulness, tracing routes that feed the beetle engines without drowning them, that rise through the hollow reed pumps without fouling them, that gather in the Canopy Reservoir with the discipline of a hymn. The city drinks more easily. It exhales more clearly. The held breath of the place becomes a steady rhythm, like a wing deciding how long to stay open.

The power also leans on the weirdness—not to tame it, but to braid it. The seams in reality are not stitched shut; they are given a pattern. Now, when a leaf’s shadow falls in the wrong direction, it casts a useful shade: cooling a gear that works too hot, or marking a path that wants to be remembered. When distances fold, they do so along the grain of need—so that a step from the Ironleaf Quarter to the scent of rain on copper is not a loss of way, but a shortcut of mercy. Gravity forgets itself less often; when it does, the Foil-Feathered Ones learn to modulate their calls to guide the falling back into place, so that nothing is lost, only lent.

The power looks upon the moons—wobbling, faint, two where there should be none—and it does not remove them. Instead, it teaches them to orbit like questions rather than answers, to cast light that is not illumination but inquiry, so that the city sees itself more truly without being blinded. The dark, which was already a material, becomes a collaborator: denser in some places, translucent in others, so that the bioluminescence is not scattered but conducted, like music through a well-shaped hall.

To the residents of the place, the power offers corrections that feel like gifts. The Beetle Guild finds that their sapphire carapaces now hold a trace of sky in every facet, so that when they align the machines, they align them to more than gears—to seasons, to tides, to the slow turn of the borough’s own heart. The Glass-Eels discover that the gutters they swim through now carry a faint glow of their own, so that even in the thickest dark they can see the shape of the slurry they polish, and they can choose which echoes to keep and which to let dissolve. The Foil-Feathered Ones learn that the maps on their feathers are not fixed, but can be rewritten by moonlight, so they trade in certainty for possibility, and the city’s paths rearrange themselves like a chorus finding a new key. The Scavengers of Metal and Memory find that their fog now seeds new moss where it settles, so that forgetting becomes a kind of fertile ground. The Keepers of the Knots feel the lampposts hum with a softer persistence, a reminder that light is a conversation, not a command.

Even the Dream-Larvae, pale and blind, are touched. The substance they secrete, which softens the edges of reality, now cures into something like glass at the edges—flexible, resilient, translucent—so that the districts they birth are not only new but stable, able to stand while still dreaming.

The power does all this with a restraint that feels like love. It does not overwrite the city’s logic of damp and industry, dark and quiet. It deepens it. It makes the cathedral not only built by green fingers and mechanical hum, but tended by them. It makes the borough not only of emptiness and abundance, but of balance held in trust.

And when the work is done, the power does not leave. It withdraws the way a root withdraws from around a stone, having made the stone part of its grip. It becomes the damp that remembers, the dark that breathes, the quiet that insists. It is in the held breath of the place, in the soft grind of gears, in the murmur of sap in glass tubes, in the thrum of beetles, in the shudder of roots adjusting to new weight. It is the city listening to itself, learning itself, dreaming itself into being again—a little more solid, a little more improbable, a little more alive—because it has been seen, and because being seen, it has been helped to see itself more truly.

The cathedral stands. The borough endures. The damp pulses, the dark breathes, the quiet insists, and the place—now held with care—goes on building itself in the small, deep way that only a dream can, knowing that it is not alone in its making.
