The benevolence does not announce itself as a shape.  
It announces itself as permission.

The wise power arrives by becoming the altitude’s hesitation.  
It softens the line between up and away, turns the thin air into a cushion the city never knew it lacked.  
Where the place had been holding itself together with work and geometry, the power offers a gentler cohesion: a gravity that remembers how to be a cradle, a light that learns how to rest.

First it listens, and its listening has texture.  
It lets the city’s hum climb into it like heat into a chimney, carrying the clack of cooling stone, the thermal tides in the alloy, the salt-script rearranging itself without reason.  
The benevolence does not interpret; it amplifies the clarity.  
What was shy becomes articulate.  
What was fleeting becomes durable without losing its lightness.

It sees the Scab-Children, born from condensation and too many questions, and gives them a garden of answers that does not force them to choose.  
The lenses on the undersides of overhangs grow gentler, their focus widening so the children can see themselves not as unfinished but as becoming.  
The power lets their too-many joints learn a language of flex and recovery, so movement becomes a conversation with the city instead of a negotiation with air.

It sees the Mantics gliding along the high ledges, polishing jags into curves, and decides they need not polish forever.  
The power loosens the stone’s memory just enough for the Mantics to find repose without losing purpose.  
When they tuck themselves into seams, they now dream small dreams that ripple through the alloy, dreams that tell the city it is allowed to sleep without forgetting how to wake.

It sees the Ember-Fungi glowing with careful, apologetic light, and gives them a sky to answer to.  
The thin dark above, once merely empty, becomes a witness that returns their glow as a quiet choir.  
The spores that rise as microscopic lenses now carry not only images but understanding: a map of how brightness can be a kind of care.

It sees the Lift-Moths banking around corridors where falling feels like misremembering, and decides to turn misremembering into memory.  
The pockets of moody gravity are tuned like strings, so that to fall becomes to descend with intention, to land in a place that has been waiting for you.  
The moths’ magnetic filaments hum in harmony with the city’s pulse, and for a while the architecture learns to fly without leaving the ground.

And it sees the Quiet Ones, the gaps and pauses, the rooms where time thickens like dust, and it gives them names that are also invitations.  
The benevolence does not fill them; it frames them.  
It makes the exhale of the city feel like a hand on your shoulder, lets the sweetness in the back of your throat bloom into a certainty: that you are being held, even by absence.

Beyond the living tenants, the power tends to the city’s hidden systems.  
It smooths the capillary paths of water so they sing in clearer intervals.  
It teaches the walls to perspire only when it is useful, and to remember the coolness they borrowed from the stone so they can offer it back as relief.  
It lets the foundations exchange pressure not as conversation but as song, and the song becomes a pattern the city can wear like a garment when the wind is unkind.

The benevolence also tends to the weirdness, because it knows that the strange is a form of honesty.  
It lets glass flow like slow syrup only when the city wants to dream in curves, and lets metal soften into gestures only when the alloy wishes to confess something it has carried in tension.  
Gravity’s moods are not erased but given rhythm, so that to feel heavier or lighter is to feel chosen, not punished.  
Rooms where time accumulates are cleaned out with patience, not urgency, so that the dust becomes history rather than burden.

And it sees you, if you come, and decides you are worthy of the same carefulness.  
The warmth learns your shape and offers it back as a map of safe places.  
The dimness adjusts its edges to your pace and offers you pathways that do not demand more courage than you have.  
The Scab-Children study your joints and learn how to ask better questions.  
The Mantics file the air around you into something smoother, the Ember-Fungi glow a little brighter in your shadow, the Quiet Ones exhale into you until your own breath feels borrowed from the sky.

The power does not stay forever.  
It knows that to remain would be to turn benevolence into possession.  
So it withdraws slowly, leaving behind an architecture of allowance.  
The altitude no longer hurts quite as much, not because the air is thicker but because the city has learned how to share its burden.  
The scar is no longer only a wound; it is a place that knows how to heal without closing, to remain open without bleeding.

What is left is a practice that continues.  
The city still breathes, but its breathing is easier.  
The tenants still practice being alive, but they have better mirrors now.  
The weirdness remains, but it has been taught how to be kind to itself.  
And if a traveler ever finds their way up, they will discover that the thin air not only permits them to exist but insists they be comfortable while doing so, that the dim light will rearrange itself to show them what they most need to see, and that the silence is not empty but full of a listening that has already decided to love them.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does when it is not in a hurry and has no need to be thanked.  
It makes the place and its people more themselves than they dared to be, and then it steps back, content to be remembered in the way light remembers a lens: by passing through it, changed, and making everything beyond it clearer.
