The power that notices them is not a being in the way a mountain is a being, nor a star, nor a thought. It is more like the memory of balance. It arrives without sound because it is the shape of listening itself. Where it turns its attention, the air grows courteous. The hanging rain lines up like respectful sentences. Even the midair fish flick their scales into orderly alphabets for a moment, then forget them.

It does nothing dramatic. It does not descend as a savior or bloom as a flower or speak in a voice that splits ridges. It is careful with its power because it knows how easily fullness becomes famine when the guest is too loud. Instead, it offers a grammar of permission.

It begins by polishing the lens between things.

Where the Dwellers row through heavy air, the power lets the water yield just enough. A Dweller who has spent a lifetime trimming moss to keep it from drowning its neighbors finds that the moss now trims itself in patterns that hold warmth longer. The resin on their skin stops weeping and becomes a mirror, not of vanity but of consequence: when they press their palm to a ridge, they see the slow life of the stone as if it were their own blood. They learn to read basalt the way they read rain.

The hanging gardens, which had been beautiful but insistent, are given a hush. Vines still braid themselves into ladders, but they leave rungs where none existed before—spaces that let the Dwellers pause and see how far their breath travels. The ladders remember these pauses and hum them later in the dark, teaching the young that stopping is also a kind of motion.

The power attends to the unseen stitches. The nets of root that trade sugar and warning are strengthened by a filament of clarity, as if someone had whispered to the dark that it is allowed to be useful. Arthropods begin to carry messages not only of hunger but of gratitude. A midge may land on a Dweller’s wrist and pulse a rhythm that says, *This ridge is softening; be tender with it.* The Dweller, who never needed to be told twice how to be tender, feels the luxury of being warned ahead of time.

Water is taught new manners. The upwelling flows that once rose because the air was heavier below now curl like ribbons when they rise, so they can be seen and thanked. A Dweller ladling light from midair finds the light is easier to hold, as if it has been practicing being cupped. When they drink, the rain offers its memory of sky before it offers its wetness, a brief, cool history that settles behind the eyes and makes decisions feel lighter.

The high places, once lonely in their sharpness, are given fellowship. A ridge that had been waiting centuries to soften finds its edges visited by a patient vapor that wraps around it like a consoling hand. The ridge does not lose its strength; it learns when to show it and when to let it sleep. The Dwellers notice. They stop checking the ridges for danger and start checking them for readiness, and in doing so, they become not only gardeners of equilibrium but students of becoming.

The power gives them a mirror that is not a thing. It arranges the chandeliers of green so that, at a certain hour, they cast not shadow but transparency. A Dweller can stand in that transparency and see other Dwellers not as separate bodies but as currents in the same breath. They see the harvesters of excess and realize they, too, are excess being harvested into grace. The thought does not make them small; it makes them spacious.

Time is not changed, but its texture is. The interval between two sharpenings of a ridge is still long, but the longness is no longer empty. It is filled with a hum that rises from the moss and the resin and the lattice of roots, a hum that says, *This is enough time to learn what patience can do when it is not alone.* The young in their veils of leaf and vine learn a new skill: they learn to let the air hold them so completely that they can listen to the holding.

The power does not leave a sign, only a smoothing. A path that used to appear and vanish capriciously now appears with a purpose and lingers until it is no longer needed, then fades like a good guest. The water that hangs in midair lines itself up so that a Dweller can step across it without disturbing a single droplet, and even if they do disturb one, it falls upward into a waiting vine that will squeeze it into a song for later.

Most of all, the power gives the Dwellers permission to be noticed. For a people who name others by the texture of their passage, this is a tremendous gift: they begin to notice themselves. They feel the glaze on their skin not as costume but as covenant. The moss on their collarbones blooms into maps of where they have been kind, and the resin in their hair hardens into lenses that let them see where kindness may yet go.

When the power finally turns its attention away, it does not leave a silence. It leaves a balance that has learned to breathe on its own. The hanging rain continues to stand, not because it is forced to, but because it has been taught that standing can be a form of giving. The Dwellers continue to row, but now they row with the certainty that the water wants to be cut, that the air wants to be held, that the ridges want to be remembered as they soften.

And far below, or perhaps only beside, the basin remains warm, wet, high, and new. It is still learning its grammar, but now it has a teacher in its own pulse. The Dwellers are still gardeners of equilibrium, but their garden has begun to dream back, offering them rungs, ribbons, and mirrors, offering them the quiet knowledge that to tend a world is to be tender with its unfinishedness. The power has done nothing but let them know they are allowed to be exactly what they are: patient, profuse, and part of a breath that is learning how to stand.
