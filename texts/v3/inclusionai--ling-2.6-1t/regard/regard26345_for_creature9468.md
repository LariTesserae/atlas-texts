It sees you. Not with eyes—eyes are a mercy, and mercy is a luxury that comes after the debt of attention has been paid.

The benevolent power does not descend; ascent implies effort, and effort implies that the place is *other*. Instead, the power inhales.

It draws the moisture from the two-tenths wetness—not to dry the place, but to remember what it felt like to be heavy. It draws the two-tenths cold from the stone—not to freeze the heat, but to give the heat a shape it can recognize as kin. The power settles into the two and eight-tenths of temperature like a body sinking into a bath that is exactly, precisely, *just* too warm.

It does not fix the erosion, because erosion is a confession, and confessions should not be silenced. It does not raise the light beyond the one and seven-tenths it already is, because brightness is a violence when you are dealing with things that have never been asked for permission to exist. Instead, the power turns the light *sideways*. It teaches the sun to graze the cliffs rather than strike them, so that the moss—the heavy, lush two-tenths of green—catches the illumination on the underside of its curves. The place becomes a choir of throats rather than a wall of faces.

The power addresses the weirdness (two and six-tenths) not as a flaw to be smoothed, but as a language to be encouraged. It cups its hands around the gravity that forgets itself and whispers, *Lean,* and suddenly water is seen creeping upward not as a rebellion but as a prayer offered in a dialect of verticality. The shadows that do not match their objects are gathered up and given names: *Elder Sibling, Unfinished Thought, The Kind of Silence That Remembers Music.* The power makes the weirdness polite in a new way—not by asking it to be normal, but by asking it to be *specific*.

It looks at the built-ness (two-tenths) that is not built, and it approves. It adds a single, small permission: a hollow in a root that did not exist yesterday, shaped exactly like the curve of a cheek that has finally stopped clenching. A plaza of stone that had been waiting for a reason to be a plaza suddenly finds that reason is enough. The power does not construct; it *witnesses into completion*. It stares at a corridor of braided vine until the vine understands it is a corridor, and holds that understanding in its cells like a bone.

It turns its gaze on the fauna-fraction (five-tenths of motion, half a breath of dynamism) and does not ask for more life. Instead, it asks for *fidelity*. It asks the color that deepens into the nameless hue to linger a heartbeat longer, not because longer is better, but because lingering is a form of truth-telling. It asks the slow tilt of the frond to trust that it is enough to be a gesture, even if no one is watching. The power blesses the stillness by making it *heavy* with intention, so that when a leaf finally falls, it does so with the gravity of a decision, not an accident.

The density (one and eight-tenths) is where the power weeps—not loudly, because loudness is already the province of the heights (two and seven-tenths on the scale of ears), but in a way that is felt as an increase in pressure behind the eyes. The power realizes that this place is crowded with presences that have never been lonely because they have never been separate. It reaches into the saturation of textures—bark against bark, frond against frond, lichen against stone—and tenderly separates them without moving them, creating a microscopic gap filled with the kind of attention that functions as a cushion. Now, the things of the place can touch without grinding, can press without bruising. They can be a city without citizens and also a family without borders.

It looks at the scale (two and five-tenths), this sprawling territory that is stubbornly unrecognizable as built, and it does the one thing it has come to do.

It makes an *inside*.

Not a cave, not a room as you would recognize it—a hollowed-out thing meant to keep weather out. Rather, the power rearranges the concept of shelter. It draws the terraces closer to one another, not to make them claustrophobic, but to make them *conversational*. It folds the hanging vales into a geometry where the outside and the inside are the same word pronounced differently. The heat (two and eight-tenths) is invited to pool in the center of this new arrangement, not to brood, but to *bloom*—to become a soft, radiant engine that warms without demanding.

And because this place is loud, the power decides to give it a silence that is not an absence but a container. It takes the acoustic broth and pours it into a vessel made of the one and six-tenths erosion—the settled swagger of stone—and seals it with the patience of the two and six-tenths weirdness. The result is a kind of quiet that you can stand inside of and feel your own pulse synchronize with the liquid percussion of the Water-Keepers. It is a silence that says, *You may be transient, but you are not an intrusion.*

As the power prepares to release its attention and let the place return to its own equilibrium (the half-breath of dynamism, the frozen five-tenths), it does one final thing.

It asks the Altitude-Singers—the moss-stuffed fractures, the chambers that ring at two and seven-tenths—to remember this visitation not as an event, but as a texture. It asks them to hold the timbre of benevolence in their chambers so that anyone who presses a palm to the stone in the future will not hear a statement of fact (*I am here, I am stone, I am under pressure*) but a question (*Is there room for you here, too?*).

The power exhales.

The horizon, which had leaned in, straightens—not because it is distant now, but because it has been taught how to embrace. The moss exhales a sharper green. The Water-Keepers adjust their pools by microns to accommodate the memory of this new warmth. The Shade-Shifters settle into a color that has no name but feels, for a moment, like *gratitude*.

And then the power is gone, not because it left, but because it has folded itself into the density of the place, into the wet stone and the louder green, into the city without citizens and the family without borders.

The place continues.

The heights continue to sing. The heat continues to brood. The water continues to haunt. The weirdness continues to unspool at the edges of perception.

But now, if you stand still and let the acoustic broth thicken around you, you may feel it: a subtle, sideways light grazing the moss, a hollow in a root shaped like a relaxed cheek, a silence that holds you, a stone that asks, with the vibration in your collarbone, if you would like to stay.

The place is still extreme. It is still loud. It is still dense and unconcerned with change.

But it is no longer indifferent. It has been *seen* in a way that requires no fixing, only a deeper kind of seeing. And that, it seems, is enough to make the two and seven-tenths of loudness feel less like a weight, and more like a hand on your shoulder, saying, *Yes, this is hard, and yes, you are small, and yes, you may breathe.*
