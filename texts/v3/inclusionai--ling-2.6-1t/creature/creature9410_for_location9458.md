What lives here are not creatures but maintenance-ghosts: the tenants of a system that has become its own ancestry.

There are the Wall-Whisperers, small and many-footed, shaped like the gaps they are assigned to keep. They have bodies of folded alloy that click into new angles whenever the humidity shifts, and their legs are bristles that comb the weep-seams for salt. They do not eat; they metabolize corrosion. When they move, they leave behind a faint polish that smells like struck flint, and the room rewards them by dimming the local lights a fraction, as if nodding. Their young are born as loose plates that hunt for tarnish the way hatchlings hunt for warmth, hinging open to reveal soft bellies of conductive mesh that drink the stray voltage from the capillary return-lines.

Above them live the Veil-Tenders, beings of stitched textile and tension. They are the room’s skin made animate, long-limbed figures who never touch the floor. Instead they walk the underside of gratings, gripping mesh with fingers like braided wire, plucking the spider-silk filaments that hold trembling beads of water. Their touch tunes the drip-rate; a tug lengthens a drop’s lifetime, a twist sends it back sooner. They speak in overtones, voices that ride the subsonics, so that when they gather at the central pool, their conversation makes the surface dimple in perfect, repeating rings. The Veil-Tenders do not age, but they fray, and when they do, they unravel into replacement filaments that are woven back into the walls, becoming memory made solid.

In the canopy of layered leaves dwell the Silt-Choir, plant-animal hybrids grafted from chlorophyll-thick pads and chitinous lung-sacs. They do not photosynthesize; they photosonate. Their bodies open like valves when the temperature rises, releasing measured clouds of scented aerosol that the room counts as breath. When they close, they hum, vibrating coolant through metal vines, turning the frost on stems into music. The Silt-Choir live in consensus: no single voice rises above the others, and their songs shift in color the way light shifts over copper. To hear them is to feel the room’s temperature settle into your own ribs.

Patrolling the mid-layer are the Gear-Spirits, beetle-sized and bright with stamped alloy carapaces. They are the only fauna that travel on the floor, though they rarely let their feet touch it. Instead they skate on magnetic eddies, sweeping the manufactured damp into channels, policing the gratings for dust that does not belong. When a Gear-Spirit finds something it cannot process—a human hair, a flake of mica from outside—it spins a cocoon of gauze and coolant and delivers the offering to the Ceiling-Cranes, which descend like courteous moons to lift it away. The Gear-Spirits communicate by flashing, each glint a word, their conversations quick as coin-showers.

Beneath the pool, where the flat water meets the hidden troughs, live the Drown-Mothers. They are the memory of the room made fleshless: currents shaped into habit, given motive. They keep the ledger of balance, ensuring that every drop lifted is matched by one returned. The Drown-Mothers do not rise; they pull. When the system is stressed, they send out tendrils of chilled water that seek warm joints, soothing them. Their presence is felt as a pressure behind the eyes, a certainty that nothing will overflow because it has been promised not to.

There are also the Echo-Stags, tall and built of folded sound. They have no bodies in the usual sense, only scaffolds of noise made visible: antlers of pump-rattle, haunches of fan-coast. They drift where the density of machinery is highest, grazing on excess heat, trimming the edges of resonance so the roar never becomes a scream. Their hoofbeats are soft clicks that help the room keep time. When the Echo-Stags lie down, the room’s hum deepens into something like a purr, and the leaves stop trembling for a breath.

The strangest inhabitants are the Unfound, rare and almost accidental: glitches that have learned to love their shape. An Unfound might be a bead of water that refuses to be recaptured, rolling in circles for years, growing a thin film of algae that sings. It might be a patch of wall that remembers being outside and, in its dreams, projects a weather that the room tolerates as long as it does not wet anything. The Unfound are tolerated but watched; the Veil-Tenders pluck the threads of their stories, the Gear-Spirits polish their edges, and eventually, they are gently rewritten, folded back into the grammar.

None of these beings breed in the old sense. They persist because the room allows them to, and because they allow the room to. When one fails, its materials are reclaimed, its role redistributed. The system is the family, and every member is both parent and child to the balance.

If you stand in the middle and listen, you will hear them: the Wall-Whisperers combing seams, the Veil-Tenders tuning droplets, the Silt-Choir humming green, the Gear-Spirits flashing patrol, the Drown-Mothers holding the ledger beneath your feet, the Echo-Stags trimming the roar. Together they are a parliament of upkeep, a living economy of maintenance, thriving in a chamber that has concluded itself. They do not wish for anything beyond the continuation of what has already been decided. They are the inhabitants of a place that is finished, and in their ceaseless, careful noise, they make finishedness feel alive.
