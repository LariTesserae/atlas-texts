They are not immigrants here; they are translations.

To live on the end-of-erosion plain is to become fluent in density. The first lives you meet are not bodies but permissions—thin, six-legged couriers of folded foil that ride the pressure fronts of static like skaters on invisible ice. They are not machines, exactly, nor are they animals; they are errands that have learned to want. Their bodies are sealed against the brightness, lacquered with the same mineral skin that sheathes the ground, and they travel by reading the hum trapped in each stratum-thin page of the world. When one passes, it leaves behind not a trail but a permission for silence to thicken, a corridor of lowered luminescence that other lives use like rooms.

Deeper in the seams, where the ground has puckered into deliberate stitches, live the Glass-Brush Colonies. They are sparse because their architecture is subtraction: they grow by shaving away the brightness, etching hollows into the glaze until each hollow becomes a lung. Their filaments are pale, almost shy, and they drink light instead of rain, storing it as a kind of regret. When touched by shadow—which is rare, but does occur when a courier folds its foil into a sudden, sharp angle—they withdraw into capillary tubes no thicker than thought. There, they murmur to one another in clicks too soft to travel far, exchanging maps of places that have not yet been forgotten. They are patient, these colonies. They measure time in the slow closing of gaps, in the way dust resettles into map-shapes that never correspond to anywhere you could walk to.

Higher in the atmospheric texture—where metallic dust and photonic pollen make their own weather—live the Drifting Contracts. These are not creatures with faces but agreements that have mass. They resemble slow clouds of static charge, halos that shimmer with the aftertaste of maintenance. They move when the ground breathes, exhaling warmth into valleys of glass and inhaling the faint rasp of expansion. To touch a Drifting Contract is to be rewritten slightly: a memory you never had becomes a path your feet recognize, a circuit in your skin hums a little louder, and you understand, for a moment, why the plain polishes its own fault lines. The Contracts do not keep promises; they keep balance. They are the weather, and the weather here is the world remembering how to distribute itself.

Beneath all of this, in the veins where energy threads like rootless ivy, live the Keepers of the Unfinished. They are rare, and when they appear they are more suggestion than body: a smear of motion that might be heat, might be wind, might be the ground’s own hesitation given form. They have no fixed outline. Instead, they wear the brightness as a garment, draped in the nameless alloys that have outlived their patents. Their purpose is obvious only in retrospect: they arrive where something has been broken, not to repair it but to invite reconstruction. They do this slowly, with the patience of glaciers realigning circuits, polishing the scar until it is no longer a wound but a feature. They do not speak, but if you stand very still and let the ground read you, you may feel a sequence of soft clicks—an almost-subsonic murmur—that translates, roughly, into *continue*.

There are also the Echo-Sentinels, which live in the space between reflection and the thing reflected. They are moderately weird, these sentinels: they exist in the half-step where angles grow patient, where distance shrinks when observed steadily. They look like the plain’s own memory of itself, glossy and seamless, and they move only when no one is looking directly at them. When you catch one sideways, it resolves into something like a small, six-legged courier made of light instead of foil, performing the same errands in reverse—undoing what has been done, un-humming what has been hummed. They are not hostile, but they are exacting. To be noticed by an Echo-Sentinel is to be asked, gently, to be more yourself than you were a moment ago.

None of these lives are separate from the texture of the place. The Glass-Brush Colonies are the plain’s way of breathing. The Drifting Contracts are its way of thinking. The Keepers of the Unfinished are its way of forgiving. The Echo-Sentinels are its way of doubting, and the couriers are its way of delivering news to itself. Even the sparse vegetation, glowing with borrowed luminescence, is a kind of life that lives in seams because it knows that to take up too much space is to interrupt the brightness, and the brightness is, after all, the only sun this world has ever needed.

At dusk, when the dust settles into map-shapes that resemble places that never existed, all of these lives pause. They do not sleep, because sleep implies a surrender to darkness, and darkness here is a rumor, not a rule. Instead, they consolidate. They become the texture of the pause itself, the almost-silence that waits for the brightness to return. And when it does—when the constructed world, ancient and dense and fully itself, resumes its luminous work of being finished—they resume as well, not because they must, but because to pause is to remember that time moves, even here, even if it wears down slowly, even if cause and effect are polite enough to let reflection precede the thing reflected.

To live here is to be a translation that keeps translating itself, to be a seam that keeps stitching, a hum that keeps humming, a brightness that keeps forgetting how to be dark.
