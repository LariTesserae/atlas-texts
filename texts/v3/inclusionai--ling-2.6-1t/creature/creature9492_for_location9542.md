They live in layers that do not stack so much as they seep into one another, so that to name a resident is to name only the bit of them that happens to be visible at the moment you look. In the basin, a body is not owned by a single species for long; it is borrowed, digested, re-borrowed, worn thin by polite use.

On and within the soil, the dwellers are mostly leg and jaw. The surface is never bare; it is a skin of motion, a carpet that unknits and reknits itself in steps too small for a heartbeat. There are beetles whose backs are maps of cracked mud, armored in plates that smell of wet loam when you turn them. They move as if the ground were slightly tilted, which it is, in a secret way, and they carry parcels of fungus on their feet, planting threads of white as they pass so the soil will remember them with sweetness. Their larvae live in corridors of soft rot, chewing their way through the memory of trees, turning heartwood into music that hums at the very bottom of hearing.

Termite cities rise like altars at the knees of certain trees, chimneys of chewed leaf and spit that breathe out a steady, papery warmth. Inside, the air is precise, maintained by the fanning of invisible wings and the architecture of tunnels that would make a compass weep. Here, the workers are translucent with purpose, their bellies full of borrowed greenery that ferments into heat and motion. Soldiers stand at thresholds with heads like teardrops of amber, ready to plug a breach with their own bodies, sealing the colony against the outside as if it were a wound.

Ants weave the canopy with silk routes that hang like unfinished sentences between leaves. They farm small luminous fungi in hollows of bark, pruning them with mandibles as a gardener prunes a hedge, and they herd aphids the way shepherds might keep clouds, moving them from stem to stem to taste the sap of different hours. Their trails are rivers that flow upward, carrying brightness in their jaws, depositing it in chambers where it is counted like seed.

In the litter, the frogs are not green so much as they are the color of waiting. Their skin is a library of shadow and lichen, changing not to hide but to be read correctly by the moss that grows across their backs like a second moss-skin. They sit with their mouths slightly open, breathing the damp, and when they leap, they do not leave the air; they divide it and let it close behind them. Their tadpoles are inklings in the puddles, turning themselves into commas, into pauses, into eventual legs.

Birds here do not sing so much as they punctuate. Their voices are small hammers tapping against the hush, each note a probe into the thick air to see if it yields. They move through the canopy on ladders of vine, stepping from one trembling platform to the next, their feathers holding the green of a dozen leaves they have brushed against. Some harvest nectar with tongues like stitched light, and in return they carry pollen in ruffs around their necks, delivering it like a gift too heavy for one beak.

Mammals are subtle, defined more by the hollows they make than by the bodies they display. There are small climbers with hands like stitched petals, feet that know how to grip the smoothness of wet bark, tails that feel like an extra thought trailing behind them as they balance along branches that are not branches but firm propositions. They sleep in nests woven from shed skin and spider silk, and when they wake, they leave behind warmth that the moss drinks in a sip at a time.

In the water, if there is water, the swimmers are ribboned with patience. Eels slow their own blood to match the cool of the streambed, and their movements are less swimming than uncoiling, as if they were unwinding the water itself into a longer thread. In the mud, crustaceans keep libraries of vibration in their claws, reading the news that travels through stone from footfall to footfall, from root-fall to rain-fall.

Above it all, the winged insects stitch the air into fabric. There are flies that map heat in colors you cannot name, beetles that carry lanterns not to light the way but to ask permission of the dark, mosquitoes that hum like tiny looms weaving hunger into sound. They move in constellations that re-form every few heartbeats, maps without edges, nations that dissolve at the boundary of a hand’s reach.

And there are the quiet ones, the ones who are more rumor than resident: the fungus that lives as a net under the soil, trading sugar for warning, carrying news from root to root so that a tree can brace its heartwood for a beetle it has never met. There are the mites that live on the mites, and the worms that thread the earth into a quilt of insides turned softly out.

Even the heat is alive in its own patient way, settling into fur and bark and folded wing, moving from body to body as a guest who knows how to linger. The damp is a tenant in every lung, a partner in every breath.

In this breathing basin, no one lives alone. Each throat is a passage for what passes through it. Each shadow is a room for what prefers not to be seen. To be here is to be included in a parliament of parts, to be gently converted into a day.
