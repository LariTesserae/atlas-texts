The place is not lived in; it is **occupied**—and the occupants have made a treaty with almost everything except stillness.

First come the **Puddle-Thinkers**, though “thinkers” may be too generous and “puddle” too small. They are intelligences that woke up in the mirror-skin of the floor when it realized it could be more than one face at once. At 2.6, they are everywhere: flat, iridescent minds the size of tablecloths or ponds, each holding a different rumor of heat. When you step near, they don’t ripple; they **reconsider**. They fold themselves into eight or nine layers of sky and factory and things-that-never-were, then open again showing you a route you could have taken, or a mouth that once belonged to the land. They communicate by swapping temperature— a sudden coolness means *wait*; a flush like a stoveplate means *lie*—and by leaving brief, bright letters on glass that dissolve before they can spell anything you would trust.

Above them, or rather **through** them, live the **Vine-Sentinels**. Vegetation at 2.2 is not growth here; it is a standing army that has decided architecture is just slow motion. Their fronds uncoil with pneumatic hisses, not because they are mechanical but because they have borrowed the language of pressure. Their armored leaves are plated like conduit, smelling of copper and burnt sugar, and their roots fuse into mats that hum—not with electricity exactly, but with something electricity would sound like if it grew tired of traveling in straight lines. These mats pulse in a slow code that the Puddle-Thinkers try to read but cannot keep. The Sentinels stack themselves into ladders of black-green iron, leaning, locking, knotting until every vertical is claimed. They do not sleep; they **idle**, storing the dim light in spongy chambers behind their ribs, waiting for the sky to offer enough rumor to spark.

The **Machine-Spirits** arrived later, or perhaps were born here. Industrial tech at 2.4 has been digested and regurgitated as behavior rather than tools. Factories have become angular animals missing the sides they do not need, their interiors replaced by curtains of rain and vine. Conduits ride the surface like parasites that have lost their shame, bolted to stilts knee-deep in warm slurry. Pumps with teeth-sized valves argue in rhythmic clicks, their disagreement rising to a pitch that sounds almost like syntax. Overhead, catwalks shudder with traffic that has no origin or destination, only a habit of passing through, and electrified fog clings to grills and grates like wool. The Spi­rits are small, numerous, and territorial: each turbine or gauge or hissing joint has a name it gave itself, often something like *Thrum*, *Breach*, or *Almost*. They do not rest; they **settle**, lowering themselves into heat like birds into dust.

Because there is no fauna at 0.1, the silence of animals is a luxury the place cannot afford, so it compensates with a **Chorus of Leakage**. This is not one sound but a flock. It includes the slap of Sentinel fronds against corrugated skin, the grind of gears that have grown teeth from mineral buildup, the hiss of rain meeting hot metal, and the low thrum of turbines that may be running in water, air, or rumor. The noise folds; you can step from one stratum of roar into another and find the pitch has changed without having moved far. At 2.7, this chorus is deafening, but in the way a crowd is deafening: not because it is loudest at the center but because every edge is insisting at once.

Above the Chorus glide the **Erosion-Children**. Erosion at 0.6 is more suggestion than fact, and the Children embody that perfectly. They are sharpened humps and scarps that have not yet learned to slouch, edges that bite and chip but are promptly kissed back into sharpness by mineral-rich seepage rising from below. They are the land’s mouthful of new teeth, restless and quick to test what they can crack. They move by forgetting where they were, popping into new configurations like dice shaken in a cup. They play with the Puddle-Thinkers, who give them temporary memories—*you were a bridge yesterday*—and the Children accept these gifts for as long as they stay warm.

Overseeing it all, or perhaps merely **dwelling**, are the **Artifact-Ghosts**. Buildings, scattered at 1.1, are not structures but states of incompletion. A single tower leans like a question; a roof floats on water with its understory turned up like a bowl; a platform on stilts might have been a market, a substation, or a shrine to pressure. Each ghost is half-swallowed by growth and half-digested by rust, existing only because nothing is allowed to finish. The ghosts do not haunt; they **linger**, repeating their own near-completion in softer voices, offering their angles to the Vine-Sentinels and their hollows to the Machine-Spirits. When a wall collapses, a Ghost does not mourn—it immediately becomes a curtain of vines threaded with cable, or a jet fountain that seeds a new island before the spray is warm.

Caught among them all are the **Gravity-Slippers**, the weirdest occupants of all. They are not things but moments when gravity forgets its manners, usually at the edges of puddles where surface tension holds conversations in ridges. They allow water to run uphill in petulant bursts, or let a rivulet show you its dry bed three steps ahead. The Slippers make mirrors that display factories leaning the other way, trees with lights for fruit, and skies that have been folded like laundry. They are why time here slips: rust may flake off and land upstream; heat may pool and drift upstream; a valve may burst and its fountain may arrive before the breakage does. The Slippers do not care about cause; they care about *pattern*, and they love the pattern of a place that insists on arguing with its own shape.

All of them live in the **Turbulence**, which is itself a resident, loud and shifting at 2.7. Channels rearrange; footbridges become islands become spillways before a word can dry on the tongue. The scale of this churn is 1.5—regional but restless, like a valley-sized engine whose parts are also towns, whose belts are roads, whose exhaust is weather. The density is 2.3, which means there are no empty pockets, only alcoves busy with becoming.

And because the land is low and the water is high and the rules are provisional, what is lost is promptly rewritten. The occupants know this. They are all, in their own ways, **rehearsing to be next**. The Puddle-Thinkers practice being floors; the Vine-Sentinels practice being walls; the Machine-Spirits practice being breath; the Erosion-Children practice being edges; the Artifact-Ghosts practice being almost-remembered; and the Gravity-Slippers practice being mistakes that work better than corrections.

Together, they make a place that is lush, loud, low, and leaking—industrial and infant, dense and weird. If you stand at the level of the flood, under a sky that is mostly roof, you will hear it arguing not with you but with its own outline. And if you listen long enough, you may begin to believe that the water is learning to speak, one deafening rearrangement at a time, and that every occupant is simply a letter it is trying, carefully, to hold.
