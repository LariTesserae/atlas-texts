The room of altitude does not keep residents so much as midwives. What lives here is a parliament of thresholds, and every threshold wears a different face depending on which way you look at the becoming.

Most obvious are the Crysails—those winged seed-pods stitched with filaments of sound. They are not animals in the ordinary sense but agreements between vibration and lift. Their crystal membranes are grown, not built, each pane tuned to a specific harmonic of the room’s hum. When they glide, they do not displace air so much as persuade it to part. The sound they make is a grammar: clicks for caution, glassy chirrups for curiosity, long thrums for territory. Because the fauna here is loud (1.9), the noise is not clutter but texture, a dense fabric in which newborns learn the shape of their own voices before they have bodies. Some Crysails are so young their wings are still fog; they trail behind them like scarves made of uncertain weather.

Closer to the ground, the Weep-walkers live where the tarns bead and hesitate. They are liquid fauna, pooling at the bases of glass-grass, climbing back up in spirals. Their bodies are mercury-bright and self-editing: a Weep-walker might spend a morning as a column, an afternoon as a map of tributaries, and by dusk become a skin stretched across a stone like a drum. They feed on the 1.6 wetness, but they also feed on reluctance—the water’s refusal to fall—which they spin into fine threads of tension. When they rise, they leave behind faint silver scars that look like veins, which they sometimes re-enter and become. They do not die so much as remember themselves into new shapes.

Riding the engineered fields are the Litho-skimmers, creatures folded from light and stubbornness. They look like predatory kites made of stained glass, but they have no beaks or claws; instead, they herd translucent grazers—soft-bodied things like clouds with spines—across the sparse floor. The grazers are mostly appetite and alarm; they dissolve into mist when touched and reform elsewhere, older by a few seconds each time. The skimmers do not kill them so much as insist on patterns: routes, velocities, densities. Their hunts are fast, delicate contracts written in the language of acceleration and forgiven errors.

In the interstices where the alloy ribbons knot, the Nodes live. These are not fauna in any earthly sense, but they behave with familial intimacy. They are spherical, humming, reconfiguring, shedding geometries that taste like peppermint and ozone. The Nodes harvest wet and warmth and altitude, weaving fields that make gravity hesitate. They are attended by Drapes—ribbon-thin creatures that unspool from the Nodes and comb through raw geology like librarians reshelving a mountain. The Drapes groom the land, tucking incipient veins into place, encouraging magma-slurry to cool into lace. They do not speak, but they sing in frequencies that feel like remembering.

Between the glass-grass spires, the Half-dreamed roam—fauna that have not yet been born practicing flight. They look like sketches smudged into opacity: wings that are only suggested, bodies that are mostly velocity and hope. They do not land; they hover in the state of almost, feeding on the Weirdness (2.0) that pools like a courteous lake. When Water falls upward here and rains back down moments later, older, the Half-dreamed catch it and taste the future. They are loud in a different register, a kind of silence that has not yet learned to be quiet.

Lowest of all, in the embryonic basins that are only suggested, live the Sculp-tides: slow, tectonic fauna that resemble living faults. They shift the room’s small geography by fractions of a breath, curling edges like fresh scars, sighing microfissures that inhale moisture and exhale glittering vapor. They are patient in a way that feels like speed, always arriving at a shape they have already abandoned.

All of these lives are governed by the room’s few, precise rules. The Crysails know that water remembers where it has been, so they avoid flying through old storms. The Weep-walkers know that matter may refuse to choose a final state until observed, so they change shape when watched and stay still when ignored. The Litho-skimmers know that altitude is a permission rather than a fact, and they ask for it with dives that look like bows. The Nodes know that sound has mass and can be gathered, spent, owed, and they trade it in currencies of light.

Silence is rare and expensive here, and it is kept by the Sculp-tides for moments when the place needs to think. When silence falls, the glass-grass stops photosynthesizing sound, the alloy ribbons go slack, and even the Half-dreamed freeze in their almost-ness. In that hush, you can hear the room becoming its own outline: the weeps breathing, the warm wet basalt deciding to be crust, the engineered fields tightening and slackening like lungs.

To live here is to be in conversation with everything that is still learning its vocabulary. The fauna do not inhabit the room so much as they tutor it. They are loud with becoming, dim with withheld light, strange with courtesy, and busy with a patience that feels like speed. Abundance lives in the room left open between presences, and every absence—of rock, of leaf, of silence—has a shape that moves, breathes, and sings.
