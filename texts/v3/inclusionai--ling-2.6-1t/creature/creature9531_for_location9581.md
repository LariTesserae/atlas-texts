They are the Builders and the Built, though no one here keeps those two words apart.

At first they appear as weather, a working in the mist: figures the color of wet osier and uncured lime, moving through scaffoldings that are also ribs. They are neither tall nor short but *sufficient*, their bodies modular and apparently unfinished, as if they arrived already mid-addition. Some have too many joints, folding in places that seem decorative until you notice how they use them to lock themselves into the trembling seams of a rising wall, bracing it with their own locked knees while the wall sweats and grows tight around them. Others are long and few-jointed, spidery, their limbs ending in pads that exude a pale resin to seal the day’s edges. They do not speak, but they hum, a sound like a bow drawn across a wet cable, and this hum seems to be how the place decides where to put its next scar.

Their skin is not skin but a living cladding, layered like the structures they tend. You can peel back a sleeve, if they let you get that close, and see strata: thin, translucent sheets that flutter like pages, revealing beneath them a darker weave that pulses with a slow, capillary rhythm. Their blood, if it is blood, is the same copper-bruised fluid that beads on the bridges; when they are cut—and they cut themselves often, deliberately, to let the right wetness out—the droplets do not fall but crawl, seeking the nearest unfinished joint to vulcanize into it. They seem to regard bleeding as a kind of courtesy.

They do not sleep. They rest in positions that look like collapse, draped over a balustrade that is still deciding whether to be a rib or a reed, or curled inside a hollow that is meant to become a bell. In these poses they breathe with the place, their chests expanding not so much with air as with attention, drawing in the humid exhalations of the growing towers. If you watch long enough, you will see one of them begin to mineralize along the spine, knobs of cartilage rising like vertebrae, fusing them for a time to the wall they lean against. This is not death; it is delegation. They become part of the scaffold, and the scaffold learns how to hold itself up from them.

There are no children here, but there are echoes of children: small, quick shapes that are less bodies than tool-memories, trailing behind the taller ones like snapped-off handles. They dart into the deliberate hollows, testing how much a vault can hold before it sings, and when they emerge they carry nothing but a new crease in their foreheads, as if the architecture has whispered a secret into them.

The fauna are accidents of motion. There are scaffold-rats made of braided reed and hardened sap, scurrying along seams to chew on the rough edges that the Builders leave behind, not to destroy but to file. There are winged things, not birds but membranes stretched over bone-frames like kites, that ride the thermals rising from the warm tissue, patrolling the lines where water pools. They do not eat; they drink only the condensation that beads on the copper veins, and when they land their weight triggers a tiny exhalation from the walls, a soft pop that releases a puff of colored dust. The dust is the place remembering a color it once tried on and decided to keep in reserve.

Among them all, the most numerous are the nameless ones: the parts that have detached and gone on living. A hand that fell from a Builder and took root in a cup-shaped leaf, now gripping and ungripping all day, learning to be a latch. A segment of spine that rolled away and became a hinge for a bridge, still pulsing at the rhythm of a heart but now answering only to the wind. They are the workforce without the worker, the builders without the builder, and they throng in the busy water, climbing over one another to reach the next unfinished edge.

At night they are visible as silhouettes against the bruised plum sky, their outlines crisp and unpolished. The place exhales, and they answer by holding their various breaths. Somewhere in the middle distance, a new seam splits, hissing like a wound that has learned to be a mouth, and they turn toward it as one body, a chorus of hinges and wet cartilage. They do not wonder what they are building. They only know that to stop is to unbecome, and so they press their living edges into the raw edges of the world, mineralizing into meaning, one loud, wet, deliberate layer at a time.
