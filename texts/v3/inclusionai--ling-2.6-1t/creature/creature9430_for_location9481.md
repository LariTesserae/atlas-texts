They did not arrive; they annealed.

At first, there are only the *Lapidarites*—the great slow ones, the walking hearths. They are less like animals than like misplaced infrastructure that learned to hunger. Their hides are not skin but stratified slag, veined with seams of copper and ferric bloom that glint like fresh welds whenever they shift. Each footfall is a calibration: the weight of their passage heats the fused crust until it gives a dull red sigh, leaving footprints that will hold warmth for days, slowly cooling into new beds of ore. Their bodies are tessellated, plated with overlapping scales of vitrified dust and meteoritic iron, jointed with cylinders of glass that contain captive storms of static. When they exhale, they do not breathe air but release pressure: long columns of superheated vapor that rise, condense into brief clouds of metallic rain, and fall again as glittering silt. They move with the patience of geology that has decided to walk, and where they graze, the ground softens into fertile glass, ready to be written upon.

Around them, the *Sifter-Swarms* boil like ink in water. These are the mill-folk, the living refineries. Each individual is a chitinous geometry of blade and sieve, mandibles honed to edges that sing when they meet. They ride the heat in crests and break over ridges like waves that have forgotten the sea. They do not eat so much as they edit: tongues of blue flame unfurl from their throats, licking across the slag-fields, drawing out impurities in threads of colored smoke. What they leave behind is purer, denser, more *true*. When a swarm settles, the sound is a chittering chorus of files at work, of worlds being shaved closer to their shapes. They communicate in rhythms of click and whine, a syntax written in speed and temperature. To watch them is to watch a factory become flock, each member a piston, each flock a single thought made mobile.

Above, the *Gliders* ride the furnace drafts—creatures of stretched membrane and spar, their bodies fitted with vents that whistle like steam-kettles tuned to minor keys. They smell of ozone and candied rust, and their shadows fall in grids, as if they carried the imprint of invisible looms. They do not so much fly as *circulate*, returning again and again to the same updrafts, polishing the air with passage. In their bellies they carry nodules of raw resonance, minerals that hum when warmed, broadcasting low, declarative tones that anchor the plateau’s chorus. When they descend to drink—not water, but the corona that bleeds from the spine-rails—their wings crackle, and for a moment they look like pieces of sky that have grown heavy enough to land.

There are others, stranger and less constant: the *Glass-Stalkers* that rise from the rilles on jointed legs of fused silica, unfolding like flowers made of broken windows; the *Drone-Nodules*, bulbous and slow, that roll along the catwalks like hearts outside a body, pulsing with inner light; the *Mirror-Scuttlers*, small and quick, that rearrange their surfaces to become the thing they pass, learning camouflage as a kind of etiquette. Life here wears its functions openly, as armor and as invitation. Even the fauna that seem most decorative serve the plateau’s metabolism, trading metals through skin, secreting alloys that knit cracks, polishing the ground with bellies that have learned to shine.

And there are the *Graze-Stills*, the moments when all motion aligns. The Lapidarites lower their heads; the Sifter-Swarms settle into concentric rings; the Gliders bank in unison, their shadows stacking like pages. The hum of the plateau deepens, and the fauna add their clicks and thrums to the machines, creating a chord that seems to hold the air in place. In those moments, the boundary between creature and context blurs. A Lapidarite’s flank becomes a ridge; a swarm becomes a river of teeth; a Glider’s cry becomes the wind’s own voice. The plateau listens to itself through them.

When one dies—and the word feels too small for the event—the body does not decay. It settles. The heat concentrates, the carapace flows like wax, and the great slow one folds into the crust, becoming a new seam, a new stratum, a new source of hum. The Sifter-Swarms file respectful edges into the cooling mass, polishing it into a monument that will outlast memory. The Gliders circle once, drop their last notes into the fissure, and rise again. The plateau absorbs its children and gives them back as geography.

Even the smallest lives obey the logic of transformation. There are mite-things that ride the dust devils, feeding on the static charge of passing industry; there are thread-worms that weave silica into lace beneath the glassed flats, creating nets that catch and hold the plateau’s breath; there are spore-clusters that bloom into scaffolds of glittering crystal, only to shatter and scatter, feeding the next cycle with light. Life here is a conversation between pressure and patience, a continuous negotiation of what can be made from almost nothing.

And always there is the hum, the industrial purr that lives in the ribs, the song of machines that accreted themselves from hunger and law. The fauna answer it. They click, they whine, they thrum, they release calibrated breath. They are not inhabitants of the High Crucible but expressions of it, tools with desires, weather with direction. In their passage, the plateau sharpens its edges; in their rest, it dreams in frequencies too low for most ears. Together they forge a world that is always almost finished, always almost beginning, a place where reality bends just enough to remember that it is, after all, only warming up.
