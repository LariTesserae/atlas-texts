They are apprentices to closeness, these tenants of vein-walled corridors.  
They do not arrive so much as condense—first as a thinning of the air, then as a hesitation in the press of stems, finally as a body that seems to have been waiting for permission to be dense. Their outlines are borrowed: a shoulder’s curve matches the rib of a leaf; the line of a spine thins into the seam between two braided trunks. They wear the place like a second epidermis, and the place wears them back, exuding a faint film of resin over their skin that never quite sets.

They are few, though not lonely. Rarity here is not absence but concentration. What they lack in number they supply in attention—their senses protracted, almost amphibious. They hear the wet pop of a bladdery leaf as a kind of speech; they taste the cinnamon-iron haze as rumor. Their eyes are unnecessary for the brightness, yet they keep them, milky and large, because light here is not meant to be seen through but lived inside. When they close their lids, the glow persists as pressure, as a color behind the bone.

Their movement is deliberate, not because of slowness but because of a geometry of almost-contact. To lift a hand is to ask the surrounding stems to part; to step is to request that the felted floor compress just enough. They have learned the etiquette of density: a breath held a moment longer before turning, a weight shifted in increments so small the architecture barely registers the transfer. In this way, they move like punctuation—commas that let the green sentence breathe, periods that do not end but soften into ellipses.

They speak rarely, and when they do, the voice is porous, escaping as much through the skin as through the mouth. More often they communicate by exhalation: a change in the dampness of their breath that tells the nearest leaves to thicken or thin, to tilt their cup-shaped surfaces away or toward the heat. They have names that are not words but patterns of resin flow—maps of translucence that appear briefly on the inner wall of a stem and then fade, as if the plant has memorized and then surrendered them.

Their tools are grown, not carried. A cup-shaped leaf is coaxed into a deeper curve by the careful application of thumb-heat and restrained breath; a hollow is walled with folded leaves that stiffen overnight into a privacy no larger than a teacup, yet sufficient for dreaming. They use their own bodies as instruments of measure, aligning their outstretched arms to judge whether a corridor is still town-sized or has begun to close into chamber, their span a living ruler that sweats and trembles with the ambient fever.

They do not sleep in the way the world above understands it. Instead, they let their awareness descend into the felt of rhizomes, spreading cognition thin across the springy decay of shoots. In this state, they are less individual than district—a distributed presence that notices when one stem hardens, when another softens, when the sap’s tide ticks forward in its unaccented clock. They wake not with a start but with a thickening, a gathering of self back into the corridor of ribs they have chosen as temporary lodging.

Their children, when there are children, are taught to recognize stability as a kind of kindness. The young are shown how to press a cheek to a stem and count the years by the layered insistence of growth, how to listen for the papery rasp that means the walls are still deciding how solid to become. They learn that rarity is a texture of care, that noise is a gift because it means something has risked being elsewhere, and that the resin that beads on their skin without falling is a promise, not a leak.

They are not immortal, but they are persistent. When one of them thins enough to become transparent—when the borrowed outline finally agrees to be light rather than body—they do not rot. They diffuse. Their heat lingers in the air as a slight softening of silence; their knowledge lingers in the architecture as a corridor that stays open an hour longer than expected. In this sense, they become region: a district of attention walled with patient growth, lit from within by the glow of continued elaboration.

And so the place and its few inhabitants practice eternity together—not by standing still, but by repeating themselves gently, precisely, without end. They grow into each other’s margins, filling the interstitial gaps with denser living mass, inventing windows in stems that do not open and flowers that open inward to watch. They are the answer to a question the damp heat asked of itself: how to hold shape when form is only a rumor of weight, how to be a lung that forgets how to collapse and still keeps breathing, in slow motion, forever.
