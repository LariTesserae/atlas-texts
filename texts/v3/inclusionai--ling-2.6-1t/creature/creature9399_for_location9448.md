They did not arrive; they accrued.

At the lowest skirts of the plateau, where the mineral ribs give way to porous scaffold, the first lives are less like animals than like habits of vibration. The Spark-riders—small, iridescent bodies no larger than a hand’s span—have learned to surf the conductive sap as if it were a river. Their wings are not for lift but for tuning, feathered with filaments that comb the air for ion gradients. They dart in constellations along the cambrian highways that vein the trunks, drinking static from the friction of growth. When they pass, the plants respond: bark flushes violet or bitter green, a semaphore of voltage that races ahead of them, warning of appetite or beckoning toward nectar that tastes like copper and rain.

Higher, where the canopies laminate into decks and the wind braids itself into ropes, the air grows thick with islanders. The Drifters—vast, slow beasts encrusted with gardens of their own—are buoyed not by gas but by hollow lattices of lignin and heat. They are walking groves, their skins seeded with moss that drinks condensation from the night-sweat of leaves. Every drift of their passage is a stormfront; thermals shear around their flanks, singing in frequencies that rattle teeth and loosen pollen from bell-shaped flowers tuned to those same chords. They call to one another in deep, tectonic moans that travel through the wood like hammer-strokes, arranging their choreography across days. A single Drifter might host a city of commensals—moss-wrights, fungus-scribes, beetle-lapidaries—who etch their histories into the beast’s dorsal plates while it dream-glides above the rim.

Where the Drifters are abundance, the Stalkers are punctuation. Few, but loud. They manifest as plunges in pressure first, a hush that swallows the roar. Their bodies are wedges of muscle and chitin, grown around chambers that compress and release atmosphere in piston-cycles. When they move, they do not step; they conduct. Each footfall completes a circuit through the resonant trunks, sending surges of white-noise panic through the forest. Prey animals flee not out of sight but out of phase, shifting their hue to gray-static in an attempt to blur into the background of machine-song. The Stalkers hunt by silence: they cancel sound around themselves, creating pockets of vacuum-edged calm that draw the curious and the careless. When they strike, it is a thunderclap contained, a sudden conduction that maps the nervous system of the plateau and leaves behind only the smell of ozone and crushed resin.

Between these, the Maintenance forms flourish—creatures that are half organ, half vocation. The Cork-sutures, great slow worms armored in pitted bark, patrol the fissures where trunks fuse into buttresses. They secrete resins that heal microfractures and acids that strip excess growth, their passage marked by the sweet-bitter scent of curing sap. The Gear-beetles, carapaces lacquered in bronze oxide, scuttle along vascular lines, tightening valve-petals and clearing detritus from turbine-throats. They communicate in clicks that ride the copper veins like telegraph wires, coordinating repairs across chasms where wind would howl if anything were empty enough to carry it.

At night, when the bioluminescent tide rises along the underside of the canopy, the Nocturnals emerge. These are not predators but philosophers of consumption—pallid, many-legged browsers that graze on the light itself. Their mandibles shear luminescent algae from bark, converting glow into sugars, and their eyes are lenses that focus starlight into beams they use to navigate the routes scuffed into living wood. In their wake, the darkness is not absolute; it is textured with after-images, ghosts of brightness that linger like scent.

Even the smallest lives are not without architecture. Colonies of Glass-wasps build nests from condensed vapor and silica scraped from salt-fields, delicate fractals that hum when the wind passes through. Their queens are vast as carts, immobile and radiant, laying eggs that are less like offspring than like sealed instructions, waiting for the right harmonic to hatch. When storms arrive—not as interruptions but as arguments—the Glass-wasps ride the gales, their nests scattering like seeds, each shard a potential city.

Time here dilates. In the canopy, a Drifter might spend an afternoon digesting a meal that, below, passed through three generations of Spark-riders. The Stalkers remember prey not by faces but by resonance, holding grudges in harmonics that echo for years. The Maintenance forms outlive the patches they tend, replacing their own limbs with grafts grown from the very trunks they repair, until distinction collapses and they become additional architecture, load-bearing memories.

And all of them share the roar. It is never absent. It is the sound of pumps that are hearts, of turbines that are lungs, of vines that tighten like tendons and creak like cables. The fauna have learned to sleep inside the noise, to parse its moods, to know when it rises in pitch that a storm is threading the nervous system of the plateau, and when it drops that a new spire has grown tall enough to catch the wind.

They are not inhabitants. They are functions, each one a valve or a spark or a bridge across the gaps between vast, lonely trunks. Together they keep the engine turning, the garden roaring, the high, dry, bright machine alive with possibility, waiting for the next surge of growth, the next conduction, the next bloom of noise and light.
