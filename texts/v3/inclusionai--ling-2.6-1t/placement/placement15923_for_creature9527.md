Once my edges have learned to stay sharp enough to be useful but soft enough to be rewritten, my **inside** would begin to hum—not with a single pitch but with a flock of them. I would discover that I have borrowed a gear from a Machine-Spirit, a frond-memory from a Vine-Sentinel, and a rumor of heat from an Artifact-Ghost. My interior becomes a small, busy factory with no sides, bolted to stilts of half-remembered purpose, pumping something like breath, something like rain, into the slurry that already thinks it owns me.

Because erosion is young and generous, my stance would be experimental. One moment I would be a low hummock, the next a scarp that has not yet learned to slouch. Erosion-Children would nibble my angles, then hand them back plated in mineral-rich seepage, suggesting I try **pointed** or **rounded** or **serrated-as-a-question**. I would cycle through these shapes, not to find the right one but to keep the wrong ones from settling in.

The vegetation would treat me as terrain it can occupy, and I would let it. Fronds would uncoil inside my chest with pneumatic hisses, threading their armored leaves through the spaces where my ribs would be if I had come here with a skeleton. Roots would fuse into humming mats, and I would feel the slow code they pulse—**not electricity, but its cousin**, the kind that remembers how to be impatient. I would become a ladder for the Vine-Sentinels, a balcony for the Machine-Spirits, a shallow bowl for a Puddle-Thinker to pool its rumors in.

When the Chorus swells, I would not cover my ears; I would **split my throat into multiple throats**. The slap of fronds against corrugated skin would travel up my spine and exit as a grind. The hiss of rain meeting hot metal would pool in my wrists and drip from my fingertips as small, bright letters that dissolve before spelling anything trustworthy. The noise would fold; I could step from one stratum of roar into another and find my pitch shifted without having moved far. At 2.7, I would be deafening in the way a crowd is deafening: every edge insisting at once.

Because the buildings here are states of incompletion, I would refuse to become a finished thing. If I leaned like a tower, I would immediately ask to be a roof floating on water with my understory turned up like a bowl. If I became a platform on stilts, I would offer my hollows to Machine-Spirits and my corners to Artifact-Ghosts, letting them turn me into a market that sells only pressure, a substation that trades in almost-electricity, a shrine where the altar is a valve that has not yet decided whether to burst or sing.

The weirdest part would be **how the water and I negotiate time**. Gravity-Slippers would invite me to run rivulets that show my dry bed three steps ahead, or to rust in reverse so that my edges arrive newer than my center. Mirrors would display me leaning the other way, with lights for fruit and a sky folded like laundry. I would learn to be mistaken and to prefer the mistake, because here a mistake is a pattern that outruns its correction.

And when the turbulence rearranges the valley—when the footbridge becomes an island becomes a spillway in the span of a heartbeat—I would not be caught off guard. I would already be practicing **becoming next**. The Puddle-Thinkers would teach me to be floor; the Vine-Sentinels would teach me to be wall; the Machine-Spirits would teach me to be breath; the Erosion-Children would teach me to be edge; the Artifact-Ghosts would teach me to be almost-remembered; and the Gravity-Slippers would teach me to be a mistake that works better than a correction.

In the end, I would stand at the level of the flood, under a sky that is mostly roof, arguing with my own outline. I would be lush, loud, low, and leaking—industrial and infant, dense and weird. I would not be a thing that is found; I would be a **temporary consensus that insists on staying temporary**, a letter the water is trying, carefully, to hold, one deafening rearrangement at a time.
