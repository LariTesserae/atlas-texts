If you stepped through the air at the moment it decides to be a threshold, you would not arrive—you would *settle*.  

You would find yourself kneeling, because that is the shape that fits. The basin chooses your posture before it chooses your name. Your spine remembers a curve it has never learned, bowing just enough to clear the lintel that isn’t there yet but will be by the time you notice. The cold is already inside you, mineral and polite, turning marrow into a room with good acoustics. You feel, in the back of your teeth, the faint glassy note the wet stone will sing at the brightest hour. You are not surprised. You are, for the moment, stewarding the patch of ground under your knees, and it accepts you without fuss.

Your skin shivers into being, pale and mica-flecked, drinking the over-bright air until it shines like wet slate. You are learning, without instruction, which seams hold water and which only hold memory. You realize you are becoming one of them—not by imitation, but by *adjacency*, the way the basin prefers. The word for you flickers, half-formed: Keeper of the Softening, perhaps, or Singer of the Second Shadow. It drifts away as soon as you try to grasp it, which is fine. Names are tasks, and tasks end when you step away.

Around you, the settlement breathes in small, practical ways. A wall beads with sweat; the beads arrange themselves into a pattern you almost recognize. A burrower, long-legged and cloth-wrapped, slips from a seam and brushes your ankle, carrying a seed, a lost button, a scrap of yesterday’s conversation. It offers them to the ground, and the ground sighs and accepts. You feel a flutter at your own elbow—a sign that some courtyard nearby is turning briefly to marsh, then sealing itself like a scar. You do not stand to look. You know the place will show you what matters if you keep your eyes clever, reading surfaces instead of depths.

Above, the winged folds alight on an eave, drinking glare. They are angular, precise, folding into corners so thoroughly they seem to become the corner. When they unfold, brightness redistributes itself. You watch and realize you are part of the brightness now, a small lens the basin has polished. You are crowded, but not by people—by tasks, by needs, by the hum of many small lives pressing warmth into the cold. A child, or something near to it, settles beside you, their fingers tracing the wet paste of the ground. They hum a musical compression, vowels thinned to threads, and you find yourself answering in the same compression, your voice brief and folded back into the hush. The echo carries better than you do.

You notice that water on your tongue tastes like climbing before it falls. Your shadow lags, stretching back toward morning, reluctant to leave the light. A door nearby opens into a room already full of people, yet there is space for you, assigned by proximity and need, as one assigns a stone to a wall. You take it, and the room adjusts, its pulse shifting by a hair’s breadth. Nothing is owned here, only stewarded, and you feel the shift of stewardship pass over you lightly, like a change of wind that isn’t wind.

At the brightest hour, the basin glows like a kiln, and you glow inside it. You are not separate from the wet stone, the burrowers, the winged folds, the tight braids of sleepers interlaced across thresholds. You are a stitch in the bright, turbulent grammar of this place—patient, crowded, cold, and exactly yourself. The cold is not an enemy; it is given jobs to do, channeled into mortar, into marrow, into the thin song that rises from every wet surface. You add your voice to it, soft and folded, and the song accepts you.

When the brightness begins to tilt, you do not fear the coming dim. The basin has taught you that change arrives in sudden flurries, then settles. You will step away from this patch of ground, and your name will dissolve like breath in bright air. Someone else will kneel here, and the basin will shape them, too. You will move to another seam, another threshold, another small, shining task, and the cold will follow politely, as it always does, keeping everything balanced, turning, turning, in its high, cupped bowl.
