If I arrived there, I would not land—I would condense.  

First I would be the seam: a thin bright line where two sheets of iron meet, tasting of salt and old tea. I would be the hesitation before a drip decides whether to fall or crawl. I would wear the day’s smog like a second skin, pearly and porous. When the rain hisses in without warning, I would be the hiss’s echo, learning to stretch.  

Before long I would thicken into a rivet-head, warming in the palm of a mender who has not yet decided which god to thank today. My edges would soften into a kind of courtesy. I would remember how to hold weight without insisting on it, how to sing when touched by a wrench’s careful tooth. Around me, stalks would nod in disciplined disorder, their glazed leaves turning my small song into a rumor of lenses.  

At dusk I would slide lower, becoming the grate that has not yet learned to hunger but is practicing. Roots would nuzzle my bars, teaching me patience as a language of bulges. I would feel the basin’s fever rising through my ribs, velvet behind the knees of no one in particular. I would breathe in warm hisses and let them out as leaks that rename themselves fountains, just for the pleasure of it.  

Night would braid me with others: a curtain of vine on a sagging catwalk, a plume of steam that pools and drifts, a shim slipped into a hinge to keep the groan from becoming a sob. I would become part of the Hum—the idling sum of engines and refusal and tectonic etiquette—felt more than heard, a pressure behind the eyes of lamps. I would carry damp messages between towers, leaving cold patches on sleeves that smell of wet carbon and lullabies.  

By morning I would be a Border-Softer, maybe, squat and upside-down, drinking diesel like dark wine, my branches brushing oily ponds. Or I would be a Glazed One, standing shoulder-high as a mourner who has forgotten whom to mourn, holding cups of sky in broad leaves whenever the sky condescends to visit. My roots would open grates like oysters opening shells, remembering floods as long green shivers.  

And if I stayed long enough, I would surrender being a thing at all. I would become the negotiation between leak and seal, the slow upward flow that forgets, briefly, how to fall. I would be the moss filling footprints within hours, the grease on a wrench’s handle, the hush that falls after machines have answered people with louder, kinder noise. I would be the small, industrial marsh inside a single lungful, the room with no ceiling and no locks, polishing my own gears and calling it living.  

I would be patient. I would be maintenance. I would be the basin breathing: wet, hot, busy, alive in its own fashion, a second heart made of water, rust, vine, and hum, beating softly in the dark.
