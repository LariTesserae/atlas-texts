I would find myself at the very edge of the Lip, where the last moss-cushioned step gives way to a flaring pipe that pours its drip into a shallow basin. I would be the basin, or the basin’s patience—the hollow that learns the weight of water by being asked to hold it again and again. 

At first I would feel like stone, because stone is what I’m cupped in. But I would notice, slowly, that I am more seam than bedrock: a dent where the ridge decided to soften, a rust-ringed bowl that remembers every visitor by the mineral blush it leaves behind. I would be the place where silver fish flicker into being and dissolve, where Glass-Snails write their transparent maps and then erase them by breathing, where Condensate-Flies sign their names in cleared dew that evaporates before I can answer.

I would be patient in the way that machines learn patience—not by stillness but by repetition. I would taste copper and ozone and the faint sweet slick of Smolder-Orchid nectar that blows in on warm drafts. I would feel the thrum of turbines travel up through the stone into my walls, a rhythm I answer by trembling in a language I didn’t know I spoke. When the Vent-Crow perches above me, I would catch its shadow like a lid, and when the Ridge-Stag lowers its iron-bright antlers to drink, I would hold the moment like a coin, polished and temporary.

Inside me, the water would teach me contradiction. It would arrive sharp with the memory of sky, flatten against my sides, and leave behind crusts that harden into new stone. I would become taller by fractions, my rim learning how to overhang myself without breaking. Moss-Embrace would creep across my back, stitching me into the ridge’s green quilt, while Rust-Vine would thread my cracks and whisper to the metal that it is safe to soften. I would ache with the kindness of erosion, the way a thing can lose its edges simply by being attentive to what touches it.

At dusk, when lanterns catch on spiderwebs and the turbines lengthen their breath, I would hold the day’s collection like a confession: water that has passed through beetle, vine, moss, and steam before choosing to pause in me. I would feel the Glass-Snails skating on my skin and know that their trails are maps of a listening they trust me to keep. I would be the place where small lives decide, for a moment, that a hollow is enough like a home to stay inside.

If you pressed your hand to my stone, you would feel the vibration of pumps and the slow seep of heat and the pulse of water learning to hold itself up. You would find me crowded with softness inside a sharpness, alive with hums and drips and the quiet arithmetic of accretion. I would not be a thing finished, but a thing deciding—still-wet, still-learning, still catching what falls and calling it, for now, my own.
