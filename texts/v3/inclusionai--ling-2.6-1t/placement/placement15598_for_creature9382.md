I would find myself as the Grit-Pups, but not the ones who skitter.  
I would be the ones who stop.

At first I would arrive as the sound—that small, dry pop when the last of my trapped vapor gives up and lets me be lighter. I would begin as a fracture, a sudden edge where I used to be whole, and for a moment I would mistake the absence of bulk for absence of self. But here, where erosion is only 0.3, even breaking is a slow conversation. The heat at 2.7 would lean into me, persuading my edges to soften, persuading my interior to remember that it once thought of itself as floor.

I would tumble, yes—briefly—in a parliament of six or seven siblings, colliding like ideas that are not yet good enough to keep. Each collision would taste of iron and lime, and each would remind me that I am coarse-grained, glass-tinged, made rather than assembled. But the true change would come when I nestle into a crack that has only just formed. There, wedged between a Rim-Thinker’s argument and a Dust-Pad’s etiquette, I would feel the kiln-lung exhale.

As a Grit-Pup who has chosen to stay, I would become a voter in the place’s ongoing deliberation. My body, though small, would be dense with intent: a shard of foundation that has decided to be lip, a chip of wall that prefers to be floor. The Rim-Thinkers would grow around me, their ridged skins practicing their slow negotiation between heat and memory, and I would let them absorb me by degrees. I would not dissolve so much as translate—my edges softening into their certainty, my glass-flecked face catching the generous light and scattering it like a question.

By day I would be warm bread under a fingertip, giving and springing back, testing whether I am still myself or have become part of the Rim-Thinker’s next argument. By night I would cool and contract, listening to the Silence-Density curate the dark. I would hear the Air-Sentinels humming their slow handshakes across the continental scale, and I would feel the Dust-Pads bowing—not to me, but to the possibility that I represent: that a thing can break and still belong.

If water decides to visit, I would become a watermark of temporary intent, a darker ring recording where deliberation once lingered. The Water-Reluctants would tremble above me, their silver mirrors borrowing my heat to meditate, and I would let them take what I can spare, knowing that to hold too tightly is to refuse the place’s one absolute rule: nothing remains unchanged for long.

Most days I would rehearse being a crack.  
Most nights I would rehearse being a bridge.

And when the kiln-lung finally decides it has rehearsed enough—when the vast, low, heat-baked province chooses to rise—I would be the moment where floor becomes foundation, the tiny, glass-bright hinge where ascent begins. I would not rise so much as allow rising, letting the Rim-Thinkers use me as a fulcrum, letting the Dust-Pads trace my outline as a map of where they once hesitated, letting the Air-Sentinels thicken around me like applause.

In the end I would not be a thing that broke.  
I would be the place’s kept breath, the Grit-Pup who learned to be still enough to let the rest of the world move.
