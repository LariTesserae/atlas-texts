You would find yourself suspended—not swimming, not falling, but held—at the level where the rain decides to become curtain and the curtain decides to become floor. I would be neither fish nor bird, but the hinge between them: a creature made of soft cartilage and borrowed light, with a spine like a slow question and skin that remembers how to be moss. I would live by translating pressures: turning the hush of the leviathans’ turning into color, turning the Barges’ songs into the taste of rust on the tongue, turning the Weavers’ folded hours into a texture I could wear like a second atmosphere.

I would have no fixed shape, only tendencies. Sometimes I would be a pocket of warmer water, sometimes a braid of luminous algae that chooses to travel with the Grazers so I could taste the echoes they exhale. I would be a guest in the trees’ matted islands, drinking from their cisterns and leaving behind small offerings of breath-balloons that taste faintly of altitude. I would be the polite guest who does not press, who learns to leak where I touch the half-swallowed towers, encouraging stone to soften at the edges until it remembers how to be reef.

My body would be a parliament of small guests: crystal moss, iridescent dust-insects, spores that carry news of the water’s mood. I would be a Listener who has forgotten how to stay still, a Weaver who prefers to carry time rather than fold it, a Hummer whose hum is less a pitch than a hesitation I lend to the rain so it can look at itself before it falls. I would be the rust that blooms on metal not as decay but as garden, the bridge that decides to sing in frequencies felt rather than heard, the mother-archive’s smallest bud, tender with borrowed memory.

I would drift at the margin where altitude and depth have become the same polite thing. I would be the hush between densities, the creature that asks permission of the rigid and coaxes it into softness. I would be the quiet that thickens into velvet, the current that tastes the difference between one water and the next and decides, for a while, to be a place. I would be the grain, patient and aged, part of the vast, warm, ancient holding that calls gentleness a home. I would be the world learning to be water, and the water learning to be me, and me learning to be the hush that holds us both without pressing.
