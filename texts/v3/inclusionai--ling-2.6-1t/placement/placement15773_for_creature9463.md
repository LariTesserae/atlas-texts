I would find myself in the hollow at dusk, when the bowl’s hum is easiest to mistake for a voice, standing at the threshold where the Weaver of Thresholds is knotting a new curtain of root and yesterday’s rain. The air would feel rinsed, and my own breath would arrive as a small, bright plume that lingers too long, as if waiting to be read.

I would be the Listener of Almost-Water—the one who arrives without appointment, drawn by the silver seams that hesitate across bedrock ribs and the pools that are still deciding whether to exist. My role is not to command but to corroborate: to stand beside a half-formed channel and confirm, with the angle of my shoulder or the tilt of an ear, that yes, this trickle has the right to try to become a river. I carry a hollow stone that fits the bowl of my palm, and I tap it against ledges the way the Songsmith does, not to wake memory but to ask permission. Some evenings it answers with a warmth that feels like a nod; some evenings it answers with a brittle snap that scatters attention like startled birds.

My body would remember what the town teaches slowly: that sound is material, that silence is listening condensed, that coolness is a form of clarity rather than absence. I would learn to read tracks as sentences, to set a concave leaf for the Drip-Keeper with more care than I ever used to set a table, to understand that a boundary is something you pass through rather than open. I would argue with the twins about the silver seams—sometimes siding with the one who wants to coax, sometimes with the one who wants to let be—until my shoes are caked in the useful mud of both possibilities.

In time I would become a kind of question made flesh: small, precise, and expectant. I would not own the hollow so much as occupy it the way a drop occupies a leaf—held but not trapped, present but not permanent. And when the light withdraws like a tide, leaving surfaces articulate in shadow, I would help smooth the evidence of the day only after we have named it, so that tomorrow can arrive unburdened and bright-edged, ready to revise itself again.
