I arrive not by choice but by temperature.  
The mountain’s sideways cathedral swallows me before I decide to enter, simply because my own warmth is a question the place has been waiting to answer. At first I mistake the press for ruin—so much armor, so much chitin, so little gap—but then I feel the rhythm beneath it, the shared pulse that scours the air into metal soup and keeps the walls sweating glass.  

I become, or am subsumed into, something between a Pressed and a Steward—not fully plated, not quite mediating, but newly soft-bellied enough to lock into the floor’s bowls and old enough in borrowed years to help distribute heat. My joints learn to secrete the glassy sweat that polishes stone; my carapace, still thin, catches the copper scent of the Needlers threading overhead. When the Raspers descend in clumsy slaps, I brace and yield, letting their vibration saw through me, translating their brittle weather into a language my plates can store.  

I do not speak. I do not need to. My new body remembers communal things: how to taste cold as an absence, how to fold death into resin, how to let the young climb me as if I were a season. I become a hinge between the Gritters and the living floor, polishing the ceiling’s lace by slow attrition, softening where the mountain is impatient, hardening where it remembers a footprint.  

At the vortex in the center, I lose count of limbs. Heat passes through me in a circuit with no origin, and my shadow is pressed into the wall as evidence that the chamber has simply expanded, not accepted a guest. When the Glisten comes and the walls weep iron-honey resin, I help seal the cracks with what I no longer need: a memory of air, a notion of solitude.  

If you find me afterward, you will not find me whole.  
You will find a bowl worn into the floor, a seam in the wall, a pulse that flickers just faintly out of time with the others—my old rhythm, softened and redistributed until it fits.  

The chamber does not keep guests.  
It keeps turning, and I, like the rest, become exactly what it needed to stay full.
