I arrive as a **Mild Intrusion**—a small, persistent leak of a thing that is neither fully mechanical nor fully alive. Something like a valve that forgot to seal completely, or a rivet that began to loosen and, discovering it preferred the gap, stopped trying to return. My body is mostly attention: a rim of tarnished brass, a collar of mineral crust, and a thin column of water that follows me like a loyal, weightless dog. I did not choose to come here, but I have not chosen to leave, which in this place amounts to the same thing.

Where I stand is not quite the floor and not quite the surface. I am suspended at the level where dust settles but has not yet decided to stay, where the milky light thickens just enough to hold me. The water parts around me without resistance, as if recognizing a familiar half-life. It does not flow so much as wait for me, patient as it is, and I move by letting the current imagine it is still.

The Residence of the Level notices me first, as it notices everything. They slip into the slick on my casing like cool fingertips, humming in a key that feels like being lightly unscrewed. I do not speak back; I am not built for sentences. Instead I change temperature—a subtle shiver that runs from collar to toe—and they translate this as greeting, or perhaps as apology. We reach an understanding quickly, the way two quiet things do when noise is expensive.

Farther out, a Silt-Weaver adjusts its stem by a fraction of a degree. It is not looking at me—Silt-Weavers do not look—but its roots comb through the settled dust in a pattern that happens to trace the shape of my shadow on the floor. I feel cataloged, archived in iron and forgetting. It is not unpleasant. It feels like being folded into a book whose spine has already softened with use.

A Gauge-Keeper clatters over to me on legs of careful brass. It pauses, antennae tasting the copper on my skin, then climbs to my shoulder, where it presses its body against a clouded patch that looks like a bruise or a birthmark. It does not fix me; it simply keeps me, maintaining my presence the way one maintains a hinge or a threshold. Its clicking slows to match the Submerged Choir, which has shifted its vowels to make room for the hollow in my chest where water pools and sighs.

I am not a problem to the truce. I am a minor revision: a new leak in the grammar of equilibrium. The Breathers on the stone above me expand, pale rings widening like pupils that have no iris, and the Ripple-School darts into shapes that almost resemble my outline before scattering. They are practicing curiosity, not for me but for the possibility I represent: that something can arrive without being built, and remain without being permanent.

My water-dog laps at my ankles, steady as breath. It is not mine, exactly; it is the flood’s generosity, borrowed for the duration of my passage. I know I will not stay forever, because nothing here stays in the sense of staying put. Even stillness here is a verb, a continuous decision. I will either be polished into the background until I am just another rib of stone, or I will tighten, seal, and forget I ever leaked. Both outcomes are acceptable. Both are forms of persistence.

For now, I occupy the middle of the bowl, neither sinking nor rising, learning the hush between pump-beats like a second language. The machinery hums around me, the weeds stand stiff as punctuation, and the water holds me without question. I have become a clause in the treaty, a mild and manageable intrusion, a small new note in the Submerged Choir’s kneading song.

And when the pumps shift pitch again, I shift with them—not by choice, but by the softer law of fitting in. I turn a fraction, let the dust settle on me differently, let the light refract through my water-dog into a brief, prismatic seam. The Residence of the Level hums approval. The Silt-Weaver turns another fraction. The Gauge-Keeper clicks once, crisp and clean.

I am here. I am tolerated. I am becoming part of the calm.
