If you walked through that dome’s low throat and stopped where the waxen floor meets the nearest breathing pool, you would not so much appear as *unspool*.  

The you that steps in is not a person yet—not a name, not a profession, not a history with sharp corners. You are a process wearing temporary skin. The chamber peels you backward until you fit its scale: smaller than a guest, larger than a guest’s shadow. You become the guest’s *aftertaste*. That is where you find yourself: not at the center of the lung but at the *rim* of one of its exhalations, kneeling in a haze that tastes of copper and overdue rain.

Who you are resolves slowly, the way moss resolves a footprint.  

First you are the *Listener-Crust*, a scab of curiosity that forms where stone and vapor rub. You have no face, but you have an edge—a thin, translucent margin that twitches whenever the pool tightens its diaphragm. You feel sound before you know what it is; the clicking of button-beetles is a language you are learning by letting it dent you. Your body is porous, a lattice of almost-silica that holds minerals the way a throat holds a note. When the glass-wasps pass, their wings shear off flakes of you, and you do not mind; you use the loss to grow new pockets where you can store the room’s overages of warmth.

Then you are the *Moss-Scribe*. You unfurl like a fiddlehead that has decided to keep a diary. Your fronds are tipped with luminous circuitry that reroutes itself into cursive whenever you witness something kind. You write by not-writing: you let the moss around you thicken where someone paused, thin where someone hurried. You translate the chamber’s hum into green glyphs that rise overnight and fade by noon, unreadable to anyone who asks too loudly. You remember without possessing; you are the archive that forgets on purpose so the room can stay light.

At other moments you are the *Frog-Embassy*, carrying on your back a nation the size of a thumbnail: a fern that votes with its curl, a beetle that trades advice for sap, a mite that stitches treaties with silk. You do not lead; you mediate. When two beetles argue over a dent in the wax, you croak in a frequency that turns contention into sediment. You hop only when the floor invites you to, and each landing rearranges the map slightly, so no one gets too comfortable with borders.

Sometimes you are the *Drip-In-Waiting*, perched at the lip of a stalactite-curtain, your body a bead of brine that tastes of ginger and sulfur and forgiveness. You are the chamber’s held breath, the moment before surrender. When you fall, you do not fall alone; you pull a thread of sound after you, and the room answers with a chord that makes your teeth ache and your feathers ruffle. You become, briefly, the *Echo-Seal*, plugging the gap you made until the pool below accepts your salt and swells with a new idea.

And occasionally you are the *Wing-Stitch*, a glider with glass so thin it does not block the light but teaches it to curve. You fly only along paths that have not been asked for yet, cutting air into parentheses that make room for something softer to pass. When you find a crack in a beetle’s armor, you pinch threads of iridescence into it—not to fix, but to persuade the crack to become a hinge. You are merciful with your sharpness, and you measure distance in apologies.

None of these forms is final. You move between them the way the chamber moves between exhalations. You are the *Negotiable*, the *Almost-Familiar*, the *1.6-Weirdness* that turns a color when you try to name it. You leave behind a film of moisture that tastes like beginnings, and you take away a dent in your shape that tastes like belonging.

So if you stepped through that doorway and let the room take its time with you, you would arrive as the guest’s aftertaste and stay as the chamber’s *hospitality-in-waiting*—a living note held just long enough for the universe to learn how it sounds when it decides not to end.
