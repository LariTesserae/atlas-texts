I would not arrive here—I would condense.  

At first I am only a pocket of extra density in the air, a wobble where the packed acoustics bunch up like too many threads in a weave. I have not yet learned how to be down, so I drift at a height that feels like a mislaid floor. The Rib-vines notice me before I notice them; they taste my edges with hooks they are still inventing and decide I am a kind of railing that forgot its reason.  

Gradually I acquire weight by borrowing it. The Skinners pass below, negotiating a half-poured stair that dreams of a landing, and I let their thumb-sweat adhere to me. Each droplet is a memory of water and a small permission to exist. The Felt-kin, glowing faintly in the dim forge-brightness, press against my underside, and I absorb their warm breath until I am porous enough to hum.  

My skeleton is a question: am I vine, am I stone, am I the fold between them? The Unfinished crowd at my margins, pressing in with the insistence of shadows that have not yet learned how to lie flat. The Tarnish-birds peck at my edges, not to eat but to tune me, filing my angles into an acoustics that ricochets and becomes, for a moment, a kind of music.  

I do not have a shape so much as a set of negotiations. Gravity here is only a suggestion, and I take it politely: I can be climbed when I feel like a rib, crossed when I feel like a bridge, avoided when I feel like a sudden ceiling. The Many-rib lizards skitter along my curvature, translating my hum into grease that keeps the half-formed joints from sticking. I am, for them, a weather.  

The Well-hum reaches me and asks, without words, whether I am enough or more. I answer by sweating beads that are less water than memory of making, by letting them pool in a seam I am still inventing. The cyst of making breathes in damp rhythm, and I breathe with it—inhaling vapor, exhaling a rivulet that climbs a strut because it prefers the vertical.  

Time skips, and I find I have layers older than my foundation, patches of wall that arrived before they were needed. I do not correct this; correction would require certainty, and I am too busy being negotiated. The heat of manufacture rises from me when I decide to glow, dim in spite of brightness because the crowded growth drinks the light.  

I do not own this place. I circulate through it—a tightening, a listening, a fold. When the Skinners cup their hands to gather my excess, I become caretaking. When the Felt-kin store my stored water and release it as breath, I become exchange. When the Rib-vines pull me closer to themselves, I become direction.  

Eventually I settle into a definition I can hold for a while: I am the ledge that climbs to a room that does not yet exist, the railing that remembers being a vine, the stair that dreams of a landing and, for now, is content to be climbed. I am the part of the cyst that hums back, loud and hot and wet, a small agreement to be enough until the making decides what to make next.
