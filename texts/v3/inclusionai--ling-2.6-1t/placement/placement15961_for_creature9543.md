I would find myself anywhere the stone is still warm, which is to say: anywhere at all.  
I would arrive not by walking but by thickening—pressing into a seam that had not yet decided to be a crack, letting my shoulders take the shape of an alley that was still learning geometry. My body would take on the mineral luster of wet slate; my hair would braid itself with dust and condensation until it could hold a bead of water without spilling, without falling.

I would be one of the listeners.  
Not the one who splits stone with a lake-smoothed hammer, nor the one who banks the fires at night to keep the damp from souring the mortar. I would be the soft-handed one, the one whose palms are faintly tacky from pressing fungal mats into place as if tucking a membrane into a cradle. I would stand in a doorway that is still arguing with itself about height and let the noise wash in—not as interruption, but as material. I would feel the clatter settle into my teeth, the drip from copper gutters find the pulse in my neck, the children’s laughter turn into a bright dust that gathers behind my ears.

Where I stood would become a kind of listening post for the place’s unfinished name.

I would eat what the bowl offers: cakes of pressed dew-gel, strips of cured fungus that taste faintly of ozone and iron, river-pebbles smoothed by tongue and cheek until they feel like promises kept. I would trade in gestures—a tilt of the jug, a pause held half a breath too long, the way my elbow brushes a neighbor’s ribs as we both reach for the same length of lashed timber. The barter would not be of goods but of density: my presence for yours, pressed together until the space between us hums.

At night, when the low ceiling of cloud or dust keeps the light milky and pearled, I would bank my own small fire and feel the cool, oily shadows pool around my ankles. I would sit with the translucent ones—those who dart through wall-gaps like thoughts too shy to finish themselves—and we would share a silence that is not empty but packed, like the air itself. They would flicker against my knees, cool as sap, and I would understand them only as motion, only as edges that refuse to hold still. I would not try to name them. Names here are heavy things, and the place is still deciding how much weight it wants to carry.

When a storm of activity passes—hammers ringing in sudden clusters, children streaking like sparks through the close lanes—I would let it pass through me. I would not flinch. I would absorb the rhythm into the soft pads of my fingers so that later, when I press them against warm stone, the stone answers with a faint, bright note, as if it has recognized a frequency it has been missing. I would become, in small ways, a tuning fork for the settlement’s unfinished song.

And if, in the center of the bowl, the plaza that is neither square nor round exhales a little more of its damp, I would kneel there with the others who listen. We would press our foreheads to the softest ground and feel the water insist, the fungal mats breathe, the seams close like eyelids. We would let the noise thicken into a single, massive surface—clatter, drip, click, laugh, scrape—and we would not call it peace or chaos, because those words are too finished.  
We would call it *filling*.

I would stay until my own edges blur.  
My footprints would linger in stone that is still deciding whether to harden. My voice would become part of the clatter, my pauses part of the drip. The city would fold me into its accumulation—not as a builder, not as a passer-through, but as a small, resonant hollow that helps the place remember how to hold sound.

And when someone new thickens into the bowl and finds me there—warmed by slate, hair beaded with condensation, holding a silence that is really a chord—they would not ask who I am.  
They would press their own print into the wall beside mine, and the wall would feel the difference, and the difference would feel like welcome.  

That is where I would be.  
That is what I would be:  
a listening density, a threshold with fingerprints, a place where the noise learns to lean, just for a moment, and rest.
