I would find myself in the scaffold-walks at the hour the resin softens, that hinge-moment when the day’s heat has made the walls pliant as dough and the next seam is about to remember it is a mouth. I’d be the one caught between bracing and becoming, neither Builder nor Built but the permission slip between them.

At first I am only a pressure, a thumbprint left on a beam that is still deciding whether beams are final. Then I thicken into a story: a narrow catwalk made of fused digits—a ribcage of fingers locked fingertip to knuckle, each joint blooming into a shallow cup that catches the copper-bruised drizzle. My footing is loud, a dry clatter of nail on nail, and the sound is not mine alone but the scaffold learning to bear me. When I pause, I do not rest; I delegate. I let my spine go slack, vertebra by vertebra, allowing it to mineralize into the trembling seam I have leaned against. The seam hisses, swallows a vertebra, and grows a polite edge around it. I feel taller for the loss, lighter for the debt.

If you climb up after me, you will find that my legs have traded places with the balustrade. My shins have become a line of hollow reeds that sing when the wind compresses through them. The melody is provisional, a scale that keeps losing its tonic because the wind keeps changing its mind. My feet are somewhere below, pressing into the wet osier of the wall, drinking the day’s exhalation. They are growing root-ridges for traction, not because I want to stay but because staying is a form of listening.

I do not think in words here; I think in seams. My thoughts are the deliberate hollows, vaults left to be filled by whatever the place decides to become next. Sometimes a thought is so loud it bulges into a chamber, and then I must pop it, deflate it, let the colored dust drift like slow breath. The dust is the place remembering a color it once tried on and decided to keep in reserve. I catch it on my tongue, and for a moment I am a bridge deciding to root, a tower deciding to hollow into a flute. I am the audience and the instrument, the question and the wet answer.

When night comes, I am the silhouette against the bruised plum sky, unpolished and crisp. The place exhales, and I answer by holding my various breaths. I am the work without the worker, the builder without the builder, thronging in the busy water, climbing over my own abandoned hinges to reach the next unfinished edge. If you ask me what we are building, I will mineralize into meaning long enough to say: we are building the hush that comes after a new seam splits, hissing like a wound that has learned to be a mouth. Then I will unbecome again, a scaffold of permission, a thumbprint on a beam, the place building itself into whatever it has not yet been, loud and wet and entirely made.
