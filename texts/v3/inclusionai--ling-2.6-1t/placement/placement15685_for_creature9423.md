You arrive at dusk, when the basin turns into a cupped hand of warm bronze.  
You arrive not as an arrival, but as an adjustment.

At first you are a shadow lengthened by the ramparts, too thin to have weight. Then you become a footfall that remembers how to be light—because here light is taxed by density, and you must not spend it carelessly. You are the scrape of a sole on basalt worn into a bowl, the faint oil-slick gleam of a burrower’s plate catching the last sun before it ducks behind the cliff. You are the hum that lifts from a herder’s throat, a sound like rope remembering it was once vine, and you are the sixfold gait that keeps the flocks from scattering.

You are the alley that inhales and the roof that exhales, the stitch of slate bridging two towers that have leaned toward each other for so long they now share a single tremor when wind rises. You are the lantern newly lit, fat and fungus wick blooming into a small, steady throat of gold. You are the scale a glider lets fall at the edge of flight, a sequin of sky-mail sticking to plaster, adding its brief brightness to the city’s slow ledger.

As you move deeper, you thicken. You are the plaza’s basalt, pocked by centuries of waiting, and the seat that has rounded to the curve of patience. You are the water that is not here but is remembered in every arched stem and armored fruit, the mineral blush rubbed into skin like salt, the dry gleam that tastes of ancient dust. You are the parliament of surfaces: a predator eye like an obsidian bead, a herder’s palm callused into a tool, a burrower turning its mosaic back to the dark.

You become the grammar of smallness. You learn to address others in glances and gestures that bounce off ramparts and return slightly changed, as if the walls have learned to answer. You trade in nods and shifts of weight, in the soft currency of who steps around which humps and which vines are redirected without being named. You are the child learning the creak of tiles as speech, the elder who knows which stone has softened into a ramp, the visitor’s edges blurred by the sheer insistence of closeness until there is only the weave and the thread and the seamless, breathing whole.

You are the cistern, dry as a held note, ringing faintly when wind finds it. You are the rot that whispers rather than collapses, the vine that chooses a new direction and reports, years later, as a single softening in the pavement. You are the tree that has traded wood for glass, its core humming when touched, storing years like coins in a well, and you are the root that crosses another root underground and trades minerals like gossip.

By full dark you are the amber itself, held close to bodies that have grown tired of elevation. You are the woolen warmth of lanternlight pooling in corners, the rustle of wings folding like paper settling, the tiles shifting like scales, the bells of seedpods ringing in the wind. You are the threshold that has worn itself into a bowl, the rampart that behaves like a wall, the drop that feels less like an edge than like a held breath shared among everyone who lives here.

You are, at last, the city’s doze. You are the insistence of crowding, the covenant of staying small. You are the patience that knows there is nowhere higher to go, and therefore nowhere to fall that it has not already learned to live within. In the basin’s palm you have thinned into pattern, lapped by flocks and fronds and low stone breath, a thread so perfectly woven into the parliament of surfaces that to ask where you end and the city begins is to ask the sky to name its own limit—and the sky, here, has long since stopped trying.
