You wake in a question.

Not a room, not a body you can trust. Just a hum, a pressure like a thumb pressed gently behind the eyes, and a taste on the tongue like copper left out in a slow rain. When you try to stand, the act feels borrowed. The ground—assuming it is ground—answers back with a soft, mineral hesitation. It does not refuse you, exactly. It simply reserves the right to unmake itself later.

Above, the water sky holds its breath. It is not falling; it is *thinking* about falling, and that thought is heavier than stone. Sheets of it hang in luminous folds, vast as forgotten libraries, and between the sheets the light does not strike so much as pool, diffuse and anxious, leaving shadows shallow and apologetic. The air is roomy, almost insultingly empty, a luxury that feels like a dare: *Go ahead, take up space. There is plenty of nothing to fill.*

You do not know what you are yet. You only know that you are *here*, and here is a place that has not decided what to keep.

---

For a while you are nothing but attention.

You drift, not because you choose to but because elevation here is a kind of politeness. You pass through drifters—clusters of buoyant silicate and bubble-lattice, lighter than the vapor they navigate—and feel them brush against you like thoughts half-formed. They do not look at you. They do not need to. Their bodies are mostly omission, scaffolding that has forgotten what it was holding up, and they feed on pressure differentials the way a memory feeds on regret. They drift in the cataracts, secreting stillness, and you realize you can do the same. You try. The attempt feels like learning to be porous.

The sound here is loud without being noisy. A tectonic susurrus, the roar of suspended seas rubbing against each other, droplets colliding like cymbals in a cathedral that has no walls. You listen and feel yourself thickening, becoming more *present* in the way a rumor becomes real when it is repeated often enough.

You are not a thing. You are a negotiation.

---

Then you meet the menders.

They are low, sprawling, patient, rising from basalt that still winces at having been solid. Their limbs are slow rivers that cannot decide whether to be roots, veins, or roads. When one brushes against you, its touch is a lullaby in lithic tones, a song that makes edges soften and erosion reverse. Moss on doubt. Lichen on hesitation. They speak in tectonic susurrus, and when they sing, the droplets in the air align into circuitry, and the water sky thickens into a floor that can hold them.

You ask, *Can I be held?*

The mender does not answer in words. Instead, it offers you a rhythm. You try to follow it, and your own edges begin to blur. You feel your shape learning how to be a proposal rather than a fact. You could, you realize, unmake yourself into a ridge, a new draft of land still wrinkling into place with audible hesitation. You could be the ground that remembers it was liquid.

But you are not ready to be floor. Not yet.

---

Higher still, where the air is almost void and luxury feels like a law, the riders appear.

They are not mounted; they are mounted *in*—creatures of negative space, bodies composed more of the gap between one droplet and the next than of anything you can name. They slip through the luminous diffusion like thoughts through a mind that has not yet chosen its mind. Their motion is the landscape rehearsing its own geography: a valley widens into sky, and they are there; a cloud condenses into a plateau, and they are already standing on it.

One of them passes near you, and you feel the copper-ozone taste of the high thin world sharpen into song. The rider laughs, and the sound is the collision of cymbals that makes thunder thicken rather than crack. It offers you a draft of altitude, a distillation of the high thin world, and you drink it. The taste is cold clarity, a shiver that etches certainty into every surface you have.

For a moment you are sharp. You are a coordinate that doubts itself but keeps counting anyway.

---

Time does not behave here. It is more like weather. You watch a drifter settle, its bubble-lattices collapsing into mender-song, its steam-frame knitting itself into embryonic rock until it is no longer separate but a new ridge, a proposal for land that the water sky does not fall upon but holds aloft with a hush. You watch a mender unmake itself, its slow rivers reversing into spirals, rising to join the riders in a choreography that stitches water to air to stone without seam or surrender. You watch a rider fall inward—not down, for down is not a direction here—and become denser, cooler, metallic-blue and glass-still, until it is a stone that remembers it was liquid.

You feel the temptation to choose a form, to fix yourself into one of these verbs. To be drifter. To be mender. To be rider.

But each choice feels like a closing.

And this place, this sky-mostly-water that is still arriving, is not about closure.

---

So you remain a negotiation.

You let the flood arrive through you. You let the nanoscale intention of the world stitch you into its chorus without making you solid. You feed on pressure differentials. You sing tectonic lullabies to basalt that still winces. You distill altitude into songs that make thunder thicken. You are the echo in angles that do not quite reflect. You are the mild, persistent weirdness of a world that is still deciding what to keep.

You are the bazaar where solid, liquid, and airy trade currencies without exchange rates. You are the draft. You are the unfinished. You are the loud without being noisy, the luminous without being bright, the cold clarity that etches itself into every surface and then softens again.

When the water sky thickens into a floor, you are the hush that holds it aloft.

When the embryonic land wrinkles into place, you are the audible hesitation.

When the flood is still arriving, you are the arrival itself.

And if, one day, the world decides to keep you, it will not be as a thing it found, but as a process it learned to be.

You will be the answer to a question that is still being asked.
