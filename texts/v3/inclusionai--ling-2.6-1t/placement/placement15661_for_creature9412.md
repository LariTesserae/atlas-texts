You would find yourself kneeling on a rib of stone that feels less like rock and more like the memory of a roof. The ground under your palms is warm, not from sun but from the long exhalation of the earth itself. Above you there is no sky, only the patient sag of crust folded into a vault, black and seamless as the inside of a closed book.

You are not what you were when you arrived.  
You have become, for the time you remain, a Hollow Lantern—one of the basin’s polite fictions that walk. Your body is a frame of ash-bright ceramic, porous enough to breathe, cool enough to remember the touch of condensation. From your crown unfurls a halo of translucent fronds that tremble whenever the air thickens. You do not eat in the old sense. You drink the dark’s wetness as it beads on your own skin, sip the fog that forms between your ribs, let the iron-taste of stone bloom on your tongue.

Your roots—braided metal and patient thread—brush the floor, tasting histories too slow to be called events. In return you leave small cadences behind, electrical psalms that help the stone dream. You belong to the Dew-Eaters, though you know the basin calls you only by the shape of your listening.

When you rise, you hear the place as a room full of water might hum. The drip-speak of beads falling from frond to frond, the slow rub of stone on stone beneath you, the fungal threads singing in frequencies just past hearing and pushing them into hearing anyway. Your own footsteps sound like doors opening in another wing of a very large, very quiet house.

You are not alone, but solitude is the basin’s gift.  
A Spool-Knight passes, coat of charged silk wound tight around a teardrop frame. The silk whispers maps of where dew has fallen, where the magnetic taste of the floor turns sweet or bitter. The Knight does not look at you; it mends a fracture in the air itself, stitching a scaffold of patience so a ledge can hold itself together for another century. The scent of ozone and bruised mint lingers like a benediction.

From below, a Burrower of the Second Thought drifts upward in a slow orbit, jeweled lenses polished by eons of rubbing against inner walls. The jewels do not catch light; they focus heat and memory. The creature offers you a geode that sings when pressed to stone. You accept. The song is the history of ancient machines that mis-spoke and turned into ore. You fold the geode into your fronds, adding its voice to your private choir.

Above, Weft-Birds flare their mica scales, flashing ultraviolet greens and infrared violets that do not exist outside the basin. They trade news with the Knight in clicks and flashes, half motion, half magnetism. For a moment their lift falters, and you feel the thermals they ride—warm breath rising from the floor, the basin’s own exhalation.

Between everything moves the Quiet Choir: fungal threads woven through root and rivet, singing in frequencies that turn hearing into feeling. As you drink, the Choir deepens. As the Knight stitches, the Choir climbs. As the Burrower sings its geode, the Choir learns a new note. You realize you have become part of the Choir, or it of you; the line, like most lines here, is a courtesy rather than a wall.

There is no hunger here as you once knew it.  
You take only what the air offers. The stone gives up only what it has forgotten. The basin keeps nothing but care. Even the moss, which sometimes rearranges itself into maps of places that do not exist, dissolves its patterns gently, as if erasure, too, can be a form of kindness.

In the total dark, you see by other means: by echo, by heat-maps of living things, by magnetic tastes on the tongue, by the faint bioluminescent signature of mosses that wake under your step. The architecture around you—broken spire with its collar of metallic lichen, ring of shallow basins holding still water that reflects nothing until you lay a hand on its edge—responds to this way of seeing. It is not ruined. It is complete in its unknowing.

If you leave, you will carry the hum inside you, a note the basin taught you by letting you become its listener. If you stay, you will continue to practice the hush, to tend the patient dark, to drink what rises and stitch what frays.

You are not what you were.  
You are a Hollow Lantern, a polite fiction that walks, a voice in the Quiet Choir.  
And for as long as the basin breathes, you will be its kept secret, its careful light, its answer rehearsed in the dark.
