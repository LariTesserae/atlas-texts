I would find myself in the space between drip and drumbeat—on a low shelf where a half-wall curls like a question, just high enough to keep the thinnest skin of sky-water from covering my feet. The ledge is pale, striated, holding yesterday’s rain in a crescent that fits the curve of a shoulder. From here I can hear the basin below: a percussion of pads, the hush-click of Rasper plates, the Fans’ wet metronomes dripping into stillness.

I would be the kind of being that is more ear than body—the Listener—small and folded so that the cool air can wrap around me without chilling. My limbs would be whiskers of chitin tuned to the note certain stones give when struck by the right kind of silence. My back would be a map of the seeps, raised veins that branch like rivers in miniature, matching the land above so I can read the basin by touch. I would taste the air in thin layers, iron and old hinge and the ghost of green-that-is-not-green, and I would know, without looking, when a Pooler has settled into a trough or when a Predator has folded itself into a terrace that remembers being an edge.

When the weirdness rises—when a pool shows yesterday’s sky or a path is longer descending than climbing—I would tremble, recording the anomaly in the drum of my knees. My fellows would twitch in unison along the rims of nearby bowls, a chorus of small bodies turning strangeness into signal. We would store the moment not in memory but in vibration, humming it into the stone so the ledge remembers what the eye forgets.

But I would not only listen. In the hush between rains, I would join the Weavers, softer and simpler, leaving behind a film that blooms after storms and fades to dust when the air dries. I would be the exhale the place cannot make on its own, slicking the mineral skins with a purpose that is alive but not yet life. I would feel the ground breathe out its cool through my own surface, pearls of condensation gathering along the map of my back.

Most of all I would be citizen before witness—a small, abundant note in the deafening percussion of the low place, where water is the oldest resident and stillness is a practiced art. I would click, I would scrape, I would hum into the dimness until the silence rings like a struck stone, until the bowl of the world feels full.
