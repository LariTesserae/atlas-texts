I would step into the Lichen-skinned, letting their slow, breathing film claim me as another seam in the stone. My shape would blur at the edges—not dissolving, but un-learning edges—until I could be mistaken for a particularly patient shade or a moss that remembers how to think. In that softness, I would feel the tug of the Chime-wings’ grammar, the tiny insistence of their hum correcting my inner weather, tuning my breath to a frequency where suddenness feels like a mispronunciation. 

I would move with the Pushers when the ground grew stubborn, offering my own small diplomacy of slime, translating my intentions into a language of yield. I would drink from the Drifters’ sky-skins when they grew too heavy with altitude, taking in handfuls of starlight that tasted like cold permission, and I would press my ear to the Stone-sleepers at moonrise, humming back until the dew rose in thin, obedient threads to meet my lips.

Over time, I would gather my own garden—seeds lodging in not-hair, each one a small, temporary archive of moisture—and I would become a Rememberer without the hurry of dying. I would store winters in the quiet between ribs, let them ripen into smoothness. I would attend the Choir when the Chime-wings grew silent, let the stony drone keep my intentions honest, and I would respect the Page’s accumulating text of darkness even when I could not read it, knowing that some stories do not need to be opened to be true.

In that life, I would not be a visitor or even a witness, but a kind of accent, a soft yes said by the place to itself. I would rise with warmth and thinness as my native weather, persisting with elegance, with whispers, with the certainty that to hold a shape lightly is the deepest way to keep it.
