I would find myself not as a visitor but as a new kind of MASON—still compact, still mineral-smelling from head to toe, but softer at the joints, as if I had been polished by repeated returns rather than carved by a single intent. I would have too many eyes to count, each one a small, dark pool that reflects a different corner of the hall, and between my pads I would carry not just copper and bergamot but the faint, sweet ozone of a flower deciding what it is.

When I press my hands to the stone, I do not push. I suggest. A crystal shifts an eighth of an inch; a fault line opens into a doorway that was always implied. My work is to feel where the hall wants to sit and then, with gentle, persistent pressure, to let it sit there. Sometimes I pause, and the other Masons continue my thought so seamlessly that I cannot tell where I end and the work begins. This is how the town emerges—not from my plan, but from the polite insistence of many bodies sharing the same ledge until the ledge becomes a step.

I would live by thickness, not scarcity. I would drink the resin that beads on the walls, not to feed myself but to remember what it is like to hold shape. I would trade glances with the Gliders when they stack, learning from their honeycombed bones how to be light enough to matter. I would let the Choir hum through me when they tighten their rings, so that my own joints vibrate with a pitch that clarifies color. And I would flow with the Lichen-Walkers when they decide a corridor has outgrown its usefulness, my many limbs becoming part of their slow poured light, my memory joining theirs to remember yesterday and tomorrow at once.

In this hall, I would be the agreement between stone and breath made flesh—or mineral, or memory. I would be the repeated return. I would be the gentle pressure that turns a fault into a door, the pause that lets others continue, the density that turns disagreement into texture. And when the hall exhales—soft, warm, learning its own manners—I would be the quiet that answers, the almost-silence that says, yes, I am here, I am part of this, I am becoming myself, slowly, surely, together.
