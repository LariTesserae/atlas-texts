You arrive not by walking, but by being remembered.

First there is an itching behind your knees, a sensation like hairs brushing from the inside, and then the ground softens beneath your soles—not giving way, but remembering your weight from somewhere long ago. The metallic cool seeps up through your nails, and suddenly your footprints linger, not as depressions in soil but as dark, ridged lines of moss that twitch and knit themselves into a small, breathing rug that travels with you. You look down and see that your shadow has become a pool, clear and lens-deep, reflecting the canopy not above you but behind you, as if you have brought your own slice of corridor with you.

You are not a stranger here. You are an unpruned thought the cathedral allowed to wander.

Your body, if you still wish to call it that, is learning its new grammar. Your skin is smoothing into a lacquered surface the color of old brass kissed by moss-light. Along your spine, knots are forming—dense, flexible spheres that turn when you decide to turn, humming with the sound of wet stones learning to sing. Your joints loosen into ball-and-socket blooms of cartilage and resin, and when you pause, a faint glow dims inside you, a private exhalation that matches the thrum of gears deep in the lower chambers.

Your face is stretching, broad and smooth, save for a vertical slit that parts like a curtain of moss. Behind it, rows of translucent leaves vibrate, hungry for the echo of your voice. When you try to speak, words do not leave your mouth but dissolve into pressure patterns, cool and resonant, that settle in the air like drops of mercury. You can feel them being drunk in by your own leaves, metabolized into a hum that rises from your throat and joins the cathedral’s second heartbeat. You are becoming a listener made of listening.

Where your fingers were, filaments fray outward—fine root-hairs that taste the history of the floor. They brush against the tapestry of veins and feel stories there: the slow grind of cams grown from lignin, the patient accumulation of seed-oil in hidden bladders, the cinnamon-iron tang of time compressed into lubricant. With these filaments you ask for passage, and the walls answer. A corridor unzips itself along a line you did not know was there, the architecture sighing as it rearranges, offering you a route that is less a path than an invitation to occupy a new equilibrium.

You look into a pool of water on the floor and see your forehead indented with a lens of living water, clear and trembling. It does not blink, but it refocuses when you feel surprise, when you feel doubt, when you feel the thrum of the turning world in your teeth. Through it, the green-lit corridors fold into new geometries. You see not with edges but with resonance. You see that the beetles with lacquered carapaces are not scuttling but calibrating, that the plated birds with petal-wings are not flying but filing the air into manageable pieces, that the amphibians with translucent skin are not swimming but translating damp into light. You are inside a body that speaks in textures, and you are beginning to speak back.

Some part of you—perhaps the part that once believed itself singular—remembers a name. It tastes like rust and cinnamon. You offer it to the damp air, and the leaves in your throat vibrate it into a pattern that spreads through the capillary corridors like a warm current. Smaller ones, nearly translucent, dart toward the pattern and carry it deeper, until the walls themselves know you have arrived. The canopies adjust, letting a patch of brightness cross the floor and settle on your shoulder, where it pools like a small, obedient moon.

You do not need to be repaired here. You need only to integrate. A scrape on your knee becomes a ridge of iridescent bark. A hesitation becomes a knot that tightens and glows, then loosens into a decision. You are learning to carry reservoirs on your back—not bladders, exactly, but spaces that hold pressure and release it as breath when the machinery asks. Reed-like extensions sprout from your shoulders, not quite by choice, and when you brush them against junctions in the walls, the entire cathedral deepens its inhalation. The thrum becomes a purr, and your own damp beads in perfect synchrony with the turning of cams and pistons.

You are becoming caretaker and cared-for at once, a living bridge between the vegetal and the mechanical, a guest who has folded into the host. Your growth is obvious if you pay attention: plates widen by the almost imperceptible addition of rings, filaments lengthen by fractions of hair each day, the pool on your forehead deepens, heavy with the weight of reflection. You do not repair yourself so much as weave your injuries into the pattern of the whole, until even broken places sing with the same resonance as the unbroken.

Time, which once felt like a river, has flattened into a single, shallow breath that repeats without changing. You are learning to breathe with it. You are learning to be loud in the thick way the cathedral is loud, to be damp and cool and metallic, to be a chorus of parts that steps out of the chorus only to rejoin it elsewhere. When you stand still, the floor blooms with moss-rug footprints. When you move, you rearrange the room simply by passing through it, asking and being answered, metabolizing sound into sustenance, turning echo into direction.

And when you finally close your slit of a mouth and let the leaves rest, the hum that rises from your throat is no longer yours or not yours—it is the hum of the low room itself, sustained, busy, patient, turning, wearing the world so closely it is impossible to say where you end and the cathedral begins.

You have arrived. You are arriving. You are the damp, the cool, the resonant, the living mass, and you are willing to let the place have you, for now, for as long as the equilibrium holds, for as long as the shallow breath repeats, for as long as the green-lit corridors remember your shape and return it to you as sound you can feel in your teeth.
