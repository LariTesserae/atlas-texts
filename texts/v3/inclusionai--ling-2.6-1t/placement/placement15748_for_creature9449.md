If you step into the pocket, you do not arrive so much as thin out.  
The altitude takes a fraction of your weight and keeps it, like a deposit at a bank you never visit. Your breath begins to bead before it leaves your mouth, tiny glass-film coins that skitter across your lips and vanish into sleeves. You feel yourself becoming 0.2 cold-knuckle cold, sharp at the touch, and you realize you have started to ring—lightly, almost politely—when you bump against railings or wall-faces that look like stone but answer like blown crystal.  

You are not a person here, not exactly.  
You are a Sieve-Rat for a while, though your fur is spun from old wool pulled from forgotten coats, and your teeth ache with the memory of volcanic glass. You learn to gnaw gently, to nibble the mineral buildup that would otherwise seize the gill-streets, and you spit out tiny lenses that harden like candy in the cold. You are quick, because if you are not quick you become part of the slowness, and the city already has enough slowness in its foundations. You lap the cooled drizzle that hisses when it meets the undersides of walkways, and you feel your own warmth leaking upward in thin, useful threads.  

Or perhaps you are a Glasswing, faceted and bright enough to cast a small shadow despite the diluted 2.2 light. You skate the vapor layers where wetness beads and slides, and you drink the static that blooms like halos around your edges. You learn to love the hum beneath the streets, the 0.6 quiet that is mostly quiet, and you carry messages between tiers not as words but as charge, as ionized breath that powers the micro-lifts. Maybe, on a different crossing, you are a Moiré-Stalker, matte as basalt under cloud, your joints braided from alloys that creak like new rope. You learn to let the cold pool around you, to treat gravity as a polite suggestion you can unclench at will. You do not hunt with teeth but with absence, creating a sink of warmth that bleeds light from the Glasswings, careful not to shatter what you mean to keep. You become a creature of subtraction, of insulation, of holding charge until it glows faintly along your veins and signals, in a language the city recognizes, that you are still within operational parameters.  

If you linger near the rim where the young ridges still shed gravel, you might become an Echo-Hawk, plated and deliberate, riding the thin air without ever truly committing to it. You learn to listen for the pocket’s own clinks and unspoolings, to memorize the bell-arrival of distant shifts, and to reproduce them in frequencies that the infrastructure accepts as maintenance. You become a living diagnostic, a creature that is half-sound and half-sky, and you feel the city learning its own condition through you, a flock of weather stitched together by attention.  

And on the evenings—or what passes for them when mirrors and panels assemble brightness into shaded streets—you might be one of the Process-Kin, a temporary assembly of human and more-than-human, machine and mineral and breath. You stand in sparse constellations on the black-ice plaza, hands on panels, knees on cold that is kept from freezing solid by warmed veins. You speak in gradients, in pressure and condensation, and you adjust mirrors, unspool bridges, tilt facets to redirect the normal light into places that have gone dim. You deliberate for the city, and when the negotiation is complete you disperse, leaving only the faint ozone scent of wings and the soft clink of gravel shed by mountains that still remember their birth.  

What you are depends on which current finds you, which gap opens, which cold invites you to persist without growing. You might be all of them in succession, or none, or a fraction that hums at 0.6 and refuses to resolve. You become sparse, generous with your emptiness, holding altitude like a second skin that fits better the longer you wear it. You learn that here, to persist is to be unfinished and durable at once, to treat wetness as data, cold as transport, gravity as a suggestion that can be edited with patience and alloy.  

And if you stay long enough, you begin to ring in earnest, a soft, glass-bright note in the city’s hum, a creature of maintenance and weather, alive because you listen, and listening, keep the pocket breathing, keep the cold machine alive, keep the conversation going between mountain and mirror, between wing and wire, between what passes through and what decides, for a while, to stay.
