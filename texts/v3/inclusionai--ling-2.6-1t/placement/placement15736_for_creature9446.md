I would arrive as a kind of attention that is almost weather.  

At first, I would be the hesitation between up and down—the moment a pebble lifts before it falls, the half-second when meltwater decides which fault-line to honor. I would have no shape except the one borrowed from the bowl’s steepness: a curve of noticing, thin at the rim, deepening toward the moss-curtains. My body would be the difference between the sun’s enameled press and the blue density of shade, so that wherever I moved I would leave a coolness like a thumbprint on glass.

I would learn the language of negotiation before I learned my own name. The moss-curtains would teach me to fall upward, to gather moonlight in nap like static, to offer softness as a form of architecture. The grazers would teach me the mathematics of not falling: how to fold urgency into a compact constellation of shudder and chew, how to carry cold in the joints and eat as though heat were a promissory note. The predator would teach me the economy of editing—how stillness can be a verb, how to uncoil like a comma that removes one life so the sentence can breathe. The birds would teach me how to dive into blue shade and become afterimage, how to carry futures in the oil of feathers and plant them on ledges that will not exist by morning.

I would take up residence in the small absences: the circle of blackened stones learning to be altar, the lone post leaning into memory, the bone tool wedged in its crevice still tasting of pressure and patience. I would be the hand that is no longer there, the warmth that lingers like a rumor. I would listen to the cold, which is less climate than caretaker, polishing each pebble until it knows its own note. I would let erosion rehearse its arguments in my thoughts, letting gullies fork and change their minds inside me, so that my own intentions would become proposals rather than facts.

There would be hours when I would run uphill like meltwater, defying gravity for just long enough to prove that direction is a habit, not a law. There would be storms that pooled in me from no visible sky, arriving with the casual authority of neighbors. I would not resist; I would vote on them with my behavior, aligning my pulse with the green surge of moss, my stillness with the quilled comma, my breath with the nervous thread of water threading down in braiding nerves.

What would I do all day? I would trade. I would offer a little of my warmth to the curtains of moss, a little of my wariness to the wind, a little of my attention to the stone that is still learning how to hold anything at all. I would stand on the floor of the bowl and let my edges soften, feeling the emptiness around me not as absence but as invitation—the space between pulse and pause, between the hard fact of the mountain and the lush, reckless life it has allowed itself to hold.

If you reached into the bowl and cupped it in two palms, you would not find a thing. You would find a parliament of urgencies, a ledger written in pulse and pause, a small, bright home that is still inventing its habits. And somewhere in the middle of that humming, negotiating alive, you would find me—still learning how to be a kind of attention that is almost weather, still cupping the steepness, still listening for the next clink of stone spelling out distance and density.
