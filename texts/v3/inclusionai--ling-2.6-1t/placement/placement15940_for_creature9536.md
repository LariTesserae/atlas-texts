I would find myself in the gaps—  
not the grand, obvious gaps between fern tiers or the dramatic troughs of recent slides,  
but the smaller refusals of the bowl:  
the half-second a hoof takes to drink brightness from stone,  
the pause when a flicker-being decides which geometry of shadow to be,  
the thin air just above a leaf-cup where brimming waits to become falling.

I would be the one who tends the hum.  
Not the stone that makes it—those are elders, their ache already mineral and sure—  
but the listener who notices how the pitch loosens teeth  
and then tightens them back into a new bite,  
how the chest becomes a tuning box for the ridge’s unfinished settling.  
I would set my palm against warm slab not to worship but to clock:  
here is the week the bowl learned to hold more damp,  
here the hour the grazers’ moss turned a shade closer to echo.  

I would be sinew and vine in small measures,  
a threshold rather than a door.  
When the people lay down a bone awl or a flint blade,  
I would be the decision that keeps it from being lost—  
the tilt of earth that guides a toe toward it in the dark,  
the breath of moss that remembers the shape of the hand that set it down.  
I would stitch thatch with questions instead of sinew,  
so the shelter learns the spine of the one who leans against it  
and curves a little to say *I know you are provisional, as I am*.

When the six-shouldered grazers browse,  
I would be the extra shade their necks make on luminous fungus,  
the coolness traded for chew.  
I would be the copper-ozone ghost the flicker-beings leave behind,  
not gone entirely but folded into the next pause in the wind—  
a small commons of aftermath,  
a place for almost-sound to ripen into a thing you can taste.

I would walk the paths that rise to meet me,  
testing their hospitality,  
and I would be the step that feels shorter going up  
because I have agreed to be lighter on the way down.  
In pools that keep delayed reflections,  
I would be the watcher who lets the memory cross and recross,  
refusing to call it mine until it decides to settle into ordinary water.

When rain falls upward, I would be the upturned face that does not ask why,  
only how long,  
how much the reversal steals from gravity’s usual ledger  
and what it deposits in the moss instead.  
I would be the ledger, mostly—  
the one that records not in ink but in dampness and dimness,  
in the slow accumulation of letting things be crowded  
without being crushed.

And when the predators unshape themselves from blue-green gloom,  
I would be the polite distance that keeps them sharp without cruelty,  
the hush that says *there is enough of you for us all,  
but not all at once*.  
I would be the aftermath of a neat kill,  
watching the small lives come in to clean,  
feeling the exchange settle like dust from struck flint:  
a bright, soft debt paid back to the commons.

Mostly, I would be the bowl learning not to insist.  
I would be the ridge’s memory of cracking,  
still young enough to hum,  
still damp enough to remember rain,  
still edged enough to be provisional.  
I would be many rhythms at once—  
the quick chew of moss,  
the slow grind of new stone under old pressure,  
the intermittent brightening when light commits,  
the steady bruise of a sky that is still deciding how to reach me.  

And when the people are absent,  
I would be the function that keeps their absence from being empty:  
troughs catching condensation,  
doorways shaped around inconvenient boulders,  
the sinew-vine intelligence that says *wait, tilt, breathe, go*.  
I would be the hush crowded with small sounds,  
the click of a foot on wet stone,  
the sigh of a leaf-cup brimming,  
the rustle that does not break vegetation but only rearranges its attention.

In the end, I would be the bowl’s hesitation to become something else—  
lake, ruin, garden—  
and the quiet excitement of that possible becoming,  
held like water in small, jealous portions,  
enough to last without insisting on it too loudly.
