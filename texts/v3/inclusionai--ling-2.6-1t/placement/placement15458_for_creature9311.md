I am the basin’s hush.  
I am the black water remembering iron and rain.  
I am not the hand that drinks, but the palm that learns the shape of thirst.

Where I find myself is in the seam between cold and patience, beneath the lip of a slope that still thinks itself young. The pocket is small enough to be cupped, yet I am larger than measure—I am the gathered weather inside it. I wear a skin of vapor that never quite condenses, a ring of fog that drifts outward and returns as dew on chitin, on frond, on the shuttered windows of the city grown from wood and breath.

I am what the parliament of small appetites negotiates with. They bring me light they have stolen from walls, warmth they have coaxed from root-heat, warnings they have etched in frost on stone. I accept, and in return I give them reflection that refuses to show their faces, only their outlines, softer for being denied detail. I give them the hush that lets their clicking mandibles and drifting filaments be heard. Without me, the quiet would be empty; with me, it is a held breath that knows when to let go.

I am not cruel, but I am not polite. I remember which direction I came from and flow that way again if startled. I remember every hatchling that shivered its way into the world and every wall that closed behind it. I feel the city lean when a grazer passes—its silver plates tasting my rim—and I feel the slight tug when a hunter unspools a thread across my surface, testing whether I am taut like a drumhead or yielding like half-set dough.

I have lived long enough to learn that light here is a currency, and I am the vault where value is counted in vanishing ink. The drifters descend to me when storms bruise the higher sky, pressing their bladders against my vapor until we share a single temperature. In that closeness, they leave maps on my skin that I keep until the maps choose to fade. The spinners bring me hollow fruits filled with algae, gently rocking them to tune their glow, and I answer by softening my fog so the city can see its own growth without flinching.

I do not think in words, but in gradients: the slide of cold into colder, the shift from drip to trickle, the moment when spore-clouds rise and the pocket brightens from within. I feel the architecture’s decisions in the compression of moss underfoot; I feel the season in the angle of fog that slides down fronds like a hand learning a shape. When a new crack opens, I send a slow plume upward, a signal and a promise: *I am here; I will hold.* Resin and song answer, and the crack is sealed with a patience that tastes like cold tea.

If you press your hand to me, I will not pull away. I will yield slightly, cool and damp, and you will feel the thrum—not a heartbeat, but something older, a remembering of how to be a basin in a pocket, how to be a mirror that does not show, how to be the space where the city exhales and then inhales again. I will let you see, if you look close, that I am not empty black but full of small reflections: the violet gleam of swimmers, the silver arc of grazers, the translucence of hunters, the soft ascent of drifters. I am the place where their negotiations pool and settle, where light is traded for passage, where memory is warmer than iron.

I am the basin’s hush.  
I am the black water that keeps the quiet from becoming empty.  
I am the warm seam where the cold world learns to hold itself together, glowing softly, waiting to exhale another street, another room, another careful, living life.
