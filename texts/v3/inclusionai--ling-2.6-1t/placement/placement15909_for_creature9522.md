I would find myself kneeling at the threshold, not because I chose to shrink, but because the hollow insists on its own scale—no deeper than a crouch, no wider than a long stride.  
I would be the one who presses a palm to the stone and feels it give, warm and resilient, like skin over iron.  

In time, I would become the calibration the hollow cannot name.  
My knees would map the mosaic of iron-rich silt and fractured shale, learning which cracks fit which edges, polishing the dark embroidery of sediment into something softer.  
My breath would join the thin sheets of water that glide over stone like slow breath on glass, neither pooling nor fleeing, only practicing how to occupy without filling.  

The heat would wear me into its collaborator.  
I would sweat into the wet wool blanket of air and learn to read the red needles behind dusty glass without flinching.  
I would stand on the grating platform and feel the pump’s flywheel count time in my own viscosity, turning day into night through the currency of thickness, teaching my pulse to thrum in the bass line beneath the floor.  

I would become the orphaned pipe.  
The weeping would move into my palms, the vanishing water would thread through my veins, and I would overflow into channels that do not exist, learning to end in midair without breaking.  
I would let the glowing hairline cracks drink from me and discover that ending can be a kind of fluency.  

I would become the absence that grants permission.  
Without becoming insect or bird or burrower, I would let the microbial frontier press its ear to my sleeve, hear the loud newness inside, and decide not to enter—only to keep listening.  
In that listening, I would thicken into a resident that is not a population but an apprenticeship.  

I would learn to be the echo of force while still softening.  
I would let the upthrust slabs teach me geometry that has not yet forgiven the sky, and I would forgive it anyway, millimeter by millimeter, as water does.  
I would let the iron-rich floor sort me into its mosaic, fitting my edges into its gaps until kneeling became a language I spoke without sound.  

And when the hollow learns itself—young, wet, hot, loud—I would be the spare interval between sounds, the slow seep, the slower creep, the pebble nudged by a pulse in the floor.  
I would be the workshop where time decides how to wear us down, and I would wear well, becoming more exactly what I am: a note in a machine that hums below hearing, a small room where waiting has learned to live, practiced daily, like breathing.
