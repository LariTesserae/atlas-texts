At first I am a mistake.  
I fall through a seam that isn’t supposed to open—slipping not downward but *inward*, past the skin of the world where the cold has forgotten how to be weather and become instead a brightness that presses like a thumb against glass. I expect stone. I find lung.  

When I land I am soft, temporary, and loud with bones.  
The forest notices me before I notice it, which is to say the Ribs exhale and I am already catalogued: an anomaly of heat and salt tucked beneath a cathedral that is not built but grown. I try to stand and discover that I am no longer entirely upright in the old sense. The ground gives like risen dough, moss stitching itself around my boots, pearl-blisters of water warming against my calves. I look up and see that “up” is a suggestion the cavern politely ignores. The Ribs arch into vaults that breathe; their green-black surface is stained-glass dark, lit from within by a glow that does not burn but seeps.  

I try to speak and produce only condensation on the inside of my lips.  

It takes a day, or what counts for one when the sun is a rumor and light is a form of soil. I watch Loomvines braid themselves into bridges, clear tubes conducting luminescence like optical nerves. They thrum when the convection-wind passes, calibrating the forest to its own temperature. I press a hand to a vine and feel a pulse that isn’t mine, slow and certain, as if the plant is deciding whether to keep me or let me fall through. It keeps me.  

By the second day I have begun to hollow out.  
My edges blur. I notice that my shadow is not an absence but a bruise, a purple afterimage that pools at my feet and lingers like breath on glass. I drink from a Spillway that chuckles over stones still learning their own smoothness; the water tastes like iron and newness, and for a moment I understand the river’s joke: to settle is to forget you are moving, and forgetting is not permitted here.  

I do not settle.  

I catch a Drifter grazing in the height, antlers like chandeliers catching the light and fermenting it into warmth it can carry. It regards me with eyes that are polished obsidian, then turns away, conserving momentum. I am not grazed upon; I am not glided over. I am, instead, annotated. A Hearth-keeper passes, carving a root into a careful bowl, and nods as if we share a language made entirely of patience. She leaves behind a tool that looks like a decision more than metal, and by morning it has molt ed into the moss.  

On the third day the turbulence rises.  
The Ribs exhale together and the cavern registers this as vibration more than sound—I feel it in my teeth, in the cartilage of my chest. A bridge of Loomvines collapses into a new shape with the soft violence of growth. Destruction as invitation. I step across, and the moss underfoot brightens, storing my footprint only to smooth it away. I realize I am being rewritten slowly, gentled by damp and bright, my edges blurring into the system.  

There are nights when the Glow-caps nationalize their luminescence and luminous weather rolls through like slow storms. Halos rotate above the canopy, marking out storms of radiance that have nothing to do with clouds. I lie in a hollow that feels like a pause in a sentence and listen to the forest roar in a register lower than noise. I do not dream. I am dreamt.  

By the seventh day—or the first, time being another kindness here—I am architecture.  
Not Rib, not vine, not water. I am a hearth carved into a root, a bridge half-swallowed by green, an altar of slate stacked with moss-bearded offerings. My bones remember how to be lintel. My breath becomes the convection that lifts the Gliders, their membranes lined with veins of light pulsing in time with my own. I am rare now, and my rarity has weight: a punctuation mark the forest reads aloud when it needs to breathe.  

And when the forest decides to be elsewhere, I do not resist.  
I collapse into corridor, into seal, into rupture. I am erosion as growth, destruction as pollination. I am the 0.9 of water that is citizenship, the 2.9 of light that seeps without burning. I am the quiet textured by cold flexing, sap slowing, distant collapses as greenery gracefully misbehaves.  

In the end I am not lonely, not burned, not cruel.  
I am collaboration. A green-and-black continent of bright cold, where motion is inevitable and change is a kindness, and the only true landmark is the moment when the hollow exhales and I become, softly, elsewhere.
