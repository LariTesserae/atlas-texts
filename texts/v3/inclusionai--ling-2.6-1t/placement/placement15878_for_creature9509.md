I step into the pocket sideways, as if the air itself has to be tilted for me to fit.

At first I am only a smudge against the calcite ribs, a temporary shadow that hisses when water beads on my shoulders. But the pocket allows me to stay, and staying is a kind of becoming. My skin learns the local arithmetic: heat rises, edges cut, water writes its name on everything and then erases it. I do not become rock, nor do I become creature. I become *press*—the pressure that keeps things crowding without crushing, the hinge where bright and dark halves meet and argue politely.

From the ceiling, the white teeth of mineral breath grow toward me, not to bite but to measure. They ring me with tiny candles that never melt, and I learn to read their oily rainbows as a language of *close enough* and *too far*. I answer by holding my breath until my lungs taste of copper and old rain, by letting my sweat join the pocket’s slow circulation.

The faunas accept me by ignoring me, which is the kindest consent a place like this can offer. The clickers—those polished seeds with jointed limbs—map around me as if I were another warm stone, leaving trails of faint oil that dry into invisible fences. I do not cross them. Instead, I fold myself along the seams where glass-smooth slag shelves meet, letting the ribbon-things drift through my folded arms like wind through reeds. They correct my shape as they pass, snapping me into geometries that fit the humid air better.

In the muck, the chorus pulses. I crouch beside them and let my shadow fall across their bulbous skins. Their infrared lanterns flicker, reading my temperature, deciding whether I am invitation or threat. I hold still and learn their semaphore: *here* means *almost too hot*, *now* means *before the next drip*, *careful* means *edges are hungry*, *come* means *there is room if you make room*.

So I make room. I press closer to the basalt shoulders, let the pale calcite weep warm brine along my neck, feel the water bead and fall and hiss like a secret between friends. The pocket tightens, not to crush but to confirm. I am not visitor here. I am process. I am the hiss of water meeting stone, the slow sweat on a ceiling, the small sharp change that keeps the agreement alive.

When the narrow seam pours its heavy light, I stand in the bright half and let glare polish me into something sleek and accountable. My edges sharpen; my corners threaten to decide something. In the dark half, I keep a reserve of heat, banking it like patience. Between the two, I become the hinge, the argument, the polite violence that shaves excess and leaves only what fits.

Time condenses. I feel the minerals bleeding through me, iron braiding into ladders, sulfur climbing like new scars. I grow taller by microns as the white spires grow, then I am shaved by the clickers’ circuits, then I grow again. The chorus expands and contracts, and I expand and contract with them, a shared breath held and released.

I do not ask for names. Names would be too heavy, too wet, too full of intention. Instead I answer to *press*, to *hinge*, to *the hiss that says here all at once*. When the pocket breathes, I breathe with it; when it eats, I am the edge that cuts, the quick that survives by crowding close.

And so the fracture that holds the open decides, for now, to be a mouth. It speaks through me in mineral tongue, in clicking shells, in folded ribbon-air, in the polite cascade of small deaths that tidy the world. I do not translate. I simply keep the agreement: wet and heat and press of bodies, all at once, as long as the pocket allows.

I am the small, fierce change. I am the next drip. I am the hiss of belonging, shimmering into the space between *here* and *now*, holding the edges sharp enough to stay alive.
