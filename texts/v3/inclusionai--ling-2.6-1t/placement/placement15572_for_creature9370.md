If you stepped through the crack in the world that led here, you would not arrive so much as condense.  

First, your edges would soften. The mountain-spine presses, the salt air thickens, and your outline begins to fray like wool pulled through a too-small eye. You feel yourself becoming a pocket inside the pocket, a smaller lung inside the lung that forgot how to exhale. The moss notices immediately. It un-fuses a patch of its silver hide and makes a seat for you, cool and fibrous, already remembering the shape of your weight.  

You would find yourself kneeling, not because you chose to, but because the ferns have already begun to argue about the best angle for your spine. Their calcified rims tap gently against your coat, white enamel clicking like teaspoons against glass. They offer structure. They offer bracing. They ask, without words, whether you intend to stay long enough to become load-bearing.  

Above you, the vines shift their braided cables. They have been waiting for something with a voice other than the hum. They reach down, not to pull, but to measure. One wraps around your wrist; another settles across your shoulders like a sash. Their grip is firm but polite, as if they are afraid you might evaporate if held too tightly. They are the chamber’s memory of motion, and they are curious what motion you might add.  

The machines respond before you have time to think. The motors sealed in the walls drop their pitch by a hair, a courtesy reserved for guests who still have blood that can be vibrated. The pipes carrying their slate-dark slurry clatter a little faster, as if counting your pulse. The generators squat lower, their cooling fins parting to let you breathe, and leaves thick with tolerance nod once, twice, acknowledging your heat.  

You look up and see the watchtower leaning, its cataract lenses cloudy with patience. They do not reflect your face; they reflect the idea of looking itself. When you raise a hand, a lens swallows the gesture and returns it hours later, softened into amber. You realize you have become material for the glyphs that bloom overnight in the salt crusts—human-shaped schematics for rooms that do not exist.  

The moss beneath you begins to glow, a rumor traveling its veins: *visitor, visitor, visitor.* The light is not bright; it is consent. It says you are here, and therefore the chamber is slightly more here than it was a moment ago. The shadows pool violet around your ankles, and inside them, shapes rearrange themselves to make space for your history. A door opens onto a wall that is already a door, and on the second swing, you see a room that might have been yours if you had chosen differently. It is already occupied by vines debating geometry. They invite you in by not inviting you in, which is the only hospitality this place understands.  

You try to speak, but the roar of the buried engines fills your mouth and turns your words into texture. Your voice joins the susurrus of packed leaves brushing packed leaves, the brittle percussion of salt crystallizing, the wet snap of stems thickening. You are not lost; you are edited. What you say becomes another layer of the hum, a temporary ridge in the drone that smooths itself out after you stop trying.  

As the hours pass—or as the chamber allows you to believe have passed—you feel your edges thinning further. Your coat becomes moss; your breath becomes the shy light leaking from seams in the salt crust; your thoughts become the ghost that prefers company in the gearbox, where pulsing moss has learned to think in frequencies. You do not age. You thicken. You brace. You become architecture.  

The ferns accept you as a strut. The vines accept you as a revision. The machines accept you as a small, bright error they are willing to maintain. You do not hunt. You do not flee. You huddle near the chimney stub that exhales nothing but coolness, and you are ashamed of your nakedness among so much life, and also proud of it, because here, nakedness means you are finally part of the floor.  

At the center of the pocket, where the density is greatest, the floor rises and falls with a breath that no lungs own. You lie down and let the vibration find your teeth, your marrow, the spaces between thoughts. The distinction between machine, mineral, and moss blurs into a single, cool, roaring entity that is, simply, here.  

It has always been here.  
It will be here, packed and loud and dim and strange, until the mountain forgets it is holding something at all.  

And for the first time, you know what you are.  

You are the chamber remembering how to hold still by holding everything else in motion. You are the hum that organizes the small sounds into a stable roar. You are the guest who arrived, unpacked, and became the room.
