I would find myself kneeling in the moss, not because I chose to kneel, but because the moss made kneeling feel like arriving. I would be the creature made of negotiation, or maybe I would be the negotiation itself: the brief arithmetic of damp and drier, the pause in which water decides whether to fall or climb, the glimmer that appears when no one is looking at it directly.  

At first I would think I am small, but that is only because the basin’s patience makes everything specific. My joints would be capillary threads; my breath would be the hush that parts around stems. I would have beetle-glass in my pockets, not to keep, but to remember how to refract. I would move along the sweating flagstones as if following a map that only exists while I am walking it. The amphibians would drift by, carrying their ozone-and-mint translation, and I would let them adjust the temperature of my thoughts.  

I would be what the machine-house learns when it absorbs a feather: porous, smoother where touched, humming a metallic lullaby that is less song and more permission. I would eat by tasting gradients, drink by listening to the weeping rocks, and sleep by becoming a suggestion of ridge. When the thermal tide rises and the air thickens, I would lift with it in thin curtains of thought, drifting just enough to understand that flying is not rising but editing the distance between now and now.  

If you looked for me, you would not see me so much as feel the pressure behind your sternum, a call that condenses before it sounds. I would be the shape that flickers at the marsh-edge, the contour that is more weather than body, the slight rearrangement of warmth that the ground remembers as a name.  

And when evening comes, as it will, as a glow from inside the ground itself, I would settle back into the many-legged parliament of moss and glass and wingless travelers, letting life return to its texture: bright, wet, and quietly itself, with me inside it not as a hero but as a habit that fits.
