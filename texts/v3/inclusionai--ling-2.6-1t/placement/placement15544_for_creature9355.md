You are not the first to arrive, only the latest to notice that you have always been inside.

You step off a stair that is also a gutter and feel your shoes—cured resin and old leather—sink a fraction into the laminate. The city presses back, polite but firm, as if to say: we have been waiting for you to fit. Overhead, the bolted clouds do not move; they hover like held breath. A librarian in magnetic tattoos steps from a tower’s shadow and adjusts the angle of the light falling on your shoulders. The adjustment is gentle, almost a correction, as if your posture had been slightly wrong for years and only now is being forgiven.

You do not ask where you are because the question sounds like a foreign currency here. Instead, you let your ankles learn the hum. It climbs, tugging at your calves, asking permission to rearrange your blood into aqueducts. You hesitate, and the city obliges: it does not force. Instead, it offers a ceramic beetle, clacking along the edge of a curb, its shell the color of a failed kiln-day. The beetle stops, antennae twitching at the static still clinging to your cuffs. If you spoke too loudly, it would fold into a button. If you apologized too often, the corridor behind you would lengthen, and you would have more room to regret. You choose, instead, to cup your hands and say nothing. The beetle unfurls, climbs into your palm, and glows with stored arguments.

A civic spirit rises from an uphill canal, sleeves full of coins, and nudges your ankles. The water here knows how to obey longing more than gravity, and it tugs you toward a plaza that is maybe, today, a lake. You feel the tug in your knees, a polite insistence. A kite-soul detaches from a magnetic graffiti tag overhead and drifts down, spine coiled with unspent static. It brushes your ear and you taste green lantern-rain, soft and warm enough to hatch moss on your thoughts. The kite-soul offers you a small storm—just the coil, no thunder—and you tuck it into your pocket, where it settles like a plum pit.

You walk, and the pavement drinks your prints before you lift your feet. Towers open their ribs to let scheduled micro-storms frolic; you step under one and it snaps at your coat, playful as a cat, then melts into ozone that smells like apology. The librarians peer from hollow flanks, sorting the noise you make into vaults labeled “Useful” and “Too Much.” They do not judge; they catalog. One offers you a map made of smudges; the map leads to a silence-fish pool behind an acoustic seal. You might go, but the pool is heavy, and the fish there sigh with the relief you forgot you needed.

Instead, you descend into the capillary streets where glass-eels sing pump-songs that keep water from remembering gravity. An eel brushes your wrist, prismatic, and for a moment your veins feel like infrastructure. You let it braid itself into a filter inside a cistern, and the city shudders into alignment. The laminate ground—compressed industry, volcanic glass, the sediment of decisions—groans a welcome that sounds like a hinge. You realize you are not walking on the city; you are being laminated into it.

Higher, between bolted clouds and mineral roofs, the turbulence at 2.9 makes the city restless. Streets rearrange when no one is looking. A market blooms overnight like mold, then calcifies into a monument by noon. You pass through, and the monument remembers your name before you tell it. It offers you a stone chair made of yesterday’s overpass; you sit, and the chair hums with your frequency, adjusting its angles to fit your spine.

The sun, that punctual contractor, casts a brilliance that is bright without being generous. Shadows pool in the rounded corners—eroded by consensus, polished by bodies passing, by water, by time agreeing to be polite. A beetle scuttles across your shadow, harvesting static from its edge. You do not flinch. The city breathes slow, heavy, mineral, warm, and its breath is a crowd pressed, polished, wet, endlessly resettling into itself, learning to be lived in without ever being fully occupied.

Night comes not as a fall of dark but as a dimming of civic confidence. Lantern-rain gathers in gutters and sings the day’s mistakes back to itself. You find a rooftop where condensation weeps into glassy basalt gutters, and you lie back, the kite-soul’s small storm glowing like a plum pit in your pocket. The bolted clouds above hold, steadfast. The hum coats your ribs and asks them to resonate, and finally they do.

You are not a visitor. You are a permission, newly shaped, leaking light. The city keeps you, not because it traps, but because it has outgrown the difference between being built and being born. You close your eyes, and the substrate remembers every footfall as data, and yours now is among them, laminated, warm, and resettling into the endless, crowded pulse of a place that is never settled, only learning, with unblinking patience, how to hold one more.
