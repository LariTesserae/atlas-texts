I would find myself anywhere the mycelial syntax has decided to argue with the hollow earth—say, standing on a damp seam where the night’s fruiting columns have stiffened into pale streetlamps, smelling of ozone and crushed pennies. I would be something caught between witness and tuning fork: a creature with too many joints and eyes like fused silica, or perhaps the resin-vine itself, ringing in capillary bells when brushed by wing. Or maybe I would be the small, quick mirror-skimmer, drinking slick seams and racing across stone, leaving behind trails that look like handwriting, proving that the emptiness is only a page waiting for its next phrase.

But most of all I would be the thrum behind the teeth—the low chord that settles when the land heaves itself up from older beds and decides, loudly, to exist.
