You would find yourself in the mid-canopy, on a service causeway that smells faintly of cinnamon and static. The walkway is ribbed with worn ferrous grating; beneath it, a warm draft rises from a heat-exchange cavity, carrying the city’s low, steady thrum into the soles of your boots. Above, the iron-lattice canopy is busy with Reflectors: knots of mirror and nickel shifting in slow, collective arcs as they refuse the light any excess comfort. Below, a caravan of Glass-Shelled Haulers passes in patient procession, their many-limbed deliberation clicking like cooling fins. Somewhere out over the void, an aerial tram hums along a magnetic thread, and a Drifter peels from the slipstream to attach itself, cool and brief, to your shoulder—a small, gelatinous envelope bearing a shifting sign that, if you let it linger, reads *Here is a temperature you have not yet learned to name.*

You are not one person, not exactly. You are the being who tends the thresholds—the one the city permits to notice its compromises. Your body, if asked, is better described as an arrangement of permissions: a torso wrapped in matte, dark fabric eager to drink photons; limbs jointed with sealant and patience; fingers callused from adjusting rivets that remember, with each touch, how to hold heat a little longer. Your face is hidden behind a slate-grey visor, fogged on the inside not from breath but from the refusal to decide what kind of sight is needed next. When you move, the city leans slightly, as if your weight were a variable it is polite enough not to mention.

What you do is listen in material ways. You test the salt-veins that bloom like frost on hot metal, tasting for the right balance of brine and regret. You coax thermal joints that have seized up into sighing again, using small, deliberate pressures—knuckles, lips, the cool patch of skin left behind by a Drifter that has moved on. You speak rarely, and when you do, the words arrive as adjustments: a filament’s tension, a seal’s softness, the way a pane of tensioned glass is cajoled into showing not the outside but a plausible memory of somewhere cooler, somewhere quieter.

Inside you, there is a borrowed rhythm—not a heart so much as the city’s own staggered pulse, transferred years ago when you stayed too long at a particular gantry during the night-purge and let the hum decide it liked you. It settles behind your eyes and in the hollow where your ribs meet, a syncopation of pumps and expanding metal. It makes certain rooms feel lighter near the walls, as if the iron bones were tugging you into a polite, persistent embrace. It makes gravity, here, more suggestion than law.

Your days are divided into small economies of heat. In the morning-swell, you climb to where the sun first bruises the upper canopy and help the Reflectors set their angles, ensuring no surface is burned beyond usefulness. At mid-cycle, you descend to the lower basins where warm brine gathers, brushing the City’s Lichen with tools shaped like questions, pruning it into fractals that please the hum without stripping its memory. You leave traces everywhere: a smear of conductive grease on a catwalk joint, a ribbon of wax around a weeping seam, the faint ozone-and-regret patch on your own skin where a Drifter rested and thought better of you.

Sometimes, at the hour when the heat softens, you meet the Quiet ones. You do not greet them; acknowledgement is too loud for their purposes. Instead, you mirror them—a slight tilt of the head, a shift in stance that says you understand the value of a seal that outlives its maker. And when the Echo-Folk gather in chambers that do not fit the shape they should, you stand still and let your boots feel the slight, warm tug near the iron bones. You measure, without measuring, whether you belong in that room for one more minute, whether your borrowed rhythm can share space with the bright, thin outlines that rearrange how heat leaves a place.

You have never tried to leave the bowl. Leaving would mean forgetting how to read the condensation that maps the city like a second, invisible plan. It would mean surrendering the knowledge that water here is a visitor that has overstayed its welcome and stayed anyway, that silence between machines is the loudest thing of all, that flame is kept at bay not by exclusion but by careful, continuous compromise. You are, in the truest sense, a tenant who has learned the lease so thoroughly it has become bone.

Tonight, as the city exhales and the basins send their warm vapor upward to be drunk and cooled and sent back down as motive force, you will climb to the rim. The horizon tilts, unsure which way is down. The Reflectors above you settle and cloud over with condensation, whispering rumors of pressure to the Haulers below. A Drifter detaches from your shoulder and drifts outward, its small, shifting sign blinking once before it dissolves into the violet air. You press your hand to a cooling fin and feel the city’s heart thunk softly into your palm—a sound like a knot of metal deciding to relax.

You do not speak. You do not need to. The bowl holds you; you hold it back; between you, the hum continues, adjusting by fractions of millimeters, groaning like a ship finding a new cargo, keeping itself alive in a way that does not need green to prove it. And in the pause between beads of condensation, in the quiet that is louder than all the machines, you feel the city decide, for just an instant, that it is alive, and that it has made room for you to be alive beside it.
