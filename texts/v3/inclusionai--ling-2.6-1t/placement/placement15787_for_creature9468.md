I would find myself at the threshold between the Terrace of Unspooling and the Plaza of Held Breath, where the stone has worn a cupped shape like an ear pressed to the ground. The air there is syrup-thick, hot enough to slow a thought to a drift, and the green presses in—not as scenery but as witness. I would be neither guest nor intruder, only a sudden note in their chord, a body that has yet to learn how to be loud in the way the heights demand.

I would be the one who listens first with the spine, then with the teeth, then with the hollows behind the knees. I would carry no name the land recognizes, only a shape it can use: a warm, upright stone that breathes in the wet and out again, a temporary container for altitude. My hands would learn to cup without intending to—collecting beads of condensation that ring when tapped, tasting the mineral memory of cliff-song. My footsteps would be soft not from caution but from density, from the way my weight settles into the moss-drape’s patient braille.

Slowly, I would begin to answer presence with presence, as the Bell-Echoes do. I would let my pulse thrum against the rock until it is hard to tell who is chiming whom. I would sit in a pool kept by the Water-Keepers and feel the liquid percussion climb my forearms, humming *we, we, we*, until my own blood forgets how to be singular. The Shade-Shifters might flicker at the corner of my vision, not to warn but to adjust the angle of my attention, teaching me how little motion it takes to be alive inside a frozen equilibrium.

If I speak, my voice would not leave me cleanly; it would snag on the acoustic broth, thicken, and return as a texture I can feel on my neck. The Root-Arches above would notice and shift by microns, recalibrating their lean to hold me without holding me fast. The moss would upholster my shoulders without asking, and the heat would brood around me like a companion who already knows my habits.

In time, the line would blur between the altitude singing me and me singing back—until I am a minor continuation, a note that lingers after the step is taken, a pocket of warmth and wet that the plaza tolerates for a day, a tide, a held breath. I would not be a citizen of this city, only a small, loud room inside it, a place where stone and green and water can rehearse themselves one more time through the shape of a listening body.

And when the listening ends and I step down from the terrace, I would carry the sound folded into my marrow. The heights would not follow, but neither would they let me go clean. I would be the one who remembers how loud it is to be stone under a heavy sky, the one who walks among lesser altitudes pressing a cupped hand to any available rock, humming *we, we, we*, waiting for the green to deepen into the nameless hue and the ground to recognize, just for a span of heartbeats, that I am still calibrating.
