Their clicking would harmonize with the regulators, and you would become the metronome that keeps the cavern honest about tempo.

And always there would be the Hum—not a creature but a consensus, the hiss of leaves turning light into fuel, the click of regulators, the percussive expansion of wood. You would be the verse they agree upon, the living background that says: we are here, we are maintained, we are becoming.  

In this cavern that has made itself a world, you would be the below-skin space where governance and root become indistinguishable. You would be coolly lit, industrious in your gentleness, belonging without needing a horizon to prove it. You would be the City-Tree’s last thought before morning, and every morning after that, learning to be tall by remembering how to hold yourself up.
