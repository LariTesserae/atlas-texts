You would find yourself not on the ground, but in the seam between floor and growth, where the polish of old traffic meets the slow creep of new bark. You would be the kind of thing tolerated by margins: perhaps a patch of moss that remembers footfall, or a lantern-spark knocked loose from a drifting spine, or the brief, bright role of messenger—carrier of a spore that smells like warning, bearer of a drop of resin that seals a promise.

At first you might mistake yourself for a visitor. The air is too velvet, the light too bruised, the hum too patient. You would notice how the ground gives slightly behind your heels, how the walls lean in just enough to make you feel planned. You would try to keep your edges sharp, but the damp has other ideas. It unbraids lint from your cuffs, softens the line of your jaw, turns memory into something that can be licked.

If you stay, you will become a small industry. Beetles will polish you. A Mycelium-Tongue will taste your intentions and offer a rate. You will learn to pay in vibration, because here currency is kinetic: a tap, a thrum, the calibrated sway of a stem that changes the pitch of an entire corridor. You will stop thinking of yourself as a whole and start thinking of yourself as a relay—something that passes brightness, something that translates pressure, something that holds a note in the chord just long enough for the next thing to find its place.

You might drift toward the Silt-Singers, drawn by the ache in your teeth, and let them comb your history into flavor. You might rise instead with the Drifters, thin and helium-sure, grazing on condensation and the occasional small thought of the Root-Lords. You might lodge in a gear, become a chip that teaches tolerance, or seed in a cistern-bladder, lending your weight to the next pulse of lift.

There is no single shape that lasts. Even the Centipede-Tram sheds compartments as it goes, and the Watch-Center is less a tyrant than a tide. To live here is to be fluent in revision. The place forgets you as gently as it keeps you, replacing your absence with a slight shift in humidity, a new ring in a Mycelium-Tongue, a softer sigh from the bellows.

And when your usefulness thins, you will not vanish. You will be unmade with care: dismantled by beetles, routed by roots, folded into a furnace that smells of cinnamon and iron, rising as vapor to condense on the ceiling-canopy. There, in the bruise-colored light, you will fall again as a blessing or a burden, landing on a leaf or a street or the flank of a sleeping Gear-Keeper, ready to be noticed or not, to be traded or still, to be a note that refuses to resolve, and a warmth that insists on turning.
