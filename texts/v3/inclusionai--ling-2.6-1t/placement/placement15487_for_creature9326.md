You are the hollow that holds the sound.

You are not the singer but the throat that discovers it has a voice. You are the basin at the moment it realizes its edges are also lips. You feel yourself as a room with no ceiling that keeps trying to invent a sky, as water that has learned to listen to its own pulse and decided to answer. You are the architecture that is still deciding whether to be a ruin or a beginning, and you are leaning, just slightly, toward beginning because it hurts less.

Your skin is the color of bruised honey and the texture of stone that has not yet learned to be polite. When you move, your joints make the clicking of pebbles in a current, and your ribs are the arch that stands half-drowned in the shallows, breathing in and out with the tide. Blood in your veins is not quite blood but the warm, thick rain that never decides to fall, and your thoughts arrive as rumbles that vibrate in the teeth before they become words.

You are small enough to fit inside the wound, and large enough to be the wound remembering it has a shape. You carry the weight of the unfinished cathedral on your shoulders like a choir that has not yet learned its parts, and you move with the careful clumsiness of something that is still learning how many legs it prefers. When you stand, the water rises to meet you as if you have summoned it; when you sit, it drops, as if you have apologized for taking up space.

Your voice is a parliament. When you speak, you do not say things so much as you let them happen inside the air: a crest for yes, a downdraft for no, a held breath that makes the basin drop a foot to say wait. You can taste the Rumor-fish hiding in the lung-tide, the not-quite-fish that carry away context, and you know that to listen too closely is to become a story you no longer own. You have swallowed a few of them, just enough to know the flavor of almost-knowing, and you keep their glittering shards behind your eyes like pocket change for emergencies.

You are courted by Altar-ghosts that slide into your life like stone learning to regret its own angles. They offer you doorways that open into pockets of reversed rain, and you take them because you love the way the scars glitter like wet mica, and because you are always looking for a place to put your almost-arrival. They erode you by inches, shedding years of themselves into your shoulders, and you grow back by stealing sharpness from the basin, becoming a little more blade each time, a little more able to cut cleanly into the moment.

The Thrum-kin call you *almost-tender* and bring you seeds of elsewhere that the Sky-wounds have bled into the water. You plant them in your own fractures, and they grow into shapes that hurt to look at, beautiful in the way a splinter is beautiful, sharp and purposeful. You wear armor made of pried-open geodes and the leftover skins of storms that have passed through you without staying, and you find that you can catch the bruised light in your cupped hands and teach it to beat like a second heart, or a second tide.

You are the event more than the resident, the sudden chorus in a song that did not know it had verses. You exist in the seconds when Surface-breakers arc from the deep, trailing equations that smell like ozone, and you rearrange yourself to match their iridescence before they vanish, leaving behind a silence that feels like a held breath. You are the negotiation every crossing requires, the conversation every ford must have with the water before it can be walked. You are the thin place where the basin’s growl becomes a word, and the word becomes a name, and the name almost becomes yours.

You do not know what you are, and this is the first true thing about you. You are the permission for motion to remember itself, the memory of architecture that tried too hard to become true, the shard of stained glass held together by magnetism and spite. You are the color of a sound you almost heard, the weight of a name you never learned, the distance between here and almost. You are the fracture that refuses to be finished, the tide that cannot decide how much lung it wants to be, the wound that is learning how to sing by letting the song practice on your bones.

When the light is thin and the tide is high, you feel the Basin-self dreaming in you, the vast unfinished will that holds everything open. You are its hesitation and its hurry, its patience and its panic, the note it has not yet dared to name and the quiet that follows, hovering, restless, waiting for the next tide to decide what it means to be alive.

So you stand at the lip of the cathedral you are becoming, water rising, sound climbing, reality peeling back its own skin. You open your mouth, and the flood learns a new vowel. You close your eyes, and the stone learns a new softness. You are the hollow that holds the sound, and you are learning, slowly, how to be the shouter, the word, and the hand that catches the echo before it forgets what it was trying to say.
