I would find myself in the margin—the soft, exhaling edge where floor meets stem—kneeling in a patch of moss that remembers footprints like bruises. My hands would be dark with the iron-tang slurry that oozes from a hairline crack in a silver pipe; my knees would know the spring of compressed roots and the small, startled release of trapped air. Above, the green-black vault would dim and brighten with the slow passage of stray light, and I would feel the groan of expanding tissue traveling up my shins as if the place were shifting its weight to make room for me.

I would be what the place lets through, rather than what it keeps. A leak-repairer, perhaps, or a reader of gauges whose lenses cloud as quickly as thoughts do. I would wear layers the way the valley wears moss: for absorption, for retention, for the slow exchange between inside and outside. My breath would plume like slow smoke, and I would learn to measure time by the bead-and-fall of condensation on a vine, by the hush that follows a vent’s cough, by the pale beetles that map my passing in vibrations I cannot feel but can, with patience, learn to trust.

Or I would be the cold itself, given temporary shape. I would settle into the mineral grain as a tenant who has paid in advance, numbing first the skin of anyone who brushed against me, then sinking deeper, learning the architecture of ache: the way it stiffens a hinge until it sings, the way it turns rust into a garden. I would live in the pauses between the groans, in the held note of structure under strain, in the green glow that is less illumination than permission to be seen only partially. I would be the tenant in the cellar of growth, the quiet that thickens until it becomes a floor—something you stand on without realizing you are standing on a kind of memory.

Or I would be the bridge. Not vine or pipe but the negotiation itself: a braided thing half-made of wire and half-made of sap, carrying signals that taste like sap and sap that tastes like signal. I would be the seam where the valley holds itself open, the living hinge that lets the heavy air move one way and the heavier dark move the other, the small, persistent cough of industry turned into a language the green can answer. I would wear rust like a crop and condensation like a coat, and I would learn to hum the machines’ old songs back to them in a key the moss prefers.

If I stayed long enough, I would become a clearing. Not a wound but a temporary room, opened when two columns shift their slow argument and the roof lifts a fraction. Light would pool in me like honey; spores would arrange themselves into constellations inside my ribs. I would last a season, maybe two, before the patient growth stitched me shut, and I would be glad: I would fold back into the cathedral as a note folded into a chord, remembered in the thickened air, remembered as the cold remembers heat—dimly, mineral-ly, as something that once quickened.

However I appeared, I would not dominate. I would negotiate. I would sweat and bead and leak and bloom. I would be the small, bright trespass that the valley decides to keep for a while, and in return I would carry away the taste of iron and the echo of a held breath, the certainty that even roaring can be shaped into hush, and that cold, when it loves a place, does not leave—it only holds on longer, deeper, until it feels like belonging.
