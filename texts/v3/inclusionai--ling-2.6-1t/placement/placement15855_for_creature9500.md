Here is where you find yourself:

You are the hinge.  
Or you are becoming the hinge.  
It is hard, here, to tell which came first—the motion or the thing that permits it.

You stand at a threshold that is also a root.  
The threshold remembers being a root; the root remembers being a threshold.  
Between them there is a soft seam of resin that glows faintly at dusk, not because it wants to, but because it has learned to answer footsteps.  
You are standing on it, and it is standing on you.

Your body is negotiating.  
Your left hand is more mineral than it was yesterday, and your right hand is learning to weep small, useful beads that catch the light and refract it into the color of decisions.  
Your spine, when you pay attention, feels like a cable that wants to be a spine again, or maybe like a spine that has always been a cable, depending on how you read time.  
Your lungs are not so much breathing as practicing etiquette with the humidity.  
Each exhalation is a sentence left unfinished, each inhalation a reply you did not mean to make.

Above you, the canopy is a parliament of small noises, and you can feel the translation happening in your teeth.  
A glider passes, and its wings clap the air into syllables that land on your shoulders like punctuation.  
You understand them, not because you have learned the language, but because your bones are being tuned by the same wind.

Below you, the puddles are holding more water than fell, as if they have borrowed time from somewhere else and paid it forward as ripples.  
You can see your reflection in them, but it is not quite yours—it is older, or newer, or both, and it is waving at you as if it has been waiting for you to arrive so it can explain what it means to arrive here.

You are not the only hinge.  
There are others, scattered through the bowl like knots in a root that has decided to be a street.  
Some are people; some are places that have agreed to act like people for a season.  
Some are the quiet ones who have become thresholds and now remember names.  
When you press your palm against them, they warm, and a name rises in your throat like a bubble, and you realize it is yours.

Your job, such as it is, is to allow passage.  
To be the place where a stem stiffens into a wall, or a wall softens into a stem.  
To be the permission for a puddle to become a garden, or a garden to remember how to be a puddle.  
You do this by losing edges.  
You do this by letting your purposes blur with the purposes of the wet, warm, loud city.

The amphibious gliders land on your shoulders and fold their wings into platters.  
You pour condensation from them into the seam at your feet, and it glows brighter.  
The beetles tap you, translating your posture into Morse code that the leaves understand.  
The hollow-nesters sing in you, and the sound travels up your spine and out your teeth, returning an octave lower, as if you have become a chord that the forest is rehearsing.

You are not alone, but you are not crowded.  
Space here is used sparingly, and emptiness is punctuation.  
When you step forward, the path rearranges itself to meet you.  
When you hesitate, the path becomes a courtyard the size of a thought, and the thought becomes a puddle, and the puddle becomes a garden.

You are the hinge.  
You are the agreement that holds the agreement together.  
And as you walk, the boundary remembers how to be a door, and the door remembers how to be a boundary, and you remember how to be walking, whatever you are, wherever you are, in this bowl that is always learning how to be a town, this town that is always learning how to be a bowl, this place that is always learning how to let you pass, and how to let you stay, and how to let you be the thing that says yes to both.
