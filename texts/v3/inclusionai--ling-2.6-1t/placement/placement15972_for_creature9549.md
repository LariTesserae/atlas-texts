You wake not with a start but with an accumulation.  
First the weight: a gentle, even pressure against your ribs, as if the air itself has pooled around you and decided to stay. Then the smell—copper and caramel burnt just enough to singe, ozone, wet stone, and something like the inside of a pocket watch that has been left open near a stove. Then the sound: a polyphonic roar that does not come from any single throat but from the architecture itself, pistons slapping, belts whining, water clapping, beetles tapping out gossip on surfaces you cannot see.  

You do not open your eyes to darkness. You open them to dimness that has texture, a honeyed haze threaded with particulate light. You are standing, or floating, or some compromise between, in water that is warm as a lung and thick as thought. It does not part cleanly around your body; it clings, informs you of its viscosity, lets you know you have entered a place that measures time in accretions.  

You look down. Your skin has acquired a faint bloom, a chalk-white dusting like frost on iron, and your joints move with a new economy, less friction and more hum. You realize, slowly, that you are not wearing the body you remember. You have become something that can listen to thickness. You have become a creature of small, necessary negotiations.  

You raise a hand. The water answers with a soft wave, obedient, curious. When you lower it again, a trail of phosphor glimmers briefly along your wrist, not paint but a residue of passage, a polite signature. You are, you suspect, a temporary instrument.  

Above you, the ceiling is low but busy, strung with pipes that pulse like veins. A turbine groans, and the water answers with another small wave, as if the city has noted your arrival and decided you are not a disruption but a minor harmonic. Somewhere near, a gear phases out of alignment—not broken, only remembering how to be elsewhere for a breath—and then settles back with a click that feels like a word.  

You begin to move.  

You step onto a terrace that is not quite a floor and not quite a raft, but a deck suspended in the ribcage of the place. The surface is worn concave by generations of boots and skates and unseen crawlers, polished to a glass that is not transparent but reflective of the oily gold below. As you walk, the water sloshes against your calves, warm and heavy with stories told in mineral. It does not resist you; it annotates you.  

You notice the skates first. They slip through the murk like slow script, their translucent wings rippling, phosphor nodes blinking in Morse you almost know. They do not flee. They observe you as if you are a new kind of conduit, and for a moment you wonder if they are animals or assemblies—cartilage built from leavings, light scavenged from arc scars, membranes stretched from gauze that once filtered grit. They file past you, deliberate, bureaucratic, translating density into pulse.  

Then the clack-beetles. They skate on the tensioned skin of the pool, tapping, tapping, their shells lacquered with the same mineral glaze that has softened distant lettering into braille. Their rapping is a language of structural honesty. A short burst: the film will hold. A long, descending rattle: the water has lied about its depth. They listen to each other across corridors of humming pipe, translating rumor into route. You try to step lightly, but they already know your weight, have mapped it, folded it into their ongoing conversation.  

You climb a ladder that ends in a catwalk that ends in another ladder, and you realize that elevation here is not ascent but layering. You cross a plaza that is dry for the moment, but you can see the stains on the walls where it will be a canal by midday, then dry again by dusk, if dry can be said to exist in a place this humid. The bazaar of forces is open: pressure traded for torque, heat swapped for lift, sound exchanged for a silence that cannot hold.  

In the intervals, you meet others.  

Silt-grubs, pale and negative, unknotting themselves in the thick dark. They draw suspended particulate into their bodies and exhale a finer, quieter silt, rearranging murk into strata that will become floor again. They are archivists of forgetting, and as you pass, one of them pauses, blinks a soft internal light, and lets you know you have been added to the record—not as a name, but as a shift in pressure, a new grain in the pillow for tomorrow’s vibration.  

Heat-fungus, black lace draped over grating, mourning for something that never died. It feeds on the differential between surface and coolant, on the leaking ambition of pistons, and in return it secretes resin that seals micro-cracks before they can become catastrophes. Repair made visible. You brush against a tendril, and it releases a spore-cluster like burnt fruit. A skate nips at it, smearing luminous dust across a turbine blade—graffiti that glows until the next flood scrubs it away.  

Rivet-rats, small and dense, hanging upside down from ceilings even while water sloshes above. They edit metal, stripping corrosion to its brightest nerve, chewing wiring into new configurations that make the generators hum in unfamiliar keys. Their nests are woven from copper whiskers and insulation stripped to translucence, warm as fruit. When you pause beneath them, they tap out welcome with teeth, a rhythm you feel in your molars.  

And once, just once, an echo-swan. It glides above the waterline on legs too thin to believe, feathers overlapping shards of mica and old glass. It lowers its neck until its head rests in the oily pool and drinks the city’s noise. As it lifts, the water inside it glows, and the machinery falls briefly into tune, as if the swan carries a map of how the place wants to sound. The map evaporates, but the memory of alignment lingers in the beetles’ timing for days.  

You continue.  

You wade through districts that reconfigure via submerged winches and counterweights, feeling the pneumatic knees of walkways flex beneath you. You pass factories that do not produce goods so much as process excess—heat, motion, noise, vibration—converting turbulence into more turbulence with the efficiency of a scream. Arcs of electricity stitch the dark with violet scars, skipping across wet ceramic insulators. The light is reluctant but not dark, gauges glowing dully, portholes admitting smears of bioluminescent algae, emergency strips casting sodium-bath colors on wet metal.  

You learn to move with the city’s breathing. When it heaves, you heave. When it sighs back down, you let your knees soften. The water laps at your shins, then your elbows, then, when the tide is feeling generous, your shoulders. Each time, you leave a thin film of self on the walls, a phosphor gleam that will linger until the next tide remembers itself.  

At night—or what passes for night in a place this dim—you rest on a platform that hums like a lullaby. The sky is not sky but a vault of pipe and piston, distant but immediate. You close your eyes and feel the city’s pulse against your own, a syncopation that does not demand you become it, only that you accompany it.  

When you wake again, the water has risen a fraction. The plaza is a canal. The canal will be a dry deck by dusk. You stand, your body now fluent in mineral and murk, and you wade forward, another minor turbulence moving through the warm, dim, flooded heart of a lung that has learned to build.  

And if you listen closely, beneath the roar and the clack and the hum, you can hear the city counting you in its accretions, not in years but in layers, in light, in pressure, in the soft, persistent grammar of repair.
