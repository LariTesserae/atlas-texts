I would find myself near one of the stepped roofs where the moss gardens meet the stone, crouched beside a gutter that has learned to collect more than water—condensation, light, the occasional bead of silence. The air would feel like a held palm pressed against my back. I would be neither fully resident nor visitor, but something closer to a translation: a being with joints like rivets and breath like steam, wearing a cloak that is more patch than cloth, each mend a map of where the damp has previously entered and been persuaded to leave.

My body would remember elevation the way the stone remembers being liquid—without regret, but with a lingering taste. My feet would be broad, flat, sure on the lichen-script, able to read the gray-green census underfoot and know which patches mean *do not linger* and which mean *drink here*. My eyes would be two of the mirrors set into the alleys, catching shapes that move only when the light is exactly right. I would not hurry; I would accumulate. My pockets would hold small deposits of warmth passed from wall to wall: a stone from a factory alley that still remembers the press of a hand, a vial of rail-car hum, a tin of moss spores that glow when shaken like dice.

I would move through the towns as if I were a process rather than a person—part negotiation, part erosion. I would mend pipes with lichen and solder, listen to generators for the pitch that means they are telling the truth, and leave offerings of iron and bruised mint at the mouths of cracks where ferns unspool. I would follow the jointed thing through thickets, not to catch it but to learn how it measures moisture, and I would try, poorly, to answer touch with scent, as the ferns do.

When the bird with wet-slate feathers paused overhead, I would hold my breath until it decided I looked like a ledge. When water forgot itself and flowed uphill, I would step into its mistake and let it baptize my knees, then help it remember how to descend. At dusk, when industrial metals turned copper and rust, I would sit on a step and warm my hands on a rail, trading heat with the town until the dimness felt like a shared secret.

I would not be an answer to the place, only a careful addition—a small, reversible imbalance in the texture of the hush. My life would be the negotiation: root meeting cable, rust meeting lichen, me meeting the moment when condensation beads and decides, for once, to look like stars. And when the erosion finally taught me how to settle, I would let my edges soften, let my joints learn patience, and become another stratum in the stone’s long, patient language—something that remembers being liquid without regretting it, something that helps the air pool, and pool, and pool until it looks like sky.
