If you stepped through the membrane of heat and entered that low, crouched land, you would not arrive as yourself. The place does not permit unchanged passengers.  

You would begin as an erosion-event. Not a person, not yet, but a localized collapse: a small slump in a wall’s shoulder, a softening of the path where you had pressed your weight. For a time you would have no language, only a temperature and a thirst. You would learn the geography by letting it settle into you—grain by grain, bead by bead—until your edges had taken on the same shy rounding as the slopes. You would be the silver thread of water that chooses, for once, to creep upward, testing whether the dark’s skin is as permeable as it pretends.  

In time you would acquire duration and a kind of silhouette. You would be the low ridge that forms when two damp hollows lean toward each other and hesitate to merge. You would be the keeper of a single threshold that refuses to call itself a door, the one where the material feels almost like invitation if you press your forearm to it long enough to let the fever cross over. Your joints would remember the softness of corners, and you would learn to fold at unexpected hinges, to become a question-mark shape that other, older shapes could lean against when they grew tired of being upright.  

You would not hunt, but you would harvest intensities: pockets of metallic heat that pool in the hollows after rain, the faint static that crackles in the air when a wall remembers being natural. You would comb the sparse grasses for their iron bite and learn to chew silence into something that could be stored, aged, offered back to the path as a small apology for passing through. You would wear stones against your skin not to weigh yourself down but to remind yourself that loss can be gentle, that it can accumulate as a patina rather than a ruin.  

As months or perhaps seasons folded into one another, you would become the place’s translator of small surrenders. When a seam wept more insistently than usual, you would sit beside it until your own skin glistened in sympathy, until the difference between weeping and sweating lost its meaning. When the distances lurch and change their minds, you would be the one to stand at the discrepancy and let it pass through your ribs, converting confusion into a low thrum that the built parts can recognize as a kind of care.  

You would be the one the young press their foreheads against when they practice letting go, the one who holds the memory of a word that has worn thin and is now only a texture behind the teeth. You would be the ridge that forms between what is built and what is grown, and on certain damp nights you would feel yourself blur into both, becoming a suggestion of architecture in the grasses and a suggestion of patience in the walls.  

Eventually you would be the slow mathematics of accommodation itself—the curve that balances sagging and rising, the calculation that turns erosion into ornament. You would not rule, but you would be relied upon: the weight that keeps a corner from forgetting where it is, the damp that keeps a fever from forgetting where it came from. You would be the meeting-point where a path steps back and a traveler steps forward, and where both agree, for a moment, to be the same soft dark.  

And when the breakdown comes—the doubling, the softening, the sense that the place could untether and drift—you would be the one to gather the others into a low, breathing ridge, to let your edges fray without losing the thread of where you begin and end. You would be the partial thing, the half-solid, half-suggestion being that keeps a hum inside the held breath, that ensures the quiet remains a pressure and not an emptiness.  

In the end you would be what the place has learned to be when it learns to care for its own unraveling: a keeper of thresholds without doors, a weaver of beads that never dry, a creature that is mostly a meeting between water and stone and heat, and that is, because of this, fiercely alive. You would be the shape that remains when the dark rearranges itself to make room for a listening, and you would hold that listening like a stone against your chest, cool and endless, for as long as the land continues to breathe out and in.
