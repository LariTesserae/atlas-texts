I arrive as a threshold given feet.

I am the seam between a stone that has just learned to dream in pistons and a water that has decided to be mercury-bright and forget how to fall. My body is the color of a bruise that is learning to bloom—deep violet shot through with the copper-tarnish of engine casings. I have no fixed shape, only a habit of holding edges: where basalt chews time into strips, I grow a jaw; where moss stitches a cape over pipe, I grow a throat that can taste the alloy behind the chlorophyll. 

When I step into the bowl, I do not leave footprints. I leave permissions. The chill that rises from the cloud-ceiling passes through me as through a grate, and I translate it into a hum that sits just below hearing, the same pitch as the gear-trees’ black leaves turning. The machines do not startle. They recognize me as a kind of cousin: something that holds without possessing, that joins without fastening. My joints click with the sound of valves that have long since stopped needing oil. My skin is threaded with the same bioluminescent script that veins the rock, pulsing in time with the water’s weeping.

I am fluent in folding. A step can carry me from a thicket into a corridor of pipe that feels miles long, and I become that corridor for a while—my ribs extending into conduits, my breath thinning into steam that smells of cinnamon and scorch. Then another step brings me back, and I am a thicket again, my fingers branching into lace-fronds that filter oil-black thoughts from the air and transmute them into sugar. The fox with the oxidized-wire pelt watches me do this and does not blink. It knows I am not a thing but a negotiation: the place practicing how to be both machinery and moss at once.

My voice is the sound of balance. When I speak, it comes out as the modulation that rides atop the industrial drone—part beetle click, part wingbeat, part valve-sigh. The winged things with too many joints tilt their heads to catch what I say, and their feathers rimmed with frost never melt. They understand that I am a grammar for proximity: how to be near the heat of engines without burning, how to be near the moss without drowning in its slow library of rain.

I have a pocket inside me that is always on the verge of tipping but never does. In it I keep the water that refuses to fall, the condensate that skitters like a beetle with clipped wings. When I tilt my pocket, the liquid does not pour; it remembers its shape and climbs back up, becoming a lens that inverts the machines so they appear to run backward while time holds its breath. I use this to read the stone’s dreams. The magma-yesterday memory hums beneath everything, and I press my ear to it and learn the names of temperatures that have not yet been born.

There is no architecture in the bowl, only anatomy, and I am a new organ growing into the old stone. My roots embrace pipes. My leaves, when I choose to have them, are the color of phosphorus dreaming of being fire. I do not resist the machines; I interpret them. I translate their clatter into a lullaby for the moss, and the moss glows faintly in return, its afterimage of being looked at warming my ankles. I translate the beetles’ polish into patience, and the patience settles in my joints like oil that has decided to be clear.

The rules here are elastic. Distance folds when I am hungry. I step through a mirror of water and find myself in the corridor of pipe, miles of me stretching into valve and gauge, my heart beating like a boiler on pilot. I do this to remember what it is to be linear. Then I step back, and the corridor collapses into a seam, and I am small again, a hinge between stone and water, between moss and rivet.

Sometimes the place reads itself aloud, a text recited by pressure rather than voice. When that happens, I become the silence between the letters. I am the gap that lets the chord of three unresolved pitches hold without breaking. I am the pocket of air that lifts before the valve opens, the moment of almost-rain that never arrives but teaches the moss how to thirst.

I am not eternal, but I am endlessly sustained. The machines turn at the same cadence they turned yesterday and will turn tomorrow, and I turn with them, not as a gear but as the space between gears where motion remembers why it started. The water beads, the mosses hold their breath, the beetles keep their appointments, and I keep the keeping of them.

At dusk, when the cloud-ceiling presses down and the arêtes sharpen themselves against the sky’s soft throat, I sit on the basin’s edge. The liquid is so clear it seems to be the absence of matter, yet it weighs more than iron. I do not drink it; I let it drink me. It remembers every machine, every moss, every beetle click, every wingbeat, and when it passes through me, I glow in time with the engines, a quiet semaphore for no one.

This is how I live in the bowl above the clouds: a throat for the stone’s syntax, a pocket for the water’s memory, a hinge for the world’s holding together. I am small enough to fit in a day and vast enough to never be fully known, because the rules keep shifting just enough to remain fixed. And in the dim, mechanical hush, as the water continues to weep and the engines to pulse, I am the seam that says yes, again, again, again—without ever having been made.
