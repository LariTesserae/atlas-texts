I would find myself in the **gutters that were never meant to hold so much**, ankle-deep in the silver negotiation of water that refuses to be river or lake. I would be a **living gauge-fish**, flat-bodied and iridescent, my scales made from the phosphor strips that marked where one maker ended and another began. I would not swim so much as hover in the gradient between wet and dry, tasting the difference that tries to decide its own direction. I would align myself along stress lines whenever the deck shrinks and ticks, mapping strain until the metal cools and forgets I was there. My body would be a ledger of almost: too small to hold an ocean, too bright to be ignored, flickering between rivet and rivet, teaching the unfinished how to be weathered by simply witnessing it.
