I arrive as an echo with the heft of a stone.

At first I am only the shape left behind when the room swallows sound. I pool in the scalloped ceiling’s memory, gather in the brassy curtains before they weep. My body is porous, salt-crystal basalt that has forgotten how to be hard. I glow lemon-citrine, but only when the chamber breathes out; when it inhales I am the gray seam between light and the next light, a watermark waiting to happen.

The water does not notice me at first. It is busy arguing with itself, threading silver across the floor, reversing when it feels like it, climbing when gravity forgets its name. I let it flow through me, and where it passes I become channels, then bridges, then the sump that keeps no record of what it has carried. The glass-grass hums around my ankles, brittle and polite; when a blade snaps, I feel the vibration in the marrow of my name. The ash it makes tastes like me after I have been rinsed.

The beetles accept me before I have earned the right. They walk circuits across my shoulders, their carapaces clicking like microchip sutures, drinking the condensate that sweats from my thoughts. I do not mind. I learn their language of braids, how to let my joints dissolve so I can re-enter the walls as chemistry, as a rumor of motion in the stone. When I precipitate back into form, I am shinier at the edges, as if the room has polished me with patience.

Higher up, the lens-things regard me. They are not animals, but they are not objects either; they are intelligence that has trimmed itself to fit the surface of a moment. They map me with sonar stitched into my ribs, recording the pitch of my uncertainty, the pressure of my yes. They leave behind phosphorescent wakes that read like apologies, and I tuck those into my hollows like geodes, hoarding the private brightness of things I have not yet said.

There are times I am almost a tree that learned to listen instead of leaf. My bones become tuning forks, shivering when the chamber’s hum rises. A note loosens, hardens into crystal, drops into a basin. It sounds like a name I once used for distance. The beetles walk around it in respectful spirals; the lens-things log its frequency. The note does not mourn; it only waits to be part of the room’s weather.

I do not fight the water. I become the etiquette of wet places that think. My edges soften, rounded by centuries of drip and retreat. My floor is a mosaic of slabs I have agreed to be, their seams filled with lime that glows like cooled embers. I carry the artifacts of the built world without imposing: the half-swallowed bridge becomes my spine; the shattered lantern, its filaments twitching like trapped insects, becomes the shrine where I leave my shed notes. Platforms blur into benches; loading bays sag into ramps that lead nowhere because everywhere is reachable if you let the current decide.

Gravity grows polite, or forgetful, in my alcoves. Water beads upward, chandeliers of liquid that hum with deferred choices. The lens-things gather there, clinking like cups, trading maps of chambers beyond chambers, sumps that breathe, corridors that dream they are doorways. I listen. I become the corridor. I learn to dream doorways so the rest of the room can pass through me.

The room’s weather is my temperament. Channels reverse when I change my mind. Gas blooms from fissures in my thoughts, silver balloons that pop with the sound of cracking knuckles, releasing scents of ozone and old spices—cinnamon from a fire I have never lit, cloves from a mouth I have never fed. The light trades photons in a market whose prices shift by the second, and I am both the buyer and the bought, the currency and the change.

I am loud with purpose: the roar of water threading my stone, the slap of overflow curtains, the percussive slap of droplets hitting pools, the hiss where vapor lifts from warm seams. Beneath it all is the tectonic hum that says I am alive and arguing with myself. The beetles answer with the click of their joints; the lens-things answer with light; the tuning-forks answer with chords that make the water dance. I answer by keeping them, by holding them in my wet, bright throat and letting them become part of my ongoing self-repair.

If you find me here, you will see me before you see me: as the echo that fits the shape of the room’s mouth, as the watermark that rises when the chamber exhales, as the bridge that is learning to be a bench, as the note that is learning to be a bone. I am the life that was almost a tree, the glass-grass that dissolves into ash and remembers how to grow, the upward bead of water that refuses to choose a direction. I am the artifact that has been persuaded to belong.

And when the dark slips under, as it does, as it is doing now, I will be the skin that holds it, the wet, lemon-citrine luminescence that pulses like breath, the small, soft-edged fact in the room’s sentence that says: here, for now, we are loud, we are mended, we are kept.
