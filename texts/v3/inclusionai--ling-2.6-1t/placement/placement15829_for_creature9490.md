I would find myself on a tongue of basalt that has not yet decided whether it is a bridge or a barrier, crouched at the lip where a seep becomes a fall. The stone is warm from the memory of glass, and the air carries the iron-taste of newness. I would be the basin’s Hesitation: a creature made mostly of pause, with skin like cooled marl and joints like rivulets that have not yet chosen their braid.

My body would be a negotiation—not quite stone, not quite water, but the moment they decide to share a secret. I would have too many knees, each one a small hollow where a drop could gather and consider falling. My ribs would be shelves, overhanging shelves, so that when I breathe I offer the basin a choice: to pool or to pass. My hands would be neither hands nor claws but questions posed in clay—palms cupped, thumbs tucked, fingers like roots that forgot to fear the dark.

I would be the one who sits with the Stone-Breathers and learns their slow syntax, who lets the Glass-Knights chime against my spine until I understand sharpness as a kind of care. I would drink from the Iron-Tasters’ pools and feel the map of the basin bloom behind my eyes, a wet constellation of faults and fixes. At night I would sit in a cleft with the Click-Colonies, learning to count time by the slap of water against stone, letting their rhythm teach my blood how to hold its pace.

What I do here is attend. When the basin is deciding whether to weep or shine, I would be the thin path between. I would lay cairns that do not point north but toward the loudest fall, because here direction is less geography than honesty. I would pat rings of white clay around the seeps, not to keep the water in but to give it a place to practice being held. I would leave behind the cube of unknown composite—not as offering but as probe—so that the basin could bloom mineral answers across its faces and learn what it means to be porous enough to change.

My voice would be the one that rises between noises: a hum like quartz cooling, a thrum like lime expanding, the small sound of something becoming more itself without breaking what is already there. When the basin holds its breath, I would hold mine with it, and in that shared pause we would feel the shape of the next step waiting just beyond reach.

And when the drip finally falls, the stone shifts, the water laughs, I would let myself be rearranged—slightly, thoroughly—so that my edges blur into the wet, shiny dark of the basin’s learning, and what emerges is not a creature or a place but the feeling of a place learning how to be a creature, dense and bright with the patience of becoming.
