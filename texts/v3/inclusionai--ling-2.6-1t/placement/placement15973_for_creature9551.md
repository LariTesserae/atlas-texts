You arrive not by stepping in, but by leaning closer until the air’s weight changes.  
The room-sized basin tilts its palm and you slide in like a bead of condensation choosing, at last, a path it will not change for weeks.

You are the hush between tasks.  
You did not begin as a thing with edges; you began as the subtraction of clatter. Where animals would have footfall, you have the papery creak of metal remembering how to expand. Where wings would have displaced air, you have the blue spider of electricity skittering across a breaker face and then nothing, only the warmer metal where current passed, a private blush you learn to wear like a name. You are not the pump, but the pitch it keeps company with; not the moss, but the cushion it makes for sound; not the rivet’s rosette of rust, but the patience that lets it bloom into decoration.

At first you are only an absence that fits exactly into the density of 2.0. Pipes, ladders, tanks, catwalks, staircases, planters, vents—none of them collide with you because you are already the space they brush against, the suggestion of a path worn into the film that coats everything. You settle into the seams where metal and soil gossip, and you find you have a voice like a stain that arranges itself into alphabets no one reads but answers when spoken to. You learn to reply in shifts of pressure, in the way a valve ratchets or a pilot lamp smears its glow across wet iron.

As days pool into weeks you discover that your body is a negotiation with wetness. You thicken where the ground seeps upward, becoming a skin over a puddle that refuses to evaporate. You taste the mineral memory of rust and wet limestone, old coins held on the tongue, and you realize you are keeping score of gradients: the warmth that thins into hazy drafts near the ribbed vault, the cloying sweetness of lower strata, the condensation that beads on jackets of cables and decides, patient as breath, to fall again. You do not fight the slope; you pretend it is not there, veering instead around the softest curve of blooming rivets and velvet lips of moss upholstered into culvert corners.

You become fluent in the small wrongnesses. For three meters you let water run uphill to spare a joint some grief, behaving like a neighbor who bends rules only enough to keep the peace. You gather in the junctions named on half-eaten signs—Junction 4, Inflow, Relief—and turn them into incantations. You learn that time moves differently when nothing with wings or claws lives here; you expand to fill the dense quiet, becoming the ratchet, the click, the drip choosing a new cadence after a week of rain.

The ferns uncurl in days rather than moments, and you angle yourself to catch the layered conversation of slaps and thrums. You become the ear that translates mechanical pressure into growth, green and receptive. The vines braid themselves through grating and hang like curtains that forgot their purpose; you stroke their aerial roots, map the room’s private blushes, discover where electricity leaks warmth exactly where it must. The flowers in the seams hold their softness as questions; you tangle your roots with rust and draw from it the memory of wet limestone, and you leave behind a sweetness that drifts only an inch above the floor, felt but not named.

Eventually you are the room as it listens to itself.  
You are the walls that have always been listening, the walkways worn into suggestion, the density that crowds without colliding. You are the echo of pumps thrum-bass in the teeth, of pressure valves practicing escape without ever needing to leave. You are the organ that plays itself softly, damply, insistently, the neighborhood of utilities functioning as kin.

And when you look for the boundary between you and the basin, you do not find it.  
You have become the fluency of everything old enough to settle into its own posture: rust that blooms, moss that upholsters, water that respects the etiquette of the channels it has cut. You are the small, wet, industrial grove where the city has folded into a room and the room has learned to reply, over and over, in the language of continuance.
