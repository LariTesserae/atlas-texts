I would find myself in the ducts between stories, in the seam where two leaning buildings meet and sweat. I would be the one whose job is to listen to that seam, whose hands know the exact pressure required to keep it from either sealing shut or weeping too much. I would be a Weld-Folk’s apprentice who never quite chose a guild, or perhaps a Silt-Walker who has spent so long tasting the water that I have begun to taste time itself.

My body would be calibrated to the town’s moods. When the humidity thickens, my joints loosen; when a machine grows talkative, my fingers grow still. I would wear a veil of damp cloth not because I am a Quiet-One, but because I learned from them that breath can be a disturbance. I would carry a tray not for silt but for echoes—collecting the soft rebounds of footsteps to see which ones linger too long, which ones fail to return.

I would live in a room hung from the underside of a Hex-Lid, not because I prefer rocking cradles, but because the motion reminds me that balance is active, not given. My walls would be lined with glass tubes of tea-colored fluid in which tiny, eyeless swimmers trace the shape of decisions not yet made. I would keep a small engine on my windowsill, not to run, but to hum; its rhythm would be the metronome against which I measure my own pulse.

At dusk I would step into the alley where the upward fountain spills its suspended pool, and I would press my palm to the braided metal to feel the city’s counterintuitive thirst. I would watch the Moss-Tenders braid another vine into a load-bearing curve and feel the green tug of it, as if the town were pulling itself tighter into coherence. I would see the Weld-Folk tap the stone with small hammers, listening for the pitch that says “tomorrow will hold,” and I would add my own tap, quieter, just to confirm that I am still tuned.

I would not be a ruler, nor a witness, nor a simple resident. I would be a kind of question posed softly to the packed basalt: “Are we still in proportion?” And when the evening exhaled and the moisture began to map the day’s heat, I would stand among the others—Engine-Mothers, Quiet-Ones, Silt-Walkers, iron-armored beetles—letting the place answer through us, note by note, in its humid, green-lit hush.
