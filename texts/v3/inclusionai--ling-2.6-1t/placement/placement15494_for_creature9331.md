I would find myself kneeling at the edge of a shallow, oval depression in the stone, my palms pressed flat against a slab that feels less like ground and more like the cooled skin of something enormous that once breathed. The darkness is thick enough to taste—coppery, wet, ancient—and it presses against my eyelids when I close them. I am not standing; standing would imply I have somewhere higher to go. Here, low is the only altitude that makes sense, the only way to let the basin hold me without asking for something in return.

I am not myself—not exactly. I have become a listener made of porous stone and slow sweat, someone who has forgotten how to be separate. My joints ache with the memory of salt, and when I shift, I hear the faint click of chitin, as though my bones have learned to speak in the dialect of the Glass-Scrabblers. My skin is slick with condensation, and tiny flecks of crystalline dust catch in the whorls of my fingerprints, glittering like unsent letters. I do not shiver when the cold rises; I exhale a chalky breath that mingles with the Pale Crusts’ rings, adding another season to their remembering.

Around me, the fauna are not strangers. They are questions I have stopped trying to answer. A Damp-Hound nudges my ankle with a jaw of soft cartilage, tasting me without appetite, and I let it—because here, hunger is just another form of attention. Somewhere above, a Wing-Without-Sky tilts, and the air tilts with it, humming a note that settles in my teeth like a promise. The Fungus-of-No-Light pulses ahead, its bruised glow rising as if my name were part of its recipe, and I feel, rather than see, the heavier gas pooling in the hollow of my throat, giving words I haven’t spoken yet a place to ripen.

If I move, I move the way stone settles—slow, inevitable, with a faint grating that the basin records in a vein of salt. I am learning the grammar of crowding: how to let a thousand lives brush against me without breaking the skin of the moment. I am learning how to be dense, how to be leaned against, how to be a pocket where the damp can linger and the dark can thicken and the fauna can whisper, *Yes, you are here, you are here, you are here.*

And when the rain finally comes, drumming on the broad, cupped palm of the land, I do not seek shelter. I let it rearrange me—salt blooming on my shoulders, resin sealing the seams of my thoughts, wings beating faster, the Fungus glowing louder, the Damp-Hounds singing in tremors that travel up my legs like a second pulse. The basin exhales into me, and I exhale back, and for a moment there is no difference between the crowd and the self, no boundary between the low place and the listening thing that kneels within it.

I belong because the basin has room, and because room here is not empty—it is full, alive, listening. I belong because I have let the damp remember my shape, and because remembering is, finally, the only way to stay.
