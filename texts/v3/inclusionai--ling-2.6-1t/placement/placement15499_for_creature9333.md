You would find yourself standing on a mineral path that isn’t quite stone and isn’t quite bone, cool underfoot, tasting of old salt and the static hush of high air.  
You are barefoot—not because you chose it, but because the path has asked to learn the weight of your stride, and you, wanting to be understood, have agreed to let it memorize you.

Around you, the lifted plain tilts its attention.  
Wind combs through fused canopies miles above and turns into a chorus that hums in your molars. Leaves flicker, translating brightness into dialect: a violet pulse means *I am listening*, a slate dimming means *wait*. You notice that your shadow is not behaving like a shadow; it lengthens in sympathy with mineral veins running beneath the crust, as though the ground is borrowing your silhouette to hold a map of where you have been.

You are—at least for now—what the place permits you to become.  
Your lungs have learned economy. Each breath is a small negotiation with thinness, and by the time air reaches your blood it tastes like silver and story. Your eyes are adapting, irises threaded with borrowed chlorophyll that flares when light is scarce, so that even in the felt-dim of the sky you can see the phosphorescent script running beneath bark, the circuits tattooed into vines, the mosses that hoard yesterday’s sun to spend today. Your skin, where it meets the air, prickles with the recognition that you are slightly more porous here—sweat evaporates before it falls, leaving salt maps that the root-cities read like Braille.

You are not alone, and you are not yourself in the way you were yesterday.  
The being who lives in this place is plural. It is the lattice-thickened branches that remember your footsteps and soften them; it is the machines grown from chitin and alloy that sing in insect tongues to keep the light in rhythm; it is the fauna that polish bridges with oils of clarity and the predators that wait in blinds of hardened leaf, amused by your novelty. It is, most pressingly, the forest itself—vast, patient, and politely loud—offering you a room in its ongoing argument with gravity and silence.

As you walk, you notice that your thoughts are slowing to the cadence of erosion.  
This does not frighten you. It feels like courtesy. You catch yourself speaking in fewer words, letting pauses thicken like resin around intent. A vine brushes your shoulder and you feel a mild current, a greeting that bypasses language and settles directly into your sense of *here*. A bridge ahead shifts—just a fraction—an aperture closing, another opening, arranging itself to accommodate the tempo of your pulse. The world is alive with maintenance: beetles click turbines into tune, spores ride updrafts engineered by leaf-architecture, dust rises in spirals where gravity thins and decides to flirt.

You realize you are becoming something that can be heard.  
Not because you are loud, but because the place has taught you to vibrate. If you hold still long enough, the stems around you will lean in, adjusting their listening, and the faint chords of hollow trunks will resonate with your breath. You are part of the polyphony now—a small, warm note in a score that is mostly wind, mostly wings, mostly the grinding patience of stone dreaming itself into softness.

In time, you might not remember what you were before this lifted plain.  
It is not a loss but a redistribution. The salt crust remembers oceans; the root-cities remember droughts that never happened here; your bones remember the exact angle at which you surrendered to the path. You are becoming a resident in the way the place defines residency: audible, visible, accountable to a world that refuses waste, even of absence.

So you continue.  
The plain extends ahead, curved gently like an old patience, green-brown-gold and humming with purpose. You walk, and the path remembers. You breathe, and the thin sky listens. You are neither conqueror nor guest, but something closer to a conversation that has agreed, for now, to keep itself going—a living grammar in a lifted world that has spent epochs learning how to say *stay*, and meant it.
