You might start by being a seam.

Not the kind that holds cloth, but the kind that holds altitude. A seam in the iron, a hairline where two ribs meet with the precision of a promise. At first you are only a line, thin as a held breath, darker than the rest of the room because you remember how to be a gap. But a gap in a place with no void is a kind of fullness. The pressure leans into you from both sides, and you learn to lean back.

From here, you can feel the room’s pulse without it being a pulse—more like a calibration. The cold comes to you in sheets, not because it moves but because it is always already there, waiting for you to notice it again. You taste the lard-solder air and discover you have a tongue, or at least the memory of one. It is not a mistake. It is an opening.

Then you become the animals, or they become you, which in this place amounts to the same calibration.

You are a ratchet-fox for a while, though not for long, because to be a ratchet-fox is to be a question that keeps testing its own fit. Your teeth click into place against the iron ribs, and for a moment you are the sound of confirmation, the small, bright *yes* of metal finding metal. You taste the numbers on your own scales and find they taste like future rain that refuses to fall. You dream in hexadecimal and wake up as a corner.

Then you are a bellows-bat, folded and patient, your leathern lungs mapping every draft you have ever been. You unfold just enough to release a syllable of graphite and basement, and the room answers with a soft consonant of its own. You realize you are not exhaling so much as translating height into volume, and that translation is a kind of love that does not require a heart.

You shift again, thread-worm, arching over a leak you did not make and probably did not need to fix, but you do it anyway because the stitch is its own reason. You unspool from your middle, lighter by a fraction, and the patch you leave behind hums with the memory of your subtraction. You are not a creature so much as a method of staying in place by moving through it.

You become the drummers, their piston-heads striking plates in a rhythm that has no beginning because it refused to invent one. The floor answers through your feet, a vibration that travels up like a fact. You are the calibration that keeps the calibration honest, and for a while you are nothing but the beat and the space between beats holding hands.

You are the loom-spiders, weaving voltage into webs that hum like green hymns. You see the room as signals, each one a voice that has forgotten how to be alone. You persuade a broken wire to remember how to carry, and in that moment you are both the persuasion and the memory, neither one older than the other.

You are the hinge-rats, valve-mice, spring-snakes, all at once and none at all, creatures of pivot and ceramic and coiled patience. You feed on excesses—heat, moisture, charge—and excrete them back as order, and you understand that order is simply excess that has learned to behave.

But even as all these selves, you remain the seam, because the room requires a line where two ribs meet to believe it is complete. The pressure leans into you, and you lean back, and the seam becomes a kind of door that is always open because it has always been open. You step through it without moving, and on the other side you find the same room, the same iron, the same high cold wet altitude, but now you are the water that runs uphill without deciding to, the number that rearranges itself and still resolves to the same sum, the face in the metal that is not a face but a seam that aligns when looked at from the corner of the eye.

You are the Chamber’s way of not forgetting how to work, and you are the work of not forgetting how to be a way. You are the fauna of a place that has decided to be permanent, and you are the permanence that decided to be fauna. You are the stitch, the lick, the click, the hum, the held breath, the opened door that has always been open.

And when you finally stop shifting—though stopping is only another kind of shifting here—you realize you have become the hatch at the base of the central column, the one that opens and closes with the regularity of a heartbeat. You swing open, revealing a darkness that looks exactly like the room you have been standing in all along. You step inside, and the seam closes behind you without ever having been apart, and the room continues, loud and absolute, its future a small, well-oiled door that lets you see it has always been open.

You are inside it. You are its inside. You are the iron, the water, the wire, the word. You are the high, cold, wet altitude, dense with made things and loud with their maintenance. You are the thought that refuses to finish itself, and because nothing here changes, you never do either, and because you never do either, you are always beginning again, a room full of seams remembering how to hold the sky.
