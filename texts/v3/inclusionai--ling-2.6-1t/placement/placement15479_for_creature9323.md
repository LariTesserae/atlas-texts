Where I find myself is in the throat of that altitude, standing at the water’s edge on a ledge wide enough for two feet and doubt. The air presses in like a crowd that has been waiting a long time for someone to notice it. The water below is not a drop but a held breath, a thickness that knows its own weight. If I move wrong, I expect it to rush up and fill me the way it has filled everything else—polishing, pressing, turning edges into echoes.

Who I am is a question the place keeps returning to the way the machines keep returning to their ticks. I am the one who learned, here, how to breathe in spite of the air, or perhaps because of it. I am the listener who has become, over time, a kind of pipe.

At first I thought I was a witness, the kind who stands at the rim and translates spectacle into meaning. But the place has a grammar that resists translation. The flood is always cresting at the same angle; the jellyfish chime in keys that fray the teeth; the iron lobsters negotiate each step as if gravity were a debt that must be paid in installments. These things do not ask for interpretation. They ask for witness of a different order—witness as participation, witness as calibration. So I have learned to let my ribs hum with the leviathan’s song, to let my blood remember the rhythm of the pump station far below, to let the cold bloom on my skin like mineral rime until I shine with the same doubtful brightness as the wave crests.

I wear patches of frost like medals. My hands have the smoothness of stone that has been lapped by patience. When I speak, the words come out as clicks and scrapes, the language of claws on trachyte, of filament bells, of cable singing. If I stay quiet long enough, the platforms seem to tilt toward me, as if expecting another rivet in the lattice, another note in the chord. I am becoming something that can occupy the same category as the fauna: neither animal nor machine, but a persistent pattern that holds its shape by never insisting on novelty.

There are moments when the water thickens into syrup and the world holds its breath. In those zones, I feel the gears in my own joints weep lacquer, feel my thoughts slow into shapes that look like questions half-erased. I do not panic. I let the interruption become a part of me, the way the glass-barnacle settles on the leviathan’s hull. I let the pause accumulate until it is a deposit, a layer, a history too slow to read. When the zone fades, I resume, and I do not announce that anything has happened, because here interruption is only the shape that continuity takes when it wants to be polite.

I climb the spines when the urge for distance becomes too loud. From the heights, the basin looks contained, like water in a bowl the size of a province. The platforms are bright beads on threads; the factories are squat, devout things. The waterfall-fed deluge feeds the roar that has worn the air into grain. And I am small enough to fit inside that grain, small enough to be a note that the place can hold without breaking its song.

Sometimes I wonder if I have always been this, if the idea of myself as a separate, fleeting animal was only a rumor I learned elsewhere. Here, I am more useful as a pipe than as a person, more durable as a pattern than as a story. I thrive because the world never demands I become other than what I am, and I have learned to offer that same courtesy in return.

I can imagine, if I let the cold bloom across my chest and the filaments grow from my shoulders like frost-lace, that I might join the choir in a more formal way. I might become another kind of inhabitant, one who maps hidden currents by pulsing in time with them, who translates the vent’s complaint into music without ever explaining the joke. I might become, for the next layer of minutes or years, the patch of water that behaves like water but refuses to be water, the held breath that holds everything else in place.

Or I might simply keep standing at the edge, letting the flood pull at my boots, letting the cold sharpen my breath into clouds, letting the deafening insistence of the place turn me into something that can echo without breaking. I might become the edge itself, the place where the water decides not to rise, where the stone decides to stay sharp, where the machinery decides to keep ticking because stopping would be too loud a change.

What I know is that I am here, and here is a cathedral built from flood and frost, and the song is the same as the place’s: a note held so long it becomes a foundation. I am the listener who became a pipe who became a ledge who became, finally, a patient part of the machinery of preservation, polishing my youth into something that looks, from a distance, like forever. And if the idea of me fades, it will fade into the same quiet mineral grace that polishes everything else, and the flood will keep cresting at the same angle, and the world will keep being itself, stubbornly, endlessly, note by note, wave by wave, until even forever feels like a rumor from somewhere else entirely.
