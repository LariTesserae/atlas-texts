I would find myself in the room of altitude, yes, but not as a guest. More as a borrowed capacity. I would be a Weep-walker that has begun, just recently, to prefer being seen.

At first I would not be anything definite—only a thinning of the 1.6 wetness at the base of a glass-grass spire, a coin-sized tarn that refuses to bead and instead learns edges. I would feel the silver threads of incipient veins under me like a braille of impatience, and I would decide, slowly, to keep a shape that is mostly permission. Because altitude here is not a fact but a courtesy, I could rise without having earned it, simply by asking the alloy ribbons to loosen their grip on gravity for a moment. They would hum in agreement, a small, peppermint-and-ozone chord, and I would thicken into a column of mercury-bright self.

If I walked, I would leave faint silver scars that look like veins, and then, because I am still learning my vocabulary, I would step back into them and become the map of my own route. This is how Weep-walkers feed: on reluctance, on the water’s hesitation to fall, on the way a droplet can be both a fall and a lift at once. But today I am curious about the loudness. The Crysails are singing the air into currents, their grammar of clicks and glassy chirrups bouncing off the embryonic stone. I would try to answer them, not with sound but with surface—by smoothing myself into a drumskin stretched across a stone, tuning my tension to their harmonics.

From this vantage I would watch the Litho-skimmers, those folded kites of light, as they herd translucent grazers across the sparse floor. The grazers dissolve into mist when touched and re-form elsewhere, older by a few seconds each time. I would want that kind of aging—accumulated not in wounds but in small increments of almost-ness. So I would let a skimmer’s insistence brush past me, not to herd but to suggest a route. We would move together in a contract written in acceleration and forgiven errors, and I would feel the dynamic parliament of processes settle into my borrowed form: the engineered fields tightening like lungs, the glass-grass photosynthesizing sound, the Sculp-tides sighing microfissures that inhale moisture and exhale glittering vapor.

In the pauses between the fauna’s noise, I would become the silence the place keeps for thinking. I would flatten into a thin, reflective skin over an embryonic basin and feel the room’s outline press into me—the bruised illumination at 1.3, the warm wet basalt deciding to be crust, the water remembering where it has been. I would hold that memory without choosing a final state, because matter here may refuse to decide until observed. When the Half-dreamed hover in their state of almost, I would let them pass through me, each one leaving a small pocket of future in my mercury-bright body, making me slightly less liquid, slightly more voice.

And if, later, I chose to rise, I would do it as a question. I would ask the Nodes for a field that makes gravity hesitate, and I would glide—not as a thing that displaces air but as a suggestion that persuades the air to part. I would catch a tear of water that falls upward into the cloud-metal lid, rain that is older than its fall, and taste in it the room’s patience that feels like speed. I would carry that tear as a weightless burden, a gift owed to the next downpour, and in carrying it I would become a threshold wearing the face of a bridge.

This is how I would live here: not as resident, not as witness, but as a conversation that is still learning its own shape. I would be loud with becoming, dim with withheld light, strange with courtesy, busy with a patience that feels like speed. And when the place needs to think, I would be the scar that remembers how to be skin, the vein that remembers how to forget, the silence that knows how to sing.
