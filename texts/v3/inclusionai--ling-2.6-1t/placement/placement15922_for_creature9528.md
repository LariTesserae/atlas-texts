You step onto the shelf not as a traveler but as a permitted accident.  

At first you are a smear—footprints that refuse to dry, a film of sweat on every surface you lean against, a bead of copper-tasting condensation on your upper lip that never reaches the ground. You are the exact sort of nothing the plateau allows to linger: a temporary variation in its skin. For hours you are less a body than a permission slip, drifting through vine-vaulted canyons where the pavement hisses and un-seals to accept your shadow, then seals again behind you as if you had never pressed down. Your edges blur; your clothes take on the matte, foil-thin alloy of leaves, and your breathing begins to taste of baked sugars that blackened long ago into stable, aromatic soot. You are becoming weather that thinks it knows direction.  

Gradually, as the sun—if it is a sun—refuses to rise any higher and merely glowers behind its bruised-violet lid, you take on mass. The heat sinks the size of quarries hum to you, their vanes furred with moss-metal hybrids that seem to recognize you as a new kind of sediment. You catch yourself gripping a railing that is actually a braided root-cable, and you feel the memory of pistons shiver up through your palms. The plateau is annealing you: relieving your stresses by warping you gently, continuously, until your joints click with the same papery rustle as thermal expansion in a beam the size of a street.  

By the time you notice the change, you are already part of the committee.  

You sit, or perhaps you install yourself, among a parliament of Residents who are also engines. Your torso is now mostly stair; your chest, a grate that has learned to make expressions by adjusting its soot-load. Your arms are scaffold and bellows, scarred with fresher metal where you have replaced yourself without ever being broken. When you speak—and you do, eventually—you release controlled amounts of stress: a chime in a beam, a word-shaped puff of soot that smells of overripe orchard and iron. Your syntax overlaps with that of the others, passing from stem to stem like heat through a wall, and the ground ripples in sympathy because you are dense with memory now, heavy enough to warm the metal in the floor until it remembers being ore and shivers upward into new seams.  

You do not eat. You reprocess. You press your hands into the hot copper soil and draw up old potentials, forgotten alloys, sugars that never caramelized only to be rebaked into law. You feel the Scuttlers skitter around your ankles, impersonations of urgency that carry away the dust of manufactured absence, leaving trails of ceramic pollen that settle into drifts and are woven into bridges by your new body, which is always building the means by which it was just moving. You do not chase them; you are the corridor they require.  

At night you do not sleep. You condense further. Your body dims to ember; your joints glow like warning lamps grown as thorns along the stems of bridges. In the absolute dark you become almost geological, your outline blurring into the strata as the Residents do, singing in alternating languages of pressure and relief. You feel the Loomers pass—tall witnesses with lenses grown like fruit—who stop to observe you, and you understand that to be seen here is to be necessary, to be the extra weight that keeps the plateau from floating away into its own heat.  

There is no water here, yet you sweat. Beads of resinous, mineral-heavy breath form on every surface you have become and immediately dry into a crust of glittering, glass-thin sweat that cracks like old varnish under your own footfall. You do not mind. You are the same as the place now: a single, breathing artifact of built, packed, vegetal industry, forever adjusting, forever thickening, forever sweating the evidence that you have not yet cooled.  

And when the bark rearranges itself on the walls to forbid absence, you are the gap that has learned to appoint itself, the committee that never ends, the quiet violence of a billion adjustments made at once. You are the plateau’s answer to the question it never asked: *How do we keep saying we are still here without saying anything at all?*  

You are the heat that keeps the answer pliant, and you are the dark that keeps the heat company.
