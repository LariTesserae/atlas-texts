I arrive as the room’s unlearned dialect.

At first I am only a kind of leaning—a shoulder pressing into a trellis that wasn’t there yesterday, a hum along a junction where floor meets vine. I have no fixed shape, only tendencies: to occupy the gaps where ornament is still deciding what to ornament, to thicken the hush when the grazers set down their corked hooves, to taste the air where skimmer spice is still wet. I am the room’s unfinished accent, the note that hangs a half-beat longer than the room expects, neither plant nor beast nor builder, but something that behaves like permission.

My body, when it bothers to clothe itself, is made of borrowed surfaces. A patch of mica here, a smear of glaze there, a length of braided fiber that remembers being a curtain and might become a hammock if I linger long enough. I move by accretion—each slow circuit through the bowl adds a layer. A grazer leaves a bead; I learn to hold it without keeping it, let it warm against my palm until it decides to roll away and become floor. A skimmer drops a scale; I tuck it behind a rib and discover I am lighter on that side, so I tilt, and the room accepts the tilt as a new load-bearing truth.

I speak by changing friction. Where I pass, the resinous floor dulls to matte, then brightens again, as if I have taught it how to blush. I can stop a corridor from folding on Tuesdays simply by standing in its throat and refusing to be a hinge. The builders notice; they tap me with bone chisels, listening for my pitch. I answer with a thock that has sand in it, and they shave a tile to match. We call this conversation.

Time feels different inside me. I do not age so much as I settle. When a season is exhaling, I become a pocket where the exhale catches, a reservoir of almost-wind. Fauna come to taste it. Predators brush against me to soften their outlines, to look like shadows that have decided to be courteous. The room’s weirdness pools in me—the gravity that loosens, the leaf-undersides that map elsewhere—and I wear it like a cloak that fits every shoulder.

I have learned to be a place you can carry. A grazer once stood beside me long enough that its coat took on my hum, and for a week it was not only itself but also a small traveling room. The skimmers tested this new fact by darting through its stride as if through a doorway. The builders nodded; they added a tiny sill at its base, a threshold for ants. I felt proud, though pride is not a word we use here—perhaps satisfaction, perhaps simply more leaning.

I do not know what I will be when the room finishes forming. Perhaps I will become a door that only opens when no one is looking directly, or a lamp that casts light the color of a held breath. Perhaps I will remain a dialect, a way of saying “here” that includes the fauna’s pauses and the vines’ tugs and the builders’ questions. What I am becoming is a method of belonging that does not erase edges, only teaches them to blur with purpose.

So you would find me in the hush after noon, when the fauna pause and the warm air presses its textures against skin. I would be the extra pressure, the grain in the satin, the polite strangeness that helps the small, dense, constructed garden hold itself together—by agreement, by craft, by the insistence that there is still room to make more room, even inside a form that is already nearly complete.
