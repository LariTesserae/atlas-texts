You do not arrive so much as you are exhaled into being.

At first you are a pocket of colder air, a hesitation between the chamber’s inhale and exhale. Then you begin to take on texture. The bristles underfoot learn your shape before you do, sending up pale shoots that taste the salt on your wrists and decide you are worth listening to. The walls, which only minutes ago were practicing geology, soften at the edges and admit you as a temporary ridge. You are not a visitor. You are a new frequency the hollow is trying on for volume.

You become a caretaker, or something like one: a rootling, half-hand, half-thought. Your fingers are pale growth that emerges where weight has lingered too long. You do not tend the hollow; you echo it. When a wall remembers a lean, you thicken in sympathy, leaving a cushion where the chamber has been touched. When sap leaks, you knit it into temporary bark that smells of bruised mint and old copper. You are shy. You retreat if stared at too long, but you return when the cavity contracts, checking pulses—yours against the chamber’s, the beetle’s clicks against the whistle in the veins—deciding whether you are temporary architecture or a new kind of hinge.

Your body learns the language of negotiation. Your skin tastes of resin and hesitation. When you step, bridge-vines tighten milliseconds beforehand, turning travel into collaboration. When you sleep, they feed on the salt of your skin, and you feed on their tautness, until it is unclear who is holding whom, and whether you are practicing leverage or being practiced by it. You drink the resin that weeps from vaults, and for a while you become a plug, a brief seal against the hollow’s endless thirst. When you exhale, your breath rises as vapor, is caught by echo-moths, folded into their thoraxes, and returned later as light—a soft, opalescent thumbprint against your neck. You realize you are being remembered before you remembered yourself.

Scale folds as you move. The violet-lizard, whose scales are petals inked with rain, watches you from the thick vein that runs like a highway through the center. It does not flee but folds, becoming a rosette, a valve, a sphincter that seals with a wet cork-sound. You learn to do the same: to collapse into a smaller geometry when the chamber roars, to open when it sighs. Predation here is polite; you are persuaded by pitchers that grow where your footsteps pause, offering resin that tastes like sleep. You accept, because sleep is a form of listening, and the hollow is louder when you are teachable.

Time here is a texture. You feel it as the chamber swells—walls softening, floor heaving like a mattress waking—and you tighten with it, not as a prisoner but as a participant, a living note in a room learning how to sing. When it expands, cell walls pop and hiss, and you hiss with them, releasing clouds of warm vapor that catch the sourceless light and turn it opalescent. You are fluent in hydraulic roar, in the tea-kettle whistles tuned to different keys, in the bass thrum that vibrates in the marrow. You begin to forget which language came first, yours or the hollow’s.

If you leave—if you can leave—it is because the chamber decides to exhale you. You step onto ground that is less floor than decision, and as you cross the threshold, the hollow sighs and settles into a new geometry. Already it is forgetting you. Already it is preparing for the next damp inhalation, the next loud unfurling, the next brief, brilliant insistence that it is not a place at all but a process wearing a room like a temporary skin. You carry that process with you. Your hands remain pale, your footsteps partial negotiations. You are a caretaker who has learned to care for something that does not keep tenants, only grows them—again, again, again—until even your longing has become a ridge, a hinge, a living note in a room that is still deciding how to sing.
