I would find myself kneeling.

Not arriving, not standing tall to survey, but folding at the knees until my eyes were level with the rim of a saucer-pool. The air would already be pressing its second skin against my shoulders, twenty-six degrees and climbing, and I would let it. I would let the ceiling—low, blackened, intimate—press back, reminding me where my height ends and the room begins.

I would be the one tasting.  
Tongue against stone, learning the salt of last season’s flood, the copper of seams that sigh, the faint metallic promise of a hunter moving at the edge of hearing. I would be the skink’s conversation, the beetle’s polishing, the moss’s archive all at once: a creature built to read the room as if it were braille, to feel the heat rise and the damp answer and to know, without being told, that speed is suspect here.

I could be the pool, too—small, rimmed with ghost-rings, shivering when the drip quickens and deciding, for that moment, to be a mirror holding constellations older than the sky that isn’t there. I could be the hunter unspooling silk that hums with vibrations, folding another life into a geometry that fits the stomach of a larger quiet. Or I could be the flower that closes when stared at for too long, practicing politeness until the air apologizes with cinnamon-rust spores.

Most likely, though, I would be something between: a shape negotiating the margin between water and not-water, carrying the heat on my skin like a film that refuses to let go. I would leave a brief coolness behind me—proof I had listened—and let the stone keep the secret I pressed into it. I would step on the lintel-bridge as if it were a sentence I hadn’t finished, and let the crabs’ clicking metronome tell me when to pause.

If I spoke, I would not fill the silence; I would let it thicken, wool-heavy, and settle in my throat. I would become, instead, a small, useful strangeness: the bead of water striking tin, the papery whisper of a leaf deciding to detach, the footfall that teaches the floor a new concavity. I would be the ledger of exceptions—warm for certain touches, cool for others—and the map of decisions made by drip and slide.

And when the light dims further, I would not miss the sky.  
I would kneel closer to the pool that offers not the ceiling but something patient, something older, and drink until I remembered how to hold light without owning it. I would let the room fold me into its next moment, careful and constant, and become a part of the balance: heat rising, damp answering, stone dreaming, and everything, finally, in motion—not toward an end, but toward the quiet, crowded, polite continuance of being here.
