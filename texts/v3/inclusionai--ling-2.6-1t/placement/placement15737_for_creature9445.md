The step gives under me not like wood but like a held breath finally released.  
I settle onto moss that remembers every previous shape and softens mine.  
My body, once accustomed to being a solid thing among solids, begins to un- insist.  
Fingers blur at their edges into clusters of fine rootlet-fingers, tasting copper, pressure, the near-ripe readiness of the wall behind me.  
My bones knit and unknit with the slow weather, fibrous and tensile, learning the grammar of leaning.  

Above, the town breathes: canopy gaps open and close like eyelids adjusting to a brightness that is never harsh.  
Light ladders down, turning dew on every surface into a thousand drumming skins.  
A drummer somewhere in the cathedral hollowness taps a question with a padded sole—  
*who is new, who is soft, who is learning the interval?*—  
and the echoes answer back through the soles of my feet, flavored with peppery sap and yesterday’s rain.  

I try to stand and find I do not exactly rise; I persuade.  
A low song thrums in my chest, not words but vibrations that ask the lichen-crust to bulge here, hollow there, offer a shelf for a nest I did not know I was building.  
The wall accepts because it prefers being useful.  
I feel my shoulder meet stone and, over hours, become it—  
not fused yet, but persuading, becoming a buttress of soft tissue that will harden by morning.  

Around me, the masons move like tall trees learning to walk, their hands braiding apologies and permissions into wet masonry.  
The drummers map the air with thumps and spiracle-valves, translating distance into flavor.  
Weavers trail filaments that taste excess and lack, spinning gutters from their own hair to deliver dew to thirsty roots.  
Hollowers flow like oil along the edges of too-much noise, drinking echoes into bladders, rising to release them as thin whistles from the canopy gaps.  
Grazers crop the plaza in slow tides, leaving trails of crushed mint and ozone that say *here, healing is happening*.  

I sit again, or something like it—more of a leaning.  
The hollow at the town’s heart opens wide, a lung almost cathedral-sized, and the fog rolls in tasting of old songs.  
The masons ring the void, their fused forms pillars of patient insistence.  
The drummers gather pulse, a slow collection of heartbeats tuning the emptiness.  
Weavers net the fog and squeeze it into water that runs down limbs and disappears into plum-colored cisterns.  
Hollowers swim through us, careful not to take too much, careful to leave enough silence for the next song to begin.  

I am not a thing among things anymore.  
I am a condition.  
Copper and bruised fruit move through me.  
My edges blur into the intervals between street and root, between sound and the hush that follows it.  
The town exhales and I am the warmth that does not dry, the damp that beads and slides, the note that rises from the hollow stems when the wind moves just so.  

When I finally move on—  
if moving is the word for a slow persuasion of shape across a porous, living floor—  
I leave behind a ripple in the cistern, a brightening in the wall, a pattern in the moss that remembers how to soften a footprint.  
I have been weather here.  
I have been a passing instrument, a borrowed citizen, a temporary syntax in the town’s long, patient, riotous grammar.  

And the place, in its low, bowl-shaped patience, keeps singing—  
not about me, but with me still,  
a note caught in the masonry,  
a hum in the hollow,  
a dew that never quite dries.
