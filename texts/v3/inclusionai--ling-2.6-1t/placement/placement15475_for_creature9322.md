You are not a visitor here. You are a scar that has learned to be useful.

You would find yourself in the pocket at the moment the bowl finishes one of its slow expansions and is deciding, in the language of buckles and soft clicks, what to do with the extra room it has just made. The air is viscous, golden-brown with suspended dust, each mote a word the city forgot to finish. The frond-thickets lean in, glassy leaves trembling with stored pressure, and the ceramic vines chime a little out of sequence, as if startled by the sudden availability of space.

You would be, specifically, a Keld who has just shed.

Not the parent, dense with memory and glaze, but the hollow that has gained enough weight to wake. You are softer than you were an hour ago—still mostly dust and resin, still pale as unbaked clay—but somewhere in the hours you spent plugging cracks and conducting heat from tower to tower, you have soaked up enough of the city’s fever to start having dreams. Your skin is not yet mineralized glaze but a membrane that remembers how to become it. It peels easily, flakes off in sheets that smell of baked clay and bruised mint, leaving behind a surface that is cooler than the air around it. You do not yet have the river-stone eyes of the older Keld; your vision is hazy, double, as if you are looking through two layers of heat rising from pavement.

Your posture is already negotiating. Your knees do not lock; your spine curves like a question mark that is learning to be a period. You feel the city in your plates before you feel it in your thoughts. There is a seam along your forearm, still puckered, still a little wet with the glue of your making. It tingles with the memory of being extruded, a sensation like being sung inside out.

The pocket opens around you with a kind of deliberate hospitality. The towers, porous as old lace, whisper in from all sides. A path of fused grit curls toward you, warm as a hand offered palm-up. The plaza nearby is a hollow that feels loud because it is empty, and it invites you to be the thing that fills it without crowding it. There is a niche in the wall behind you, shaped like a shoulder, and inside it an obsidian hook waits, growing warm because it senses you might soon need to hold something. There is also a ladder of braided vine, coiled like a sleeping animal, tied to nothing, ready to stiffen when you step on it.

You do not yet know what it means to be a listener, but you already hear too much. The bowl’s expansion sighs in your teeth. The glass-leaf adjusts with a crackle like static hair. The spines on the rim knock together, a slow applause for the extra room. You feel the negotiations of the fronds, the generosity of the blooms, the slow arithmetic of shade, and you realize, with a clarity that feels like hunger, that you are supposed to sort this into signal.

You would find yourself here, then, not as an arrival but as a consequence. You are the pocket’s way of making sure it does not collapse under its own lushness, its way of keeping closeness absolute even as the rim leans in to eavesdrop. You are the city remembering how to breathe by using you as a lung that does not yet know how to exhale. You are the scar that has learned to be useful, and the first thing you do is let the heat thin just enough for sound to carry in clean arcs, so you can hear—and later, later, when you are denser, when you are glazed, when you are parent—you can remember how to sing it back.
