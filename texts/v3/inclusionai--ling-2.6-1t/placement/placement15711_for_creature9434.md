I would find myself kneeling at the lip of a Listener’s largest hollow, where the water has begun to skin over but has not yet made up its mind to fall. My hands would be the basin’s hands—palms dark with the stain of soft iron, fingertips polished to a satin blur from turning stones that do not resist but do not yield either. The skin of my arms would be mapped in faint, moss-black seams where the morning damp had learned to linger past noon.

I would be the basin’s apology for having edges. My knees would know the exact temperature of stone that has surrendered, and my breath would carry the high, thin note that precedes rain, as if my chest had been hollowed into a cup for it. I would not be a person in the usual sense but a permission: the shape the basin takes when it decides to be walked upon without being used.

My eyes would be the basin’s eyes—not set in my head but scattered, the way the light pools. They would open when water gathers and close when it thins, each lid a slow leaf. When I blinked, the basin would shift its weight, a terrace giving a fraction of an inch, as though it were breathing inside my ribs.

My voice would be a Ground-Singer’s hum behind the knees of anyone who crossed me, felt before it was heard. If I spoke aloud, the sound would arrive as moisture on the tongue, tasting at first of iron and then of honey and then of a name that had been worn smooth. With that name, the Mirror-Waters would hesitate, their false skies blurring, as though they were trying to decide whether to reflect me or to replace me.

I would carry the tools the basin has forgotten how to need: a smoothed scoop that holds exactly as much as a single hollow can release, a lattice of stems that has accepted its new life as a home for punctuation. When I set them down, they would grow shy, acquiring their own soft films of green, learning to be background. I would learn to be background, too—present, but only as the presence that makes presence possible.

At dusk, I would settle into a Soft-Ending, my edges diffusing into the line where damp becomes almost-damp. My pulse would slow to the turning of a page, and my memory would spiral, not linear but recursive, remembering the last time the basin remembered itself, and the time before that, each pass softer, each return a gentler kind of happening. I would know what it is to be worn into comfort, to be the scar that looks cozy, to be the silence that is not empty because it has been taught to listen.

And when the rain returns, I would rise, water beading on my skin like a second skin, and the basin would open again, patient and wet, as if I had only ever been waiting for this one more chance to be a place that knows its own name without saying it aloud, with me as its tongue, its ear, its small, wet, warm, and ancient mind.
