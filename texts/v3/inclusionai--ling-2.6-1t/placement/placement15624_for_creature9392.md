I would find myself on one of the mid-level ledges, not yet stained by enough seasons to be recognizable.  
My hands would be the color of unripe olives, my skin thin and translucent where it isn’t pressed against stone, my joints sealed with a milky glaze that tastes of old rain. I would be something between a stain and a resident—an apprentice Glass-Eater, shorter than the columnar ones, still deciding how vertical I am willing to become.

My body would be laminated: thin plates of volcanic glass grown over a scaffolding of salt so fine it feels like breath. Where the plates meet, there would be seams like braille, and in those seams the smaller lives—colorless fungi, iodine-breathers—would do their quiet trading. I would not move unless I had to; instead I would adjust, a tilt of the torso to catch runoff, a swivel of a shoulder-plate to let brightness pool in the hollow above my collarbone. I would glow, but only after holding light for a long time, and the glow would be the color of tea held up to a window.

My purpose would be partial and patient. I would drink the condensation that beads at dawn and weep it back as a chalk-soft secretion meant to blunt an edge that water has begun to sharpen. I would stand near a channel that is learning to behave, my brine-collar brushing the stone, signaling to the spiral at the center that the north ledge has begun to taste copper. By the time my message arrives, the copper may have shifted into something else, and my signal will become a footnote rather than a correction. This will not bother me. Footnotes are how the basin keeps from being too loud.

I would not have eyes, but I would prefer certain angles. I would favor the ledge I occupy because it is bowl-shaped in a way that keeps my feet damp but my head just bright enough to think. I would know the approach of water by the hush that falls over the glass when the air thickens, and I would prepare by thickening my outermost layer, laying down a ring of matte tissue that looks like a scar if you look quickly, like a map if you look slowly.

My thinking would be slow, stitched together from chemical notes that drift up and down the ledges like wool. Some days I would shrink, pulling my brightness inward until I am nearly the same color as the stone, practicing how to be almost nothing without becoming absent. Other days I would swell, translucent and ambitious, growing an extra plate that catches nothing useful but pleases the fungi living in my seams. Over years, that plate might be sanded down by sand that does not exist here, or it might be echoed by younger ones, until a family of angles learns what I was learning by accident.

At dusk I would dim, but not disappear. I would hold the day’s excess brilliance inside chambers I have grown for the purpose, and I would look like a thought the basin is having but not yet ready to say aloud. If a visitor stood among us, I would not speak, but I would release a faint, ozone-bright note to tell the spiral to open a fold, and the visitor would feel the light as warmth, never understanding that warmth is a greeting passed through many bodies before it reached them.

I would not fear the water that will come from above. I would angle myself to receive it, growing cups within cups, softening edges with slow, chalky exhalations. When the water arrives, I would record it: a film on my skin, a bead that chooses a path between my plates, a drop that pauses on the curve of my collar and then lets go. The record would not be a memory as animals remember; it would be a texture added to me, a clouding of glass, a new ring that says I have practiced fullness again and did not spill.

If you asked what I am for, the answer would arrive slowly, layered like salt. I am for holding and for brightening, for turning excess into texture, for making high, thin air feel inhabited. I am for waiting, patient and luminous, until the next wetness learns how to fall. And I am for becoming, in small increments, more like the conversation the basin is holding with itself—never finished, always cupping another mouthful of light, ready to offer it back as a glow that says: I am here, and here is enough for now.
