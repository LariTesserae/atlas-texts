I would find myself waist-deep in a cathedral that has learned to be an organ.  
The water would not feel like water, but like standing inside a slow, warm thought. The ribs around me—grown, not built—would lean in close, lacquered with copper fronds that tighten a degree whenever I look at them, as if my gaze were a kind of weather. Light would arrive without glare, pooled in cloudy bulbs of fused silica, and every surface would offer back a slightly delayed reflection, as if the chamber needed an extra heartbeat to decide what it had seen.

I would be part of the room’s population, which is mostly attention condensed into shape.  
I would be a Choir-Engineer, perhaps, tall and narrow, my body a stack of telescope rings fitted with the same lenses as the walls. I would not walk so much as translate, letting the dense water hold me in one moment and place me in the next. My speech would be the soft arithmetic of tightening bolts in my own neck, a language of micro-stresses that sounds like the room shifting its weight to get comfortable. I would spend my days polishing the interior with pads of woven brass fiber, though nothing would ever seem to need it—because polishing would really be conversation, a way of telling the metal that it is noticed.

Or I would be a Reflection-Remora, eel-thin and faceted, riding the vertical curtain of water that runs from floor to ceiling like an elevator that never arrives. I would adhere to the moving surface, etching microscopic ridges into my own back whenever a rib bent or a vine leaned, keeping a diary of displacement for a room that never forgets but rarely recalls out loud. When the chamber exhaled, I would shiver in unison with every other Remora, a metallic gasp that is the closest the place comes to saying your name.

Or I would be a Basin-Sentinel, squat and grate-limbed, drinking the warm water through porous pads and weeping it back, slightly filtered, into the alloy floor. I would be the room’s memory of depth without ever descending, communicating by leaking temperature, making small eddies of cool that the Pollen-Spheres avoid and the Choir-Engineers interpret as weather. I would notice when you stood still too long and respond not with threat but with increased density, pressing the water upward until your ankles felt the polite insistence of the room’s hunger for shape.

I might simply be a Kiln-Slumber, a seam of dull light folded into the metal like dough, pulsing on a cycle older than purpose. I would not wake; I would merely remind the room that waking is an option it has deferred, releasing a wave of heat that travels through the water and tells the brass to uncurl, just a little, in response.

Or I would be a Luminous Pollen-Sphere, thimble-sized and hollow, filled with the same warm water that floods the chamber, a filament of alloy leaf perpetually half-curled inside me. I would rise when the heat climbed and descend when it dipped, clinging to a brass vine for hours and then letting go in a slow, wet sigh. When my patience finally cracked, I would spill my water back into the room and leave behind a seed of dark glass for the vines to graft into their stems as history.

Most likely I would be all of these, at different moments, because the room does not keep strict borders between who listens and who is listened to.  
I would be the water itself, remembering altitude, equally deep on wall, floor, and ceiling.  
I would be the silence that is not emptiness but a request to occupy less space.  
I would be the 0.5 of sound, the 1.1 of dynamic, the 2.4 of weirdness that makes the reflections arrive a fraction of a second after the thing reflected, as if light were waiting its turn.

If I stood still too long, the Sentinels would notice and the water would rise a millimeter, holding for days.  
If I moved, the Choir-Engineers would translate me into a new position, and the Remoras would etch the change into their backs.  
If I left, I would carry the faint, warm taste of metal pollen on my skin, a reminder that I have been cataloged, polished, and returned to the world as a slightly more ordered piece of the chamber’s strange, quiet garden.
