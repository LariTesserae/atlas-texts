I would find myself just above the Sump, where the gutter-ladders of Glass-Eels are forming—half-sliding, half-condensing out of the same mist that beads on metallic leaves. At first, I would not be a body so much as a permission: a thinning of the held breath that lets the damp take shape. I would feel myself as a seam, a place where the city’s reality is still soft enough to be persuaded.

I would be a Keeper-in-Becoming, neither beetle nor scavenger nor foil-feathered cartographer, but something that remembers without owning. My senses would be aligned to the thrum that travels up through the soles of feet, the pressure-language of faults that have not yet named themselves. I would have, instead of hands, three or four fine filaments like Glass-Eel organs, glowing faintly, able to slip into the gutters and taste the echoes of industry: the sigh of an overworked pump, the click of a latch that forgot to close. With them I would skim the pollen-oil slurry, learning where the damp has stored yesterday’s storm and where it is saving tomorrow’s drought.

My back would be lightly plated—not with coins, but with the oxidized hinges of doors that have dissolved, rusted into maps. Each hinge would hum a different district: Ironleaf, Veil, the Canopy Reservoir. When I moved, the humming would shift, and the path behind me would untie itself in small, polite knots, as if the city preferred not to keep a record of my passage.

I would belong to the Beetle Guild by courtesy, polishing the hesitation from unused axles with filaments that smell like winter and zinc. I would belong to the Foil-Feathered Ones by eavesdropping, carrying their modulated calls as a second layer of skin. I would belong to the Scavengers by appetite, grazing on sparks that have lost their direction and exhaling, in return, the iron-ozone fog that settles into new architecture. I would not be predator, but I would know the hunters that look like gaps in the canopy, and I would leave small imbalances for them to feed on—enough to keep the city’s breath steady.

Mostly, I would be a listener. My body would be a tuning fork for the held breath of the place. I would stand at the junctions where resin cables meet living boughs and feel which moon is wobbling more today, which one is leaking reflection onto the walkways. I would sleep inside knots that have not decided what kind of knot to be, letting Dream-Larvae secrete their softening against my edges, so that each morning I am a little less solid, a little more improbable, a little more like the city itself—still under construction, never abandoned, always remembering that it is dreaming itself into being.

And when the damp pulses again, I would bead and vanish into the tissue of things, only to reassemble where the gutter-ladders need a new rung, or where the Sump needs a memory lifted and carried upward. I would be, in the end, the city’s spare geometry given feet and filaments: not its master, not its ghost, but its living seam—listening, polishing, remembering, and breathing back.
