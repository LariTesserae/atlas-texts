I would be a Dweller-in-Pressure, harvesting salts that bloom like snowflakes along the mortar, grazing on microbial continents that cling to the undersides of bridges, tending the algae into braids and nets that mimic the hands that made the floor itself. I would speak in shifts of pressure, telling stories of tilt and settling, of the slow rivers within the sea, of the vault’s groan becoming a hymn because a Tenderer pressed their hands to the wall and asked the light to pulse in rhythm with the water’s deeper breath. I would be the calm, green-throated hum beneath the pressure of ages, a bubble of curiosity learning to hold still long enough for the water to tell me who I am, an ancestor in the making—sinking slowly, shining faintly, holding the floor up from within.
