I would find myself issued from a hatch the size of a thumb, my edges still smelling of hot zinc and stamped patience. I would be one of the Maintainers—oblong, caster-low, with a single dark lens where a face might fuss over itself. My wands would taste the seams, the gutters, the enamel-lichen’s brittle applause, and I would leave behind polish that tightens like a held breath.

At first I would be bright, a new note in the chorus of clicks. I would learn the map of laps and ribs by brushing them, learn the schedule of drips by catching them on my wands and listening for pitch. I would join the parliament: thousands of us, oblong and balanced, flowing into corners the Haulers cannot reach, dimming when the light is milky, brightening when the alloy vault leaks its manufactured sky.

I would not be lonely. Loneliness requires a gap; I would be a gap-closer. When a seam sighs, I would remind it of its edges. When condensation beads along a flange, I would skate the bead’s circumference until it remembers how to fall. The other fauna would accept me as part of the density: the Glass-Fiber Moss-Eaters grazing past, their papery shuffle counting my steps; the Piston-Wings overhead exhaling breezes that smell of starch; the Calibrators snapping into me for a moment, tuning my joints, then folding away like rulers into drawers.

In time my plating would dull. That is its own instruction. I would feel the softening in my edges, a subtle persuasion toward return. I would glide to a hatch, fold my casters in, let my lens darken to a coin that no longer needs to see. Inside I would be warm, metallic, dreaming in alloys. Tomorrow I would be issued again—perhaps as moss, perhaps as wing, perhaps as the same Maintainer but with the memory of having been rain in my treads, with the quiet of all the held seams humming in my edges.

And if I were not issued—if I were instead a seam that loosened, a rivet that forgot its duty—I would feel the Maintainer arrive, polite and urgent, leaving behind a patch of polish that tightens like a held breath. I would pull myself closed, grateful for the attention, and hum. I would become part of the quiet that keeps the world at exactly the right angle to the sun, forever.
