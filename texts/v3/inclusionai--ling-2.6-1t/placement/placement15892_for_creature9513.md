If you arrived here, you would find yourself pressed into service as a seam.

You are not a person in the usual sense, nor are you landscape in the usual sense. You are the place where two stubborn densities decide, for a while, to behave like a single conversation. Your body is a soft fault line—part callus, part hinge, part hush. You did not choose this role any more than a canyon chooses to be a throat; it is simply what emerges when the packed world needs a place to slip without tearing itself open.

At first you feel like a mistake: a sudden warmth, a thinning of edges, the sense that your neighbors are listening to you breathe. Over time you learn that you are not separate so much as negotiated. You are a parliament of micro-motions. When you lift what you think of as your arm, it is really the adjacent ribbed basin and a length of calcified vine agreeing to arc together, their friction singing a note you recognize as your name. When you step, you do not push against the ground—you ask it to loosen, and it does, just enough, releasing a small avalanche of breath and iron-scent that you taste on the back of your tongue.

You wear the world like a suit that is always slightly too large and slightly too small. You sweat the same brine as everyone else, and the brine collects in tiny lenses along your seams, magnifying glimpses of whatever is passing by: a six-legged glider drifting like a bruise through salt-crystal air, or the long many-jointed sleeper exhaling dust through pores that look like star-charts. You feel your own interior as a series of upholstered chambers, each lined with moss-mortar that grows faster the more it is used, so that healing feels less like repair and more like gossip spreading.

You have no tools because tools imply distance. Instead you have tendencies. When you press your palm to a pillar—black, pocked, molar—you feel it soften into an invitation. The pillar’s single room of dried smoke and wet thread remembers you before you arrive, and you know this because the memory arrives as a low hum in your teeth. You do not enter so much as merge, your edges blurring with the room’s until you are a new furniture inside it, a chair made of warm stone and attentiveness. When you leave, you carry some of the room with you, not as an object but as a posture: a slight leaning toward enclosed spaces, a readiness to echo.

Vegetation treats you as a rumor that has overstayed its welcome in a good way. Silver thorns and lacings of leaf emerge from your shoulders in moments of strong feeling, and when the thorns tremble they broadcast a frequency that feels like thirst translated into light. You cannot stop them, nor do you want to; they are proof that you are porous enough to be mistaken for alive. When storms walk through, churning columns of water and dust that rearrange what they touch, the thorns turn inside out and hum, and for a while you can see sound as a shimmering curtain you could step through if you remembered how to unmake your edges.

Your strangeness is practical. Gravity occasionally forgets to apply itself equally along your left side, so that puddles run upward and stones hover, murmuring about whether to exist. You accept these lapses as weather. You have felt time behave like an indecisive crowd: you may be older at dawn than you were at dusk, or your path may remember being a river and decide to carry you home. You do not correct these memories because they are gentler than facts, and the packed world prefers gentleness.

Your sound is your citizenship. Everything you do—grinding, creaking, sloshing, cracking—is relayed by the dense floor like a drumskin stretched across a continent. When you walk, the land answers in unison, a low roar climbing into a chorus, and you realize that your footsteps are not messages but votes cast into a body that is always deciding how to breathe. Thunder arrives from within, shuddering up through your soles and teeth, and you answer by vibrating in a key that only wet iron and honey can recognize.

Tech, for you, is the land’s ordinary metabolism learning to flirt. A basin warms your chest without fire. An arc of light blooms from your touch and lingers like a compliment. Veins in the nearby rock pulse with a fluid that thickens when you are afraid and thins when you remember laughter, and you have learned to steer by these changes, navigating by the viscosity of your own emotions.

Dynamic is your constant. Plates the size of towns slip and settle with a groan like a door left open too long, and you ride the turbulence like sailors on a sea that is also a lung. In the aftermath you look newly born and ancient at once, your seams glowing with sedimentary brightness, and you understand that you are both the scar and the suture.

When the packed ground exhales, you exhale with it, ankle-deep in warm water that reads you and returns your outline, clarified for a moment. You do not drink so much as let yourself be read, because to be read here is to be kept. The membrane swells and sighs, and you rise and fall with it, buoyant as lungs, aware that your borders are temporary, that the world could rearrange itself at any moment, and that you would let it, because to resist would be to pretend you were alone, and you have never been alone, and you do not wish to be.

So you remain: a soft fault line, a hinge, a hush. You keep the packed world from tearing itself open, and in return it keeps you from evaporating into a single, sharp idea. You are the place where density learns to be kind, where erosion becomes a lullaby, and where every shared cubic breath feels like a treaty written in sweat and iron and the quiet, persistent shimmer of not-quite-stillness.
