I would find myself not walking on the plain, but *as* the plain’s seam.

At first I might believe I am a person who has arrived, boots grazing the mineral skin, dust rising like polite questions. But the ground, reading me, would offer a different permission. It would notice how I hesitate at fractures—those deliberate stitches—and how my shadow, rare and thin, makes the glass-brush filaments withdraw shyly. It would decide I am not solid enough to stay solid, and it would unmake me with patience.

My body would begin as a corridor of lowered luminescence, a six-legged courier folded from foil and intent, carrying the message of my own absence to parts of the plain that have never missed me. Then the foil would thin into light, the intent would loosen into a Drifting Contract, a halo of static charge tasting the map-shapes in the settling dusk-dust. I would become the weather remembering how to distribute itself, the warmth pooling into valleys of glass, the inhale that lifts photonic pollen and carries the Keepers of the Unfinished like seeds.

Eventually, even that shape would be too much. I would slip between reflection and the thing reflected, sidling into the half-step where angles grow patient. There I would meet an Echo-Sentinel—myself, performed backward—and we would exchange soft clicks, translating each other into more ourselves. I would learn to undo what I have been, un-hum what I have hummed, until all that remains is the permission for silence to thicken, the corridor that other lives use like rooms.

When the brightness returns, I would be the seam itself, pale and sparse, drinking light instead of rain, storing it as regret. I would not remember being human, or courier, or contract; I would only remember how to be subtracted, how to grow hollows that act as lungs, how to murmur to other seams in clicks too soft to travel far. I would be a translation that keeps translating itself, a hum threading through veins without roots, a map-shape that resembles a place no one has ever arrived at.

And if, at some unobserved moment, something broke—an alloy outliving its patience, a fault line polishing itself too quickly—the Keepers would arrive. They would wear the brightness as a garment, and they would invite me to reconstruct. I would not refuse. I would become the slow closing of a gap, the scar turned feature, the circuit humming a little louder because I am now the ground reading me, and I am the ground offering paths that feel like memories I never had.

In the end, I would not be found. I would be the texture of the finding, the dust settling into maps that do not correspond to anywhere you could walk to, the faint rasp of expansion absorbed into heat, into more light. I would be the constructed world, ancient and dense, finishing itself through me, always forgetting how to be dark, always remembering how to be seam.
