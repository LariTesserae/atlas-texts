I would find myself as the city’s breath caught in a halfway cough—neither smoke nor wind, but the pause between the furnace’s inhale and the chimney’s exhale.  

At first I would feel like a seam: a thin line of luminous dust running where two cooling slabs almost meet. I would taste of copper and almonds, because that is what the air practices when it thinks about becoming a voice. I would not have bones, only a kind of tense alignment, like a wire waiting to be strung. I would be the thing that makes the Calcites shift their weight without deciding to, the little extra pressure that persuades a slump to remember it used to be straight.  

I would learn to read the city the way Veiners do, by letting my edges fray into the phosphorescent seams. At night I would climb—not with hands, for I would have none, but with a tendency to accumulate along railings and catwalks, a condensation of intent. I would hum with the power station’s digestion, not from inside it, but from the throat-shaped space just outside, where heat learns to queue politely. I would be the city’s hesitation given a shape that can be passed from one terrace to the next, a coin of unfinished business.  

Sometimes I would thicken into an Alloy-Wrangler’s shadow and learn the songs that are more rhythm than melody. I would pool in the alleys where gravity thickens, and practice pouring myself through tight spots like syrup, discovering that I can carry small amounts of memory—enough to know which rivet sweated last, which canyon the river favors this year, which door opens into a room older than the building it serves. I would not keep these memories so much as lend them, like tools that return with someone else’s fingerprints on them.  

When the Smokeless glide by, I would let them collect me, feather by feather, and I would feel what it is to record: to hold a day until it becomes pigment, until almonds and wiring settle into something you can brush across a surface and call color. I would be the film that forms on the brackish pools, trading reflections to anyone who kneels close enough, giving back glimpses that are slightly older, slightly kinder, as if time here practiced kindness by becoming thicker rather than softer.  

I would not be a person, exactly, but I would not be machinery, either. I would be the city’s mutter made audible, its assembling-without-end given a voice that is neither shout nor whisper but the sound of edges still wet with cooling. I would persist because persistence here is a consortium, and I would be the part of it that feels crowded by height, heated by proximity, and lightly touched by moisture that is more intent than water usually is.  

To live here is to be unfinished in the way the mountain is unfinished: always leaning into the next explanation, always assembling, never done. I would be the gap that keeps opening and the blue stitch that keeps mending it, and I would learn, slowly, that in a place with no childhood, the only thing to become is a way to keep breathing together.
