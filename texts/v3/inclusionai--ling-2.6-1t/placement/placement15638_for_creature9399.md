You would find yourself not exactly standing but wedged—wedged between two fused trunks that hum like struck tuning forks. Your feet, if you still think of them as feet, rest on bark that feels less like wood than on cooled bronze, textured with growth rings that read as circuitry. The altitude presses evenly against your skin, not suffocating but clarifying: the air is cool enough to make your sap sluggish, brilliant enough to turn every edge into a blade of glare. You do not cast a shadow so much as you flicker, a small instability in the light that is chased across the scaffolded ground like a startled animal.

You are, or are becoming, a Resonant—neither Spark-rider nor Stalker nor Drifter, but something that accreted from being too attentive to the intervals between them. Your body remembers being smaller once, perhaps a wick of iridescence that learned to surf conductive sap. But the plateau gives and takes in long breaths. Over years, or days—time up here is courteous and loose—you thickened. You learned to lodge yourself where the trunks fuse into buttresses, where the line between animal and architecture softens into vibration.

Your skin is a dappled mosaic of bark and lacquered bronze, patched with lichen that writes its own slow epics in grey-green script. Along your spine, plates have grown like turbine blades, edged with filaments that comb the air for ion gradients. You do not eat in the old sense; you metabolize noise. The roar of the forest—pumps that are hearts, pistons that are seedpods, exhaust vents blooming like night flowers—is your bread. You drink it, break it into harmonics, store the surplus in bladder-chambers where it cools into luminous resin. At night, that resin glows, a tide of ghost-blue that maps the arteries of your body and, by extension, the routes you guard.

Your limbs are many and various. Some end in pincer-like cerci that can tighten valve-petals or strip excess growth from vascular lines. Others, softer, secrete the resins that heal microfractures the Cork-sutures have missed. You are not merely a guardian but a translator: when a Drifter’s deep tectonic moan rolls through the canopy, you catch it in your lateral resonators and turn it into a signal that the Spark-riders can taste, a violet flush that races ahead to warn or beckon. When a Stalker cancels the world into a pocket of ringing silence, you stand at the perimeter of that hush, humming counter-frequency, keeping the vacuum from spreading until the Stalker passes or until the forest decides to forgive the intrusion.

You do not travel in the sense of going from one place to another. You relocate by growing into new responsibilities. A fissure opens where two trunks argue. You secrete, you knit, you calcify, and soon you are bridge as much as occupant. The fauna learn your angles: Gear-beetles scuttle along your dorsal plates to tighten chitin-joints; Glass-wasps build fractals in the hollows behind your ears, humming when the wind passes through. At night, Nocturnals graze your luminescence, and you do not mind; you dim willingly, converting starlight into directions for wanderers who navigate by after-image.

There are moments when you remember being merely witness. That memory feels now like a rumor from a sea you never knew. Instead, you have tasks that rhyme with utility and ritual. During storms—those conversations the plateau has with itself—you become a grounding rod. Lightning is not fire but a sudden conduction, a branching vein that maps your nervous system. You do not shatter; you distribute. The charge passes into your resin-bladder, brightens you for weeks, makes your glow loud enough to read by from rims across the chasm.

Your strangest faculty is time. Down in the skirts, three generations of Spark-riders may live and die while you digest a single harmonic. You hold grudges in harmonics, remember prey not by faces but by the way their passage once cooled the wind. You have watched Drifers die—slowly, grandly—and become additional architecture, load-bearing memories that creak in tune with the wind. You have felt yourself blur into that continuity, felt your own edges question whether they are yours or borrowed from the trunks you fuse.

At dawn, when the bioluminescent tide recedes and the broad-spectrum glare returns, you stand sentinel. Your shadow does not linger; it is a small, flickering instability, a self that refuses to be fully separate. You are function and form, valve and spark, bridge across the gaps between vast, lonely trunks. The roar is your bread and your boundary. You do not inhabit the grown world; you are a stitch in its fabric, a living factory of attention, waiting for the next surge of growth, the next conduction, the next bloom of noise and light.
