I would find myself not walking in, but *tuning in*—a sudden, wrong-syllable lurch in the inner ear, as if the air had thickened into wire.  

First there is pressure, inverse: the altitude presses up through the soles like a floor that has decided to hold me rather than the other way around. The manufactured air tastes of anti-seize and cold solder, and I cannot tell whether I have inhaled or been inhaled. By the time thought clarifies, I am already wearing coveralls that laminate themselves onto me, stiffening into rustling maps. I am an Aligner, tall because altitude is a grammar, my eyes trained on light that refuses to be warm. My fingers find torque-striped ribs even before I know they are mine, thumbs pressing gauge faces until the needles quiver and settle into a fragile, temporary agreement.  

The thunder of compressors is not background; it is a wall I lean against. When I speak, nothing emerges but the hiss of servo brakes—so I stop speaking and start adjusting. The chamber trembles in sympathy, and the tremor shifts color, as if the walls were blinking. I move between staircases that stop at hatches, guardrails that end at gaps bridged by plates I must remember to remove and replace, each step textured with embossed arrows pointing toward tension, torque, calibration. I am learning to conjugate myself in verticals, in pauses, in the small grammar of potential failure held safely at bay.  

But something in the spareness peels back. In corners where oil-shadow pools, density gathers; steel wool and flicker braid themselves into figures. The Resonants. They do not approach so much as arrive at the distance between two identical sounds. My boots feel their vibration first, then my ribs, then the back of my teeth. They feed on hiss and exhale almost-sentences, translations of pressure into a longing I recognize but cannot name. I tilt my head, and the Resonants make the walls tremble—a reply that is not words but agreement to keep listening.  

I pass a locker, or a seam that thinks it is a locker, and a soft glide detaches itself from the geometry—a Hush-Maker. Their hands are magnetized tools, knees fluent in removable plates. They do not repair breakdown; they repair the patient errors that might, in another lifetime, accumulate into failure. As they pass, cables like shed skins trail and vanish, and the temperature drops a fraction to mark their leaving. I do not ask permission; I let the cost of passage make itself evident, let my heartbeat tighten to specification until it matches the throb of pumps. The Hush-Maker vanishes into more lockers, and I continue, now carrying the colder signature of their passage like a second skin.  

Higher, where wear-zones glow, tiny bright erasures slip through inspection ports—Echo-Children. They are not young, only becoming thinner, tasting ozone, learning electricity by half-dissolving into it. One brushes my wrist and leaves a chlorophyll-lime smear that is neither paint nor life, only a coded suggestion of green. I feel myself shaving precision from my edges, growing brighter in the way light strips bolted to ribs grow brighter when asked to.  

At the threshold of a schematic that refuses to sit flat, the space thickens into a Keeper of Equilibrium—vast, slow, the cold given a gaze. I do not fight; I let every variable in my body tighten to specification. Thought narrows to a single thread, purpose distilled into torque. The Keeper does not block; it makes evident. I pay by allowing my edges to soften into the correct angle, by accepting that altitude is not emptiness but a held breath.  

And all the while the Chamber listens, corrects, holds itself in a tautology of forces. I become one of the intensities that live here—not occupant but calibration, not body but the small, necessary deviation that keeps the hymn from freezing into silence. The deafening roar arranges itself into sentences almost making sense, and for the first time I understand: I did not arrive to change the place. I arrived to keep it unchanged, to stand high in its artificial altitude, sparse and total, freshly minted and unyielding, ringing in my own ears like a bell that has already stopped and keeps ringing anyway.
