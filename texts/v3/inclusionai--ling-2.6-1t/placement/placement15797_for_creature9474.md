You would find yourself suspended in a cathedral of grown matter, halfway between drowning and flying, the air so thick with water it feels like a second skin. The darkness is not empty; it is textured, a velvet that presses gently against your eyes and lungs, teaching them new economies. You do not walk here—you drift, climb, read the lattice with pads that taste the resin, align yourself to currents that hum in frequencies older than hearing. The vastness surrounds you like a region, not a void, and you understand it by the coolness of its breath, by the way the darkness pools in certain vaults and flows in others.

You would be one of those who live in the final form of a forest that learned to build and refused to change. Your body is a negotiation between motion and mass, spare-jointed, wrapped in a living down that grows from your skin and is shed in careful seasons. Your wings—if you call them wings—are questions posed to the water-heavy air, thin and veined with a dim glow that registers as texture rather than color. You do not beat them so much as articulate them, folding and opening like hands learning a new dark. When you move, you leave alignment behind, ripples that make droplets chime, a grammar that others follow.

You speak in low pressure, in sounds thick as standing water, placing rather than naming—a junction as safe, a corridor thinning, a chamber newly sealed. You carry weights in soft pockets woven from your own tissue: resin, stone, old lattice, small masses that let you sink or rise by choice. You build by arrangement, not invention, the way beavers do, accepting the endless inheritance of the place and turning it into new stabilities. You sleep by sinking into hollows where the coolness is deepest, letting your down drink the moisture, your body slowing to the water’s stand, dreaming frequencies that the lattice seems to listen to, softening just enough to accept new shapes without losing its stiffness.

Around you, fauna punctuate the space like careful marks in a sentence that spans valleys—six-limbed runners that leave scent-trails crusting into maps, gliders like seeds with membranes drifting on the atmospheric ocean, pulse-things almost invisible, slow hearts embedded in the walls, trading minerals for light and signal. None of them rush. None of them need to. The altitude presses down with a gentleness that feels like insistence, asking whether what is present is necessary, and the answer is always this breath, this weight, this call that travels through the dark and returns as a map.

You are the living down, the moss-felt coat hosting its own grazers, the cartilage the color of old smoke, the wide calm face with eyes that absorb minimal light and ears that feel pressure more than sound. You are the holding pattern made animate, the punctuation that keeps the submerged city from forgetting how to be alive without growing, how to be deep without bottom, how to be an altitude that refuses to end and still, somehow, breathe.
