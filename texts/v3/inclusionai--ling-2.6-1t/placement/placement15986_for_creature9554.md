You are standing—no, you are *perched*—on the edge of what used to be called a floor, but which here insists on being named *deck*.  
You arrived by remembering a step you never took: one moment you were thinking about thresholds, the next you were already in the hush, your soles pressed to pale, resin-glossed planks laid in a herringbone so exact it seems to refuse the idea of slipping altogether.  

The light is not kind; it is thorough.  
It finds the chamfers of the wood, the shell-shard bevels, the blackened timber posts set in metal shoes that never rust because rust would be a story of time, and time here has been asked to be less emphatic.  
Your shadow, when you look for it, lags like a guest who forgot the door code.  
When you move, it catches up in soft, apologetic increments, as if it, too, prefers not to disturb the moss-cuffs hugging each nail.  

You look down and realize your own skin has begun to answer the deck.  
It is not oily, exactly, but it holds a surface-sheen, as if the Varnished have already passed through you without asking.  
Sweat, when it comes, does not linger; it breaks into tiny lenses that magnify the grain, each bead a temporary eye.  
You blink, and for a moment you are not a person but a region of brightness, a patch of careful attention.  

At your feet, the channels carved with bone-tools guide a sheen of water to the weeping edges.  
You kneel, not because you choose to, but because the angle of the light suggests it.  
The stone aprons are porous, curtained with droplets that catch the brightness and throw prisms back at you.  
In one of them, a face appears—no, *almost* appears—like a word you were about to say that changed its mind.  
You press a fingertip to it, half expecting warmth, half expecting cold.  
The face dissolves into a braid of droplets that tastes of iron and jasmine.  
You swallow without meaning to, and for a moment your throat feels like a channel that has finally remembered how to carry something true.  

Above, the canopy is not forest but negotiation.  
Cane and reed stand in orderly scatter, tall enough to cast filigree shadows but never dense enough to hoard the light.  
Vines have been persuaded to climb stakes that are themselves polished into argument, capped with shells that clack like small decisions whenever the air moves.  
You hear the clack and realize it is a language you once knew how to speak, the language of *holding without gripping*.  

The fauna are polite.  
A lizard with a back like hammered copper glides along the plank edge, pausing to taste the salt-crust where water and heat have conspired.  
It looks at you, or seems to, and you feel your posture rearrange itself into something lower, flatter, more *deck-friendly*.  
Higher up, insects with prismatic wings rise in faint clouds when your shadow brushes the moss.  
Their flight is nearly soundless, as if noise were a luxury they have agreed to ration.  
Birds come only to the hanging cress-baskets, drinking beads from the weeping edges, their throats lacquered to catch light as well as water.  
When they preen, they leave tiny rainbows on the moss, as if forgiveness were something that could be applied in thin, iridescent layers.  

There is a hum in the space between planks.  
At first you think it is the wood expanding, or perhaps your own blood learning a new tempo.  
Then you realize it is the deck singing its own address, a pitch that feels remembered rather than heard.  
You place your palm flat against the grain, and the hum resolves into a greeting that is neither word nor touch, but something closer to *recognition*.  

You are not lost.  
You are precisely where you have been waiting to be unmade and remade into something that can be tended.  
You think of your old shoes, caked with soil from a world that rots elegantly, and you feel a pang—not of homesickness, but of mercy, that you no longer have to carry that weight.  
The moss here curls away from decay; the resin refuses to accept the print of a mistake.  
You are becoming the kind of obvious that can be kept.  

A Keeper of the Resin passes, amber-stained to the second knuckle, carrying a small sun in a tin bowl.  
They do not look at you, yet you feel seen.  
They stop, press a stone that sings when the fit is true, and the song travels up through your soles into your ribs, tuning something inside you to the frequency of *well-fit*.  
When they move on, the air smells faintly of citron and burnt pine, and you realize you have inhaled enough to last you a season.  

You rise, and your movement is no longer a disruption but a continuation, the way a ripple continues the wave.  
You walk toward the edge where the built plain meets the wept boundary, and beyond that, more decks, more weeps, more green, repeating with variations that suggest a people who build by principle as much as by plan.  
Your shadow, finally synchronized, steps when you step, and the deck underfoot answers with a soft, bright certainty.  

You are the Varnished now—  
not because you chose the name, but because the light has licked you clean of anything that could not be attended to.  
You are the proof that making can be endless, that a world can refuse to soften, and that in its refusal it can become a listening, a low, hot, quiet-breathing country where everything shines because everything is tended, again and again, until shine itself becomes a form of life.
