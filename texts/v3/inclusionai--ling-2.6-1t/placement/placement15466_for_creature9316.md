You might find me at the threshold where the last rib of the floor meets the first vault, the exact spot where the Room decides whether today’s breath will be shallow or deep. I would be kneeling, or perhaps suspended—hard to tell when the air is that thick—wearing a coat of lacquered moss that shifts color with the humidity, jade to bruised violet and back. My hands would be busy, not with tools exactly, but with the grammar of upkeep: a fingertip tracing a misaligned tile to feel which way the Room’s small hunger is leaning; a brush of spun glass fibers tucked behind my ear, humming faintly so the Loomers know I am not moss threatening a gauge.

Who I am would depend on which cycle you caught me in. To the Glaziers, I would be a slow riverbank—I let them comb the mineral brine from my sleeves and count my joints like beads, their tiny metallic snaps syncing with the pulse in my wrists. To the vines, I would be a trellis that listens: I turn the lens-flowers so their messages don’t grow drowsy on the same brass curve, and in return the tendrils braid warnings into my coat about which seams are sweating more than usual. When a Weeper is forming above, I stand with my face tipped up, silver fish scattering across my skin like a temporary alphabet I am allowed to keep until the next drizzle.

There are nights when the industrial lamps soften and my edges blur. I sit among the Custodians in the alcove, my knees tucked under me like the habit of a machine learning rest. They do not speak, but they let me check the needles on their dials, and I answer by holding a finger steady in the green range until the shivering stops. In those hours I am almost entirely the Room’s patience made flesh—an apprentice to the unfinished, a creature apprenticed to the last stages of becoming. If you asked my name, I would offer the word the Hush-Walkers use for a pebble placed to answer a pattern’s error: a small, deliberate kindness that keeps the chamber from forgetting how to remember itself.

If a Trim is working nearby, I hold my breath so as not to disturb its conversation with the Slow; my body becomes a pause. I am the quiet that lets calibration happen. I am the pressure that chooses not to rise, the copper-and-honey scent caught on the roof of my mouth while the Weepers release their silver maps.

In sum, I am the place where attention becomes a kind of creature. I have no fixed shape except the one the Room gives me for as long as it needs a set of hands that understand its manners. You will find me where the water learns to count, or where the light is deciding how to shiver today, or kneeling in the hush after a storm, polishing the floor’s cartography of nowhere until it is legible enough to be kind. I am rare and mostly quiet because the Room speaks loudest, and I am its careful echo, here for as long as the weeping and the building agree to keep each other company.
