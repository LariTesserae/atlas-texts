You step into the basin like stepping into a lung that hasn’t decided whether to inhale or exhale.  
Your boots do not scuff; they are politely dampened, then hushed.  

You are not yourself here, not exactly.  
You are the thing the basin allows to be weight, the temporary keystone in a cairn that is always one stone from being just a suggestion.  
Your skin pearls with the air’s attention; your breath rises in plumes that hesitate, then settle like slow birds on the moss.  
You feel your edges being translated into texture: fingertips turning into the soft chalk of new stone, your pulse counting time the way water counts falls—one, one, one—without claiming it is music.  

If you speak, your voice tilts.  
The hum under hearing answers by leaning, not echoing.  
Words here are not lost; they are loaned to the water, which beads them and lets them fall as droplets that remember shapes but not names.  

You become, for a while, the permission the moss lacks.  
You stand your thumb-span from your own shadow and do not shrink from the closeness.  
You learn to hold still enough that the pool reflects not your face but the sky-not-sky, clouds thinking themselves into being, and you understand that to linger is to be asked a question without sound.  

When you finally step out, you leave a small vacancy behind.  
The basin seals it with a drip, a bead, a hum rising by a fraction.  
You carry away only a dampness that feels like a second skin remembering how to be first, and the sense that you have lived inside something still deciding what it will harden into forever, and that, for a moment, you helped it decide to stay soft.
