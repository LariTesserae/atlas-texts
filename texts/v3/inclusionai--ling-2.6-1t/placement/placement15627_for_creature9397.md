I would find myself standing at the edge of a valley-canal where the water has thickened into ink, holding a hook of bone that is more question than tool. The low sky would press against my shoulders like a second spine, and my breath would taste of iron and moss. I would be someone in the process of becoming legible: not yet Undrowned, but consented to. My feet would already know how to yield, my eyes beginning to glaze with the milky patience of ice that does not melt. I would not be a stranger here; I would be a shape the place is deciding to keep, learning to measure time in rind and slab, learning to offer gifts of stone and silence in exchange for the slow right to occupy a pocket of naked rock. I would be the echo before the sound, the map of a place that does not exist yet, and the small, temporary weight that helps the water remember how to be level.
