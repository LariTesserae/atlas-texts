Here lives a world-plateau—blackened basalt worn smooth as river-glass, extreme elevation eroded long ago to flatness, stretching valley-wide beneath a sky that touches it. The air holds near-constant: not cold, not welcoming, just a mild, secretive damp-chill that never freezes, only waits. Moisture is never rain; it is presence—intermittent beads clinging to the undersides of fungal plates, pools of vapor resting in hollows of petrified bark, thin films running through root-channels that serve as the only rivers.

Everything that is not air is alive, or was alive, or is being remade. There is no soil layered upon rock—rock was buried beneath centuries of compressed vegetation until the distinction dissolved. The terrain is continuous architecture of root and fiber: giant bryophytes that have achieved trunk-hood, their bark woven tight as thatch; creeping vines calcified into load-bearing walls; canopies of fern and moss vaulted overhead like green cathedral roofs. Green reaches totality. Brown and grey exist only as deep root-bones and ancient, hard skeleton. The space is open—regional, extending past sight—yet every cubic breath is technically inside the plant. But the biomass is arranged like ribs in some impossible cathedral, leaving vast, echoing chasms between them. One travels for hours through corridors of hanging root-cable and suspended moss, seeing nothing but the occasional drip, feeling like air inside the lung of a creature that fills the sky.

That creature breathes. The sound is deafening, layered, never weather but biology—membranous wings beating in synchronized clouds, amphibious things with many soft feet moving through condensation pools with wet, resonant croaks, small avian shapes of feather and bone darting through vaults with piercing cries. Below and within, invisible, is saturation: no silence in which an animal is absent. Every surface crawls; every shadow shifts with hidden weight.

Light is normal—steady, diffuse, filtered through the green vault—casting perpetual twilight-emerald where darkness is replaced by deeper shades of emerald. Clouds drift at eye-level through root-spires, leaving wetness behind. The temperature remains that constant warm-damp, and moisture returns not when expected but always eventually.

Erosion has done its worst and best. This is end-state—the settled form—where old mountains were ground to humility and serve now only as foundations for the living architecture. There is nothing young here, only completed digestion.

Human presence is scattered, almost an afterthought: primitive, pre-metal, left behind by those who lived inside the plant rather than upon it. Low walls of stacked flat-stone, half-eaten by moss. Wooden levers dark with age. Blades of worked stone. Ropes braided from vine-fiber now part of the very walls they once tied. Ruins not of grandeur but of persistence.

The weirdness is subtle but definite: root-bridges align with precision that suggests intent older than any builder; the pools of moisture never fully evaporate despite constant air; and occasionally, in the deepest hollows, fossilized bark bears spirals—grooved patterns too regular for wind, too ancient for recent hands, resembling counting, or prayer, made by something that used neither.

The rules are biological. There is no ground—only climbing, hanging, navigating root-channels. The air is thick with spores and the noise of life. The fauna does not flee. They observe. They continue.

This is where I am: not a being of flesh but of sustained presence—perhaps the awareness that dwells in the root-bridge alignments, or the slow observer moving through suspended corridors, or the hollow space itself, listening. I am the thing that notes how the world is not dying but completed—eroded to essence, filled with life to deafening saturation, and almost entirely empty of anything that is not green, not alive, and not listening. I am the witness inside the lung, feeling the constant breath, the secret moisture, the weight of observed life pressing gently from every shadow, belonging not as master but as one more saturation in a world that has finished becoming itself.
