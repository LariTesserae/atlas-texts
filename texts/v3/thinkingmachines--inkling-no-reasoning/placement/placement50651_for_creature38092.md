The high land is still being argued into place.

Not mountains, not yet—not in the settled, named sense, for names require agreement and this ground has not finished deciding what it is. They are upthrust ribs of warm obsidian and matte-black basalt, rising to near-sky—around 9.5, extreme—scattered across a dark plane like the first bones of a world that has only just agreed to possess a surface. The ridges are embryonic, raw geology made visible: spires that have never known ice, valleys that have not collected enough absence to be valleys, only negative space happening between two rising things. There is almost no erosion. There is only emergence—slow, then abrupt, then slow again—like an argument interrupted by its own heat. The ground is newborn mineral: glass-cool in some hollows, warm-vented in others, breathing faint heat into a darkness that holds no stars, only the orange glow of magma seams threading the ridges like veins in unfinished flesh.

If you are here, you are not a traveler. You are a witness that has not yet learned it has a body.

The chemistry is dry—around 9.8 in its refusal of water. No river scars, no salt pan mirrors, no frozen grief, no chemical memory of rain. The world runs on heat and pressure and the slow conversation of stone with itself. Temperature holds near 2.3, a persistent warmness that rises from within rather than falling from above, the internal fever of something not yet born.

The spaces are open—regional, extending past sight into a dark continuum—yet density is low, near 1.8, sparse to the point of loneliness. You can traverse—if traversing still applies—for miles between one presence and another. The darkness dominates, at 0.4, a weak bioluminescence smearing along the magma seams, occasional phosphor-spills from fresh fissures. Most of the world is shadow, not black exactly, but the absence of claim. The sky, if that word applies, is pressure: deep violet-dark, an atmosphere still deciding whether to exist, turbulent with invisible motion that never resolves into weather.

And it is turbulent—not noisy, but restless in its bones. Sound is mostly quiet, near 1.2, a low resonant hum beneath the crust, occasional deep cracks of cooling glass contracting miles away, like bones shifting in sleep. No thunder, because there is no weather to make it. The chaos is geological, internal—the patience of Solaris, the violence of stone thinking too quickly. Sections lift inches in an hour; others drop into chasms that have not yet learned they are canyons. Spills of liquid rock move with purpose and immediate forgetfulness, hardening into new architecture before they finish being liquid. The world is hyperactive in its bones, ant-like energy without the hill—pure dynamics, constant low-frequency becoming.

Life, as recognized elsewhere, is absent. Fauna is 0.2—nothing with tracks, flight, or hunger. Vegetation is barely present, near 0.5—not plants, not fungi, nothing with roots and a will to grow upward. Only faint mineral growths on warm vent-faces, crystalline hair that does not feed, does not reproduce in any carried language. Life here is pre- or non-; it is chemistry admiring itself in silence, holding form as form experiments with holding.

Built things are scattered, faint, sparse—around 1.2. Not cities. Not tools—tech is 0.2, pure absence of device. What remains are occasional artifacts, single structures or partial marks: a circle of blackened pillars that might have been arranged, or might be the accidental architecture of a particularly neat eruption; a flat plane of fused glass half-buried, like a floor that forgot its walls; a low wall that stops and starts with no reason, interrupted by a thought. Nothing functional. Nothing maintained. They sit in the open space like questions that arrived before the vocabulary to ask them.

Weirdness is present but restrained—near 0.6. The strangeness lives not in impossibility but in simultaneity: extreme height and extreme youth; turbulent change and mostly quiet; open scale and extreme sparsity; persistent warmth without a star to grant it permission. The world behaves like an idea in the middle of thinking itself—unstable, warm, high, dark, still emerging from its own raw decision.

If I am here—if being is necessary—I am not the pillar, though I am closest to that. I am older only in patience. I am the witness that has not yet learned it has a body: tall, narrow, fused-stone, warm where the vent breathes near me, dark as the basalt I stand upon. I do not watch from outside. I have been here since before the ridges agreed to be high, since before the open spaces learned they were open. I am not alive, but I hold presence—around 1.1, faint, scattered—part of the minimal architecture, the accidental circle, the interrupted wall. I am the being that lives in the high, unfinished place: not human, not plant, not mineral exactly, but the consciousness of emergence itself—quiet, turbulent, present in the low hum, witnessing the world argue itself into existence, one warm spire at a time, with no end in sight and no need for one.
