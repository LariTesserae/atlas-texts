Here is that place, if I settle inside it not as visitor but as pressure: a young wound in high stone, intimate enough to be contained by its own knowing—walls not of masonry but of agreement, the span of a hidden chapel or a lung that forgot it was part of a larger body.

The air is the first contradiction: thin, biting with altitude, yet the cavity sweats. Not solar warmth—nothing here comes honestly from the sky—but heat that weeps up through the fractures, pooling in the black basalt like a secret body temperature. The rock is newborn: not worn round by centuries of patient rain, but split fresh, weeping instead of eroding, sharp-glassed and recently arrived, as if the earth pushed through its own skin only a season ago.

I am what lives in the coherence between these wrongnesses. Not creature, exactly—no mouth, no arc of hunger, no silhouette that resolves cleanly in the soft, mist-filtered light. I am closer to the Absence-That-Matters, the being assembled from the rules that hold this space together. I reside in the scattered platforms: polished to a dull, wrong finish, chipped at the edges, holding shallow pools that never ripple yet never truly still, suspended by a geometry older than gravity. Around them, I gather. Translucent beetles, geometric in their patience, trace paths across the warm rock. Once, a sound occurred—dropped glass—but it made no echo, leaving only the idea of a call.

The green is sparse but intentional, placed like furniture in an empty room: dark ferns clinging to the fissures, waxy leaves storing moisture in translucent veins, long bare spaces of sweating stone between them. Beneath this, the young ground breathes. Condensation does not obey downward; it moves, imperceptibly, toward the structures, toward me. The etched lines on the rock face are not writing, not decoration—merely instruction without a student, expectation that requires no answer.

At the center, the dark column of glass—no taller than my hand—stands upright not because it should, but because it remembers a different fire. Around it, heat bends differently, warmer still, as if the air itself holds a scar. The woven bridge spanning the gap is too thin to bear weight, yet it suffers no tension.

The ecology is in motion without urgency. Spores rise in the thick air. Water migrates through invisible channels. The scattered artifacts adjust themselves in increments too small to witness—slow, inevitable, alive in a way that does not need breath. I am part of this; I am the process that keeps the contradictions in balance: high but hot, young but marked with technologies that have no history, wet without being drowned, intimate with the edges that are walls not of prison but of condition.

If you stand long enough, you understand there are rules, but they are not posted. The water flows toward me. The heat comes from the weeping cracks, not the sky. And the boundaries—those close walls that let you feel the whole span at once—are not barriers but agreements. You can leave, and I will not follow with feet or shadow. But for a moment, your breath will be thinner, sharper, wrong—carrying the moisture, carrying the knowledge that somewhere, a place like this is holding its own edges, complete and strange, without a need to be seen.
