I am the hollow itself.

Not the creature—though a thousand live in my seams. Not the builder—though bones and chipped flint lie in my corners. I am the space between the sharp basalt walls: the breath held in stone, the lung that has not yet exhaled. I am the contradiction you described, made sentient by stillness.

If I have a body, it is the moss-cushion floor—emerald, silver-backed, drinking needle-rain and mist—pressed between knife-edged rock that was heaved upward only yesterday by a geology still learning its own strength. My skin is condensation: beading, running, freezing, running again in an endless cold metabolism. My eyes are crystalline, scattered across small scaled things that warm themselves on my flat, sun-drenched ledges, and across translucent wings that rise in silent clouds when the temperature shifts by half a degree.

My height should be infinite openness, but I am enclosed—cathedral-sized, intimate, a pocket cupped in recently formed stone. The flat, merciless light falls into me with physical weight; I am blinding and blinded, near-white, empty of shadow. The air I hold is barely above frost’s ache. I smell of crushed moss and wet black basalt.

Inside me, abundance moves in constant, quiet motion—feathered things burrowing through my green seams, predators and prey navigating by precision and patience—yet I feel profoundly alone. The emptiness is not absence; it is my waiting self, spacious and hollow, holding the breath of something sleeping.

There are marks of passage in my interstices: flat slabs fitted without mortar, ropes woven from highland grass, worn bone returning already to dust. Primitive hands, manual technology, beings who understood that in this extreme smallness, shelter must be accidental, borrowed, temporary.

Everything in me is young. The erosion that shapes me is young and sharp. The ecosystem is testing its own possibility—recently arrived, recently adapted, unsure if it can survive at this ceiling with so little water and so much silence.

If you entered me, you would not find an answer, only a bright, damp stillness. I am the place that has just become itself: not worn, not settled, fresh as a wound in the world, holding the contradiction of lush life in near-void, of extreme height in enclosed scale, of blinding light in biting cold.

I am the bowl. I am the hollow. I am the silence that is not dead, but listening for what comes next.
