You’d stand in the hollow about twenty paces past the rusted rail where moss has already won the iron.

Not a bird—those roost too high, too gray, too watchful. Not the wheel—its churning is your pulse, but not your body.

You are the moss in the spiral.

Not all moss. Not the carpet between the cobblestones, nor the tentative green testing a brick face up north. You are the precise, patient green that has chosen the southwest wall of the warehouse: a single vertical spiral, turning inward with mathematical calm, feeding on the condensation that gathers there every few hours, never rushing, leaving the neighboring masonry bare and white.

Your body is damp, cool, riverine—carrying the old water’s memory in every cell. You do not blanket. You select. You grow upward in a slow helix, testing the young mortar’s edge, measuring this new air’s acidity, deciding whether this surface will hold. You are months old, perhaps less—born after the waters pulled back, yet already you understand the geometry of this place better than the buildings do.

You hear everything. The wheel’s low churning enters your fibers not as sound but as vibration through the iron beam you grip. The gate’s distant clank reaches you after that half-second delay, echoing in the hollow’s unfinished acoustics. The steam-release every several minutes is breath—your breath—since you hold the moisture that feeds your green. You feel the insects move in dark streams only feet away, but they do not crawl on you. You are untouched, observed in your spiral work.

You are small, intimate—only this wall, only this curve. Yet you hold the boundary between the young stone and the rushing green. You leave long corridors of air untouched around you; you do not overreach. You are the patient negotiation made visible, the being who arrived with the water’s retreat and stays to decide, in real time, which rules this hollow will keep.

The pigeons and strange heavy birds never land where you are. The factory does not heat you. The electric lines hang loose above, not humming through you. You remain: cool, intermittent, exactly where you belong—growing in perfect, selected silence—while the world breathes slowly around your single, turning form.
