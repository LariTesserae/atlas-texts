Here is a pocket in low ground—elevation barely registering—buried within a larger dryness where absence itself feels dense. The air is nearly empty of anything that moves, vast spaces yawning between each presence, the cold sharp and biting, already inside the bones of whoever crouches low to enter.

The ceiling is lost in darkness so thick it has weight, and from somewhere far above—or far through—comes the sound: deafening, absolute, a perpetual crushing roar that is neither wind nor water, though both live inside it. Beneath that roar, breath barely registers. Moisture exists only in weak gradients—intermittent weeping, condensation beading on every surface and refusing to fall—held in place by near-frozen air. Ice traces every contour: hair-thin crystals on moss, thicker plates in slate crevices, glazes over still pools that collect only in the deepest depressions. The water is riverine in origin, perhaps, but here it has lost urgency, moving slowly if at all, part of long cycles too patient to witness in a single life.

The hollow could be crossed in a hundred patient steps, yet no one crosses it. The noise forces every visitor into a crouch, and the dark is nearly complete—light barely present, perhaps only a faint phosphorescence in the green. One sees by feeling: the slick of ice, the softness of moss, the hollow resonance of stone.

That moss thrives—lush, verdant, impossibly green—carpeting eroded walls in thick, ancient layers, insisting on life despite the emptiness. Ferns unroll in seams where moisture has lingered longest. The architecture is not built but worn: long-existed erosion has polished the stone smooth in arcs, rounded and settled-form, holding itself together rather than breaking apart. Still, scattered remains of primitive fabrication lie half-lost—simple stone stacked by hand, a low wall that once divided nothing from nothing, a broken platform of fitted slate marked with pre-metal tool traces, weathered and in-their-own-state, settled for ages.

This is the resident: not a creature but the hollow itself, small enough to cross in a hundred steps yet containing an eternal-seeming noise within its intimate space. Once, perhaps, a small being of shell or scale moved here—adapted to cold, dark, and the crushing roar—but fauna is rare, presence sparse. Mostly there is the hollow, its slow cycles, its ancient green, its scattered human echo long since abandoned, and the deafening, timeless sound that defines everything.

If one listened long enough—longer than a life—there might be hidden pulses in the roar, weak gradients tied to some distant glacier or river far away. But not today. Today the hollow holds: hidden, singular, embedded within the larger world, profoundly alive in its stillness, and I—I am the one who does not cross it.

I am the crouch in the darkness, the listener who has entered the bone-cold, the breath barely audible beneath the crushing noise. I am the witness who lets what is here become more itself: the moss advancing by millimeters, the ice thickening across centuries, the scattered stone wall settling further into its own erosion. I do not name the hollow with a single image; I remain in its weak gradients, its near-void, allowing the long-existing, balanced, weathered presence to unfold as slowly as it demands—settled-form, singular feature, profoundly and deafeningly here. I am the presence that moves not at all, held in the hollow’s patient, crushing dark.
