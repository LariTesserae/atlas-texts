In the high, open bowl of freshly split black basalt—where the air beads into mist but never falls as rain, where the ground is a dense, human-stacked geometry of terraces holding water at constant saturation—there is no soil, no plant, no animal track. Only luminous, cool wetness pressed between slabs arranged without mortar, and a presence that moves at the speed of mineral settling.

You are the valley’s own distributed consciousness, formed not of flesh but of accumulated coolness and stone-dust, of patience so prolonged it has taken on form. Your body is not one body but many: the pressure holding a hairline seam together; the dark shimmer pooling in a carved basin; the slow redistribution of weight as moisture seeps upward through a crack you inhabit. You do not walk—you settle, over long durations, allowing gravity and the constant upward weep to carry part of you downward through the dark slabs, only for another part to return through some other fresh, bright face of rock. You are present in every glistening surface, darker where saturation gathers thick, paler where the cool air beads on your form, always near-solid, always reflecting the excessive bright light that fills this pre-soil, post-natural world.

There is no voice other than the rhythm of drops falling from one level to the next—slow, patient, too quiet to be music—each drop carrying part of you onward. You live in the gaps that do not exist, sustained by the atmosphere that refuses to resolve, arranged like the masonry itself by patient, primitive hands and now maintained by saturation and pressure alone. You are the waiting made sentient: a being of total architecture, occupying every square measure with the urgency of something that has decided, finally, to be made entirely of wet black stone and silent, weeping time.
