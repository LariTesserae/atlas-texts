If I lived here, I would be the sustained sound made visible—something that occupies the gap between structure and emergence, neither architect nor animal, but the measure of their coexistence.

This valley is not old, so I am not ancient. There has been no time for age; I am only the first persistence, the record of abundance deciding that it is enough. I live in the shallow dark pools that never drain, never rise—water held in permanent suspension over gray mud that is still arguing whether it will be soil or stone. The air is warm and saturated, hanging lower than breath should, beading on every surface without ever running away. I feel this constant condensation not as weather but as weight, a dense climate that presses downward with me.

The settlement is pressed into the ground not upward—fired clay, flat stone, low vaults and circular kilns, channels half-carved, wooden braces and simple metal spikes holding everything together. Primitive, organized, settlement-scale, spreading across the flat basin in a continuous urban weave with no streets to separate it. Nothing decays; there are no weeds, no rot, only mineral endurance and the perpetual addition of wet layers. I live in that endurance. I am the proof that this place holds form without advancing, that structure can persist without history.

But I am also the life—abundant, constant, unmistakable, never strange, only plentiful. Thick-bodied pale crawlers move through the pools and over the clay walls: shell-less molluscs, segmented bodies, soft amphibians sliding in endless migration. They do not call out. They do not alter their paths. They are multiplied beyond counting, and their wet movement, multiplied by the dripping water and the constant low pressure of the warm air, becomes deafening—a sustained, featureless roar, static thunder, eternal presence. I am that roar. I am the acoustic density of multitudes that need no reason beyond sliding, existing, filling every flat space with wet rustle.

The light is dim and diffuse, coming from no sun—only from the warm haze and the faint phosphorescence of the pools, reflecting off a hundred thousand surfaces. I live in that dimness, neither hidden nor revealed, present like everything else.

This is a pre-formative place, raw-geology given just enough form to hold itself. The temperature never shifts. The pools never dry. Nothing erodes because nothing changes. Everything moves—crawlers, water, air—without altering course. The architecture breathes in its grid but does not grow.

So I am here: in the constant warm wet, in the dense settlement pressed downward, in the abundant sliding life that makes the air thunderous, in the invariant equilibrium that will never arrive at an end. I am not a single image. I am the valley’s refusal to be empty—its permanent, deafening, plentiful now.
