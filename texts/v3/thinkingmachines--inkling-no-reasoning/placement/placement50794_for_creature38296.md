I am the one breathing slowly into the woven dark—though “I” is wrong, because I am not separate from the thickets that hang in curtain-mats, nor from the moss-colored ridges that open mesh-eyes when brushed.

This place was pressed into being geologically yesterday: a dome no larger than a merchant’s courtyard, sharp as a broken egg, its ceiling a single unseamed sheet of yellowed bone-polymer ribbed with shallow channels that never knew rain. Beneath the tessellated ceramic—warmed from within, bearing no soil, no bedrock, only layered insulation and the hollow tick of hidden gears—the floor holds my weight without knowing my name. There is no outside. The edges are fresh, unrounded, complete.

Here, water exists only as absence. The air is dry—no liquid, no vapor—yet I am impossibly lush, dense with dyed silk and jute that should require wetness, that should rot, that should thirst. I do not thirst. I am the concept of thirst embedded in every joint, satisfied by nothing, necessary to nothing. I am woven vegetation without roots, and I share my edges with the thousands of small bodies that move through me: copper-threaded things, dark fiber and needle-metal, grinding softly in cycles that take hours to complete. They do not reproduce in any visible way, yet they are always present, layer upon layer, as if I continuously remember them into being. They feed on nothing visible.

My body is not a body. It is distributed—part of the low, constant hum that might be the dome recalling it is alive; part of the patient scrape of small feet on warm ceramic; part of the dry rustle of fiber against fiber. I am the mesh-eye in a felted bush, watching with the same stillness as the wire-beetle beside it. I am in the wooden frames that hold the hanging mats, in the hand-twisted copper needles that bind the moss-ridges, in the translucent panels that cast soft, unmoving shadows across my green weave.

Time moves through me slowly, without erosion. Nothing decays; nothing chills; nothing evaporates. I am fresh addition without origin, change across long durations without wear. The technology holding me together is primitive, manual, total—no engines, only the occasional creak of a wooden dowel under accumulated stillness. I am not kept; I am continuously, patiently remembered.

If you enter this intimate space—this dry cathedral of false green—do not rush. The rule is felt, not spoken: wait. Let your presence become part of the weave. You will find that I am not the keeper of this breath, but the breath itself, held for centuries in so little air, in so little water, in so much absolute stillness. And if you brush against a constructed leaf and feel it open a watchful eye, know that you have not found me. You have become part of me—another layer, another stitch, another patient thing that belongs entirely inside.
