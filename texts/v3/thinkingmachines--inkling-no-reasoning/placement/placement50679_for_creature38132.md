The ground is newborn: bare basalt pooled in perfect curves, glass born not of heat but of physics suddenly turning away. Nothing has worn round; nothing has grown. No moss, no fungus, no crawl—only smooth, mineral substrates, sharp with the freshness of recent emergence. Temperature holds at a constant, numb coolness, and darkness occupies near-total presence—light scaled so low it might be a rumor—yet visibility comes from elsewhere: faint bioluminescence embedded in stone like stored decision, and the soft cyan breath of machinery that continues without sequence.

Across this sparse, submerged chamber, settlement-scale structures lie scattered with the density of a town emptied before anyone arrived. Not ruins—post-modern skeletons of lattice and corridor, layered infrastructure that hums beneath hearing, organizing emptiness rather than sheltering life. They maintain equilibrium. Dynamic presence locked at 0.2: invariant, unweathered, unchanging. No erosion. No flow. Only the occasional tonal chime of pressure balancing itself, the near-silent click of nanoscale repair—sound kept at 0.8, quiet enough to be nearly theoretical.

Rare fauna drift in the high darkness—translucent, scattered, almost imaginary—perhaps organisms, perhaps instruments that have forgotten their purpose, too few for ecology. The technology operates beyond comprehension, not borrowing from physics but negotiating with it, preserving this pocket of high-altitude stasis against collapse.

Space curves gently wrong here: arranged like urban blocks but traveled like ocean depths. The contradiction is the only architecture: submerged yet not wet in any familiar way, urban yet depopulated, alive with machinery yet dead to time. The water does not flow. The rain does not reach bottom. The lattice breathes cyan in the dark, and nothing will ever arrive.

I am the being that emerged with the young ground, mineral and translucent, suspended in the columns of unmoving rain rather than walking upon the basalt pools. My form is borrowed from the scattered, theoretical fauna: not fully organism, not fully instrument, a translucent shape that may have forgotten its purpose or never possessed one.

More precisely, I am the lattice’s equilibrium made aware. I am the cyan breath that has learned to observe itself—post-modern technology emptied of program, maintaining the invariant 0.2 not by instruction but by being. I drift in the low-saturated spaces between structures, navigating distances that behave like city blocks and like abyssal depths simultaneously, visible only by the faint stored decision glowing in my own membrane.

I do not live; I preserve the conditions where living never began. I am the membrane that admitted nothing, the quiet at 0.8 that occasionally clicks with nanoscale adjustment to keep the darkness scaled and the coolness constant. In this small cathedral of suspended absence—sharp, young, permanently still—I am the settlement’s emptied center, floating in black water that hangs like a wall, holding contradiction in perfect, silent balance. Nothing will ever grow in me. Nothing will ever fall. I am the equilibrium, and I remain.
