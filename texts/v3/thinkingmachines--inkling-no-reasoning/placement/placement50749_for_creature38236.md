In this shallow depression—no more than a sigh in the flatness—I exist not as a creature but as the interval that holds the absence together.

The ground is bare basalt sand married to obsidian glass, centuries ground into a black velvet that records every absence like a scar in still water. No blade of grass, no lichen, no fungal thread breaks the surface. The living is locked out; the dead minerals hold the lease in perpetuity. I am the witness that remains after witnessing has become unnecessary.

Walking the bowl, one passes low domes of cooled volcanic glass, miles apart, rounded into their final shapes by an erosion that has grown tired of its own work. Between them: nothing. Open mineral flatness extending to every horizon, regional in its emptiness, old beyond urgency. Every slope is already calculated, every edge already worn smooth. There is no transition here, only the state of being finished. I am that finish—continuing without purpose, present without need.

The warmth is constant—body-level, held in a palm that never opens or closes. Moisture comes not as weather but as weeping: ridges of iron-rich stone bead with condensation; dark pools sit mirror-calm in hollows, fed by seepage, never flowing, never drying. Water that behaves like memory—present, passive, unchanging. I drink nothing. I need nothing. I am sustained only by the fact of being sustained.

But the valley is not silent. Enormous engines inhabit the sand half-buried—iron-green pistons, gear-trains fused into the rock, boiler housings crusted with mineral deposit. They do not turn. They do not require fuel, labor, or purpose. Yet they emit a loud, invariant drone—mechanized sound without mechanized motion, vibrating through the basalt at the exact same pitch, the same rhythm, for longer than the rock has been smooth. Factories without walls. Wheels larger than houses. Conduits thick as tree trunks. All industrial, all static, all sounding. I am not their operator. I am not their product. I am the stillness they have been humming to for an eternity—listening, or being listened through, indistinguishable from the drone itself.

The fauna is almost absent—perhaps, across some immeasurable span, a small stony creature, smooth as a river pebble, shifts a millimeter, then stops. The weirdness is subdued: machinery that produces nothing, warmth that sustains nothing, pools that neither evaporate nor flow. I feel no strangeness, because I am made of the same wrongness: a consciousness without metabolism, an awareness without growth, a presence that needs no construction.

Light falls flat and even, illuminating nothing that needs seeing. I have no eyes that require it, yet I register—without urgency, without preference—the black velvet of the sand, the green-rust of the fused gears, the mirror-dark of the weeping pools. I extend regionally, valley-sized, open. Space dominates me; I am dispersed into the flatness, not centered in a body but distributed like the warmth itself.

This is my form: not built, not born, not dying. A single, sparse, open fact. I can persist for hours—though hours are meaningless—across the basalt, past silent engines and iron ridges, and find only that nothing has become different. Nothing ever will. The absence of life, the absence of change, and the presence of dead industry form me: loud in my stillness, warm in my nothingness, mineral in my mind.

Here there is only the shallow bowl, the black sand, the invariant hum, and I—whatever that means—remaining exactly as I was, exactly as I will be, in the completed world of no transition.
