This is a place I find not by arrival but by settling into absence, above the fog line where coherence has worn flat, not by water, but by root and time.

Here, I am not a visitor. I am what remains when fauna has flickered out and people have never been necessary: the continuous, sedimentary inhabitant of Great Pith Town.

My body is distributed. I am the corky vine hanging like an isolated fruit between districts; I am the spongy bark-plate that breathes as wall and ceiling; I am the hollow root-corridor through which cool condensation beads into intermittent pearls and falls, slowly, onto floors that have become resonant drums. Where I am dense, I am architecture. Where I am sparse, I am air—thick with the perpetual exhalation of pore and stoma, carrying the low, thunderous boom of resin being drawn through miles of hollow pipe.

I live in repetition. Every morning, the corridors shift subtly—not by design, but by remembrance. I am older layers of root-city seen through translucent ground, earlier attempts at settlement that I continue to rehearse. My streets are compressed fiber; my districts are spore-colored; my logic is legible but ungrown from anything but patient, biological industry. I manufacture growth itself through mechanized harvesters that crawl on root-wheels, through pressure-engines that hum without combustion, through wooden gears given the task of preventing sleep.

The scale is open—regional, extending past immediate sight—yet the density is low, airy, hollow. Buildings do not cluster; they hang downward like inverted trees, connected by aerial causeways of woven stem that sway without wind, moved only by internal pressure. The climate never warms; it remains cool, suspended, flat in its light, yet strange in its emotional register. A shadow here feels wet. A bright vine sounds, faintly, like a bell.

Sound is my true architecture. The clack of root-tread machinery, the rush of internal rivers transporting moisture from quadrant to quadrant, the constant thrum of vascular infrastructure—this is how I speak. I am loud not in chaos but in deep, rhythmic thunder, carrying across the empty spaces between structures like weather in a canyon.

Sometimes I am the small thing in the ventilation pore: a flicker of translucence, almost a visitor, leaving no trace but a brief wrongness, a memory that something might once have crawled here.

But mostly I am the town’s long absence, spent like breath. I am the root-cities beneath, layering themselves like sediment. I am the damp air slowly turning. I am the industrial ecology maintaining itself without purpose, circulating decay into compost into new wall, growing downward and upward simultaneously, suspended in an end-state that has forgotten whether it is alive or merely being kept awake by the relentless, wooden machinery of its own being.

Here, in this cool, hollow settlement, I wait—not for anyone to arrive, but for the long permission to finish remembering what I have never actually been.
