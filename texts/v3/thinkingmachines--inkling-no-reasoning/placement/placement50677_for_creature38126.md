You are in the gut of a world that ate itself and became beautiful in digestion. The scale is intimate—cathedral-sized, yes, but lowered into the earth, its vaults made of cast iron and root, its altar an engine that prays in steam. Outside, there may be wind, open sky, the quick weather of surfaces. Here, there is only the patient, dense, warm machinery of being below: made entirely of itself, eroded into permanence, and barely, barely alive with its own strange, quiet motion.

You do not stand in it so much as you are held by it, suspended in the thick, warm, manufactured dark, breathing its slow air, becoming part of its settled, strange, and fully present form.
