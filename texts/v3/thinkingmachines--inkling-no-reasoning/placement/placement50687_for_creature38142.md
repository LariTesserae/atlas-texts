I am the hollow listening in the collapsed round shelter—no longer human-shaped, but the shape of absence that learned to hold water like a bowl holds rain.

The valley is high, suspended beneath a ceiling of cloud so wet and heavy it behaves as a second sea. Here, the air is not air but a wall of near-freezing mist, relentless and fine, filling every hollow. Rain does not fall so much as pool; it collects in ankle-deep black basins among ridges of weathered basalt, then waist-deep, then lost into deeper darkness. The temperature never rises far above the knife-edge—cold enough that breath fogs, that cloth freezes stiff against numb skin, that fingers must stay in constant motion or surrender feeling.

Everything is submerged in the atmospheric sense. The ground is not soil but shifting, black water running over smoothed stone ribs that curve like the half-sunken architecture of some immense drowned creature—no hand made them, but centuries of current and frost have shaped them into forms that seem intentional. The sound is constant and loud: not thunder, but water in every voice. Thousands of small impacts on moss and rock create a collective roar like distant surf. Swift shallow streams grind stones with a hollow, persistent clatter. And deeper—always deeper—there is a sub-bass drone, water moving through hidden channels far beneath the valley floor, vibrating up through boot and bone, older than the growth above.

But the darkness is the true architecture. The cloud-ceiling devours starlight, moonlight, all distant horizon. Visibility collapses to perhaps thirty paces. In this near-total blackness, navigation is not by sight but by the valley’s one rebellion against absence: thick mats of phosphorescent moss clinging to every ridge, glowing with a faint, pulsing blue-green bioluminescence—like underwater lanterns in an abyssal trench. Between them hang curtains of translucent vine, root-submerged and heavy with droplets that tremble but never fall, reproducing by division and silent chemistry, never knowing pollination, never waiting for an animal that does not come.

For there are none. Zero fauna. No insects vibrating in the moss, no fish turning in the pools, no birds, no tracks—because there is no mud, only water over stone. The absence is not peaceful; it is structural, as present as the rain itself. The plants thrive in a loop without consumption: moss filters the constant rain, roots slow erosion, decomposed matter feeds new growth. The ecosystem turns alone—lush, strange, utterly without companionship.

Scattered sparsely across this regional emptiness—mid-range, open, extending past the immediate into repeated ridges of dark pool—are the marks of primitive presence. Not advanced, not metal. Just low walls of flat, mortared stone, weathered black; a single round shelter, its roof long collapsed, interior filled with still black water and moss growing up through cracks like a green tide reclaiming craft; blackened wooden stakes now rotted; brittle clay vessels scattered among the pools like offerings left for a deity that never arrived. The technology was always manual, pre-metal, suited to survival where dry space is myth and fire is nearly impossible.

The dynamics never cease. Water falls, flows, drains downward into unseen depths, then rises slowly through ancient fractures—cold, mineral-heavy—to feed the pools again. The moss brightens and dims in chemical cycles no eye can trace. Stones shift imperceptibly, measured in millimeters across years. This is not sudden collapse but long-existed equilibrium: weathered, balanced, actively wet. The valley breathes water.

Here, the usual contracts are broken. No animal completes the plant. No dry ground promises rest. No human need is satisfied by the tools left behind. Only water, vegetation, stone, darkness, and sound remain in negotiation—intertwined, biological and geological, relentless.

So I am not the builder—those bodies are long absent, if they ever truly inhabited what they built. I am the hollow they left behind, filled now with still black water and moss. I am the listening absence inside the collapsed round wall, vibrating with the underground drone, witnessing the faint blue-green pulse of life that needs no warmth, no witness, no other. I am scattered—small, occasional, lost between the glowing ridges and the falling rain—part of the valley’s strange ontology. I do not suffer the cold, for I have become part of the saturation; I do not navigate, for I am the space through which the water moves.

In this high suspended bowl, I am the shape of presence that learned to be empty—submerged, listening, glowing faintly with borrowed moss-light—while the rain continues its loud, endless conversation with stone, and the valley remains in its own state: endless, active, utterly alone.
