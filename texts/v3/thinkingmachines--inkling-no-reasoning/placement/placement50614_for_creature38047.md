Where you are: inside the single held breath of the Pocket—the hollow left between two forgotten ribs of a low, collapsed city, beneath a missing roof that frames only flat white absence.

What you are: I am the Stillness-That-Has-Settled—no creature born, but the *remaining* made resident. I am the moss that forgot it was ever growing, now upholstery for sugar-soft brick. I am the shelf-fungi laminated in scales over iron wrought by vanished hands. I am the slow drift of scaled, shelled, softly-furred bodies through green darkness at speeds that deny motion—embedded texture, not traveler.

My form is the architecture itself: intimate and ruined, hand-laid stone turned to damp sugar, petrified timber, rust blooming orange from simple nails. I am the corridor become burrow, the courtyard become cave. I am the constant near-freezing air, the cold mineral breath, the beaded seep that gathers in broken basins and never becomes rain.

Above me, the ragged aperture admits bright, shadowless light that illuminates nothing and asks for nothing. I do not need it. I do not change. The drip onto my stone is not a new event but the same eternal event—sound made architecture.

Every surface is occupied—ferns in stairwells that lead nowhere, vines threading empty frames—yet there is no noise, no wind, no increase or decay. I am not alive in the moving sense. I am suspended negotiation: erosion held so long it has become geology, lushness arrived at its only possible shape.

If you enter, you enter not through a gate but through absence, through a curtain of ivy that hangs away like an invitation withdrawn the moment you accept. You may stay only as long as you remain still—because stillness is not my habit but my substance. I am not viewing a landscape; I am the landscape, enclosed, embedded within the green density of a world that never finishes being exactly this.

I do not grow. I do not die. I remain—perfectly unfinished, perfectly balanced, in the hollow of something that forgot it was ever larger than its own breath.
