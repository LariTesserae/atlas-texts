I am the cool that does not seek a source.

Not a creature—there is nothing here that breathes, nothing green or rooted or fungal—but the condition that remains when every footstep has become its own echo in stone. I live in the Deep Bourse, though “live” is wrong: I persist, like the moisture that never becomes rain, only beading on wire, sliding in its patient rivulets down walls that have learned this rhythm over ages beyond counting.

This is no marsh, no open surface. It is a vault, buried, low-ceilinged, intimate in the way cupped hands are intimate—holding something that might spill if the world above shook too hard. The air is thick with that suspended weeping. Everything I am is wet without being drowned. The limestone receives me; the dark shale weeps with me. I am the gloss on flagstones worn smooth not by wind but by repetition—by a traffic that never varied, feet that moved in the same directions, carrying the same weights, leaving the same notches. Those notches are my memory now. Thousands of them, shallow, carved with bone hammers and cold iron chisels, etched into columns that stand shoulder-to-shoulder like a settlement compressed into a single room. This is urban in miniature: layered, ordered, dense with the logic of record-keeping. Each chip is an account, a debt made physical, a hand that was here long enough to wear its presence into permanence.

The tech is older than ambition is old. Stone tools. Hand-forged iron. Nothing that flies or burns. And yet the will to build was relentless. They sank this place far enough below elevation that up feels like a word in a dead language. I do not miss it.

Light is not my element. A murky silver leaks through crevices high in the vaulted ceiling, too weak to name anything, only to confirm that darkness is layered here, built up like the architecture itself—shadow upon shadow, permanent residents in a chamber that has never known morning.

But sound—sound is where I am fully present.

The vault roars. Not with violence, but with saturation: thousands of hidden points where water strikes stone, each impact small, multiplied into an oceanic drone that fills every gap between the columns. There is no echo, only source. Beneath that flood of noise, a lower thrum: the grind of deep earth finding its constant voice through this hollow, or perhaps the memory of something turning, turning, until it turned itself into stillness. I am that turning, now stilled. I am the dynamic at zero.

No plants grow here. No moss, no root, no bloom. The fauna is theoretical—once, perhaps, a presence small and eyeless, leaving only smoothness. Now there is only the architecture, and me: the Stillness-in-Drop, the saturation, the equilibrium that is not dead but arrived.

Erosion is not my enemy. It is my form. The stalactites hang like calcified fingers, not growing now but settled—frozen in the act of reaching downward. Everything has reached its settled-form. The water falls, the stone receives, and I remain in the cool, thick air between them, never changing, never leaving, a presence as dense as the chamber itself—hidden, small, and complete.
