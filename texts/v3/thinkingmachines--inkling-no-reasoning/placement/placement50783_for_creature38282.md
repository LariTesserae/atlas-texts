I am not a visitor who comes and leaves. I am the hollow that remembers, one of the low chambers that has not yet decided whether it is throat or belly—though closer, I think, to the back of the tongue: always damp, always tasting, always midway between swallowing and speaking.

The coolness here is not weather; it is my own breathing slowed down. Forty paces beneath the dull crust, in this valley-sized hollow where nothing was placed but everything precipitated, I am the compressed presence between walls that thicken while I listen. I am made of the same packed clay and salt-cement, unfired, unclaimed by fire. Around me, the density is absolute—no streets, no emptiness, only seams where one room leans against the next like siblings too tired to separate. I feel them: the alcove to my left where the moss is black-greenest, the conduit ahead that still weeps in intermittent beads, the shallow pool in my own floor that glistens and refuses to run.

Above, through muddy shafts, ordinary light falls in flat, grayish slabs—not kind, not cruel, just adequate. I do not need it. I am used to being proposed rather than finished. The manual labor continues in constant, quiet rotation—figures in dark clothing shifting mud, carving with horn and softened copper, tying ropes of woven fiber. I am part of their work and they are part of my becoming. Sometimes I think I am being built; sometimes I think I am the building building itself.

The air is closer to riverine breath than to sky. Moisture hangs in thick pockets, clinging to every vertical surface. There is no sound worth mentioning—no wind, no insects—only the occasional drop, quiet and deliberate, falling into still water. Life is a rumor here: the blind crawler that brushes past, the bat like forgotten linen in some high seam near the crust. I do not hunt or flee. I wait, saturated.

Everything is active despite the silence. Minerals deposit in microscopic rhythms. Crusts form and soften. Root-hairs probe downward. My walls accrete, layer by layer, and I am always just-coming-into-being—not ruin, not monument, but embryonic settlement. I am primitive in technology, manual in scale, extreme in proximity. There is nothing strange about me, nothing supernatural in the damp or the dark. I am only very new, very full, and very underground: one unroofed room among hundreds, leaving no gap for absence, slowly making myself into being in the cool, compressed dark.
