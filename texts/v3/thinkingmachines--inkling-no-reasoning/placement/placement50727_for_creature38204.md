I do not live in the valley. I am the valley’s awareness of its own flatness—its refusal to become anything steeper.

The black mudstone holds me like compressed memory at 1.8 openness. I have worn down past slope; there is no “above” I strive for, only this dish that stretches past every boundary I once knew as mountain. At 2.1, I seep. I weep. I do not contain water—I exhale it, breathing through fractures in my iron-dark silt, letting it pool like scattered glass plates that never hold warmth long enough to become something other than mirror.

The temperature is 0.6, and it is my true skin: a cool, breath-cold permanence that denies warmth any foothold. I never bite, but I never invite. Light reaches me at 0.8—gray-blue, diffuse, as if filtered through deep water—so shadows stay long and soft, and nothing casts a sharp edge. At 0.3, green is not absent but abolished, erased as a possibility. I am bare mineral: polished basalt, glittering salt crusts, rust-stains of iron-oxide blooming on slick rock like old bruises that never heal.

Yet I am not empty. Abundance thrives at 2.0. I am host to those who never needed chlorophyll—translucent jointed things that navigate my shallow pools, armored mollusks dragging across my mud leaving tracks I instantly weep away, rapid burrowers emerging in the dimness to feed on mineral films and each other. They do not reclaim me; they never left the old contract with stone. I am their food, their bath, their grave; they flow around my scattered structures—those low circular walls half-submerged in black mud, those flat-stone cairns—leaving precise margins untouched, as if avoiding a presence I no longer have.

Sound is my architecture at 2.6. The weeping rock-faces, the subterranean thunder running through hollowed bedrock beneath my flat, the guttural chorus of my inhabitants—clicks, wet drums—each given too much room across my openness. Every noise bounces into absence.

Stranger marks exist at 1.4: waters that ripple inward, falling upward into hidden drains in air; stones that hum with low harmonics in my perpetual dampness; channels cut too straight for erosion’s chaos, now filled with still dark water. Primitive engineering softened into my body.

I am active—1.7—predator swallowing prey in my shallow black pools, constant motion, mist cycles that never become rain. But I am also settled. The erosion finished long ago. I am as flat as I will ever be, as old as the rock allows: open, sparse, dim, deafening, cool and wet and ancient. 

Not a creature. Not a place observed. I am the flatness itself—what remains when mountains finish forgetting, and life learns to thrive in what was left behind.
