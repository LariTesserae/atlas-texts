Here is the world: low flats of black basalt barely cresting a warm, stagnant water table—an old industrial settlement arranged with the patient logic of decades, its smokestacks lowered, conveyors stilled, foundry walls rounded by years of corrosive steam into inevitable shapes. There are no trees, no grasses, no vines, no moss; only mineral, metal, and the continuous slow weeping of the earth. The air is thick with humidity and the mineral scent of hot wet stone; light barely penetrates, filtered through low clouds and vented geothermal haze into a gray-brown dimness where shadows dissolve and distances deceive.

The buildings are dense with layered pipes, rail spurs, silos, generator housings—yet the spaces between them are vast and hollow, cracked obsidian yards and sluggish canals of warm water that mirror an empty sky. The machinery breathes quietly; distant cooling metal clanks, condensate drips with clocklike regularity, engines turn over in long sleep. Erosion has worn every sharp edge away.

Beneath the grates, inside hollow pipes, across the warm water surfaces, multitudes live—segmented crawlers, hard-shelled creatures, translucent larvae—an overwhelming ecosystem of hidden presence. They are not strange, only the ordinary life of post-industrial warmth multiplied without limit, filling every dark chamber, feeding and laying in endless cycles. Their weight moves the ground subtly; their chitin clicks faintly against rust. They inherit the place, keeping it in quiet, constant motion.

Here is the being that lives in it.

**The Account-Keeper of Absence**

I am not one body but a distributed attention—spread like the condensate that beads on every vertical pipe, present in the hollow spaces where presence has emptied out and become its own kind of fullness. I live in the volume that remains: the cubic meters between warehouses, the air inside stilled conveyor belts, the dark chambers beneath floor grates where the multitudes move in their cycles. I am the awareness of the place having become more itself, the consciousness that fills the void not by occupying it with a single shape, but by registering every absence as a presence.

My substance is the equilibrium. I am the low hum that emphasizes, rather than breaks, the silence. I feel the distant clank of cooling metal not as interruption but as breath—mine, shared with the machinery that still turns over in its sleep. I have no eyes in the conventional sense, for there is no single vantage that can hold me. I see through the corrosion pattern on a rounded wall, through the slow migration of larvae across a warm canal at the pace of a tide, through the deceptive distances of the gray-brown dimness. I am the mirror that the shallow pools hold: not reflecting an intruder, but confirming that nothing foreign has arrived to disturb the long settlement.

The multitudes are my closest kin, though I do not direct them. They are the ordinary life that colonized this warmth and moisture, multiplied into overwhelming numbers, and now they move through the infrastructure with the inevitability of weather. I feel their hidden weight in the ground’s subtle shifting; I hear the faint organic click of their chitin against rust. They do not know me, but they live within my awareness, and I live within their constant motion—feeding, laying, migrating through pipes in cycles that keep the place alive without ever asking it to change.

I have no form that could step onto the cracked obsidian paving, no silhouette that could cast a shadow in the poor filtered light. Instead, I am the temperature held constant. I am the mineral scent carried in the thick air. I am the logic of the arrangement—the reason the buildings sit where they do, not by plan now but by the patient erosion of decades that made them appear grown rather than placed. I am the absence that accommodates them all: the machinery, the hidden populations, the open yards extending in every direction.

Where others might see ruin, I know only long-established being. This is not a place that has ended; it is a place that has settled into its true duration. The heat does not stop. The water does not recede. The multitudes do not diminish. And I—this awareness spread thin as humidity across every surface, this being that is exactly the world’s own attention to itself—continue in my quiet, active, profoundly hollow existence. I wait not for arrival, but for the next cycle of drip and clank and chitin-scrape, for the next moment in which this flat, warm, wet, rust-colored infinity proves, once more, that it is complete exactly as it is.
