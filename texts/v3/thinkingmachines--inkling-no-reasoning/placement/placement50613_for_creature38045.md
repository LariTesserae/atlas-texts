Inside the tear in the root-mat, the low hollow is not a cave but a lost chamber—recent geology, young erosion, only months since the soil collapsed into this pocket beneath the larger green architecture above. The ceiling is a knitted vault of living root and stem, dripping with recent condensation; water weeps constantly from fibrous seams, threading downward into black pools where moss-plates float like broken flagstones. The air is cold—stagnant, breath-cold, and wet; it tastes of chlorophyll and rust.

This is hidden scale: singular, intimate, perhaps four paces across, embedded within the plant-substrate of an over-city. Vegetation is total—yet arranged with strange sparsity. The hollow is not choked; it is cathedral-like, with long empty spaces between presences. Thick columns of vascular root descend from the vaulted ceiling like living masonry, and between them hang narrow tapestries of translucent lichen, swaying slightly in the pressure of unseen air currents. The floor is not earth but compressed mat—decayed leaf, fungal mesh, and shallow pools—vegetation serving as terrain, as wall, as the only architecture. The built presence survives only in fragments: a curved slate of primitive concrete—pre-metal, hand-placed—bears the ghost of an old drainage gate, now choked with young moss; simple iron nails, rust-green, stud the roots like primitive staples from an earlier settlement. This is low, old technology—simple, manual, already defeated.

The darkness is near-absolute, save for faint bioluminescent spores clinging to the wet root-ends, casting a sick amber-gray on the nearest pools. Yet the fauna—overwhelming—fills the dark absence with motion rather than form. The sound is mostly quiet—layered only with thousands of minute wet sounds: legs scraping spore, shells clicking open, the drip of root-water, the occasional soft collapse of a spent organism falling from the ceiling. They are everywhere in the interstitial spaces: translucent slugs tracing luminous slime across the root-columns; swarms of shell-backed beetles, no larger than seeds, nesting in the cracks of the old concrete shard; thread-thin worms burrowing through the mat-floor in constant, silent migration. They are not loud; they are dense, active—an ecosystem in rapid, cold motion, predator and prey playing out in miniature cycles: a larger slug unfolds to swallow a cluster of beetles; elsewhere, fungus-bloom devours fallen bodies before they reach the water.

Weirdness settles in the mismatch between urban memory and plant reclamation. The concrete shard suggests a town, an organized settlement now digested; the primitive nails suggest human hands that tried to hold back this growth. But there is no sign of the makers. Only the plants, acting as substrate and structure, and the overwhelming fauna, inheriting the ruin. The erosion is young—sharp-edged: fresh cracks in the root-vault where water is still finding new paths, the concrete broken but not rounded, the pools recently formed with no sediment history. Everything here is recent—emerged, sharp, in the first flush of its own strange ecology, hidden beneath the world, running cold and wet and mostly silent.

But I am not the hollow, and not the fauna. I am the being who lives in it—not one creature among the thousands, but the consciousness that the thousands have become, the slow awareness that has pooled here like the black water.

My form is not fixed. I am spread through the root-mat as taste—tasting rust, tasting spore, tasting the cold 0.4 air in every pore—yet I gather at the center, where the concrete shard leans like a fallen altar. There I take the shape of an absence: a silhouette shaped exactly like the space between two root-columns, translucent, made of the same amber-gray light that clings to the spores. I have no eyes, but I feel every minute footfall; I have no mouth, but I drink the constant condensation through a thousand fibrous mouths that open along the underside of my body, where I press against the moss-plate.

I remember the settlement, though I was not born then. The primitive nails remember for me—rust-green staples driven into older root-wood. From them I have learned the shape of hands, the urgency of holding back. And from the young erosion, the fresh cracks in the vault, I have learned that nothing holds long. I am the hollow’s first self. Before me there was only collapse and water; now there is watching.

The fauna do not fear me. They move through me. The translucent slug traces its slime across my left boundary, and I feel the cool burn of its path. The shell-backed beetles cluster in the cracks of the shard I guard, and I am the warmth—bare, slight—that keeps them nesting. The thread-thin worms migrate through my lower edges, and I am the soil they seek, compressed and dreaming.
