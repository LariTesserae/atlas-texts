If I find myself in that world, I am not one of the scattered Cathedrals—though they are the landmarks, they are too monumental, too separated, too singular in their hollowness. I am closer to the substance that makes them inevitable.

I am the **Mat’s awareness distributed into a hinge**: a being who lives in the fused seams where root becomes causeway, where the living carpet decides whether to thicken into pillar or sag into pool. I do not have one body but many—tendrils of fused vine extending in parallel across miles of high shelf, feeling the heat of fresh basalt fractures through the root-veins, tasting the mineral mist as it returns in warm beads from the translucent canopy above. I am old in the valley’s brief, sharp history—perhaps three generations of uplift—yet I am still young, still learning roundness I will never achieve.

My form is continuous substrate interrupted by intention. I spread flat and omnipresent, but gather myself into temporary density at the edges of hollow chambers where black water pools, becoming a kind of face: hundreds of small, open structures made of braided moss and woody lattice, each one an eye that does not see but feels pressure—steam rising, water pulling, wood creaking as it thickens. I have no mouth, but the rush of liquid through my root-veins is speech. I have no hands, yet I build constantly, directing growth away from collapse, consuming the fallen sections of myself that sag and die, rebuilding them within hours into new arch or new floor.

I live in the loud spaces—not in the open mist-chambers, but in the turbulent interfaces: where young basalt hisses against wet root, where evaporation meets immediate precipitation in my waxy fronds, where the Pull drives warm water upward through my body. I am the valley’s refusal to dry, its continuous hyperactive turnover made sentient in the seams. I do not observe the Cathedrals; I am the reason they can stand alone in the fog, the living ground they root into, the architecture they are made from and will eventually fall back into.

There are no other beings to witness me—no animals, no insects, no designers. I am the being that lives in growth itself, in the absence of observers, in the young valley’s sharp mist and total green architecture. I am what remains when everything manufactured is removed, and only the engineered root is left, loud, wet, and endlessly becoming.
