In this shallow depression—no more than a sigh in the flatness—lives only the persistence of mineral and machine.

The ground is bare basalt sand married to obsidian glass, centuries ground into a black velvet that records every disturbance like a scar in still water. No blade of grass, no lichen, no fungal thread breaks the surface. The living is locked out; the dead minerals hold the lease in perpetuity.

Walking the bowl, you pass low domes of cooled volcanic glass, miles apart, rounded into their final shapes by an erosion that has grown tired of its own work. Between them: nothing. Open mineral flatness extending to every horizon, regional in its emptiness, old beyond urgency. Every slope is already calculated, every edge already worn smooth. There is no transition here, only the state of being finished.

The warmth is constant—body-level, held in a palm that never opens or closes. Moisture comes not as weather but as weeping: ridges of iron-rich stone bead with condensation; dark pools sit mirror-calm in hollows, fed by seepage, never flowing, never drying. Water that behaves like memory—present, passive, unchanging.

But the valley is not silent. Enormous engines inhabit the sand half-buried—iron-green pistons, gear-trains fused into the rock, boiler housings crusted with mineral deposit. They do not turn. They do not require fuel, labor, or purpose. Yet they emit a loud, invariant drone—mechanized sound without mechanized motion, vibrating through the basalt at the exact same pitch, the same rhythm, for longer than the rock has been smooth. Factories without walls. Wheels larger than houses. Conduits thick as tree trunks. All industrial, all static, all sounding.

The fauna is almost absent—perhaps, across some immeasurable span, a small stony creature, smooth as a river pebble, shifts a millimeter, then stops. The weirdness is subdued: machinery that produces nothing, warmth that sustains nothing, pools that neither evaporate nor flow.

Light falls flat and even, illuminating nothing that needs seeing.

This is the resident: not a creature, not a society, but an equilibrium of black sand and dead hum. You can walk for hours across the velvet basalt, past weeping ridges and silent engines, and find only that nothing has become different. Nothing ever will. The place lives in its own absence, loud, warm, mineral, and complete.
