Here, at the extreme edge of elevation—where the air is not given but held like a breath in a closed palm—the world collapses into a single chamber without ceiling. It is intimate: a cathedral of root and worn stone no larger than a hamlet, pressed flat against the summit designated 3. There is no horizon, only walls of dark moss that rise like the flanks of an impossible green city, dense with presence, layered and near-touching, shutting out every elsewhere.

The rock has finished its erosion. It wears its final, primordial face—smooth as bone polished to marrow, flattened and settled into deep-age stability. Nothing shifts quickly. Change moves on a patient clock, measured not in days but in centuries of root-growth and the slow migration of spores. Water is absent in every form—no dew, no running vein, no salt, no vapor—yet the vegetation thrives on absence alone, feeding on mineral fog and the warm, still air that pools in the hollow like a held breath.

The settlement is urban in its logic, though built entirely of living tissue and primitive craft. Towers of woven lichen stand in ordered rows, connected by bridges of braided vine and hand-laid stone, a vertical town suspended at the world’s top. The technology is simple—rope twisted by hand, shelves of pressed earth, tools of carved shell and soft copper—enough to organize, not enough to dominate. Every surface is occupied. The density is absolute: life upon life, moss over moss, insects threading through it all with relentless purpose.

Light comes faintly, filtered through the green architecture, leaving everything in warm shadow. Sound is loud, relentless, but never chaotic. The roar of a thousand small bodies—wings, legs, mandibles—fills the dimness with a constant, rumbling hum, a congress without end.

The fauna—abundant beyond counting—owns the space. They crawl, burrow, feed, and sleep in the layered walls, not as invaders but as the structure itself, part of the ecology, part of the wall. Here, at three, there are no predators and no prey, only the slow, loud, patient gathering of beings in a warm, dry, high place that reached its end-state long ago and decided, quietly, to stay.
