The Plate-World breathes wrong. Its air is bright enough to hurt, cold as dead space (temperature 0.1/3), and holds still—no wind, only thermal flatness over a surface that extends not in distance but in exhaustion (scale 2.6/3). The elevation (2.0/3) is not ancient; it is newborn, a thrust-block shoved upward with edges still raw, unrounded by time (erosion 0.9/3). There are no rivers, only moisture (water 1.1/3)—beads of condensation caught in spiraling horn-shells, frozen droplets hanging in the glare like suspended glass, veins of dampness threading through tissue rather than soil.

There is no rock, no dirt. The ground is root-mat—lush (vegetation 1.8/3), not green but silver, glowing faintly with analog chlorophylls that drink the relentless, merciless light (2.9/3). Density here is absolute (2.8/3). Every inch is claimed. The world is full—overfull—crowded with bodies that have erased the idea of emptiness. Built structures are nearly absent (built 0.3/3), yet this is no wilderness; it is a cathedral without an architect, a factory floor without walls.

What lives here are not separate species but a single, overwhelming presence (fauna 2.9/3)—millions upon millions of mechanized organisms, packed so tightly they form living geology. Each is a factory. Inside translucent bodies, gears of calcified song mesh with pistons of pearl-cartilage; membrane-lungs refine captured moisture into fuel, sound, motion. The technology (2.2/3) is not applied but grown—industrial purpose made biological, electrified by chemical thunder rather than wire.

The sound (2.1/3) never stops. It is not loud in the sharp sense, but layered—an endless choral drone of internal engines, wet grinding, the hiss of pressurized dampness converted to energy. Everything processes everything. The light reveals all of it: no shadows, no dusk, no refuge (2.9/3).

And the change is glacial (dynamic 0.8/3). In the cold (0.1/3), motion slows to epochal pace. A limb extends across centuries; an organ shifts production across millennia. They are strange (weirdness 2.5/3)—no faces, only functional surfaces; no voices, only seismic pressure waves felt through shared tissue. They do not hide. They do not sleep. They are present—absolutely, brightly, densely present—working slowly on purposes that outlast the very idea of an observer.

Step onto it, and you do not step on earth. You step into crowd, into mechanism, into a bright, cold, living plate still fresh from its birth, and still, in every packed, humming inch, refusing to be anything but full.
