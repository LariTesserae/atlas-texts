Here lives no one singular—no king in a throne of root, no singular intelligence directing the hollow. Instead, life is distributed, a shared tenancy between the mechanical and the vegetative, the slow and the immediate.

What lives here is the **rootsystem itself**—not as flora but as architecture with intent. The spongy plant-cylinders are the primary inhabitants, load-bearers that do not merely grow but *govern*. They form the streets and the walls, and in their hollow spaces nest the small, multi-eyed furred ones, silent decomposers and spore-carriers. These creatures do not dominate; they circulate. They live inside the buildings as organs live inside bodies, exchanging breath for shelter.

Then there is the **machinery-in-symbiosis**—the foundries and mycelium wires, the bark-cased engines that refine ore not through conquest but through digestion. The technology here is inhabited: pipes lined with tissue that heals like skin, conveyor vines that tense and relax as if feeling the weight they carry. The industrial presence is not an invader but a partner, powered by geothermal breath and compressed lignin, producing the cool, metallic ozone that flavors every exhalation of the cavern.

Above all, there is the **process of becoming**. The settlement is not finished; it is barely begun. The rock is still soft, eroding not downward but *opening*, releasing dry heat in geological sighs. The plants are constantly repairing, constantly grafting, turning stone into substrate. Even the insects—translucent, wingless, moving in streams—are agents of this emergence, carrying spores through districts that have no names yet.

What lives here, then, is a collective: a cool, dry, subterranean urbanity where vegetation and machine share a bloodstream of circulation and repair, where erosion is not decay but introduction, and where the inhabitants—root, creature, engine, spore—are all expressions of one continuous, growing thing deciding, in the 0.6-dark, what form it will eventually take.
