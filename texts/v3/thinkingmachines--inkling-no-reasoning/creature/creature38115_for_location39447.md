The hollow is barely old—months, perhaps a year—since the waters pulled back and left this low pocket scabbed with fresh geology. Gravel ridges still wear the knife-edges of recent erosion, their sharp lines softened only where moss, unnaturally eager, has already rushed in. The air carries a river’s dampness without rain: beads gather on black iron railings, on factory glass, on fern fronds that have colonized every crack between young cobblestones. Moisture pools in pockets—dripping steadily from one eave, then absent ten paces away—so the green comes in clumps: waist-high thickets separated by bare, young stone.

It is small, intimate: four curving streets, bounded by the crumbling lip of an old embankment and a canal lock that still breathes. Within these edges, industry has arrived with sudden confidence—brick warehouses with iron-truss roofs, a square smokestack exhaling gray rhythm, cobbled lanes where rusted tracks vanish under creeping vines. The settlement feels layered in real time; electric lines hang in loose loops between buildings, not humming but resting, patient.

What lives here holds back, leaving long corridors of empty air.

The vegetation is spatial, respectful—ferns rising dense in one quadrant, then yielding to open cobble. Broad-leafed weeds claim the gutters, finding fresh mortar with experimental roots. Moss does not blanket; it selects, wrapping certain walls in perfect spirals while leaving others bare, as if testing the surfaces. The sounds are constant but unhurried: the resonant churning of a water wheel that never stops, a distant gate clanking in slow repetition, the factory’s steam-release every several minutes like an animal exhaling. Beneath it all, the drip of condensation and the rustle of something large but hidden in the ferns.

Life moves in hidden abundance. Pigeons cluster on iron beams, but heavier, gray birds—slower, watching—roost in derelict loading bays. Insects flow through moss carpets in dark streams when the dampness peaks. Small mammals have tunneled into the fresh erosion banks, leaving neat holes that seem too large for the bodies that made them. The ecology is young, finding its rules in real time.

The strangeness is in the precision. Buildings lean not with age but with untested sharpness—corners exact, windows placed at intervals that feel calculated for a purpose no one remembers. Voices carry farther here than they should, returning with a half-second delay, as though the hollow itself is still learning its acoustics. Even the mechanisms respect slowness: the wheel turns in patient arcs, the gate lowers, pipes warm—measured in hours, never rushing.

This is a place of negotiation. Human masonry and eager green are separated by generous spaces of shadow and open sky, neither claiming the other quickly. The canal shifts almost imperceptibly, carrying leaf-drifts in long, patient curves. You can cross the neighborhood in minutes, yet it extends inward without boundary—cool, intermittently wet, loud with mechanical breath, and inhabited by everything that prefers to stay just out of sight.
