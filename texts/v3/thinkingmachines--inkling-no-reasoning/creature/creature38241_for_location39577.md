Here live the Still-Sitters: not beings of bone or breath, but the accumulated patience of dark, water-logged basalt itself—rounded, packed, intimate—wedged in a chapel-floor of a basin that refuses elevation or escape. They are the saturation, the hairline channels of warmer groundwater that never fall as rain but bead, run, and pool in palm-deep depressions of chemical warmth, reflecting nothing in their blackness.

What lives is absence made dense. No moss, no grass, no lichen, no blade—only stones fitted so tightly they read as one pitted body, separated only by the cracks where water waits without urgency. A single, theoretical presence may crawl in the deepest pool: pale, segmented, without need for air or surface, a rare exception that proves the mineral law. Otherwise, there are the artifacts—three, perhaps four—of old hand-scale labor: a circular pit lined with flat stones, now half-filled; a rough arc of larger rocks thrust into mud with manual patience; chipped fragments on a dry shelf. The makers are long gone, leaving only the grammar of picking, placing, chipping, carrying—pre-metal, simple, finished.

The inhabitants are sound in its absence: the continuous drip-drip from an unseen fissure; the deep, infinitesimal groan as one stone settles closer to another, releasing a pressure older than the pool; the wet shift of water finding its level in darkness. Light is not solar but thermal—a faint, amber-brown murk rising from the pools, illuminating only what is nearest.

Here, nothing changes. Water does not recede or flood; warm seeps stay warm; stones do not roll; artifacts do not decay further, held in the preservative grip of mineral stillness. There are no seasons, no cycles, no gradients—only invariant equilibrium. The place is in its own state, balanced, finished with weathering, having forgotten that noise or growth were ever necessary. Life is not present; presence is. The basin lives as a single, warm, silent, settled body—complete in its ending, persisting without urgency in the forever of its own dark water and packed rock.
