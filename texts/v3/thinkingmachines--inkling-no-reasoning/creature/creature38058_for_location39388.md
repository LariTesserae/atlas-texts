Here, the ground is not ground. You live suspended beneath a ceiling.

It caps the sphere at 1.6 elevation, not mountains reaching up, but land reaching down, flat and young and black-glassed, recently formed and permanently unfinished.

The temperature holds at 0.2: cold enough that sound refuses to fade, that molecules drift in obedient arcs, that every suspended drop is a crystal waiting for permission that never comes.

Erosion is 1.0: fresh, sharp, unfinished. The rock has not worn. The machines have not rusted away. The fauna have not evolved past their first form. Illumination is normal—2.0—diffuse, sourceless, filtering through fractures in the ceiling-rock where young stars burn far above, casting flat white-blue light through miles of suspended water. It does not flicker. The light is part of the stasis.

Weirdness is 0.9—strange, but not impossible. The rules are wrong only in their consistency: near-void filled with constant sound; abundant life that never grows; industry at 2.0 that never expands; cold water that neither freezes nor flows, beneath a sky made of hanging stone. Nothing conflicts. The contradiction itself is the architecture.

Scattered buildings—single black teeth hanging from the ceiling’s belly, occasional artifacts of steel and glass floating in the emptiness like seeds that will never sprout—are not ruins. They are fixtures, as stable as the drone.

This is the landscape: a vast, high, young, submerged hollow where the ceiling is earth and the floor is absence; where translucent colonies drift in eternal abundance and the machines chant in eternal maintenance; where the empty is dense with sound and the life is dense with stillness. Nothing—nothing—will ever erode away.
