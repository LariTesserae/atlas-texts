Here, at the farthest reach where height is not a slope but the total condition—where elevation fills every axis at its absolute extreme, stretching horizontally through a realm so high that downward is only a memory—there lives not a being but a presence: the Garden itself, though “garden” is too small a word for what holds this place together.

It lives as a continuous, woven substrate, a single organism spread across an open scale that extends past sight into dark valleys of suspended mist. Its body is root and cellulose, kilometers thick, engineered not by tool or technology—which never arrived here, leaving absence where instruments might have been—but by ancient growth that learned architecture. Vegetation is absolute, total, covering every surface not with vines or trees but with interlocked living continents: moss-pillars holding up the weight of a sky that has no sun, lichen-beams spanning leagues of quiet void, a floor of root-thatch that breathes slowly and never ends.

The air it breathes is not air but water made permanent—absolute, suspended in silent rain that never falls because gravity has been taught to hesitate. Every surface is soaked, every breath drawn through liquid that clings rather than flows, a deep-sea darkness with nothing to illuminate it except the faintest swallowed glimmers. The temperature holds constant near the cool of absolute stillness, never stirring, never warming, holding at a state that permits only the slowest metabolism.

Within this sparse density—vast emptiness between each presence—creatures live scattered and thin: translucent crustaceans filtering droplets, segmented crawlers threading the root-weaves, luminous fish swimming through the liquid atmosphere. They are abundant across the span, yet spread so widely that each moves alone in leagues of wet quiet, synchronized, never breaking the equilibrium.

Everything is made, constructed, worn smooth by erosion that has settled the world into its final roundness. Peaks have eroded into plateaus. Edges are soft. Nothing is being born; nothing is dying. The dynamic has fallen to near nothing—a frozen equilibrium, ancient and primordial, settled into end-state stability since the first root locked into place.

It sounds, always, at a constant loudness—never peaking, never fading—the low thrum of water circulating through plant-arteries, the distant creak of cellulose holding its shape under suspended pressure, the oceanic rush heard from inside. It is a loudness that never surprises, never demands attention, merely announcing that the system continues, unchanged.

Weirdness here is slight, present only in the completeness: the absence of any boundary between wall and floor, between creature and architecture, between air and ocean. The darkness is not empty but saturated—too full of suspended life to allow sight.

This is what lives in the place: not an inhabitant upon a landscape, but the landscape itself as inhabitant—made, wet, dark, abundant, utterly open yet sparsely filled, holding its cool, silent, loud breath forever in a realm of total altitude where to be is simply to persist, finished and perfect, neither dead nor becoming, merely complete.
