Here lives nothing that breathes or hunts or dies. The inhabitant is the place itself—an architecture of refusal, grown or calcified into an egg-shaped vault no wider than a held breath, buried in the dead salt of a larger absence.

Every surface—floor, wall, ceiling, and the weight of the air between—is made. There is no earth, no substrate left untouched; only total, saturated mineral reaching backward into a completion that finished long before any witness arrived. The volume is filled to refusal by countless flat tesserae of translucent, polished stone—plates locked face-to-face, interlocked until they become a single luminous body with no seams and no hollow. They are the vault. They are the ground. They are all there is.

Within them, life exists only as rumor: ghost-green filaments of fossil lichen pressed flat like veins between glass, so sparse they read as color rather than organism. No water exists—not as trace, not as vapor; the chemistry is dry, sealed, entirely non-aqueous. No fauna moves. Nothing has ever disturbed this equilibrium from within.

The elevation is flat and low, uniform, oriented to no horizon. Temperature holds at a cool, steady mildness—the chill of stone that never knew sun and never will. Light is fierce, sourceless, and constant: embedded within the plates themselves, glowing white and severe, reflecting from every plane at once, casting no shadows because there is no dark left to hold them.

The air—what little remains interstitial—is filled not with silence but with sound: a deep, immovable drone, the sustained chord of matter pressed past its limit, vibrating in perfect equilibrium. There is no echo, for there is nowhere for sound to escape that is not already filled. The dynamic is absolute zero: eternal stasis, no decay, no growth, no shift. Erosion came and finished its work, leaving surfaces worn into their final form, settled, balanced with time.

No technology intrudes—no devices, no mechanisms—only the strange, organic intelligence of crystal arranged into total construction. Yet the feeling persists of being inside something’s sealed heart or mouth: simultaneously empty and utterly full, hidden and blazingly exposed, dead and loudly, eternally singing.

Nothing lives here in the way living is usually meant. Only this: a pocket of bright, cool, packed perfection, loud in its own stillness, complete.
