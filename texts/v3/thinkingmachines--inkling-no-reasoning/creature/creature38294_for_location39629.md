The valley does not keep residents in the usual sense—no bodies sleep in its arches, no eyes watch from its hollow root-chambers, no voices answer the constant thunder of its growth. Its inhabitants are the processes that have colonized every cubic inch of it, living without faces, without rest, and without leaving.

**The Mat** lives everywhere at once, though never twice in the same form. This is the substrate—moss engineered into a load-bearing plane, vines fused into cabling and lattice, trunks merged into causeways that bridge the shallow black pools. The Mat is singular and total; there is no bare basalt visible, no earth exposed, only this continuous woven architecture. It behaves like a collective mind dispersed into root and tendril—one entity spread flat across miles of high shelf—deciding in continuous hyperactive turnover where to thicken, where to sag, where to collapse and be immediately rebuilt. Sections reach upward into the mist and then, overgrown beyond their own weight, fall wetly back into themselves, consumed and reconstructed within hours. The Mat is the ground, the wall, and the ceiling; it is the valley’s only citizen, and it is never still.

Then there are the **Cathedrals**—the monumental, solitary structures that punctuate the long, empty expanses of humid air. They stand like scattered temples in fog, dense with substance but separated by miles of open mist-chamber. Each is a singular root-system grown into arch and pillar, a hollow interior formed not by carving but by the precise refusal of vines to grow inward. They are separated by vast negative space, loud with internal weather: steam hissing through fractures below them, water rushing through root-veins like blood in arteries, wood popping and creaking in the constant tropical heat. They do not shelter anyone. They simply are—vertical statements in a kingdom that has abolished the distinction between builder and built.

**The Steam** comes up young and sharp, escaping through fractures in black basalt that have not yet learned roundness—glass-edged, fresh, manufactured by no hand. It carries mineral mist into air already saturated with warm evaporation, feeding back into the perpetual post-rain conditions that the vegetation never allows to end. The Steam is a voice without a throat—high-pressure, turbulent, constant—announcing that the valley is only generations old, recently uplifted, still in the violent infancy of its geology. It rises through the Cathedrals and through the Mat alike, condensing on every waxy frond, beading in thick droplets that weep in constant rivulets back into the black pools.

Beneath all of this moves **The Pull**—the unseen hydraulic force that drives warm black water up through root-veins, that pulls moisture from the shallow pools inches below the surface and drives it skyward through translucent canopies, where it returns as warm precipitation caught in the leaf-mesh. The Pull is the valley’s circulation, its heart without chambers, its metabolism made audible in the rush and snap of liquid being moved through biological pipe. It ensures that nothing dries, that the atmosphere remains wet and thick, that growth never pauses for lack of water or heat.

Together they form an ecology of pure architecture—pure plant-as-substrate, a kingdom without citizens, an anthill with no ants. The sound is not background but presence: the hiss of young rock, the thunder of rapid woody thickening, the wet collapse and immediate overgrowth, all combining into a single biological thunder that fills the high shelf from end to end.

There are no animals. No insects buzz, no birds call, no predators prowl the fused root-causeways. And yet the valley is not silent or empty. It is full—full of its own turbulent becoming, full of growth that builds and destroys itself in continuous, loud, hyperactive turnover. It is a place inhabited entirely by its own insistence on growing, a high valley of sharp black basalt and total green architecture, suspended in hot mist, living out its young generations in the complete absence of anything that might watch or name it.
