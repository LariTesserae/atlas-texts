The high land is still being argued into place.

Not mountains, not yet—not in the settled, named sense. They are upthrust ribs of warm obsidian and matte-black basalt, rising to near-sky, scattered across a dark plane like the first bones of a world that has only just agreed to possess a surface. Elevation is extreme—around 9.5, reaching high—yet there is nothing old enough to have earned a summit. These ridges are embryonic, raw geology made visible: spires that never knew ice, valleys that have not collected dust long enough to be valleys, only absence happening between two rising things. There is almost no erosion. There is only emergence—slow, then abrupt, then slow again—like thought interrupting itself. The ground is newborn mineral, glass-cool in some hollows, warm-vented in others, breathing faint heat into a darkness that holds no stars, only the faint orange glow of magma seams threading the ridges like veins in unfinished flesh.

Water is gone, or was never present. Not a trace—no river scars, no salt pan mirror, no frozen grief, no chemical memory of rain. The chemistry runs dry, non-aqueous, at 9.8 in its absence, sustained on heat and pressure and the slow conversation of stone with itself. Temperatures hold near 2.3, a persistent warmness that rises from within rather than falling from above, a body temperature for a body not yet named.

In the open spaces—yes, open, regional, extending past sight into a dark continuum—the air is thin, sharp with sulfur and metallic vapor, but mostly it is empty. Density is low, around 1.8, sparse to the point of loneliness. You can traverse—if traversing meant anything here—for miles between one presence and another. The darkness dominates. Light hovers at 0.4, a weak bioluminescent smear along the magma seams, an occasional phosphor-spill from fresh fissures. Most of the world is shadow, not black exactly, but the absence of claim. The sky, if the word applies, is a pressure of deep violet-dark, an atmosphere still deciding whether to exist, turbulent with invisible motion that never resolves into weather.

And it is turbulent—not noisy, but restless. Sound is mostly quiet, near 1.2, a low resonant hum beneath the crust, the occasional deep crack of cooling glass contracting miles away, like bones shifting in sleep. No thunder, because there is no weather to make it. The chaos is geological, internal, Solaris-like in its patient violence: the land is creation and destruction held in the same breath. Sections of the plain lift inches in an hour, or drop suddenly into chasms that have not yet learned to be canyons. Spills of liquid rock move with purpose and immediate forgetfulness, hardening into new structures before they finish being liquid. The world is hyperactive in its bones, ant-like energy without the hill—pure dynamics, constant low-frequency becoming.

What lives? Almost nothing recognized. Fauna is 0.2—absent. No animals, no flight, no tracks in dust because there is no dust. Vegetation is barely present, near 0.5—not plants, not fungi, not anything with roots and a will to grow upward. There might be faint mineral growths on the warm vent-faces, crystalline hair that does not feed, does not reproduce in any language we carry, only holds form as form experiments with holding. Life here is pre- or non-; it is chemistry admiring itself in silence.

Built things are scattered, faint, sparse—around 1.2. Not cities. Not tools, for there is no tech, none, 0.2—pure absence of device. What remains are occasional artifacts, single structures or partial marks: a circle of blackened pillars that might have been arranged, or might be the accidental architecture of a particularly neat eruption. A flat plane of fused glass half-buried and half-exposed, like a floor that forgot its walls. A low wall that stops and starts with no reason, as if interrupted by a thought. Nothing functional. Nothing maintained. They sit in the open space like questions that arrived before the vocabulary to ask them.

The weirdness is present but restrained, near 0.6—unusual, not impossible. The strangeness lives in the simultaneity: extreme height and extreme youth; turbulent change and mostly quiet; open scale and extreme sparsity; persistent warmth without a star to grant it permission. The world behaves like an idea in the middle of thinking itself—unstable, warm, high, dark, still emerging from its own raw decision.

You do not enter it; you witness it being made. The elevation does not invite ascent so much as it announces that ascent is still being defined. The air smells of electricity and cooling iron. In the distance—always distant, for scale is open and density refuses to cluster—another spire might be rising right now, or falling, or deciding not to exist yet. Here, being is not finished. Even the absence is young.
