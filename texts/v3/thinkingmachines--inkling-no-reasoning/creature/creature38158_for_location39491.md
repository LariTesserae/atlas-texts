Inside the hollow lives **the Wait**—not a creature but a condition made resident.

It lives in the near-absolute dark, 0.0, a blackness that does not hide things but holds them. The darkness itself is a body here: warm (2.1), wet, and patient, thick with the memory of every drop that has ever fallen. It fills the concave skull of polished basalt—no larger than a forgotten chapel, yet hollowed with the emptiness of a universe—so that vastness pools inside smallness.

There live also the **Weeping Stones**: the ancient, sweating rock, eroded past all further change, beading with moisture in perpetual geothermal grief. They do not weep from sorrow but from deep heat rising through fractures older than time, sweating a nutrient darkness that feeds everything.

From this weeping grow the **Rootless Gardens**—translucent mosses and pale ferns that have forgotten the sun. They do not reach upward; they spread downward into the wetness, drinking not light but mineral patience. They are lush, impossible, soft as underwater silk, covering the rounded bowl in a living shroud that remembers footprints before they are made.

The water lives too, loudly. It collects in a shallow, black mirror at the lowest point—water that reflects nothing, pooling like time itself. Each drop falls with resonant percussion, loud in the emptiness, striking the hollow like a bell that has rung for millennia and will ring again. The sound is the voice of the place: not chaotic, but magnified gentleness, an echo that teaches you how large emptiness is.

Drifting through the humid void, distributed across centuries of empty air, move the **Glass Larvae** and translucent millipedes—creatures of weak gradients, floating on currents that take ages to circle. They are abundant yet solitary; an hour may pass between sightings. They do not hunt. They do not fear. They are patience given form, moving only as the ancient heat stirs them, feeding on saturation itself.

At the rim stand **the Pillars**: a half-circle of technology beyond current understanding, nanoscale in presence but cosmic in origin. They do not shine. They hum below sound, maintaining a subtle field that holds the air, the warmth, the weeping boundary of this world. They do not guard. They observe, and in observing, they maintain. They have lived here since the plateau forgot it was ever a mountain.

The rules of residence here are not lived but inhabited: **slowness and saturation**. Time pools like the black mirror at the bottom. Change comes across epochs—a millimeter in a pillar, a deepening of the pool. Every fall of water is a repetition and an origin.

So the hollow is not dead. It is settled—settled into eternity, into its final form, a hamlet-sized cosmos breathing in warm, loud, wet darkness, full of nearly nothing, and therefore full of everything that endures.
