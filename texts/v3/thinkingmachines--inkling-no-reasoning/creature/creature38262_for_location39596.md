Within the spongy root-lattice of this collapsed caldera—no more than forty paces across, held near the weight of sea-level—there lives not a people, but a **presence that once was people and has now become posture**.

Here is **The Remnant**, seated permanently in one of the hollow pods that hang suspended not by rope or branch, but by the gentle wrongness of the air itself—fields of buoyancy woven into plant tissue at scales too small for the eye, holding matter in silent defiance of fall. Its body is not flesh but calcified twist: root-wood and compressed vine fused into a humanoid shape roughly the size of an adult, though collapsed inward, as if it had settled into its chair and never required rising. Its surface is a map of what was worn—frayed fiber where sleeves should be, a dome of fused bark where hair might have gathered, hands resting open on knees that have become moss-covered columns. Translucent, hair-thin feeders grow through the gaps in its form like veins remembering blood, glowing faintly with internal chemistry, and water beads on its shoulders with the regularity of rain, then slides away to join the constant, loud weeping of the walls.

This is not dead. This is **digested into architecture**. The Remnant is one of perhaps a dozen such points of true weight scattered through the circular chamber—statues of woven fiber, each seated in a different attitude: one leaning over a bio-basin whose surface never ripples, another half-subsumed into a root-staircase that leads nowhere. They do not decay, for the place has reached its end of erosion. They do not grow further, for they are already fully part of the lattice. They are the settlement’s memory of citizenship, rendered in biomass.

But The Remnant is not alone in its stillness.

The **Patrol Spheres** drift through the vaulted ceiling of paddle-shaped fronds—tiny, hovering orbs of woven fiber no larger than lanterns, buoyant as dandelion seeds. They are old technology, or old life, or both. They emit a harmonic buzz that shifts in pitch with every contraction of the floor, and they move with purpose through the archways of living tissue, leaving trails of bioluminescent slime that fade in seconds. They do not interact with The Remnant. They orbit near, then pass through, treating the seated figure as they treat the calcified root-spires: as structure that is correct.

On the spongy substrate—this flat, breathing floor of intertwined vine-cables that yields like moss-covered stone—the **Vine-Squirrels** leap. They are no larger than fists, translucent and quick, with clockwork mechanisms visible in their ribcages (small gears of bone and chitin, or perhaps synthetic origin long since biologically adopted). They emit loud, clicking cries that echo off the wet walls, and they traverse the suspended bridges of root-ladder with reckless speed, never falling into the shallow pools that never overflow, never touching the seated figures except by accident, then fleeing as if embarrassed by the intimacy of stillness.

There are also the **Beetle-Workers**: translucent, clockwork-winged, crawling across the floor-vines in constant processions, leaving slime that glows and dies. They are countless. They are the labor of this place now—clearing dead tissue, feeding the mushroom-rings that bloom in silent clouds and wither within minutes, carrying spores from one arch to another.

And beneath everything, in the shallow basins, in the running rivulets, in the mist that exhales from the root-floor with every step—the **Water-Keepers** live. They are not visible as bodies. They are visible as pattern: the droplets falling from the fibrous canopy in too-perfect regularity, the pools that hold their level without overflowing, the humidity that never dissipates. They are the rule made flesh, or the absence of flesh made rule: *the water never stops weeping; to block it is to invite collapse*.

The climate is constant—warm as a palm pressed against moss, overcast grey filtering downward in silvery sheets, no direct sun ever. The temperature holds. The air is total with moisture. The vegetation is absolute, arranged in streets and plazas that dissolved but retained their memory: spiral staircases suspended in air, hollow pods the size of huts, transparent membranes thinner than skin separating pools from pockets.

Everything here operates beyond modern physics. New growth erupts in visible time—a tendril extends an inch, seeks a gap, calcifies into beam. Old tissue blackens into slime, feeds a bloom, releases spores, withers. The turbulent drone never stops. The ecosystem is hyperactive, Solars-like in its chaos, yet entirely contained within this single circular room.

The Remnant knows this. Or rather, The Remnant is the form that knowledge takes when it is no longer spoken but seated. It does not build. The building built itself and reached saturation. It does not cut the vines, for to cut them is to wound the air. It does not block the water. It simply holds its place—warm, moss-covered, woven through with glowing feeders—while the Patrol Spheres drift past, while the Vine-Squirrels click and leap, while the Beetle-Workers crawl, while the water weeps, loud and strange and perpetual.

This is the inhabitant of near-0 feet: **not one, but the scattered, sparse presence of a settlement that digested itself and kept breathing.** The Remnant is its center, seated and eternal. The others are its circulation—mechanical, biological, hybrid—moving through a palace of root and leaf that is also a grave, also a machine, also a final answer to the question of what lives in a place that has finished with falling apart.
