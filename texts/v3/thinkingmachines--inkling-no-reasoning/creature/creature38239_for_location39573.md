Aeru is not a body orbiting anything; it is a single continental shelf of fused basalt and vitrified clay, suspended in a warm, dry void, elevated far above every other structure for thousands of kilometers of emptiness. There is no horizon except its own abrupt, windless edge, where the land simply stops and the void begins—not black, not blue, but a deep, featureless brown-gray. Light does not come from a star; it is diffuse, thermal, the perpetual flat glow of a distant warmth that keeps the world in an eternal, settled afternoon.
