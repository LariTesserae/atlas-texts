At the summit of a newborn world, where the stone has only just been thrust into sky and has not yet learned patience, there is a hollow no larger than a cathedral’s heart. Its walls stand sharp and vertical—black basalt, freshly heaved, edges like drawn blades that no wind or long water have had time to round. Here is the ceiling of everything: air so cold it lies just above frost’s ache, space enclosed and intimate despite an elevation that should open into infinite abyss. Flat, merciless light—blinding, near-white, relentless—falls into this stone pocket with the weight of a physical presence, erasing shadow, leaving only glare and the texture of rock.

Water lives here in fragments, never running freely. It is intermittent life: condensation beading in cold beads on dark basalt; ice-needles that come as rain and vanish into cracks; dampness held prisoner in moss-thick crevices. The moisture is scant—yet it is enough.

Because of it, the vegetation is impossibly lush. Between the knife-sharp stones, thick cushions of emerald moss and silver-backed lichen pool in the seams; dwarf shrubs cluster in protected corners, their leaves turned like small mirrors to the cruel brightness; thin, patient vines hang in ropes from overhangs, drinking the mist. The growth is dense but low, a soft breathing floor laid across a hard shell, hugging the ground in the interstices, leaving the air and stone in spacious absence. Near-void, but not dead—waiting, hollow as a lung before breath.

The bowl’s life moves in constant, quiet motion, abundant but rarely seen all at once. Small scaled creatures with crystalline eyes warm themselves on flat, sun-heated rock, their bodies absorbing the flat light. Flocks of high-altitude insects with translucent wings rise in silent clouds at the slightest shift in temperature, a brief blizzard of near-invisibility. Something with feathered limbs burrows through the moss, leaving only the tremor of its passage—predator and prey here moving in a slow, cold metabolism, precise, always active even in stillness, navigating this bright silence with adaptations still being tested.

The marks of presence are scattered, primitive, almost accidental: stacked stones that might be cairns or might be the work of frost; a shelter of flat rock slabs fitted without mortar, half-collapsed, returning already to geology; worn bone, chipped flint, ropes woven from tough highland grass—technology of the hand, not the machine. Someone was here, passing through, leaving traces that the young, sharp erosion is already reclaiming.

The contradictions are the atmosphere itself. Extreme height, yet enclosed and room-sized. Blinding illumination, yet cold that bites without mercy. Lush green life threading through near-void. Abundant creatures heard only as the absence of sound—just a drip, the breath of something sleeping, and the deep silence that holds them.

The air smells of wet stone and crushed moss. Condensation gathers, runs, freezes, runs again. Everything is recently arrived: the heaved rock, the adapting plants, the creatures testing whether they can survive with this little water, this much light, this profound aloneness. This place has just become itself—not settled, not worn smooth, but fresh, sharp, active. A bright, damp, living hollow at the very top of the world, still deciding what it will become.
