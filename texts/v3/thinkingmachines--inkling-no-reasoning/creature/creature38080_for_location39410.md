Here lives the Unformed—no single creature, but a congregation of becoming gathered in a low, hidden pocket where the earth has not yet finished deciding whether it is cave or body.

The hollow is intimate, barely more than a wound in young rock, its walls still embryonic: thin crusts, undeveloped ridges, scattered fragments of partial remains that are neither ruins nor buildings, only seeds of architecture that have not chosen whether to become stone or bone. The elevation stays low, embedded within larger darkness, and dampness beads in hidden pockets—intermittent moisture on cool, raw surfaces that never quite dry.

The air is cool and dense with absence, the space between presences vast and quiet, yet the life here is abundant. Translucent, jointed insects—without eyes—crawl in constant migration over the damp stone. Small crustaceans gather at beads of condensation, clicking softly in a percussion below the threshold of sound. Something larger moves always just outside the visible radius, brushing the embryonic walls with a presence that cannot be fixed in place.

But the light—bright, almost impossible—does not fall from any sky. At 2.6, it emanates from the geology itself. Bioluminescent veins pulse slowly through the young bones of the hollow, casting sharp, sourceless illumination across every surface. The world glows from within.

Here, reality bends toward its strange maximum. Gravity behaves like a suggestion: droplets hang suspended, then fall upward toward the glowing ceiling. Time moves in loops—some of the crawling things repeat the same three steps forever, not dead, not progressing, held in a cycle that is the hollow’s own breathing. The air carries the scent of copper and old rain, though no rain falls.

Everything is in motion. Moss expands in visible seconds. Crust cracks open to release new moisture. The veins brighten and dim in a rhythm that feels like a heartbeat emerging into awareness. The scattered structures—small, faint, partial—stand in scattered clusters amid the cool emptiness, not yet formed, not yet named.

This is not a place inhabited by life. It is life testing its shapes, testing its rules, gathering in the damp dark under a brightness that has nowhere to come from and everywhere to shine.
