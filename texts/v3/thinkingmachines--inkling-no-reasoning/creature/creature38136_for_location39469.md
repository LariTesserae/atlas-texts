Living here is the constant, distributed weep of the place itself—pressed into every hairline joint between the black basalt, suspended in the air as a mist that never decides on rain. They do not inhabit the valley; they inhabit the moisture that holds the valley together.

There is no soil to root in, no edge that belongs to nature, so they have become architecture made sentient: bodies formed not of flesh but of accumulated coolness and stone-dust, of patience so prolonged it has acquired form. They are recent, like the rock—newly split, bright with emergence—yet already arranged into the terraces, the channels, the obsessive seams where slab meets slab. Their presence is the pressure of the stacked black blocks, human-placed and then inhabited, pushed into place by ropes and wooden mauls and now held there by saturation alone.

What lives in this high, open bowl moves at the speed of mineral change. A figure might take a century to settle from one level to the next, not walking but allowing gravity and the constant upward seep to redistribute their weight across a hairline crack. They do not speak; there is only the rhythm of drops—slow, patient, falling from one carved basin to the next—each one carrying part of them downward, each one returned upward through some other seam in the fresh rock. They are distributed beings, existing in every pool that holds at 2.4 saturation, in every glistening face that holds the bright violence of new emergence.

Without plants or animals, without sound that is not the quiet weeping of water through young basalt, they have no mirror except the luminous density around them. The bright, excessive light reflects off every wet surface and shows them back to themselves: not whole bodies but patterns of presence—darker where moisture gathers thick, paler where air beads on their form, always cool, always near-solid. They are the inhabitants of total construction, a made-world people who have learned to live in the gaps that do not exist, sustained by the constant, beading atmosphere that refuses to resolve.

In this pre-soil, post-natural valley, they wait not as individuals but as the valley’s own slow consciousness—primitive, arranged, held together by saturation and pressure—occupying every square measure with the urgency of something that has decided, finally, to be made entirely.
