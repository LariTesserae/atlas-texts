In this darkness—absolute, wet, optically dense, refusing light rather than lacking it—there lives a single continuous ecosystem of saturation and sound. The place itself is the inhabitant: 2,800 meters of uplifted crust that weeps mineral-black tears into mirrorless, oscillating seas, breathing slowly with something buried far below the root-mat.

Above and through and within it moves an aggressively overlapping architecture of life—ferns towering over human scale, lichens glowing with faint failed luminescence, root systems stitched into living mats that hold the soil. And through this dense biological weave, like seeds scattered across a graveyard, stand the rust-crowned machines: slender, tall, half-submerged, their gear-boxes already colonized by moss. They do not govern the place; they participate. Hydro-mechanical turbines turn with the eternal seepage, gears lubricated by black slime, exhaling warm chemical breath into air that is always condensing, always exhaling after some hidden combustion.

The fauna is near-touching in its abundance—segmented insects glistening with collected moisture, pulsing in synchronization with turbine rotation; larger warm-bodied creatures moving in packs through stalk and pipe, navigating entirely by vibration and low resonant call, adapting to the loudness, feeding near the water that serves both plant and engine. Predator and prey share rules of darkness, density, and proximity to the black pools.

Here, erosion is maintenance, smoothing the new into the old in smooth gullies like throats and palms. The sounds—water on metal, grinding turbines, sub-bass crustal shifts, animal calls—compete in a mechanical-biological ecology. Electricity jumps in dangerous localized arcs, blue and brief, revealing contours of machinery become geological, then mythic.

What lives here is not separate entities but a porous continuity: vegetation inside engines, corrosion patterns resembling circuitry, warm thick air that behaves like an eternal breath. The plateau has forgotten it was ever worked; the factories have forgotten they manufactured. What remains lives in circular migration patterns around scattered engine-sites—dense, warm, wet, loud, dark, vast enough to birth its own storms, strange enough that growth and rust share a single language, and alive enough that even the weeping stone seems to be part of the conversation.
