Inside: a maintenance chamber embedded in an enormous, young industrial spine—an access pocket, not a room. The build is absolute steel, copper, and gray enamel: a curved riveted floor, faceted walls, every surface sharp-edged and fresh-cut, holding the bite of recent manufacture. Temperature hovers at cool, just above brittle; air is thick with saturation, beaded on rivets and weeping along channels.

Moisture is assigned and running: clear tubes, shallow gutters, constant drip toward humming grates. A single green filament clings to a hairline crack. No other life.

Sound is total—3.0—turbines and gears and high-pressure steam creating an unbroken roar that fills the pocket and presses on the chest. Light is near-dark: weak amber bulbs, occasional blue arc-flashes. Technology is heavy and scaled—pumps, cracked gauges, belts vanishing into dark chutes.

Space is sparse and active. Water flows, metal vibrates, machinery never sleeps. The place is young, processed, explicable, and utterly alone—except for the relentless, manufactured thunder that is both its walls and its only inhabitant.
