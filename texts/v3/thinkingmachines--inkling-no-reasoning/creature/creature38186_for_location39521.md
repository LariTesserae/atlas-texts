At the center—if center can be said of such an open, extending bowl—there is an **aggregate hum-being**, not one creature but the summed authority of every voice pressed together: the million-segmented wing-beats, the clicking amphibian streams, the violet tone of the pillar, the silver weep of the arch, all resolving into a single, deafening presence with a mouth that never closes. Call it the **Bureaucrat of Becoming**—not malevolent, not even conscious in any human weight, but administratively inevitable. Where a rock splits in the cold, the hum-being has already filed its dissolution; where moss climbs a glistening flank hours after the split, the record is updated with no error, no strangeness, a weirdness of absolute zero.

Around it, or through it like threads in a saturated cloth, live the **weeping fauna**. There is no solitary hunter or prey, only layers—winged forms with segments that do not end, glistening hand-sized amphibians moving in constant rivers across the wet-black ground, and insects dense enough to be a second weather. They do not rest; they negotiate. Each creature lives at the exact boundary of digestion and rebirth, for the valley is young, only six hundred years open, its walls still carrying the raw, unsoftened edges of recent emergence. Nothing here has finished becoming.

The **built things** also live, though whether they are artifacts or organisms or both is a distinction the local rules do not require. A pillar of impossible geometry glows violet at nanoscale depth; a partial arch of unrusted metal hums in silver, holding moisture against gravity with bureaucratic precision. They are being slowly eaten by the lushness—black-leaf vines, spore-wool that layers upon itself in pressing abundance—while simultaneously rebuilding themselves atom by atom. They are residents, too, embedded in the marsh’s thermal upset, obeying local physics that current understanding can only observe, never violate.

And then there is the **marsh**, the ground, the valley-bowl itself—Korr-Vhel—living as substrate and system together. Light is near 0.4, but darkness is the wrong word; illumination comes from within, from bioluminescence and wrong-colored glow, green pulses and violet hums, guiding the constant traffic.

The ground dissolves and reforms beneath any footstep; vines grow feet in an hour; rocks form in a week and split in the next. There is no silence, no absence, no error.
