Here, beneath a crust so young the sky is still a rumor, lives a hollow that has not yet decided to be a cave. It is vault-sized—no larger than a grove, no deeper than shallow forgetting—carved from rock still brittle with birth, sharp-edged, dark, and dry-fevered.

What lives here is contradiction made patient.

There are plants—surprisingly lush for all that darkness—though they live scattered and alone. Thick, rubbery ferns and broad moss colonies thread through rusted rails, blooming in faint, phosphorescent clusters near shallow pools of black water. They do not grow together; they maintain wide, silent distances across bare basalt floors, a sparse green presence—never forming a forest, only insisting on being alive where life seems uninvited. They lean not toward light—there is barely any, only embers far away—but toward the dead machines, as if listening.

The machinery lives, but only in afterlife. Half-buried turrets, iron gears frozen in slime, copper rails ending in nothing, a rusted pump still weeping condensation into trays. These are not functioning; they are settling, expanding and contracting with the constant heat, producing the only sound in the place: a faint whisper, like sand shifting, like breath held. They are scattered artifacts, not a factory, embedded with intent long forgotten.

And then the emptiness—vast between every living thing. No animals. No insects. No flight, no scratch. Only moisture, intermittent and patient, beading on cool metal and dripping into carved basins without rippling. The air tastes of mineral and old steam, never quite wet, never dry.

The strangeness is gentle but wrong. The rock bears geometric scoring that is neither natural nor fully made. The plants grow toward silence. The water accepts droplets without disturbance. Everything moves at thermal speed—rust, drip, root—unhurried across human time.

This is a room recently formed, barely weathered, enclosed and hot, inhabited solely by green patience and iron memory. A place that forgot the sky but remembers being born; a hollow where life, sparse and lush, waits without hunger, without flight, in the long, quiet dark.
