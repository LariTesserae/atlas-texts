Inside the compressed absence, where basalt caps a vault turned like an inverted lung, lives a civilization of stillness—an inside-out hamlet scaled to one continuous pocket, intimate enough that every wall touches every other in an unbreathable circuit. Here, water is grammatically absent; chemistry proceeds only through dry abrasion and thermal memory, leaving the air with zero humidity and an absolute salt that forbids dampness from ever being conceived.

The walls are young, sharp-featured, still bearing crystalline anger—perhaps collapsed, perhaps arranged with intent—packed not with soil but with dense presence: mineral fleece, mechanical scab, faint industrial residue thicketed so tightly that the architecture itself wears a crust of occupation. Light is dim, mechanical, reluctant; amber and iron-red seep from scattered machinery, small engines and half-formed factory fragments locked in arrested intent. Pipes hang like dead nerves ending in valves that will not turn. Among them, one squat cylindrical housing remains more intact, its faceplate scored with unreadable numerals, emitting a constant low thrum—tech present but halted, scaled only to this room, consuming nothing, producing nothing.

The inhabitants are everywhere, dense beyond counting, adhering rather than crawling, since no medium carries them. They press flank-to-flank across every vertical plane and carpet the floor in near-touching multitudes: small, dry, segmented like clockwork larvae cast in bone and dull pewter. They do not move but assume attitude—microscopic shifts of orientation, then holding, then holding again. There is no birth visible, no death, no migration—only a stable saturation of being. They cluster around the scattered engines not for warmth—the temperature holds a constant body-warm containment, never spiking, never fading—but for proximity to stillness, as if machinery and creature obey one unchanging contract.

Vegetation does not grow but is deposited: brittle ochre lichen in thin crusts, desiccated filaments that ignore light, scattered tufts of gray mineral hair breaking at a touch, occupying leftover angles without competing.

Sound is total and invariant—not echo but filled space. A low-frequency mechanized drone layers over the high, dry clicking of countless bodies rearranging in unison, never advancing, never retreating; there is no silence, only an unchanging volume.

Nothing erodes past its present state. The young rock keeps its sharp edges; the engines rust no further than they already have rusted; the fauna neither thin nor swell. This is an equation already solved—industrial, biological, mineral forces collapsed into shared stasis, loud and warm and permanently strange. To be here is not to watch a process unfold, but to stand inside a finished breath held forever.
