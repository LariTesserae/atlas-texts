Its elevation is absolute—2.4 high—though “ground” does not exist. Vegetation is not growing upon a surface; it is the surface, the architecture, and the air. Density, however, is nearly void—0.5. For every cubic volume of this continental chamber, there is almost nothing. There are no rivers; instead, water migrates through the plant-tissue itself, collected in translucent bladders embedded in the walls, dripping back into the mesh. The weirdness—2.7—does not announce itself with spectacle but with wrongness scaled up. Time is tissue-deep; some parts of the wall are centuries old and have become translucent, allowing one to see the dim glow of distant vaults through layers of plant-skin. The reality here is layered, constructed, then constructed again by the plants themselves, which have continued the builders’ logic without them.

And the emptiness—0.5 density—is populated.

There are larger presences—things that hang in the dark spaces between vaults, suspended in the humid air, feeding on spores, on condensation, on each other. They do not roar; they are part of the silence, breaking it only with the soft sound of membrane unfolding, of a mouth—if it is a mouth—opening against a leaf.

The rules of this world are those of perpetual, manual maintenance made autonomous. The plants are substrate, architecture, and weather. The water is internal and weeping. The built environment has become ecology; the ecology is architecture. Nothing is wild because nothing was ever wild—yet it lives, breathes, and slowly moves. The emptiness is not absence; it is the necessary hollow through which this vast, warm, dim, silent, constructed organism circulates its own breath.
