The valley is not old. There has not been time for oldness. Water stands everywhere in shallow, dark pools—never draining, never rising—over beds of gray mud that are themselves still deciding whether to be soil or stone. The air is warm and saturated, hanging lower than breath should, condensing on every surface in fine, constant beads that never run or evaporate. No plants have arrived. No roots split the ground, no moss softens the angles. Only mineral and the dense, layered architecture of a settlement that seems to have grown not upward but downward, pressed into the marsh by its own weight.

This is a pre-formative place, raw-geology given just enough structure to hold form. The ground is dense with presence—walls of fired clay and stacked flat stone, low vaults and circular kilns, channels half-carved, foundations left open to the wet—occupying every flat patch of the valley floor in a continuous urban weave without streets or separation. The buildings sit shoulder to shoulder, hand-built with primitive tools, simple metal spikes and wooden braces holding the masonry together, organized but not advanced, settlement-scale spreading across the low basin like a breathing grid. Light is dim, diffuse, coming not from sun—there is no clear sky—only from the warm, wet haze that fills the air and from the faint phosphorescence of the water itself, reflecting off a hundred thousand small surfaces.

Above and through this dense construction moves life: abundant, constant, unmistakably present. Not strange—nothing here is strange, nothing violates expectation—just plentiful. Thick-bodied, pale-crawling things move slowly through the pools and over the clay walls; they are molluscs without shells, segmented crawlers, soft amphibians that do not call out but rustle constantly, their bodies sliding over stone and through shallow channels in endless migration. The sound they make, multiplied by their numbers, multiplied by the water dripping and the constant low pressure of the warm air, becomes deafening: a sustained, featureless roar of wet movement, a static thunder of presence that never crests and never fades.

The climate is invariant. The pools do not dry. The temperature does not shift from its deep, enclosing warmth. The settlement does not decay—there are no weeds, no rot, only mineral endurance and the perpetual addition of new wet layers. Erosion has not begun; nothing wears away because nothing changes. The fauna moves but does not alter its path. The architecture holds but does not grow. Every element is locked in a dense, warm, wet equilibrium, a town of clay and stone suspended in its first breath, forever emerging, never arrived, under a deafening and eternal sound.
