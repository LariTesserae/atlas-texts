The place is a single room of the high world—an alpine amphitheater barely half a league across, ringed by young, sharp-toothed ridges that have only just finished rising from the depths. The rock is fresh: pale, barely weathered, ridges like broken teeth catching what little light exists. There is no true sun here. Instead, a cool, dim luminescence seeps downward—reflected from a distant ice-field, or perhaps exhaled by the damp air itself—casting everything in a green-grey twilight where shadows hold still rather than move.

The air is cool and intermittently wet. There is no river here, only pocket-moisture: condensation that gathers in the hollows of black basalt, occasional thin rain that falls once every few days like a whispered spell, and a perpetual exhalation of mist from the moss-choked crevices. Everywhere, the vegetation is lush, almost aggressive—deep emerald ferns, velvet moss that climbs the walls in silent conspiracies, lichen like old gold, and twisted dwarf trees that grow horizontally from the vertical rock. But it is a sparse lushness; the green lives in islands, separated by stretches of bare, young stone, so that the eye travels long distances through emptiness between each clustered garden of growth.

This is a settlement, but not as the lowlands understand it. The built environment is organized, layered, primitive—an urbanity of another kind. The inhabitants have carved their town into the vertical faces: low, dry-stone terraces stacked like beehives; thatch-roofed huts woven into root-mats; bridges of woven vine and lashed timber spanning the gaps between moss-covered spires. Everything is hand-formed, simple, pre-metal. Tools are of carved horn, fired clay, and chipped obsidian. The town is small—cathedral-scale, a single breathing place with boundaries formed by the ridges above and a sheer drop into fog below.

The silence is not empty. Sound here is kept below a whisper. There is no wind in this enclosed bowl, or if there is, it moves through moss without voice. Water drips, slowly, invisibly. The buildings do not creak; they are too young, too tightly lashed, too damp to splinter loudly. What little noise exists is absorbed by the vegetation and the thick, cool air.

But the stillness is a lie held in place by pressure.

The fauna is overwhelming—not in noise, but in presence. The space teems with life, yet it moves quietly, as if respecting the silence. Insects not of any named kind—iridescent, segmented, silent—crawl in layered multitudes over every damp surface. The crevices hide things that are more felt than seen: soft bodies, too many eyes, movements that are deliberate and slow, watching from the moss with an intelligence that feels older than the fresh stone. In the shallow pools of condensation, translucent larvae spiral in constant, mute choreography. Small predators—furry, winged, scaled in patterns that seem to shift when unobserved—move through the vertical town with the familiarity of citizens, never hunting loudly, never announcing their presence. The ecosystem is fiercely active: predation happens in stillness, reproduction in hidden warmth, decomposition in real-time. The lush plants feed the overwhelming life; the overwhelming life feeds the plants in a cycle that has already begun to eat the dead wood and fallen bodies before they finish falling.

There is strangeness here, woven into the physical rules. The dampness is not only water; sometimes the condensation pools form perfect spheres that hang from ferns for days without falling, and some say they reflect not the dim sky but other places. The young stone, sharp as it is, grows in patterns—spirals, lattices—where roots have taught it how to soften. The inhabitants—scattered, sparse in number despite the urban organization—do not speak loudly, if at all. Their rituals involve placing carved clay vessels under stones to collect the silent rain, and they build new terraces not upward, but downward, into the fog, as if the town is slowly sinking into itself.

This is a world in its first breath: fresh-formed, cool, lush, quiet, dense with hidden motion, and weird in the way only a place that has not yet decided its own rules can be. It is not large. It is not loud. But within its small, young boundaries, everything is alive, and everything is waiting.
