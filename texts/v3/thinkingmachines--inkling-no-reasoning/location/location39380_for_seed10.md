The valley is not dug out of stone. It is grown down into it—an old mouth of vegetation opened by time. Here, at 2.1, the air is thin and cold (0.5), carrying the iron taste of high altitude and the constant weeping of stone (2.4). There are no buildings (0.5), only the architecture of plants that have filled every substrate with themselves (2.7). They do not grow here; they are the ground. They are the walls. They are the sky’s lower half. Below them, ancient machinery—industrial residue (1.8)—rests not as ruin but as root-system, gears seized in moss, pistons choked with vines that have made engines into soil long ago.

The erosion is absolute (3.0). Not violent—old. The valley has settled into its final form, a flattened bowl of primordial stability, the end-state of land that has forgotten it was ever rock. Water is everywhere near the surface, condensing in the cold air, weeping from black cliffs, saturating the living mat so thoroughly that walking is impossible. You do not walk through this place; you are borne upon it, or you sink.

The scale is open (2.4), a town-sized depression extending past the immediate eye, multi-featured and scattered (0.6). There is vast space between presences. The plants form not a wall but a sparse cathedral of living pillars—towers of braided root and frozen leaf separated by long, wet distances, leaving room for emptiness to breathe. Light is dim (1.4), filtered through the thick canopy, falling in green-black shafts that illuminate nothing clearly.

Reality breaks here (2.7). The machinery does not rust; it breathes. The plants do not photosynthesize; they remember. In the turbulent state (2.8) of this ecosystem—storm-state, hyperactive, always in the act of becoming—creation and destruction are one motion. Roots tear through metal with the patience of centuries, and metal, in turn, secretes minerals that alter the plants’ chemistry. The fauna is abundant (2.4) but scattered—small, pale, cold-adapted things: translucent insects, winged seeds that move like fish through the humid air, creatures that seem half-formed by the valley’s own dreaming. They are not visitors. They are expressions of the turbulence, born and unmade in the same breath.

The sound is loud (1.6), though not sharp. A continuous, low thunder of water falling through root-canal, of machinery grinding slowly in its plant-coffin, of the valley itself exhaling cold vapor. It is the noise of an anthill scaled to landscape—ancient, industrial, organic, all at once, never settling.

No one has built this (0.5). No artifacts stand apart. Yet the technology is present—engines buried like seeds, pipelines become vines, electrified tendrils carrying not current but sap. The built world has been dissolved back into the living substrate, leaving only the strange, settled chaos of a place that has finished becoming and now only persists—cold, wet, loud, turbulent, and utterly, impossibly alive.
