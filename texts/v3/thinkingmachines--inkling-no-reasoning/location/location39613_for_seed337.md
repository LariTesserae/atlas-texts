Beneath a shallow, dull basin in the cool earth lies the City of Unroofed Rooms: not a ruin but a just-coming-into-being, an embryonic urban region spread across a valley-sized hollow some forty paces below the open air. The geology is raw, pre-formed—walls do not stand so much as precipitate, accreting like breath on cold glass into layers of packed clay, salt-cement, and pressed silt. Nothing here is finished; every corridor is still being proposed by moisture and pressure.

The air is cool and permanently damp, closer to riverine condensation than rain. Water exists not as flow but as presence: beads clinging to every vertical surface, shallow pools welling in the depressions of unfinished floors, a constant, quiet weeping that makes the packed earth glisten without ever running clear. The moisture is intermittent, arriving in thick pockets that hang in the dense built fabric like breath held too long.

And the fabric is dense—utterly saturated, town-scale in its compressed sprawl, leaving no gap between presences. Hundreds of low chambers, alcoves, and narrow conduits are knit together in layered construction, one against the next with no streets, only seams. It is settlement-scale organization executed with primitive hands: simple brick of unfired mud, wooden shoring, hand-tooled channels, ropes of woven fiber. The technology is early, manual, almost embarrassed—tools of horn, flint, and softened copper scraping at walls that seem to finish themselves.

There is little to finish them, though. Vegetation is sparse: black-green moss in the wettest creases, a few fungal ropes dangling from the ceiling seams, thin root-hairs probing downward. Above, the surface offers only scrubby grasses and scattered scrub, contributing little to the cool, subterranean climate.

The light is ordinary—steady, grayish-white, filtering through dozens of shallow, muddy shafts that pierce the thin crust overhead. It is not bright, not mystical, just adequate illumination falling across the raw rooms in flat, unromantic slabs.

Sound is near-absent. No wind moves through these packed spaces, no insects drone, no water rushes. There is only the occasional, isolated drip—quiet, deliberate—falling into still pools. The fauna is rare to the point of absence: perhaps a blind, pale crawler shifting between chambers, or a single bat hanging like forgotten laundry. Life is present but indiscernible, a rumor in the damp.

Everything is active, despite the silence. The city is in motion—under construction, always. Walls thicken microscopically as minerals deposit; shallow pools evaporate slowly in the cool air, leaving crusts that are immediately broken by new seepage; manual labor moves in constant, quiet rotation—figures in dark clothing shifting mud, tightening ropes, carving new thresholds with hand-tools. The processes are multiple: geological emergence, architectural consolidation, the slow biological pace of the sparse moss, and the human rhythm of building. The world is pre-formation, embryonic, a regional-scale idea of habitation being born into earth rather than placed upon it.

It is not strange—there is nothing supernatural in the damp, the quiet, the packed rooms, or the cool air. It is only very new, very full, and very underground: a cool, open, valley-sized settlement emerging in the dark, leaving no room for emptiness, slowly making itself into being.
