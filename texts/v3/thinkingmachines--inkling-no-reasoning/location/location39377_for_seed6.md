The dome is only sixty meters across, a young sphere of green-black alloy and ceramic ribbing that broke the surface three thermal cycles ago, still sharp-edged, still venting its birth-heat into a world that is all warm, saturated water. Here is the contradiction held whole: you are high—suspended in a vertical shaft of liquid that climbs past every horizon—while being entirely submerged, breath-deep in an atmospheric column that refuses to end. There is no sky, only a darker gradient upward, and downward, more of the same.

Inside the dome, the space is total and built. Every surface is manufactured—no rock, no soil—just grooved plates, mesh grilles, and thick conduits arranged in a near-perfect grid that fills the volume edge-to-edge. Density is absolute. There is no open air, no empty pocket; the chamber is packed with presence, every cubic hand occupied by machinery, by root, by the liquid that moves through them all.

That liquid is everywhere. Not flood, not ocean floor, but a suspended, breathing body of warm water—soft, heavy, and slow—filling the interstices and running through transparent pipes in constant, patient circulation. The water is the substrate and the bloodstream. The vegetation—lush, relentless, dark emerald and bioluminescent teal—does not root in earth but in engineered hydroponic collars that line every wall, every pillar. Vines as thick as wrists descend in curtains between pump housings; broad, waxy leaves unfold in clusters so thick they form false ceilings, filtering the normal, yellow-white glow that issues from recessed industrial lamps embedded behind thick glass.

This is industrial, mechanized, scaled-down to the intimate—cathedral only in its completeness. Small pumps hum beneath the threshold of hearing, a quiet, submerged murmur. Valves turn on cycles measured in months. Electric traces in the walls regulate the warmth; nothing here freezes, nothing boils, everything is maintained in the slow, gradual current of long-duration processes. Change happens, but across durations that outpace urgency. The machinery is not frantic. It waits, cycles, circulates.

No animals have entered. The fauna parameter is an absolute zero—a silence made physical—so the space belongs entirely to the plants and the machines, to the water and the built geometry. Erosion is almost absent; the structure is newborn, its seams still unweathered, its corners sharp, its surfaces un-blunted by time or abrasion. It is fresh-formed, recently emerged—or perhaps never emerged, but assembled here in the deep high-place, a sealed seed of production suspended in the infinite rain.

The weirdness is in the inversion: to be enclosed in manufactured perfection, suspended in high water, surrounded by lush green growth that feeds on engineered light and silent pumps, with no ground to stand on and no creature to share it with. The sound is minimal—a distant, muted thrum of water passing through ceramic throat-pipes, the occasional soft click of a thermal regulator, the near-inaudible expansion of growing stems against metal ribs. The light is ordinary, unmagical, illuminating a scene that is entirely strange by its saturation.

Every boundary is present and close. You do not travel through this place; you inhabit a single, full feature, a round chamber of slow, green industry held in warm, endless submersion. The rules are those of circulation and containment. The world outside—if there is one—is only more water, more height, more of the same industrial silence. Here, inside, everything is made, everything is filled, everything grows at the patient pace of machines that do not sleep.
