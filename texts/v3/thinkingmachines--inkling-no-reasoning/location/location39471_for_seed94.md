This is a place that has reached its own state—erosion settled, form established, rules internal. No one built it, and yet it is not wild in the absence of civilization; it is wild in the presence of something older and more patient. The landscape holds its contradictions—vast and packed, lush and cool, industrial and untouched—without resolving them. They have become the climate here. The water continues to weep. The moss continues to fill. The machines continue their silent, root-cracked sleep. And the cool air, moving across it all, carries nothing but the faint, constant sound of being fully, impossibly, quietly alive.
