It is a hollow the size of a clenched heart, buried far beneath anything that remembers the sky. No water has ever entered—there are no rivers, no seep, no dew—only a warm, mineral-thick air that holds at about half-summer, unmoving, and a dim amber glow that comes from neither sun nor flame but from the slow, constant burning of the moss-fiber walls. The place is young, sharp-featured: the ceiling is fresh-spalled crystal in blade-like ridges, not yet worn round, and the floor is a flat plate of black basalt that cooled in this shape and will not change. Elevation is low—this is a pocket below the surface, a sealed chamber in the bedrock, discovered not long ago in geological terms and perpetually unchanged since.

Everything is scattered and sparse within it. There is space everywhere—vast emptiness between the things that live here, low saturation, a room that feels half-abandoned even where it thrives.

The life is lush but strange, thriving without a drop of water. Thick vines of pale gray-green serpentine hang from the sharp ceiling in isolated curtains—never touching, always separated by yards of naked crystal—pulling what they need from the mineral air and the warm darkness. Their leaves are broad, waxy, translucent like wet parchment, and they do not grow; they are present, fully formed, eternal in form. Between these hanging gardens, the black floor is dotted with small, round cushions of moss—some the size of a child’s hand, others no bigger than coins—spaced far apart in an unbroken pattern that has never shifted. They are emerald, velvet-dark, and they do not spread.

The fauna is abundant but never crowded. Tiny, eyeless, white-shelled beetles click and crawl across the basalt in loose, intermittent processions, always leaving gaps between groups. They feed on the moss cushions without damaging them, and they never reproduce into swarms—there is exactly enough of them, spread across exactly enough emptiness, as there always has been. Higher up, along the vine-curtains, hang clusters of translucent, six-winged insects that hang motionless except for the constant, loud vibration of their wings—a sustained, resonant hum that fills the hollow with sound, never dropping, never intensifying, a single chord held for eternity. The noise is loud, omnipresent, a deep mechanical drone that makes the crystal ceiling shiver in sympathy without cracking. Small, pale, hairless rodents—no larger than fists—sit scattered among the root-knots of the vines, breathing slowly, watching nothing.

There are artifacts, scattered and primitive. A single, hand-woven ladder of dried vine-fiber hangs forgotten from one shard of ceiling-stone; its rungs are brittle, its purpose lost. Near one wall lies a flat, circular grinding stone of dark basalt, worn smooth in one shallow depression, with the faint outline of a simple, hand-carved mark—two lines, almost a figure—scratched into its surface with something harder and older. There is no settlement, only these occasional marks: a small heap of broken crystal fragments arranged in a shallow circle that has never collapsed, a single wooden peg driven into a floor crack, holding nothing. The technology is hand-level, pre-metal, manual—someone once was here, or something with hands, and they left only these sparse, scattered signatures.

The world is weird in its perfection. Nothing changes. The vines do not grow longer, the moss does not spread, the beetles do not die out or multiply. The temperature holds at its warm plateau; the dim amber light neither brightens nor fades. The loud hum of the insects is invariant, a sound without echo or decay. There is no erosion—no wind, no water, no breaking—only the young, sharp edges of the crystal ceiling, fresh-formed and preserved in eternal stasis. There is no weather, no season, no shadow that moves.

This is the place: a hidden pocket, intimate as a closed mouth, below the world’s surface, locked in an equilibrium that never tilts. Lush in its scattered life, loud in its unceasing drone, strange in its absolute refusal to become anything other than itself. A dry, warm, dim chamber where vegetation and fauna share a perfect, motionless abundance across vast spaces of emptiness, beneath a ceiling of fresh blades, upon a floor of black stone that remembers nothing and expects nothing.
