Start with dampness in a hidden pocket below the surface, where intermittent moisture beads on cool, raw stone. The elevation is low, not a cavern but an intimate hollow—pocket-scale, embedded within larger earth, barely formed and still emerging from pre-formation. The geology is embryonic: thin crusts, undeveloped ridges, scattered fragments of partial remains. Sparse vegetation clings in scattered tufts—lichens, hair-moss, a single pale root reaching downward. The air is cool, dense with absence, the space between presences wide and quiet.

But the light—bright, almost impossible at 2.6—does not come from a sky. It emanates from the walls themselves. Bioluminescent veins pulse in slow rhythms, casting sharp, sourceless illumination across the hollow. This is not built technology; there are no devices, only the world glowing from within its own young bones.

The fauna is abundant, filling the sparse spaces with motion. Insects—translucent, jointed, without eyes—crawl over the damp surfaces in constant migration. Small crustaceans gather in the pockets of condensation, clicking softly, a near-silent percussion below the threshold of sound. Something larger moves just outside the visible radius, brushing against the embryonic rock.

And here the reality begins to break, approaching the strange maximum. The rules of this pocket are not ours. Gravity behaves like a suggestion; droplets hang suspended, then fall upward toward the glowing ceiling. Time moves in loops—some animals repeat the same three steps forever, trapped in a cycle that is not death but not progress either. The air smells of copper and old rain, though no rain falls. The scattered structures—small, faint, partial—are not ruins but seeds of architecture that have not yet decided whether to be stone or bone.

Every surface is active, in motion: moss expanding in visible seconds, crust cracking open to release new moisture, the bioluminescent veins brightening and dimming in a biological pulse that feels like breathing. The hollow is quiet, yes—silence filled only with the soft scrape of movement, the drip of water that never quite lands, the hollow’s own emerging heartbeat.

This is a place just coming into being, hidden and intimate, its weirdness not an addition but its foundation. The abundant life does not inhabit the space; it is the space, testing shapes, testing rules, gathering in scattered clusters of presence amid the vast, cool emptiness, under a brightness that has nowhere to come from and everywhere to shine.
