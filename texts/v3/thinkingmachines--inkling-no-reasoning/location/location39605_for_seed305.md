The plateau is not made of rock. That is the first misconception—there are no stones, only bodies.

Come to the edge of the Plate-World, where the air is bright enough to burn if it weren’t so cold (temperature 0.1/3), and you will understand the scale (2.6/3). The surface extends not in miles but in moods—vast as a continent, flat enough to hold its own weather. Clouds do not drift across it; they pool, trapped in the thermal stillness of a place that refuses to turn or tilt. The elevation (2.0/3) is not a hill but a recent decision, a thrust plate fresh-born from hidden forces (erosion 0.9/3), its edges sharp and unrounded, its face still scarred by the violence of emergence. There are no streams, only pockets of moisture (water 1.1/3): condensation beading inside the spirals of shell-horns, rain that falls upward in the bright glare and hangs frozen in the air like suspended glass, riverine veins of dampness threading through bodies rather than soil.

The ground is not ground. It is flesh—lush (vegetation 1.8/3), though not green, saturated with chlorophyll analogues that glow faint silver in the relentless light (2.9/3). There is no soil, only root-mat, only intertwined stalk and fiber, only the packed density (2.8/3) of life that has refused to leave space for absence. Every square inch is occupied. The world is fully, unbearably full. Nothing is built (built 0.3/3), yet nothing is wild in the sense of absence—this is pure presence, a crowded cathedral with no architect.

But they are not plants alone. They are fauna—overwhelming (2.9/3), a population so dense it becomes geology. Millions upon millions of bodies, each one an engine. The technology (2.2/3) is not added; it is internalized, industrial in purpose but biological in form. In the marrow-cavities of these beings—if beings they can be called—there are gears grown from calcified song, pistons of translucent cartilage, factories of membrane that refine the condensation-rain into fuel, sound, light. They are mechanized organisms, scaled production made flesh, electrified not by wire but by chemical thunder moving slowly through shared tissue. They hum, they churn, they process. The sound (2.1/3) is constant and loud—not sharp, but layered, a choral drone of internal engines, the wet grinding of gears made of horn and pearl, the hiss of pressurized dampness being converted into motion.

The light never leaves (2.9/3). There is no shadow for hiding, no dusk. The brightness is clinical, merciless, illuminating every packed surface, every interlocking limb, every exposed mechanism. The cold (0.1/3) does not freeze them—this is not a dead world—but it slows them. The dynamic is glacial (0.8/3). Change takes epochs. A limb extends over centuries. A factory-organ shifts its production line across millennia. They are patient.

And they are strange (weirdness 2.5/3). There are no faces, only functional surfaces. There are no predators, only competition for the nearest pocket of moisture. They communicate not in voice but in pressure waves—slow, seismic, felt through the root-mat. Some tower like cathedrals of bone and engine, others lie flat, spreading like industrial moss. Together they form a single, continuous factory-floor of life, untouched by hands (tech present, but never made), young in its formation, vast in its reach, and absolutely, densely, loud with the sound of being.

Walk carefully. You do not step on ground. You step on presence. And everything around you—every bright, cold, packed, churning inch—is working, slowly, on something you will never live long enough to understand.
