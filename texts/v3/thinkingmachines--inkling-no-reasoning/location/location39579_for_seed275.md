Beneath the sealed crust, there is no sky and no rain—only the slow, dry exhalation of machinery and root. The hollow is vast, a valley-scale cavern with the dimensions of a buried town, its ceiling a membrane of compressed silica-glass and dead-calcium arch that holds back the weight of the world above. Elevation is absolute zero: everything here lives under the surface, in the cool, 0.6-dark of pre-formation geology that is only now deciding what it wants to become.

There is no water. Not a stream, not a seep. The air is mineral-dry, carrying only the faint, metallic taste of ozone and powdered root-bark. Moisture exists only in sealed loops, circulated through industrial veins that hum with a low, constant thrum—quiet, but present, like blood in a distant limb.

The vegetation is total and architectural. Every wall, every bridge, every structural pillar is a living mass. Thick, spongy trunks of fibrous plant-cylinder form the load-bearing bones of the settlement, woven so densely they replace concrete and steel. Between them, the air is filled with hanging gardens of needle-thin leaves, root-curtains, and translucent seed-pods that glow faintly with stored bioluminescence—normal light made strange by its origin in chlorophyll and phosphor. The plant-substrate is the city. To build is to graft; to pave is to lay a mat of compressed moss-stone that breathes and grows over months, sealing cracks with living callus. The density is sparse despite the totality: there is enormous space between the great plant-columns, open plazas of bare root-mesh where wind—generated by distant ventilation engines—moves in slow, cool currents.

The settlement is urban and industrial. Foundries operate in the hollows of dead-growth, powered by geothermal taps and the slow combustion of compressed lignin. Conveyor belts made of woven vine-cable carry raw mineral up from the raw geology below, feeding into spires where plant-machine hybrids refine ore into structural fibers. Electricity crackles along fungal mycelium wires suspended in the air, casting sharp, cool shadows. The technology is mechanized but symbiotic: engines are cased in bark, pipes lined with living tissue that heals cracks automatically. The place is active—constantly building itself, constantly repairing, constantly becoming.

Erosion is emerging, barely begun. The rock beneath the root-pavements is still young, half-formed, crumbly with the softness of recent origin. In the far reaches of the hollow, new fissures open not with violence but with a slow, geological yawn, releasing dry heat or cold mineral breath. Nothing is worn smooth yet; edges are sharp, forms are embryonic, and the settlement looks as though it was planted yesterday even as it reaches for permanence.

Fauna is abundant but scattered, adapted to the sparse, cool emptiness. Insects—thin, translucent, wingless—crawl through the root-weave in streams, harvesting dead cellulose and carrying spores between districts. Small, furred creatures with too many eyes nest in the hollow spaces within plant-walls, their presence marked only by sudden movements in the leaf-curtains. They are part of the machinery of life here, not decoration—pollinators, decomposers, sensors in an ecosystem that has no predators and no prey, only exchange.

The sound is mostly quiet. The industrial processes are dampened by miles of root-fabric and moss-insulation. You hear the occasional distant clank of a mechanized press, the whisper of ventilation, the soft cracking of growing wood as it asserts its shape against stone. The silence is deep, cool, and inhabited.

Weirdness pervades without spectacle. The air smells of copper and growing things. The plant-buildings respond slowly to pressure, tightening or loosening like muscle. Time moves differently in the cool dark—growth is visible, measurable in the slow curl of a tendril over an hour. Nothing here is dead; even the stone is being digested, incorporated, turned into substrate for new growth. The world is strange because it is alive in every structural plane, an urban cavern of dry mineral and all-encompassing vegetation, building itself upward and inward in a place that should be impossible: a cool, waterless, subterranean garden-city, emerging.
