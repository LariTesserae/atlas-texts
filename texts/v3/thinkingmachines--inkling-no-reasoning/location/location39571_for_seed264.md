The valley is low—only a shallow depression in a wider, unremarkable plain, elevation barely registering against the flatness around it. There is nothing built here. Not a wall, not a post, not a path worn by feet. The ground itself is bare mineral: dark basalt sand fused with shards of obsidian glass, ground smooth across centuries into a kind of black velvet that holds footprints like scars.

No vegetation breaks the surface. Not grass, not lichen, not fungus. The ecology is locked at zero, a mineral world suspended in a post-biological equilibrium. The temperature is warm—steady, body-level warmth that never climbs and never drops, like air held in the palm of a giant. There is occasional moisture: not rainfall, but intermittent weeping. Condensation beads along low ridges of iron-rich stone; shallow riverine pools sit in hollows, dark and mirror-calm, fed by slow seepage rather than flow.

The landscape extends. It is open—regional, a valley-sized bowl that repeats itself for miles, scattered with low domes of cooled volcanic glass. Space dominates presence. The domes stand miles apart, and between them there is only empty mineral flatness. The place is old. Erosion is established—not active, not violent, but settled into its own form. Every edge is rounded, every slope calculated by deep time into its final angle. Nothing here is in transition. The dynamic is absolute stillness: no weather fronts, no seasonal shift, no gradient to pull change through.

And yet it is loud. Industrial machinery is present—not as buildings, not as architecture, but as presence embedded in the land itself. Enormous engines sit half-buried in the basalt: iron-green pistons, gear-trains fused with volcanic rock, boiler-housings crusted with mineral deposit. They do not move. They hum. Or rather, they emit a continuous, loud, invariant drone—mechanized sound without mechanized motion—electrified resonance that has been vibrating through the valley at the exact same pitch, in the exact same rhythm, for longer than the rock has been smooth. Factories without walls. Scaled production without product. The technology scale is unmistakable—wheels larger than houses, conduits thick as tree trunks, all of it industrial, all of it static.

The light is normal—flat, even, without drama—illuminating a world that does not need illumination. The fauna is rare: perhaps once in a great span, a small stony creature, shaped like a smooth pebble, shifts position by millimeters, then stops. The weirdness is present but subdued, a quiet wrongness: machinery that should require fuel, labor, change, but demands none; water that pools but never evaporates or flows; warmth that sustains nothing.

This is a place of established equilibrium, never changing, where the absence of life, the absence of construction, and the presence of dead industry form a single, sparse, open fact. You can walk for hours across the black sand, past weeping iron ridges and silent engines, and find that nothing has become different from what it was, and nothing ever will.
