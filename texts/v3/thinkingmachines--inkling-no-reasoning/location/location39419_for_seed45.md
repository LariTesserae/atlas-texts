The high land is still being argued into place.

Not mountains, not yet—not in the settled sense. They are upthrust ribs of warm obsidian and matte-black basalt, rising to near-sky, scattered across a dark plane like the first bones of a world that has only just decided to have a surface. The elevation is extreme, but nothing here is old enough to have earned a summit name. These ridges are embryonic, raw geology made visible: spires that never knew ice, valleys that have not collected dust long enough to be valleys, only absence happening between two rising things. There is almost no erosion. There is only emergence—slow, then abrupt, then slow again. The ground is newborn mineral, glass-cool in some valleys, warm-vented in others, breathing faint heat into a darkness that has no stars, only the faint orange glow of cooling magma seams threading the ridges like veins.

Water is gone, or was never present. Not a trace—no river scars, no salt pan mirror, no frozen grief. The chemistry is dry, non-aqueous, running on heat and pressure and the slow chemistry of stone talking to itself. The temperature holds at 2.3, a persistent warmness that rises from within rather than falling from above. In the open spaces—yes, open, regional, extending past sight into a dark continuum—the air is thin, sharp with sulfur and metallic vapor, but mostly it is empty. Density is low. Things are sparse. You can walk—if walking meant anything here—for miles between one presence and another.

That darkness is dominant. Light hovers at 0.4, a weak bioluminescent smear along the magma seams and the occasional phosphor-spill from fresh fissures. Most of the world is shadow, not black exactly, but the absence of claim. The sky, if the word applies, is a pressure of deep violet-dark, maybe an atmosphere still deciding whether to exist, turbulent with invisible motion.

And it is turbulent. Not noisy—sound is mostly quiet, around 1.2, a low resonant hum beneath the crust, the occasional deep crack of cooling glass contracting miles away, like bones shifting in sleep. No thunder, because there is no weather to make it. The chaos is geological, internal, Solaris-like in its patient violence: the land is creation and destruction held in the same breath. Sections of the plain lift inches in an hour, or drop suddenly into new chasms that have not yet learned to be canyons. Spills of liquid rock move with purpose and forgetfulness, hardening into new structures before they finish being liquid. The world is hyperactive in its bones, anthill-energy without the hill—pure dynamics, constant low-frequency becoming.

What lives? Almost nothing recognized. Fauna is 0.2—absent. No animals, no flight, no tracks in dust because there is no dust. Vegetation is barely present, 0.5—not plants, not fungi, not anything with roots and a will to grow upward. There might be faint mineral growths on the warm vent-faces, crystalline hair that does not feed, does not reproduce in any language we carry. Life here is pre- or non-; it is chemistry admiring itself.

Built things are scattered, faint, sparse—1.2. Not cities. Not tools, for there is no tech, none, 0.2—pure absence of device. What remains are occasional artifacts, single structures or partial marks: a circle of blackened pillars that might have been arranged, or might be the accidental architecture of a particularly neat eruption. A flat plane of fused glass half-buried and half-exposed, like a floor that forgot its walls. Nothing functional. Nothing maintained. They sit in the open space like questions that arrived before the language to ask them.

The weirdness is present but restrained, 0.6—unusual, not impossible. The strangeness is in the simultaneity: extreme height and extreme youth; turbulent change and mostly quiet; open scale and extreme sparsity; persistent warmth without a star to grant it permission. The world behaves like an idea in the middle of thinking itself—unstable, warm, high, dark, still emerging from its own raw decision.

You do not enter it; you witness it being made. The elevation does not invite ascent so much as it announces that ascent is still being defined. The air smells of electricity and cooling iron. In the distance—always distant, for scale is open and density refuses to cluster—another spire might be rising right now, or falling, or deciding not to exist yet.

This is the place: a pre-formed high desert of dry mineral, under turbulent internal construction, mostly dark, mostly silent, almost empty, almost alive, and not finished.
