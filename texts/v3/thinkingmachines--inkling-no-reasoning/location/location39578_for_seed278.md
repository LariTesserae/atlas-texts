There are no sharp edges, only long-existed forms in their own state.

Water is present at 1.0—intermittent, furtive, industrial in its discipline. Not rivers but a plumbing of absence: seams of condensation weeping from black basalt, shallow pans of gray melt that hold for three days then vanish into stone pores, rain that falls as cold needles and is immediately claimed by the porous plate. Moisture lives in pockets, in larger dryness, never enough to claim the surface.

Built structures are 0.3—none. No walls, no roads, no markers. Yet tech is 2.0—embedded, mechanized, scaled and patient. Weirdness is 1.1—unusual in its refusal to cohere. The silence is not empty but occupied. In the stillness, one notices that shadows fall in two directions, faintly, as if lit by sources that do not exist. The black basalt does not echo; it absorbs even the idea of reflection. The condensation pools hold shapes that remain for minutes after a disturbance—memory in water, holding form against physics.

The atmosphere is one of settled vastness. Wind moves slowly, carrying no scent except cold mineral and the faint copper tang of the hidden engines. Light comes dim, gray-blue, without source—diffused, eternal twilight that makes distance infinite and proximity strange. Nothing happens quickly. Change is patient, across long durations: a rock splits over centuries, a machine cycles every ten thousand years, a lichen cluster expands its radius by a millimeter per century.

This is a world without artifacts because it has digested its own machinery. No ruins, only integration. The industrial and the eroded have reached agreement. The fauna operates at overwhelming scale in hidden dimensions, and the silence holds all of it—not as absence, but as the only sound vast and old enough to contain them.
