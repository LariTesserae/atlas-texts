Down.

Not far—just beneath the crust, in the hollow between root and soil, but far enough that the sky is a rumor. Here, elevation is negative space: a valley carved downward, a subsurface amphitheater of young rock and older thirst. The stone is sharp-featured, fresh-broken, barely worn by the patience of water. This is young erosion, not polished but split—recent fractures holding the cool moisture of intermittent rain that finds its way through cracks. Dampness pools in pockets: condensation beading on limestone lips, shallow riverlets threading through fissures, then vanishing into porous dark. The dryness is larger, framing these small, stubborn wetnesses.

The scale is open—regional, valley-wide, a town-sized absence where the ceiling hangs low and distant, and the floor rolls away into shadow. Distance is measured in echoes.

Light is dim, not dead. A faint phosphorescence clings to fungal threads, while bioluminescent larvae pulse in rhythm—too faint to illuminate, only enough to suggest shapes. Most of the space is readable only through sound and touch.

Vegetation is sparse. Not forest, but punctuation: clumps of pale, root-like growth hanging from fractures, moss only where moisture lingers long enough to invite it. Much of the terrain is bare mineral—clay, cooling basalt, sediment packed in layers like pages.

There are no structures. No artifacts. No tools left behind. The built environment is absolute zero. Whatever is here was not made; it emerged.

But there is life—overwhelming in number, if not in mass. The fauna dominates every cubic breath. Insects with translucent carapaces crawl in continuous carpets across the damp walls. Worms, blind and glistening, thread the sediment in densities that turn soil into motion. Small crustaceans cluster at every seep, antennae twitching in unison. The air—what air moves here—carries the constant, loud percussion of mandibles, of wing-cases rubbing, of thousands of feet scraping stone in a dry, relentless thunder. The sound is loud, not explosive but absolute: a region-scale white noise of biological machinery running at equilibrium. You do not hear individuals. You hear the aggregate, the unbroken chorus of presence.

The temperature holds cool—steady, near-cold, the thermal signature of earth that has never seen sun.

The world is static. Never changing. No weather moves through these chambers. The riverlets do not swell or shrink. The insects do not migrate, do not breed in visible cycles, do not die in noticeable numbers—though they must—because what dies is replaced in perfect, invisible balance. There are no gradients driving change: no wind, no current, no seasonal tilt. Time here is invariant, locked in a frozen equilibrium. The sharp rock does not round. The moss does not spread. The sound never fades.

This is a place that has recently become itself and, having arrived, refuses to become anything else. A young cave system, broad and open, cool and dim, barely vegetated, saturated with animal presence and the loud, unchanging song of its own stability. Water comes only in pockets, intermittent, enough to sustain but not to transform. And below it all, in the sparse spaces between bodies, there is an overwhelming emptiness—room enough for everything, and nothing to fill it except the sound of life holding still.
