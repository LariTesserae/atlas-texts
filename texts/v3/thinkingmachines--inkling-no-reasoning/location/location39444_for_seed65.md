Above the reach of clouds, on a plateau that breathes thinner air than any valley remembers, lies a region without name because nothing here was ever built to bear one. The elevation is extreme—three-quarters of the sky is black even at midday, pierced by a sun that feels closer, its glare unfiltered by moisture or haze, blinding white against rock that has been scoured to a pale, spectral gray. The temperature never climbs; it holds at a perpetual cool that is not cold, exactly, but an arrested chill, suspended in equilibrium. The dynamics are dead. No front moves in, no shadow races across stone, no season turns. The wind, too, has forgotten its purpose; it does not gust, it does not settle. It is present as a constant, high-pressure murmur, part of the background roar that makes this open valley—if valley it is, for it extends past every visible ridge into a regional emptiness—feel like the inside of a bell that never stops being struck.

The sound is the first thing the body registers. Deafening. Not a single noise but a layered, eternal percussion: the crash and recoil of water falling somewhere far below and feeding back up through fissures; the grinding of rock against rock in an erosion that has finished its work and now merely confirms itself; and beneath both, a subsonic thrum that vibrates in the teeth. At 2.9 out of 3, it is not chaos—there is no variation, no crescendo, only the unchanging volume of a place that refuses silence. You do not hear the fauna over it; you see them, and understand that they, too, have become silent instruments inside the noise.

Nothing here is waiting. The place has achieved its form and listens, deafeningly, to itself.
