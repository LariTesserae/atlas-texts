The power did not arrive as a sun.

It knew better than to bring a fixed brightness into a country where shadows were an injury, where a clear horizon would be a kind of wall.

It did not arrive as a hand, either. No hand could touch that plain without becoming wet, granular, historical. No hand could lift the deep labor from beneath the crust without taking with it the routes of the sievebacks, the stored storms in the whistle-bodies, the unborn choices on which the fore-runners fed. The land was not broken. It was difficult. These were different conditions.

So for a long while the power did nothing visible.

It attended.

It attended to the low world through its own methods, which were not sight, not hearing, not thought exactly. It received the ring-colonies’ subterranean chord. It received the minute strain within a shelf-worm’s thread as it tasted a seam of ancient compression. It received the fearful hesitation in a grazing animal’s twelfth foot. It received the pressure of all the almost-steps beneath the marsh-skin, the millions of possible crossings consumed and carried onward by the shoreless swimmers.

It received, too, the deep labor.

At first it seemed to the power like pain.

The grinding. The endless opening and closing. The pressure accumulated over eras and released in blows that shook the water from the pores of stone. A lesser kindness might have mistaken this labor for torment. A lesser wisdom might have silenced the engines, cooled the vents, laid the world flat at last and called the quiet mercy.

But the power listened longer.

It found no plea in the deep labor.

There was strain, certainly. There was expenditure. There were cycles in which too much pressure built and whole regions lost their old arrangements. There were deaths by the uncountable million: little colonies boiled from cracks, flocks emptied of their gathered sounds, warm-bearers cooling while the herd went on around them, a measurer folding itself into the mud after failing to hear the rhythm of its own hunger.

But beneath all this was not a cry to be ended.

It was an immense attention.

The land was attending to itself.

It knew every pressure it had inherited. It knew the weight of vanished seas, the push of old ice, the buried mineral weather of worlds long dissolved. It was not trying to become still. It was trying, with all its slow and terrible ingenuity, to keep becoming.

The benevolent power understood then that intervention could be a form of vanity: the wish to make another being resemble the conditions under which one knew how to love it.

And so it did not still the plain.

It did not make plants grow there.

It did not bring clear water, or seasons, or a sky with a reliable color. It did not teach the hunters pity in the simple sense, because the measurers’ cruel work was braided too deeply into the herd’s survival. It did not make time run forward, because the fore-runners would starve, and because too much of the country’s memory was held in the small eddies where before and after touched.

But neither did it leave entirely.

There was one thing the power could give without making the place less itself.

It gave the country a margin.

Not a boundary. Boundaries would have been lines, and lines did not last in the wet.

A margin.

In the deepest layers beneath the sediment wheels, beneath the heat seams and the old porous stones, the power made room for one additional possibility: a place within the land’s own turning where no pressure needed immediately to become action.

Not stillness. Stillness was dangerous.

Not safety. Safety, too, would have been a foreign law.

It was a pause with movement inside it.

When the deep labor gathered toward one of its great coordinated exertions—when the slate plates began their fractional rising, when the vents tuned their mineral mouths, when the ring-colonies lowered themselves into the tones that meant the world was about to change—the margin opened.

It opened first as an error in the weather.

Rain fell and remained rain for three heartbeats after landing.

Then it opened as a narrow region of fog in which noises arrived neither early nor late, but precisely when they were made.

Then the sievebacks found it.

They did not recognize it as a refuge. Their kind had no word for refuge. They recognized only that, inside the gray breadth of it, their feet could agree with one another. A body could place all its weight on the ground without testing twelve competing versions of the next moment. Warm-bearers entered first, surrounded by calves and limping adults. The herds stood close. Steam gathered between their enamel plates and rose in soft continuous veils.

The measurers came to the margin’s edge and stopped.

For the first time in their long black lives, they could hear their own rhythms without the earth’s machinery beneath them.

Some recoiled. Some folded their limbs inward and waited outside, listening to the clean beat of their own internal arrangements as if hearing a stranger. One walked into the fog. Its reflected eyes did not vanish, but dimmed. It found a failing sieveback there, an old animal whose heat chambers had become unreliable. The measurer approached.

The silence-clamp began to close around them.

Then it did not.

The hunter drank only enough rhythm to steady the old creature’s collapse. It took the disorder from the sieveback’s body and held it in itself, trembling. When it withdrew, the old animal remained alive for another several cycles. Not healed. Not young. But able to walk with the herd when the margin thinned and the country resumed its work.

The whistle-bodies learned of the margin by sound.

A flock passed above it carrying stolen shrieks from a rupturing seam. They released their usual alarm into the fog. The sound did not become disaster. It did not awaken a crack or frighten a herd into a wrong future. It spread outward and returned to them altered: not diminished, not erased, but answered.

Within the answer was the sound of rain on water.

Only rain.

The flock circled for days. They fed little. They sang to one another in their borrowed hammering, their old factory whistles, their tectonic roars. And gradually, among those inherited noises, new sounds began to appear: soft clicks, low throat-notes, the ticking of enamel cooling, the breath of a sleeping warm-bearer.

The flock had always exchanged catastrophes in courtship. Now, sometimes, they exchanged continuance.

The fore-runners hated the margin at first.

There were fewer almost-steps within it. Fewer unstable futures. A path there did not fan into a hundred alternatives merely because a creature raised its foot. The swimmers circled beneath its shallow wetness, restless and hungry.

Then one of them entered.

It moved through a moment that did not rush away from itself.

For an instant—perhaps an hour, perhaps a generation—it encountered something unprecedented: a present not defended by being already past, nor diluted by having not yet happened.

The fore-runner surfaced.

Those who saw it later said its phosphorescent trail did not run ahead of it.

It ran beside it.

Afterward the margin became part of their migration. They did not breed there; their young belonged to uncertainty. But the oldest fore-runners came there when their sensory pits filled with too many competing sequences, when the world’s maybes crowded them until they could no longer move. In the margin, they rested in the mud and let one future become real enough to leave.

The mineral colonies changed more slowly.

A tint-mat spread into the region and found that its colors no longer needed to argue so violently over heat. It became a color none of its neighboring colonies had possessed: a dark, gentle blue, almost invisible except under the pressure of a hand that was never placed upon it.

Ring-colonies tuned themselves to the margin’s tone.

It was not a note anyone could hear. It was the interval between the notes: the small generosity by which one vibration ended before another began.

Over many thousands of cycles, they carried that interval out into the rest of the plain.

Not enough to stop the deep labor. Not enough to make the country tame. But enough that, during certain great releases, a seam might open a little less violently. A herd might have time to turn. A whistle-body might keep one precious sound in its throat instead of spending it on alarm. A shelf-worm might find a new fault before its old stone folded shut.

The power made no law requiring this.

It made no demand that pain justify itself, or that survival become grateful.

It merely left the margin available.

And, because it was wise, it made the margin mortal.

Nothing in it could stay unchanged. No sanctuary could become a permanent island of fixed peace; such a thing would have turned mineral, then window, then something hungry. The margin shifted with the land. It moved beneath fog. It appeared in a different place after every loud hour. Sometimes it was broad enough for a herd. Sometimes it was only the size of a puddle in which a single rubble-mote could retain its small sharp outline for one more rain.

Sometimes it did not appear at all.

This, too, was mercy.

For the power knew that a gift which could never be lost would soon become another engine, another pressure, another thing the land must labor beneath.

The deep labor noticed.

Not at once. It had no face with which to notice, no single center in which recognition could gather. But its next great turning came differently.

The fissures opened. Steam rose. Slate rattled in long metallic fields. Ring-colonies struck their thousand-thousand bells. The flocks rose in gray spirals, filling themselves with pressure-sound. The herd moved in its slow negotiated wave. Hunters unfolded from the fog. Blue light ran beneath the marsh-skin toward events not yet decided.

Then, under all of it, the colossal press began to close.

It had closed this way before.

It had closed this way for longer than anything on the surface could count.

But this time, in the vast downward force of it, there was a minute accommodation.

A fraction of a fraction.

A space left uncrushed.

The ground released its pressure.

Fog settled.

Water resumed its crawling.

The country looked empty again: flat, cold, wet, ancient beyond usefulness.

Yet far beneath the surface, in a place no creature could reach and no instrument could name, the deep labor had altered its rhythm.

Not because it had been corrected.

Not because it had been forgiven.

Because, somewhere within its endless work, it had encountered the possibility that labor was not the only proof of being alive.

And because it was alive, it learned.
