The power does not arrive as a light.

Light would be too simple a verdict. It would strike the black water into mirrors, scatter the blind fish, wake the old human shapes in the creature and call them by names they have long since dissolved. It would make the room choose: ruin or sanctuary, grave or garden, wound or door.

The power has no wish to make it choose.

For a long while, it only attends.

It attends from the pale breadth that sometimes gathers above the broken ceiling. It attends through the clear cold water entering through the seam. It attends in the silence between the ticking stems. Nothing in the basin is hidden from it, but it does not turn knowledge into possession. It understands that the creature is not trapped in the flooded room exactly, nor born there exactly. It understands that the room is not merely drowned, that the crack is not merely a crack, and that the impossible water has not risen from below by accident.

It sees the old shape beneath the new ones.

Once, there had been a room built for a human purpose. Once, there had been weather. Once, there had been an event no surviving thing could describe properly: water entering, clay opening, boundaries loosening. The place had not been destroyed. It had been asked a question for which architecture had no answer.

So it became a different answer.

The creature, too, is an answer.

It has gathered all that could not leave cleanly: the pressure of flood, the unfinished memory of hands, the persistence of root and mineral, the small patient intelligence of silt. It does not suffer as a person suffers, though there are remnants within it that remember suffering. It does not need rescue. But neither is it beyond the reach of care.

The benevolent power recognizes this distinction.

And so it does one small thing.

It gives the place permission.

Not permission to become anything in particular. Not permission to rise, drain, heal, return, or be restored. It does not command the waters to recede. It does not seal the seam in the clay. It does not rebuild the walls or lead the creature upward by a hand of fire. Such mercies would be forms of fear.

Instead, it alters the oldest law in that drowned room.

Until then, everything which entered the place had had to become part of it eventually. Leaves were softened, clay was grayed, metal darkened, memories released in bubbles, even the paths of fish and snails were absorbed into its slow, receptive body. Nothing was lost, but nothing could remain entirely itself.

The power changes this by the width of a breath.

Now, once in a great while, a thing may pass through the basin without being claimed.

A leaf may descend from the flooded world and remain a leaf: bruised, brown, carrying the map of its journey in its torn veins. A snail may cross the old wall and leave behind a trail that is not a preparation but a record. The blind fish may enter the doorway and emerge from a place that exists, truly exists, on the other side. A silver mote may rise through the ceiling and arrive not in emptiness, but beneath a reed-bed where wind moves over shallow water.

Most importantly, the creature may gather itself at the crack, kneeling in its translucent wholeness, and hear something other than the immense patience below.

It hears, very faintly, a possibility.

Not a voice. The power knows better than to speak in words to a being whose language is pressure, mineral taste, inclination, and the long correspondence of roots. The possibility comes as a new current in the clear water: not drawing outward, not drawing downward, simply continuing.

A current that does not end at the walls.

The creature listens.

Its many fingers rest against the sharp clay. Its faceless depth stirs with images: the dry room, the bright window, the green distance beyond the transparent stone. For the first time, another image appears among them.

A room not flooded.

Not restored, not returned to what it was. A room open on every side, filled with rain-bright air and tall grasses growing through its floor. The brass handle green in sunlight. The jar still holding its dark round water. The ladder rising three rungs and then becoming the branch of a willow. The creature present there, not made solid, not made human, but visible in the shade beneath the waterline of a pond that no longer needs to be a prison or a hiding place.

It does not understand the image.

It does not need to.

The power leaves it there, not as a promise but as a seed.

Then it withdraws without departing.

In the following season, no miracle is noticed from above. The basin remains flooded. Storms pass. Reeds bend. The dark water keeps its secrets. The walls continue leaning by almost nothing. The cold spring continues its slow impossible rise.

But at the narrow tear in the clay, beside the white root, another root appears.

It is not white.

It is green.

It grows upward for a while through mud where no light has ever reached. Then it turns—not toward the ceiling, not toward the old doorway, but toward the wall beyond which no room was built.

The creature notices.

It does not pull the root free. It does not guide it. It only places one pale hand nearby, close enough that the new growth can feel, if roots feel such things, that it is not growing alone.
