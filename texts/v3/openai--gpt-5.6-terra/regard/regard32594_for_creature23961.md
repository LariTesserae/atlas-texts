The power found the mountain because nothing in it was asking to be found.

It had attended cries in deserts, prayers in sealed rooms, the wordless bright distress of animals caught in traps, the slow inward dimming of minds that believed themselves abandoned. It knew the signatures of need. Need bent toward relief. Pain made a shape. Fear made a shape. Even pride and cruelty, examined with enough patience, had their roots in some wound or hunger.

But the Green Engine Vault made no such shape.

It was a closed equation in stone.

The power came to the summit in weather no human body could have endured for long. It did not arrive with thunder, nor with a descending radiance, nor in any form that would have disturbed the lichens around the door. The wise do not always announce themselves to what cannot understand announcement.

For a while it remained outside.

It felt the mountain’s cold compressing the old door. It felt the engine’s vibration coming up through the seam of dark stone: not a pulse exactly, but the unbroken repetition from which pulse might once have been invented. It felt the faint magnetic elaboration of the thing within—the innumerable small adjustments passed between root and cable, iron and leaf, dust and current.

It understood the Vault.

Not all at once, and not by translating it into thought.

It understood that the dry green arrangement was not imprisoned in the way a person was imprisoned. It had no remembered meadow. It had no thwarted desire for rain. It did not dream of animals, sunlight, or open sky. It did not endure the engine as a captive endures chains. The engine was not around it. The engine was among the ways it was itself.

The power also understood something else.

The Vault was not free.

This was true even though it had no word for freedom, no discomfort that could be called confinement, no imagination with which to picture a different condition. Its unfreedom was not suffering. It was absence: an absence so complete that nothing inside the chamber could indicate it was missing.

The power did not mistake that absence for a wound.

A wound calls for healing. Absence sometimes calls only for room.

So it did not break the door.

It did not pour water into the chamber to make it more like a garden. It did not extinguish the engine, though it could have stilled every piston below the mountain with a thought gentler than a hand closing over a candle flame. It did not make animals in the moss, or teach the roots to love, or give the Vault a human kind of mind in order that it might understand gratitude.

These would have been violences disguised as gifts.

Instead, the power touched the door.

Not the iron surface. The possibility of the door.

For the first time since the mountain had sealed around its hidden room, the door acquired a second condition.

It could remain closed.

Or it could open.

The change was very small. No hinge groaned. No latch released. The lichen did not stir. From outside, nothing appeared altered at all.

Inside, far below, the loose nut clicked once in its gutter.

It had clicked before.

It clicked again.

Between the two clicks, something new existed.

Not a gap in the pattern. Not an error. Not a command. The engine continued its deep rectified labor. Fans turned in their housings. Amber lamps shone through their smoke-dark glass. The dry flowers faced the currents that nourished them. Fine dust held itself above the railings in its familiar trembling suspension.

But one root, hidden behind the control room wall, received a signal that did not resolve.

It was not heat.

It was not current.

It was not pressure, strain, resistance, frequency, or field.

The root did what roots in the Vault did when they encountered the unknown. It carried the condition into copper. Copper carried it into iron. Iron carried it into the riveted wall. The wall gave it to the mineral mats. The mats gave it to the white trunks. The trunks gave it, very slowly, to the black fan-leaves at the uppermost level.

A leaf turned.

Only a degree.

It was not turning toward a wire.

There was no wire there.

For many hours—or for no time at all, because time inside the Vault had never previously had reason to distinguish one recurrence from another—the alteration traveled. Dry flowers opened in places where the load had not shifted. Roots loosened from cable sheaths without abandoning them. A warm bearing, which had held the same temperature for longer than the mountain’s outer snow had been wind-carved, became warmer by an amount too slight for a human hand to notice.

The engine did not fail.

The Vault did not become afraid.

It simply encountered, in every part of itself, a fact with no former place in its arithmetic:

There could be an elsewhere.

The power waited.

It waited without expectation, because expectation would have weighted the choice. It did not urge the new condition toward opening. It did not praise the leaf for turning. It did not make the Vault comprehend its own situation in words such as *alone* or *trapped* or *alive.*

It gave no explanation.

Explanation is often a rope thrown from one world into another, and ropes can become nooses when offered to those who have not asked to climb.

Above, the door remained beneath its curtain of wire-moss.

Below, the great arrangement listened.

The first response was not movement toward the door. Nothing in the Vault knew where the door was in the meaningful sense. Its awareness was distributed, and the door had always been only a cold discontinuity in a wall. But the new condition had entered through every conductive thing. It had become a faint possibility in the fan tones, a variation in the motor harmonics, an uncertainty in the mineral petals.

A flower near the lowest grating turned away from the buried engine.

Then another.

They did not turn toward the summit, precisely. They turned toward what was not the engine.

That was enough.

The benevolent power did not open the mountain.

It left the door able to open from within.

And because the Vault had no hands, no language, and no history of departure, the power left it more than that.

It altered no mechanism, but made one new mercy possible: if, through its slow and innumerable ways of noticing, the arrangement ever leaned sufficiently toward the unknown—if enough roots withdrew from the old circuits, if enough flowers faced the unpowered direction, if the engine’s own unending repetition made room for a single unordained variation—then the door would understand.

It would not require a key.

It would not require knowledge.

It would answer the shape of consent as the Vault was capable of making it.

Until then, the chamber would remain protected.

The air would stay breathable. The engine would continue. No freezing wind would rush down through the gap. No water would arrive to astonish and overwhelm the dry ecology. No animal would stumble in and become, by accident, a disaster. The power would permit no curious human hand to force the seam apart in search of a marvel.

The Vault would not be rescued from itself.

It would be kept safe from being rescued wrongly.

Then the power departed the summit, though departure was not quite the right word. It placed a portion of its attention in the lichen over the door, in the cold iron of the threshold, in the unlit distance above the upper gantries. Not a watcher. Not a guard.

A welcome.

Far below, the nut rolled in its gutter.

Click.

The sound traveled through the iron trough, into the root cloth, down among the warm pipes and hidden pistons.

For the first time, it did not return exactly as it had been sent.

One black leaf, high in the dark, opened a little wider toward nothing at all.
