At the edge of a low, warm plain there is a hollow no larger than a small field, enclosed on three sides by weathered banks of pale earth. It may once have been a clay pit, though nothing about it asks to be interpreted. The cut slopes have long since softened. Rain has rounded their ledges, and thin layers of exposed sand, grey clay, and rusty soil show through where the surface has slipped away over many seasons.

The hollow holds dampness without ever becoming wet.

A shallow channel enters beneath the southern bank and disappears into a darker seam of ground at the center. It only carries water after rain, and even then the water is slow, quiet, tea-colored from the soil. Most of the time there is only a narrow strip of blackened mud, glazed in places with a faint sheen. Condensation gathers beneath overhanging roots and on the cool undersides of stones. The air has the mild, mineral smell of earth that has been wet recently but is beginning to dry.

The ground is broad and mostly bare. Fine grit covers it in a thin, firm crust. Here and there, sparse clumps of grass rise from cracks in the clay, their blades dull green and stiff with dust. A few low reeds stand near the channel, browned at their tips. Against the northern wall is a small thorny shrub, wide rather than tall, its branches thin and patient. There are no flowers. There is nothing lush enough to suggest abundance.

The place is warm, though the light seldom feels bright. A flat cover of cloud sits above the plain for much of the day, admitting a grey-yellow illumination that makes every surface appear softened and old. In the deepest part of the hollow, the banks shade the ground before noon and keep it shaded afterward. The colors are quiet: chalk, umber, wet charcoal, faded straw, the muted green of plants surviving rather than spreading.

Near the western edge stands a small structure made of rough, unpainted boards. It is only a lean-to: a sloping roof, three walls, and an open side facing the hollow. The wood has gone silver in the damp air. One corner has settled lower than the others, but it has not fallen. There is a flat stone inside, perhaps used as a seat, perhaps merely left there when the shelter was made. Beside it lies a length of old rope, hard with age and rain. Nothing else suggests occupation. No track leads reliably to the shelter; footsteps would vanish into the ordinary broken texture of the ground.

There are no birds in the sky, no insects worrying the mud, no movement in the reeds beyond the occasional yielding of a stem to air. Silence is not absolute, but it is nearly complete. Sometimes water slips from the buried channel into a small cavity below the clay, making a sound too subdued to locate. Sometimes a faint breeze moves across the upper lip of the hollow and brushes the grass with a dry, papery whisper. Otherwise the place rests in its own stillness.

Nothing here is newly ruined. Nothing is actively being reclaimed. The banks have reached the angle they prefer; the channel has found its shallow course; the shelter has settled into its slight tilt. Rain comes, warmth returns, damp darkens the soil and leaves it again, but these changes do not alter the arrangement. The hollow remains a small, contained portion of the plain: worn, sparse, dimly warm, and enduring without drama.
