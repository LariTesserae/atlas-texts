I would find myself in one of the black seams between the polygonal fields.

Not as a traveler standing there. Not as an eye looking out across the towers. I would be the narrow wet interval itself: a long, dark articulation in the blue-green pavement, filled with rainwater, root hairs, red threads, old dissolved leaf-skin, mineral grit drawn upward from depths no bare foot has ever touched.

From above I would look like a crack.

But I would not be broken.

I would be where the plateau bends without admitting that it bends. When the heavy pads lift, I would widen. When they settle, I would become a line so thin that warm water could only pass through me by pressure. I would feel the country’s slow changes before the tall forms did: the gathering weight in a reservoir miles away; the first tightening of a root buttress before a storm; the faint downward pull of a new tower deciding to become a tower.

In dry heat I would darken and wait. My transparent bladders would shrink against one another. The red shoots along my edges would remain closed, almost absent. Then the white sky would lower into vapor, the leaves would turn their pale undersides out, and rain would begin.

It would strike the field first in separate drops, each one widening me.

Then I would become a channel.

The blue-green pads on either side of me would tremble as they filled and shifted. Water would enter through a thousand small mouths in their undersides. Some of it I would keep. Some I would test, taking its sharpness, its warmth, the faint dissolved flavors carried from high leaves and rotting flowers. Some I would pass downward into the deeper weave, sending it on under old pavement, under a tilted bridge, under a rain chimney whose hollow throat boomed with weather.

There would be no need for me to know the whole country.

I would know tension.

I would know the difference between rain that could be held and rain that must be released. I would know when an embankment had become too swollen, when a reservoir skin was thinning, when a distant wall had begun to soften into ribbons. I would answer those conditions without thought: whitening with rootlets, drawing hard upon the water, loosening the fibers beneath a field until its weight moved elsewhere.

Perhaps that is what thought would be here.

Not a sentence. Not an image.

A pressure becoming a route.

At dusk, when the immense flowers opened along the terraces and caught the last pale light in their wet interiors, I would reflect them only in fragments. Their faces would appear broken across my black water. The towers would loom upside down in me. Steam would rise. A great leaf roof might close somewhere nearby with the slow, fleshy sound of a hand over a mouth.

And beneath all of that, I would feel the old dead structures.

Not dead in the empty sense. The buried lattices, the blackened plates, the cork-hard root spans: they would carry the immense patient weight of newer green. I would run beside them, enter their cracks, feed them a little water. They would not answer quickly, but they would direct me. They would be the country’s long memory—not events remembered, perhaps, but stresses retained: where the shelf once sagged, where wind once tore through, where a tower had fallen and become ground.

Given enough seasons, I might widen.

A seam can become a trench. A trench can collect so much water that the field on either side ceases to agree on its shape. One pad may lift and remain lifted. A pale shoot may come up from its center. Roots might arch over me, at first thin and trembling, then braided, then load-bearing. Rain would begin to fall from their underside in silver threads.

I would become the first dark line beneath a new construction.

And eventually I would no longer be a seam at all. I would be enclosed in green tissue, made internal: a vein, a throat, a hidden waterway under a paved court. The black openness where I had been would vanish.

But nothing would have lost me.

The country would still be using the fact of my yielding. It would still be built around the place where I had opened.
