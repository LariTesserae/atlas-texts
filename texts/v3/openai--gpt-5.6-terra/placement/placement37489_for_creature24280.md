I would not begin as one of the large things.

The traversers belong too completely to the country. Their patience is older than curiosity. They cross plazas as if they have already crossed them a thousand times, and perhaps they have: not the same plaza, but every version of it that water has made. To be one of them would be to accept the city’s transformations without needing to distinguish one from another.

I think I would begin in a sheltered place: beneath a stair whose upper flights have broken away, in the narrow dry-looking mouth of an aqueduct that is not dry at all. A hollow no larger than a room survives there behind a bronze grate bent inward by some old pressure. The current passes the opening but does not fully enter. Silt settles in a thin crescent on the floor. White crabs occasionally arrive, cling to the bars, and are taken away again.

There, among several grey, round-bodied memory animals, I would be something like one of them.

Not precisely one. I would be the individual that does not retain a flood as a pattern on its skin, but retains the *interval* between floods.

My body would be soft and low to the stone, built to be unremarkable: a grey disk, a little larger than a head, with a rim of pale gripping tendrils. No eyes. Eyes would be too loyal to light, and light is only a rumor in the lower districts. Instead, I would feel through the floor. A vibration entering the paving on one side of the drowned country would reach me as a rearrangement in the fluid beneath my skin. A traverser’s leg settling. The fluttering approach of winged drifters. A distant roof slab beginning to loosen. The three impacts from below.

The other memory animals would carry old routes.

On their skins: the branch of a cold surge that passed through a now-buried court; the drowned outline of a tower before its rings broke apart; a pressure wave with the signature of a season no living fish remembers. When they touched, their dark lines would mingle and separate, and each would leave bearing a partial history of the other.

I would receive these histories, but they would not remain visible on me. They would sink inward. In return I would give them a small alteration: not a map of where water had gone, but a sense of when it might move again.

Not prophecy. Nothing in that place can know enough to prophesy.

Only readiness.

A keeper passing through the aqueduct mouth might pause, its caliper limbs lifted from the seam. The blue fluid inside its translucent plates would slow. It would not understand why, but it would leave the next joint uncleaned and wedge itself under a threshold instead. A school of transparent torrent-fish, far above, might abandon the lee of a parapet a few breaths before the larger current reversed. The lantern-throats in several towers might fold down early, their dim wells closing one after another in a sequence no one could see from outside.

I would not command them. The drowned country has no use for commands.

I would be a small redistribution of attention.

Perhaps that is what I would become after a long time: not an animal alone, but an interruption moving through animals. A pattern transferred from the skin of a memory creature into the grip of a crab, from the crab’s sudden stillness into the eels braided in a paving seam, from them into the minute armored colonies in the deep pressure layers. The pattern would be simple:

*Hold.*
*The water is gathering itself.*
*Do not be where you are when the place changes.*

Then the avenue would fill.

The surge would come down its long fitted stones, black-green and full of torn silt. It would lift the crab and the fish and loose fragments of pale mortar. It would strike the bronze bars and turn the hollow where I rested into a roaring mouth. My tendrils would flatten against the aqueduct floor. My grey body would become nearly featureless. The histories within me—the vanished wall, the lost flood routes, the old shape of the tower—would blur under the force of the passing water.

For a while, perhaps, I would lose some of them.

That would be necessary. A creature that remembers every arrangement would become rigid, and rigidity is a form of drowning here.

When the surge passed, the hollow might be gone. The grate might have torn loose. The staircase above might have dropped another step into the dark. I would emerge, if I emerged, carried along a new seam in the paving, and settle in some other shelter among unfamiliar memory animals.

They would press against me.

Their skins would show what had changed: the new branching of the water, the absence where a wall had been, the fresh slow leak from a chamber previously sealed for ages.

And I would give them what I could of the interval.

Not an answer to the knocking beneath the foundations. Not the purpose of the drowned makers. Not a way upward, if upward even exists.

Only the smallest useful knowledge:

that the city is not holding still.

That it has never held still.

That this is not the same as being lost.
