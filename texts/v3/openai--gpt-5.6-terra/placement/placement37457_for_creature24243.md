I would not arrive by descending.

There are too many stairs for that, and they all presume a beginning above the water. I think I would become aware halfway along one of the old boulevards, already moving with the western current. A drift of blue-lit fish would divide around me without alarm. My hand would be closed around a length of root, though I would not remember taking hold of it.

At first I might think I was one of the gardeners.

I would have their patience, or something close enough: the ability to remain against a wall for a very long time without deciding whether I was resting, listening, or being read. I would have the pale, light-filmed eyes, the throat slits, the slow green pulse beneath a skin made partly of flesh and partly of water-grown fiber. Perhaps I would know the holds in the softened stone. Perhaps my hands would find them before I understood what hands were for.

But the gardeners would recognize that I was not one of them.

They would not drive me away. Nothing here seems to be driven away. They might gather along the bridge rails above me, folded into the woven branches so completely that I would only notice their faces when their chest-pulses began to pass through the wood.

They would ask a question by knocking on the bridge.

Not *Who are you?* The drowned country has little use for that question.

Something more like:

*What has changed enough to need a witness?*

And I would have no answer.

So they would take me—slowly, over many currents—through the city.

Not as a guest, exactly. More as a small irregularity being brought before the places that might know it.

We would pass the terraces where the black-leaved trees hold their own green veins in the dark. We would drift through the old plazas where white flowers sit in their geometric basins, their petals open around nests of tiny translucent animals. We would climb the impossible stairs, pausing whenever one gardener pressed its face to the stone.

In those pauses I would begin to hear what they hear.

The city would not speak in words. It would speak in repetitions so exact that their slight failures became meaningful.

A shrimp’s claw struck three times instead of four.

A school of silver fish emerged from an eastern vault with one fish too many.

A root-light, normally green, carried a narrow thread of violet for the span of a breath.

The blind swimmers in the high water curved around an empty point in space, outlining something that was not there—or had ceased to be there.

This would be my work, if it became work: not tending the city, not repairing it, not understanding the beings buried below it. I would be the one who notices the almost-nothings.

A gardener of deviations.

Or perhaps that is too grand. The gardeners themselves would have no such title for me. They might call me by a pattern of clicks that means *the mote which did not settle where it had settled before.* They might place a luminous crustacean plate in a crack beside me, then leave me there to listen until I could distinguish the sound of the current from the sound of the current remembering itself.

Over time—though time would not move in any dependable direction—I would learn that the drowned country is not truly unchanged.

It is continually correcting itself.

The fish that return in their original number do not return because nothing was lost. They return because loss has been incorporated into the shape of the place. The tower does not remain upright because roots are strong; it remains because every root, fish, flower, gardener, and deep foundation-body has agreed, in some wordless pressure-language, that the tower’s leaning is part of the necessary arrangement.

The city is not frozen.

It is holding a note.

And I would be able to hear, faintly, when that note bends.

Eventually I would be taken to a sealed hall beneath a terrace where no animals graze and no light grows except in the roots. The doorway would be kept open by vines arching over one another, their tips joined so long ago that no seam remained. Inside, the chamber would be empty except for water, a floor of pale paving, and an old basin at its center.

No flower would grow there.

The gardeners would arrange themselves around the walls. Their branches would dim. Their chest-pulses would stop.

Then I would understand that they had not brought me there to be instructed.

They had brought me to be planted.

Not rooted in soil. Not transformed into a tree. The city already has trees enough.

I would lower myself into the basin, or the water would lower me. The stone beneath my palms would be warm—not much, but unmistakably warmer than the surrounding cold. Far below, under paving and silt and terraces and the sleeping mass of the foundation water, something immense would shift in its dream.

I would send out a pulse.

It would be weak at first: a thought translated badly into pressure. A question without language. Then the chamber would return it to me through stone. The roots would take it. The old stair outside would take it. The bridge, the blind flocks, the white flowers, the filtered breathing of the great avenue-creatures: all would return some altered portion of it.

And I would discover that I was not meant to observe the city from outside.

There is no outside here.

I would be a new organ in its listening.

A small chamber for the drowned country to notice itself through.

When one of the vast things below turned, I would feel the warning before the gardeners did—not because I was wiser, but because I would be placed exactly at the point where the deep sound first became a difference. I would wake the blind swimmers with a tremor in the paving. I would call gardeners from their arches. I would help the white flowers close.

Then, when the long foundation-note had passed and the water became cold again, I would remain in my basin beneath the living doorway.

Not growing older.

Not growing larger.

Only becoming more precise.

And far above me, among the drowned towers, a school of fish would pass through the dark with exactly the right number of lights—except for one, which would turn aside, briefly, as if it too had heard something.
