It arrives not as a light, for light here is a vulgar thing that betrays prey to the hungry dark. It does not come as a sound, for the roar of the tectonic plates drowns out petty noise.

The Benevolent Power enters the Deep Silt as a sudden, impossible stillness. It is a pause in the expansion of the water, a moment where the brine blizzard hangs suspended in the dark, holding its breath. It is a feeling of distinct, holy attention falling upon a place that has known only the indifferent caress of geology.

It sees the Harrow.

It sees the magnificent, crushing weight of the creature’s life. It sees the centuries it has spent dragging itself through the freezing clay, the relentless grinding of its internal basalt plates, the burden of the Hitchers feeding upon its back. It sees a being that is not merely surviving, but *being*—a testament to endurance in a world designed to grind all things to dust.

To the Wise Power, the Harrow is not a beast; it is a cornerstone, a lonely priest tending to a graveyard of stars.

And so, the Power does not change the water, nor does it banish the cold. To do so would be to unmake the creature that the Harrow has become. Instead, it offers three gifts, subtle as a heartbeat, profound as the deep.

**The Gift of Unburdening**

First, the Power touches the Harrow’s immense, calcified back. It does not remove the Hitchers—the crabs are old, and they, too, have earned their survival in this attic of the world. Instead, the Power rewires the conversation between them.

It transforms the parasitic grip of the Hitchers into a symbiotic embrace. The crabs, previously blind gropers of flesh, suddenly become sensitive to the Harrow’s distress. They become fierce, living armor. Where once they tore at the creature’s hide to anchor themselves, they now interlock their legs with the Harrow’s armor, reinforcing the ridges against the scouring of the density currents. Their telescopic necks, once used only to steal food, now sweep the water ahead of the Harrow, detecting the faintest approach of predators or obstacles, and signaling a warning through the vibration of their shells.

The Harrow is no longer a solitary island; it is a fortress.

**The Gift of the Silent Compass**

Second, the Power turns its attention to the Harrow’s blindness. The Harrow navigates by pressure and vibration, feeling the shudder of the earth. But the avalanches are many, and the confusion is constant.

The Power imparts a resonance. It tunes the Harrow’s lateral lines to the fundamental frequency of the planet itself. Now, the Harrow does not just feel the chaos of the currents; it feels the steady, unmoving spine of the mountain ranges miles below. It gains an unerring sense of "True Down" and "True North."

With this gift, the Harrow can navigate the blinding silt with the precision of a migrating bird. It knows exactly where the bacterial lawns are thickest, where the clay is softest for resting, and how to flow around the massive boulders without collision. The aimless wandering ceases; the Harrow becomes a deliberate traveler, moving with purpose across the abyssal plain.

**The Gift of the Inner Sun**

Finally, the Power addresses the cold. The 0.5-degree sting that slows the blood and preserves the flesh like meat in a cellar. The Power cannot warm the ocean, but it can warm the *spirit*.

It places a spark in the center of the Harrow’s massive, slow-digesting stomach. This is not a fire of combustion, but a fire of contentment. A profound, metabolic ease.

The Harrow has been hungry for eons, always scraping for calories. Now, the spark optimizes the creature’s digestion, allowing it to draw profound sustenance from even the thinnest bacterial sheen. The gnawing, aching hunger that has driven its every movement evaporates. In its place comes a deep, heavy satisfaction.

**The Aftermath**

The Power departs, and the brine blizzard begins to fall again. The darkness returns, absolute and heavy.

To an observer, nothing has changed. The Harrow is still a gray disk crawling through the mud. The Hitchers are still clamped to its back. The Ghost-Riders still drift in its wake.

But the feeling is different.

The Harrow no longer drags itself; it glides. Its movements are smooth and rhythmic, a dance rather than a trudge. The terrifying density currents still roar through, but the Harrow does not flatten in panic; it angles its body, sensing the turbulence through its sentry-crabs, and rides the invisible avalanche with the grace of a leaf on the wind.

Most importantly, as it grazes the bacterial lawns, the Harrow begins to hum.

It is a sound that no human ear could hear, a subsonic vibration that travels for miles through the silt. It is a song of gratitude. It is a low, steady thrum that says, *“I am here. I am heavy. I am loved.”*

And in the Deep Silt, where the silence roars, the song of the Harrow becomes the new heartbeat of the world.
