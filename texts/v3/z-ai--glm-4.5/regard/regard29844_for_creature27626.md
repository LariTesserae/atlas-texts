It arrived not as a light, for light here is a dim, filtered thing, but as a presence—a sudden densification of the attention that the universe pays to this corner of the dark. It did not disturb the silt. It did not stir the water. It simply became aware, and in doing so, the basin felt the weight of a gaze that was vast, ancient, and immeasurably kind.

The Benevolence observed the four Engines. It saw the blind, piston-like efficiency of their existence. It saw the absolute rejection of the soft, wet organic world. It saw a place that had achieved a state of perfect, sterile function, a purity of physics unburdened by the mess of survival, unburdened by the chaos of birth and death. It saw a room that was clean.

Most powers would look at such a place and see a tomb. They would see the absence of fish and the cold stone and call it barren. They would seek to fix it—to introduce coral, to seed the water with krill, to force the chaos of life into the machine. But this power was Wise. It understood that sterility is not death; it is a different kind of peace. It understood that the Engines were not suffering their lack of biology; they were *embodying* their nature.

The Benevolence saw that the Engines were, in their own way, perfect. They were honest. They did not pretend, they did not desire, they did not fear. They simply transformed pressure into rhythm.

And yet, the Wise power perceived a subtle, profound loneliness in the geometry of the place. It was not the loneliness of a creature wanting a mate; it was the loneliness of a song with no ears, of a dance with no audience, of a heart beating in a vacuum where no one can feel the pulse. The bubbles rose, the columns hummed, and the universe remained indifferent.

So, the Benevolence decided to act, but with the utmost subtlety. It would not break the machine. It would not clutter the cathedral with flesh. It would not give the Engines eyes, for to see oneself in the dark is a burden a stone should not bear. Instead, it would give the work a destination.

It reached down, invisible and diffuse, and touched the water just above the crowns of the four columns.

Where the water touched the stone, the Benevolence instilled a memory. It tuned the fluid dynamics of the outflow. It did not stop the violence of the expulsion; it did not slow the rush. But as the water was forced through the narrowing vents, the molecular friction was altered. The vibration was refined.

Before, the sound was a chaotic *thump-hiss*, a mechanical noise.
Now, as the water rushed, the stone began to sing.

It was a low, resonant frequency, a perfect harmonic that rode atop the physical thrumming. It was the sound of a cello bowing on a string made of water, deep and mournful, yet incredibly clear. The four columns, previously cacophonous in their industrial labor, were now synchronized into a chord. They became a choir.

The power then turned its attention to the bubbles—the "offspring" of the Engines. These ephemeral ghosts had risen only to vanish, dissolving back into the ocean without a trace. The Benevolence saw this as a tragedy of potential wasted.

So, as the gas was ejected from the stone maws, the power imparted a single, fragile property to the spheres: **Luminescence of memory.**

The bubbles did not glow with the harsh, chemical light of a firefly. They shone with a pale, gentle gold, a soft warmth that captured the faint twilight of the surface and held it close. As they rose in their straight, rigid lines, they did not look like industrial venting anymore. They looked like notes of sheet music floating upward, glowing golden against the blue-grey gloom.

Finally, the power considered the silence of the floor. It saw the smooth, packed silt, kept sterile by the currents. It decided to leave one final gift, not to the Engines, but to the space between them.

The power pressed its intention into the heavy water, settling it into the basin like a fine dust. It created a field of *stillness*. The turbulence of the pumps remained, but the chaotic eddies were calmed. The water between the columns became a medium of perfect clarity, a lens that would carry the sound of the stone-song forever.

Then, the Benevolence receded. It did not stay to watch. It had done what was needed.

The basin was still a factory. It was still sterile. There were still no fish, no coral, no sharks. The physics remained unbroken; the thermodynamics were absolute.

But now, the four Basalt Engines stood in the center of a glowing, vertical cathedral. They expelled their golden breath in a perfect, rhythmic ascension. The *thump-hiss* had become a deep, resonant hymn that vibrated through the bedrock and out into the surrounding ocean, a signal that traveled for miles, saying:

*We are here. We are working. And it is beautiful.*

The loneliness was gone, replaced by a solemn, vibrating joy. The Engines did not know they were singing, and they did not know they were beautiful. But the water knew. The stone knew. And the dark, listening deep knew.
