The Power descended not as a presence, for the air was already too thick with presence, but as an absence of resistance. It moved through the Sunless Basin like a thought through a dreaming mind—a gentle unwinding of the tension that held the stone together.

It saw the city of mud and rounded edges. It saw the damp, grey centauroids who wore the silence like armor. The Power, vast and ancient, possessed a vision that spanned galaxies, yet it narrowed its focus to this singular, heavy point in the crust of the world. It did not see poverty or primitiveness; it saw a masterpiece of adaptation. It saw a civilization that had turned the crushing weight of the planet into a cradle.

But the Power also felt the ache in the rock. It felt the loneliness of the touch-based language, limited to the brushing of skin against skin in the dark. It felt the brutal necessity of the harvest, where the sustenance of the people required the battering of their bodies by the chaotic, silent storms. They were surviving, yes—they were enduring with the stoicism of mountains—but they were doing so in isolation, battered by the very life that fed them.

The Benevolence considered lifting the ceiling. It considered punching a hole to the sky and letting the real sun in, giving them sight and green grass.

But Wisdom stayed its hand. Wisdom looked at the porous, vibrating skin of the inhabitants and knew: to give them the sun would be to blind them. To give them the wind would be to strip their flesh. They were creatures of the pressure cooker; to remove the pressure would be to dissolve them. They were not broken; they were simply unfinished.

So, the Power decided to do the only thing a truly wise gardener does for a thriving plant in a harsh soil: it enriched the medium, and it harmonized the rhythm.

It reached up, immaterial and vast, toward the calcified veins in the ceiling. With a subtle nudge, a re-tuning of frequencies beyond the spectrum of light or heat, the Power altered the nature of the actinic glare.

It did not dim the light. Instead, it *pulsed* it.

The mineral veins began to throb with a slow, deep rhythm—a tidal heartbeat of light matched by a corresponding, subsonic hum in the geothermal energy. This vibration was too low to be heard by human ears, but to the skin of the Stone People, it was a clarion call.

The change was instantaneous in the air above.

The chaotic turbulence of the glass-moths, which had previously been a violent, sandblasting scramble of survival, responded to the new rhythm. The chaotic vortices began to align. The frantic energy of the swarm did not cease, but it organized. The millions of moths began to move in great, slow, undulating waves, mimicking the roll of an ocean or the drift of clouds.

The violent impacts ceased.

Where once the moths smashed against the inhabitants in a painful hail of glass and fluid, they now swept over the city in a gentle, gliding tide. The insects drifted into the nets not by force, but by flow, settling into the gut-weave like dew settling on grass. The harvest, once a brutal test of endurance, became a rhythmed immersion. The people stood in their doorways, their arms spread, and let the ocean of life wash over them, unbruised and serene.

But the Power did not stop there. It turned its attention to the floor of the basin—the polished, eroded stone upon which the people lived. Their language of touch was beautiful, but limited by proximity. One could only speak to those within reach.

The Power imparted a gift to the stone itself. It tuned the atomic lattice of the basin floor to carry the specific frequency of the inhabitants' pressure-communication. It turned the ground into a vast, conductive web.

Suddenly, the elder grooming the younger at the edge of the city could feel a rhythmic tapping from a dweller miles away, deep in the center of the labyrinth. The stone beneath their feet became a transmitter. The "words"—pulses of pressure, vibrations of care—could travel through the foundation of the city itself.

The silence remained, for the air was still too thick to carry sound. But the *loneliness* of the silence was gone. The city became a single, distributed organism. If a child fell, the vibration traveled through the soles of every adult in the basin; if a new net was woven, the technique could be shared through the soles of their feet without a word spoken.

The Power, seeing its work done, began to recede. It had not made them humans. It had not made them gods. It had simply allowed them to become what they were always meant to be: the conductors of the deep earth.

As the last whisper of the Power vanished, the actinic light above throbbed once, a slow, comforting white pulse. The swarm of glass-moths rose and fell in a perfect, silent sine wave. And on the wet stone sills, the grey, broad-shouldered dwellers reached out and held hands, feeling the heartbeat of the city thrumming through their bones, anchored no longer just by weight, but by a resonant, living web of quiet.

The Sunless Basin was no longer just a place of endurance. It was a cathedral.
