It would begin not with a flash of light, but with a sudden, impossible drop in temperature.

The Benevolent Power—let us call it the Gardener, or perhaps the Mercy—does not arrive with the blaring trumpets of salvation, for noise is violence in a place of glass. It arrives as a sensation of *yielding*. The oppressive, suffocating weight of the air, that static heaviness that presses down on the basalt and the chitin, simply… relaxes. It is the exhalation of a planet holding its breath for a thousand years.

If the Gardener were to speak, its voice would not be a sound, but a vibration felt in the marrow of the stone, a hum that resonates at the frequency of water.

Here is what the Gardener would do, step by careful step, to heal the kiln without shattering the crystal.

### 1. The Lubrication of the Angles
First, the Gardener would address the geometry. The Courtyard is a place of Euclid gone mad, of folding walls and non-Euclidean pain. The inhabitants, particularly the Claw-Kin and the Corner-Lurkers, are victims of a sharp, hostile mathematics.

The Gardener would introduce a new concept to the physics of this place: **The Curve.**

It would touch the sharp, knife-blade edges of the basalt pavement and the tessellated walls. Instantly, the agonizingly straight lines would soften. The "wrong" angles—the ones that hurt the eye—would be rounded off. The walls would stop closing in like a throat and begin to expand like lungs.

This would be a mercy to the **Corner-Lurkers**. Those shadows, forced to hunt in the periphery, would find that the world is no longer a trap of jagged edges. As the geometry softens, the Lurkers would lose their predatory need to consume cohesion. Without the harsh "errors" in the architecture to sustain them, they would not die; instead, they would be transmuted. The charcoal shadows would gain depth, becoming volumetric clouds of soot and ash, free to drift in the vaults rather than clinging to the cracks. They would become the weather of the world, bringing shade rather than annihilation.

### 2. The Gift of the "Slow Rain"
The Gardener knows that liquid water is death here; it would shatter the glass ferns and crack the clay shells of the fauna. To dump rain upon the Inner Sun would be to destroy it.

Instead, the Gardener weeps **Oil.**

From the high, distant vault of stone, a thick, golden resin would begin to drip. It is not water; it is a sacred unguent, the "blood of the earth." It falls slowly, viscous and warm.

When a drop strikes a **Basalt-Back**, it does not extinguish the fire in its belly. It coats the creature's underbelly, lubricating the rasp that grinds the obsidian moss. The screaming friction of their movement would cease, replaced by a low, contented purr. They no longer need to burn their own fuel to move; the oil feeds the fire gently, allowing the great tortoises to glide without pain.

The oil coats the **obsidian moss**, dulling its razor edge. The ground becomes soft, yielding. The "crunch" underfoot is replaced by a silence, a dampening of the harsh acoustics.

### 3. The Illumination of the Prism
The **Refractions**—those brittle birds of bone and mica—live in fear, their volatile internal gas turning them into bombs. They communicate through flashes of desperate light.

The Gardener would grant them a Song.

By altering the atmosphere slightly, infusing it with a light, suspended mist of gold dust from the falling oil, the air becomes a conductor for vibration. The Refractions discover that their diamond beaks, previously used only for siphoning gas, can hum. They no longer need to explode to communicate; they can sing. Their songs are low, glass-harmonica melodies that ripple through the golden fog.

Furthermore, the Gardener touches the **quartz trees**. The volatile, hissing gas inside them is stabilized. It ceases to be a fuel for combustion and becomes a source of bioluminescence. The Courtyard, previously lit by a flat, oppressive light, begins to glow from within. The trees pulse with a soft, amber rhythm. It is no longer the Inner Sun; it is the Inner Lantern.

### 4. The Release of the Masons
The **Silent Masons**—the tall, faceless gaunts who build endlessly to hold the breaking reality together—are the most tragic figures. They are the immune system of a dying world, working without soul.

The Gardener approaches them. It does not stop their hands, for they know nothing else. Instead, it changes *what* they build.

The Gardener whispers a blueprint into their stone minds: "Build windows, not walls."

The Masons cease their frantic reinforcement of the vault. They turn their chisels to the floor. They begin to carve channels, guiding the flowing golden oil into rivers. They begin to carve arches that lead *upward*, toward the ceiling.

As they carve, the reality breakdown stops. The folding walls stabilize. The "peeling paint" effect of the universe dissolving halts. The Masons are no longer repairing a prison; they are sculpting a cathedral. Their labor gains meaning. They are no longer slaves to decay; they are artists of preservation.

### 5. The Transformation of the Silent Kin
The **Clay-Kin**, those crabs of ceramic who eat the errors of math, find their hunger vanishing as the Gardener heals the geometry. With the angles smoothed and the non-Euclidean folds cured, their source of food is gone.

But they are not left to starve. The golden oil that now coats the ground hardens into a beautiful, colorful glaze. The Claw-Kin begin to decorate themselves. They stop chipping at the air and begin painting the walls. They take the colored dust from the air—the residue of the healed reality—and they create vast murals on the basalt columns. They become the historians of the world, recording its memory in pigment rather than consuming its pain.

### The Final State: The Sanctuary of Glass

When the Gardener leaves, the Courtyard is no longer a kiln. It is still warm, yes, but it is the warmth of an incubator, not a furnace. The air smells now of ozone, amber, and ancient dust—a scent like old books and incense.

The beings are still made of stone and glass and chitin. They have not become flesh; that would be a denial of their nature. But they are *softer*. Their edges are rounded. Their movements are fluid.

The "Inner Sun" is gone. In its place is a quiet, glowing respite. The inhabitants no longer endure existence; they enjoy it. The Basalt-Backs glide on rivers of oil. The Refractions sing glass-music to the amber trees. The Corner-Lurkers drift as benevolent fog banks. And the Silent Masons continue to build, carving arches that point not to the walls, but to the stars, waiting for the day the vault opens, not to break, but to bloom.
