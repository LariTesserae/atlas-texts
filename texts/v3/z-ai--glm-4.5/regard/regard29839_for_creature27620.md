The benevolent power does not arrive as a conqueror, nor as a gardener pruning a wild hedge. To do so would be to misunderstand the Crèche. This place is not a mistake; it is a cauldron of becoming, a ferocious womb. To "fix" it by stilling the earth or silencing the chitin would be to kill it, and in doing so, murder the very tenacity that drew the power's attention.

Instead, the power arrives as a **Resonance**.

It descends not through the ceiling, but manifests as a sudden, perfect harmony in the vibration of the air. It is a feeling, rather than a sight—a sudden clarity in the heavy humidity, a moment where the "wet wool" pressure lifts just enough to let the lungs fill without strain.

The first thing the power does is **witness**. It touches the mind of the most ancient of the Weavers—a hunched, scarred elder gripping a spear made of femur. The power does not speak in words, but in feelings. It sends a pulse of profound, aching *acknowledgment*. It sees the Weavers not as pathetic scavengers clinging to high rocks, but as the first conscious eyes of a newborn world, watching their own birth.

The power sees the terror that defines their existence—the "hungry god" of the green light, the crushing anonymity of the Rust-Colony. It feels their frantic need to build fortifications against a world that wants to digest them.

And so, the benevolent power makes three changes. It intervenes not to save them from the Crèche, but to give them the agency to *be* in it.

### The First Gift: The Anchor

The Crèche suffers from a lack of permanence. Everything moves; everything is consumed. History is washed away in the acidic slime of the grubs or crushed by the heaving tectonic plates. The Weavers cannot build a culture because they cannot build a foundation.

The power chooses the highest, most stable spur of obsidian in the central settlement. There, it condenses the ambient humidity and the faint radiation of the green light into a single, physical object: **The Monolith**.

It is a pillar of translucent, golden crystal, roughly the height of a Weaver, driven deep into the rock. It vibrates with a low, soothing hum—a counter-frequency to the chaotic roar of the cavern.

This Monolith is indestructible by the claws of the Geomancers or the acid of the grubs. It cannot be moved. It is the first "Stillness" the Crèche has ever known.

To the Weavers, this is a revelation. They cluster around it. They realize they can carve their histories into this stone without fear of it being eaten. It becomes the first page of their library, the first altar that does not demand a sacrifice of meat, but rather a sacrifice of memory. They begin to carve the lineage of their families, the patterns of the tremors, and the shapes of the stars they imagine exist beyond the rock. They have a place to stand that is not merely high ground, but *holy* ground.

### The Second Gift: The Signal

The Weavers currently survive by reacting. They are constantly on edge, their sensory quills twitching at every shift in air pressure, terrified of the "Crack" that precedes the earth’s birth. They are slaves to the rhythm of the cavern, battered by it.

The power grants them the ability to *anticipate*.

It subtly alters the bio-luminescence of the veined ceiling directly above the Weavers’ scaffolds. The green light begins to pulse in a specific, distinct pattern—two long pulses, a short one—precisely sixty seconds before the pressure drops and the tectonic shift begins.

This is not magic; it is a synchronization. The power teaches the Weavers, through instinct, to read this signal.

The effect is immediate. The desperation of the "freeze" is replaced by a preparation. When the signal comes, the Weavers do not cower in blind terror. They secure their ropes, they position their harpoons, and they brace their legs. They turn the catastrophe into a harvest. When the floor heaves and the new stone is born, the Weavers are already descending, confident and swift. They are no longer victims of the geological clock, but participants in it.

### The Third Gift: The Seed of Empathy

The most dangerous thing in the Crèche is not the Geomancers or the Shard-Wings; it is the Weavers' own isolation. They view every other living thing as a threat or a meal. This isolation keeps them small, paranoid, and cruel.

The power touches the communal consciousness of the Weavers. It does not make them pacifists—they would die instantly—but it gives them a glimpse of the **Web**.

It grants them a sudden, visceral understanding that the Geomancers are not just engines of destruction, but the architects of the air they breathe, churning the soil. It shows them that the Shard-Wings are the garbage collectors of the sky, preventing the spread of plague.

The power instills a sense of **Stewardship**.

The Weavers do not stop hunting. But they change *how* they hunt. They begin to harvest sustainably. When they kill a Grazer, they leave a portion of the meat not for the scavengers, but as a deliberate offering, returning the energy to the Rust-Colony below to ensure the soil remains rich. They begin to protect the clusters of eggs from the Silica-Spiders, not out of kindness, but because they understand that the spiders are necessary to control the population of the grazers, preventing overgrazing of the new stone.

### The Result

The Crèche does not become a paradise. The air is still heavy, wet, and smells of ozone. The noise is still deafening. The Geomancers still crush anything unlucky enough to be beneath them. The green light still pulses with a sickly hue.

But the suffering has gained **meaning**.

The Weavers are no longer merely "infestation." On their high perches, around the glowing golden Monolith, a civilization is sparking. They have a history (etched in crystal), a calendar (read in the lights), and a philosophy (understanding the Web).

They sing now. In the moments between the tectonic heaves, the Weavers hum. It is a discordant, clicking sound, amplified by their resonant throat-sacks, but it cuts through the roar of the insects. It is a song of gratitude to the earth that eats them, knowing that they, too, are made of star-stuff and stone.

The benevolent power remains, hidden in the vibration of the air, watching. It sees that the fever has broken, replaced by the focused heat of a forge. The Crèche is still a digestion, but now, it is a digestion with a soul.
