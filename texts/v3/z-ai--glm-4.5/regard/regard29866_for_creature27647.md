The Benevolent Power, let us call it the ** Weaver of Frequencies**, does not descend with a fanfare of trumpets, nor with a blinding flash to rival the sun. Such displays are vulgar here, redundant in a place of absolute light. Instead, the Weaver arrives as a sudden, perfect stillness between heartbeats—a pause in the frantic rhythm of the Hoppers that feels like a benediction.

It perceives the Spire’s Garden not as a desolate rock, but as a vibrating string instrument stretched tight across the roof of the world. It sees the "frantic, hidden riot" of the Hoppers and recognizes the anxiety within their energy—the constant need to move, to pulse, to communicate before the cold swallows them. It feels the jagged loneliness of the Silent Hunters’ ruins and the precariousness of the thermal anomaly that breathes life into this stone lung.

Moved by compassion and wisdom, the Weaver decides to act, but its actions are subtle, woven into the very fabric of the geology and biology so as not to shatter the delicate balance. It does not change the nature of the place; it deepens it.

**First, the Weaver tunes the Stone.**

The limestone of the Spire’s Garden is young and fractured, dead to the ears of most. The Weaver reaches down into the bedrock, a presence felt as a gentle, warming hum that resonates in the soles of the feet, and aligns the crystalline structure of the shelf. It turns the entire three-mile plateau into a sounding board.

*   **The Gift of Resonance:** Previously, a Hopper’s thump could only travel a few dozen yards. Now, the stone carries their vibrations with perfect fidelity across the entire shelf. A "danger" thump at the western cliff edge now resonates instantly to the nurseries in the east. The frantic, repetitive signaling ceases; the Hoppers no longer need to shout over the silence. Their communication becomes a symphony of efficiency, a shared consciousness of vibration that allows them to conserve their precious energy. They become calmer, their movements less erratic, no longer driven by the isolation of the vacuum.

**Second, the Weaver sanctifies the Ruins.**

The Power perceives the grief in the abandoned tools of the Silent Hunters—the bone harpoons and the stone bowls. It sees that the Hoppers use them, but they do not understand them. The Weaver infuses these artifacts with a lingering, passive warmth, a memory of the hands that shaped them.

*   **The Eternal Hearth:** The stone bowls used as birthing pools are blessed. The Weaver connects them to the deep thermal anomaly below, causing the bowls to generate a gentle, constant heat, regardless of the sun's position. The water within them never freezes. The mortality rate of the newborn pink Hoppers, once high in the sudden chill of twilight, plummets.
*   **The Sentinel Song:** The bone harpoons and stacked cairns are tuned to the wind that blows *above* the shelf. While the air on the shelf is still, the wind aloft is fierce. The Weaver enchantes the ruins so that they hum a low, sub-audible tone when a storm is approaching from the peaks above. The Hoppers, feeling this vibration through their feet, know to retreat to the mossy caverns hours before the gale strikes. The ruins become true sentinels once more, protecting the inheritors of the land they once watched over.

**Finally, the Weaver calibrates the Lens.**

The "shadow" at the cliff's edge, that weird thermal distortion, is the engine of this ecosystem, but it is chaotic. The moisture it brings is inconsistent. The Weaver touches the air above the precipice, smoothing the turbulence of the updraft.

*   **The Mist of Grace:** The thermal chimney is stabilized. The rising air now lifts in a consistent, rhythmic pulse, matching the slowed heartbeat of the Hoppers. The mist that condenses from it becomes finer, more nutritious—a vapor that settles not just as cold wetness, but as a carrier for microscopic minerals drawn from the deep earth. The moss and lichen, drinking this enriched fog, become vibrant and resilient, their sugary secretions becoming abundant enough that the Hoppers no longer need to compete for them.

The Weaver does not stay. A benevolent power knows that protection can easily become suffocation. Once the changes are set—once the stone sings, the bowls glow with warmth, and the mist rises steady—the power withdraws, leaving only a feeling of profound peace embedded in the limestone itself.

To an outsider, the Spire’s Garden looks exactly the same: bright, cold, and jagged. But the silence is no longer heavy; it is protective. The frantic energy of the Hoppers has evolved into a rhythmic, pulsing harmony. They are no longer survivors in a void; they are the conductors of a great, frozen orchestra, playing upon a stage that was built for them by the earth, and tuned for them by the stars.
