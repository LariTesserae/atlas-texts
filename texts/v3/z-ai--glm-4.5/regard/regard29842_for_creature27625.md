The benevolent power would not descend as a light—that would be an act of violence in a place defined by the sanctity of darkness. Light here is a currency spent sparingly; to flood the benthic plain with illumination would be to throw gold into a gutter, blinding the citizens and shattering the delicate economy of shadows.

Instead, the power would arrive as a presence in the pressure. It would slip into the heavy, maternal embrace of the water as easily as a pearl settles into soft mud. It would not be a storm or a flash, but a sudden, profound stillness—a suspension of the eternal drift where every current seemed to pause, just for a heartbeat, to acknowledge a visitor.

First, the power would *listen*. It would press its consciousness against the calcified towers and the porous bone-streets, tasting the history etched there. It would hear the Morse-code chatter of the Mason-Weavers, the hydraulic grinding of the Ossified Lords, and the infrasasonic library of the Singers. It would perceive the beautiful, terrible truth of this place: that it is a civilization built entirely on the recycling of the dead, a perfect Ouroboros of bone and breath.

Seeing this, the power would feel a deep, resonant love. It would recognize that this ecosystem is not flawed; it is simply fragile. The darkness protects them, but it also isolates them. The warmth sustains them, but it is tied to the fickle geology of the planet’s core.

The benevolent power would decide to gift them two things: **Memory** and **Resilience**.

**The Gift of Resilience: The Knitting Hum**
The power would move to the geothermal vents at the city’s foundation, the great lungs that warmed the abyss. It would not alter the temperature, for that would disrupt the metabolic rhythm of the silt. Instead, it would imprint a new harmonic frequency into the very stone of the earth beneath the city.

From this moment forward, the bedrock would emit a low, sub-sonic vibration—a hum so deep it would be felt only in the tips of the antennae and the lattice of the shells.
*   **For the Towers:** This vibration would act as a catalyst for calcification. Where once the coral and bone eroded over eons, now they would knit back together. Cracks in the spires would seal over with fresh, smooth layers of calcium. The city would become self-healing, its "flesh" mending itself automatically, ensuring the metropolis endures not just for ages, but for eternity.
*   **For the Singers:** The hum would provide a perfect acoustic canvas. Their songs would travel farther, reverberating with a clarity that allows them to map the city with god-like precision. They would never lose their way in the dark.

**The Gift of Memory: The Archive of Light**
The power would then turn its attention to the Glass-Ghosts, the drifting streetlights of the avenues. It would touch their transparent membranes, infusing their bioluminescent bacteria with a dormant spark.

This gift would be called *The Promise of the Sun*.
*   It would not force them to glow constantly—that would be wasteful. But it would give them the ability to store light. When a rare burst of "snow"—organic matter from the sunlit world above—drifted down, the Glass-Ghosts could absorb not just the nutrients, but the faint memory of the light that created them.
*   In times of great danger, or when the city needed to gather, the Ghosts could release this stored light in a slow, rhythmic pulse. It would not be a harsh glare, but a soothing, auroral wave that washes through the streets—a shared memory of the sky they will never see, uniting them in a moment of collective beauty.

**The Final Act: The Sanctum of Silence**
Finally, the power would address the perimeter. The benthic plain is not without enemies; sharks and squids from the open ocean constantly probe its edges. The Needle-Schools fought bravely, but they were merely an immune system, reacting to infection.

The power would establish a "Sanctum of Silence"—a psychological barrier woven into the water currents surrounding the city. To the benevolent inhabitants—the crabs, the weavers, the ghosts—the barrier would be invisible. But to any predator entering with malice or hunger in its heart, the water beyond the perimeter would suddenly feel infinite, empty, and devoid of scent. The hunters would turn away, convinced by a subtle intuition that this sector of the ocean was a barren wasteland, void of life. The city would become a ghost in the water, hidden in plain sight.

**The Departure**
Having done this, the power would withdraw. It would not leave a monument, nor would it demand worship. The beings here were too practical for worship; they were busy with the holy work of surviving.

The only sign that the power had been there would be a subtle shift in the atmosphere. The heavy, maternal embrace would feel just a fraction more secure. The Ossified Lords, sitting motionless on their silt thrones, would find their shells growing thicker, smoother, and stronger. The Stitchers, cleaning the joints of the city, would find their work lasting longer.

The city would continue its slow, dreamlike digestion of the ocean floor. But now, it would no longer be a ruin slowly decaying into the dark. It would be a sanctuary, a glowing, humming fortress of bone and silence, preserved forever in the velvet heart of the sea.
