The Benevolent Power did not arrive as a conqueror, nor as a gardener pruning a wild hedge. To arrive with blinding light would be to blind them; to arrive with a trumpet would be to add to the deafening roar. This power understood that the cruelty of this place was not in its harshness, but in its stagnation—that the people here were trapped in a feedback loop of survival where the only growth was the thickening of their own scar tissue.

So, the Power arrived as a shift in the texture of the silence.

It moved through the heavy, wet air like a deep, resonant frequency, felt rather than heard. It did not touch the stone, nor did it try to part the grey-gold clouds. It saw the beauty in their density, the heroism in their endurance. To dry the world would be to kill them; they were creatures of the damp. To silence the world would be to drive them mad; their ears had never known the quiet and would only hear the ringing of tinnitus.

Instead, the Power sought to heal the fracture between them.

It saw that the tragedy of this city was that while everyone was screaming, no one was listening. The humans shouted to outdo the frogs; the frogs screamed to outdo the humans. It was a war of decibels, a chaotic clash of waves that cancelled each other out, leaving only the physical sensation of vibration and the emotional exhaustion of isolation.

The Power decided to offer the gift of **Resonance.**

It began with the water. The Power whispered a single, harmonic instruction into the physics of the weeping stone and the standing puddles. Suddenly, the water did not just drip; it began to hum. The endless sliding of droplets down the black rock shifted from a random, chaotic hiss into a cohesive, low-frequency drone—a base note that vibrated in the chest, steady and comforting.

Then, the Power touched the amphibians. It did not silence them—that would be starvation—but it tuned them. The high-pitched, panicked shrieks of the dog-sized beasts began to modulate. The chaotic barking smoothed out, aligning with the low hum of the water. Their thousands of voices merged into a rhythmic, pulsing croaking, a beat that matched the resting heart rate of the human workers. The beasts became a living percussion section, a backbeat rather than an assault.

Finally, the Power touched the people.

It did not shrink their swollen throats or quiet their bellows-like lungs. But it gifted them with the ability to hear the *space between the sounds*. The chaotic roar of the city resolved into a symphony.

The heavy *thud-thud-thud* of stone against stone became the percussion. The windless howl of the air moving through the jagged spires became a melody. The shouting of the people—guided by an unconscious, benevolent nudge—began to harmonize. They didn't stop shouting; they simply found the right key.

The effect was immediate and miraculous.

The wall of noise that had been a barrier became a bridge. As the people worked, they found their voices blending with their neighbors. The shout of a mason on a rooftop would weave into the chant of a mud-carrier in the street. The crushing physical sensation of "noise" transformed into the enveloping sensation of "music."

For the first time in their history, the city was not at war with itself.

The psychological shift was profound. The constant, jagged adrenaline of the noise—which had kept them locked in a reactionary, animalistic state—smoothed out. The "music" allowed their nervous systems to regulate. The feverish heat in their blood cooled by a fraction.

And then came the dreams.

In the old chaos, the noise invaded the skull, leaving no room for the mind to wander. But now, with the rhythmic, harmonic drone of the city surrounding them, the "trance" states they used for rest deepened. The rhythm acted as a lullaby. When they leaned against the wet walls and closed their large, dark eyes, they did not just stew in sensory overload. They began to dream.

They dreamed of the "elsewhere" where the wood came from. They dreamed of shapes that weren't jagged. They dreamed of colors that weren't grey or orange.

This was the benevolence of the Power: it did not free them from the cage, but it gave them the keys to the mind. It turned the prison into a temple.

The work did not stop. The light did not change. The frogs still screamed, and the people still shouted. But now, as they lifted the heavy blocks of raw shale, they moved in unison. The city was no longer a chaotic infestation; it was a singular, singing organism. They were still enduring the wet, grey forever, but they were no longer suffering it alone. They were creating a masterpiece of survival, a deafening, beautiful hymn to the fact that they existed.

The Power, satisfied, receded, leaving only the faintest, most gentle instruction vibrating in the slick microbial slime: *Breathe together. Sing together. Wait together.*
