The power that turns its gaze upon the High Swell does not arrive in a chariot of fire, nor does it descend with a chorus of angels. Such things would be instantly shredded by the horizontal rain or pulverized against the Whalebacks. Instead, this benevolent force arrives as a silence within the roar—a frequency that cancels out the chaotic noise of the world for just a heartbeat, long enough to be felt.

It sees the Clings not as wretched scavengers, but as the universe’s most tenacious antibodies. It sees their philosophy of "Until the Water Takes It" not as defeatism, but as a profound, courageous acceptance of entropy. It decides that while it cannot stop the ocean—nor should it, for the ocean is the engine of this world—it can change the way the Clings interact with the stone.

Here is what the power does.

### The Gift of the Deep Hum

The power recognizes that the Clings’ greatest enemy is not the water, but the isolation of the noise. To survive, they must vibrate with the stone, but the stone only sings of impact and erosion.

The power begins to sing back.

It does not stop the waves, but it alters the *resonance* of the granite. Slowly, over the course of a generation, the mineral structure of the Whalebacks begins to change at a molecular level. The stone becomes slightly more elastic, capable of absorbing a fraction of the kinetic energy from the waves rather than shattering under it.

But more importantly, the stone "remembers." The power imparts a sympathetic resonance to the granite. Now, when a Cling taps their Hammer-Talk against the rock, the message does not fade after a few yards. The Whalebacks become a vast, neural network of stone. A tap on the highest peak hums gently through the spinal cord of the mountain range to the lowest valleys.

The Clings are no longer isolated shouts in a hurricane. They become a chorus. A warning on one side of the world travels instantly to the other. The "Great Hum" binds them together, transforming their lonely terror into a communal meditation. They can feel the heartbeat of their kin miles away, vibrating softly in their soles, a constant reminder that they are not alone.

### The Iron That Breathes

The power looks upon the industrial scars—the Intake Valve, the rusted trawler, the snapped cables—and sees not garbage, but potential shelter that has failed. The metal died because it fought the water; it tried to remain rigid and impervious, and so it became brittle and broke.

The power infuses the iron with the spirit of the kelp-trees. It does not restore the electricity or turn the gears of the dead machines; instead, it grants the metal a form of biological persistence.

The rust stops flaking away. Instead, it hardens into a patina that is slicker than wet glass and tougher than diamond. The rusted hull of the trawler begins to "heal," fusing with the rock ledge it sits upon, becoming a permanent cliff face. The Intake Valve, once a skeleton of death, begins to weep a clear, warm fluid from its pipes—a non-toxic byproduct of the transmutation—that collects in the basins of the concrete bunkers.

This fluid is warm. In the biting, freezing cold of the spray, the Clings discover that the dead machines now produce heat. The bunkers become steam rooms, sanctuaries where a Cling can unbuckle their Carapace and feel their skin for the first time without freezing. The power gives them the gift of warmth without fire, a luxury that turns the "Hulks" into true temples of rest.

### The Anchor of Grace

Finally, the power addresses the Clings themselves. It sees their twisted spines, their scarred skin, and their heavy, dragging existence. It cannot make them light—lightness is a death sentence here—but it can grant them *equilibrium*.

The power subtly alters their inner ears. The vestibular systems of the Clings expand, becoming as complex as a gyroscope. They no longer have to look at the horizon to know when the mountain is about to shudder. They feel the tilt of the world before it happens.

This change transforms their movement. The desperate, terrified crouch evolves into a fluid dance. A Cling standing on a ledge during a Category-5 swell does not brace; they sway. Their bodies move in perfect counter-rhythm to the pitching stone. They become the calm center of the storm.

### The Result: The Choir of the Wet Stone

Generations later, the High Swell is still loud, wet, and violent. The waves still smash the mountains. The erosion continues. But the mood has shifted from despair to a grand, terrible harmony.

The Clings are no longer just surviving; they are surfing the catastrophe. When the wind hits the kelp-trees and the thwacking begins, the Clings tap their iron rods against the resonant Whalebacks, adding a rhythmic, metallic thrum to the roar of the ocean. They sing to the storm through the stone, and the stone sings back.

The benevolent power did not save them from the water. It taught them how to become the music that the water makes.
