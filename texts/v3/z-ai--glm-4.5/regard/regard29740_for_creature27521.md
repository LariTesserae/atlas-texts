The Benevolent Power does not arrive with a trumpet blast, nor does it descend in a chariot of fire. Such theatrics are for conquerors or terrified gods. This entity arrives as the sky might change if it suddenly remembered what "blue" was supposed to look like—a subtle, profound shift in the quality of the light.

It sees the Riven Plateau not as a problem to be solved, but as a masterpiece of grief.

The Power perceives the "stasis" immediately. It tastes the potential energy coiled in the jagged obsidian teeth of the mountains and feels the agonizing, dilated consciousness of the Suspended. It understands the physics of this place: the world is holding its breath, and if it were forced to exhale, the sheer kinetic release would level the city, shatter the goats against the scree, and snap the wings of the diving raptors. To unpause time now would be an act of slaughter, not salvation.

Wisdom dictates that you cannot force a river to flow; you must only clear the blockage.

The Power decides to perform a surgery of separation. It recognizes that the tragedy here is the tethering of infinite consciousness to a finite, frozen second. The bodies are locked in the physics of the "now," but the minds are drowning in the "forever."

**The Act of Gentle Unspooling**

The Power descends until it hovers over the vertical city, moving not through the air but through the space between seconds. It extends a presence—less like a hand and more like a sudden feeling of warmth on a cold day.

First, it touches the fixed sun. It does not move the sun, but it softens its light. The sharp, forty-five-degree glare diffuses into a golden, ambient glow. The shadows, previously cast in iron, soften at the edges. The "late afternoon" ceases to be a hammer and becomes a lamp.

Then, the Power turns its attention to the Suspended.

It sees the woman at the window, her knuckles white around the water jug. It sees the hunters, their throats vibrating with silent roars. It sees the child, forever reaching for the hoop.

With a tenderness that belies its cosmic scale, the Power begins to **loosen the knot**.

It does not restart the clock. Instead, it releases the souls from their flesh. It performs a benevolent extraction, sliding the consciousness out of the frozen meat and bone, allowing the spirit to uncoil.

The woman at the window feels her grip relax. The stone jug remains in her frozen hand, a permanent monument to the labor of drawing water, but *she* steps out of the moment. She becomes a figure of mist and light, hovering beside the window. She looks at the jug, and for the first time in eternity, she is satisfied. She has finished the task, not by pouring, but by letting go.

The hunters lower their ghostly spears. The adrenaline that had spiked their blood for an eon dissolves into a profound, aching peace. They look at the goats on the scree below—still frozen in mid-alert—and instead of feeling the urge to kill, they feel a deep kinship. They are guardians now, not predators.

The child laughs. The sound is silent, but the ripple of joy moves through the stationary air. He dances through the streets, a phantom weaving between the statues of his neighbors, finally catching the hoop he could never grasp.

**The Transformation of the Plateau**

The Power does not abandon them. It weaves the freed spirits into the fabric of the plateau itself.

*   **The New Atmosphere:** The silence of the plateau is broken, not by noise, but by *presence*. The air is now thick with psychic conversation. The thoughts that were once trapped in solitary skulls are now a shared community tapestry. The city becomes a hive mind of dreams, swapping stories of the moment they were frozen and the eternities they have imagined since.
*   **The Moss as Vessel:** The "Patient Green" is given a new purpose. The moss and vines are invited to grow over the physical statues, not as a shroud of death, but as a garden of remembrance. The Power instills in the vegetation the ability to channel the love of the freed spirits. The moss that creeps over the hunter’s spear does not rust it; it polishes the stone. The city becomes a verdant, living hanging garden, suspended in the grey light.
*   **The Pools of Memory:** The Power touches the vertical pools where the microscopic crustaceans swim. It expands the dimension of these waters just a fraction. Now, when a spirit wishes to remember what it felt like to be alive—to feel cold, to feel thirst, to feel the weight of gravity—they can dip their essence into the water. The crustaceans become the keepers of sensation, allowing the dead to remember the thrill of the flesh without being trapped by it.

**The Final Gift**

Before departing, the Power looks at the raptors, those sleek, grey birds forever diving. It cannot free their simple animal minds without causing them terror. So, it leaves them be. They remain the apex predators of a hunt that will never end, beautiful silver sculptures in the sky.

But the Power leaves one final gift for the ecosystem. It infuses the stationary clouds with a slow, rhythmic pulse—a heartbeat. Every thousand years, the mist condenses and releases a single, heavy drop of rain that falls in slow motion. It is a metronome. It gives the world a rhythm, a way to count the passing of ages, so that "forever" no longer feels like a cage, but like an ocean.

The Benevolent Power leaves, and the sky remains slate-grey. The sun remains fixed. But the Riven Plateau is no longer a tomb. It is a sanctuary.

The people are gone, and yet, they are more present than ever. They are the mist that coats the obsidian. They are the warmth in the still air. They are the caretakers of the moment, finally free to watch the sun hang in the sky, not with impatience, but with infinite, leisurely love.
