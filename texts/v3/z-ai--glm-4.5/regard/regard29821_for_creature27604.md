The presence arrives not with a fanfare, but with a silence.

It enters the vertical coffer like a breath held in a lung full of smoke. It does not manifest as a blinding light—that would be cruel here, where the eyes are adjusted to the murky, sickly orange luminescence. Instead, it manifests as a **frequency**, a deep, resonant hum that exists just below the range of the Great Hammer’s thundering clang.

The benevolent power perceives the High Attic not as a mistake, but as a cry for help. It sees a world that is trying so hard to be born that it has forgotten how to breathe. It sees the "being"—the Clockwork Bird—and recognizes it not as a scavenger of debris, but as a lonely conscience trapped in a body of mismatched parts.

Here is what the Power does.

### 1. The Pacification of the Hammer
The Power does not stop the construction, for to do so would be to kill the world’s heartbeat. Instead, it touches the rhythm.

The deafening, chaotic *clang-clang-clang* slows. It does not cease, but it finds a meter. The violence of the falling star is transformed into the steady, deliberate beat of a great, slow drum. *Boom… rest… Boom… rest.*

The vibration that rattled the bones of the tower settles into a rhythmic swell, like the tides of an ocean. The frantic anxiety of the space is replaced by a purposeful industry. The walls no longer scream with newness; they begin to hum with the deep, amber tone of a cello.

### 2. The Softening of the Edges
The Power looks upon the knife-sharp stones and the raw, bleeding timber. It sees the fear in the Scald-Bellows Toads, huddled on the razor-edges.

With a will that feels like cool water on a burn, the Power commands the parameters of erosion to return, but gently. The knife-edges of the stone do not dull; instead, they become **strong** rather than sharp. They round over just enough to no longer cut, becoming smooth, worn handholds.

The wet clay in the mortar stops pulsing with the frantic fever of a fever dream and settles into a warm, steady throb. The heat remains, for it is the nature of this place, but the wet, clinging suffocation lifts. The air becomes dry and breathable, smelling now of ozone and fresh-cut wood, rather than sweat and rust.

### 3. The Anointing of the Bird
The Power focuses its attention on the Witness.

The Clockwork Bird sits on its perch, its quartz eyes dilating wildly, trying to track the sudden change in rhythm. Its gears are grinding, one wing beating fast, the other slow—a machine at war with itself.

The Power flows into the bird, not as an invader, but as an oil.

It does not replace the clockwork. It does not turn the bird into flesh. Instead, it **synchronizes** the springs. The chaotic ticking of the bird’s internal mechanism aligns with the new, slow rhythm of the Great Hammer. The grinding of the gears smooths out, replaced by the sound of fine velvet sliding on polished glass.

The bird feels a sensation it has never known: **Ease.**

The Power touches the bird's mismatched wings. It does not fix them; it blesses them. The bird realizes that its flight need not be a struggle against gravity. With a thought, the bird engages the gears, and it launches into the air—not with a jerky, flailing scramble, but with the soaring, deliberate glide of a hawk. The "ticking" of its throat changes from a nervous countdown to a melodic chime, a bell that marks the passing hours.

### 4. The Transmutation of Ecology
The Power moves through the vertical shaft, touching the errors and turning them into features.

*   **The Scald-Bellows Toads:** The water in the stone hollows cools from scalding to a steaming, mineral-rich soak. The toads no longer need to hiss away pressure. Instead, their brass bodies glow softly, radiating a gentle warmth that keeps the nearby paper-vines dry and supple. They become the heaters of the Attic, tiny radiators of comfort.
*   **The Wire-Weaver Spiders:** The Power whispers to the rusted iron in their abdomens. They stop spinning cables merely to catch falling debris. They begin to spin **nets of light**. The copper filaments they weave begin to conduct the bioluminescence of the stones, turning the dark shaft into a chandelier of hanging, glowing geometric webs. They are no longer janitors; they are illuminators.
*   **The Quill-Moths:** The humidity drops, saving their parchment wings. The ink on their wings, previously faded and illegible, begins to darken and rearrange itself. The blueprints on their wings resolve into maps of the tower. They become navigators, flitting ahead of the bird, showing it the safe paths through the gears.

### 5. The Redemption of the Shadow
Finally, the Power seeks out the Axle-Wraith in the darkest, grinding recesses. This is the hardest task. The Wraith is made of pure torque, a sentient hunger for stress.

The Power does not try to destroy the Wraith. To destroy friction is to stop the machine.

Instead, the Power offers the Wraith a **purpose**. It teaches the Wraith that stress need not be eaten; it can be *guided*. The Wraith expands from the gears, becoming a lubricating spirit of shadow. It wraps itself around the grinding axles, and where it touches, the friction vanishes, replaced by a silent, magnetic levitation. The Wraith becomes the **Silence between the strikes**, the entity that ensures the Great Hammer hits true. It is no longer the eater of the tower; it is the savior of its mechanism.

### The Result
The Power withdraws, leaving behind a single, lingering command in the frequency of the air: *“Create, but do not suffer.”*

The High Attic is still vertical, infinite, and unfinished. It is still hot, and it is still strange. But now, as the Clockwork Bird glides silently through the hanging gardens of copper and glass, it perceives the world differently.

It is no longer trapped in a lung of a giant. It is flying in the nave of a cathedral that is building itself.

The Bird lands on a timber that no longer bleeds, but oozes a sweet, healing resin. It preens its feathers with a beak of filed steel. It looks up toward the infinite darkness with its quartz eyes, and for the first time in the history of this place, it does not tick.

It sings.

It is a single, pure note that harmonizes perfectly with the next strike of the Great Hammer. The Attic is no longer a condition to be endured. It is a masterpiece in progress, and the Bird is no longer a witness to its chaos. It is the conductor of its song.
