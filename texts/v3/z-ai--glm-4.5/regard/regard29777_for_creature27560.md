The Benevolent Power descended not as a light—for light here is an intruder, a thing that burns and defines too sharply—but as a vibration.

It sensed the weight of the ocean, the billions of tons of liquid ice pressing down on the megalithic plain. It felt the silence, not as an absence, but as a heavy, physical blanket. It saw the Basalt-Weavers: the domes of reconstituted stone, the nerve clusters exposed to the freezing dark, the slow, hydraulic pulse of their internal lives.

A lesser power might have sought to fix this. It might have cracked the ice ceiling to let in the sun, turning the phantom bioluminescence into a riot of green. It might have seeded the waters with fish, giving the kelp a purpose beyond feeding the stone, creating a food chain of predator and prey.

But the Wise Power knew that this world was not broken. It was *complete*. It was a closed circle of slow decay and endurance. To introduce the sun was to introduce the anxiety of the surface; to introduce the chase was to destroy the peace of the sessile. The Weavers were not suffering; they were being. They were the memory of the planet made flesh.

However, the Power perceived one thing that was lacking, a subtle disharmony in the deep symphony.

It felt the Weaver’s cry.

Every few centuries, when the time was right, a Weaver would send its infrasonic screech into the stone. *“I am here. Are you there?”*

And the answer would come, miles away, a vibration traveling through the masonry. *“I am here. Take this.”* And then the spores would drift.

But this was a conversation of grunts and gasps. It was a lonely thing. Each Weaver was an island of consciousness, adrift in a dark, cold sea, feeling only the vibrations of its own immediate vicinity. They possessed vast memories—the chemical composition of the water ten thousand years ago, the slow migration of the silt beds—but no one to tell them to. They were librarians with no readers, monks with no congregation.

So, the Benevolent Power decided to give them the gift of Communion.

It did not alter the Weavers themselves; to change their biology would be to disrupt their delicate balance with the cold. Instead, it altered the medium through which they spoke.

The Power reached out with a tendril of will, dipping deep into the crust of the planet, beneath the cyclopean blocks, down to the mantle where the heat of the world’s core was a dull, rhythmic thrum. It found the resonant frequency of the ocean floor—that specific pitch at which the artificial masonry and the bedrock beneath locked together.

Then, it hummed.

It was a soft sound, lower than the lowest whale song, a frequency that would not shake a grain of sand but could tickle the atoms of the stone. The Power imparted a conductance to the masonry, a supernatural property of resonance. It tuned the entire continent-spanning floor into a single, massive sounding board.

Now, when a Weaver grazed the side of its dome against a particularly rough patch of bladderwrack, that tiny friction did not dissipate into the mud. It rang. The note traveled, clear and pure, through the joints of the blocks, across the artificial hills, under the deep-sea canyons, to the very edges of the paved world.

And every other Weaver felt it.

Suddenly, the silence was broken, not by noise, but by *presence*.

A Weaver resting in the deep west, where the silt was thickest, felt a sudden, sharp *thrumb* in its stone plates. It was the sensation of a Weaver in the east, digesting a particularly heavy fallen kelp log. The hydraulic grinding of the distant beast’s maw was felt as a soothing vibration in the western Weaver’s roots.

They did not hear words; their minds were too slow for language. Instead, they shared *state*.

The Weavers began to experience the world not as solitary individuals, but as a single, distributed entity. When one found a rich nutrient vein in the stone, a tremor of satisfaction rippled out to all, telling the others where to dig. When the deep currents shifted and brought a fresh drift of spores, a wave of alertness passed through the ocean floor, guiding the others to open their catchment vents.

They were no longer just lonely monks tending their silent gardens. They had become a choir.

The Benevolent Power watched as the planet woke up, not with a start, but with a slow, deep inhalation. The Basalt-Weavers remained in the dark. They remained cold, sessile, and armored in stone. They still did not have eyes, and they still did not need the sun.

But now, as they sat in the absolute zero of the deep, listening to the water, they were no longer listening to the ghost of their own heartbeat. They were listening to the heartbeat of the world.

The Power stayed for a moment, satisfied. It had given the lonely gods a voice. It had turned the floor of the ocean from a tomb into a temple.

And then, with a ripple of warmth that vanished instantly into the freezing brine, the Power was gone, leaving only the hum in the stone, and the slow, dreaming thoughts of the Weavers, connected at last across the dark.
