The presence arrived not as a light, for light here was dim and filtered, nor as a sound, for sound was a thief quickly caught by the thirsty air. It arrived as a sudden, perfect clarity—a synchronization of the vibrating dust.

This Benevolent Power, ancient and possessing a wisdom that spanned eons, did not look upon the Fenestration and see a wasteland. It saw a cathedral of endurance, a triumph of life over scarcity. It saw that the "dryness" was not a curse, but a discipline. The beings here were not suffering; they were devout practitioners of existence, armored in necessity and bound by the silent rhythms of stone.

However, the Power perceived one profound lack in the masterpiece of the basin: isolation.

In this vast, vertical city, a Column-Eater died on the stalks and became dust without ever knowing the gratitude of the Litho-Mimics below who cleared its path. The Silencer struck in the dark, and the prey vanished without a ripple in the community. The Amber-Floaters sang to the wind, but their songs were swallowed by the canopy. The silence, while heavy, was also a wall. The civilization was physically crowded, yet spiritually solitary.

The Power decided to grant a gift that would not break their armor, nor soften their shells, nor wash away their dust. It would give them a voice that could travel through the very bones of their world.

The Power descended into the amber gloom and permeated the "sap" of the Fenestration—the thick, tarry fluid that acted as the blood of the mineral stalks. It infused this fluid with a new property: **Resonance**.

The change began subtly.

High above, an Amber-Floater brushed its net-legs against a branch, shedding a layer of cinnamon spore. Before this moment, the action would have been silent. Now, the contact sent a harmonic thrill down the stalk—a pure, bell-like tone, faint but clear, traveling instantly through the fibrous mineral wood to the very roots below.

Down in the deep canyons, a Litho-Mimic, scuttling over the gravel, felt the vibration through its feet. It paused. It was a signal. The dust above was thick with nutrients. The Mimic turned its segmented body upward and began to climb, navigating the verticality with a purpose it had never known before.

The Column-Eaters, the great siege-towers of rust-red chitin, were the next to understand. As they ground their powerful mandibles into the bark, the stalks began to hum. The grinding was no longer a dead noise; it became a rhythmic thrumming that echoed through the connected root system of the forest. It served as a beacon. A grazer miles away could feel the specific frequency of a feeding mate and adjust its own path to avoid overcrowding, ensuring that the forest was grazed evenly, with mathematical precision. The "silent industry" became a coordinated symphony of labor.

Even the Silencers were touched. The ambush hunters, flattened against the wood, could now feel the footsteps of their prey not just through the air, but through the entire stalk they clung to. They could sense the *weight* of a creature’s intent. This did not make the hunt easier; it made it a duel of wills. The grazers learned to move with "light" steps, to dampen their own vibration, while the Silencers learned to still their own hearts until they were truly like stone. The hunt became an intricate dance of silence and resonance, a high-stakes game of hide-and-seek played across the strings of a colossal instrument.

The greatest gift, however, was reserved for the cycle of death.

When a creature finally perished, its shell cracking and its fluids drying, the Power ensured that its passing was not a demolition, but a notification. As the body settled and shattered, the impact sent a specific, mournful chord through the gravel floor. It was a call to the scavengers. No longer would potential nourishment be lost to the random luck of a wanderer. The dead summoned the living. The Litho-Mimics would arrive not as janitors of chance, but as honor guards, processing the fallen with a deliberate, ritualistic efficiency.

The Fenestration did not become a garden of softness. It remained jagged, sharp, and dangerous. The air remained motionless and thick with cinnamon dust. But now, the basin was alive in a new way. It was a single, resonant organism.

If one were to stand on the edge of the depression and listen, the silence would still appear absolute. But if one pressed an ear to the gravel, or touched a hand to the rough, fibrous bark of a tower, one would feel it: a deep, thrumming song. It was the heartbeat of a civilization that had finally learned to sing to one another through the stone.

The Benevolent Power, satisfied, withdrew its presence, leaving behind a world that was no longer just a collection of survivors, but a chorus of endurance. The dust continued to fall, dry and silent, but now, it drifted down upon a world that was listening.
