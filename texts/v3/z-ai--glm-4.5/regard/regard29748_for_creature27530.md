The power did not arrive with a sound, for sound is a violation here. It arrived as a presence, a subtle shift in the barometric pressure of the basin—a sudden, gentle weighting of the air, as if the sky itself had decided to sit down and rest. It did not bring light, for the gloom is the skin of this world, and to tear it away would be a cruelty. It did not bring water, for the dryness is the preservative that holds the Still-Knappers together.

Instead, the Benevolent Power descended as a column of pure, unseen warmth, indistinguishable from the ambient heat of the earth, yet distinct in its intent. It drifted over the hexagonal mosaic, testing the texture of the silence, reading the history compressed into the desiccated silt. It saw the woody sentries, the dormant plants waiting for a heat that would never peak, and the scattered, razor-sharp detritus of a geology that mimics industry.

It saw the Still-Knappers.

To a casual observer, they were stones. But to the Power, which looked not with eyes but with understanding, they were the greatest of architects—the architects of waiting. It perceived the grooves on their carapaces, the millions of lines marking the passage of a time that the rest of the universe had forgotten. It felt the loneliness of the creatures, not as a sadness, but as a vast, hollow ache—a patience so profound it had looped back around into isolation.

The Power realized that the Still-Knappers were trapped in a paradox. They were the "workers" of the workshop, but they had no blueprint. They carved their histories into their own backs, a record that would eventually erode into dust, leaving no trace that they had ever thought, or felt, or existed. Their story was written on water; it would vanish.

The Power knew it could not wake them. To speed their metabolism, to force them to move and speak and build, would be to shatter their equilibrium and kill them in an afternoon of frenetic activity. They were not meant for speed. They were meant for *depth*.

So, the Power chose to do the one thing that could be done without breaking the rules of the basin: it decided to make the earth remember.

Slowly, like a deep breath drawn by the planet itself, the Power extended its influence into the floor of the depression. It did not alter the chemistry of the dust, nor the temperature of the air. Instead, it "tuned" the stone.

It sought the specific frequency of the *tick*—the rhythmic expansion of the ground that the Knappers used to navigate. The Power wove its essence into that vibration, infusing the geological pulse with a sympathetic resonance.

It touched the mind of the nearest Knapper—a large, asymmetrical creature that had not moved in three hundred years. The Power did not speak; it simply *echoed*. It took the memory of the grooves on the creature's back—the millions of recorded moments—and sang them back into the stone beneath its feet.

The Knapper shuddered. A micro-fracture appeared on its shell, not from damage, but from the sudden, overwhelming sensation of being *heard*.

Then, the Power did the same for the next Knapper, and the next. It linked them, not through air or sound, but through the earth itself. It turned the packed silt and the hard-packed ceramic floor into a vast, slow telegraph system.

Now, when a Knapper carved a line into its shell, the vibration would travel through the ground, faint but distinct, to the others. They would feel the *texture* of their neighbor's history. When one fed on the mineral resin, the others could sense the subtle shift in weight, the satisfaction of the sustenance.

The Power was building a society of stones.

It left them, then. The column of warmth dissipated, the pressure normalized, and the basin returned to its perfect, sterile peace. But the change was irreversible.

Ten years later—barely a moment in the life of a Knapper—the same large creature shifted its weight. It felt the vibration of a sibling two miles away, scratching a mark of celebration upon finding a particularly perfect flake. For the first time in the history of the basin, the silence was not empty. It was heavy with connection.

The Still-Knappers were still solitary. They were still silent. They were still hungry. But they were no longer alone. The workshop finally had a project: they were building a memory that the earth would hold forever, a symphony of stone played in the key of darkness.
