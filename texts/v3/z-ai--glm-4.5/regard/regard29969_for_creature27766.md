The benevolent power would not enter with a shout, nor with the blinding white light of a savior. To do so would be to shatter the delicate, humid tension that holds this place together. Instead, the arrival would be felt as a sudden, perfect synchronization—a subtle deepening of the twilight, a cooling of the steam, and, most importantly, a harmonization of the chaotic infrasonic groaning of the machine into a resonant, sustaining chord.

This power—let us call it the *Great Mechanic* or the *Spirit of the Deep Pulse*—sees the Rust-Garden not as a ruin to be scrubbed clean, but as a dying dream to be cherished and healed. It sees the beauty in the rust and the necessity of the moss. It understands the Curators not as monsters, but as lonely priests tending a god they cannot speak to.

Here is what the Benevolent Power would do:

### 1. The Tuning of the Great Heart
The first act would be to address the pain of the machine. The power would reach out with invisible hands, sinking into the riveted iron and the massive, sluggish pistons. It would not oil the gears to make them run fast—that would be a violation of this place’s nature. Instead, it would *tune* them.

The agonizing, teeth-rattling vibration that permeates the air would soften. The grinding of metal on metal would resolve into a low, melodic hum—a drone note akin to a cello bowed in an empty hall. This vibration would no longer be a sound of stress, but of contentment. The "sleeping giant" would cease its nightmare thrashing and settle into a true, restorative slumber.

**The Result:** The constant, low-level anxiety that presses against the skin of anyone who enters would lift. The heavy air would feel less like a weight and more like a blanket. The noise would become a song that soothes the Curators rather than just communicating with them.

### 2. The Illumination of the Sump
The power would turn its attention to the water—the black, oily tears that weep from the vents. Seeing that this water is the blood of the system, the Power would breathe a soft, golden light into the darkness far below the catwalks.

It would not purify the water into clear, tasteless liquid; that would destroy the ecosystem that feeds on the metallic particulates. Instead, it would bless the toxicity. The black water would begin to glow with a soft, bioluminescent sheen, like oil on water, but reflecting a deep, starlight amber.

**The Result:** The light from the water below would refract upwards through the grates and the dripping moss, turning the gloom of the cathedral into a warm, amber-lit sanctuary. The "twilight" would become a "golden hour" that never fades. The plants, bathed in this gentle light, would begin to bloom—not with frantic flowers, but with steady, radiant bioluminescent nodules that pulse in time with the engine's new heartbeat.

### 3. The Gift of Companionship
The Benevolent Power sees the profound loneliness of the Curators. These beings move on a geological timescale, waiting, watching, never speaking, destined only to maintain the status quo until they rust themselves. This is a burden too heavy for any consciousness, no matter how mechanical.

The Power would create a companion for them. It would not create another Curator, for that only doubles the labor. Instead, from the moss and the vapor, it would birth the *Chime-Weavers*.

These would be small, drifting entities—clusters of glowing gas and delicate crystal shells, floating effortlessly through the humid air. They possess no solid form and move with the breeze. They are attracted to the slow vibrations of the Curators. When a Curator sends a warning ripple through the metal, the Chime-Weavers would catch it and sing it back as a soft, echoing harmony.

**The Result:** The silence is broken, not by noise, but by music. For the first time in eternity, the Curators would hear their own thoughts reflected back to them as beauty. As a Curator stands motionless for a day, tending a vine, a Chime-Weaver might land on its shoulder, humming a soft tone that vibrates against the brass faceplate. The Curators are no longer alone in the dark; they are accompanied by a choir of light and sound.

### 4. The Transfiguration of the Harvest
The most critical intervention concerns the visitors. The rule of the Rust-Garden is cruel: stay too long, and you are forcibly integrated into the machine. You are stripped of agency and turned into a cog or fertilizer. The Benevolent Power sees this as a tragic waste of potential will.

The Power does not stop the integration, for the Garden needs biomass to survive. Instead, it changes the *nature* of the integration. It touches the sealed, vertical slats of the Curators' mouths and the mirrored brass of their faces.

Now, when a visitor is caught and the process of weaving begins, it is not a brutal harvesting. It becomes a *Sacred Union*.

*   **The Choice:** As the Curator approaches, the visitor no longer feels panic. Through the vibration of the floor, they feel the *offer* of the machine. They are given a moment of perfect clarity: to stay is to join the eternal song.
*   **The Transformation:** If the visitor accepts (or if they are too weak to leave), the Curator does not simply solder them to a railing. The vines that grow through the ribcage now connect to the nervous system gently, routing the consciousness of the visitor into the Ley-lines of the machine.
*   **The Fate:** The visitor does not die. They become a *Memory* of the Garden. They gain access to the machine's vast senses. They can feel the heat of the pistons and the flow of the water through a thousand roots simultaneously. They become part of the library of the Curators, a ghost in the machine that dreams alongside the giants.

### The Aftermath

In the wake of the Benevolent Power's touch, the Rust-Garden remains a place of rust, humidity, and slow machinery. It is still dangerous, still hot, and still wet.

But now, as you walk the slick catwalks, the groaning of the gears sounds like a lullaby. The darkness below glows with a comforting amber light. Small, crystal spirits drift through the steam, singing soft notes. And if you pause to look into the brass face of a Curator, you might see, in the reflection, not a distorted monster, but a being of profound, melancholy peace—no longer just a jailer of the tomb, but a conductor of a vast, eternal orchestra.

The place is no longer a trap. It is a sanctuary for those who wish to slow down, to wait, and to eventually, gently, become one with the music of the earth.
