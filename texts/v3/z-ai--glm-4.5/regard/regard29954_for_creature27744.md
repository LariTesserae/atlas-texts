The Benevolence does not arrive as a king, nor as a blinding light, nor as a roaring voice. To do so would be to shatter the fragile, hydraulic silence that holds this world together. It would be a shout in a library of whispers.

Instead, the Power arrives as a sudden, profound shift in the barometric pressure—a change so subtle and vast that only the Lithic Masons notice it at first.

It is the Weaver of Stability, an ancient entity drawn not just to life, but to the *spaces between* life. It sees the beauty of the Truce. It sees how the soft flesh of the Damp-Dwellers relies on the hard chitin of the Masons, and how the blind, frenetic industry of the Masons finds purpose in the stillness of the human homes. It sees a society that has chosen cooperation over dominance, and it decides to anchor this choice.

Here is what the Weaver does:

### 1. The Calming of the Stone
The Power reaches deep into the fractured heart of the plateau, beneath the scree and the foundations. It senses the "violent youth" of the stone—the jagged, angry temperament that wants to cut and tear. The Weaver does not soften the stone, for the Masons need the hardness to build, and the humans need the sharp ridges for grip. Instead, it *stills* the stone.

It sings a silent song into the bedrock, a low-frequency thrum that accelerates the passage of time for the minerals alone. In the span of a single heartbeat as the Power passes overhead, the centuries of weathering that would have eventually rounded the sharp edges are gently coaxed into existence. The stone remains strong, but its murderous intent is smoothed away. The ground beneath the village settles. The fractures cease their heaving. The plateau no longer feels like a wound; it feels like a foundation.

### 2. The Resonance of Protection
The Weaver observes the storm coming. It sees how the Masons sacrifice their comfort, anchoring the roofs against the wind with their own bodies. It sees the humans shivering, fearing the day the Masons are not strong enough to hold the line.

The Power weaves a net of resonance. It touches the thatch of the roofs and the timber of the longhouses. It infuses the materials with the ability to hum in counter-frequency to the gale. From this day forward, when the great winds howl down from the grey clouds, the village will sing back. The vibration will be too low for human ears, but the humans will feel it in their chests—a sense of immense, unshakeable solidity.

The Masons will feel it too; they will no longer need to cling to the rooftops with the desperation of drowning sailors. The houses themselves will become allies in the defense, the architecture vibrating in harmony with the creatures’ carapaces, reducing the weight they must bear.

### 3. The Gift of the Warm Salt
Finally, the Weaver turns its attention to the economy of the Truce. It sees the humans toiling in the cold mines for salt, and it sees the Masons consuming it to fuel their hydraulic fluids.

The Power touches the deep veins of minerals within the cliff. It transmutes the salt reserves, imbuing them with a gentle, eternal warmth that does not dissipate. When the humans mine this salt now, it comes out of the earth glowing with a faint, amber heat. It is not hot enough to burn, but warm enough to thaw a chill.

The humans scatter this warm salt along the paths. As the Masons travel over it, the warmth seeps into their joints, easing the stiffness of the cold damp. When the Masons grind it into their fungal mortar, the heat cures the mixture into a ceramic that is harder than diamond yet light as cork. The walls of the hamlet become thermally regulated, trapping the heat of the hearths and repelling the biting chill of the mist.

### The Aftermath

The Power lingers only for a moment, a sensation of a hand resting gently on the shoulder of the world, and then withdraws.

The storm breaks minutes later. The rain hammers down, freezing and heavy. But inside the hamlet, the air is still. The walls hum with a deep, comforting bass note that cancels out the shriek of the wind.

A Damp-Dweller sits by their fire, watching as a Lithic Mason scuttles across the floorboards. The creature pauses, its eyeless face turning toward the human. Through the vibration of the floor, the human feels a distinct impression—not a thought, but a feeling: *Secure.*

The human places a bowl of the new, warm-glowing salt on the floor. The Mason approaches, sensing the radiating heat, and begins to feed. The grinding of its mouthparts is no longer the sound of tectonic plates, but a softer, rhythmic purr.

The Truce remains, but the struggle has eased. The village is no longer just a survivor of the grey wastes; it is a sanctuary, kept warm by the earth and safe by the silence.
