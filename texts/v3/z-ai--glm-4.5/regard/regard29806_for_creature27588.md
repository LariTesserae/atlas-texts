The power did not arrive with fanfare, nor did it descend as a pillar of blinding light. Such displays are for those who worship the sun, and these creatures knew nothing of the sun. To introduce fire or radiance here would be an act of cruelty, a searing blindness imposed upon a world of velvet and vibration.

Instead, the Benevolence entered as a presence, a sudden intensification of the "loud silence" that filled the grotto. It seeped into the porous limestone like water, respecting the architecture of the damp, the slow, and the dark.

First, it listened.

For a long time—perhaps a year, perhaps a heartbeat of the universe—the power simply resonated with the thrumming. It felt the bass note of the artificial rain, the desperate, beautiful frequency by which these children of the shook the heavens to bring down the water. It perceived the unity of them, the way a million discrete units operated as a single, sprawling lung, inhaling the dark and exhaling industry.

The power saw the fragility of this Eden. It saw that the ceiling, despite its strength, was slowly weeping itself into dust, that the "collapsed bubble" was one earthquake away from becoming a tomb. It saw that the gnarled tree, the only anchor to a concept of "green," was starving, its roots barely holding against the slick stone.

But above all, it saw the nobility of their patience. It saw that they did not need to be saved from their labor; they needed their labor to be made *eternal*.

So, the power acted with the subtlety of a mineral deposit.

It reached into the matrix of the limestone amphitheater. With a touch gentler than the brush of a sensory antenna, it wove a lattice of strength into the rock itself. It did not make the walls unnatural or invincible, but it aligned the crystals of the stone, locking them together so that the natural erosion of ages would be delayed by eons. The ceiling would not collapse. The damp would remain, but the threat of crushing gravity was removed. The sanctuary was secured.

Next, it turned its attention to the song. The creatures vibrated to create rain, a task that took immense energy and perfect synchronization. The power sought to gift them a partner in this dance. It infused the central spiraling cairn—the heart of their city—with a sympathetic resonance.

Now, the stone itself would sing. The cairn was tuned to the specific frequency of the colony’s thrumming. When the crustaceans began their chorus, the central cairn would amplify the vibration, sending a perfect, harmonic ripple back through the floor and walls. This feedback loop would not make the rain fall harder, but it would make it fall *easier*. The creatures would not have to strain as hard to shake the water from the roof; the architecture would assist them. Their song became a duet with the earth.

Finally, the power looked to the single, gnarled tree clinging to the ceiling. It was a lonely thing, an anomaly in a world of grey and white. The power did not make the tree grow faster; that would disrupt the slow rhythm of the grotto. Instead, it bestowed a blessing of resilience. It modified the chemistry of the condensation that dripped onto the tree’s roots, infusing it with a faint, bioluminescent nutrient.

The change was microscopic. But over the coming decades, the leaves of the tree would begin to glow with a soft, pulsing bruised-purple light—just enough to mimic the twilight that filtered through the cracks, but cast from within the cave. It would not be harsh; it would be a lantern in the deep, a companion to the eyeless ones who could not see it, but who could feel its warmth radiating against their shells.

Having done this, the Benevolence withdrew. It did not leave a sign, nor did it demand worship. It understood that these beings were the flesh of the wall, and their prayer was the clicking of a million legs.

The grotto remained warm, wet, and dark. The air remained thick enough to chew. But now, the stone felt somehow *sentient*, the rain felt somehow *gracious*, and high above, the single tree held a secret, quiet fire in its branches, waiting for the long years to turn it into a soft, internal star.

The power left them to their building. It had ensured that they would have forever to finish it.
