**Traveler’s Advisory: The High Drown (Needle-City)**

**Estimated Risk Level:** High (Environmental)
**Physiological Stress:** Severe (Respiratory & Vestibular)
**Tech Compatibility:** Low (Primitive Environment)

Visiting the High Drown is not a trip; it is an immersion in the literal sense. You are not walking on a surface; you are suspended within a column of warm, thick fluid miles above the old ocean floor. This environment requires a total abandonment of your expectations regarding "air," "ground," and "gravity."

Here is how you prepare to survive the verticality and the silence of the Needle-City.

### I. Physiological Conditioning: The "Lung" Training

The atmosphere here is rated 2.9 on the submersion scale. This is the most critical factor. You are not breathing air; you are inhaling a warm, green aerosol.

1.  **Humidity Tolerance:** You must acclimate your lungs to process moisture that is nearly saturation-level. In the weeks leading up to your departure, spend time in steam rooms or humidors while exercising. Your body needs to learn to gasp without choking. When you arrive, breathing will feel like drinking a fine mist. If you have respiratory issues, do not go.
2.  **Olfactory Overload:** The air smells of wet stone, mineral-rich brine, and decaying vegetation. It is cloying. Train yourself to breathe shallowly and rhythmically. Hyperventilating here leads to fluid buildup in the lungs faster than you can cough it out.
3.  **Vestibular Adjustment:** Gravity is "gentled" by buoyancy. You will feel lighter, but sluggish. Movements will have a delayed reaction time. Practice floating in water, specifically treading water with your eyes closed, to get used to the lack of a hard "down." You will often lose track of which way is up; trust the pull of your inner ear, not your eyes.

### II. Skin and Protection: The Oily Sheen

The environment is a 2.9 submersion—this means you are constantly wet. The water has an oily sheen and leaves a salty precipitate on everything it touches.

1.  **Barrier Creams:** You cannot stay dry. Accept this. Instead, apply a thick, medical-grade silicone or lanolin barrier to your entire body before dressing. The water is warm and cloying; without a barrier, your skin will prune, soften, and eventually macerate within hours, leading to open sores that the "weeping" rock will infect.
2.  **Eye Protection:** The "light" is silt and shadow. It is a murky, tea-colored gloom that is hard on the eyes. Do not wear contact lenses; they will fuse to your eyes. Wear goggles with a high-contrast yellow or amber tint to cut through the green-gold twilight. Prescription dive masks are ideal.
3.  **The "Slick-Suit":** Do not wear cotton. It will absorb water and weigh you down, dragging you down into the abyss. Do not wear heavy synthetics that squeak when wet. You need a tight-fitting, slick, neoprene or shark-skin suit. It must be smooth to prevent snagging on the **glass-sharp basalt** (Erosion 0.4) that defines this region.

### III. Gear and Equipment: The Primitive Mandate

The civilization here is Tech 0.6. There are no metals, no gears. If you arrive with a stainless steel knife or a digital watch, you will be an anomaly—and a sharp, hard one in a world of soft wood and flesh.

1.  **Ditch the Metal:** Leave your steel, aluminum, and titanium at home. Metal rusts instantly in the mineral-rich air and cuts the local materials (rope, wood, flesh) too easily. Carry tools made of bone, obsidian, or high-impact ceramics. If you *must* carry a knife, make it one you can lose.
2.  **Silent Movement:** The sound is muffled (1.3), but sharp noises carry alarmingly far as vibrations through the water. Your gear must be silent. Tape your buckles. Use soft sacks instead of hard cases. Do not bring anything that beeps, whirs, or clicks. The locals move with a slow, deliberate grace; clanking armor will mark you as a threat or a fool.
3.  **Locomotion:** You are climbing vertical escarpments. You need grip. Your boots should have soft, sticky rubber soles (like approach shoes for rock climbing) but with aggressive suction cups or spiked points for the precipitate-covered stone. Carry a locally made grappling hook—bone and sinew—to navigate the gaps between the Spines.

### IV. Cultural Integration: The Bioluminescent Code

The people of the Needle-City do not speak loudly. Their sound is a hushed tapestry. To communicate, they use light.

1.  **Light Discipline:** Bring a light source, but make it dimmable to near-zero. A standard flashlight is a weapon here; it will blind the locals and disturb the ecology. Use a chem-light or a bioluminescent glow-rod.
2.  **Learn the Patterns:** The locals signal one another with cool, pale patterns on their skin. If you can, obtain a vest or paint that mimics this bioluminescence. Flashing a light three times might mean "hello" in your world, but here it might mean "predator" or "rockslide." Watch the silhouettes. Move when they move. Rest when they rest.
3.  **The Economy of Patience:** Time here is Dynamic 1.1—glacial. Do not rush. Do not try to shake hands quickly or force a transaction. If you want food, you wait. If you want shelter, you wait. The High Drown does not reward haste; it rewards those who can cling to the rock and breathe the mist without panic.

### V. Environmental Hazards: The Living Stone

The weirdest element of this location is the geology (Weirdness 1.8). The rock is not dead; it heals.

1.  **Beware the Weeping Walls:** The basalt seeps a mineral-rich fluid that crystallizes. Do not lean against the walls for long. You might find your clothing crystallized to the stone, or your skin calcified. Keep moving. The city is constantly fighting its foundation; do not get caught in the "repair" zones where locals chip away new stone growth.
2.  **The Vertical Drop:** There are no guardrails. The urban sprawl is density 1.6—a hive of walkways carved into vertical cliffs. One slip, and you fall miles into the tea-colored depths. Tether yourself. Always have a line attached to something solid.
3.  **The Leviathan:** Fauna is sparse, but the deep things exist. You will likely feel the pressure drop before you see it—a sudden tightening of the chest, a change in the water’s density. If you feel this, *freeze*. Do not thrash. The Leviathans ignore the static; they hunt the moving. Become part of the silt. Become part of the shadow.

### Summary of the Experience

You will arrive feeling heavy, your skin slicked in oil, your breathing labored. You will see a city of bone and wood clinging to black glass towers, suspended in a green twilight. You will hear the creak of wet timber and the thrum of deep water.

You will be cold, then warm, then pruned. You will fear the drop. You will fear the rock that bleeds salt.

But if you move slowly, if you respect the silence, and if you learn to breathe the water as the locals do, you will witness one of the multiverse's strangest paradoxes: a city that drowns in the sky.
