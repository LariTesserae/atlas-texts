To visit this location is to accept a role that is equal parts astronaut, deep-sea diver, and ghost. You are not entering a habitat; you are entering a sensory deprivation tank that spans a continent. For an ordinary human, the environmental hostility here is total. The water is not a medium you swim in; it is a vacuum you are crushed by.

Here is how you must prepare for the submerged cenotaph.

### I. The Physical Shell: Pressure and Thermal Integrity

Your greatest enemy is not the wildlife, for there is none. Your enemies are physics: Pressure, Cold, and Asphyxiation.

**1. The Exosuit (The "Coffin")**
You cannot wear a wetsuit. You cannot wear a standard soft-shell atmospheric diving suit. The pressure here—described as the weight of the ocean above combined with the "heavy suspension" of the brine—suggests depths that would implode a submersible not specifically designed for hyper-baric extremes.
*   **Requirement:** You need a rigid, anthropomorphic exoskeleton, essentially a one-person submarine made of titanium or hyper-compressed carbon ceramics. It must be a "pressure-equivalent" system. The interior *must* maintain one atmosphere of pressure. If the interior pressure matches the outside, your bones will crush and your nitrogen will narcotize you instantly. You need a bubble of Earth air around you.
*   **Articulation:** The joints must be semi-rigid, using pressure-compensated fluid rotary seals. You will not be fluid; you will be mechanical. Your movements will be slightly delayed, clunky, and heavy.

**2. Thermal Regulation (The "Furnace")**
The description notes a cold that "penetrates time." This is hyper-cryogenic. Just above freezing in water conducts heat away from the human body twenty-five times faster than air.
*   **Requirement:** Your exosuit requires redundant active heating. You are not trying to stay warm; you are trying to keep the water from turning you into a statue. You need a miniature nuclear radioisotope thermoelectric generator (RTG) or a high-density chemical fuel cell dedicated solely to heating the layers of your suit.
*   **Preparation:** Treat your body as if you are entering deep space. Layered thermal underwear with active heating filaments against your skin is a necessity, in case the suit’s primary heat fails.

### II. Sensory Translation: The Blindness

The light rating is 0.2. Your human eyes are useless here. If you rely on vision, you will be blind. To navigate the Cyclopean masonry and the kelp forests, you must replace your eyes with technology.

**1. Acoustic Imaging (Sonar/Lidar)**
The world is texture and shadow. You need a helmet display that converts sound into a 3D wireframe map.
*   **The Setup:** High-frequency forward-looking sonar to detect the "redwood" kelp strands before you swim into them. Side-scan sonar to appreciate the scale of the megaliths.
*   **The Mental Prep:** You must learn to trust the machine overlay over your own eyes. When you look out the viewplate, you will see absolute blackness. Your brain will panic. You must train yourself to ignore the blackness and watch the HUD (Heads-Up Display). The "world" exists only on that screen.

**2. Haptic Feedback**
The prompt suggests navigating by texture. The suit should be equipped with external sensors that transmit the "feel" of the stone to your hands.
*   **The Glove:** Haptic feedback gloves that simulate resistance and texture. When you touch the ancient, polished masonry, you need to feel the slickness, the rounded edges, and the "wet velvet" of the algae without actually exposing your skin to the freeze. This connection is the only thing keeping you sane.

### III. Navigation and Mobility: The Kelp Maze

The vegetation is a "choking empire" with a lushness of 2.4. This is thick. This is dense.

**1. Propulsion**
Do not use large, loud thrusters. They will slice the kelp (disturbing the ancient ecosystem) and, more importantly, they will kick up silt. In 0.2 visibility, a silt cloud equals blindness.
*   **The Setup:** Use silent, low-velocity vectored thrusters, or better yet, a "cyclops" finning system attached to the suit's legs. You need to move slowly.
*   **Tethering:** In a forest of redwood-sized kelp, disorientation is fatal. You must trail a thin, high-strength monofilament line behind you anchored to your entry point, or use a dead-reckoning inertial navigation system. If you lose your heading in the gloom, you will never find the edge of the continent-sized plain.

**2. Cutting Tools**
The bladderwrack will try to entangle you. It is "rope-like."
*   **The Loadout:** Carry a high-frequency acoustic cutter or a laser cutter. Mechanical shears might jam on the dense, fibrous strands. You need something that can sever a cable the thickness of a tree trunk instantly.

### IV. Psychological Preparation: The Waiting Room

This is the most dangerous part of the mission. The environment is static (Dynamic 0.6). There is no history, no movement, no sound (Silence 0.8). It is the "End of History."

**1. Auditory Stimulation (The Sanity Anchor)**
The silence will press in on you. The only sound is your own heartbeat and the creak of your suit.
*   **The Prep:** Prepare a playlist. Not loud, chaotic music, but something rhythmic and human. Low-volume podcasts, ambient noise, or audio books. You need to remind yourself that human thought still exists, that time is still moving elsewhere. However, keep one audio channel open to the external hydrophone. Listening to the *absence* of whales or shrimp is the key to understanding this place.

**2. The Agoraphobia of the Deep**
You are on a plain that stretches forever. It is flat, tiled, and infinite.
*   **The Mindset:** You are an ant crawling on a bathroom floor that has been abandoned for a million years. You must accept your own insignificance. If you try to find the "meaning" of the ruins, you will spiral into existential dread. The ruins have no purpose. They are just stone.
*   **Combatting The Chill of Time:** The "cold that penetrates time" is a psychological hazard. You will feel slow. You will feel that returning to the multiverse is impossible. You must set rigid timers. "Explore for 45 minutes. Return." Do not rely on "feeling" like it's time to go. Your perception of time will dilate in the absolute dark.

**3. Solitude Training**
There are no animals. There is nothing looking back at you.
*   **The Simulation:** Spend 24 hours in a sensory deprivation tank. Then, spend another 24 hours in a pitch-black tank with only a humming sound. This is the closest you can get to the "profound silence" of the cenotaph. If you panic in the simulation, you will panic here.

### V. Summary of the Loadout

*   **Vessel:** A single-operator, hard-shell atmospheric diving suit (ADS).
*   **Power:** High-density isotopic battery (minimum 48-hour life).
*   **Sensors:** Forward-looking sonar, terrain-mapping lidar, external hydrophones.
*   **Display:** Helmet-integrated HUD with haptic mapping (visualizing sound).
*   **Tools:** Acoustic cutter for kelp; sediment blaster for clearing inspection sites.
*   **Personal:** Haptic interface gloves; Urine collection system (you cannot leave the suit); Thermal heating layers.
*   **Mind:** A rigid itinerary and a library of audio files to fight the silence.

**Final Warning:**
When you step out onto the masonry, you will feel the urge to take off your helmet. The silence is seductive. It promises a quiet, eternal peace. The cold feels like a cessation of worry. Do not listen. The "god" who built this floor left eons ago, and the only thing remaining is the moss and the dark. You are a tourist in a graveyard. Keep your head down, watch your sonar, and keep moving.
