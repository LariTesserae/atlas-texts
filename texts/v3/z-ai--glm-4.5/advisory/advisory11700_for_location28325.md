**Survival Guide for the Obsidian Render**

**Target:** The Unbuffered Basin / The Glitched Geode
**Traveler Profile:** Ordinary Biological Human (Baseline Vulnerability)

Listen to me closely. If you are used to standard terrestrial worlds—worlds with yellow suns, dirt paths, and reliable gravity—this place will try to unravel you. This is not a nature preserve; it is a construction site with a pulse. The environment here is hostile less out of malice and more out of unfinished business. You are walking on the wet, jagged edges of a reality that hasn’t fully loaded.

Here is how you prepare to survive the "Becoming."

***

### 1. The Wardrobe: The Knife-Edge Protocol
The ground is your primary enemy. The terrain (0.6 erosion) is a landscape of broken glass and serrated basalt. If you trip, you do not get a bruise; you get a fillet.

*   **Footwear:** Standard hiking boots are suicide. You need heavy-duty, **full-shank mountaineering boots** with rigid soles. The soles must be Vibram or equivalent, capable of gripping wet, slick obsidian without slipping. More importantly, the uppers must be armored. Consider reinforced toe caps and Kevlar uppers. If a shard of obsidian shifts under your weight, a leather boot will be sliced open like an envelope.
*   **Lower Body Armor:** Forget denim or cotton trekking pants. Wear **abrasion-resistant tactical pants** or heavy canvas work pants. Carry **gaiters** (the kind used for glacial travel) to seal the gap between your boot and pant leg. The "weeping" air means water runs down your legs and into your socks instantly if you are not sealed.
*   **The "Suit":** Because the humidity is absolute (1.0), you will be wet constantly. Cotton will rot and chafe you into immobility within hours. You need a **base layer of synthetic wicking material** (polypropylene or merino wool) and a **hard-shell waterproof outer layer** (Gore-Tex Pro or equivalent) that is fully seam-sealed. You are not wearing this to keep rain out; you are wearing it to trap a micro-climate of dry air against your skin because the outside world is a warm, foggy sponge.
*   **Protection for Hands:** You *will* put your hands down to catch yourself. Wear **mechanix-style gloves with leather palms** at all times.

### 2. The Atmosphere: Breathing in the Buffer
The air here is thick, warm, and saturated. It feels like breathing through a warm washcloth.

*   **Hydration Discipline:** This is counter-intuitive. You will feel wet. You will feel like you are drinking the air. You will not feel thirsty. **Do not trust this feeling.** The heat (1.7) means you are sweating constantly, but the humidity stops evaporation, so you won't feel the cooling effect of sweat. You will dehydrate rapidly while feeling waterlogged. Carry a hydration bladder with a tube. Force yourself to drink water on a strict timer, every 15 minutes.
*   **Respiratory Support:** The air is "heavy." It may be rich in volcanic particulate or simply dense with unknown vapor. Carry a **supply of N95 respirators** or a half-face respirator with filters. The dampness can encourage fungal growth in the lungs; don't let the local biota colonize your alveoli.
*   **Vitamin D and Circadian Rhythm:** There is no sun (0.8 brilliance), only a bruised purple twilight. Your brain will stop producing melatonin correctly. You will become depressed and disoriented. Bring **high-potency Vitamin D supplements** and a **SAD (Seasonal Affective Disorder) therapy lamp** or a high-lumen headlamp with a warm filter. Use it for 30 minutes every "morning" to trick your body into knowing what day it is.

### 3. Navigation: The Glitch Compass
The world is rendering in real-time. This is the most dangerous psychological aspect of the trip.

*   **Paper Maps Only:** Do not rely on GPS satellites. The ground beneath you phases out into grids of code. Satellite signals will be nonsense. Carry a **topographical map printed on synthetic waterproof paper**, and update it manually as you go.
*   **The "Anchor" Method:** When the reality flickers (the "weirdness" of 2.4), you might experience vertigo or motion sickness. Carry a **physical, non-digital anchor**. A heavy stone, a lead weight on a string. When the ridges vibrate and the visual world strobes, hold the anchor. Look at the object in your hand. Remind yourself: *I am solid. The world is the dream.*
*   **Accept the Re-Route:** If a ridge of black glass phases out, do not try to run across the code grid. Wait. It will buffer back in. If you are halfway across when it glitches, you could be merged with the rock. Move slowly. Treat the landscape as if it is thin ice.

### 4. The City: The Impossible Commute
The structures here are separated by miles of wet wilderness. This is a decentralized nightmare.

*   **Teleportation Sickness:** The gravity bridges and folding air (Beyond Modern tech) act on your inner ear. Before you step into a teleportation arc, take **dimenhydrinate (motion sickness medication)**. Eat a bland, high-carb meal beforehand. The first time you fold space, you will vomit if you don't.
*   **Long-Haul Gear:** There are no convenience stores between the spires. If you miss the bridge, you walk for two hours on razors. Carry an **emergency bivy sack** or thermal blanket. If you get stuck in the wilderness, the humidity will sap your body heat the moment the sun (or whatever provides the light) dips lower.
*   **Data Interface:** The technology here manipulates moisture into data screens. You likely cannot touch these screens. Bring a **ruggedized tablet** loaded with translation algorithms. If the local architecture tries to interface with you, it will likely try to interface with your bio-electricity. A Faraday pouch (a bag that blocks signals) is essential for sleeping. You don't want the city's AI trying to update your firmware while you dream.

### 5. The Flora and Fauna: The Distant Giants
You are insignificant here. The vegetation (2.1) is massive and sparse; the fauna (1.2) is mechanical and oblivious.

*   **Visual Isolation:** Do not try to climb the giant helical ferns. They are likely slick with condensation and their cellular structure might be incompatible with your weight (they might crumble or dissolve). Admire them from a distance.
*   **Robot Etiquette:** If you see the mechanical deer or geometric moths, **freeze**. They are rare. Do not approach them. They are part of the system's background rendering. If you interact too aggressively, you might trigger a "delete" command from the local operating system. Take pictures quickly, with a shutter speed fast enough to catch them before they evaporate.
*   **The Silence:** The sound dampening (1.1) is oppressive. The only noise is a sub-bass hum. This "brown noise" can cause anxiety and hallucinations. Bring **noise-canceling headphones** and a playlist of music with sharp, high-pitched vocals or violins—something that pierces the damp quiet. You need to hear a human voice, even a recorded one, to keep your ego intact.

### 6. Medical: The Wet Rot
The combination of heat, moisture, and sharp rocks creates a perfect storm for infection.

*   **The "Flesh" Kit:** Your first aid kit should be less about headaches and more about trauma. Carry **cyanoacrylate (super glue) for wound closure**. Sutures take too long to dry in this humidity. Glue the wound shut immediately.
*   **Anti-Fungals:** Between your toes, in your groin, under your arms. Athlete's foot and jock rot will set in within hours. Apply **anti-fungal powder** every morning and night.
*   **Eye Care:** The perpetual bruised light is hard on the eyes. The constant squinting combined with glare off the wet obsidian will cause headaches. Wear **photochromic (transition) lenses** that darken in the gloom and protect against UV.

### Final Psychological Warning
You are visiting a place that is "becoming." It is a test run.

Do not expect logic. Do not expect safety. When you look at the horizon and see the source of the light that isn't there, you will feel an urge to walk toward it. Do not. That is the "loading bar" calling you.

Pack light. Pack dry. Watch your step. And keep your eyes on the ground—because the ground is the only thing here that might actually decide to exist today.

**Safe travels.**
