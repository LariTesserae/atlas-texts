Preparation for a visit to the Heart-Measure is not merely a matter of packing a bag; it is a physiological conditioning exercise. You are preparing to enter the gut of a living god, a biomechanical titan the size of a mountain. An ordinary human body is soft, fragile, and accustomed to a world where silence exists and gravity feels static. To survive here, you must harden yourself against the vibration, the damp, and the crushing verticality.

Here is how you prepare.

### 1. Auditory and Skeletal Fortification
The first and most immediate threat is the roar. The description calls it a "wall of vibration." This is not hyperbole. In the Heart-Measure, sound is not just heard; it is felt. The resonance will agitate the fluid in your inner ear, potentially causing nausea, vertigo, and immediate hearing damage.

*   **The Hardware:** Standard foam earplugs are useless here. They dampen volume, but they cannot stop the low-frequency baritone thrum that travels through bone. You need industrial-grade, active noise-canceling headphones capable of counteracting low-frequency infrasound. Furthermore, you must wear a shock-dampening cradle or neck brace to isolate your skull from the constant shudder of the deck plating.
*   **Dental Care:** Visit a dentist before you depart. The description notes the "teeth ache." Fillings, crowns, and loose teeth will vibrate agonizingly against the bone. Ensure your dental work is pristine; otherwise, the machine’s pulse will become a torture device inside your mouth.
*   **Communication:** You will not be able to speak to a companion standing next to you. Do not rely on shouting. Invest in a throat-mic radio system that transmits directly to your companion’s headphones, bypassing the air entirely.

### 2. Atmospheric and Thermal Management
You are stepping into a pressurized tropical rainforest suspended in the stratosphere. The environment is aggressive: hot, wet, and thin on oxygen outside the immediate zone of the vents.

*   **Respiration:** The air is "manufactured" and heavy with humidity. Bring a high-capacity desiccant breathing mask or a rebreather. While the vegetation pumps oxygen, the air near the floor will be thick with spores, metallic dust, and steam. You want to filter the particulate matter without drying out your throat. Keep hydrated; you will sweat constantly in the tropical heat, but the mist will make it feel less apparent than it is.
*   **Visibility:** The air is full of "perpetual mist" and oil slicks. Standard glasses will fog instantly and stay fogged. Contact lenses are a liability if the air is full of metallic particulates. Your best option is a sealed visor or prescription goggles that can be wiped clear with a silicone cloth.
*   **Clothing System:** Do not wear cotton. Once cotton is wet with oil and condensation, it will chafe and rot. You need a full-body coverall made of a synthetic, hydrophobic weave (like heavy-duty nylon or ripstop Gore-Tex). It must be oil-resistant.
    *   **The Layer:** A moisture-wicking base layer to handle the 90%+ humidity.
    *   **The Shell:** A heavy, reinforced outer shell to protect against abrasion from rusted iron and sharp thorns on the "slick, black" vines.
    *   **Footwear:** High-traction, magnetic-soled boots if possible. The floor is "riveted iron plate," "warped by age and heat," and covered in "puddles shimmering with oil slicks." It is a death trap. Your boots need aggressive tread (Vibram or similar) and ankle support to prevent twists on the warped plating.

### 3. Verticality and Fall Protection
The architecture of this place is designed to induce vertigo. You are walking on a mesh floor over a bottomless cylinder, surrounded by a ten-story drop.

*   **The Harness:** You must wear a climbing harness at all times. Do not rely on guardrails; the text says the machine sends a "shudder through the guardrails" every few minutes. A railing designed for a static building may not withstand a seismic shudder, or worse, your grip may fail during a vibration spike.
*   **Tethering:** The facility likely lacks modern safety lines. Bring your own dynamic climbing rope and heavy-duty carabiners. You need to anchor yourself to the stationary columns (the Great Shafts) before moving near the edge of the central void.
*   **Equilibrium Training:** Practice working at heights before you arrive. The "deceptively small" scale of the room combined with the dizzying verticality can cause spatial disorientation. If you look down into the void, you risk the "high-place phenomenon"—an intrusive urge to jump. Train your eyes to focus on the mechanical details, not the drop.

### 4. Biological Interaction
The ecosystem here is symbiotic with the machine. The plants are not passive; they are squeezing the iron, anchored in the rust. The fauna has adapted to the noise.

*   **Hand Protection:** Never touch the vegetation with bare skin. The vines are "thick as a man’s torso" and have "slick, black leaves." They likely secrete oils to counteract the rust or possess tough, needle-like defenses. Wear heavy, mechanic’s gloves (Kevlar-reinforced).
*   **Fauna Protocol:** The lemur-like creatures and birds are "immune to the deafening noise." This implies they have evolved specialized physiology; they may not hear you creeping up on them, or they may startle easily if they *do* feel vibrations. Do not try to feed them. They are part of a closed loop; introducing foreign food (or your fingers) could disrupt their gut biome or provoke an attack.
*   **Parasite Control:** Damp, warm, enclosed environments breed parasites. Apply a heavy-duty insect repellent (DEET or Picaridin) to your clothing and exposed skin. The "teeming ecosystem" of insects near the warm vents will likely be curious about the only warm-blooded mammal in the room that isn't covered in metal fur.

### 5. Mental Preparation and Etiquette
Finally, you must prepare your mind. The Heart-Measure operates on a "glacial" timescale. You are a frantic, ephemeral creature visiting a deity that thinks in centuries.

*   **Patience:** You cannot rush here. The gears move once every few minutes. If you need to cross a walkway that is powered by the machine, you may have to wait for the "clank." Bring a book, or a meditation practice. Do not let the impatience of the human clock frustrate you.
*   **Acceptance of Intrusion:** You are a contaminant. The text emphasizes this is a "closed loop." You are an outsider who breaks the seal. Move with reverence. Do not litter. Do not leave biological waste. The machine consumes its own waste to feed the moss; your waste might be toxic to this delicate balance.
*   **The Darkness:** The lighting is dim, provided by "high-glare lamps" and "bioluminescent green" moss. Your night vision will be crucial. Bring a high-lumen headlamp, but use a red light filter to avoid blinding yourself or disturbing the creatures that nest in the warning lights.

### Summary Checklist for the Traveler:
1.  **Ears:** Active noise-canceling headset + Earplugs (double protection).
2.  **Body:** Neck brace, full-body oil-resistant synthetics, heavy boots, Kevlar gloves.
3.  **Safety:** Climbing harness, 50ft of dynamic rope, locking carabiners.
4.  **Health:** Anti-nausea meds (for vertigo/vibration), strong insect repellent, water filtration (don't drink the condensation).

Go softly. The Heart-Measure does not know you are there, and it will not stop if you fall.
