Visiting this location is not an expedition; it is an immersion in a biological pressure cooker. You are not entering a habitat where you can camp; you are inserting yourself into a digestive tract the size of a cathedral. If you treat this like a cave dive or a mountain climb, you will be consumed within minutes.

Here is how an ordinary multiverse traveler must prepare to survive the "Living Vault."

### I. Life Support: The Hermetic Seal

The atmosphere here is chemically stable but physically hostile. You are dealing with high-altitude hypoxia combined with geothermal heat and a sickening miasma.

*   **Full-Body Environmental Suit:** Do not rely on standard breathable fabrics. The air is hot, dry, and carries the scent of copper and ozone. More importantly, the humidity is effectively zero; your own mucous membranes would desiccate instantly. You need a sealed, hard-shell or heavily armored soft suit with full life support.
*   **Active Thermal Cooling:** The ambient heat is a "geothermal simmer." Passive insulation won't cut it; you will cook. Your suit must have an active liquid cooling system, capable of dumping heat for hours. The external temperature is likely enough to cause heatstroke through a suit if you aren't actively circulating coolant.
*   **Atmospheric Scrubbing:** The smell of "cooling blood" and ozone suggests high biological particulate count and reactive gases. Your scrubbers need to handle heavy metallic particulates (from the pulverized limestone) and whatever biological aerosols are drifting in the "purple haze." Do not open your visor for any reason.
*   **High-Altitude Oxygen Mix:** The "thinness of the breath" at this elevation means your suit needs to pressurize slightly or provide an enriched oxygen mix. You will be physically exerting yourself, so anticipate a high oxygen consumption rate.

### II. Locomotion: The "Wading" Configuration

The greatest physical challenge is the ground itself. You are not walking on stone; you are walking on a writhing, slippery mosaic of living creatures.

*   **Distributed Load Footwear:** Standard boots will sink, potentially causing you to trip and be swallowed by the mass. You need "snowshoe" or "mudflat" platforms attached to your boots to distribute your weight over a larger surface area. The creatures are "soft-shelled," but they are dense; you need to float atop them like a leaf, not stomp like a boot.
*   **Non-Stick, Non-Porous Coating:** Your suit and wading platforms must be coated in a high-slip, non-reactive polymer (think Teflon on steroids). The creatures produce "internal fluids" that glow. If you stick, you die. If the fluid is acidic or enzymatic (given the predatory nature), you want it sliding off, not eating through your seal.
*   **Traction Spikes (Retractable):** The floor is a "living pavement" that rotates. While you want to float, you will eventually need to anchor yourself. Your platforms need retractable spikes—not for the creatures, but to dig into the hard, ceramic-like sub-floor if you punch through the layer of fauna to the actual rock beneath (though this should be avoided).

### III. Sensory Management: Navigating the Gloom

Human eyes are useless here. The bioluminescent violet haze is low-contrast and disorienting. The silence is a lie.

*   **Sonar/LIDAR Imaging:** Do not rely on ambient light. The "purple haze" renders everything in monochrome and shadows. You need active scanning to understand the topology of the swarm. You need to see the "waves" before they hit you.
*   **Audio Isolation & Amplification:** The environment produces a low-frequency "wet rustling" that vibrates in the teeth. This can cause nausea and disorientation. Your helmet needs active noise cancellation to dampen the constant *susurrus*. However, you must keep a directional microphone open. You need to hear the wet *snap* of a predator striking. If your audio software detects that specific sharp acoustic spike, it should flash a proximity warning in your HUD.
*   **Scent Masking:** You must smell like nothing. The ecosystem relies on chemical cues. If you smell like "wet meat" or "human," you become a beacon in the dark. Your suit’s exhaust ports must be filtered through activated charcoal to scrub your scent entirely.

### IV. Operational Tactics: The Survival Dance

You cannot stand still. The ecology of the vault is a conveyor belt of death.

*   **Move with the Vortex:** The floor rotates clockwise. Do not walk against the flow. You will exhaust yourself and be trampled or tipped. Move *with* the rotation, using your momentum to glide over the creatures.
*   **Avoid the Walls (The Kill Zone):** The text states that those on the perimeter are pushed toward the walls to desiccate. The walls are radiators. If you touch the wall, or are pinned against it by the swarm, your suit's cooling systems will fail, and you will be baked. Maintain a position in the "middle tier" of the swarm—not the deep center (where the giants are), and not the edge (where the heat is).
*   **Do Not Stand Out:** The creatures react to motion and vulnerability. Move slowly and deliberately. Do not thrash. If a creature is crushed beneath you, do not recoil. Maintain your balance. If you fall, you are likely to be smothered or stripped by the scavengers.
*   **Zero Interaction Protocol:** You will see the "built" elements—the metallic spire, the geometric engravings. **Do not touch them.** These are not ruins; they are furniture covered in millions of bacteria and parasites. Touching them is a secondary concern; disturbing the creatures that use them as scratching posts is the primary danger.

### V. Mental Preparation

This is the hardest part. You are standing on millions of living beings that are silently eating each other.

*   **Dissociation Training:** You will feel the "living pavement" shifting under your feet. You will feel the vibrations of their movement. You must psychologically accept that you are causing damage simply by being there. You are stepping on living things. If you freeze up because of the morality of crushing a dinner-plate-sized isopod, you will die.
*   **Claustrophobia Management:** It is a "claustrophobic, total" density. Even though the cavern is cathedral-sized, the *floor* is infinite and moving. The darkness is "absolute" beyond the glow. You are in a pressurized can of flesh.
*   **Acceptance of the "Silent Lie":** Your brain will scream that something this big and this moving should be loud. It should roar. The silence will feel predatory, like the quiet before an ambush. You must intellectually override this instinct. The silence is the method of the hunt.

### Summary of Loadout
1.  **Sealed, Cooled Hard-Suit** with active thermal regulation.
2.  **Wading Platforms** (oversized, low-pressure feet).
3.  **LIDAR/Sonar HUD** for navigation in the violet haze.
4.  **Directional Audio** tuned to detect the "predator snap."
5.  **Scent-Scrubbing Exhaust System.**
6.  **Grappling Hook/Magnetic Winch** (for emergency extraction if you begin to sink or are swept toward the fatal heat of the walls).

**The Golden Rule:** You are a stone in a river of flesh. Do not fight the current, do not bleed, and do not stop moving.
