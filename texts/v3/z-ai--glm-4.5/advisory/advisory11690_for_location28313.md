**Subject:** Field Preparation Guide for Destination ID: "The Raw Crust" (The Embryonic Geology)
**Traveler Class:** Ordinary Human (Baseline Biological)
**Threat Level:** Extreme Environmental / Psychological

To understand how to survive here, you must first abandon your Earth-born conception of "landscape." You are not visiting a place; you are stepping onto a living surface that is currently in the process of being born. On Earth, geology is a slow history book; here, it is a realtime conversation. The ground does not support you—it tolerates you.

If you treat this location as a hiking trip, you will die. If you treat it as a quarantine zone, you might live. Here is how you prepare.

### 1. Respiratory and Thermal Systems
The atmosphere is your first enemy. It is described as heavy, viscous, and thin—an oxymoron that implies a high-density gas with low oxygen partial pressure, or perhaps an atmosphere thick with suspended particulates.

*   **The Suit:** You require a full-pressure, self-contained environmental suit. Do not rely on a simple filtration mask. The air is "dry" and filled with silica-rich powder. Inhaling even a micron of this "frozen foam" dust will petrify your lungs in minutes. The suit must be heavily insulated against radiant heat, but equipped with an active cooling liquid-loop system. The heat radiates from the *ground*, not the sun, so your boots and suit's lower half need thermal reflective plating to prevent cooking you from the feet up.
*   **The Air Supply:** The "viscous" air suggests high density. Standard regulators may struggle to push air into your lungs against the ambient pressure, or conversely, the thin oxygen at altitude will starve you. You need a **demand-fed rebreather** with a positive-pressure setting. This ensures that despite the strange atmospheric density, you are forcefully receiving the oxygen you need at the altitude your body expects.

### 2. Sensory Adaptation (The Darkness and The Thrum)
Your eyes and ears are liabilities here. The darkness is "profound," and the silence is a lie filled with low-frequency vibration.

*   **Visuals:** Your eyes will dilate fully to hunt for light, leaving you vulnerable to the sudden flash of a crystal shard extruding or a rock fall. You must use **multi-spectrum imaging**. A helmet display combining thermal imaging (to see the heat radiating from the earth) and light-amplification is essential.
    *   *Psychological Note:* Do not look down into the abyss for long. The scale is deceptive. Your brain will try to interpret a tower-sized monolith as a nearby rock, leading to vertigo when you realize the true scale. Keep your heads-up display (HUD) distance markers active at all times.
*   **Auditory:** The "low-frequency thrumming" vibrates the chest. This can induce nausea and disorientation over time. You need **active noise-canceling ear protection** that specifically targets sub-bass frequencies (infrasound). This won't stop the vibration in your bones, but it will prevent the nausea caused by your inner ear screaming that the world is collapsing.

### 3. Mobility and Anchorage (The Vertical Trap)
This is a world of verticality. The ground pitches and rolls. There are no flat valleys.

*   **Mag-Lev or Tethered Movement:** Do not walk freely. The "rolling motion" of the ground is not an earthquake; it is a respiration. If the ground pitches while you are standing on a spine of rock, you will be thrown.
*   **Anchoring Gear:** You must have a **motorized winch and auto-tether system**. You are essentially spacewalking on a planet. Whenever you stop moving, you must anchor yourself to the obsidian. But be careful: the stone is "frozen foam" and may be brittle. Your pitons need to be **expansion-bolts** designed for crumbling concrete, not standard rock-climbing gear.
*   **Traversal Method:** Do not jump. The distance between spires is deceptive. Use a grappling system with a tether reel. If you fall into the void, there is no bottom to hit—you will simply fall until the atmosphere crushes you or you drift forever.

### 4. The Geological Hazard Protocol
The landscape is "turbulent." It destroys itself as fast as it creates itself. This is the hardest part to prepare for because it requires you to unlearn your survival instincts.

*   **Avoid the "Beautiful":** You will see arches, bridges, and fortress-like hollows. **Do not approach them.** On Earth, an arch implies stability; here, it implies a momentary pause in a collapse. Those "fortress windows" are likely where a slab is about to peel away like a blooming flower. If you see a structure that looks intentional, it is a trap.
*   **The Salt-Crusted Quartz:** The text mentions spires growing inches per hour. This is incredible, but lethal. If you camp near one, you could be pinned to a wall or crushed in your sleep by fast-growing crystal. Maintain a **dynamic exclusion zone** of at least 50 meters from any active extrusion site.
*   **The Slab Fall:** When a cliff face "unfolds," it happens silently. Shockwaves travel through the ground, not the air. If you feel a dull vibration through your boots *before* you hear a crash, you are too close. The impact of falling monoliths sends dust clouds. These are silica storms. Seal your suit immediately upon feeling a tremor.

### 5. Psychological Conditioning
This is the most critical preparation. The human mind relies on static landmarks to orient itself. Here, the map rewrites itself hourly.

*   **Accept Insignificance:** You are a speck of dust on a thrashing beast. The "raw state" of the world does not care about you. Trying to assert dominance over this terrain will lead to panic. You must adopt a mindset of **passive observation**. You are a ghost passing through a construction site.
*   **The Silence:** The "mostly quiet" can break a human mind. We rely on bird calls, wind, rustle. Here, there is only the thrum and the silent destruction. Bring an audio loop of familiar "Earth" sounds (rain, forest noise) to play in your helmet at low volume to prevent the onset of sensory deprivation psychosis.
*   **Fear of the Deep:** The gloom is a physical weight. You may feel an overwhelming urge to lie down and sleep, drawn by the heavy warmth. **Resist this.** The warmth is suffocating; the ground is unstable. Set alerts in your suit to wake you if your biometrics show signs of lethargy or heat exhaustion.

### 6. Essential Kit Summary
1.  **Hard-Shell Exosuit:** Silica-proof, puncture-resistant against sharp obsidian.
2.  **Active Cooling System:** Vital to counter the radiant earth-heat.
3.  **Positive-Pressure Rebreather:** To handle the thin, viscous air.
4.  **Thermal/Low-Light HUD:** To navigate the reddish gloom.
5.  **Infrasound Dampeners:** To counter the chest-thrumming nausea.
6.  **Auto-Retracting Tether:** You are climbing constantly; never be untethered.
7.  **Seismic Sensors:** To detect the "silent" unfolding of cliffs before they happen.

**Final Warning:**
There is no water here. Do not look for it. Do not expect respite. This is a gallery of violence in slow motion. Your goal is not to conquer the spines of rock, but to witness the "turbulent, silent dance of becoming" without becoming part of the geology yourself.

Move lightly. Anchor often. Keep your eyes on the dark.
