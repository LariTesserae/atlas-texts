**Preparation Brief: The High-Altitude Aquatic Plateau of the Oar-Stalks**

To visit the Oar-Stalk city is to step into a place where geology and biology have negotiated a truce. For an ordinary human, this environment is deceptively beautiful. The clear blue water and diffuse light suggest a tropical paradise, but the "biting chill" and immense depth betray a lethal reality. You are not visiting a beach; you are visiting a vertical, submerged cathedral at high altitude, where the pressure is immense, the cold is absolute, and the sensory input is overwhelming in its subtlety.

Here is how you must prepare.

### 1. Physiological Fortification: The Cold and The Pressure

The primary threats to your biology are thermal and barometric.

*   **Thermal Protection (Active Heating):** Do not rely on standard wetsuits or passive drysuits. The water here is of glacial origin and possesses a density that leaches heat from the body faster than standard seawater. You require a **heated, pressurized exposure suit**. Ideally, this should be a lightweight **Atmospheric Diving Suit (ADS)**—a rigid, anthropomorphic exoskeleton. This serves two purposes: it maintains a one-atmosphere internal environment (eliminating the need for decompression stops and protecting you from the crushing pressure of the "miles below") and allows for active, battery-powered heating. You will need to be warm enough to remain motionless for hours to observe the slow dynamics of the city without shivering.
*   **Respiration:** Because the atmosphere is "loud" and the acoustic environment is critical, you should avoid open-circuit SCUBA. The constant hiss of escaping bubbles is sonic pollution in a place defined by the "resonant hum." A **closed-circuit rebreather** (if outside the ADS) is essential to maintain silence. If using the ADS, you will have normal air, but ensure the system is heavily dampened so the mechanical whirring of your life support does not disturb the peace.

### 2. Acoustic Calibration: The "Loud" Silence

The description warns of a "profound, resonant hum" and a bass-heavy drone felt in the chest. For a human, this environment can induce disorientation, nausea, or even panic.

*   **Auditory Protection:** Your helmet or ears must be equipped with **active noise-canceling dampeners** that specifically target low frequencies (infrasound). You need to filter out the oppressive pressure-wave of the "organ pipe" stalks without losing the ability to hear the high-frequency clicks of the fauna or the sharp sounds of the inhabitants’ tools.
*   **Communication:** Voice communication will be distorted by the density of the water and the reverb. Do not rely on speaking. Prepare a **pre-arranged set of hand signals** or, if your technology permits, a bone-conduction comms system that utilizes ultrasonic frequencies outside the hearing range of the local ecology.

### 3. Psychological Conditioning: The Pace of the Deep

The most difficult adjustment will not be physical; it will be temporal.

*   **Slowing Your Internal Clock:** The city moves on a "decade-long current." A human’s instinct is to dart, to grab, to react quickly. This will mark you as an anomaly, a chaotic spark in a place of slow fire. You must meditate on **stillness**. Before entering the water, practice slowing your heart rate. You must be prepared to drift for twenty minutes simply watching a single leaf-mat being woven.
*   **Confronting the Void:** The "plaza of giants" offers clear sightlines for hundreds of meters into the deep blue. There is no ground beneath your feet—only an unseen abyss miles below. This triggers primal vertigo. You must prepare yourself to trust in buoyancy and to accept "suspension" as your natural state. Visualizing yourself as a floating seed in a vast jar will help ground your psyche against the urge to scramble for a non-existent floor.

### 4. Gear and Equipment: The Primitive Protocol

The locals possess "primitive tech" and operate with profound patience. Arriving with glowing LED screens, stainless steel, and whirring electric motors is not just rude; it is invasive.

*   **Material Constraints:** **Leave all metal behind.** Do not wear dive knives with metal blades or clips with steel springs. Use **tempered glass, ceramics, polished shell, or high-density plastics**. Your gear should look like it grew there. Carbon fiber is acceptable if it is matte-finished to resemble dark wood or stone.
*   **Lighting Discipline:** The light here is "bright, diffuse, and perpetually cool." Do not use high-intensity spotlights. Use **diffused, amber-gel lanterns** that mimic the ambient glow of the canopy. Your goal is to see, not to spotlight. A harsh beam will blind the inhabitants and startle the "glassy" crustaceans.
*   **Tools:** If you must bring tools, bring stone or bone replicas. A sharpened flint knife or a heavy shell hammer will allow you to interact with the environment—perhaps helping to prune a stalk or splice a bridge—without introducing foreign chemistry to the ecosystem.

### 5. Cultural Integration: The Gardener’s Guest

You are entering a living home. The "buildings" are hollow stalks; the "walls" are bark.

*   **Touch:** Be hyper-aware of what you touch. The bark is worn smooth by "friction of countless generations," but it is also alive. Your suit should have **soft, gripper-textured palms** to prevent scraping the mossy slick that feeds the crustaceans.
*   **The "Gardeners":** You will share the avenues with massive, ray-like herbivores. They are the municipal maintenance crew. **Do not swim above them.** In their world, the silhouette blocking the light from the "shimmering ceiling" is a threat to their grazing. Swim low, near the "floor" of the plateau, or stay tucked close to the stalks, moving in the spaces between the pillars.
*   **Etiquette:** If you encounter the inhabitants, remember they are breath-holders. If you are in an ADS with a constant air supply, you are an immortal, metal-skinned giant to them. Keep your distance. Observe their pruning. If you wish to interact, offer a tool of bone or shell. Approach slowly, from the front, and mimic their drifting pace. **Never make a sudden grab.**

### 6. The Exit Strategy

The dynamic of the place is a "brittle, beautiful truce." It is stable, but it is not robust.

*   **Tethering:** Because the "floor" is miles below, you cannot simply "descend" to rest if you tire. You must remain neutrally buoyant within the city layer. Carry a **reel-based tether**. In the event of a suit failure or loss of propulsion, you cannot risk sinking into the abyss. Anchor yourself to one of the massive Oar-Stalks. They are the only solid thing in the world.

**Summary of the Traveler's State:**
You will enter the water as a warm, slow-moving bubble of glass and ceramic. You will hum gently to yourself to mask the silence. You will drift between the pillars like a piece of drifting plankton, armed with nothing but a stone knife and a patience that spans hours. You are here to witness the architecture of time, carved by water and pruned by hand. Move like the current, and the city will accept you. Move like a machine, and you will forever be an intruder in the cathedral.
