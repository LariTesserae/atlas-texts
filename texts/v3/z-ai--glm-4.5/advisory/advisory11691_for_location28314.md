**Traveler’s Advisory: The Sunken Cistern / The Buried Lung**

**Classification:** Closed-Ecological Subterranean Construct / Active Bio-Mechanical Ruin
**Hazard Level:** High (Environmental, Psychological, Faunal)
**Est. Duration of Stay:** Do not exceed 4 hours without "Surface" decompression.

Visiting this location is not like exploring a cave or a dungeon. It is like stepping inside the lung of a sleeping, rusting giant. The environment is less a place you occupy and more a substance you are immersed in. To survive here with your sanity and health intact, you must prepare for three simultaneous assaults: sensory overload, biological hostility, and the psychological weight of being watched.

Here is your comprehensive preparation guide.

### 1. Biological and Respiratory Fortification

The air is described as a "breathable soup." It is thick, humid, and laden with organic particulates.
*   **Respiratory Protection:** Standard dust masks are insufficient. You need a respirator equipped with filters capable of blocking particulate matter (P100 rating) and organic vapor cartridges. The air smells of "wet timber, slow-decaying moss, and unseen rust." This indicates a high concentration of mold spores and fungal mycelium from the glowing walls. Breathing this unfiltered for more than a few minutes will likely trigger respiratory distress or allergic reactions.
*   **The "Fog" of the Tree:** The central tree creates a fine mist. While likely water-based, in a closed ecosystem, mists often carry reproductive spores or microscopic parasites. Ensure your eye protection is sealed (goggles, not glasses) and covers the vents of your respirator to prevent fogging.
*   **Hydration Protocol:** It is counterintuitive, but in 100% humidity, you will dehydrate rapidly because your sweat cannot evaporate to cool you down. You must drink water constantly, before you feel thirsty. Bring an electrolyte mix. The "soup" air will make you feel heavy and lethargic; maintaining salt balance is critical to avoid heat exhaustion despite the lack of direct sun.

### 2. Tactile and Defensive Warding

The flora and fauna here are not merely present; they are organized. This is an occupied territory.
*   **Clothing:** You need a full-body coverage suit. Think "hazmat light" or heavy beekeeping gear. The environment is damp; cotton will rot and chafe. Wear synthetic, quick-drying materials with a tight weave.
    *   *The Rust:* Do not expose skin to the rusted surfaces. "Tetanus" is the least of your worries; ancient, alien subterranean rust often holds unique bacterial colonies. Your suit must be impervious to scraping.
    *   *The Moths:* The moths are large and trail dust. This dust is likely scale or fine powder that can irritate skin and eyes. A hood is mandatory.
*   **Footwear:** The floor is a "chaotic mosaic" of unstable tiles, rusted grates, and slick stones. High-top, waterproof mountaineering boots with aggressive ankle support are essential. You will be stepping on things that crunch, shift, and slide. Do not wear soft-soled shoes; you need to feel the instability through the boot to adjust your balance.
*   **Deterrents, Not Weapons:** The fauna (dog-sized rodents, moths) act as "custodians." They are observing you. If you act like a predator, they will act like a defending swarm. Do not bring firearms; the noise will echo disastrously in the vaulted ceiling, and the smell of blood will trigger a feeding frenzy in the scavengers.
    *   Bring **sonic deterrents** (high-frequency emitters) to create a "personal bubble" of noise that the rodents will find unpleasant to cross. If you must engage, use a heavy, shock-absorbing walking stick or cattle prod to push them away, not to kill.

### 3. Sensory Management

The greatest danger here is the noise. The "bone-shaking thrum" of the water wheels and the cacophony of dripping water will erode your cognitive function rapidly.
*   **Hearing Protection:** You *must* wear noise-canceling headphones or high-grade earplugs. However, you cannot deafen yourself entirely; you need to hear the "shift" in the machinery or the approach of a large animal.
    *   *Recommendation:* Use electronic shooter’s earplugs. They dampen the constant decibel level of the thrum (the damaging low-frequency rumble) but allow sharper sounds (like a squeak or a metal snap) to pass through.
*   **Lighting Discipline:** The ambient light is a "sickly green." This plays havoc with human depth perception and color recognition. You *will* need a light source, but use it with extreme caution.
    *   Use a **red or amber light mode** initially. Bright white LED lights will blind the local fauna (who are adapted to the dim glow) and may be seen as an act of aggression. Furthermore, bright light will reveal the true scale of the decay and the "arranged" debris, which can trigger vertigo or panic. Keep your beam focused on the floor; do not illuminate the vaulted ceiling unless absolutely necessary. The sensation of the "ceiling pressing upon consciousness" is real; seeing it in stark relief will induce claustrophobia.

### 4. Psychological Navigation

The environment possesses a "nagging inconsistency." It knows you are there, and it is studying you. This is the hardest part to prepare for.
*   **The "Guest" Mindset:** You are an intruder. Accept this. Do not touch the "composed" ruins. The description states the debris feels "arranged." Moving a crate or dislodging a piece of rusted iron might be the equivalent of knocking over a pillar in a temple. The architecture is a still-life painting; do not become the paint splatter that ruins it.
*   **Sanity Anchors:** The lack of a sky and the "breathing" nature of the room can cause dissociation. You need a focal point. Wear a mechanical wristwatch (no electronics, as the damp atmosphere and magnetic fields from the iron might kill them). Watching the second hand tick is a lifeline to linear time. This place operates on "geological time" or "metabolic time." You need to remember that seconds still pass.
*   **Expect the Weird:** You will see condensation flow *up*. You will see rats watching you with intelligence. When this happens, do not freeze and do not run. Acknowledge it, document it mentally, and keep moving. To react with fear validates their dominance over you. To react with curiosity might be seen as an invitation to interact. Maintain a bored, professional demeanor. You are a plumber checking the pipes, not an explorer discovering a new world.

### 5. The Exit Strategy

Getting out is harder than getting in. The "verticality" of the scaffolding and crates means you might have to climb.
*   **Rope and Grappling:** Do not trust the wooden scaffolding. It is "primitive," "creaking," and "protesting." It is part of the room's metabolism, meaning it moves. If you must climb, rely on your own gear.
*   **Decontamination:** When you leave, you will smell of the place. The scent of wet rot and rust is oily and pervasive. Seal your used suit in a bag immediately upon exit. Do not bring the spores of the Buried Lung back to your home dimension.

**Final Warning:**
The text mentions the place is a "tomb that has forgotten it is dead." Do not remind it. If you see a piece of machinery stall, do not try to fix it. If you see the central tree "shedding" excessively, do not stand under it. This place is alive in a way biology does not explain. Respect the humidity, fear the machinery, and ignore the eyes in the dark.

Good luck, Traveler. Watch your step on the grates.
