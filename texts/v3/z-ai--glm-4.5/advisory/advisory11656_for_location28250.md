Visiting the Wet-Loom is not like visiting a city, a forest, or a factory. It is like stepping inside the lung of a sleeping titan that is in the process of dreaming a world into existence. As an ordinary human, you are biologically fragile compared to the aggressive, hybrid nature of this place. Here, the boundary between the built and the born is porous, and the environment is actively rewriting itself while you watch.

To survive and traverse the Wet-Loom, you must prepare for a reality that is wet, jagged, loud in spirit but quiet in acoustics, and fundamentally unstable.

### 1. Respiratory and Dermal Protection: The "Suspended Life"
The first thing you will notice is the air. It is described as "thick," not with pressure, but with suspended biological and industrial matter. The humidity is tropical and suffocating, and the air is likely laden with spores from the engineered ferns, metallic particulates from the grinding gears, and aerosolized chemicals from the "milky" puddles.

*   **The Mask:** Do not rely on a simple N95 dust mask. You need a full-face respirator with P100 (or equivalent) filters. The air is a cocktail of moisture and microscopic debris. You need to ensure your mask has a **venting valve** to prevent the lens from fogging up in the 100% humidity.
*   **The Suit:** You need a suit that is waterproof yet breathable. The condensation falling from the ceiling is constant—it is a rhythmic drumming that will soak you to the bone within minutes. However, a standard rubber rain slicker will trap your body heat in the "thermal 2.2/3" environment. Invest in a high-quality **Gore-Tex shell** with sealed seams.
*   **Eye Protection:** The light is "curated" and bright—gold-white and intense. The "amber glass" ceiling likely acts as a massive greenhouse, amplifying UV rays to feed the aggressive vegetation. You need **polarized, wraparound sunglasses** with high UV protection. Crucially, these must be sealed or gasketed against your face (like tactical goggles). The "weeping" water from the ceiling is full of lime and chemicals; you do not want that dripping into your eyes.

### 2. Thermal Regulation and Hydration
The environment is a paradox of hot concrete and cool drafts, wrapped in wet heat.
*   **Layering:** Wear lightweight, moisture-wicking synthetic base layers (no cotton). Under your shell, you should wear clothing that covers your arms and legs entirely. While it is warm, the "milky" chemical water and the aggressive oil-slicked plants suggest that exposed skin is a liability. You want full coverage without insulation.
*   **Hydration System:** You will sweat profusely. The humidity prevents evaporative cooling, meaning your body’s cooling mechanism is inefficient. You must drink water constantly. Carry a **hydration bladder** with an insulated tube (so your water doesn't get scalding by the ambient heat) and drink electrolytes religiously. Do not drink the local water, even if filtered; it is full of lime and machine effluent.

### 3. Footing and Traction: The "Fresh" World
The most dangerous physical aspect of the Wet-Loom is the ground. It is "low erosion," meaning it is jagged, angular, and raw. You are walking on fresh greycrete, slag-glass, and rust.
*   **Boots:** You need heavy-duty mountaineering boots with a **Vibram or equivalent aggressive lug sole**. The surface is rough and uneven, requiring ankle support to prevent twists on the slag-glass.
*   **Puncture Protection:** The ground is littered with jagged industrial debris. Ensure your boots have a **steel or composite shank** in the sole to protect your feet from punctures.
*   **Grip:** The environment is constantly wet. The concrete is slimy with moss and chemical iridescence. Your boots must be designed for wet rock or technical sailing—soft rubber compounds that grip when wet are essential.

### 4. Navigating the Dynamic Architecture
You cannot map this place. The "map" is being written in real-time.
*   **The Shifting Floor:** The ground moves via massive pistons. You must develop a rhythm of walking. Watch the floor plates. Listen to the "muffled, hydraulic grace." Do not step onto a section that looks like it is settling—wait for the *hiss* of the piston to finish.
*   **Verticality:** This is a "vertical jungle." Do not plan to walk only on the floor. You may need to climb over piled excavated material or duck under vines. Wear **mechanix-style gloves** that offer dexterity and grip. You will need your hands free to balance.
*   **The Drop:** The floor has grates, and clouds drift below. **Acrophobia** will be a major issue. Trust the grates (they are likely structurally sound as part of the "cathedral-scale vault") but do not linger on them. The visual of infinite sky beneath your feet is disorienting.

### 5. Flora and Fauna Protocol
The life here is "engineered biology." It is aggressive.
*   **Contact Protocol:** Do **not** touch the plants. The description notes the boundary between organic and mechanical is non-existent. A vine might be "thick as an electrical cable" because it *is* a cable. Touching the "electric green, spongy moss" could result in a bio-electric shock or a chemical burn from the oils the machines "photosynthesize." Treat the greenery with the same respect you would treat high-voltage wires.
*   **The Silence of the Fauna:** The birds and drones are silent observers. If you see the chrome crab maintenance drones, **freeze**. They are likely programmed to prune "interference." If you stand still, they may categorize you as part of the architecture. If you run, you become a "resource" or a malfunction. Move slowly and deliberately.

### 6. Psychological Preparation
The Wet-Loom is "unnerving." It is a place of constant, quiet creation.
*   **Acceptance of Incompleteness:** You must accept that you are standing in a place that is not yet "done." The anxiety of seeing a world form around you—the accretion of land, the rapid sprouting of ferns—can trigger existential vertigo. You are witnessing a time-lapse in real-time.
*   **The Silence:** Despite the machinery, the sound is a "low, resonant thrum." This is a brown-noise environment. It can lull you into a trance. The lack of bird calls or animal noise (only the *plink-plink* of water) creates a sensory deprivation effect. Talk to yourself, hum, or bring a non-electronic music player (the magnetic fields of the machines might fry electronics) to keep your mind anchored.

### Summary Checklist for the Traveler:
1.  **Respirator:** Full-face, P100 filters.
2.  **Suit:** Waterproof, breathable, full-coverage shell.
3.  **Eyes:** UV-protective, sealed goggles.
4.  **Feet:** Heavy, puncture-proof, high-grip boots.
5.  **Hands:** Dexterous work gloves.
6.  **Mind:** Patience. Wait for the pistons. Respect the silence. Do not touch the vines.

Move through the Wet-Loom as a respectful ghost. You are a visitor in a nursery that builds its own children. Stay dry, keep your balance, and do not interrupt the work.
