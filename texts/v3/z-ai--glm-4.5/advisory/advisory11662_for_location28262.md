To survive the **Limestone Lung**, you must prepare for an environment that attacks the senses through sheer intensity—blinding light, deafening acoustics, and oppressive warmth. This is not a dive; it is an intrusion into a functioning, sterile machine. Here is how an ordinary human multiverse traveler must prepare.

### 1. Visual Defense: The Eclipse Protocol
The light here is not just illumination; it is a physical force and a weapon. Ordinary dive masks will not suffice; they will simply funnel the violence into your retinas.

*   **Ocular Shielding:** You require goggles or a full-face mask with lenses rated for "Welding Grade 5" or higher. The light column from the aperture is concentrated enough to burn the retina. Ideally, use photo-reactive lenses that darken instantly upon entry.
*   **Mirror Finish:** Your lenses should have an external mirror coating. This reflects a percentage of the photon "weight" and prevents the "blinding white" from penetrating the glass directly into your eyes.
*   **Skin Coverage:** The light is described as having clinical clarity and high contrast. UV and radiation burns are a near-certainty. You must cover every inch of skin with a opaque, full-body suit. Even in the warm water, bare skin is a vulnerability to the violence of the column.

### 2. Acoustic Stealth: The Silence of the Stone
The greatest danger in this location is the **Loud** parameter. The room is an echo chamber where a hand movement becomes a boom. You are not entering a library; you are entering a cathedral of sound where silence is mandatory for your own sanity.

*   **Bone Conduction Dampening:** Standard earplugs are insufficient because water conducts sound directly into the skull bone. You need a specialized neoprene hood combined with custom-molded, solid-core earplugs designed to block bone-conducted vibration.
*   **Equipment Muffling:** Every piece of gear you carry must be silenced. Tape your buckles. Wrap your tanks in neoprene sleeves. Use lubricant on your O-rings to prevent the squeak of rubber on stone. The "roar" of the room is constant; you do not want to add to it, nor do you want the shockwave of your own movement disorienting you.
*   **Communication:** Voice communication is impossible. Hand signals are dangerous because they displace water, creating sonic booms. You must rely on pre-arranged, tactile signals (tapping your partner’s tank) and hard-wired luminous displays if you absolutely need to convey data.

### 3. Respiratory Engineering: The Closed Circuit
Do not bring standard Open Circuit SCUBA gear. The bubbles released will rise in "straight, unwavering lines," shattering the sterile illusion and violently disturbing the acoustic landscape. Furthermore, the "ecology is null"—there is no life to replenish oxygen, and the bubbles may trigger unknown hydraulic responses in the machine.

*   **Closed-Circuit Rebreather (CCR):** You must use a rebreather. This recycles your exhaled gas, scrubbing the carbon dioxide and adding oxygen. It ensures you emit no bubbles. You become a ghost in the water, acoustically invisible.
*   **Gas Composition:** The water is warm (approx. 37°C / 98.6°F). This mimics body temperature. Breathing dry gas from a tank can cause severe thermal shock in the throat. Ensure your rebreather includes a heat-moisture exchanger (HME) to humidify the breathing gas, preventing your airway from drying out and cracking in the "clinical" atmosphere.

### 4. Buoyancy and Thermal Strategy
The liquidity is "warm," "heavy," and "clinging." It behaves more like a dense saline solution or a gel than standard water.

*   **Weighting:** The "heavy liquidity" will provide significant natural buoyancy. You will likely need less lead weight than on Earth, but the "clinging" nature of the fluid increases drag. You need to be neutrally buoyant, hovering without kicking, because kicking displaces water and creates noise.
*   **Thermal Protection:** The water is roughly blood temperature. While this sounds comfortable, indefinite immersion in water at body temperature prevents your body from shedding metabolic heat, leading to hyperthermia. You need a suit that provides thermal protection *from the heat*. A "warm-water" wetsuit (thin, 1-2mm) is necessary to create a temperature gradient, allowing your skin to breathe and cool slightly, rather than stewing in the ambient temperature.
*   **Hydration:** The water tastes of limestone (calcium) and is hyper-saline or mineral-heavy in a way that tastes "sterile." Do not drink it. The mineral content is likely toxic for human consumption. You must bring your own water supply, ideally cooled, to counteract the ambient thermal pressure of the room.

### 5. Psychological and Spatial Anchoring
The geometry is disorienting. It is a "hidden pocket," a "micro-environment" that feels infinite but is actually a single room. The "sharp edges" and "high contrast" can induce visual vertigo (the *flicker vertigo* effect).

*   **Tethering:** Visual navigation is compromised by the blinding column and the repetitive stone blocks. You must carry a physical guideline reel. Attach a line to the entrance point (the sluice gate) before venturing deeper. If the light disorients you, the line is your only truth.
*   **Mental Fortitude:** You are entering a sterile vacuum. There are no fish, no distractions, just the "thrum" of the stone. This sensory deprivation mixed with overload (bright light, loud noise) can induce trance states or hallucinations. Before entering, meditate on the concept of *inertia*. Accept that you are a small biological intrusion into a massive, non-living mechanism. Do not look for meaning; look for geometry.

### 6. The Exit Protocol
The room is a "trap" or a "waiting lung." The only exit/flow is the partially open sluice gate.

*   **Sluice Awareness:** The convection current is sluggish but deliberate. Do not fight it. If you need to leave, drift toward the gate.
*   **Decompression:** Because the fluid is "heavy," the ambient pressure at depth might be higher than the physics of the room suggest. Treat the dive as a high-risk decompression dive. Ascend slowly. Do not rush toward the light; it will only burn you as you breach.
