**Subject:** Field Preparation & Survival Guide: The Obsidian Mesa / "The Cooling Fin"
**Clearance:** Class-4 Dimensional Traveler (Ordinary Human Physiology)
**Rating:** High Environmental Hazard; Moderate Technological Hazard; Extreme Psychological Impact.

To the traveler,

You are not going to a beach. You are not going to a forest. You are going to stand on the exposed cooling fan of a machine the size of a planet. This location—let’s call it the Obsidian Mesa—is a transitional ecology. It is violent, wet, sharp, and alive in a way that does not care about your biological fragility.

If you treat this like a standard hiking trip, you will die of heat stroke within an hour, or you will slip on a moss-covered razor and sever an artery. If you respect it, move slowly, and prepare for the physics of this specific shard, you will witness one of the most beautiful libraries of transmutation in the multiverse.

Here is how you prepare your body, your gear, and your mind.

### I. Respiratory and Thermal Management
The atmosphere here is the first enemy. It is described as a "heavy, wet blanket" at high elevation. This is a "super-humid" hyperbaric micro-climate. The air is thick enough to taste like ozone and wet iron.

*   **The Lung Conditioning:** Do not arrive cold. Your lungs need to be acclimated to high humidity and potential trace gases. Spend three days prior to arrival in a high-humidity environment (sauna, tropical biome) doing cardio. You need to maximize your oxygen efficiency before the "heavy" air makes breathing feel like lifting weights.
*   **The Filtration:** The air tastes of ozone. This suggests high electrical activity or radiation from the artifacts. You need a specialized respirator—not a filtered dust mask, but a positive-pressure half-mask with cartridges rated for ozone and volatile organic compounds (VOCs). You won't wear it constantly, but you must have it accessible when the "metallic" taste becomes palpable on the back of your throat.
*   **Cooling Strategy:** The heat is geothermal and ambient. Cooling vests are useless here because the humidity is too high for evaporative cooling. You need phase-change cooling packs that fit against your major arterial regions (neck, armpits, groin). Wear loose, woven-mesh fabrics that allow the air to circulate but prevent the "soggy paper" feeling of cotton.

### II. The "Glass and Ink" Footwear Protocol
The terrain is violently young obsidian and glass. It is sharp, serrated, and hot. Standard hiking boots will fail you. The leather will rot from the constant weeping of the rocks, and the soles will melt or slice open on the raw stone.

*   **The Exoskeleton:** You need footwear that resembles mountaineering boots more than hiking shoes. Look for vulcanized rubber uppers (chemical resistant to the iron-tasting water) with rigid, carbon-fiber midsoles. You need the sole to be stiff so that if you step on a jagged spike of obsidian, the rock does not pierce through to your foot.
*   **Traction:** The moss is "the only mercy," but it covers a "razor-sharp edge." Wet moss on polished glass is frictionless. Your boots must have vibram or similar aggressive lugs, ideally with a tungsten-carbide tip embedded in the heel for braking on slick descents.
*   **Gaiters:** Mandatory. The "weeping" rocks create a constant slick of near-boiling water at ground level. You need knee-high, insulated gaiters to prevent scalding water from entering your boots, while also protecting your calves from the serrated undergrowth.

### III. Hydration: The "Boiling Tears" Paradox
This is the most counter-intuitive aspect of this location. There is water everywhere, but you cannot touch it.

*   **The Water Discipline:** The water "sizzles and evaporates" on contact. The runoff is superheated by the black rock. Do not attempt to cup your hands and drink. You will scald your throat.
*   **Condensation Gear:** Your primary source of water must be your own tech. Carry a passive atmospheric water generator—a portable dehumidifier. With 100% humidity, a battery-powered unit can fill a canteen in an hour.
*   **The "Cold" Cache:** Do not carry all your water on your back. The heat will make it tepid and unpalatable. Carry a vacuum-sealed, insulated thermos of *ice water* specifically for treating heat emergencies. Keep this separate from your drinking supply; it is your medical kit.

### IV. Sensory Protection (The "Thrum")
The silence here is a lie. It is filled with sub-audible vibration from the artifacts.

*   **The Teeth Test:** The description mentions feeling the hum in your teeth. This is low-frequency infrasound. Long-term exposure to infrasound causes nausea, dizziness, visual hallucinations (seeing "ghosts" in the peripheral vision), and panic.
*   **The Counter-Frequency:** You need earplugs that are rated for noise reduction but specifically designed to dampen low frequencies (musicians’ earplugs or shooter’s plugs). This will take the edge off the visceral vibration without isolating you from the crucial *drip-hiss* sounds of the environment.
*   **Visual Strain:** The light is "diffuse" but reflects off electric green ferns and obsidian. This is a high-contrast environment (glossy black, neon green, white steam). Wear polarized photochromic lenses to reduce the glare from the wet rocks.

### V. Navigating the "Anomalies"
You are sharing this plateau with functional, ancient technology. It is not ruins; it is machinery.

*   **The Magnetic Variances:** Those spinning pyramids and alloy needles suggest strong localized magnetic or gravitational fields. A standard compass will spin uselessly. Rely on dead reckoning and triangulation of physical landmarks.
*   **No Touching Policy:** The alloys are likely thermally conductive. Touching a needle might freeze your skin instantly (heat sink effect) or burn it (active conduction). Treat every geometric shape like a live high-voltage wire. Maintain a 5-meter exclusion zone from all "built" anomalies.
*   **The "Chrome Bird" Protocol:** The fauna appears to be part of the maintenance system. The "tuning fork" sound is sonar or data transmission. If you see the chrome bird:
    1.  **Freeze.**
    2.  **Do not make eye contact** if it feels aggressive (predatory mimicry).
    3.  **Do not emit radio signals.** Turn off your walkie-talkies or GPS comms. The bird might interpret the signal as a malfunction in the system that needs "repairing" (by removing you).

### VI. Psychological Calibration
The most dangerous part of this trip is the feeling of being a "microbe."

*   **The Agoraphobia of the High Plateau:** You are on a shard detached from the world. Looking down at the clouds will trigger a primal "void-glitch" fear in the human brain. To prepare, spend time at high altitudes on Earth (cliffs, mesas) to condition your vestibular system.
*   **Acceptance of Impermanence:** The landscape is "deciding what it wants to be." The path you walk in might look different when you walk out. Do not rely on maps being accurate hour-to-hour. Be prepared to scramble. Be prepared to climb.
*   **The Solitude:** There are no flocks, no swarms. You will be alone with the "drip-hiss." This silence amplifies internal monologue. If you are prone to anxiety or existential dread, bring a companion. Do not come here alone. The "active quiet" can trick the brain into hearing voices.

### Summary Checklist for the Drop:
1.  **Suit:** Full-coverage, woven-mesh, tear-resistant (Kevlar/Cordura blend), light color (reflects heat).
2.  **Armor:** Knee and elbow pads (hard shell)—you will fall; the ground is unforgiving.
3.  **Gloves:** High-heat resistance silicone grip pads (for scrambling on hot rocks).
4.  **Audio:** In-ear filters for infrasound protection.
5.  **Water:** Atmospheric generator + 1L emergency cold water.
6.  **Tech:** Analog watch (mechanical movement, not digital/battery), notebook, graphite pencil (ink runs in the humidity).
7.  **Emergency:** A "space blanket" (mylar)—not for cold, but to reflect radiant heat if you get too close to a geothermal vent.

Go lightly. Do not try to conquer the mesa; it is younger and stronger than you. You are a transient ghost in its cathedral. Move like the mist: observe, dampen the rocks, and evaporate before you cause damage. Safe travels.
