Traveler, if you intend to walk the High Loam, you must first accept that you are not going to a place so much as you are going to a *state of being*. This is not a land you conquer; it is a land you endure. It is the geologic equivalent of a deep sigh, the final resting place of a planet’s energy. To survive here, you must protect yourself from the damp, the silence, and the insidious flexibility of the reality itself.

Here is your preparation guide.

### 1. Thermal and Moisture Management: The "Wet Cold" Protocol
The single greatest physical threat in the High Loam is not the freezing temperature, but the dampness. At 0.3 degrees, water exists in a hateful, sluggish state that seeks to leech the heat from your body faster than dry ice ever could.

*   **The Shell:** Standard waterproof gear will fail. The moisture here is ambient; it does not just fall from the sky, it sweats from the rock and hangs in the air. You need a **fully sealed, non-breathable external layer**. Think of a diving dry-suit or a vulcanized rubberized canvas suit. You must be encased. If you use breathable membranes (like standard Gore-Tex), the external humidity will saturate them instantly, and the "damp" will find its way to your skin.
*   **The Insulation:** Beneath the shell, wear wool. It is the only fiber that retains insulative properties when wet. Synthhetics will hold the chill against you. Do not cotton; it will kill you.
*   **The Extremities:** Your boots must be hip-waders. The ground is a "wet mattress" of sphagnum and saturated loam. You will sink inches with every step. Seal your gloves to your sleeves and your boots to your pants. There must be no gap for the wet to enter.
*   **Heat Sources:** Do not rely on fire. The air is described as "dense with ancient oxygen." In a high-oxygen environment, fire behaves erratically and explosively. Use electric heaters or chemical warmers, but keep them sealed inside your dry suit to prevent the ambient damp from shorting them out.

### 2. Respiratory Protection
The air is "heavy, dense with the scent of wet dust and ancient oxygen."

*   **Filtration:** The "wet dust" is likely composed of eroded basalt and microscopic spores from the aggressive fern-life. You must wear a respirator with a P100 rating or equivalent. Long-term exposure to ancient rock dust can petrify the soft tissues of your lungs (silicosis), and you do not want to introduce foreign biology to your system.
*   **Oxygen Toxicity:** Be wary of the "ancient oxygen." If the partial pressure is significantly higher than Earth standard, you risk oxidative stress and neural toxicity. You may need a rebreather system to dilute the local air with a neutral buffer gas like nitrogen, limiting your oxygen intake to survivable levels.

### 3. Nutritional Logistics (The "Self-Contained" Rule)
You will find no food here.

*   **The Hunt is Null:** There is no fauna. The ecology is biomass without a heartbeat. You cannot hunt, trap, or fish.
*   **The Flora is Poisonous (Likely):** The vegetation is dark, leathery, and aggressive. It is thriving on a damp, mineral-rich soil that sounds geologically active. Do not eat the ferns. Do not eat the moss. Given the "Cold Preservation" rule, where things turn to stone or moss rather than rot, the local biology is likely incompatible with human digestion. You might find your own stomach turning to basalt.
*   **Carry All Calories:** You must pack every calorie you intend to burn. Estimate a high caloric burn rate (keeping the body warm in damp cold is energy-intensive), and carry 150% of that.

### 4. Navigational Philosophy: The Law of Intention
GPS is useless. Satellites cannot see through the cloud layer, and the "flexible" physics of the location mean that distance is relative, not absolute.

*   **Mental Discipline:** Navigation in the High Loam is psychological. The "Law of Relative Distance" dictates that fear stretches the horizon. If you are terrified of reaching the city, the city will recede forever. You must practice meditative focus. When you walk, walk with the absolute certainty that you are arriving. Doubt is your enemy; it adds miles to your step.
*   **The Anchoring Method:** To avoid getting lost in a time loop or a spatial repetition, carry physical "anchors"—heavy, mundane objects from your home reality (a lead weight, a specific piece of industrial steel). Bury these at intervals. The High Loam cannot easily overwrite the solidity of things brought from elsewhere; they will serve as breadcrumbs in a reality that likes to repeat itself.
*   **Visual Markers:** Do not look at the horizon; it lies. Look at your feet. Watch the moss. Navigate by the immediate texture of the ground, not the distant shapes of the hills.

### 5. Communication Protocols: The Tactile Standard
You will be screaming into a void if you try to speak normally. Sound does not travel here; the air swallows it.

*   **Touch-Talk:** You must establish a system of tactile communication. If you are traveling with a partner, you must hold hands, link arms, or carry a rope between you at all times. Squeezes, taps, and pulses will replace words.
*   **Bone Conduction:** Standard earpieces may fail. Use a throat mic or a bone-conduction headset pressed firmly against the skull. If you can vibrate your own bones, you bypass the air's refusal to carry sound.
*   **Solitary Warning:** If you travel alone, accept that you will be truly alone. You will not hear your own echo. This can induce a specific kind of madness called "Cotard’s delusion"—the feeling that you do not exist. Keep a recorded log of your voice. Play it back directly into your ears via bone conduction to remind yourself you have a voice.

### 6. Psychological Fortitude: The Silence Treaty
The silence here is predatory. It is an active vacuum.

*   **The "Stage Set" Anxiety:** The absence of animals—the lack of birds, insects, or ticking watches—creates a sensory deprivation tank effect. Your brain will start inventing noises to fill the gap. Do not trust them. If you hear a whisper, it is the wind sighing through the ferns, or the rock settling, or your own blood pumping. It is not a ghost. There are no ghosts here, only stone.
*   **Time Perception:** You will watch a fern uncurl and feel that seconds have passed, but realize hours are gone. This is not time travel; it is a dilation of perception caused by the sensory deprivation. Wear a watch that vibrates against your skin every hour. Do not rely on the sun or the sky; the light falls sideways here. Trust the vibration.

### 7. Architectural Etiquette: Do Not Break the Skeleton
The city you will find is not just a ruin; it is the skeleton of reality in this dimension.

*   **The "Grey Fog" Danger:** Under no circumstances should you attempt to dismantle, damage, or excavate the stone huts and longhouses. The prompt warns: breaking the built structure causes local space to collapse into a featureless grey fog. This fog is likely the raw, unformed chaos that exists *behind* the reality of the Loam. If you break a wall, you fall out of the world.
*   **Shelter:** You *can* use the structures for shelter, but only as they are. Do not drive a tent stake into the floor of a longhouse. Do not carve your name into the basalt. Enter, sleep, and leave without altering the placement of a single stone.

6.  **Tether/Rope** (To maintain contact and prevent separation).
7.  **Vibrating Alarm Watch** (To anchor you in linear time).

Traveler, the High Loam is not cruel. It is simply finished. It is a place that has forgotten how to be young, and it will treat you as a temporary, fleeting irritation—a brief spark of noise in a symphony of silence. Tread lightly, keep your wits sharp, and do not let the damp settle in your soul.
