Visiting the Sump-Garden is not a hike; it is an act of submission. You are not entering a location where you are the apex predator. You are a foreign antigen entering a humid, metallic lung. To survive here with your sanity and biological integrity intact, you must abandon the standard survival playbook. Fire is useless, metal is a liability, and speed is impossible.

Here is how an ordinary multiverse traveler must prepare to walk on the garden.

### 1. The Philosophy: The "Soft" Gear Rule
The first and most critical rule of preparation is the **abandonment of durability as you know it.**

In most worlds, you prepare for the wild by wearing heavy boots, canvas, leather, and carrying steel tools. In the Sump-Garden, that gear will betray you. The humidity is not just wet; it is chemically aggressive. Leather will rot and soften into a pulp within six hours. Steel will bloom with orange rust within a day, seizing hinges and snapping blades.

**The Wardrobe:**
*   **Base Layer:** You need a full-body wetsuit made of closed-cell foam or high-grade neoprene. It must be sealed at the wrists and neck. This serves two purposes: it keeps the "sweat" of the garden from macerating your skin, and it provides a neutral, slick barrier against the friction of the root-mattress.
*   **Outer Layer:** Forget breathable fabrics; the air is too thick for breathability to work. You need a smooth, synthetic shell—something like a drysuit or a coated nylon. Avoid zippers and metal snaps. Use magnetic closures or heavy-duty velcro. The goal is to be amphibious.
*   **Footwear:** Do not wear heavy hiking boots. They will get sucked off your feet in the spongy root-floor. You need **split-toe diving boots** with aggressive, rubberized tread. The terrain is uneven and treacherous; you need the tactile feedback of the ground to avoid stepping into a hollow pocket between roots.

### 2. Respiratory and Sensory Conditioning
The air here is described as "thick enough to chew." It is warm, wet, and heavily laden with spores, pollen, and the metallic tang of copper. For an outsider, this atmosphere can induce nausea, dizziness, or panic.

**The Respiratory System:**
*   **Filtration:** A standard dust mask is insufficient. You need a respirator with filters specifically rated for volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and heavy metal particulates. The smell of "wet copper" suggests trace metals in the mist, possibly from the digested machinery.
*   **Thermal Management:** The air is body temperature or warmer. Breathing hot, wet air is exhausting. If possible, acquire a respirator with a slight cooling plate, or simply acclimatize yourself in a sauna for several days before departure.

**The Auditory System:**
*   The "deafening thrum" of transpiration and hydrostatic pressure can cause severe ear fatigue and migraines. High-decibel earplugs are mandatory, but you need to be able to hear the *pings* of dripping water to navigate.
*   **Recommendation:** Carry custom-molded filtered earplugs that dampen the low-frequency thrum (the rumble) but allow high-frequency sounds (the pings and snaps) to pass through.

### 3. Tools: The Stone Age Protocol
Because electronics fail and metal corrodes, you must regress technologically. You are entering a place where the machine is dead; you must act like the creature that outlived the machine.

**The Arsenal:**
*   **Cutting Tool:** Bring a machete or a large knife, but make it **ceramic, obsidian, or high-grade flint**. Steel will rust into a jagged tooth the moment you draw it. A ceramic blade will bite through the vine-flesh cleanly and remain chemically inert.
*   ** traversal Aid:** The ground is unstable. You need a **staff or walking pole**. Do not use aluminum. Use a length of dense, sealed fiberglass or, ideally, hardwood that has been varnished against the rot. You will use this to probe the root-mattress before every step to ensure you aren't stepping onto a hollow rust-bucket or a deep pocket of water.
*   **Lighting:** The canopy blocks the sky. Headlamps are electronic and prone to failure in the humidity. Bring **chemical light sticks (Cyalumes)** in abundance. Tie them to your staff or your wrist. They offer a low-grade, waterproof light that won't short-circuit. If you must use a flashlight, waterproof it in a housing and carry no spare batteries—the humidity will kill the contacts.

### 4. The Route: Walking on the Lattice
You cannot walk a straight line. The garden is a "dense, knotted mattress."

**Movement Technique:**
*   **The Shuffle:** Do not lift your feet high. Slide them along the surface of the roots. Lifting your feet creates suction against the wet, spongy mat and expends unnecessary energy.
*   **Weight Distribution:** If you stop, do not stand still. The plants are reacting to weight. Find a "node"—a convergence of large roots or a pipe-arch—to rest. If you stand still on a vulnerable root, the slow-motion violence of the garden may swallow you while you blink.

### 5. Mental Preparation: The Geologic Timetable
This is the most dangerous part of the preparation. The Sump-Garden operates on a timescale that will break a human mind.

**The Zone-Out Danger:**
*   The environment is monotonous—green, wet, and humming. The isolation is absolute. Without active vigilance, you will succumb to the "green trance." You will stop moving. You will sit down against a warm pipe. You will watch the sloth blink once every ten minutes. And you will let the moss grow over you.
*   **Countermeasure:** You must set an internal rhythm. If you have a waterproof mechanical watch (automatic winding, no battery), check it every 15 minutes. If not, create a ritual—tap your staff on metal three times every hundred paces. You must create artificial events to pierce the ambient timelessness.

### 6. Interaction with Fauna
The sloth-creature is a master of camouflage. It is the only thing moving, aside from you.

**The Rule of the Sloth:**
*   It is likely a filter-feeder or a scavenger of the nectar. It is not a predator, but you are an anomaly.
*   **Do not touch it.** Its fur mimics the environment, meaning it likely hosts a unique ecosystem of bacteria, mites, or irritants. Touching it could introduce a pathogen your immune system has never seen.
*   **Do not stare.** In the silence, prolonged eye contact with a creature that barely breathes can induce a form of ecological horror. Acknowledge it, mark its position, and move on.

### 7. Extraction and Decontamination
You cannot simply walk out and take a commercial flight home. You are carrying the Sump-Garden with you.

**The Exit Protocol:**
*   **The Boundary:** Walk up the slope until the green thins. Do not stop immediately. Keep walking until your boots crunch on dry, normal earth.
*   **Sterilization:** Before re-entering the multiverse transit hub, you must strip. Burn the clothes you wore. The root-spores and moisture-seeking bacteria in the weave of your suit will devour a spaceship's upholstery given half a chance. Scrape your skin, clean your nails, and flush your ears. The "sweet, cloying scent" is a biological marker; if you smell like the garden, things from other dimensions might try to hunt you.

### Summary Checklist for the Traveler:
1.  **Suit:** Full-body sealed neoprene/wetsuit. No leather, no metal zippers.
2.  **Breath:** VOC respirator (replace filters hourly).
3.  **Ears:** Frequency-filtering earplugs.
4.  **Hands:** Ceramic blade; fiberglass or wooden staff.
5.  **Light:** Chemical glow sticks (red preserves night vision, green for mood).
6.  **Mind:** Rhythmic counting to avoid the trance.
7.  **Soul:** Accept that you are small, soft, and temporary. The garden is big, hard, and forever.

Prepare to sweat. Prepare to be deafened by the sound of life eating metal. And most importantly, prepare to move slowly.
