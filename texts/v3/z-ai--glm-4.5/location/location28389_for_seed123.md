The place is not a valley, nor a canyon, but a hollow—a singular, cup-shaped depression in the earth, roughly the size of a grand theater or a small amphitheater. It feels less like a geographical feature and more like a room that the floor simply forgot to build.

The heat here is physical and heavy, a warm, wet blanket that sits against the skin (Temperature 1.7). The air smells of wet iron, ozone, and the sharp, peppery scent of crushed stone. Because the elevation is low (0.9), the atmosphere pools here, unable to drift away. The light is normal (2.2)—a honest, unfiltered daylight that strikes the center of the hollow with clinical brightness—but the sheer density of the environment casts the corners into deep, bruising shadows.

The ground is a chaotic jigsaw of "young" rock (Erosion 0.7). This is a fresh wound in the world’s crust. The stone hasn’t been smoothed by centuries of wind or rain; it is jagged, angular, and aggressive. Slabs of slate and granite jut upward like broken teeth, their edges sharp enough to cut careless hands. There is no soil to speak of, only raw, fractured mineral.

Water (1.1) does not flow here; it weeps. The rock faces are damp, perpetually slick with a glistening sheen of condensation. Occasionally, a drop gathers weight on a stalactite of raw ore and falls, striking the stone floor with a single, resonant *plink* that echoes disproportionately in the quiet (Sound 1.3). It is an intermittent rhythm, a slow clock ticking in the silence.

Vegetation (1.1) has failed to take hold in this hostile, sharp-edged cradle. There are no trees, no grass, just a few pioneering clusters of what look like rust-colored lichens clinging to the high, unreachable ledges. The green is absent, replaced by the greys and blacks of the stone and the muddy browns of the dampness.

But the emptiness of plant life is misleading. The hollow is packed (Density 2.9). Every inch of available space between the jagged rocks is occupied.

The "Built" presence (1.1) is scattered but undeniable. It looks as though a factory from a different epoch was pulverized and rained down into this pit. Massive, rusted gear-teeth half-buried in the gravel; a piston rod the size of a tree trunk lying shattered across two boulders; sheets of perforated metal leaning against the walls. There is no structure to it, no architecture, only the debris of industry—metal bones (Tech 1.7).

And upon this industrial graveyard, the ecology (Fauna 1.8) thrives.

The space is abundant with life, though calling it "animals" feels insufficient. The inhabitants are small—crab-like, beetle-like, things with too many legs and shells of burnished copper and chitin. They swarm over the scattered debris. They do not scuttle so much as they flow, a living tide of carapace and claw navigating the sharp rocks with impossible agility.

This is the "Weirdness" (2.3) of the place. The creatures are not just living on the machine parts; they are integrating with them. You see a beetle with a gear fixed into its back, spinning as it walks. Another creature seems to be eating the rust, turning it into fuel, its abdomen glowing with the dull, orange heat of digestion.

It is an "Active" system (2.2). It is not a peaceful glade. It is a frenzy of silent industry. Thousands of creatures dismantle the scattered pipes, carry shards of metal to new locations, assemble small, useless-looking towers from the detritus. It is a predator-prey cycle where the prey is the landscape itself.

The sound is "mostly quiet" (1.3), yet the silence is deceptive. Up close, the air hums with the friction of a thousand tiny legs on stone, the clicking of mandibles on metal, the soft grinding of internal gears. It is the sound of a watch ticking loudly inside a lead box—muffled, constant, and intense.

The atmosphere is claustrophobic despite being open to the sky. The sheer density of the movement—the way every square centimeter of ground is covered by a working creature or a sharp piece of scrap—creates a sense of suffocating saturation.

Standing in the center of the hollow, you feel like an intruder in a digestive tract. The world here is young, sharp, and wet. It is a pocket-sized engine room, isolated and sealed, running on a fuel of rust and water, processing itself in a warm, damp, eternal hum.
