The air here tastes of iron and wet moss. It is a cold, damp breath that clings to the skin, heavy with a chill that seeps through cloth and settles in the marrow. This is a place of verticality—a jagged, cathedral-scale spire of dark stone rising out of the gloom, its edges too sharp to be the work of ages. The rock is young, newly birthed from the earth’s crust, fractured into sheer cliffs and knife-like ridges that have not yet known the softening touch of wind or rain.

There is no sun, or at least, none that penetrates the thick, low-hanging ceiling of cloud. The light level is a perpetual twilight, a dim gray suffusion that does little to clarify the shapes in the distance. Despite the darkness, the vegetation is lush, suffocatingly so. Every available inch of the vertical stone is choked with life. Great mats of dark, slick ferns and vines drape from the ledges, knotted together so tightly they form a single, heaving organic wall. The green is deep, almost black, the color of bruised leaves and shadows.

The silence is absolute, yet the volume is deafening.

This is the engine room of the world. There is no animal life here to cry out or scurry; there is only the roar of the environment itself. It is a chaotic, industrial cacophony. Great vents in the cliff face blast scalding steam with a rhythmic *huff-chug-huff* that sounds impossibly like the pistons of a massive, subterranean locomotive. The pressure release is violent, shaking the very stone beneath your feet.

Water is everywhere, but it is not a placid presence. It is intermittent and violent. Condensation slicks every surface, making the hand-over-hand climb impossible, but the true danger comes from the sudden deluges. Without warning, a fissure in the rock will open, spewing a jet of high-pressure water that hisses through the air like a ruptured boiler pipe. These jets strike the opposite wall with the force of a hammer, eroding the stone in real-time. You can watch the landscape changing—minutes ago, there was a ledge; now, it is a cascade of falling debris and slurry.

The density of the growth amplifies the sound. The thick, spongy moss absorbs nothing; instead, the thrumming of the earth vibrates through the root systems, creating a low-frequency hum that rattles the teeth. It is a loud, violent ecosystem functioning at maximum capacity.

Geology mimics industry. Streaks of raw, oxidized ore run through the black rock like rusted pipes. Sulphur vents belch yellow clouds that curl around the grasping tendrils of the vegetation. The ground is unstable, constantly shifting as the "young" earth settles. Rocks shear off and plummet into the unseen abyss below, sparking as they strike iron deposits on the way down, brief flashes of lightning in the dark.

It feels as though you are standing inside a machine that is still booting up. The turbulence is constant, a storm-state that never breaks. There is no stillness, no moment of respite. The water hisses, the vents thud, the steam screams, and through it all, the lush, damp vegetation continues its voracious climb, indifferent to the crushing noise and the chaotic rebirth of the stone it clings to.
