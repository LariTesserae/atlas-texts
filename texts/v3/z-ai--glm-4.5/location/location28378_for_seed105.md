This is the High Drown, a world where the concept of "sea level" has climbed the mountains and kept rising, drowning the jagged teeth of the planet in a cold, eternal suspension.

You are submerged. Superimposed over this is the sound of the vegetation. As the strong currents sweep through the Kelp-Firs, the fronds rub against one another, creating a dry, rasping chorus—like the bowing of a million cellos. It is a cacophony of friction, a "singing" of the forest that never ceases.

You find cairns—piles of rough-hewn stones, stacked carefully by hands that have long since dissolved. Some are arranged in circles, others in lines pointing toward the unseen surface.

They are the only evidence that something once breathed here and looked up through the crushing dark, wondering what lay above. The builders are gone, leaving only their stone fingerprints in a world that has no use for them.

**The Dynamics: The Sifting Snow**
The world is active (1.7). The current here is not a river but a tidal breath; the water level rises and falls slightly with the passage of unseen celestial bodies, causing the pressure to squeeze and release. When the pressure drops, "marine snow"—a blizzard of organic detritus and calcified dust—rains down from the darkness above, turning the water into a swirling fog. This is the dynamic heartbeat of the region: a blinding, noisy storm of silt that buries the stone cairns and feeds the grasping roots of the Kelp-Firs, only to be swept away by the next surge of the current.

It is a cold, loud, beautiful graveyard. A high-altitude ocean where the only movement is the wind in the trees and the drift of the earth itself, waiting in the dark.
