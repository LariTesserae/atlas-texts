The heat here is a physical weight, a heavy, wet blanket that presses against the skin from every direction. It is not the dry, burning heat of a desert, but the suffocating, sweltering oppression of a boiler room sealed for a century. The air is thick enough to taste—metallic, oily, and carrying the unmistakable tang of warm, standing water.

You are standing in a confined, claustrophobic space, though the ceiling is lost in darkness high above. This is the "Under-gallery," a cavity buried deep beneath the surface of the world. There is no natural rock here, no earth or stone. Every surface is constructed: riveted iron plates, rusted mesh walkways, and pitted concrete. It is a room entirely made by hands, or machines, long since departed.

The scale is deceptively intimate. While the floor area is no larger than a modest cathedral nave, the density of the space makes it feel microscopic. Every cubic inch is occupied. Giant pistons the size of tower columns rise from the unseen floor below, their surfaces weathered to a smooth, dark patina (erosion 2.2). These pistons do not move quickly; they are locked in a glacial, slow-motion dance, shifting maybe an inch a day. This is the dynamic of the place: a patience so profound that movement is almost imperceptible, detectable only by the faint, rhythmic groan of metal expanding in the heat.

The lighting is nonexistent to the unaided eye. It is a darkness of absolute measure (light 0.1). However, the environment is not devoid of illumination sources. The machinery itself glows with a faint, bruised-purple heat—a thermal luminescence that reveals the outlines of pipes and valves only when you stare directly at them, allowing the afterimage to paint the room in ghostly negatives.

Water is present but sparse, an intermittent weeping rather than a flow. Condensation gathers on the upper reaches of the machinery, forming heavy, swollen droplets that hang for hours before succumbing to gravity. When they fall, they *plink* loudly into the darkness below, the sound echoing briefly in the silence. The water hits hot surfaces and flashes instantly into steam, contributing to the damp, foggy atmosphere that clings to the lower levels. The moisture has encouraged a singular, ecology—a sparse (vegetation 1.1) patch of velvety, grey mold clinging to the joint where a hot-water pipe meets a cooler valve. It is the only living color in a monochrome world of iron and shadow.

The "built" nature of the environment is total. Even the floor beneath your feet is a grating, revealing more machinery churning silently in the void below. There is no solid ground, only layers of construction. Pipes, insulated with crumbling asbestos-like wrapping, bundle together in thickets so dense (density 2.4) that you could not squeeze your arm between them. They snake vertically and horizontally, a nervous system of a dead leviathan.

Sound is rare (sound 0.8). The silence is not empty; it is filled with the pressure of the heat and the low-frequency thrum that lives in the bones of the structure. Occasionally, a "tick" echoes—a sound like a cooling engine contracting—but mostly, it is a heavy, expectant quiet.

Fauna is rare (fauna 1.3), but not absent. If you stand still for a long time, you might hear the skitter of hard-shelled things—blind, albino crustaceans that have evolved to feed on the grey mold and the sludge of oil and condensation. They move with lightning speed in stark contrast to the slow machines, flashing briefly through the purple gloom before vanishing into the cracks.

The "weirdness" lies in the preservation. Despite the heat and the damp, nothing is rotting away. The rust has reached a state of equilibrium; the iron is red and crusty, but it does not flake. It feels stable, fossilized. The machines are clearly industrial (tech 1.5), yet their purpose is obscure. There are no gauges, no control panels, no signs of human interaction—just the relentless, slow compression of pistons and the weeping of the pipes. It feels like a tomb for a process that the world above has forgotten, churning on in the dark, hot and patient, waiting for a change that may never come.
