The plateau sits high above the sea line, a vast, dissected mesaland where the horizon is broken not by trees or crags, but by the skeletal silhouettes of industry.

The air here is heavy and holds its breath. It is warm (2.0), a tropical, stagnant heat that clings to the skin, but it is not dry. The atmosphere is saturated (1.8), perpetually thick with moisture that precipitates not as rain, but as a constant, weeping condensation. Water does not fall from the sky; it bleeds from the world. Every surface—steel, stone, concrete—slick with a film of tears, gathering in rust-colored rivulets that run down the flanks of the great structures and pool in the uneven flagstones.

This is the Rust-Grave, a city that has forgotten it was ever built.

The urban sprawl (2.5) is immense, stretching for miles in every direction, yet it feels strangely empty. The density is low (1.2); the buildings are spaced apart like monuments in a park, connected by arterial pipelines and elevated causeways that rust slowly in the humid air. There are no crowded streets, no bustling plazas. The architecture is industrial (2.1) and colossal: refineries the size of cathedrals, gear-work assemblies taller than towers, and blast furnaces that have been cold for centuries, their mouths hanging open like caves.

Vegetation is scarce (0.6), unable to take root in the metal-heavy soil, yet life persists in the margins. In the shadows of the great machines, where the condensation gathers into shallow marshes, thick, grey mosses carpet the ground. Occasional hardy ferns, their leaves blackened by soot, unfurl from the cracks in the pavement. They do not photosynthesize in the traditional sense; they vibrate, feeding on the low-frequency thrum that permeates the air. There are no birds (0.8), no rodents scurrying in the walls. The ecology is minimal, reduced to the damp lichen and the solitary, drifting shapes of air-jellies—translucent, gas-filled sacs that float through the fog, their bodies pulsing in time with the machinery.

The sound is the first and only truth here (2.6). It is deafening, a roaring ocean of mechanical noise that drowns out thought. It is not the chaotic clash of a busy factory, but a deep, resonant drone, the bass note of a planet-sized engine idling. It is a vibration felt in the teeth and the bones. Giant bellows, half-buried in the hillside, exhale steam with a sound like a dying god, rhythmic and slow. Huge pistons, the size of office blocks, plunge into deep shafts, shuddering the ground with every impact.

Yet, despite the violence of the noise, the change is glacial (0.9). The erosion is established (2.5); the sharp edges of the industrial age have been worn smooth by centuries of dripping water and oxidation. The buildings look less like construction and more like natural geologic formations, iron outcroppings that have always been there, rusting in slow-motion. The processes visible are patient, cyclical. A massive flywheel spins in the distance, completing a single rotation over the course of an hour. Condensation gathers, drips, erodes, and reforms. The city is a machine performing a function that takes millennia to complete, moving with the terrifying majesty of tectonic plates.

There is a strangeness here (1.6), a dissonance in the logic of the place. The infrastructure is too vast for the population—which appears to be zero. Who tends the fires? Who greases the gears? Walking through the wet, open avenues, you feel like an ant inside a watch that no one has wound in a thousand years, yet the hands still move.

Light filters through the constant mist (1.8) in diffuse, grey beams. It creates no shadows, only a damp, uniform twilight. In the distance, beyond the rusted spires, the land rises further, the high elevation (1.5) offering a view of a sea of clouds below, and above, the dark shapes of dirigibles—docked to mooring towers that sway gently in the hot, wet wind.

It is a place of oppressive grandeur. A temple to entropy and production, sitting alone in the clouds, weeping water and screaming silence, waiting for a cycle that has not yet finished.
