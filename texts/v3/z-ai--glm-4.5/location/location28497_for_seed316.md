The first thing you register is not the sight of the place, but the feeling of it in your teeth. The air is so thick with suspended moisture and sediment that it possesses a weight, a viscosity that presses against the skin like a second, heavier atmosphere. But it is the noise that defines existence here. It is a deafening, constant roar—not the chaotic crash of a storm, but a deep, thrumming vibration that seems to emanate from the atomic structure of the world itself. It is the sound of a billion tons of wet matter grinding against itself, a low-frequency groan that vibrates the marrow of your bones.

When you force your eyes open, the light is dim, a bruised and humid twilight that suggests a sun permanently filtered through leagues of drifting fog and condensation. There is no horizon line, only a gradient of shadow fading from the muddy gray of the ground to the obsidian gloom of the sky.

You are standing on the Flattened Lands.

The terrain here is the end result of an eternity of erosion (2.8/3). There are no mountains, no hills, not even a jagged stone to stub a toe against. The world has been worn down to its absolute nub, a planetary plane of absolute smoothness that stretches on for thousands of miles. This is the vastness (3.0/3) of a dying god’s skeleton. The elevation is so low (1.1/3) that the distinction between "earth" and "water" feels like a technicality rather than a reality.

The ground is packed (3.0/3)—a hyper-saturated, indistinguishable mash of silt, clay, and decayed organic matter. It is warm (2.0/3) to the touch, holding the heat of a world that has forgotten how to cool. Every step sinks slightly, the ground yielding with a wet, sucking sound, though the sheer density of the material prevents you from sinking deep. It is less like walking on mud and more like walking on the surface of a massive, slow-moving muscle.

Water (2.2/3) is everywhere, yet there are no oceans or lakes. The water is trapped in the geometry of the soil. The rocks, which are scarce, are "weeping"—smooth, river-worn boulders the size of houses that are perpetually slick with runoff. The water doesn't flow so much as it bleeds out of the earth. The atmosphere is supersaturated, constantly condensing into droplets that hang in the air before joining the slick film that coats everything. It is a landscape that is always, eternally, post-rain.

Despite the wet and the warmth, vegetation is sparse (1.2/3). The ground is too turbulent, too chemically active, and the noise is too great for roots to take hold. Instead, life survives in isolated, bizarre pockets. You see thick, leathery stalks that rise twenty feet high, devoid of leaves, vibrating violently as they channel the sound of the planet into the ground. They are the only anchors in a shifting world.

This is a place of high turbulence (3.0/3) and reality breakdown (2.7/3). The stability of the ancient landscape is a lie. The ground beneath you is not still; it is slowly, visibly churning. Because the land is so flat and the soil so liquefied, the tectonic breathing of the planet creates massive, slow-motion waves. The ground swells and falls like the breathing chest of a sleeping giant. Huge fissures open up, revealing a glowing, bioluminescent underside to the crust, only to seal themselves moments later as the "skin" of the earth slides over it.

The physics here feel wrong. The density of the packed matter creates gravity pockets. You might walk up a slope that feels flat, or find that a thrown stone stops in mid-air, held in suspension by the sheer density of the humid atmosphere.

Fauna (1.4/3) is rare, solitary, and immense. In the distance, sliding through the mire on a lubrication of its own making, is a single, vast creature. It has no distinct shape, resembling a mound of sentient silt the size of a cathedral. It moves by peristalsis, rippling across the landscape, absorbing the nutrients from the perpetual decay. It makes no sound; it is mute in a deafening world, perhaps having evolved to communicate through the vibrations of the earth rather than the air.

Scattered (0.8/3) across the endless plane are the marks of the past—structures that are barely recognizable. They are not built of distinct bricks or metal (tech is 0.4/3), but rather seem to be calcified growths, ancient coral-castles of a bygone era that have since been smoothed down by the relentless erosion. They stand as monoliths, rounded and featureless, their origins erased by time. They serve no purpose but to break the wind, creating small, silent vortices in the screaming air.

To be here is to exist inside a lung. It is a biological processing plant on a continental scale. The dynamic is one of aggressive consumption and reformation. The land eats itself, digests itself in the warm, wet dark, and excretes itself again as the smooth, packed plain.

There is no shelter. There is no high ground. There is only the endless, vibrating flatness, the weeping rocks, and the roar of the world settling into its final, chaotic form. It is a deafening, dim, and crowded solitude—a place where you are surrounded by the intense presence of matter, yet utterly alone.
