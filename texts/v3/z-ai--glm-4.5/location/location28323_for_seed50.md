This is the sound of creation happening too fast, the geologic scream of a world trying to solidify before it dissolves.

The elevation is immense, lending the air a thin, biting quality, but the cold (1.2) is wet and invasive. It is a chill that soaks through clothing and skin, driven by the sheer volume of water.

The sky above is not a vault of blue but a roiling, slate-grey ceiling of condensed liquid. It is a "wall of rain" that falls with the force of a hammer, a perpetual, torrential downpour that creates a semi-fluid medium. Moving through it is less like walking through a storm and more like swimming through a violent current of air and liquid. The ground is never truly dry, only varying degrees of saturated.

Beneath this relentless deluge, the terrain is raw, embryonic, and undeveloped (0.4). This is a landscape of *emergence*. There are no worn, smooth river stones here, no ancient canyons carved by millennia of flow. Instead, the basin floor is a jagged chaos of "new" rock. The water falling from above is supersaturated with minerals and dissolved stone; as it impacts the ground and splashes, it precipitates instantly, building the earth upward.

The geology is a nightmare of accretion. Stalagmites and pillars rise like frozen fountains, some growing visibly taller by the hour, their surfaces slick and glistening with wet, grey slurry. The rock is sharp—glassy obsidian and fragile, porous pumice that shatters underfoot. It is a construction site without the builders, a chaotic accretion of matter where the land is actively generating itself in violent, spasmodic spurts. The terrain feels unfinished, a rough draft sketched in jagged lines and wet clay.

The pillars of stone are spaced far apart, isolated monuments in the gloom. Between them lies only the falling water and the echoing vastness. The density of matter is low; it is a world of negative space, a hollow cathedral defined by what is *not* there.

They grow flat and hard, almost indistinguishable from the rock itself, protectively sealed against the hammering water. They are the color of bruised iron.

Their tech is manual, immediate, and disposable. They leave no footprints, for the rain erases them in seconds, and the ground grows over them in minutes.

The light (2.5) is normal, filtered through the thick clouds of the downpour into a diffuse, shadowless grey. There is no hiding from the light here, nor from the wet.

The weirdness (2.2) lies in the physics of the place. The turbulence (2.7) is hyperactive, Solaris-like. The stone pillars hum with kinetic energy, vibrating at frequencies that make the eyes water. It is a place of instability, where the ground beneath you is literally being born as you stand on it, expanding and shifting, threatening to swallow you or crush you with new walls.

It is a loud, wet, raw, and lonely room—a high-altitude crucible where the world is being forged in a hurry, loud and messy, with plenty of space to watch it happen.
