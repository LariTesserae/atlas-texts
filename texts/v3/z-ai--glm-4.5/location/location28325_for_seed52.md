There are no stars and no sun, only a thick, suffocating glow that suggests a source just beyond the horizon. This perpetual twilight creates a world of silhouettes and soft edges, yet the ground beneath your feet contradicts the gloom with its violence.

The terrain is distressingly young—a mere 0.6 on the erosion scale. This land has not settled. It looks as though the crust of the planet was shattered only a week ago and has since cooled just enough to walk upon. The rocks are serrated knives; the ridges are broken teeth. There are no rounded hills here, no weathered sands smoothed by the millennia. The edges are fresh, raw, and black, glistening wetly.

A ridge of black glass might vibrate and phase out for a second, revealing a grid of glowing code beneath, before solidifying again. The young erosion is not merely geological; it is computational. There is no chirping, no wind rustling through trees, for there are no forests and little wind. The only constant noise is a low, sub-bass hum—the sound of the earth generating, the sound of the invisible gravity bridges holding tension, the sound of the water dripping endlessly into the deep pools. It is a silence that feels heavy, a quiet that demands you hold your breath.

The fauna (1.2) is rare and elusive. You might catch a glimpse of something moving at the periphery—a shape that defies biology. Perhaps a quartet of mechanical deer grazing on the metallic moss of a basalt outcrop, their legs pistoning softly. Or a swarm of silent, geometric moths that evaporate when they touch the ground. They do not fear you; they simply do not register you as part of the active ecosystem.

The sharp obsidian ridges are slowly grinding against each other, a tectonic groan that you feel in your teeth rather than hear. The city-spires pulse with light, shifting configurations. It is a warm, wet, dimly-lit orphanage of a world. It feels like a testing ground for gods who got bored—a place where the ground is sharp enough to cut you, the air is thick enough to drink, and the buildings are beautiful enough to make you forgive the loneliness. It is a place that is becoming, moment by moment, violently and quietly new.
