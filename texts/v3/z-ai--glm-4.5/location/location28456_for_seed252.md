The light here is absolute. It is a blinding, clinical white that seems to emanate from the very air itself, erasing shadows and leaving the landscape exposed and naked. You are underground, yet it feels like high noon on a cloudless day. This is a pocket, a singular enclosed cavern of modest proportions—perhaps the size of a cathedral nave—but because of the intensity of the illumination, the walls feel close, pressing in.

The space is young. Geologically, it is an infant. The rock has not had time to soften. The walls are jagged sheets of obsidian and basalt, thrust up in violent, angular fractures that look like they happened only moments ago. The edges are knife-sharp, untouched by the smoothing hands of wind or time. It is a mineral world, sterile and absent of anything green. There is no moss, no ferns, no lichen clinging to the stone. The black rock is bare, slick, and gleaming.

The heat is a physical weight, a heavy, wet blanket that settles immediately on the skin. The air is saturated, holding nearly as much water as it physically can. This is a weeping world. Every surface is slick with condensation. Water does not fall here as rain; it bleeds from the stone. Because the rock is fresh and hot, and the air is cooler than the depths of the fracture, water constantly precipitates out of the atmosphere. It runs in glossy, continuous sheets down the sheer black walls, pooling on the uneven floor.

The floor is a scatter of broken slabs, tumbled together like a chaotic puzzle. Between the stones, shallow pools of water have collected, perfectly still and reflecting the blinding light above. The water is clear and hot, tasting of minerals and iron. There are no ripples except for the steady, rhythmic *plink, plink, plink* of condensation falling from the ceiling stalactites—sharp, dripping icicles of stone that are growing longer by the hour.

It is a sparse place. There is no clutter of debris or soil, just the clean, hard lines of wet stone and the geometric shapes of the shadows cast by the rock formations. The emptiness is balanced, a negative space that feels intentional. The silence is mostly absolute, broken only by the water dripping and a low, barely perceptible hum that vibrates in the teeth—a vibration that suggests the heat and light are not natural occurrences, but the product of an immense, sleeping engine buried in the rock beneath your feet.

Life here is an accident. The parameters are wrong for biology; it is too bright, too wet, too hot, and too empty. Yet, movement catches your eye in one of the deeper shadow pools. A single, translucent creature—more a collection of flowing fluids and simple geometry than an animal—drifts through the scalding water. It has no eyes, for eyes are useless in this glare, and no distinct limbs. It is a rare, simple scavenger, feeding on the mineral-rich slime that coats the underwater rocks. It moves with a slow, deliberate grace, unconcerned by the oppressive heat.

Despite the strangeness of the sunless sun, the atmosphere feels oddly normal. It is a sauna built by tectonic forces, a clean, wet room of stone and light. The water cycle is accelerated here; you can watch it. The water drips from the ceiling, hits the floor, runs into the pools, and the heat from the unseen tech below turns it instantly back into vapor, rising to join the heavy, misty air, only to condense on the cool ceiling and begin the cycle again. It is a dynamic, self-contained system of purification and erosion, spinning in a bright, wet loop deep beneath the earth.
