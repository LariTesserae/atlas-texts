There is no light here. Not a shade, not a spark, not the bioluminescent flicker of decay. This is a darkness so absolute it presses against the skin like a physical weight, a velvet shroud that smells of cold dust and ancient, dry cinnamon.

You are standing in the Void-Hollow.

The first thing that establishes reality is the sound. It is not quiet. It is a vast, resonant cacophony—a deep, rhythmic thrumming that vibrates in the chest cavity, overlaid with a high, keening whistle that rises and falls over glacial periods. It is the sound of a billion hollow reeds singing in a draft you cannot feel. It is loud, a roar of silence that fills the ears until blood pulses in time with the room.

Stretching your hands out, you do not touch stone. You touch wood.

Great pillars of it rise from the floor and vanish into the darkness above. They are not round, but faceted, planed flat by forces unknown, growing in perfect, geometric hexagons. This is the city-forest. The vegetation here does not merely grow; it builds. The "buildings" are trees, or perhaps the trees are buildings. They are composed of a dense, fibrous material that resembles mahogany or petrified oak, stripped of all sap and moisture. They are as hard as iron and dry as baked clay.

You walk. The ground is not earth; it is a compacted pavement of shed bark and fossilized spores, eroded over eons into a perfectly flat, hardpan plane. It crunches softly underfoot, the only sharp, percussive sound in the wash of the ambient drone. The air is cool, perhaps five degrees Celsius, and aggressively arid. It sucks the moisture from your lips with every breath. This is a desert of wood.

The scale is deceptive. You feel you are in a vast, open cathedral, but the spacing of the pillars tells a different story. You walk for what feels like minutes, guided only by the tactile pressure of the air currents, before you brush against the next massive trunk. The density of growth is incredibly low; these "trees" are spaced kilometers apart, solitary monoliths guarding a hollow emptiness. It is a landscape of isolation, a contained pocket universe where the distance between entities is measured in the time it takes to shout into the dark.

This is an urban environment without citizens. The architecture is crude, primitive in its joinery—vines that were used like ropes have now calcified into rigid steel-strength cables, lashing the massive spires together in a web of suspension bridges that no foot has ever crossed. There are no nails, no smelted metal, only the cunning use of thorn, root, and tension. It is technology at the dawn of tool-use, yet scaled to the size of gods.

The ecology is a closed loop of absolute minimalism. There is no water cycle. The plants do not drink; they have adapted to metabolize atmospheric trace gases or perhaps feed on the deep vibrations that shake the room. They are xerophytic titans, preserving every ounce of moisture within their dense, woody hearts. They have settled into their final form. There is no rustle of leaves, no flutter of wings—there are no animals here. The fauna parameter has dropped to zero; nothing crawls, nothing scuttles. The only movement is the slow, tectonic shifting of the wood itself as the temperature gradients shift over centuries.

The "weirdness" lies in the geometry. As you trace the lines of the structures, you realize they are not random. They are arranged in a complex, auditory array. The entire cavern is an instrument. The whistling sound is created by the wind of the world’s respiration passing through specially grown apertures in the trunk-towers. The wood acts as a resonator, amplifying the frequency. It is a temple built to sustain a single, endless chord.

The erosion is ancient. The edges of the walkways and the sharp corners of the trunks have been worn smooth, rounded down by the friction of dust particles over millions of years. This place has reached its end-state. It is no longer growing; it is maintaining. It is a fossil of a living city, preserved in the cool, dry dark, humming its own name into the void, waiting for a listener that does not exist.

You are a ghost in a machine of wood and silence, drowning in the loud, dry dark of the hollow earth.
