This is not a cave, or at least, not one formed by the slow, patient digestion of stone. It is a hollow, a negative space swallowed by the earth but not yet digested—a pocket universe of raw geology about the size of a grand cathedral's nave, though with none of the architecture.

**Light and Geometry**

The first sensation is the violence of the brightness. Here, beneath the skin of the world, the light is absolute. It does not come from the sun or a flame; it seems to emanate from the molecular vibration of the air itself. It is a sterile, actinic white, so intense it casts no shadows, flattening the world into a high-contrast photograph. There is nowhere for the eye to hide.

The space is defined by sharp, jagged walls. Erosion is non-existent here; the "emerging" quality of the rock means the edges are raw, as if the stone cleaved five minutes ago. It is a landscape of glassy obsidian and porous pumice, frozen in the moment of its violent birth. The geometry is unstable—corners that look convex from one angle appear concave from the next, a visual glitch that suggests the laws of optics are merely suggestions in this room.

**Atmosphere and Water**

The temperature is aggressively cool—a clammy, subterranean chill that settles into the marrow. It smells of ozone and wet iron. The dampness (Water 1.0) is not a pooling of liquid, but a constant, heavy weeping. The walls sweat. Thick, viscous droplets of condensation coalesce on the raw stone faces and hang there for impossible durations, defying gravity, suspended like glass beads before finally dropping with a heavy, rhythmic *plink* that is the only punctuation to the silence.

The air is breathable but thin, tasting metallic. It is a quiet atmosphere (Sound 0.5), a vacuum of noise where the silence feels pressurized, like the calm before a scream.

They are brittle, hollow, and filled with a clear, antiseptic fluid.

**The Glitch**

The defining feature of this place is the unreliability of its existence. The "weirdness" (Weirdness 2.9) is palpable. If you stare at the far wall, the texture of the stone seems to vibrate, shifting from granite to flesh to circuitry and back again.

Occasionally, the "processes" of the room stutter. A falling drop of water will freeze in mid-air for ten seconds, hanging like a lens. A skittering mite will suddenly blink out of existence and reappear three feet to the left, continuing its path without pause. The space feels embryonic, a test run for a reality that hasn't fully rendered. It is a room that is constantly deciding what it is, caught in the frantic, silent act of becoming.
