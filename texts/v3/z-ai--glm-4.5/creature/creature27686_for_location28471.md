To exist in the Ossified Deeps is to embrace a paradox: one must be hard enough to survive the crushing abrasion of the dust, yet fluid enough to adapt to the slow, tectonic drift of the living towers. The inhabitants of this vertical desert are a testament to this duality. They are not merely visitors; they are a biological and mechanical graft, a species that has evolved alongside the industrial exploitation of their home.

They are the **Lith-Singers**, though they call themselves simply *The Tethered*.

Physically, they appear terrifyingly fragile at first glance, standing upright on two spindly, digitigrade legs that are multiples of a human’s height. Their bodies are encased not in soft skin, but in a segmented, lattice-like carapace of grey chitin that resembles the bark of the towers they inhabit. This armor is not grown; it is scavenged and grafted. As a Lith-Singer matures, they shed their natural exoskeletons and immediately bond plates of the flaked, ceramic bark from the towers directly to their own bodies using a secretion of amber sap. Thus, every adult is a mosaic of the living city, their outer shells a patchwork history of the specific tower they were born upon.

Their heads are sleek and tapering, devoid of noses—moisture is a precious commodity here, and an open nasal passage would desiccate the lungs in seconds. They breathe through a series of spiracles located along the neck, protected by dense filaments of hair-like cilia that filter the mineral dust. Their eyes are massive, black orbs, shielded by a permanent, secondary transparent eyelid that creates a porthole effect, allowing them to stare directly into the pearlescent brightness of the ceiling-light without blindness.

The Tethered do not speak with mouths. Their vocal cords have atrophied; instead, they communicate through the infrastructure itself. They possess a specialized, drumming organ in their chests—a resonant plate of cartilage that strikes their armored ribs. By pressing their bodies against the metal piping or the dense, lignified wood of the towers, they transmit complex, rhythmic thrums that travel through the buildings. To a human observer, they are silent,save for the faint clicks of their joints. But to another Lith-Singer pressing an ear against a girder, the air is filled with the roar of data: shift schedules, structural stress warnings, and mating calls, all vibrating through the metal ribs of the world.

Their society is dictated entirely by the verticality of their environment. There is no "north" or "south" in the Deeps, only "Up" and "Deep."

The **High-Born** live near the canopy, where the crystalline spines of the plants catch the light. They are the astronomers and the light-harvesters, tasked with monitoring the bioluminescent moss on the ceiling and adjusting the mirrors that focus light down to the lower levels. Their armor is polished to a sheen, and they are responsible for tending the gliders—the leathery, sandpaper-kites that roost on the high scaffolds. These creatures are not pets; they are atmospheric filters. The High-Born scrape the mineral dust that accumulates on the gliders' wings, processing it to extract trace metals essential for the city's repair.

Below them, on the industrial levels, live the **Sap-Wrights**. These are the engineers and the surgeons of the towers. Their hands are multi-fingered, tipped with tools that have evolved into bone-shears and laser-lances. They spend their lives maintaining the hydraulic pulse of the city, ensuring the amber sap flows. They are constantly moving, rappelling down the flanks of the towers on cables, welding cracks in the bark as the plants expand. They are the ones who intimately understand the "slow violence" of their home; they have seen colleagues crushed when a growth-spurt widened a corridor overnight, or sheared in half when a root-burst buckled a catwalk. Consequently, the Sap-Wrights are fatalistic and deeply religious, worshipping the *Groan*—the subsonic thrum of the plant—as the voice of a hungry god.

Deep in the roots, where the light fades and the heat rises, dwell the **Root-Breakers**. They are stockier, broader, their armor thicker and pitted from the scree. They work alongside the **Mantis-Harvesters**. These massive, insectoid beasts are not tame, but they are symbiotic. The Root-Breakers use rhythmic chest-thrums to guide the Mantis-Harvesters toward sections of the root-mass that need pruning, or toward dangerous parasites that bore into the wood. In return, the Mantis-Harvesters are allowed to feed on the richest, nutrient-dense layers of the inner bark that the Root-Breakers expose. It is a brutal, dangerous partnership, forged in the heat and the dark, where the air is thick with the smell of ozone and pulverized stone.

The children of the Tethered, known as **Grubs**, do not play. They are kept in pressurized crèches deep within the hollowed-out nodes of the towers. Here, the humidity is kept artificially high by condensation units—a luxury unknown to the adults. Their larval stage is soft and pale, and they spend this time learning the *Language of the Vibration*. They press their hands against the walls, learning to feel the heartbeat of the tower. A child who cannot feel the stress-fracture in a girder before it snaps is not considered fit for the surface.

 nutrition in the Deeps is a cycle of hydrocarbon efficiency. The Tethered do not eat food; they drink. Their diet consists entirely of the refined, amber sap. It is thick, sludge-like, and incredibly caloric. They consume it at public dispensaries—hollowed-out nodules in the bark where the sap is tapped and mixed with mineral supplements. The act of drinking is communal and silent; lines of Tethered stand hooked up to IV-like tubes, their throats pumping rhythmically, their eyes half-closed in a nutrient-induced trance.

At night—if the rhythmic pulsing of the ceiling moss can be called night—the Tethered retreat into the hollowed-out chambers of the towers. They do not sleep in beds, but in "hammocks" of woven steel cable that hang suspended in the vertical shafts. This is a survival mechanism; if a tower shifts or a root bursts, the sleeper is suspended in the air rather than crushed against a wall. They strap themselves in, hooking their exoskeletons to the mesh, and drift into a state of hibernation, their breathing slowing to match the hydraulic pulse of the tower.

They are a people of constant maintenance. Their lives are a war of attrition against entropy. Every bolt they tighten, every crystal they polish, every drop of sap they drink is a testament to their refusal to let the Ossified Deeps reclaim them. They are the antibodies of a stone giant, forever scurrying over the skin of a world that would forget them if it could, holding on to the living rock with hands made of bone and iron, singing their silent songs into the dust.
