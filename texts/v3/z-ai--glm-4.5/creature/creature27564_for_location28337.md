To survive in the Sump-Garden is to accept the status of a parasite, or at best, a symbiotic passenger. The "Total Mass" of vegetation is the dominant organism here—a single, sprawling, vegetative leviathan that consumes volume and excretes humidity. The animals that live here do not exist in harmony with nature; they exist in the truce of the occupied territory.

Because space is the premium currency, the fauna here has evolved toward the flat, the patient, and the mimicked. There are no grazers, for there is no grass to graze. There are no predators that chase, for there is no room to run.

**The Floor-Dwellers: The Iron-Lice**

If you were to lift a segment of the pulsing root-mat, exposing the wet, dark interface between the organic felt and the rusted pipes below, you would find the sanitation crew of the Sump.

These are the Iron-Lice. They are not insects, but something closer to woodlice or trilobites, grown to the size of a dinner plate. Their exoskeletons are not biological chitin, but a metallic composite they secrete, bonding the rust they consume directly into their armor. They look like living, crawling nuts and bolts.

They are blind. The world beneath the root-mat is absolute darkness, punctuated only by the chemical taste of oxidizing iron and the vibration of the water coursing through the pipes. They move with a slow, clicking precision, their flattened bodies designed to slide through the microscopic gaps between the knotted roots and the iron they encase. They are the dentists of the garden; they scrape the corrosive buildup from the interior of the visible pipes, smoothing the metal even as the roots seek to crush it. Without them, the pipes would have long since dissolved into sludge. In return, the garden tolerates their presence, allowing them to drink the metallic runoff that sweats from the joints.

**The Vertical Mimes: The Bolt-Head Weaver**

Higher up, where the root-mat fuses with the vertical pillars of woody vine, lives the only truly active resident of the Sump.

From a distance, it looks like a mistake in the architecture—a rivet head that is slightly too large, or a rusted flange that has slipped out of alignment. This is the Bolt-Head Weaver, a spider-like arboreal creature that has abandoned the web for something more tangible.

It does not spin silk; it spins a resinous, bio-concrete that it harvests from the nodules of the giant orchids. It builds small, spherical shelters directly onto the sides of the "tree-trunks," camouflaging them to look exactly like the protrusions of the rusted machinery or the boils of the bark. It sits inside these shelters with its legs folded against its body, waiting for the vibrations of a wandering Iron-Lice or a smaller, soft-bodied grub.

The Weaver is the beneficiary of the Sump’s auditory violence. The constant, deafening *thrum* of the garden masks the sound of its own movement. It can crawl across the wet vine-flesh without making a sound that is distinguishable from the ambient groan of the settling flywheel. Its eyes are small and black, hidden behind a pane of clear resin that keeps the humidity out, looking for all the world like a glass gauge on a steam engine.

**The Canopy Drifters: The Pneuma-Moths**

In the few feet of air space between the ceiling of leaves and the floor of roots, the air is so thick with moisture that it barely qualifies as air. It is a heavy aerosol, a breathable fog. Here, drifting aimlessly, are the Pneuma-Moths.

They do not have wings in the traditional sense. Their wings are vast, thin sacs filled with a gas lighter than air, generated by a bacterial symbiosis in their gut. They cannot flap; they can only drift and steer. They look like translucent plastic bags caught in an updraft, bouncing gently off the wet trunks and the hanging chains of orchids.

They are the mourners of the Sump. They feed on the "sweet, cloying scent of decaying nectar," dipping their long, needle-like proboscises into the pneumatic bulbs of the orchids. Because they move so slowly, they are constantly at risk of being entangled in the hanging aerial roots. To survive, they secrete a lubricating oil from their skin that makes them impossibly slick. If they snag a root, they simply slide free, leaving the root coated in a shimmering, oily residue that the Weavers later harvest to waterproof their concrete nests.

**The Apex: The Stagnant Walker**

And then, there is the keeper of the silence. You saw it from a distance—the solitary entity resembling a sloth. But to call it a sloth is a disservice to the weirdness of its biology. It is the Stagnant Walker.

It is large, perhaps the size of a black bear, but it hangs not by strength, but by hook. Its fur is not fur; it is a dense, filamentous moss that grows *from* its skin, a symbiotic garden that cultivates its own camouflage. It smells exactly of wet copper and crushed ferns. It has no natural scent of its own.

It hangs on the pipe-arches, motionless for days at a time, its metabolism slowed to a crawl that mimics the geologic slowness of the crushing vines. It is not sleeping; it is waiting. It is listening with its entire body. The bones of its inner ear are connected to its claws, feeling the metal vibrations of the pipes and the roots.

It eats the Weavers. It eats the Iron-Lice. Occasionally, it reaches up with a slow, agonizing deliberation to pluck a drifting Pneuma-Moth from the air. But mostly, it simply hangs. It is a creature that has realized that in the Sump-Garden, movement is death. To be still is to become the furniture. To be still is to be safe.

It has been on that pipe for ten years. The moss on its back has grown over the rust, blending perfectly. In another decade, the vines will grow over its legs, and the pipe will rust through beneath it. It will not move. It will simply become part of the cathedral, another stone in the wall of the green, breathing machine.

**The Ghost in the Machine: The Echo-Locators**

And finally, there is the life you do not see. Deep inside the burst pipes, where the roots have infiltrated and turned the iron into a cage, there is a sound. A high-pitched clicking, faster than the beat of the Walker's heart.

These are the Echo-Locators—pale, eyeless newt-like creatures that swim in the sludge at the bottom of the pipes. They navigate the dark, flooded tunnels of the industrial past using sonar. They are the only thing that lives *inside* the machine, protected from the crushing pressure of the Total Mass above. They eat the roots that clog the pipes, keeping the water flowing.

In a way, they are the reason the Sump still breathes. They are the microscopic engineers in the bowels of the beast, uninterested in the world above, ensuring that the metal continues to groan, the water continues to weep, and the slow, suffocating life of the Sump-Garden continues its relentless march forward.
