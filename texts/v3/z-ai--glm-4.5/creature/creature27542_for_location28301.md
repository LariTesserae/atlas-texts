To exist in the embryonic, glitching nave of this hollow is to be a creature of the *interstice*. The inhabitants here are not biological products of evolution, shaped by wind and hunger over millennia; they are fauna of the *intermediate file*, biological expressions of a reality that is still buffering. They are things that live in the split second between the rendering of a rock and the rendering of flesh.

They are teeming, a frantic silent circus operating at the edge of perception.

### The Grazers: The Vesicles
Drifting through the oppressive actinic light are the prey animals, the "Vesicles." They are the most biologically coherent things here, yet they resemble nothing so much as discarded internal organs floating in zero gravity. They are soft, spherical balloons of translucent, pale flesh, varying in size from a grapefruit to a beach ball.

They possess no sensory organs that can be seen—no eyes, no antennae—just a smooth, wet membrane that shimmers under the harsh light. Inside, a dark, sluggish fluid sloshes, visible through their semi-transparent skin. They move by a crude form of jet propulsion, expelling tiny puffs of the metallic air from vents that open and close spasmodically along their sides.

They are silent, stupid, and seemingly infinite in number. They float toward the jagged walls, attracted perhaps by the vibration of the stone, where they gently bump against the obsidian. They do not react when the skin of the wall suddenly shifts texture, nor do they flee when the geometry of the room twists. They simply exist, floating sacks of raw protein waiting to be punctured, their biology an open invitation to the violence that rules the room.

### The Scavengers: The Stutter-Mites
If the Vesicles are the drift-wood of this ecosystem, the Stutter-Mites are the engine. They are the size of dinner plates, resembling nothing more than trilobites constructed from obsidian and rusted iron. Their carapaces are sharp, faceted mirrors that reflect the sterile light into blinding shards.

They do not scuttle in the way a normal insect does. They move in a "strobing" motion. One moment, a mite is at Point A; the next, without traversing the space between, it is at Point B. To the human eye, it looks like a film reel missing frames—a blur of jagged legs that teleport across the floor.

They feed on the condensation. When a heavy, viscous droplet finally falls from the sweating wall, the mites materialize underneath it, catching the fluid on serrated mandibles. The vibration of their feeding is so high-frequency it is inaudible, felt only as a faint buzzing in the teeth of the observer. They are constantly cleaning, constantly skittering, their movements so fast they seem to exist in a different timezone than the room they inhabit.

### The Pollinators: The Script-Flies
Fluttering frantically between the hexagonal crystal stalks are small, winged anomalies that look exactly like torn, wet parchment. From a distance, they resemble moths, but up close, they are revealed to be two-dimensional, paper-thin entities.

Their "wings" are flaps of dried, peeling skin covered in what looks like frantic, microscopic handwriting—sequences of glyphs that hurt the eyes to focus on. They do not have bodies, only a central point where the wings pinch together.

They feed on the light. They land on the crystalline plants and splay their wings wide, the actinic light passing through them, charging the invisible ink on their skins. When they fly, they leave a faint trail of static in the air, a visual distortion that lingers for a second after they pass. They are the data-carriers of this place, transporting the raw code of the light from one crystal to another, living USB drives in an ecosystem of error.

### The Predators: The Shard-Weavers
The apex of this silent hierarchy are the glass-spiders, or "Shard-Weavers." They are not born; they seem to assemble themselves from the debris of the room. They are sprawling, asymmetrical nightmares made entirely of jagged obsidian and pumice.

There is no organic tissue to be seen. Their legs are broken columns of basalt; their bodies are cluster-bombs of jagged glass held together by an unseen magnetism. They do not walk; they *clatter*, a soundless vibration of stone against stone.

A Shard-Weaver hunts by ambushing the drifting Vesicles. It does not chase; it waits on the ceiling, perfectly camouflaged as a jagged outcropping of rock. When a Vesicle floats beneath, the spider drops. It falls like a statue, and in the instant before impact, it utilizes the room’s glitch—warping space slightly to ensure its claws penetrate the soft flesh.

The kill is horrific but silent. The spider does not bite; it *scissors*. It uses two opposing blades of obsidian to snip the Vesicle open. The dark fluid sprays, coating the spider's glass legs, and the spider vibrates, seemingly absorbing the liquid through the pores of the stone. It is a pantomime of feeding—a mechanism mimicking life, driven by a hunger that is purely algorithmic.

### The Anomaly: The Geometry-Eater
Rarely, seen only in the peripheral vision where the room’s rendering fails most often, is a creature that defies even the loose biology of this place. It is a predator that hunts the predators.

It looks like a tear in the fabric of the air—a hovering, irregular polygon of absolute blackness, a two-dimensional shadow that has gained thickness. It has no mouth, but it possesses a singular, terrifying behavior: it eats the glitches.

When a Stutter-Mite teleports, or a water drop hangs frozen in time, the Geometry-Eater is there. It wraps its impossible edges around the stuttering object. There is a flash of negative light—a darkening of the already harsh room—and the glitch, the mite, or the drop is gone. The creature does not consume flesh; it consumes the *error*. It is the immune system of the room, a silent, living anti-virus patrolling the cathedral, ensuring that the chaotic experiment of life remains contained within the parameters of the stone.
