To survive in the High Exhaustion is not merely to endure cold or hunger; it is to be incorporated into the engine. The fauna here do not exist as distinct entities separate from their environment, but rather as mobile components, fuses, and regulators in a planetary machine that is constantly overheating. They have evolved not just biology, but a kind of industrial physiology.

**The Ground Technicians: The Shear-Shell Crabs**
The most ubiquitous inhabitants of the conduit-grass plains are the massive, multi-legged armaments that scuttle through the mist. These are the Shear-Shells, creatures that look less like animals and more like living tanks forged from black chitin and rusted iron.

They are heavy, broad-bodied things the size of compact cars, moving with a surprising, hydraulic smoothness on dozens of segmented, spiked legs. Their primary purpose in this ecosystem is maintenance. They trundle the endless circuit board of the plain, using massive, serrated pincers to "prune" the conduit-grass. They snip the black, cable-like tendrils with a sound like bolt-cutters through copper. This pruning prevents the grass from overgrowing and short-circuiting the sub-scape geological grid.

When they shear a cable, a shower of sparks and blue fluid erupts. The Shear-Shells have evolved to feed on this discharge; their mouths are funnel-shaped receptors that absorb the raw voltage and the sugary, superheated sap inside the grass, converting it into the kinetic energy that drives their heavy bodies. Their shells are pitted and scorched, evidence of a lifetime spent working dangerously close to live current. They are blind—the mist is too thick for eyes to be of use—navigating instead through the detection of magnetic fields and the low-voltage hum of the earth.

**The Thermal Climbers: The Resonance-Bats**
Clinging to the towering, smokestack trees are the Resonance-Bats. They are not true bats, but rather membranous gliders that possess a skeletal structure of translucent, heat-resistant cartilage. They wrap themselves around the translucent patches of the tree-titans’ bark, absorbing the industrial-orange glow of the superheated sap pumping through the veins.

They have evolved a symbiotic relationship with the trees. The trees, in their biological combustion, suffer from "pressure knots"—blockages in the vascular tissue. The Resonance-Bats possess long, needle-like proboscis that they plunge into these knots. Once attached, they emit a high-frequency screech that vibrates at the exact harmonic frequency of the tree’s internal pumping mechanism. This sonic vibration liquifies the blockage, allowing the bat to gorge on the pressurized, nutrient-rich fluid while relieving the tree of its pressure.

They hang in clusters like dark fruit, their wings translucent and glowing with the heat they’ve absorbed. When they take flight, they are clumsy in the heavy air, beating their wings with a wet, leather slap, navigating by the thumping subsonic heartbeats of the titans.

**The Apex Predators: The Glass-Wolves**
Hunting the Shear-Shells and unwary climbers are the Glass-Wolves. These are sleek, hexapodal predators with coats that mimic the wet, grey stone of the plateau. Their name comes from their teeth and claws, which are not made of bone but of biologically deposited silicon glass—sharp, transparent, and nearly unbreakable.

They are solitary hunters, designed for stealth amidst the noise. They have developed a method of "auditory camouflage." The folds of skin along their flanks can inflate and deflate, allowing them to mimic the rhythmic *plink* of dripping condensation or the low-frequency thrum of the conduit-grass. They creep up on their prey while broadcasting a soundscape of innocent background noise, masking their approach until it is too late.

When they attack, they do not bite to kill; they bite to ground. Their glass claws slash through the rubbery insulation of the Shear-Shells or the cartilage of the bats, instantly discharging the electrical energy stored within their prey. The Glass-Wolf then laps up the resulting stew of bio-fluids and electricity, its own internal nervous system acting as a lightning rod to digest the charge.

**The Aerial Grazers: The Static-Whales**
Drifting high above, where the mist thins into the twilight, are the Static-Whales. These are colossal, balloon-like creatures, filled with a lighter-than-air gas harvested from the geothermal vents. They have no wings; they move by manipulating the static electricity that permeates the air.

They are essentially living capacitors. Their skin is a metallic mesh, bristling with fine, hair-like cilia that attract the ambient static charge of the storms. By discharging this energy in controlled bursts from their underside, they propel themselves through the thick air with a slow, majestic drift. They graze on the swarms of Spark-Moths, opening cavernous mouths that act as vacuums, sucking in the hawk-sized insects by the hundreds. Their digestion is incendiary; the moths are burned internally to produce the hydrogen gas that keeps the whale afloat. Occasionally, a whale becomes too charged, and it explodes in a silent, blinding flash of lightning, raining down organic debris that fertilizes the moss below.

**The Entropy Scavengers: The Cubic-Rotters**
Perhaps the strangest inhabitants are those that feed on the erosion itself. When the hills crumble into geometric shapes—cubes, spheres, pyramids—the Cubic-Rotters emerge. They resemble pale, amorphous slugs, but they possess a geometric, crystalline internal structure.

They slither across the wet stone, secreting an acid that dissolves the "unfinished" reality of the geometric rocks. As they consume the perfect shapes, they excrete a black, oily sludge that eventually hardens into new, raw stone. They are the recyclers of the plateau, the immune system of the planet that breaks down the aberrations of physics and returns them to the chaotic, organic mud. They move with a jerky, stop-motion quality, as if they are glitching in and out of existence, blinking between the physical world and the mathematical void that birthed the rocks.

**The Deep-Dwellers: The Vein-Tapeworms**
Beneath the sponge of moss and mud, where the raw, pulsing "veins" of the planet lie exposed, live the Vein-Tapeworms. These are massive, segmented worms, measuring meters in diameter, that burrow through the living rock. They possess a ring of saw-like teeth that rotate like a drill bit, allowing them to penetrate the stone crust.

They tap directly into the planet’s geothermal bloodlines, anchoring themselves to the deep magma flows. Their bodies act as heat exchangers, cooling the planet's rage and releasing it into the air as the steam vents that characterize the weather. They are sessile once anchored, becoming living pillars of the landscape. Their backs often become overgrown with moss or even small trees, blending seamlessly into the terrain until they decide to move, triggering localized earthquakes as they dislodge their massive forms.

In the High Exhaustion, everything eats, everything processes, and everything hums. It is a terrifying, loud, and beautiful cacophony of life functioning as a single, sprawling, sweating factory.
