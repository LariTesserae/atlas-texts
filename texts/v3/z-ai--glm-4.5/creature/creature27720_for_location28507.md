They are a people carved from the same bronze-green silence as the world they inhabit. To call them "human" is a convenience of classification, for they have been shaped by the High Strata as thoroughly as the stone has been shaped by the wind. They are small—rarely exceeding five feet in height—a stunted evolution necessitated by the scarcity of resources and the capricious violence of the winds. Their limbs are long and ropy, the muscles dense and ropy like cured root, possessing a tensile strength that belies their frail appearance.

Their skin is the color of dried pitch and old bark, weathered into a toughness that repels the biting, microscopic insects that drift on the thermals. It is a landscape of scars, every map of survival etched into their backs and arms. You will not see softness here. Their flesh is tight against the bone, minimizing drag, a biological optimization for a life spent battling air currents.

**The Senses of the Void**

Living in the deafening roar of the aerial ecology has stolen their voices. They possess vocal cords, but using them for conversation is a waste of breath in a world where sound is a warning flare, not a casual greeting. Instead, they have evolved a complex, tactile language. Their hands are incredibly dexterous, the fingertips swollen with nerve endings. They "speak" through pressure and vibration—a hand on a companion’s shoulder can convey grief, a warning, or the location of a water-glider with the same efficiency a lowlander uses words.

Their ears, unlike those of lowlanders, are small and recessed, protected by heavy growths of cartilage and coarse hair. They can close these auditory canals at will, sealing themselves off from the constant thrum of the sky to sleep, or to listen for the specific, low-frequency vibration of the Titan’s breathing beneath their feet.

**Children of the Thermal**

They are born into the saddle. From the moment a child can walk, they are strapped into a harness of woven fiber. Their lives are defined by verticality. They do not "walk" in the horizontal sense; they traverse the bronzed hills of the Titan’s back with a loping, climbing gait, always gripping the textured bark, always aware that a single misstep leads to a three-mile descent.

Their most profound adaptation, however, is their relationship with the wind. They possess an innate, almost supernatural sense of air pressure. They can read the thermals rising from the heated "ground" the way a sailor reads the sea. They know when a downdraft is forming seconds before it strikes, crouching low, pressing their bodies against the warmth of the living wood to anchor themselves.

**The Sky-Hunters**

To eat, they must hunt the sky. They are not merely gatherers of sap or scavengers of fallen leaves; they are aerial predators in their own right.

Their primary tool is the *Wind-Kite*, a glider constructed not from metal, but from the tanned, leathery membranes of the giant rays that inhabit the upper atmosphere. The frame is formed from the lightweight, hollow bones of the great dart-birds, lashed together with vines capable of supporting tons of tension.

A hunter does not jump; they *launch*. They stand on the edge of a bark-blister, facing the wind, waiting for the thermal to catch their wings. Once airborne, they become extensions of the storm. They carry harpoons carved from dense bone, tipped with neurotoxins derived from the resin of the Titan’s plate-leaves. They swoop upon the schools of needle-beaked fish-birds or grapple with the smaller glider-leathers, striking and returning to the safety of the "ground" before the larger raptors can intervene.

**Architecture of the Parasite**

Their homes, the "Blisters," are grotesque and beautiful. They do not build *on* the Titan; they carve *into* it. Using primitive but effective chisels of sharpened obsidian and heavy stone, they hollow out the raised calluses and boles that form naturally on the tree's ancient back.

The entrance to a Blister is always a narrow tunnel, facing away from the prevailing wind, often hidden behind a curtain of hanging moss. Inside, the air is thick, humid, and redolent with the smell of burning sap—their only source of light and heat. The walls are polished to a mirror sheen, smoothed by generations of backs resting against them. There are no chairs, only hammocks woven from strong, elastic vines that cradle the occupants in a fetal position, simulating the embrace of the wind.

Because the Titan "breathes"—the ground heaves slowly in a geological rhythm—their architecture is flexible. Nothing is rigid; every bridge, every shelter, every tether has slack. A rigid structure would be shattered by the slow expansion and contraction of the living mountain.

**The Psychology of the Fall**

Above all, they are a people defined by the fear and love of the Void. Their religion is simple: the sky is both mother and murderer. They do not fear the predators of the air; they respect them as equals. But they fear the *Fall*.

When one of their number dies, they are not buried. To put flesh into the Titan is an insult to the tree that already carries them. Instead, they conduct the "Last Launch." The body is dressed in their finest glider, weighed down with heavy stones, and cast off into the abyss. It is a grim necessity; a body left on the surface attracts scavengers, and space is too precious for graves. As the body falls, disappearing into the misty canopy miles below, the mourners watch in silence, their hands moving rapidly in the sign of the long goodbye.

They are lonely, infinitesimal specks clinging to the back of a god. They shout without speaking, love without touching, and fly without wings. In the vast, warm cathedral of the High Strata, they are the only things that are trying to stay still.
