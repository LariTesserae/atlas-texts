In the crushing violet twilight of the High Drown, where the heavy fluid breathes like a living lung and the temperature holds the world in a stasis just shy of freezing, the populace is not what you expect. There are no eyes here to watch the dark, no teeth to tear, no muscle to pulse in fear. The zero-fauna constraint means the silence of the animal kingdom is absolute, but in its place rises a civilization of green.

**The Citizens: The Kelp-Firs**
The dominant—and perhaps only—sentience belongs to the **Kelp-Firs**. They are the custodians of the drowned peaks, a forest that behaves with the unsettling dynamism of a frightened flock. They are not the passive, drifting algae of the old oceans; they are trees that have learned to swim.

Standing rooted firmly in the silt and stone of the mountain slopes, a Kelp-Fir possesses a central "trunk" of fibrous, cartilage-like wood that is rigid enough to withstand the pressure, yet flexible enough to bend into a perfect bow under the stress of the currents. Rising from these trunks are the long, trailing fronds that give them their name—needles of vibrant, chemiluminescent green that burn with a ghostly light in the 0.5 darkness.

Because there are no fish to dart or crabs to scuttle, the Kelp-Firs have evolved to fill the vacuum of motion. They possess an internal hydraulic system, a circulatory network of dense fluids that they pump rhythmically through their fronds. This allows them to move with a terrifying autonomy. They do not merely wait for the water to push them; they flex, they reach, and they curl. In the dim light, the forest looks less like a grove of plants and more like a gathering of deep-sea anemones mimicking the shape of evergreens, swaying in a slow, hypnotic dance that never ceases.

**The Society of the Sway**
The lives of the Kelp-Firs are defined by a frantic, silent competition for the one resource they cannot synthesize: the currents. In this open, sparse world, to be in the still water is to choke. The current brings the "marine snow"—the calcified dust and organic detritus that rains down from the churning violence above.

To catch this snow, the Kelp-Firs stretch their glowing fronds upward, vibrating their internal hydraulic pressure to shake loose the settling silt from their neighbors. It is a war of motion. A tree in the center of the cluster will thrash and sway, creating a turbulence that steals the nutrition right out of the "mouth" of a smaller tree downstream. They crowd one another aggressively, their glowing root systems tangling and wrestling beneath the silt, choking out the weak to ensure the strong have the space to dance.

**The Choir of Green**
If you were to drift among them, you would realize they are not a quiet people. The High Drown is loud, and the Kelp-Firs are the orchestra. As the tidal breath squeezes the world, forcing the heavy fluid through the channels of stone, millions of Kelp-Fir fronds are rubbed against one another.

Because their flesh is tough and fibrous to resist the cold, this friction produces a sound—a dry, rasping keen. It is the "singing" of the forest, a cacophony that sounds like the bowing of a million cellos. This sound is their communication. It is a vibration that travels through the dense fluid and the very rock they cling to, signaling the shifts in pressure, the arrival of a fresh storm of silt, or the slow death of a neighbor. It is a constant, low-frequency roar that vibrates in the chest of anyone—or anything—listening.

**The Cycle of Spores**
Their reproduction is a moment of blinding beauty in the gloom. When the pressure drops and the world exhales, the mature Kelp-Firs release their spawn. They do not release seeds gently; they explode.

The spores are microscopic, bioluminescent capsules, and they are released in such clouds that they turn the surrounding water into a glowing green fog. This is the "fluttering of green" that defines the ecology. These spores drift violently through the currents, carried miles across the muddy floor. They are the scouts of the species, seeking new purchase on the naked stone of the drowned peaks.

When a spore lands, it does not wait. It roots instantly, digging a chemical hold into the stone with an acidic urgency. Within hours, a sapling is pushing upward, a single glowing needle straining toward the violet gloom, ready to join the dance.

**The Ghosts of the Cairns**
The Kelp-Firs are the inheritors of a world they do not understand. They live among the Cairns of the Lost, those primitive stone stacks left by hands that dissolved eons ago. To the Kelp-Firs, these cairns are just geography—high ground in the battle for current.

You will see Kelp-Firs growing directly out of the centers of the stone circles, their roots cracking the careful arrangements, their glowing fronds obscuring the lines that once pointed toward the surface. The trees drape over the rough-hewn rocks, softening the sharp edges with lush, heavy curtains of green, claiming the intelligence of the past as merely a foundation for their own sprawling, silent empire.

In the High Drown, life is not a chase; it is a siege. The Kelp-Firs are the besiegers, slowly, relentlessly burying the grey stone and the forgotten memories under a vibrant, cold, and suffocating tide of green.
