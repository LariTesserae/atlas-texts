To exist in the High Rind is not merely to survive; it is to be employed by the architecture. There are no passive observers here, only components that have learned to walk. The fauna of this place are not distinct from the industrial ecosystem; they are its mobile agents, its immune system, its repair crews, evolved over eons to interface seamlessly with the living machinery of the Pillars.

**The Pavement-Lords**

On the lignite plains, moving with the agonizing slowness of geological shifts, are the Grazers. To the uninitiated eye, they appear to be loose boulders or geometric glitches in the pavement—low, jagged mounds of compressed wood and stone. But watch a plaza for an hour, and the mound will have inched forward.

These are the Pavement-Lords, massive, tortoise-like beasts whose shells are composed of the same fossilized lignite as the ground. They are solitary and immense, their bodies shaped like flattened, irregular polygons. This is not accidental; their shape allows them to fit perfectly against the roots and the raised vascular "streets," sealing themselves against the wood to listen.

They do not eat the living wood. Instead, they possess mouths that are grinding, rotary mills located on their undersides. They scrape the polished floor, consuming the accumulated moss, the conductive pollen, and the fine, metallic dust that settles in the runnels. In doing so, they prevent the vascular channels from clogging. Their waste is a hyper-concentrated, oily slurry that seeps into the ground, feeding the ancient roots below. They are the filtration system of the floor, silent, heavy, and indispensable. Their calls are low-frequency seismic rumbles that travel through the soles of their feet, communicating over miles through the lignite pavement.

**The Valve-Keepers**

High up on the oily bark of the Pillars, invisible against the shimmering metallic background, live the Valve-Keepers. These are spidery, arthropodal creatures, but built on a massive scale—the size of vans or small trucks. Their exoskeletons mimic the bark perfectly, segmented plates of chitin that have the same slick, oily sheen, overlapping like biological armor.

They possess limbs that end not in claws, but in specialized, multi-jointed wrenches and faucets. They are the plumbers of the vertical city. They spend their lives traversing the sheer vertical faces of the Pillars, scuttling over the vents and pressure seals. When a vent hisses, releasing too much steam, a Valve-Keeper is often already there, clamping its limbs over the aperture to tighten the biological sphincter, or scraping away mineral deposits with a rasping tongue.

They live in colonies inside the hollows of the bark, hanging in suspended cocoons woven from vascular filaments. During the "shift change" signaled by the deep horns, thousands of them emerge, swarming the Pillars like ants on a log, performing the necessary maintenance to keep the sap pressure stable. They are blind—the light here is too dim for eyes—and instead "see" through vibration and pressure sensitivity, feeling the heartbeat of the Pillar through their feet. To touch one is to touch a live wire; they conduct the bio-electricity of the trees, using it to power their own nervous systems.

**The Canopy-Drifters**

The "large and sinuous shapes" glimpsed in the twilight above are the Drifters. They are the giants of the air, the equivalents of whales in this ocean of mist. They have no wings; flight would be impossible in the thin, variable air currents. Instead, they are buoyant.

The Drifters are essentially immense, living bladders, filled with a hydrogen-methane gas produced by specialized bacteria in their gut. Their bodies are long, ribbon-like, and translucent, stretching hundreds of feet. Through their skin, the internal glow of their alchemical digestion is visible, a soft, pulse-like light that rhythmically brightens and dims.

They float nebulously through the high canopy, grazing on the thick clouds of pollen and spore-dust that hang in the air. Their mouths are wide, ventral slits that act as ram-scoops, filtering the air through massive, comb-like gills. In return, they act as the pollinators for the high city. As they brush against the interlocked branches of the canopy, their sticky, electrostatically charged skins gather pollen from one Pillar and deposit it on the next.

They are silent because they have no vocal cords and no muscles to flap against the air. They move by contracting their long bodies in peristaltic waves, a slow, graceful undulation that propels them through the mist like ribbons in water. When they die, they do not fall; they lose buoyancy and slowly rise, drifting into the upper atmosphere until they burst, raining a nutrient-rich slurry back down upon the roof of the world.

**The Fruit-Symbiotes**

Inside the translucent, cathedral-sized "processing centers"—the bulbous fruit that hang from the branches—live the Symbiotes. These are the chemists of the Rind. They are gelatinous, amoeboid entities, sprawling masses of protoplasm that fill the bottom third of the fruit’s interior.

The fruit captures rainwater and sunlight, brewing an alchemical soup of sugars and proteins. The Symbiotes live within this soup, and their metabolic process introduces the necessary catalysts to transform the soup into the complex, nutrient-dense sap that feeds the Pillar. In return, the fruit provides them with shelter and a constant food source.

They are intelligent, displaying a form of collective consciousness. They manipulate the internal valves of the fruit with pseudopods, adjusting the mix of the brew, correcting pH balances, and regulating the temperature by absorbing or releasing heat. They are the "brain" of the fruit, and their thoughts are chemical. If a fruit is cut open, the Symbiote does not bleed; it hisses, releasing a cloud of volatile neurotoxins that smell of almonds and rust, a defense mechanism evolved over millions of years.

**The Geometric Scavengers**

Finally, in the distance, among the geometric "ruins" of dead plants, live the Scavengers. These are crab-like scavengers with perfectly square bodies and pincers that snap with a hydraulic precision. They are detritivores, feeding on the calcified remains of the Pillars that have reached the end of their cycle.

They are obsessed with order. As they consume the dead wood, they shape it. They pack the lignite dust into perfect spheres and cubes, using their bodies as templates. They build nests that look like scale models of the city itself—miniature grids of perfect blocks arranged in neat rows. No one knows why they do this; it may be a vestigial mating ritual, or perhaps they are attempting to recreate the perfection of the living Pillars in miniature. They are the architects of decay, ensuring that even in death, the geometry of the High Rind is maintained.
