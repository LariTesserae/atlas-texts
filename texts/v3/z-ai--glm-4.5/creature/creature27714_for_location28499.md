At first, there is nothing but the turbulence of steam and the strobing violence of blue light. The senses are overwhelmed by the sheer scale of the machine, leaving no bandwidth for the small. But if one watches the shadow cast by the safety railing—a shadow that snaps and heaves with the thrashing of the pistons—a discrepancy reveals itself. A secondary shadow moves within the first, fluid and counter-rhythmic.

It is a Grate-Walker.

It is a solitary, spindly thing that seems less like a beast and more like a spilled liquid that has decided to walk. It is perhaps four feet tall when standing, though it rarely stands upright; it prefers a crouch, a compressed coil of muscle and pale cartilage that hugs the slick, knife-edged floor.

Its skin is the precise color of the raw metal around it—a pale, bruised silver-blue, lacking the warmth of blood. It is slick with the perpetual condensation, a hydrodynamic sheen that allows it to slide across the greasy iron without friction. It lacks fur or clothing, presenting a streamlined form that sheds the "weeping" water instantly. Its limbs are disproportionately long, with joints that seem to have too many degrees of freedom, allowing it to fold itself into the narrowest maintenance gaps. The hands and feet are wide and spatulate, ringed with rows of microscopic, hooked hairs that act like biological gaskets, sealing the creature against the vibrating floor so it is not shaken off into the abyss.

The Walker has no ears. The sides of its head are smooth, scarred over with thick, bone-like plates that vibrate in sympathy with the gears. It does not hear the scream of the steam; to hear such a thing would be to die. Instead, it feels the pressure waves against its skin, reading the machine’s language through the tactility of the air. It knows when the intake fans are about to spin up because the hair on its arms stands on end a second before the wind hits. Its eyes are black pools, heavily shielded by thick, transparent membranes that slide shut the instant the welding flashes arc outside, protecting its vision from the blinding strobes while still allowing it to see in the amber gloom.

It is an opportunist in a sterile land. Its primary interaction is with the "biological accident" in the corners. The Walker tends to the neon-green synth-moss with a reverence bordering on the obsessive. Using fingers that are delicate despite their suction-grip, it combs through the patches, clearing away metallic shavings that might choke the growth. It does not eat the moss directly; instead, it waits for the moss to filter the toxic air and excrete tiny droplets of hyper-oxygenated fluid, which the Walker laps up with a tongue that is rough and textured like a file. It supplements this with the distilled water that bleeds from the walls, cupping its hands under the rivulets and drinking greedily, chasing the metallic taste with a desperate thirst.

In the "confessional" nature of the room, the Walker performs a ritual of synchronization. When the bass-heavy *thrum* of the pistons peaks, the creature flattens itself against the laser-welded joints of the wall, pressing its chest and forehead against the cold iron. It aligns its own rapid heartbeat with the frantic, chaotic pulse of the industrial complex. It vibrates in place, becoming a blur of motion that mimics the visual chaos of the flywheels, effectively rendering itself invisible to any scanning sensors that might be hunting for biological stowaways. It is trying to become part of the noise, a ghost in the machine's throat.

It is a lonely sentinel. There are no others of its kind; the vibration and the drop ensure that companionship is a fatal risk. It lives in a state of hyper-alertness, constantly checking the drain funnels, ensuring the water flows free, ensuring the moss pulses. It is the unintended immune system of this small pocket of air, a biological response to a mechanical wound.

When the steam vents blast their white clouds, the Walker vanishes, merging completely with the fog and the grey metal. It waits in the blind spot, holding its breath, counting the seconds between the grinding of the gears, waiting for the moment of stillness that never truly comes.
