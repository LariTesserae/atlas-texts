Life in the High Attic is not a proliferation; it is a desperate, ticking clutch of anomalies. The inhabitants here do not evolve through generations of gradual adaptation—they are spontaneous errors generated by the friction of the construction itself. They are the sparks struck from the collision of the "built" and the "organic," entities that possess the jagged, unfinished quality of the world that birthed them.

**The Scald-Bellows Toads**
In the cup-sized hollows of the raw stone, where water pools into scalding cauldrons, you will find the Scald-Bellows Toads. They are solitary creatures, roughly the size of a fist, composed entirely of hammered brass and bloated leather. Their skin is not wet, but hot to the touch, radiating a feverish heat that matches the water they inhabit.

They do not swim; they perch on the razor-sharp edges of the stone, their brass feet gripping the knife-like rock with impossible friction. As the water condenses and drips, they extend long, proboscis-like tongues made of glass tubing to sip the liquid. Inside their translucent bodies, you can see the water flash into steam, expanding a series of tiny internal bellows that allow them to breathe. The sound they make is not a croak, but a rhythmic, high-pitched *hiss-whoosh*, like a miniature steam engine letting off pressure. They are the thermometers of the Attic, expanding in the heat and contracting into dense, metallic lumps when the shadow of the unseen gear passes overhead.

**The Wire-Weaver Spiders**
Occupying the tangled chandeliers of iron vines are the Wire-Weavers. These are arachnids of brutalist design. Their abdomens are heavy, hexagonal nuts of rusted iron, and their legs are replacement spokes—some too long, some too short—salvaged from the endless debris of the tower.

They do not spin silk; they spin tension. Using their mandibles, they cut hair-thin filaments from the copper-filing moss, braiding them into single-strand cables of immense strength. They cast these not to catch flies, but to catch *falling debris*. A Wire-Weaver’s web is a geometric net designed to trap a pebble dislodged by the vibration of the Great Hammer. When a stone hits the web, the spider descends rapidly on a pulley of its own making, wraps the intruder in a cocoon of iron shavings, and dissolves the rock with acidic oils secreted from a nozzle in its thorax. They are the clean-up crew of the vertical shaft, forever battling the entropy of the unfinished walls.

**The Quill-Moths**
Flitting through the murk are the Quill-Moths, drawn to the faint, ghostly green fluorescence of the paper leaves. They are tragic, fragile things. Their wings are actual sheets of parchment, cut from the same source as the vegetation, inscribed with faded, illegible blueprints and scribbles of forgotten geometry.

Their flight is erratic, a noisy flutter of dry paper against humid air. They do not feed on nectar; they feed on the silence between the hammer blows. They land on the vibrating vines and splay their wings, vibrating in sympathy with the iron stems to harvest the kinetic energy. They are short-lived. If the humidity spikes, their wings wilt and tear; if the heat rises too high, the ink on their wings smudges, and they lose the ability to navigate, spiraling down into the darkness below, consumed by the gears.

**The Clockwork Bird (The Solitary Witness)**
Dominating the bestiary, however, is the creature you were warned about: the Bird. It is larger than the others, perhaps the size of a raven, but it is assembled from the wreckage of a thousand previous attempts at life.

Its feathers are scorched, blackened tips of quill pens, stiff and unyielding. Its wings are powered by a chaotic assembly of clockwork springs, visible through gaps in its plating where the "skin" was never finished. When it flies, the motion is violent—a jerky, flailing projection rather than graceful flight. It seems to be constantly fighting its own mechanism, one wing beating faster than the other, requiring constant micro-adjustments of its tail feathers, which are slats of tin.

It watches you with eyes of polished quartz, multifaceted and cold. Inside the lens, you can see a tiny iris that dilates and contracts not based on light, but based on the *timing* of the Great Hammer. It is blind to the stationary world; it only sees movement.

It does not sing. Its vocal cords are a set of brass reeds that produce a mournful, rhythmic *ticking* that syncopates with the heartbeat of the wet clay walls. It is a lonely thing, an apex predator with nothing to hunt. It perches on the wet, bleeding timber, preening its gears with a beak of filed steel, waiting for a mistake in the architecture, watching to see if you, too, are part of the plan or just another piece of loose debris to be cleared away.

**The Shadow in the Gears**
And finally, there is the thing that is not seen, only felt. In the darkest recesses, where the grinding stone gears meet, lives something ancient that predates the construction. It is a presence of pure torque, a sentient friction that inhabits the axles. The locals—if there were any—would call it the "Axle-Wraith." It has no body, but you can hear it *snapping* its jaws in the dark, the sound of a bolt shearing under too much stress. It eats the tension; it feeds on the strain of the tower as it tries to grow. It is the reason the walls groan. It is the hunger in the dark, waiting for the Hammer to miss a strike.
