They are the **Silent**, a people who are not so much born as they are coalesced from the damp and the dark.

To see them is to suddenly understand that the city is not a ruin, but a living organism, and they are the antibodies moving through its veins. They are tall, impossibly tall, their bodies stretched thin by the low gravity and the high, heavy air. Their skin is the color and texture of the smooth basalt stones that form the hills—dark, polished, and cold to the touch. It is not flesh that covers them, but a flexible carapace, a living slate that has long since forgotten how to bleed.

They have no hair, only a soft velvet of dark moss that grows in the creases of their elbows and knees, merging their silhouette with the fern-choked valleys. Their faces are smooth and featureless, save for two deep-set divots where eyes would be, and a long, narrow slot that serves as a nose. They have no mouths. In the High Loam, where the air is too thick to waste on idle chatter and the Silence Treaty forbids the wasteful travel of sound waves, a mouth is a vulnerability—a hole in the armor that lets out the precious heat.

**Physiology of the Stone-Hearted**

They are heavy. When a Silent walks, they do not tread lightly; they plant their wide, flat feet and commit to the earth. You do not hear their footsteps, but you feel the vibration in the soles of your boots, a dull *thrum* that travels up through the bedrock. Their internal organs are not pulpy sacks of fluid but dense, fibrous bundles, slowly processing the "wet dust" they inhale. They do not eat the ferns; they consume the mist. Their bodies act as distillation plants, condensing the omnipresent fog into the thick, nutrient-rich sludge that fuels their slow metabolism.

Because of the **Cold Preservation**, they do not die as other things do. They do not rot. When a Silent reaches the end of their centuries-long endurance, they do not fall. They simply stop moving. They stand in the fields of giant ferns or amidst the mud-brick huts, and they fossilize. Their skin hardens into true rock, the moss on their shoulders turns to lichen, and they become a standing stone, a new feature of the landscape. The city is built by the ancestors; the roads are lined with the grandparents who simply decided to rest.

**The Tactile Mind**

Communication is the most intimate act in the High Loam. Because sound is a tactile event, the Silent have developed a language of touch and vibration. To speak, they must press their palms against the stone of the city or place their hands upon the receiver’s chest.

They speak through the architecture. A Silent standing in a hut in the northern hemisphere can press a hand against the wall, sending a low-frequency tremor through the mud-brick and the stone foundations, traveling for miles along the rigid lines of the roads to another hut, where the recipient feels the words shivering through their own floorboards. It is a language of hums, thrums, and pressures—a conversation that shakes the dust from the ceiling.

When they meet in the flesh, they engage in "hand-melding." They interlace their long, spindly fingers, pressing palm to palm, and the vibration transfers directly. It is a startling experience for an outsider; to speak to a Silent is to have your bones hum with their voice, to feel their emotions as a flutter in your own chest cavity.

**Masters of Relative Distance**

The Silent are the masters of the **Law of Relative Distance**. They do not walk as we do, putting one foot in front of the other in a desperate calculation of mileage. Instead, they move by *intention*.

They understand that the space between point A and point B is a suggestion, not a law. A Silent will stand at the edge of a valley, looking at a hut that appears miles away. They close their eye-slits and focus entirely on the *need* to be there. They banish the fear of the distance, the dread of the trek. And then, they step.

To an observer, they flicker. They vanish and reappear ten yards forward, or they seem to slide through the air without moving their legs, their bodies blurring as the hills stack and restack behind them. They do not teleport; they simply negotiate with the weird elasticity of the world, convincing the space that they are already where they want to be. This is why their roads are straight lines of intention cut into the chaos—literal paths of least resistance where the distance has been beaten into submission by centuries of focused will.

**The Gardeners of Geometry**

They are the source of the Euclidean vegetation. The Silent do not farm; they sculpt. Because reality flickers and time is thick, they can shape the growth of the moss and ferns with their bare hands.

You will find them kneeling in the spongy earth, their stone-fingers working the cold black biomass. They stroke the head of a fern, forcing it to grow into a perfect spiral. They crush the moss into dense, hard-edged cubes. These geometric shapes are not natural; they are art, a way to impose order on the eroding chaos. They are trying to stabilize the world. The **Architectural Imperative** drives them to maintain the structure of reality. They believe that if they stop building, if the perfect spheres of moss are allowed to wilt back into chaos, the High Loam will dissolve completely into the grey fog.

**The Ghost in the Machine**

Ultimately, the Silent are lonely. They are a civilization of one, scattered across a continent of fog, connected only by the trembling of the floors. They crave the warmth of other living things, which is why they are fascinated by the rare visitor. They will not hurt you; they will simply stand too close, their cold, stone-skin radiating a chill that makes your teeth ache, pressing their foreheads against yours to read your thoughts through the vibration of your skull.

They are the memory of the High Loam made flesh. They are the patience of the stone, the heaviness of the water, and the silence of the air, walking endlessly through a city that is slowly turning them into statues.
