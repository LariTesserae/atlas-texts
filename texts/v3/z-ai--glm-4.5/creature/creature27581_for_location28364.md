If you stand still for an hour, the "void" begins to resolve itself. The silence is not empty; it is a tapestry of stillness. The inhabitants of this place are not absent, but they are so perfectly attuned to the mineral nature of the world that to the cursory glance, they are invisible. They do not scurry; they do not flutter. They endure.

**The Mimes**

The most common life forms are what a casual observer might mistake for loose rubble. Scattered across the white pavement are creatures that look identical to the calcified blocks that make up the terrain. They are low, squat, and angular—roughly the size of a coffin or a large paving stone.

They are solitary grazers known as Mimes. Their exoskeletons are composed of the same calcium-heavy lattice as the ground, grown in a seamless, jagged pattern. They possess no visible limbs, only a shallow, undulating "fringe" of tough muscle along their underside that ripples like a centipede’s legs, allowing them to slide with agonizing slowness across the razor-sharp stone.

They are deaf to the air; they "hear" through vibration. When the distant groan of a growing pillar vibrates through the basin, the Mimes lift themselves slightly, suspending their bellies above the cutting edges of the stone, riding the seismic waves. They feed on the greyish moss that crusts the bases of the monoliths, rasping it away with a mouthplate that spins like a drill bit, leaving smooth, geometric scars on the pillars—the only marks of erosion in this embryonic world.

**The Spire-Stalkers**

In the vertical world, clinging to the sides of the towering monoliths, live the Spire-Stalkers. They are terrifyingly thin, elongated creatures that look like dried ligaments stretched over bone. They are perfectly white, blending into the stone, except for a series of dark, heat-sensitive pits along their flanks.

They move only when the wind blows the steam plumes, using the momentary cover of moisture to scuttle sideways, their sharp, hooked feet finding purchase in the microscopic pores of the calcite. They are ambush predators. They hang motionless for days, folded flat against the side of a pillar, indistinguishable from a shadow or a fracture in the rock.

When a Mime drifts too close to a base, the Stalker drops. It does not pounce; it falls like a dead weight, aiming to land on the softer underbelly of the grazer. Its impact is cushioned by a collapsible exoskeleton. Once attached, it injects a solvent that begins to liquefy the Mime’s mineral shell, turning the creature’s defenses into a slurry that the Stalker drinks. The feeding takes hours, during which the Stalker looks like nothing more than a stain on the white stone.

**The Vaporettes**

The only things that break the geometric rigidity of the life here are the Vaporettes. They live exclusively in the stagnant, scum-filled pools that gather in the square basins.

They are amorphous, gelatinous mats of translucent grey matter, pulsing slowly in the green water. They have no shape, only the capacity to conform. If the pool evaporates or is crushed by the rising earth, the Vaporette desiccates into a hard, spore-like crust that can blow away in the wind, waiting for the next condensation cycle to rehydrate.

In the water, however, they are active filters. They extend microscopic filaments into the scum, digesting the algae and heavy metals. Occasionally, if a succulent spiked "shrub" grows too close to the water's edge, the Vaporette will send out creeping tendrils to strangle the plant, dragging it down into the stagnant pool to dissolve it in a slow, wet embrace.

**The Apex: The Geode-Construct**

There is a rumor of something larger, though it is rarely seen. The "fauna parameter" might suggest a void, but the silence has a hierarchy. At the top of this invisible food chain is the Geode-Construct.

It is a massive, mobile hollow—a living cavern. It resembles a gliding segment of the ruin, a square archway or a collapsed roof that has somehow detached itself from the ground. It is not a single organism, but a colony organism, a hive of calcified worms that have fused together into a single, shifting architecture.

It moves by contracting its interior chambers, expelling air to push itself across the flat basin. It sounds like a sighing rock slide. It does not hunt for food; it hunts for space. It seeks out the freshest, most active "construction sites"—where the steam vents are strongest and the new pillars are rising.

There, it positions itself over the fissures, letting the super-heated, mineral-rich steam fill its hollow chambers. The heat sustains it, and the minerals repair its shell, thickening its armor. It is a creature of pure consumption, not of flesh, but of energy and volume. When it moves, the ground trembles, and the smaller Mimes freeze, pressing themselves flat against the earth, mimicking the pebbles, praying that the moving mountain above them does not choose to settle.

**The Rhythm of Life**

Life here is a game of timing. It is dictated by the *drip* and the *groan*. When the earth grows, the creatures freeze, becoming stone. When the earth is still, the shadows lengthen, and the sharp, white landscape begins to crawl. They are a pale, silent people, living on the knife-edge of a world that is trying to crush them, evolving not to fight the geometry, but to become it.
