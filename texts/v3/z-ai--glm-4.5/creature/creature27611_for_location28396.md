In the absence of sight, the inhabitants of the Blind Rotunda exist as pure texture, sound, and thermal shadow. They are a collection of evolutionary miracles and biological oddities, forged not by the sun, but by the pressure of the dark and the rhythm of the machines.

First, there are the **Wall-Striders**, the vermin of the vertical space. They inhabit the geometric foam of the Weeping Walls, creatures that are less solid than they are sponge. They possess no distinct skeletal structure; instead, they are gelatinous masses of porous flesh that cling to the ancient lattice. Their skin is ridged with microscopic cilia that vibrate in tune with the rushing water, allowing them to "hear" the texture of the stone through their bodies. They feed on the slick bio-algae that grows where the humidity condenses. To a blind observer brushing against the wall, a Wall-Strider feels like a patch of warm, wet slime that shivers and contracts away from the touch. They communicate by clicking their hydration sacks, producing sharp, wet *pops* that cut through the roar of the falls, sounding like pebbles dropped in a deep well.

On the floor, moving through the spongy moss with the slow inevitability of tectonic plates, are the **Thrum-Grazer**. These are the giants of the Rotunda, the primary consumers of the cathedral-sized ferns. They are massive, hexapedal beasts, standing as tall as a house, covered in a dense, velvet-like fur that absorbs sound completely. They are the "silence" in the noisy room. Their eyes have atrophied into vestigial bumps beneath the fur; they navigate solely through the soles of their feet. They are exquisitely sensitive to the subsonic drone of the tech-nodes buried in the floor. By feeling the minute variations in the vibration, they can map the location of the giant ferns, which they approach with slow, deliberate steps to avoid triggering the splashing of the moss. Their mouths are small, serrated beaks hidden beneath a curtain of muscular tendrils, which they use to strip the leathery fronds. When a herd of Thrum-Grazers sleeps, they huddle together to merge their bio-rhythms with the machine’s hum, effectively disappearing from the sonic landscape of the chamber.

Hunting the Grazer are the **Resonant Stalkers**. These are the apex predators of the dark, evolved to hunt in a room that never stops screaming. They are lithe, reptilian creatures with elongated limbs and skin that mimics the thermal signature of the surrounding rock. Their primary adaptation is a biological larynx that can perfectly mimic the sound of dripping water. They can sit motionless for hours, reproducing the specific *plink* and *drone* of a nearby rivulet, effectively camouflaging themselves acoustically. When they attack, they do not snarl or roar; they utilize a burst of focused infrasound—a concussive blast of low-frequency energy that stuns the nervous system of the Grazer. The kill is silent, marked only by a sudden, wet commotion and the heavy thud of a giant collapsing into the moss, followed by the tearing sounds of feeding that are swiftly lost in the roar of the walls.

Between the floor and the ceiling, the air is filled with the **Pressure-Weavers**. These are avian analogues, but they lack feathers and wings. Instead, they possess long, trailing membranes of electro-sensitive tissue that stream behind them like kites, sometimes spanning twenty meters. They do not fly so much as swim through the thick, hot air, riding the convection currents rising from the heated floor. They navigate by detecting the minute displacement of air caused by the falling water droplets; a drop falling meters away creates a ripple that hits their membranes like a physical blow. They feed on insect swarms—clouds of microscopic, clicking mites that drift in the humidity. The Pressure-Weavers are solitary, and their presence is often only felt by the sudden, sharp drop in air pressure as a massive body sweeps past one’s face, the membranes brushing against the skin with the dry, static shock of a balloon rubbed against wool.

Living in symbiosis with the ancient tech are the **Copper-Scavengers**. These are small, crustacean-like creatures that scuttle across the surface of the monolithic nodes buried in the walls. They are attracted to the electromagnetic fields generated by the machines. Their shells are impregnated with metallic dust drawn from the air, giving them a distinct, metallic tang to the scent and a cold, hard feel to the touch. They do not eat organic matter; they gnaw on the mineral casing of the tech-nodes, processing the raw conductive materials. In doing so, they act as living maintenance bots, clearing the oxidation from the machinery. They move with the jerky, erratic energy of clockwork toys, their legs making a distinct, dry *skittering* sound that contrasts with the wet noises of the rest of the chamber.
