The inhabitants of this place are not merely living within it; they are wearing it down, dissolving the boundary between the organic and the manufactured. To look at the floor is to realize that the "stone" is not stationary. It is a shifting, heaving mosaic of carapace and chitin.

First, there is the foundation of the ecosystem: the **Roach-Tide**. These are not the skittering pests of the surface world. These are sleek, oil-slick giants, some the length of a human forearm. Their exoskeletons have adapted to the gloom, shifting from a deep, oily black to a translucent, iridescent gray that blends perfectly with the wet limestone. They do not scatter individually; they move as a singular, viscous fluid. When they flow around the base of a concrete pillar, it looks like the stone itself is melting and running downhill. They are the silent grazers of this underworld, feeding on the black mold and the sludge that coats the water, their millions of tined legs sounding like the ripping of heavy canvas.

Navigating through this tide are the **Stone-Crabs**. They are the heavy artillery of the cavern, titans with shells the color and texture of wet concrete, rough and pitted. They have evolved to look exactly like the jagged slabs of bedrock they traverse. When they sit still, which they do for long hours to conserve energy in the suffocating heat, they are invisible—a landscape of stumbling rocks. Only when they move do they reveal themselves, raising massive, crushing claws that are stained rust-red from the iron deposits in the water. They are aggressive opportunists, snatching up unwary roaches or smaller crustaceans, but they are also the cleaners, using their powerful claws to scrape the nutrient-rich slime from the rusted pipes, contributing to the constant erosion of the infrastructure.

Above the floor, occupying the suffocating vertical space, are the **Moth-Lords**. These are the "dinner plates" mentioned, but up close, they are more akin to flying manta rays. Their wings are plush and dense, coated in a thick, fur-like pile that repels the moisture and insulates them against the heat of the lights. They are deafened by their own biology, their obsession with the sodium-vapor glow a fatal neurological glitch. They batter themselves against the cages with the force of thrown fruit, their wings dusting the air with a shimmering, hallucinogenic powder that floats down into the fog, feeding the fungi below.

But the true masters of this gloom, the predators that rule the dark spaces between the pillars, are the **Pipe-Weavers**.

They are not seen often, for they have perfectly mimicked the industrial skeleton of the cavern. They are elongated, spindly things, possessing long, multi-jointed limbs that wrap around the rusted iron scaffolding. Their skin is wet and rubbery, the exact color of oxidized iron—bubbled, flaking, and weeping a fluid that smells just like the steam vents. They hang upside down from the pipes, motionless as rust, indistinguishable from the machinery that supports the ceiling.

The Pipe-Weavers are the architects of the chaos. They do not build webs of silk; they build nets of conductivity. They trail long, dripping tendrils of bio-conductive slime down into the steam, sensing the electrical hum of the lights and the vibration of the distant engines. To them, the noise of the room is a map. When a Moth-Lord falls from the sky, exhausted or stunned, or a Stone-Crab strays too far from the safety of the rock piles, the Weavers descend.

They do not pounce; they unravel. They drop on thin, nearly invisible threads of mucous, snaring their prey in mid-air or snatching it from the puddles. Their bodies are distensible, capable of wrapping around a crab the size of a dog and constricting with the slow, irresistible power of a hydraulic press. They feed slowly, hanging in the gloom like strange, discarded bundles of rags, while the steam vents hiss around them, hiding the scent of blood in the scent of ozone.

Finally, deep in the recesses where the light fails entirely, in the shadows beneath the leaking concrete pillars, live the **Gloom-Dwellers**. These are pale, sightless things—amphibian-like in their wet, fleshy skin, but possessed of a gripping, prehensile tail. They are the clean-up crew, the bottom feeders that wait for the Weavers to drop their scraps. They have no eyes, only sensitive pits along their snouts that detect the heat of living flesh. They float in the stagnant, oily pools, their skin absorbing the toxins that would kill anything else, turning the industrial waste into flesh. They are white, bloated, and silent, glowing with a faint, sickly bioluminescence that mirrors the dying lights above, creating a ghostly, underwater reflection of the ceiling high above.

Together, they form a closed loop of consumption: the roaches eat the waste; the crabs eat the roaches; the moths feed the floor with their dust; the Weavers harvest the crabs and moths; and the Dwellers eat what remains. All of them are adapted to the heat, the wet, and the dark, turning a place of abandonment into a thriving, suffocating metropolis of the unseen.
