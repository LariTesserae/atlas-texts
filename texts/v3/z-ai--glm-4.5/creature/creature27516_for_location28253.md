To survive in the High Roar is to be a master of vibration, friction, and silence. The life here has not just adapted to the noise; it has internalized it, becoming an extension of the wind’s endless chord.

**The Lithic Grazers**

On the open, slick expanses of the calcified stone, where the wind screams loudest, live the **Oithoi**. They are not graceful creatures; they are biological anchors. Resembling living, flattened boulders, the Oithoi possess a low-slung, triangular profile composed of dense, heavy bone plating covered in a rough, pelt-like texture of interlocking mineral hairs.

They do not walk so much as they slide and skid, their underbellies coated in a secretion of slick, viscous oil that matches the stone’s sheen. They move with the turbulence, allowing the sudden downdrafts to press them flat against the rock, skittering forward only when the chaotic eddies provide a momentary lift. They possess no visible ears; their auditory canals are sealed by thick flaps of cartilage, protecting them from the deafening howl. Instead, "eyes" run in a lateral line along their flanks—sensors capable of reading the microscopic tremors of the wind against the rock. They graze on the invisible: the carpets of bacteria and slick, oily algae that grow on the wettest parts of the windward stone, rasping it away with tongues like sandpaper.

**The Resonant Predators**

Hunting the Oithoi is the **Wind-Stalker**, though the locals of the lower altitudes call them the "Silencers." These are the furred, sail-eared entities mentioned in the lore of the peaks, but their reality is stranger. They are incredibly lithe, possessing a skeletal structure that is not rigid but semi-flexible, allowing them to bend like reeds in a gale without breaking.

Their hunting strategy is a terrifying use of the environment’s physics. They do not run; they surf the pressure waves. A Stalker will wait in a wind-shadowed hollow until it detects the specific frequency of a grazing Oithoi’s heartbeat through the stone. Then, it leaps into the wind, spreading its limbs to catch the updrafts, gliding with silent precision until it drops onto its prey. The impact is instant, the Stalker’s weight driving the Oithoi into the stone, and because the kill happens during a moment of high-turbulence roar, the victim’s death screams are swallowed entirely by the mountain.

**The Flora’s Parasites**

In the "islands" of violent green—the leeward ravines where the black rubber trees grow—lives the **Glint-Fly**. These are not true flies, but hummingbird-sized beetles with wings of iridescent, transparent chitin that vibrate at the exact harmonic frequency of the wind passing through the fluted rock.

This makes them invisible to the eye and inaudible to the ear; they move as ghostly distortions in the air. They feed on the blood of the creatures that shelter in the ravines, but their primary ecological role is pollination. The rubber trees have flowers that only open when exposed to a specific high-pitched whistle. The Glint-Flies, by beating their wings at this frequency, force the flowers to bloom, inject their proboscis, and depart before the blossom realizes it has been robbed. To the observer, they look like heat haze—shimmering, impossible accidents of light and air.

**The Echo-Masons**

Then there are the inhabitants of the ruins. The primitive circles are not abandoned; they are the hives of the **Echo-Masons**. These are social insects, roughly the size of a human fist, with bodies of white, porous ceramic-like exoskeletons. They are the descendants of the builders who abandoned the peaks, evolved over eons into a hive mind that creates rather than destroys.

They do not build hives; they build instruments. The Masons secrete a mortar that hardens into a stone acoustically identical to the mountain itself. They spend their lives adding to the low, circular walls, smoothing them, curving them to catch the wind. Within these circles, they cultivate a fungus that feeds exclusively on the low-frequency thrumming of the atmosphere. The louder the wind, the more the fungus vibrates, and the more it grows. The Masons huddle inside their stone rings, safe in the eye of the turbulence, farming the sound itself.

**The Silent Kings**

Finally, deep within the largest of the wind-sheltered ravines, where the mist is thickest and the stone has been worn into smooth, bowl-like depressions, live the **Sentinels**. These are massive, solitary beings—reptilian but with skin like wet velvet. They are the largest things on the High Roar, slow-moving and ancient.

They are blind and deaf, having sacrificed these senses to the chaos. Instead, their backs are covered in sensitive, feathery antennae that rise vertically from their spine, tasting the chemical composition of the mist and measuring the barometric pressure with terrifying accuracy. They are the weather-prophets of the mountain. When a Sentinel shifts its weight from one foot to the other, the smaller creatures know: the wind direction is about to shift. They are worshipped, in a primitive way, by the ecosystem; their movements dictate the survival schedules of every other living thing on the peak. They eat the rubber trees, crushing the tough wood with jaws that can snap granite, and in doing so, they create the clearings necessary for new life to begin. They are the gardeners of the storm, moving with a glacial, terrifying grace through the beautiful, hostile dark.
