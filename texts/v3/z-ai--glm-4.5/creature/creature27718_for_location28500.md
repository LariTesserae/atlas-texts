Life in the Oculus does not just inhabit the noise; it is composed of it. The creatures here have evolved not to ignore the grinding roar of the Engine, but to embody it, their biology syncing to the piston’s heave and the electric crackle of the air. They are things of wet chitin, conductive metal, and vibrating muscle.

**The Weavers of the Static**
On the vertical ledges, where the electric-green moss carpets the stone, live the **Spark-Looms**. They are not spiders, though they occupy that niche. They are arachnid-like constructs, their bodies elongated and translucent, revealing a quicksilver fluid that pulses in time with the room’s static charge. They do not spin silk; they spin conductive filaments of copper-imbued webbing.

These webs stretch across the cracks in the obsidian, draping the walls like metallic gossamer. When the piston reaches its zenith and the blue-white lightning arcs between the walls, the webs catch the voltage. The Spark-Looms don't just survive the shock; they feed on it. The electricity travels down the filaments and into their bodies, charging the fluid within them with a blinding strobe-light intensity. During the flash of a storm, they are invisible, lost in the glare; in the dark moments between the piston’s cycles, they glow softly with stored energy, pulsing like embers in the dark.

**The Grazers of the Vertical**
Clinging to the slick, wet rock above the water line are the **Basalt-Striders**. These are heavy, low-slung creatures, resembling a grotesque hybrid of a gecko and a crab, with shells formed of dense, iron-heavy stone. They are the primary grazers of the mossy ledges.

Because the floor is a lethal churn of grinding rock, the Striders cannot afford to fall. Their limbs are terminated in powerful, hydraulic clamps that can crush rock, allowing them to grip the smooth, wet obsidian with unyielding strength. They move only during the "inhale"—the moment the piston drops and the vacuum pulls air upward. They freeze solid during the "exhale," locking their muscles against the sudden hurricane-force down-draft. Their eyes are wide and milky, adapted to the gloom, but their bodies are covered in sensory hairs that feel the vibration of the stone through their feet, allowing them to "hear" the piston's position through the rock itself.

**The Tethered Predators**
The Gale-Bats fill the air, but they are not the masters of it. That title belongs to the **Anchor-Maws**. These are leviathans of the air, pale, bloated sacks of flesh that hang from the ceiling of the Oculus, thousands of feet up near the pinprick of gray light.

They do not fly. They float, filled with bladders of lighter-than-air gas harvested from the steam vents. But they are anchored. Long, cable-thick tendrils, some hundreds of feet long, trail down from their bodies into the void. These tendrils are coated in a sticky, paralytic resin.

When the Gale-Bats ride the updraft in their screaming spirals, they fly blindly through the forest of descending tendrils. Upon contact, the bat is snared, reeled in slowly by the retracting cable of the Anchor-Maw. The predator waits overhead, a silent balloon in the storm, its digestion powered by the heat of the stolen prey. The silence of the Anchor-Maw is the only counterpoint to the roar of the room; they are the ghosts in the machine.

**The Dwellers of the Piston**
The Engine itself is inhabited. On the surface of the rising obsidian column, where the water sheets like a horizontal rain, live the **Friction-Eaters**. They look like armored slugs, flat and wide, with shells of diamond-hard carbon. They clamp themselves tightly against the piston’s face, enduring the crushing scrape of the water and the grinding of the rock.

They feed on the heat generated by the friction of the moving piston. As the column rubs against the water and the air, the surface heats to scalding temperatures. The Friction-Eaters huddle in the warmest crevices, absorbing the thermal energy through their underbellies. When the piston vents steam, they extend feathery gills to catch the mineral-rich moisture, filtering out iron and sulfur particles. They live entirely on the violence of the machine’s motion, clinging to the beating heart of the world as it heaves up and down, wearing their shells smooth like river stones.

**The Singers**
Deep in the porous rock of the outer walls, invisible to the eye, live the **Resonators**. They are worm-like, colonial organisms that bore into the stone, creating vast, labyrinthine tunnels. They are the chorus of the Oculus.

The room is loud, but the Resonators do not hear it as noise; they treat it as a medium. They vibrate their own bodies in perfect harmonic sympathy with the grinding Engine. By vibrating at a slightly offset frequency, they generate a constant, low hum that modulates the room’s roar. This hum serves a purpose: it acts as a sonar, mapping the movement of the water, the position of the piston, and the flight paths of the Gale-Bats. All life in the Oculus, from the smallest Spark-Loom to the largest Anchor-Maw, has evolved to listen to the Resonators. They are the nervous system of the room, the hidden conductors of this industrial symphony.

They live inside the walls, eating the stone, singing the song of the tectonic heartbeat, ensuring that even in the blinding steam and the deafening grind, no creature is ever truly lost in the dark.
