To look upon the Rust-Grave and see it as empty is to misunderstand the nature of its occupancy. The life here does not scramble, chatter, or build; it endures. It is a life woven into the pauses of the machine, existing in the damp interstices between the grinding of tectonic gears.

The primary inhabitants are the **Attuned**.

They are solitary figures, tall and attenuated, standing motionless against the rust-colored skyline. At first glance, they appear to be misplaced architecture themselves—vertical pillars of wet, grey concrete or perhaps scavenged pipes bundled into a vaguely humanoid shape. They do not wear clothes; their skin is a smooth, porous keratin that mimics the texture of water-weathered stone, absorbing the perpetual weeping of the atmosphere.

They have no faces in the conventional sense. Where a mouth or nose should be, there is only a smooth, sloping curve of bone-like plate. Their eyes are hidden behind thick, translucent membranes that protect them from the flying grit and the stinging humidity, granting them the appearance of blind, eyeless statues. They do not need to see the city; they feel it.

The Attuned are acoustic vampires. They do not eat the moss or hunt the air-jellies. Instead, they position themselves against the vibrating flanks of the great blast furnaces or sprawl across the giant, slow-turning flywheels. They press their elongated bodies against the metal, siphoning the kinetic energy of the city’s heartbeat. The deafening bass note that would shatter a human mind is their sustenance. They feed on the resonance, the low-frequency thrum that permeates the bones of the world. It is a meager diet, which explains their emaciated, skeletal frames and their glacial metabolism. An Attuned might stand in the same spot for a century, its skin slowly oxidizing to match the pipe it leans against, entering a trance of deep communion with the machine.

When they must move, it is a terrifying sight. They do not walk; they flow. Their joints do not hinge; they telescope, sliding smoothly over one another with the sound of wet silk. They move only in rhythm with the city’s pulse—lunging forward during the exhale of a bellows, freezing rigid during the inhale. To see an Attuned cross a causeway is to watch time-lapse photography in real-time, a stuttering, impossible motion that defies the eye’s persistence of vision.

Sharing the space with the Attuned are the **Silt-Skimmers**.

Smaller, more numerous, yet still invisible to the casual glance, these creatures inhabit the shallow, rust-red marshes that pool in the cracks of the pavement. They are amphibious, their bodies flat and wide like stingrays, composed of a gelatinous, translucent bio-metal that feels cold and oily to the touch. They glide through the viscous, chemical-heavy water, filtering the heavy metals and industrial runoff through their gills.

The Silt-Skimmers are the grave-keepers. They are the ones responsible for the slick, clean appearance of the horizontal surfaces. They extend long, ribbon-like tongues—rough as a wire brush—to scrape away the accumulating rust and grime, ingesting the oxides to reinforce their own metallic exoskeletons. They are communal creatures, huddling together in masses that look from a distance like spilled oil slicks or patches of dark moss. They communicate not by sound, which is useless here, but by bioluminescence, flashing patterns of dull, rust-orange light across their backs to signal danger or the location of a particularly rich vein of iron deposits.

Higher up, in the gloom of the ceiling where the dirigibles dock, live the **Gas-Gargoyyles**.

These are the only creatures that fly, though they fly poorly. They resemble manta rays made of parchment-thin skin and calcified cartilage. They possess no wings for flapping; instead, they possess massive air sacs that they fill with the buoyant, humid gases rising from the city’s vents. They drift on the thermal currents, bumping gently against the mooring towers and the undersides of the walkways.

They are scavengers of the vertical axis, feeding on the colonies of bacteria and fungi that grow in the warm exhaust plumes of the refineries. Their beaks are small and hooked, designed to pry crusty growths from the rivets high above the ground. They hang upside down from the pipelines like bats, sleeping in vast, swaying clusters that block out the diffuse grey light, their gas-filled bodies expanding and contracting with the slow, rhythmic sighing of the city’s lungs.

And then there is the rumor of something else.

The prompt asks who tends the fires, who greases the gears. The Attuned do not. The Skimmers do not. Yet, occasionally, an observer might catch sight of a **Grease-Monk**.

They are rarely seen, and never directly. They are shadows within shadows, small, hunched figures wrapped in suits of oil-stained canvas and lead. They wear heavy, complex respirators that hiss with every breath, filtering the toxic air. They are the only things here that move with urgency, darting across the catwalks during the moments of silence between the great piston-strikes.

They do not speak. They do not seem to sleep. They carry buckets of unguent and tools that predate the current era, maintaining the immense machinery with a terrified reverence. It is unclear if they are the original builders, immortalized by the strange atmosphere, or a later evolution of vermin that has learned to worship the engine that sustains them. They are the priests of this place, and they are as much a part of the machine as the gears they tend.

Life in the Rust-Grave is not a battle for survival; it is a surrender to process. It is a slow, wet digestion, a communal hallucination where the line between the living and the mechanical dissolves into the red, dripping rust. They are all waiting for the cycle to complete, patient as stone, vibrating in the dark.
