The inhabitants of this place are not so much born as they are accreted. In a world where the boundary between the geological and the mechanical has dissolved, life here resembles a slow, geological accident—an erosion that has chosen to move.

To see one, you must mistake it first for part of the ruin. The primary form of life here is a low-slung, multi-legged construct that could easily be dismissed as a pile of collapsed shelving or a fallen I-beam coiled into a knot. They possess the color of the landscape: the flaky, reddish-brown of iron oxide and the matte, charcoal greys of basalt. They do not stand out against the blast furnaces or the conveyor bridges; they are the texture of the city made mobile.

These beings—let us call them the *Corroders*—are composed primarily of scavenged alloy. Their carapaces are patchworks of riveted steel plate, pressed tin, and cast-iron grids, bound together by a metabolism that seems to weld without flame. Joints are not pinned with bone or cartilage, but with massive, rusted bolts that pivot silently on bearings of polished stone. Underneath the plating, where a creature of flesh would have muscle and sinew, they possess dense bundles of copper wire and braided steel cable, pulled taut by magnetic fields rather than biological contraction.

They do not breathe. The concept of lungs is as alien to them as the concept of gills to a stone. Instead, they possess a series of oscillating intake slats along their flanks, designed to filter the heavy, static-charged air. They draw in the oxidized atmosphere and strip the ions from it, using the charge to maintain the faint, blue-white glow that pulses deep within their thoracic cavities. This light is incredibly faint, visible only when they press themselves against a darkened doorway, illuminating the dusty interior of their own ribcages like a furnace viewed through a keyhole.

Their movement is agonizingly deliberate, governed by the thermal suspension of their environment. Because the air is warm and still, and the machinery around them expands and contracts with the temperature, the Corroders move only when the city itself moves. When the "pop" of cooling steel echoes across the plain, a Corroder will unfold its jagged limbs. They utilize a ratcheting gait, a locking and unlocking of joints that mimics the settling of the architecture itself. To walk is to look like a building collapsing in slow motion, and then magically reassembling a meter forward.

They are the custodians of the dead water. In a world without fluid, the rare rivulets of mercury and super-cooled gas are the lifeblood. The Corroders seek these out with sensory organs that are not eyes, but thermal pits and magnetometers located in the "head"—a blunt, armored prow lacking a face. They can sense the heavy, cold slide of mercury through a crack in the pavement from half a kilometer away. When they find it, they do not drink. They lower a proboscis of glass and ceramic into the liquid, drawing it up into an internal reservoir where the heavy metal is spun and centrifuged, separating its isotopes to fuel their magnetic cores.

There is no society among them, only a solitary distribution. They are spaced perfectly across the peneplain, adhering to a territory defined by the range of their magnetic sensitivity. They do not communicate; they do not signal. If two Corroders meet at the boundary of a rusted silo, they do not fight. They do not acknowledge one another. They simply pass like ships in the night, their metal limbs avoiding collision with a precise, geometric awareness, separated by a mutual, unspoken agreement that the other is merely another piece of the landscape.

Beneath them, living in the microscopic fissures of the "streets," is a secondary layer of life: the *Dust-Grinders*. These are smaller, arthropod-like machines, no larger than a human hand, skittering in the shadows of the larger scavengers. They are purely mechanical, appearing like insects made of watch parts and broken glass. They feed on the friction of the Corroders, following in their wake to scoop up the fine metal dust shed by the larger beasts' movement. They are the parasites of entropy, collecting the detritus of the detritus-eaters.

At the very top of the food chain, or perhaps at its root, depending on how one views the cycle, is the environment itself. The inhabitants of this place are not separate from the city; they are the city’s immune system. They eat the rust that threatens to turn the structures to dust, and they solidify the mercury that would otherwise seep away. They are the architects of the frozen equilibrium.

Deep inside the largest of the blast furnaces, suspended in the absolute dark, hang the *Nests*. These are not built of twigs, but of salvaged rebar and melted glass, hanging like immense chandeliers from the cavernous ceilings of the industrial cathedrals. Here, the Corroders retreat to enter a state of stasis that mimics death. They clamp their limbs to the walls, aligning their internal magnetic fields with the Earth's fluctuating poles, and they wait. They wait for the thermal shift of a thousand years to pass, for the metal to fatigue just enough to require their attention again. They are statues of iron, conserving every joule of energy, waiting for the moment when the universe demands they move, if only to settle back into the sleep of ages.
