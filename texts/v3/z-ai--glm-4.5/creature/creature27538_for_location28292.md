To survive here is to accept the premise that geology is biology, and that stone is merely slow flesh. The inhabitants of this calcified metropolis are not distinct from the city; they are the maintenance crew of its ruin, the parasites of its grandeur, and the architects of its final decay. They are silent, mostly, because in a damp, porous world, sound is a luxury that betrays you to the things that feel the tremors in the stone.

Here, life mimics the inanimate.

**The Mason-Mimics**
The primary grazers of the plateau are a species of broad, flattened creatures that look, for all the world, like detached chunks of the coral-city floor. They are the "Sidewalks" given legs. Imagine a crab the size of a coffee table, but flattened by a hydraulic press, its carapace not the shiny chitin of a normal crustacean, but a rough, pitted limestone grey. They grow calcified nodules and ridges on their backs, perfectly matching the texture of the ancient reef.

They move with a glacial, hydraulic fluidity. Because the air is aggressively wet, they do not scuttle; they flow. They secrete a thin, lubricating mucus from their undersides that allows them to slide over the razor-sharp mineral deposits without injury. They are the grazers of the vertical walls, using powerful, gripping legs to traverse the "skyscrapers," feeding on the crustose lichen—the rust and mint-colored leather that clings to the high-rises. You will not see them move unless you stare for minutes; a nodule on a wall will simply shift position, a shadow will detach and slide around a corner. They are the city’s pigeons, if pigeons were made of concrete and weighed four hundred pounds.

**The Gutter-Weavers**
In the deep, winding crevasses—the "streets" where the water trickles—live the ambush predators. These are pale, multi-legged organisms that look disturbingly like spilled internal organs. They lack exoskeletons; their skin is translucent, wet, and gelatinous, glowing faintly with the bioluminescence of chemosynthetic bacteria they farm within their tissues.

They anchor themselves in the shadows of the overhanging buttresses, stretching long, sticky filaments across the narrow gaps where the wind dies. They do not weave webs of silk; they weave nets of viscous mucus that hang invisible in the misting air. They feed on the "Mist-Moths"—fragile, scale-less insects that evolved in the cloud layer and drift down to die in the cold stone canyons. When a moth touches a filament, the Gutter-Weaver reels it in with a twitch that is too fast for the eye to track. They are the sole source of the "skittering" sound one hears, a wet, slapping noise as they drag their prey back into the recesses of the stone to dissolve it in enzymatic pools.

**The Spire-Shearers**
High above, where the clouds drag their bellies against the needle-thin spires, live the great aerialists. These are the pterosaur-like gliders mentioned in passing, but up close, they are more akin to flying manta rays. Their wingspan is immense, perhaps fifteen feet across, constructed not of skin and bone alone, but reinforced with calcified struts that match the density of the rock they navigate.

They possess beaks designed not for killing, but for grinding. They are the demolition crew of the city. They land on the precarious, needle-thin tops of the fossil towers and scrape away, feeding on the softest, newest limestone deposits and the colonies of microscopic algae that cloud the surface of the wet stone. Their activity is constant; they are the reason the spires do not pierce the heavens but are worn down to nubs. When they launch, they do not flap vigorously; they simply catch the updrafts of the mist, their heavy, stone-reinforced bodies sinking into the air with a terrifying momentum. They are silent as owls, passing overhead like thunderclouds given shape.

**The Vein-Burrowers**
Beneath the surface, within the porous "skeleton" of the city itself, lives the true dominant lifeform. The plateau is not solid rock; it is a sponge. Inside the millions of microscopic tubes and ancient coral polyps live worm-like creatures that operate on a timeframe that makes the Mason-Mimics look frantic.

These are the Vein-Burrowers. They are essentially living stomachs, lined with grinding teeth that digest the calcium carbonate of the city itself, excreting it as a finer, harder paste that lines their tunnels. They are responsible for the "low-frequency groans" that the traveler feels in their teeth. As the temperature shifts, the stone contracts, and the Burrowers—millions of them, miles deep—shift in their sleep. They are the geology. When a bridge collapses or a skyscraper settles, it is often because the Vein-Burrowers have decided that section of the city was dinner. They are never seen, only felt as a subtle vibration in the soles of your boots, a reminder that the ground beneath you is eating itself.

**The Salt-Gardeners**
Perhaps the strangest inhabitants are the tiny, ant-like swarms found around the "sidewalk" puddles. These are not social insects in the earthly sense; they are a mobile fungal colony. The "ants" are actually spore-carriers, scurrying to harvest the white crusty mineral deposits left by the evaporating water. They carry these grains of hardened lime to the deepest, wettest crevices, where they堆 (pile) them into intricate, ziggurat-like gardens.

Here, in the darkness, they farm a specialized slime mold that breaks down the stone, releasing the trace minerals locked within. The ants then consume the slime. It is a sedentary, industrial agrarian society. If you shine a light into a dark alley, you might see thousands of tiny white grains moving in a river-like flow, governed by a collective intelligence that cares nothing for the sun, only for the chemistry of the rock.

All of them—the grinding Mason-Mimics, the wet Gutter-Weavers, the gliding Spire-Shearers, the groaning Vein-Burrowers, and the industrious Salt-Gardeners—are components of a single, slow metabolic process. They are the digestive system of a dead ocean, processing the city of the past into the dust of the future, in a silence so profound it feels like pressure.
