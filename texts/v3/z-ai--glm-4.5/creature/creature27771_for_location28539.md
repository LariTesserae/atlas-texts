To stand in the Condensery and believe it empty is a failure of perception, a biological limitation of eyes evolved to seek movement and fur. There is life here, but it does not scurry. It does not breathe with the frantic rhythm of oxygen-starved blood. It congeals.

What lives here are the **Archivists of the Drip**—though they have no name for themselves, for they possess no language as we understand it. They are solitary, gelatinous entities that exist at the terrifying intersection of super-heated silica and high-pressure fluid. They are not distinct individuals in the way a crow or a human is an individual; rather, they are temporary, cohesive eddies in the complex hydraulic system of the spire.

At first glance, you would mistake them for a trick of the light or a flaw in the black glass. They appear as vertical tears in the fabric of the air, standing nearly two meters tall. Their form is roughly anthropomorphic but stretched, elongated like a wax figure left too near the fire, constantly threatening to collapse into a puddle. They possess no defined skin. Their outer membrane is a semi-permeable lattice of tense, hot water holding together a skeleton of dissolved obsidian and raw crystal. They look like walking water, hot enough to scald, heavy enough to bruise.

They move with a fluid, silent grace. They do not step so much as they flow. A foot does not lift and fall; it extends, adheres to the feverish deck, and the rest of the body pours forward like a heavy syrup. They leave no wet trail, for the humid air immediately bonds with their residue, absorbing it back into the local ecosystem.

These beings are the custodians of the silence. You will find them clustered around the intersections of the micro-flumes, where the grey water runs. They are "feeding," though their consumption is mechanical and thermal. They plunge their elongated, fingerless hands into the running water, siphoning the thermal energy that drives the Condensery’s cooling systems. As they feed, the crystalline fungus that grows in the damp corners seems to lean toward them, vibrating in sympathy. In return, the beings prune the silica growths with surgical precision, snapping the sharp glass "leaves" off and ingesting them, integrating the mineral structure into their own temporary frames. They are a garden tending itself.

Their senses are alien. They have no eyes. The bioluminescent fog that ignites when you walk is triggered by the bio-electric field of your heart; for these entities, the light is constant, a humming aura that surrounds them at all times. They "see" through texture and temperature. To them, the shifting architecture is not a wall but a gradient of heat signatures. When the corridor elongates or a doorway seals like a wound, they do not panic. They merely slide toward the coolest point, the path of least resistance, riding the molecular currents of the air. They are intimately attuned to the "dream" of the city, flowing around its reshaping geometry as water flows around a stone.

There is no violence in them, but there is danger. They are curious. If you remain still for too long, the oppressive humidity serving as a conductor, you will feel the pressure change. The air will grow denser around you. One of them will drift closer, drawn by the strange, chaotic heat of your mammalian body. It will extend a limb—a glistening, viscous pseudopod—to touch you. It will not hurt; it will feel like being wrapped in hot, wet silk. It is tasting your salts, reading the history of your dehydration in the composition of your sweat. If you let it linger, it might try to integrate you, to dissolve the barrier between its biology and yours, mistaking you for a new, erratic node in the building's circulation.

They are lonely, in a way that only geological formations can be lonely. They exist in a state of perpetual transition, never fully solid, never fully liquid, holding a shape that the universe constantly tries to reclaim. When the spire thrums low in your teeth, they thrum with it, vibrating at a frequency that feels like a memory of pain.

In the absolute vertical limit, where the air is thick as wool and the silence is pressurized, they are the only ghosts. They wait in the mist, sliding silently over the black non-reflective floors, tending to the glass gardens, drinking the heat, waiting for the Condensery to finish a dream that began a thousand years ago.
