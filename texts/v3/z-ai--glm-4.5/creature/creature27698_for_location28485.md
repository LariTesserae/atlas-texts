Life here is not grown; it is machined by necessity. In the absence of sun, in the crushing absence of the organic cycle of bloom and decay, the fauna has evolved to mirror the geometry of the ruins that entomb them. They are the sons and daughters of the megastructure, biological entities that have adapted to the artificial, their forms dictated by the relentless logic of pressure and current.

At the foundational level, clinging to the vast, eroding flanks of the synthetic spires, are the **Lith-Feeders**. These are not crustaceans in the traditional sense, but slow, sprawling colonies of calcified plates that slide across the smooth, dark stone. They resemble nothing so much as a patch of living geometry—a moving fractal of interlocking hexagons. They possess no obvious eyes or sensory organs, only thousands of microscopic, vibrating cilia that taste the mineral density of the water. They graze on the structure itself, secreting a mild acid that dissolves the ancient, synthetic stone into a slurry of metallic compounds. Over eons, as they graze, they contribute to the perfect rounding of the towers, acting as the biological sandpaper that smooths the world into spheres and cylinders. When the currents become too violent, they clamp down, their plates locking seamlessly together to become indistinguishable from the cliff face, waiting for the chaos to subside.

Drifting in the open water, filling the void between the towers, are the **Vectors**. These are the silvery, needle-thin things that flash like sudden thoughts. They are devoid of scales, possessing instead a skin of polished, fluid metal—chromium and mercury textures that offer no purchase for the water’s friction. They have evolved for an existence of pure momentum. Their bodies are rigid spindles, tapering at both ends to needle points, minimizing the drag of the viscous liquid. They do not swim so much as vibrate, utilizing a high-frequency oscillation of a dorsal muscle to glide through the heavy water with terrifying efficiency.

They possess a collective intelligence. A school of Vectors is not a loose aggregation of fish but a rigid, shifting lattice. When they turn, they do not scatter; they rotate in unison, maintaining a precise distance from one another, a cloud of silver specks moving as a single, fluid machine. They feed on the suspended precipitate—the "dust" of the dying world—and on the smaller, gelatinous drifters that float in the gloom. To watch them is to watch data being processed in real-time, a living visualization of the water’s turbulent algorithms.

Hiding in the crannies where the synthetic plates of the megastructure have buckled under the pressure are the **Hydra-Spiders**. These are multi-limbed ambush predators, nightmare creatures of spindle-legs and gripping pincers. Because the surfaces here are smooth and vertical, they cannot rely on friction; instead, their limbs terminate in powerful, magnetic suckers that adhere to the ferrous content of the artificial stone. They are perfectly flat, their bodies compressed like a disc, allowing them to slide into the hairline cracks between the "built" blocks. They wait with the patience of the dead, their bodies vibrating in sync with the low-frequency thrum of the currents. When a Vector school passes too close, they do not lunge; they simply release their magnetic hold and drift, opening wide, cavernous maws that engulf the prey by simply flowing into it.

But the true masters of this dark aquarium are the **Leviathans**, the vast, plate-like shapes seen in the distance. They are the ultimate expression of mimicry in a world built by giants. To the unobservant eye, a Leviathan is simply a section of the wall that has decided to move. They are massive, flat-bodied creatures, shaped like the shields of ancient warriors or the hull plating of a dreadnought. Their skin is thick, rubbery, and textured exactly like the eroded stone of the spires—pocked, cratered, and colored in deep bruising purples and charcoal greys.

They possess no discernible head or tail, only a long, undulating ribbon of muscle and armor. They navigate not by sight, but by sonar and pressure sensitivity, using the "quiet violence" of the world as their map. They move with a terrifying, silent grace, gliding between the towers like ghosts in a machine. Their eyes are not simple organs, but vast, sensory arrays running along their lateral lines—pits of bioluminescent gel that pulse with a faint, violet light, illuminating the immediate darkness. They feed on the turbulence itself, filtering the microscopic life churned up by the colliding currents, and occasionally, with a slow, grinding motion of vast, internal jaws, consuming a drifting cluster of Lith-Feeders. They are the living mountains of this place, mobile monoliths that remind the smaller creatures that even here, in the ghost of a machine, there is something bigger, something slower, and something hungrier.

Finally, darting through the periphery of one's vision, are the **Echoes**. These are small, translucent spheres, barely visible, resembling air bubbles in their buoyancy. They are not solid creatures, but semi-organic sacks of gel that maintain a perfect internal pressure, defying the crushing weight of the deep. They do not eat; they do not seem to live in a biological sense. They simply exist, resonating with the low-frequency vibrations of the megastructure. When the "grinding tectonic plates" of current collide, the Echoes pulse brighter, syncing their rhythm with the planet's thrum. They are the memory of the world, the ambient biological background noise, drifting in the high, lonely dark, waiting for a silence that will never come.
