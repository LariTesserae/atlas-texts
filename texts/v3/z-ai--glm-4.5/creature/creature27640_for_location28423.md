To exist in the Green Hall is to be a component, a cog in the vast, wet machinery of the place. There is no "nature" here in the sense of a chaotic struggle for survival; rather, the fauna has evolved into a workforce, specialized laborers maintaining the eternal hum of the biological factory.

**The Wall-Walkers (The Maintenance Crews)**
Clinging to the immense, polished pillars are the primary custodians of the hall. They are multi-legged, flat-bodied creatures resembling a cross between a crab and a commemorative plaque—their carapaces are smooth, unsegmented, and colored the deep, bruised purple of old wood. They possess no obvious eyes, having evolved in the shadowless, gray-green gloom. Instead, they navigate through a dense sensitivity to vibration and the chemical signatures of decay.

Their front limbs have evolved into dual-purpose tools: a hardened, scraping blade and a delicate, tweezer-like feeler. They move in jerky, synchronized choreography along the vertical surfaces. When they encounter a patch of slow-growing moss or a fungal intrusion that threatens the efficiency of the bark, they descend upon it with the rapid-fire *click-click-click* of mandibles, stripping the surface clean before moving on. They are the sanders of the world, ensuring the ancient wood remains smooth and unimpeded. They communicate through sharp, percussive chirps that echo against the pillars, sounding like the clatter of metal tools dropped on a concrete floor.

**The Siphon-Moths (The Thermals)**
The air is filled with the "electric whine" mentioned earlier. This sound comes from the Siphon-Moths, creatures with wingspans as wide as a man’s hand but bodies no thicker than a pencil. Their wings are not made of soft powder like earthly moths; they are glassine, rigid, and solar-transparent, vibrating at a frequency that creates the constant, high-pitched drone.

They do not land. They are perpetual fliers, hovering in the columns of humid air rising from the floor. They are biological heat exchangers. Their long, proboscis-like tongues are permanently inserted into the steam vents of the giant flowers that hang from the canopy. They suck in the superheated, mineral-rich vapor, their internal cooling systems condensing it into pure water which they then rain down upon the root-floor below. A swarm of Siphon-Moths looks like a living cloud of static, their wings catching the diffuse light, turning the damp air into a shimmering, vibrating haze.

**The Root-Ballasts (The Floor-Dwellers)**
On the spongy, woven floor live heavy, slow-moving orbs of muscle and chitin known as Ballasts. They are low to the ground, looking like smoothed river stones until they extend their six powerful, stubby legs. They are the compactors of the Green Hall. As the mycelial lattice grows and swells with the moisture, the Ballasts slowly traverse the floor, their immense weight pressing the sponge down, maintaining the flat, paved integrity of the ground.

They are silent operators. Their movement is a slow, heavy lurch—*shhh-thud, shhh-thud*—that provides a rhythmic bass note beneath the thrum of the walls. They graze not on plants, but on the excess moisture and slime that coats the floor, filtering the water and excreting dense, dry pellets that patch holes in the root-pavement. They are immune to the damp; they are the anchors that keep the hall from dissolving into a swamp.

**The Valve-Spiders (The Mechanics)**
The giant, sedan-sized flowers are not autonomous. They are operated.

Living inside the petals, hidden deep within the vibrant, hissing interiors, are the Valve-Spiders. These are skeletal, almost robotic-looking arachnids with elongated limbs. They sit directly atop the flower's "hydraulic" nodes—the swollen bases of the petals. The *clack-clack-clack* heard throughout the hall is the sound of these spiders physically cranking the flower’s mechanisms open and shut to regulate heat release.

When the internal pressure of the flower becomes too great, the Valve-Spiders engage their ratchet-legs, leveraging their body weight to force the petals open. They are covered in a heat-resistant, silver-colored fur that steams constantly as they work. They are the only creatures that seem to possess "personality"; if a flower fails to open, a Valve-Spider will strike the mechanism aggressively with a specialized club-leg, banging on the flower like a frustrated mechanic fixing a stubborn engine.

**The Strainers (The Giants)**
High in the canopy, invisible in the darkness above, live the Strainers. These are massive, filter-feeding entities, resembling immense, semi-transparent jellyfish that float in the upper reaches of the hall. They have no moving parts; they simply float, tethered by long, sticky cables to the ceiling vines.

They catch the dust, spores, and stray insects that rise on the heat currents, digesting them slowly. Their presence is announced only by their "respiration"—a deep, resonant belching sound that ripples down every few minutes. This is the expulsion of compressed, filtered air, the "digestive flora" clearing its throat. The sound is so deep it vibrates in the chest of anyone standing below, a reminder that the ceiling above is not empty space, but the roof of a living lung.

**The Ecosystem as a Whole**
There is no predator and prey dynamic here. The Siphon-Moths are not eaten by the Wall-Walkers; the Ballasts are not hunted. The inhabitants of the Green Hall are symbiotic to the point of being a single, distributed organism. The Wall-Walkers clean the machine; the Valve-Spiders run the pistons; the Siphon-Moths manage the cooling; the Ballasts maintain the foundation; the Strainers filter the air.

They live in the "end of life" state because they have transcended the need to struggle. They are the biological automata of a world that solved the problem of survival eons ago, leaving them only with the task of maintenance. They are loud, busy, and blind, perfectly adapted to a humid, mechanical eternity.
