To live in the Gear-Sump of Oakhaven is to exist in a state of permanent negotiation with the impossible. The inhabitants of this damp, resonant chamber are not merely surviving the environment; they have evolved to embody the strange synthesis of flesh and piston, of root and rivet.

**The Tuners (Humanoid Inhabitants)**
The primary residents are a stoic, industrial subculture known as the Tuners. They are a people shaped by the acoustic architecture of their home. Over generations, the constant *thrum* has altered their physiology; their cheekbones are high and wide, acting as natural baffles to protect the inner ear, and they possess a unique, thickened cartilage in their throats that allows them to sub-vocalize—to speak in rumbling frequencies that cut through the mechanical noise, communicating through the vibration of their bones rather than the air.

They dress in layers of oil-treated wool and heavy, waxed canvas that squeaks when they move, adding a small counter-rhythm to the dominant soundscape. Their eyes are perpetually narrowed, not against the light, but against the constant spray of condensation. They do not walk so much as they trudge, moving in a heavy, heel-to-toe gait that mimics the slow, deliberate cadence of the excavators.

The Tuners do not build houses. To build a foundation here is an act of futility, as the earth beneath them is "yesterday." Instead, they inhabit the "interstitial spaces"—the hollow catwalks suspended inside the smokestacks, the corrugated metal sheds bolted to the sides of gear-towers, and the brickworks that cling to the limestone cliffs like barnacles. Their living spaces are vertical, cluttered with hammocks and drying racks for moss-harvest. They are nomads within a fixed space, constantly migrating as the machines devour the ground they walked on yesterday.

**The Steel-Plumaged Avians**
High above, perched on the sagging high-tension wires, live the *Copper-Cliffe Rooks*. These are massive, heavy birds, flightless in the traditional sense but capable of long, controlled glides on the thermal updrafts created by the steam vents. Their plumage is a biological marvel; the feathers are not keratin but a metallic, enamel-like substance that mimics the surrounding copper piping.

In the overflow ditches and the stagnant pools where the artificial rain collects, the *Sump-Bellied Newts* make their kingdom. These are amphibians the size of dinner plates, with skin that possesses the texture and sheen of wet, rusted iron. They are camouflage made flesh; when they sit motionless on the mud, they look exactly like discarded scrap metal.

They are ambush predators, feeding on the sleek, black mammals that scurry too close to the water’s edge. Their croak is not a sound of air, but a hydraulic *hiss*, generated by contracting their throats rapidly, forcing water out of specialized vents. This hiss syncopates perfectly with the *clack-hiss* of the pistons, a sonic camouflage that renders them invisible to all but the Tuners, who know the rhythm of the machine better than they know their own hearts.

**The Green Rust (Aggressive Flora)**
Perhaps the most alien inhabitants are the *Velvet-Strangler Vines*. These are not passive plants but active, aggressive colonizers. They are electric green, spongy to the touch, and they "eat" the infrastructure. The vines wrap around the pistons and gear-troughs, secreting a mild acid that weakens the metal, allowing the vine to sink roots into the iron itself.

During the steam-release cycles, these plants "bloom." When the scalding vapor rises, the moss opens its pores to catch the minerals in the steam, glowing with a faint, bioluminescent heat. The Tuners harvest this moss carefully, wearing thick gauntlets, to boil into a potent, nutrient-rich broth. It is a dangerous harvest; to touch the moss during a steam release is to suffer burns that look remarkably rust-like.

**The Grazing Excavators**
Finally, there are the machines. While the Tuners operate them, the dormant excavators—those massive, wheeled beasts sitting in the gravel plains—are considered by the locals to be a form of megafauna. The Tuners speak of the machines as if they were livestock, referring to them by temperamental personalities. The excavators are often draped in moss, birds nesting in their treads, their metal skins sweating condensation. They are the "anchor species" of the Gear-Sump; without them churning the earth, creating the raw stone, the moss would have no purchase, and the cycle of erosion would halt. They are the architects of the chaos, and in their dormancy, they look like great, iron mountains waiting to wake.
