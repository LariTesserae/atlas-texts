To live in the Wet Throat is to exist in the belly of a beast that is digesting time itself. The inhabitants here do not merely survive the environment; they are physically authored by it.

They are known, if they are known at all, as the **Lag-Glass**.

They are not introduced to the eye all at once. In the bioluminescent gloom, they possess no distinct silhouette. You look at the wall of warm, nanite-stone, and the wall looks back—a section of the grey, seamless curve detaching itself with a sound like wet velvet tearing.

**Physique and Texture**
The Lag-Glass are humanoid only in the broadest sense of bipedalism, though their proportions are twisted by the verticality of the Spire. They are elongated, their arms reaching past their knees, fingers fused into tapering, spatulate tips designed to splay across the vibrating curves of the tunnel walls. They have no feet, only reversed knees that end in prehensile, root-like clusters of cartilage that grip the grating without a sound.

Their skin is the colour of deep bruising—a mottled, translucent purple-grey. It is slick, not with sweat, but with a bio-oil secretion that matches the hydrophobic nature of the floor. They leave no footprints, only a temporary disruption in the dust which the water instantly washes away. This skin is thick, resembling wet silicone, absorbing the constant *whump-whump* of the sub-sonic drivers. You can see the vibration traveling through them; ripples run continuously across their backs and shoulders, distorting their forms like a reflection in a troubled pool.

**The Sensory Apparatus**
They have no eyes. In a place where light lags and darkness is absolute, vision is a liability. Instead, their faces are dominated by a vertical, slit-like opening that runs from the hairline down to the upper lip. This is not a mouth, but a massive, hyper-sensitive tympanic organ.

They do not hear the roar; they drink it. The great *whump* of the ventilation drivers acts as their heartbeat, regulating their metabolism. When the great fans cycle down, they slow, becoming lethargic and almost statuesque. When the bass kicks up, they shudder with manic energy.

Their "teeth"—the reference point for any traveler feeling the vibration—are actually bony plates lining the inner throat. These plates rattle constantly against one another, creating a secondary, harmonic frequency. This is how they communicate. They do not speak; they vibrate. A conversation between two Lag-Glass sounds like two pieces of grinding metal, a dissonant symphony that travels through the iron grating rather than the air.

**The Temporal Lag**
The most unsettling aspect of the Lag-Glass is their interaction with the "ghost water."

Because the water that coats the Wet Throat possesses a temporal reflection delay, the inhabitants have evolved to perceive time non-linearly. They hunt by watching the walls, not the open air. When an Echo-Mite scurries across a pipe, the Lag-Glass does not look at the animal; it looks at the dripping water film on the wall beside it. It sees the ripples that show where the mite *was* a second ago.

Consequently, the Lag-Glass moves with a disjointed, anticipatory grace. They will step aside before you have rounded a corner, having already "felt" the vibration of your footsteps through the water’s delayed reflection on the wall. To an outsider, their movement looks jerky and impossible, as if they are editing themselves out of reality frame by frame. They seem to stutter through space, vanishing and reappearing a few feet closer, always compensating for the one-second delay of their world.

**Behavior and Ecology**
They are solitary scavengers, the custodians of the ghost town. They do not build; they inhabit the spaces left behind, sleeping in the hammocks made of rusted wire that hang from the cathedral-sized vents.

They are omnivores of the mechanical. Using their spatulate fingers, they scrape the pulsating moss from the walls, eating the vibro-synthetic fungi which glows in their translucent stomachs. But they also consume metal. They gnaw on the non-ferrous grating, filing down the iron to supplement their diet with the minerals needed to maintain their vibrating bone plates.

When the "scheduled rain" comes—the sudden deluge from the shifting vents—the Lag-Glass do not hide. They tilt their elongated heads back and open their cranial slits, letting the warm water flush out their sensory organs. It is their only moment of stillness. Standing statuesque in the downpour, illuminated by the violet shift of the power cycle, they look for all the world like the forgotten idols of the machine, shaking in the dark.

**The Presence**
To encounter a Lag-Glass is to feel a profound sense of uncanny valley. They mimic the warmth of the room, the vibration of the air, and the slickness of the walls. If you stood still on the catwalk and closed your eyes, you might not know if one was standing inches from you, smelling of ozone and wet stone, listening to the rattle of your teeth, waiting for the water to show them who you used to be.

They are the echoes of the Wet Throat, given flesh. They are the city’s memory of itself, vibrating endlessly in the dark.
