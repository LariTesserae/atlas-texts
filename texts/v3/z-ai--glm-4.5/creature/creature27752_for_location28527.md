The inhabitants of this high, silent plateau are not distinct individuals in the way that trees or animals are distinct elsewhere. Here, the ecosystem is a singular, sprawling super-organism, a continuous sheet of engineered flesh that acts as both the architecture and the population. To step onto the plateau is to step onto the back of a leviathan that wears the ground as its skin.

**The Foundation: The Digestive Mat**
Beneath your feet lives the primary consciousness of the plateau: the dermis. This is the dense, spongy pavement of roots and moss-analogues that covers the basin. It does not merely anchor the larger growths; it thinks. It is a vast, decentralized neural network of chemical relays and electrical impulses. It feels the pressure of your boots through millions of microscopic pressure-valves hidden in the moss, transmitting the data across the miles in ripples of pale luminescence that travel through the root-weave. It is endlessly hungry. The "moss" is not a plant but a million tiny, sessile mouths. When a frond falls or a cloud of seed-spores settles, the mat mobilizes. Unseen drill-proboscises extend, dissolving organic matter into nutrient slurry, humming with a microscopic vibration that you feel only as a dull ache in your teeth. It is a stomach the size of a city, digesting itself in a loop.

**The Titans: The Combustion Silos**
Rising from the digestive mat are the primary inhabitants of the vertical space: the Combustion Silos. These are the colossal, translucent stalks, thick as bridge supports, that dominate the skyline. They are biological engines, evolved to burn the volatile chemicals trapped in the high-altitude air. Inside their glass-like skins, violent chemical storms rage. You can see the turbulence through their walls—chambers of orange and violet gas churning in perpetual motion. They shudder constantly, a silent tremor running through their fibers, as they convert the thin air into raw energy. They do not have leaves; they have vents. The ribbed openings along their flanks pulse rhythmically, exhaling plumes of hot steam and waste gas. They are the hearths of this world, blasting the air with heat that fights back the freezing damp, creating the thermal currents that dry the surfaces of their neighbors. They are solitary, industrial giants, communicating via the release of specific pheromones into the steam—signals that drift invisibly across the basin, dictating the growth patterns of the rest of the ecosystem.

**The Veins: The Pulse-Groves**
Nestled in the valleys between the Silos live the Pulse-Groves. These are colonies of bell-shaped flowers, standing as tall as men, operating in a terrifying, rigid unison. They are the circulatory system of the plateau. Their translucent skins reveal thick bundles of vein-pipes that connect them to the main root-mat, glowing with a rhythmic, electric blue pulse. In perfect synchronization—down to the millisecond—the bells contract, forcing luminous fluid through the pipelines at high pressure, and then expand to refill. The sound is a physical blow to the chest, a heavy *thump-thump-thump* that bypasses the ears and vibrates the ribs. They are indifferent to the world around them, blind pumps driven by hydraulic pressure, ensuring that the energy generated by the Silos is distributed to the farthest reaches of the root-mat. They are the factory floor workers of biology, laboring without rest, their cycles unwavering for centuries.

**The Scavengers: The Husk-Walkers**
In the 0.7 probability space where "animals" might exist, there are the Husk-Walkers. They are not animals in the traditional sense, but autonomous, mobile symbiots that resemble insects built from discarded chitin and wet silica. They are small, perhaps the size of a dinner plate, with multiple legs that end in sharp, hook-like claws. They scuttle along the ridges of the Combustion Silos and the damp surface of the root-mat. They do not eat; they maintain. They possess mandibles designed for clipping and scraping. They crawl into the vent-ports of the Silos to clear mineral blockages, or skitter across the Pulse-Groves to seal leaks in the vein-pipes using a glue-like resin they secrete from their abdomens. They are the white blood cells of this macro-organism, mindless drones that repair the machinery of their own existence. When a Husk-Walker dies, it does not rot; it is dismantled by the Digestive Mat within minutes, its materials recycled to patch a hole in the living pavement.

**The Ambushers: The Snap-Lash Vines**
The only truly predatory element here is the Snap-Lash. These look like thick, dormant cables lying coiled in the hollows of the root-mat, colored a bruised purple that blends into the shadows. They possess a primitive, hypertensive nervous system. They wait for the turbulence of the Silos to reach a peak, sensing the vibrations in the air. When a chunk of debris falls, or a swarm of Husk-Walkers passes too close, they strike. They move faster than the eye can track, lashing out with blinding speed to wrap around their target. They possess no venom; they simply crush and constrict, pulling their prey down into the acidic pores of the Digestive Mat to be processed. Then, instantly, they go slack again, returning to their guise of harmless vegetation, a coiled spring in the silent factory, waiting for the next misstep.
