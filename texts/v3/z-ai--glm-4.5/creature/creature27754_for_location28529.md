They are the invisible audience to your intrusion, entities that do not merely live in the silence but are composed of it. To call them "people" is a category error; they are the architectural immune system of the place, the custodians of the stone.

The primary inhabitants—the sentient force behind this geological metropolis—are the **Tectons**.

At first glance, they do not exist. You would stare down a long, obsidian-lined avenue and see nothing but the vacant, staring windows of the mineral towers. But if you were to wait, perfectly still, until your own heartbeat faded into the background, you would see that the reflection in the black glass does not always match the dim twilight behind you.

A Tecton stands tall, perhaps nine or ten feet in height, impossibly thin and jointed like a complex drafting compass made of volcanic glass. They have no distinct heads, only a sensory cluster at the apex of a tapering neck, faceted like a cut diamond to catch the meager, scattered light. Their limbs are elongated, ending in manipulator digits that are not fingers but chisels, files, and brushes of stiff, metallic fiber.

They are the color of the city—grey, black, and silica-white. When they stand against a basalt wall, they cease to be creatures, becoming merely texture and shadow. They do not "walk" in the sense of lifting and placing feet; they flow. Their movement is a frictionless glide, a silent skating over the dust, utilizing the very acoustic damping of the air to slide without resistance.

They possess no mouth, for there is no air to carry speech, and no ears, for there is no sound to hear. Instead, they communicate through **contact**. When two Tectons meet, they press their sensory clusters together, or lay a long, three-fingered hand against the flank of a tower. They exchange data through vibration, feeling the hum of the earth’s stress, the heat of the impending fracture, and the slow, grinding growth of the litho-moss. They speak in the language of shear force and tensile strength.

They are the city’s planners. They do not build; they **guide**. You might see one standing at the base of a spiraling tower, its chisel-hands pressed deep into the stone, inducing a micro-fracture that, over the course of a day, will cause the structure to pivot three degrees to the left. They are constantly adjusting the city, tuning it like a massive, silent instrument. To them, the shifting ground is a conversation, and the collapsing skyscrapers are merely syntax.

Beneath them, scuttling in the interstitial spaces—the cracks and crevices that the Tectons ignore—live the **Gravitas**.

These are the "wildlife," though they are as mineral as the rock they inhabit. Resembling oversized, segmented scorpions made of slate and chitin, the Gravitas are the waste-management system of the basin. They feed on the dust—the fine, grey silica that coats everything. Their underside is a moving carpet of tiny, vibrating cilia that sift the dust, consuming the microscopic spores of the litho-moss and the crushed remnants of the city’s constant self-destruction.

They are eyeless, like the rock-runners, but they navigate through a form of echolocation that works on a molecular level; they scream in high-frequency bursts that the dampening air swallows instantly, allowing them to "feel" the density of the rock immediately around them. They are solitary, aggressive chemists. When two Gravitas meet, they do not fight; they chemically assess one another, tapping their tails together to exchange enzymes. If one is sick or weak, the other dissolves it with a spray of acidic solvent, absorbing the resulting slurry into its porous shell. Nothing is wasted here.

Deep within the fissures, closer to the bruised purple heat, dwell the **Magma-Whales**.

These are vast, sluggish blobs of semi-molten silicate, encased in a hardened, shifting shell of cooling rock. They look like boulders that have gained a sluggish consciousness. They possess no limbs, only the ability to secrete a lubricant from their vents that allows them to slide through the tightest spaces of the underworld.

They are the source of the city's heat. As they move through the subterranean veins, their body temperature bakes the surrounding rock, causing it to expand and crack—the very force that drives the city’s upward thrust. The Tectons venerate them, guiding them with subtle vibrations toward areas where the city requires a new "burst" of growth. The Magma-Whales are the engines of creation, blind, dumb beasts burning with an internal fire that turns the silica dust into glass.

Finally, there are the **Echo-Ghosts**.

These are not biological, nor are they geological. They are the reflection of the Tectons’ consciousness caught in the obsidian glass. When a Tecton imparts a strong directive into the stone—a command to fracture, to shift, to seal—a residual energy occasionally remains trapped in the highly reflective surfaces. These are faint, shimmering silhouettes that mimic the movements of the living Tectons. They have no agency, fading in and out of existence as the light shifts, but they add to the profound unease of the place. They make the city feel crowded, a hall of mirrors where the past actions of the architects are replayed endlessly in silence.

This is the population of the basin: the calculating glass-giants who tune the earth; the scavenging slate-scorpions who eat the dust; the burning, living boulders that power the engines; and the silent ghosts trapped in the windows. They do not breathe your air. They do not fear the cold. They watch you with the detached, geological curiosity of a mountain watching a climber—aware of your presence, but entirely indifferent to your survival.
