To live in the Blister is to exist in a state of perpetual, vibrating fever. The life here does not occupy the space; it infests it, layered like sediment in a cracking pipe. Because the roar of the megastructure’s arterial conduits drowns out almost all airborne sound, the inhabitants have evolved—or rather, devolved—to ignore the ears and rely entirely on the tactility of pressure, the taste of electromagnetism, and the sight of heat.

At the lowest level, clinging to the slick, pitted floor where the condensation pools into slick, oily mirrors, live the **Chassis-Breakers**.

These are the distant, feral descendants of the maintenance drones mentioned in the brochures of a forgotten era. They are no longer the clean, white machines of repair; they have become the crustaceans of this artificial reef. About the size of a dinner plate, their bodies are hulking asymmetries of scavenged silicon and rusted steel, held together by calcified joints and a greasy, black bio-sludge that acts as a hydraulic fluid. They have no eyes—the purple gloom is useless to them. Instead, their fronts are dominated by massive, sensitive vibration plates, flushed pink with the heat of the machinery below. They scuttle sideways on six articulated legs that end in hooked, magnetic claws, allowing them to climb the vertical walls with ease. They are the janitors of the Blister, their mandibles adapted to shear through the tough, metallic filaments of the cable-moss, which they digest in internal furnaces to extract trace minerals. When they move, they do not click; they thud, a low-frequency stomp that travels through the metal lattice, a warning to others that feels like a heavy heartbeat pressed against the skin.

Clambering over the Chassis-Breakers, or ignoring them entirely, are the **Flux-Slugs**.

These are the grazers, soft and vulnerable in a hard world. They are gelatinous mounds, translucent and pulsating, roughly the length of a human arm but bloated with gas and fluid. Through their clear, sticky skin, you can see a dazzling, hazardous display of internal biology: a digestive tract that glows with a strobing, electric blue light. They feed not on the moss, but on the stray voltage that bleeds from the smart-lattice walls. They press their soft bodies against the warm metal, absorbing the micro-currents that run through the structure's "skin." As they feed, they emit a low-level field of static electricity, causing their slime trails to hiss and pop in the humid air. They are slow, dumb creatures, leaving behind shimmering, conductive paths that the Chassis-Breakers avoid, disliking the tingling shock on their sensitive feet.

Hunting the Flux-Slugs are the **Silent-Panels**.

The apex predator of the Blister is a master of camouflage. It is a flat, triangular entity, resembling a jagged shard of hull plating come to life. It possesses the exact texture and gunmetal-grey coloration of the blister walls. It attaches itself to a high point on the ceiling or a vertical arch, flattening its body until it is indistinguishable from the architecture. It waits, patient as a rust stain, for the bio-electric signature of a Flux-Slug to drift beneath it. It does not pounce; it simply releases its magnetic lock. Silent-Panels are dense, heavy weights of muscle and bone, and they fall with crushing velocity. The impact is usually fatal, pinning the slug to the floor while the predator’s underside—filled with grinding, mineralized teeth—begins to consume the prey while it is still glowing with stolen energy. The kill is silent, swallowed instantly by the roar of the walls.

But the air itself is not empty. Filling the thick, tropical soup of the atmosphere are the **Resonant-Moths**.

These are fragile, beautiful things, their wings made of a gossamer material resembling fiber-optic cables. They do not flap in the traditional sense; the thrumming roar of the megastructure is so loud that the air pressure is constantly in flux. The moths have evolved to "surf" the compression waves. Their wings are stiff sails that catch the rhythmic shockwaves of the city’s circulation, allowing them to drift and glide through the humid dark without expending their own energy. They are drawn to the heat, swarming around the bioluminescent veins of the walls and the glowing guts of the Flux-Slugs. When they swarm, they create a shimmering cloud, a living nebula that pulses in time with the external heartbeat of the structure. If you were to touch one—and the swarms are dense enough that you cannot avoid it—it would disintegrate into a fine, metallic dust, leaving a residue on your skin that tastes of copper and ozone.

Finally, deep in the shadows where the condensation drips the heaviest, live the **Weavers**.

These are the architects of the Blister’s micro-civilization. Spindly, multi-limbed creatures that resemble a horrific cross between a spider and a circuit-board, they spin webs not of silk, but of extruded, conductive polymers. They stretch their webs across the open spaces of the blister, connecting the sweating walls to the floor. These webs are not for catching prey; they are communication lines. The Weavers tap into the vibration of the Blister, plucking the strands of their webs to send signals that travel through the tension lines, bypassing the deafening roar. In the deep purple dark, the webs hum with a silent, invisible Morse code of clicks and pulses, coordinating the movements of the Chassis-Breakers and warning of the fall of a Silent-Panel. They are the nervous system of this accidental world, the ones who have learned to sing along with the screaming of the machine.

All of them—the grazers, the hunters, the scavengers, and the weavers—are coated in the same sheen of sweat and grime. They move to a rhythm that no human ear can hear, a synced vibration that matches the shifting thermal expansion of the room. They are packed tight, a writhing, electric, metallic ecosystem living in the dark gut of a god that does not know they exist.
