Life here does not just occupy the Choir of the Ribs; it is composed of it. The inhabitants are less like biological animals and more like biological instruments, evolved over eons to turn the deafening violence of the stone into a language, a shelter, and a food source.

To see them, you have to stop looking with your eyes and start looking for the interruptions in the vibration.

**The Moss-Stalkers**
On the vertical faces of the towers, where the neon moss grows thick and spongy, live the Moss-Stalkers. They are small, arboreal creatures that resemble a cross between a gecko and a lemur, but their physiology is entirely unique to this acoustic hellscape.

They have no visible ears. Instead, their entire skeletal system acts as a resonator. Their bones are hollow, lattice-like structures that hum in sympathetic vibration with the stone pillars. This allows them to cling to the wet, vertical granite without fear of being shaken off; they are essentially vibrating at the same frequency as the mountain.

Their fur is the most striking feature—long, trailing strands of coarse, oily hair that hang heavy with water. This fur serves as a "visual silence" in the shimmering mist. They do not move so much as drift, their motion perfectly synchronized with the swaying of the wind-tossed moss. They feed on the moss itself, scraping the vibrant, pulsating neon layers off the rock with teeth that are flat, resonant plates. When they eat, the vibration of their chewing transmits through the moss, alerting others in the colony, triggering a ripple of feeding that moves up the pillar like a wave.

**The Aeolian Kites**
Drifting through the alleyways and the vast, hollow spaces between the pillars are the Aeolian Kites. You might mistake them for plastic bags or scraps of cloth caught in the updraft, but they are living creatures—jellyfish of the upper atmosphere.

They have no wings. They are comprised of a central, gas-filled sac (lighter than the thin air here) and trailing, ribbon-like tentacles that can stretch ten or twenty feet. They do not flap; they surf.

The Kites have evolved to read the acoustic pressure maps of the plateau. They can feel the invisible waves of high pressure created by the wind rushing over the stone pipes and they ride these currents, hovering in place for hours, completely motionless. Their trailing tentacles are studded with sticky, electro-static nodes designed to snag the levitating water droplets that the sound waves hold suspended. They drift through the mist, "drinking" the floating spheres of water, their transparent bodies turning cloudy blue as they fill. They are silent, ghostly grazers in a screaming storm.

**The Pipe-Dwellers**
The stone pillars are hollow, and within the darkness of these geological wind instruments live the Pipe-Dwellers. They are an offshoot of something avian, though they have forgotten how to fly.

They are pale, eyeless things with vestigial, feather-covered wings that they now use as feelers and rudders. Their legs are powerful, grasping talons with textured pads like sandpaper, allowing them to climb the slick, wet interior of the pillars. They live deep inside the stone, sheltered from the cutting wind, hanging upside down like bats.

The Pipe-Dwellers are the acoustic masters of the Choir. They can modulate the pitch of their home. By widening their wings or bunching their bodies together inside the hollow shafts, they alter the airflow, changing the note the pillar produces. They use this as a form of sonar and communication. The deep, bowel-loosening booms that occasionally shudder through the plateau are not just random weather; they are the Pipe-Dwellers signaling to other towers. They are territorial notes, warning others away from the best pockets of warm air rising from the deep earth.

**The Lithic-Crabs**
Down on the "streets," in the narrow alleys where the wind accelerates to a shriek and the dancing pebbles jitter, live the Lithic-Crabs.

These are heavy, low-slung crustaceans with shells made of a material indistinguishable from the grey-brown basalt of the plateau. They are scavengers, feeding on the detritus that falls from the moss mats above or the carcasses of Kites that get dashed against the stone.

But their most defining behavior is their obsession with the geometric patterns of the dancing stones. The Lithic-Crabs are architects of vibration. They use their heavy claws to arrange the loose pebbles on the ground into intricate, circular cairns and geometric runes. They aren't doing this for art; they are building dampeners. By arranging the stones in specific fractals, they create small zones of relative silence—pockets where the jaw-rattling hum cancels itself out. They retreat into these silent circles to sleep and to mate, hiding from the relentless noise of the world. If you see a perfectly balanced stack of stones in a chaotic alley, it is not the wind that made it; it is a crab trying to dream in peace.

**The Gloom-Walker (The Sentinel)**
Finally, there is the apex predator, the Gloom-Walker mentioned in whispers. It is a towering, heron-like creature, standing nearly ten feet tall on legs that are nothing but skeletal muscle and tendon.

It is the only creature that moves freely between the vertical pillars and the horizontal ground. Its legs end in wide, splayed toes that distribute its weight, allowing it to walk atop the thick, spongy moss mats without sinking into the water beneath.

The Walker moves with the "jerky motion" of stop-motion animation, but this is a biological necessity. It freezes in place, locking its joints, to let the wind wash over it, rendering it invisible to the vibration-sensitive senses of its prey. It hunts the Moss-Stalkers.

The Walker's head is a terrifying adaptation. It is long and beaked, but the beak is lined with sensitive bristles. It does not use eyes to hunt; it uses the timing of the echoes. By snapping its beak, it creates a sharp click that bounces off the wet stone and the moss. It listens for the micro-echoes that come from the wet, shivering bodies of the Moss-Stalkers. In a world of deafening noise, the Gloom-Walker is the master of the silence between the notes, striking with lightning speed when the wind pauses to take a breath.

Together, they form a strange, hidden ecosystem: the drifters catching the water, the climbers eating the vibration-fed moss, the blind singers inside the walls, and the silent hunters moving between them, all living inside the ribs of a screaming earth.
