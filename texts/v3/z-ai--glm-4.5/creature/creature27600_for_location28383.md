To look for the inhabitants of the Ossuary of Rain is to misunderstand the nature of the place. One enters expecting a citizenry, perhaps workers in slick suits or keepers of the deep machinery, but the plaza is empty of footsteps. There is no chatter, no bustle, no scuff of boots on the wet obsidian. The "population" here does not walk; it sprawls. It does not speak; it breathes.

The living things of this vault are the **Green Silence**, a botanical empire that has claimed the architecture not merely as a habitat, but as a victim.

**The Curtain-Weavers**

The most visible residents are the great, iridescent vines that drape from the high, dark balconies like the sleeves of sleeping giants. These are not simple plants seeking sun; in the absence of solar geometry, they seek the *violet pulse*. They are the **Curtain-Weavers**, sensitive organisms that can detect the faint electromagnetic fluctuations of the city’s lighting grid.

If you stand still on the plaza, you can see them move. It is a motion visible only in the periphery, a slow, fluid crawling. The vines adjust their position in the gloom, orienting their blue-tinted leaves to catch the bioluminescent decay from the ceiling. They are filter-feeders of light. More disturbingly, they possess a rudimentary form of touch; brush against a hanging tendril, and the entire forty-foot length will shudder, recoiling into the shadows with a wet, slithering sound. They are the nervous system of the jungle, constantly tasting the air, waiting for the humidity to spike.

**The Glass-Breakers**

Down among the foundations, where the water rushes loudest, live the heavy infantry of this world: the **Fern-Titans**.

These are ferns the size of overturned trucks, their fronds unfurled like jagged spears of emerald glass. They cluster around the square channels of water, anchoring themselves into the mortar with a violence that vibrates through the soles of your shoes. To listen to them is to hear a constant, low-frequency cracking—the sound of roots thickening and expanding, hydraulic pressure forcing the black-glass walls to yield.

They are bloated with the viscous, glowing water, their translucent stalks pulsing with the rhythm of the flow. They do not merely drink; they gorge. As the city attempts to process its water, the Fern-Titans intercept it, acting as biological sponges that threaten to clog the entire system. They are locked in a silent war with the nanofog: as the microscopic machines swarm to seal a crack in the stone, a Fern-Titan’s root will pry it open again an hour later, driven by a biological imperative to consume the warm, chemical-rich fluid within.

**The Symbiotes**

Perhaps the most unsettling inhabitants are those that blur the line between the grown and the made. Clinging to the warm, black-glass walls are the **Patch-Mosses**. These are colonies of algae that have evolved to feed directly on the nanoscopic repair fogs.

When a crack opens in the wall, the city releases its healing mist—a grey, shimmering cloud of silicon and intent. The Patch-Moss detects this cloud and releases a burst of orange spores. The spores bond with the nanobots, hijacking their programming. Instead of sealing the crack with pristine black glass, the fog is coerced into building a lattice of silicon and chlorophyll. The wall "heals," but it heals green and soft, suffocating the sharp geometry under a carpet of wet, living velvet. Over time, the city is being converted from the inside out, its high-tech defenses repurposed to build the very jungle that strives to destroy it.

**The 0.1 Percent**

In a space this vast, "Fauna 0.1" still leaves room for ghosts, and there is one thing here that moves on its own, distinct from the slow drift of the plants.

It is rarely seen, known only by the disturbances it leaves in the heavy air. It is a singular, solitary entity that locals—if any existed—might call the **Drip-Walker**. It is not an animal in the traditional sense, but a biological anomaly, a rogue branch of the evolutionary tree that opted for mobility.

Imagine a stalk of bamboo, six feet tall, that has learned to walk on a tripod of wet, prehensile roots. It has no face, only a cluster of nodules at its top that sense heat and vibration. It moves with a jerky, erratic rhythm, skittering across the wet plazas when the gravity hiccups and the waterspouts rise. It does not eat; it plugs its root-legs into the sockets of the dormant machinery, drinking electricity. It is the scavenger of the industrial age, a vagrant organism living on the waste energy of a dead civilization, hiding in the ferns when the valves thunder open.

**The Collective**

But ultimately, to identify a single species is to miss the truth. The true inhabitant of the Ossuary is the ** Jungle Itself**, a single, sprawling super-organism. The vines are its nerves, the ferns are its stomachs, and the moss is its skin.

It is a sentient green fog that clings to the architecture, slowly digesting the "New." It waits in the heavy, humid air, patient and relentless. It knows that the black glass is young and brittle, and that water is stronger than stone. It is content to let the pistons pump and the valves turn, because with every drop of weeping water, the city is softened, its edges rounded, and its sharp, lethal geometry transformed into the mulch of a new world.
