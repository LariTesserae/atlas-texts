In a place where biology has been outlawed by light and chemistry, where the ground is a city of stone and the air is a solid wall of sound, life does not bloom; it calcifies. It does not grow; it accretes. The creatures of the High Drip are not animals in the traditional sense of flesh and blood—they are mobile minerals, biological engines fueled by the dissolution of the landscape itself.

They are as white and hard as the world they inhabit.

**The Grazers: The Glass-Walkers (Skimmers)**
The most common movement in the High Drip belongs to the Skimmers, known to the few who study this place as Glass-Walkers. They are the foundational herbivores of a realm without plants.

To look at a Glass-Walker is to look at a mistake in vision. They are nearly invisible—a composition of translucent chitin and clear, viscous fluids that refract the blinding white light, rendering them as shifting distortions on the wet limestone. They possess no eyes, for sight is useless here; instead, their heads are dominated by massive, vibration-sensitive plates that ripple with the constant roar of the falling water.

They do not walk; they skate. Their legs are tipped with microscopic, hooked setae that grip the wet, mineral-slicked stone with unnatural friction. They move in frantic, sideways dashes, terrified of stillness. This is not just a fear of predators, but a physical necessity: the High Drip is constantly dissolving and re-precipitating. To stand still for too long is to risk being cemented to the pavement, becoming part of the architecture.

They feed on the slime. They scrape the mineral mucous—the precipitating "sweat" of the rock—with serrated mandibles, digesting the heavy metals and sulfates. Their excrement is a finer, purer white dust, which they leave behind in puff clouds as they scuttle, marking their territory in a world of scentless white.

**The Flora: The Organ-Pipes**
In the absence of chlorophyll, the niche of "stationary life-form" is occupied by the Organ-Pipes. These are not animals, strictly speaking, nor are they plants. They are colonies of filter-feeding polyps that secrete a hard, white, tubular exoskeleton identical to the surrounding travertine.

They grow vertically from the wettest crevices, looking for all the world like spears of stone thrusting upward. They are often found in clusters, creating natural wind tunnels—or rather, water tunnels. The Colony possesses a collective intelligence distributed through a root-like system deep in the rock.

They feed on the mist. As the heavy, wet air is forced through the tunnels between the stone spires, the Organ-Pipes extend feathery, translucent tendrils into the flow to catch microscopic particulates and aerosolized minerals carried by the drip. Their true strangeness lies in their resonance: the tubes of their exoskeletons are perfectly tuned to the frequency of the falling water. They vibrate perpetually, a low-frequency hum that adds a bass note to the high-pitched hiss of the Drip. If a Glass-Walker disturbs the correct frequency of vibration, the Organ-Pipes retract their tendrils instantly, snapping shut like stone lips.

**The Predators: The Gargoyles**
High above the slick pavement, perched on the Impossible Bridges and the razor-thin fins of karst, sit the Gargoyles. They are the apex of this sterile food chain.

They are masters of mimicry, evolved to look exactly like the eroded stone formations they hunt upon. A Gargoyle can remain motionless for decades, its skin slowly oxidizing to match the exact shade of wet limestone, growing patches of moss-like mineral deposits on its back (the only non-threatening "moss" in the Drip).

They are enormous, heavy creatures, relying on gravity and the element of surprise. Their wings are not for flying—the air is too thin and the turbulence of the dripping water too chaotic—but for gliding between the towering stone spires. Their primary sense is not touch or sight, but pressure. They can feel the displacement of air caused by a Skimmer running on the ground fifty feet below.

When they hunt, they do not swoop with a screech; a screech would be lost in the roar. They swoop in absolute silence, their wings absorbing sound, their talons outstretched. They strike with the force of a falling rock, shattering the calcite shell of the Skimmer instantly. They consume the prey whole, crushing the chitin and bone, digesting the calcium to reinforce their own stone-like skin.

**The Mystery: The Null-Spheres**
Deep in the tunnels where the light refracts into suspended rainbows, there exists a phenomenon that might be alive, or might be a geological anomaly given agency. These are the Null-Spheres.

They appear as perfect, floating spheres of water, hovering in the mist without falling. They are composed of the hyper-saline, heavy water of the aquifer, but they maintain a shape that defies gravity. Local folklore—among those mad enough to live near the Drip—says they are the "eyes" of the mountain.

Biologically, they are theorized to be massive, single-celled organisms, a form of hyper-evolved slime mold that has internalized the water cycle. They move by rolling, their surface tension so strong they can climb walls. They are dangerous not because they bite, but because they absorb. If a creature touches a Null-Sphere, the sphere’s surface envelopes them, and the intense osmotic pressure begins to leach the fluids and minerals from their body. They are living traps of dehydration, floating ghost-like through the rainbow tunnels, waiting for the unwary to brush against the "beautiful water."

**The Sound of Life**
In the High Drip, there is no birdsong, no chirping of insects, no rustle of leaves. The sound of life is the sound of stone clicking on stone—the skitter of the Glass-Walkers, the grinding snap of the Gargoyles, the low hum of the Organ-Pipes. It is a hard, cold soundscape, indistinguishable from the geologic erosion that surrounds it. To live here is to be just another phase of the rock, moving for a brief, terrifying moment before settling back into the eternal, blinding white.
