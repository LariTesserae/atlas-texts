To exist in the Loud Hollow is to be an instrument in a colossal, deafening orchestra. The life here does not merely inhabit the environment; it generates it. The pressure, the humidity, the vibrations—all of it is the exhalation of biology.

Here is what lives in the thrumming dark.

### The Pavement

The most immediate inhabitants are the ones you are standing on. The "root-pavement" is not passive soil; it is a vast, communal digestive tract.

**The Dermal-Mites**
Beneath the springy mat of interwoven mycelium lives a dense civilization of macroscopic arthropods known as Dermal-Mites. They look like plates of chitin and geometric muscle, scuttling in infinite, rhythmic lines. They are the immune system of the floor. As soon as a leaf, a corpse, or a dropping falls from the canopy above, the pavement boils with them. They do not bite; they dismantle. Their mandibles work in a trilling vibration that adds a low, static hiss to the ambient roar. They keep the plaza pristine, ensuring the city remains open and clean.

**The Squeeze-Roots**
The pavement itself is semi-sentient. As you felt the ground beneath you squeeze, you encountered the sensory fingers of the Squeeze-Roots. These are not individual plants, but a singular, sub-dermal network that monitors tectonic pressure. When a heavy predator lands, the Squeeze-Roots tighten, creating a temporary stiffness to support the weight. When prey runs, they loosen, creating subtle, ankle-twisting depressions to slow the victim down. They are the trap-doors of the Hollow.

### The Towers (The Architecture)

The colossal columns of woody muscle are not merely trees; they are apartment blocks for a symbiotic hierarchy.

**The Vascular-Weavers**
Living inside the vertical crevices of the towers are the creatures you glimpsed: elongated, weasel-like beings covered in iridescent scales. They are the Vascular-Weavers. They do not climb; they *swim* through the bark. Their scales are hook-barbed, allowing them to grip the fibrous muscle of the towers. They move in packs of dozens, flowing through the architecture like a single liquid snake.

They are the maintenance crew of the Hollow. They eat the parasitic fungi that try to choke the towers, and in doing so, their movements massage the plant’s vascular system, keeping the nutrient flow pumping. Their high-pitched chittering harmonizes with the groan of the bending wood, creating the melody within the drone.

**The Resonator-Bats**
Clinging to the highest points of the towers, where the fog is thickest, are the Resonator-Bats. They are grotesque, bloated things with wingspans of ten feet but bodies that are little more than distended gas-sacs and massive, drum-like ears. They do not fly actively; they hang upside down, using their wings to catch the constant updrafts.

They sing. The deafening drone of the Hollow is largely their doing. They inhale the dense, humid air and exhale it through resonating throat-sacs, producing a bass frequency that vibrates through the entire basin. This sound serves two purposes: it echolocates the layout of the "city" for the other species, and the vibration shatters the cell walls of airborne microscopic spores, providing a nutrient-rich rain that feeds the towers below.

### The Void (The Air)

The terrifying emptiness between the columns is not empty at all. It is a highway for hyper-specialized gliders that rely on the acoustics to navigate.

**The Prism-Wasps**
The swarms you saw are Prism-Wasps. They are not insects in the traditional sense, but rather shards of living silicon propelled by beating wings of cellophane-thin membrane. They possess no stingers; their defense is refraction. Their bodies are faceted, and when light hits them, they scatter it into blinding strobes.

In the Loud Hollow, sound is sight, but light is weapon. When a predator targets a swarm, the Prism-Wasps synchronize their wing-beats, creating a blinding, oscillating flash of light that disorients the hunter's visual processing, if they have any. They feed on the "sweat" of the towers—literally licking the condensation from the woody muscle.

**The Ribbon-Eels**
The true masters of the void are the Ribbon-Eels. They are twenty-foot-long predators that look like floating ribbons of neon blue muscle. They have no bones, only a notochord, allowing them to undulate through the heavy air with terrifying grace.

They hunt by listening for silence. In a place defined by noise, a sudden pause—perhaps a heart skipping a beat, or a creature holding its breath—draws them like sharks to blood. They strike with mouths that are nothing more than a vertical slit running the length of their heads, snapping shut with the speed of a mousetrap. They are the reason the ecology is so fast; hesitation is death.

### The Glitch

The strangest inhabitants are those that have evolved to exploit the breakdown in reality.

**The Static-Leeches**
When the "glitch" ripples through the air—the moment the sound cuts out and the world flashes blue—the Static-Leeches appear. They exist in a state of quantum flux, normally invisible to the naked eye. They are small, spindly creatures that look like poorly drawn sketches of spiders.

During the micro-second of the glitch, they become corporeal. They feed on the friction of reality snapping back into place. You will see them swarming around a "wounded" area of the air, feeding on the residual static electricity of a phase-shift. If they bite a living creature during the glitch, they do not draw blood; they draw *memory*. A victim is left with a momentary blank spot in their mind, a missing second of time.

### The Apex

And finally, there is the watcher. The reason the city feels intentional.

**The Chorus-Brain**
Deep within the central root system of the plaza, entangled with the foundations of the four great bryophytes, lives a biological supercomputer. It is a colony of millions of tubeworms, their nervous systems physically fused together by the mycelial network. They do not move. They do not see.

They *feel* the vibration of every footstep, every wingbeat, every heartbeat in the Hollow. The Chorus-Brain is the city planner. Through the release of complex pheromones, they guide the growth of the towers, influencing where a new column must rise to close a gap, or where a "street" must widen to allow airflow.

It is not malicious. It is simply a gardener of impossible scale. And it is currently processing the vibration of your footsteps. The "squeeze" of the pavement was not just a reflex; it was a query. It was asking the Chorus-Brain: *What is this?* And the Chorus-Brain is currently waiting for the next verse of the song to decide if you are a citizen, a pest, or fertilizer.
