To call the entities of this place "inhabitants" is a charitable inaccuracy. "Inhabitant" implies a distinction between the living thing and the container in which it resides. Here, that line is hopelessly blurred. In a city of bone and stone, life has not adapted to the environment; it has mimicked it, until the fauna is geology and the geography is biology.

The first and most populous "species" is something you would likely step on before you saw it. From a distance, the uneven pavement of hexagonal basalt columns appears merely weathered. Up close, however, the pattern disrupts itself. Scattered among the true stones are the **Basalt-Mimics**.

These are not animals in the vertebrate sense. They are solitary, slow-moving gastropods of immense density, their shells evolved to perfectly replicate the texture, color, and hexangular fracturing of the surrounding pavement. They are low, jagged mounds, perhaps three feet across, lacking discernible heads or limbs. They exist in a state of perpetual, glacial motion. If you were to stare at one for an hour—a feat of patience few would attempt in this oppressive gloom—you might see the mineral rugosity of its "back" shift by a fraction of an inch.

They do not eat the vegetation. They are lithovores, consuming the wet, black slime of bacteria that coats the stones and, slowly, the stones themselves. They grind the basalt into paste with an internal muscular hydrostat that feels like grinding tectonic plates. The only sign of life is the sound: a low, gritty *shhh-click* that happens perhaps once an hour, as they adjust their position to better catch the weeping condensation from the walls. They are the clean-up crew of the basin, swallowing decay and mineral dust, leaving behind waste that is indistinguishable from the gravel they traverse.

Higher up, clinging to the slick, bone-like walls of the towering cyclopean structures, live the **Wall-Scabs**.

If the city is a skeleton, the Scabs are its attempt at a healing scab that has gone wrong. They are colonies of soft-bodied, filter-feeders that resemble wet, discarded parchment or strips of pale, internal organs. They adhere to the vertical faces of the towers, clustered around the perpetual drips of water.

They lack eyes, legs, or any defined shape. They look like splashes of wet concrete or melted wax frozen in mid-drip. They possess a network of microscopic cilia on their exposed surface that vibrate in the humid air, creating a localized, barely-perceptible hum—a counter-frequency to the earth’s tectonic groaning. They feed on the water’s mineral content and the ambient radiation bleeding from the stone. When the water flow is heavy, they engorge, swelling from flat patches to bulging, translucent sacs that pulse with a sluggish, gray rhythm. When the water dries, they desiccate instantly, crumbling into a harmless dust that the wind quickly scatters, their spores waiting in the stagnant pools below to reattach and begin the cycle anew. They are the city’s immune system, filtering the poisons out of the air and water, processing the toxicity of the bruise-light into something inert.

But the true masters of this strange ecology, the only creatures that seem to acknowledge the observer with any intent, are the **Mirage-Weavers**.

They dwell in the "impossible" spaces—in the alleyways that loop back upon themselves, or the canals where distance is negotiable. They are creatures of the heat haze and the fractured light. To look directly at a Weaver is to induce a nausea akin to motion sickness; they exist in the periphery, in the corner of the eye.

They are constructs of refracted light and displaced air, roughly bipedal but possessed of a fluid, shifting anatomy that defies physics. They have no skin, only a constantly shifting boundary that shimmers like the air over a desert road. They do not walk; they slide, utilizing the non-Euclidean geometry of the city to traverse miles in a step. One moment they are at the end of a street; the next, they are beside you, having stepped through a fold in spatial reality.

They do not eat. They do not drink. They seem to feed on the "weirdness" itself, the friction between the observer’s mind and the impossible architecture. They are attracted to confusion. When you struggle to understand how a shadow can stretch without a light source, or how a tower can be ten feet away and an hour’s walk simultaneously, the Weaver draws closer. They are electromagnetic parasites of cognition. They "drink" the cognitive dissonance of the observer.

They are silent. They produce no vibration. If you see one—if the ripple in the air coalesces into a tall, slender, headless abstraction of a human form—it is best to close your eyes. To look at them is to invite them to synchronize with your neural rhythm. They do not attack; they merely observe, their presence causing a dampening of the spirit, a slowing of the heart, a sensation of one’s own thoughts becoming as thick and sluggish as the humid air. They are the gallery guards of this dead museum, ensuring that the confusion of the living remains pure and unpolluted by understanding.

Finally, in the deepest stagnant pools, where the water is black as ink and still as glass, there lies the **Depth-Blank**.

The fauna rating is zero, the assessment claimed, and it was technically correct. The Depth-Blank is not a creature; it is a single, vast, colonial organism that exists *within* the water of the entire basin. It is a mat of microbial life, a biofilm that has achieved a kind of diffuse consciousness. It covers the bottoms of the canals, a thick, gray sludge that, if disturbed, ripples with a coordinated, muscular wave.

It has no mouth, but it absorbs. If a Basalt-Mimic dies and falls into a canal, the Depth-Blank envelops it within hours, dissolving the shell and absorbing the soft tissue. It is the stomach of the city. It thinks in geological time. Its "thoughts" are the chemical shifts in the water’s pH, the release of gases that bubble up to the surface with a soft *plop*, adding to the heavy atmosphere.

It is the reason the silence is so expectant. The Depth-Blank is waiting. It is slowly, over millennia, filtering the city, digesting the minerals, cleaning the bone-structures, preparing for a return of life that will never come. It is a patient, hungry mud, dreaming in the dark, connected by a network of chemical signals running through every stagnant puddle in the bruised gloom.
