They are the Tenders, the damp-glide folk who move through the Boiler-Grove not as masters of their domain, but as components within it, as essential to the mechanism as the moss that lubricates the gears.

To see them is to spot a discrepancy in the texture of the mist—a silhouette against the steam where no silhouette should be. They are encased in "skins" that are not stitched leather or canvas, but layers of accumulated grease, soot, and natural secretion that have hardened into a flexible, waxy carapace. This coating protects them from the humid, metallic air, which would otherwise choke the lungs of any outsider. Over time, the Tenders have become chemically indistinguishable from their environment; their sweat carries a high iron content, and their pores have adapted to excrete the excess minerals they inhale, leaving a faint, salty crust on their brows.

They are not tall, but they are wide—solid, low-center-of-gravity physiques evolved for navigating the spongy, unstable root-floors. Their legs are bowed and powerful, designed to straddle the massive, slowly turning flywheels without stumbling. Their hands are their most defining feature: broad, spatulate fingers with calluses thick enough to turn a riveted bolt by hand or strip a heavy vine without a blade. They do not carry tools; their harnesses are their tool belts, laden with hooks, pry-bars, and valves that hang from their waists like the organs of some metal beast, clinking softly as they walk.

Movement here is an act of reverence and necessity. The Tenders move with the agonizingly slow deliberation of the machines they serve. To rush is to risk falling out of sync with the thrum. They step in time with the piston pumps—*clank-hiss, step-and-sway*—their weight settling into the root-mat just as the vibration subsides, minimizing the disturbance to the fragile equilibrium of the grove. Watching a Tender work is hypnotic: they will spend an hour adjusting a single pressure valve, turning the wheel with microscopic increments, listening not with their ears but with the bones in their jaw, waiting for the perfect pitch in the machine’s exhale.

Their society is silent. The constant roar of the heat-exchange tower makes speech impossible, and so they have evolved a complex language of touch and vibration. A hand on a vibrating pipe conveys a warning; a specific sequence of taps on a fellow Tender’s shoulder signals a blockage in the northern flues. They communicate through the soles of their boots, sensing the deep, resonant shudders of the earth that precede a steam vent opening. They "hear" the distress of the grove before it manifests as a visible problem.

They are symbiotic with the vegetation that strangles the architecture. The Tenders do not clear the Boa-constrictor vines; they braid them. They weave the creeping roots around the crumbling brickwork, guiding the plant-life to act as mortar and support. They harvest the thick, oily algae that slicks the lower rungs of the infrastructure, pressing it into cakes that serve as their food. It is a nutrient-dense, bitter sludge that fuels their slow-burning metabolism, allowing them to work for twenty-hour shifts without exhaustion.

The most revered of the Tenders are the "Divers"—those skilled enough to descend into the water-table itself. They strip off their waxy outer layers and slip into the opaque, oily water that pools beneath the floorboards. They swim in the dark, tightening the submerged bolts of the great foundations, clearing the intake filters of the hydraulic pumps, and communicating through a series of tugs on a rope held by their surface partners. When they emerge, dripping and shivering, their skin is stained a deep, bruised black by the water, a mark of honor that never fades.

They do not age as outsiders do. The constant humidity preserves their flesh, while the heavy labor and the rich, metallic diet hardens their bones. The elders of the Boiler-Grove are barely distinguishable from the machinery they tend—joints that creak and click with rust, breath that whistles like a steam valve, eyes clouded by cataracts that look like frosted glass. They are a people arrested in time, living fossils in a place that refuses to decay, eternal keepers of the slow, wet fire that burns at the heart of the world.
