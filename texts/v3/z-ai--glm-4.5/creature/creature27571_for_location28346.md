Here, life does not grow; it accretes. It is a biological sedimentary layer, caking the surfaces of the infinite city like a living crust. To look out over the Riven Shelf is to watch a fever dream of motion, a writhing carpet of flesh that coats the iron ribs of the world.

At the foundational level of this ecosystem are the **Oxidation Grazers**. These are not the delicate insects of a temperate garden, but armored, hexapodal beetles the size of a human hand. Their carapaces are dull, pitted metal—ceramic-plate steel evolved to mimic the very flooring they traverse. They move in swirling rivers of iridescent black and oil-slick green, flowing around the massive stanchions of the machinery like water around a stone. They are silent, save for the clicking of a million jagged legs on concrete. They do not eat plants; there are none. Instead, they possess rasp-like mandibles capable of scouring the slow-growing rust and mineral patina from the girders. They are the geology’s immune system, constantly consuming the decay of the city, converting the oxidation of the industrial age into the chitin of their own bodies.

Rising above the grazers are the **Conduit-Stalkers**, the reptilian lords of the verticality. These are massive, slow-moving creatures that evoke the primitive image of a crocodile, but stretched and elongated to fit the industrial geometry. Their scales are not organic shields, but hardened keratin plates that perfectly mimic the heads of rivets and the hexagonal nuts of bolted joints. A Stalker at rest is invisible against a wall; it looks merely like a cluster of protruding hardware. They are cold in the blood but warm in the habit, draping their heavy bodies over the vast, heating pipes that run through the upper tiers of the Shelf. They are ambush predators of the utmost patience. They lie for days, their body temperature matching the warm metal beneath them, indistinguishable from the architecture, until a cluster of Grazers passes beneath. Then, with a speed that belies their bulk, the jaw snaps shut. They are the mechanism of control, the hydraulic press of the food web, keeping the grazing swarms from denuding the city entirely.

The air is filled with the **Shear-Wings**. These are the leathery birds mentioned, though "bird" is a generous term for things that seem more like flying manta rays. They have no feathers, only membranes of tough, translucent skin stretched over bone spurs, giving them the silhouette of a kite. They are deafening. Their cries are not the melodic chirping of sparrows, but the screech of tearing metal and the hiss of escaping steam. They navigate the smog using a sophisticated echolocation, clicking loud enough to be felt in the teeth, painting a sonic map of the dangling chains and walkways. They nest in the updrafts of the cooling towers, their eggs looking like smooth, grey stones to fool the scavengers. They dive into the pools of standing water, snatching up the amphibious **Sludge-Eels**—blind, sinuous things that writhe in the oily runoff, their skins translucent enough to show the mechanical sludge digesting in their guts.

Beneath it all, in the places where the catwalks have rusted through or the ventilation grates have been pried away, live the **Sump-Rats**, though they are hardly rats. They are multi-limbed scavengers, pale and sightless, possessing an oversized sense of smell and a kinesthetic sense that lets them run at full speed along slick, vertical pipes. They are the refuse eaters, the creatures that consume the dead. When a Stalker dies, toppling from its perch to smash on the tiers below, the Sump-Rats are there within seconds. They are the clean-up crew, efficient and remorseless, stripping bone and armor until nothing remains but a stain on the concrete.

And then there is the strangest of them all, a creature that has evolved to hack the energy of the Shelf directly: the **Volt-Parasites**. These are small, furry mammals that hang in clusters from the buzzing high-voltage lines that crisscross the upper atmosphere like spiderwebs. They have evolved thick, rubbery insulating pads on their hands and feet, and teeth capable of piercing the lead sheathing of cables. They do not eat food; they siphon the trickle-charge of the city’s grid, living directly off the electricity. They are perpetually twitching, their fur standing on end with static, their eyes glowing with a faint, bioluminescent electric blue. They are the only creatures here that have no predators, for to bite them is to be shocked.

Everywhere, there is noise. The Grazers click, the Stalkers hiss, the Shear-Wings scream, and the hum of the Volt-Parasites vibrates in the air. It is a chaotic, frantic overlay of life that mimics the industry below. They have colonized the skeleton of a dead god, or perhaps a sleeping one, building their nests in its lungs and drinking its blood. They are the rust that walks, the pestilence that flies, a biological infestation thriving in the warm, wet, eternal belly of the machine.
