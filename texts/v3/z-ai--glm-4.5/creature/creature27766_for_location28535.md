To stand in the Rust-Garden and believe it is empty is a mistake easy to make and dangerous to hold. The silence here is not the vacuum of the void, but the held breath of a hiding thing. If you look closely at the shack-like structures welded high onto the flanks of the boilers, or squint through the humid haze at the dark gaps between the pistons, you will see them.

They are the Curators, the silent architects of this impossible balance.

They are not animal in any sense that a biologist would recognize. They possess no fur, no warm blood, no frantic beating heart. They do not breathe the air; they filter it. They stand roughly humanoid in silhouette—a vertical adaptation to fit the narrow, vertical city of the catwalks—but they are perhaps seven feet tall, gaunt to the point of emaciation, with limbs that are too long and joints that bend in too many directions.

Their skin is the texture of the place itself: a layered, calcified armor of rust and patina that matches the iron beams perfectly. Over this carapace, they wear a "clothing" of thick, rubbery moss, draped across their shoulders like the heavy mantles of monks. This moss is not merely parasitic; it is symbiotic, connected directly to the Curator’s vascular system, filtering the heavy metallic particles from the air and converting the machine’s exhaust into the sustenance they need to persist.

They are so still that you could stare at one for an hour and assume it was a peculiarly arranged pile of scrap metal. They move on the same geological timescale as the pistons. When a massive valve needs turning, or a vine threatens to choke a critical intake fan, a Curator will step from its shadow. It does not walk so much as flow, its movements a series of slow, hydraulic jerks that mimic the grinding of the gears around them. To raise an arm might take ten minutes. To close a hand around a lever might take an hour. They are the embodiment of the Rust-Garden’s primary law: *waste not, want not, rush never.*

Inside the shacks, there is no furniture for sleeping or eating. There are only nests of copper wire and humming crystals that act as capacitors, storing the excess energy of the engine. The Curators retreat here to "digest," hooking themselves directly into the Ley lines of the machine’s nervous system, communing with the vast, slow intellect of the engine. They do not speak; their mouths are sealed or non-existent, replaced by vertical slats that vibrate with the infrasonic groan of the floor. They communicate through the metal itself, sending vibrations through the catwalks that ripple out like pebbles in a pond—warnings of pressure spikes, signals of impending rain, or the mourning hum of a failing piston.

They are the shepherds of the green. You will sometimes see one perched on a girder, motionless for days, its long, multi-jointed fingers entangled in the roots of a strangler vine. It is pruning the plant, not with blades, but with a chemical secretion from its fingertips that dissolves the aggressive growth, redirecting the plant’s energy to where it is needed most. They are the reason the "absence of animal life" is so absolute; anything with a heartbeat that enters the garden is either harvested by the Curators to feed the soil, or gently herded toward the sump, repurposed into the biomass that keeps the moss alive.

To encounter a Curator is to look into the eyes of a machine that has learned to dream. Where a face should be, there is only a smooth, curved plate of brass, polished to a mirror sheen by centuries of dripping water. In this surface, you see only your own distorted reflection, shivering in the wet heat. They are not hostile, but they are relentless. They are the immune system of the Rust-Garden. If you stand still long enough, if you stop moving and begin to rust, if you let the moss find a purchase on your skin, a Curator will eventually approach. It will not attack you. It will simply, slowly, begin to weave you into the mechanism. It will solder your hand to a railing. It will guide a root through your ribcage.

In this place, you are either a visitor who leaves quickly, or you are a part of the machine. The Curators are the ones who ensure that eventually, everyone becomes the latter. They are the wait, given form.
