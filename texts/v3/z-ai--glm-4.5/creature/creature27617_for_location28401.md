To exist in the Green Cloister is to accept a life of constant, damp pressure. The creatures here are not the wild beasts of the lowland plains; they are the microscopic citizens of a damp, slow-motion metropolis. They are the mechanics and the commuters of this organic city, small enough to navigate the corridors of vine and shelf, yet complex enough to survive the freezing, oxygen-rich toxicity of the air.

The most common denizens are the **Glasswrights**.

These are not merely spiders, though they share the ancestry. They are the architects of the air. Their bodies are translucent, comprised of a silica-lattice chitin that renders them nearly invisible against the mist and the glowing walls of the hollow reeds. They do not build webs to catch prey randomly; instead, they weave suspension bridges and "catenaries" of silk between the towering fungal shelves. Their silk is thick, structural, and sticky with a bioluminescent enzyme that glows with a faint, bruised-purple light in the dim overcast. You see them moving along these tension lines, adjusting the vibrations of their threads. They are listening. In a world where sight is limited by the enclosing fog, they read the world through vibration, sensing the footfalls of prey and the groaning of expanding wood. They are the aerial traffic controllers, trimming the excess growth of the city with surgical mandibles, harvesting the menthol-scented leaves to wrap their eggs in preservation bubbles.

On the "ground"—that deceptive, spongy mattress of moss and root—live the **Litho-Mites**.

Imagine a woodlouse, but evolved for the specific purpose of navigating a world that is digesting itself. They are flat, segmented, and plated in armor that mimics the texture of the lichen they traverse. Their legs are multiple and fine, designed to distribute their weight so they do not puncture the skin of the sponge-floor or sink into the weeping stone beneath. They are the gravekeepers of the geology. Where a young, sharp stone has punctured the green shroud, the Litho-Mites gather in the hundreds. They do not eat the stone; they scrape away the slime and the beginning-growths of moss, keeping the wound open. They cultivate the raw, young earth, ensuring the stone continues to weep. They rely on the mineral-rich tears of the rock for hydration, clustering around the seeping fractures like devotees at a shrine, their antennae twitching in the freezing air.

Navigating the hollow, translucent towers of the giant reeds are the **Stem-Divers**.

These are sleek, neotenic salamanders, no longer than a finger, evolved to never leave the water. The reeds are filled with a vertical column of stagnant, nutrient-rich water that rises from the root mats deep below. The Stem-Divers swim in this silent, internal ocean. Their skin is perfectly clear, revealing the pulsing of their two-chambered hearts and the coils of their intestines. They possess no eyes; the inside of a reed is pitch black. Instead, they navigate by smell and a lateral line sense so acute it can detect the displacement of a single falling water droplet. They enter through tiny boreholes near the base and ascend to the "canopy," where they poke their snouts into the open air to snatch the Glasswrights’ glowing egg-sacs. They are the ghosts in the machine, silent swimmers in a vertical sea, unseen by the world outside the translucent green walls.

Rare and beautiful are the **Moss-Phantoms**.

This is the apex predator of the Cloister, a creature no larger than a mouse but possessing the gravity of a tiger. It is an ambush hunter, a master of stillness. Its body is soft and mutable, able to flatten itself against the leathery surface of a fungal shelf. It mimics the texture and color of the "shingles" it rests upon—a mottled grey-green with fuzzy, hair-like projections. It does not hunt by speed, but by infinite patience. It can remain motionless for days, its breathing synced to the rhythmic *plink... plink... plink* of the condensing fog, indistinguishable from the natural rot of the city. When a Litho-Mite or a careless insect strays too close, the Moss-Phantoms do not pounce; they simply bloom. A layer of sticky, narcotic mucus ejects from pores in their side, engulfing the prey in a chemical trap. The prey is numbed instantly by the concentrated menthol toxins of the environment, dissolved slowly while the Phantom waits, a lump on the wall, digesting in the cold wet silence.

Finally, there is the **Drift**.

While not a single creature, the Drift is the collective mass of microscopic life—pollen spores, rot bacteria, and single-celled organisms—that floats suspended in the heavy air. Because the fog is so thick and the wind so absent, the Drift has achieved a kind of sentience. It moves through the Cloister like a slow, invisible tide, responding to chemical signals. When a plant is injured, releasing the sharp scent of crushed menthol, the Drift shifts, swimming through the mist toward the wound, accelerating the breakdown of the dying tissue. It is the immune system of the Green Cloister, a airborne, microscopic hive mind that ensures nothing goes to waste, turning the raw, young stone into the flesh of the world, cell by microscopic cell.

These are the residents of the bruised plateau: the weavers of light, the keepers of the stone, the swimmers of the vertical sea, the patient lumps of death, and the invisible tide of life. They are few, they are small, but they are the gears that turn the green cathedral, shivering in the freezing mist, locked in the slow, silent war of survival.
