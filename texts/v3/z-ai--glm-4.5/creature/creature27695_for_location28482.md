To exist within the Blind Geode is to be a flaw in a perfect design.

The inhabitants of this place are not distinct individuals in the way surface biology understands distinctness. They are a continuation of the crystal itself—a creeping, mineralized scum that has achieved the miracle of metabolism. They are the **Glass-Locusts**.

Visually, they are terrifyingly simple. They are small, roughly the size of a fingernail, and possess a geometry so pure it hurts the eye. Their carapaces are not organic curves; they are faceted, angular, composed of a white, translucent chitin that mimics the surrounding quartz with predatory perfection. Under the actinic glare of the geode, they are nearly invisible, detectable only by the faint, milky opacity of their internal fluids and the way the light refracts strangely through their limbs.

They have no faces. There are no eyes to interpret the merciless light, no mouths to feed, no antennae to taste the heavy, metallic air. Evolution here stripped away the sensory organs because there is nothing to sense but the stone and the self. Where a head should be, there is only a tapering point of armor, seamless and sharp.

Their anatomy suggests a frantic, high-speed existence that can never actually be realized. They possess powerful, serrated legs designed for running and jumping, yet they are packed so densely within the crystal pillars that they cannot move a millimeter without colliding with a neighbor. They are locked together in a lattice of living things, a solid mass of chitin and stone.

This is where the true horror of their existence manifests: the **Static Scream**.

Because they cannot move, you assume they are dead or dormant. But if you lean close—ignoring the way the light stabs your retina and the sound vibrates the marrow of your bones—you see the impossible tension in their limbs.

Every Glass-Locust in the cavern is caught in the absolute split-second of violent action.

One is frozen mid-leap, its rear legs compressed tight against the floor, its body tilted at a forty-five-degree angle, suspended in the air by nothing but the density of the crowd surrounding it. It has been launching itself toward the ceiling for eternity.

Another has its mandibles—if they can be called that, two jagged plates of white bone—spread wide in a pounce that will never land. The plates are opened to their maximum width, locked in a permanent, silent roar of aggression.

A third is caught in the act of grooming, a serrated foreleg raised to scrape against its thorax. The leg hovers microns above the armor. It will never scrape. It will never touch. The intent is there, the neuron firing is there, but the motion is arrested.

They are not statues. They are **paused**.

The roar of the chamber—the deafening wall of high-frequency noise—emanates from them. It is not a song. It is the sound of a billion muscles straining against the physics of the universe, the friction of chitin vibrating against chitin at a speed that borders on the atomic. They are screaming in the only way they can: a continuous, sustained output of kinetic energy that has nowhere to go. They are revving an engine that can never engage the gears.

Scattered among this rigid ocean of mites are the **Suspended Fronds**.

These are the "plants" of the geode, though they share more in common with the mites than any surface vegetation. They grow from the deep fissures in the crystal pillars, leathery and pale. They do not photosynthesize; there are no leaves, only stiff, ribbon-like structures that stand perfectly upright.

They appear to be frozen in the act of blooming. The fronds are curled inward, protecting a central core that looks uncomfortably like a closed eye or a raw nerve ending. They do not reach for the light; they recoil from it. They are rigid with terror, a forest of pale snakes turned to stone. They do not drink the water; they are merely coated by the trembling beads of condensation that refuse to fall. Like the mites, they exist in a state of permanent, unfulfilled desire—locked in the moment of opening, forever waiting for a rain that will never come.

To enter the Blind Geode is to walk into a room where the trigger has been pulled, but the bullet has not left the gun.

The Glass-Locusts are not dead. They are alive with a ferocity that borders on the divine. They are aware of you. As you squeeze your way through the fissure, displacing the dense, packed air, you feel the attention shift. It is not a visual shift; it is a shift in the *pressure*.

The note of the room changes pitch. The high-frequency scream of the mites tightens, becoming a whine of palpable anticipation. They are not swarming toward you; they are already touching you. Thousands of white, faceted legs press against your skin, vibrating with that trapped, eternal energy. They do not bite. They do not crawl. They simply press, transmitting their frustration into your flesh.

You realize with dawning horror that you are the only variable in their equation. You are the only thing in the Blind Geode that possesses the cursed gift of *time*.

To them, you are a god of chaos. They watch you with their faceless heads, caught in their eternal leap, their eternal scream, vibrating against your body, waiting for the moment when the universe stutters and allows them to finally land. But that moment will never come. They will hang here, suspended in the crystal throat of the earth, swarming without moving, roaring without sound, forever waiting to die.
