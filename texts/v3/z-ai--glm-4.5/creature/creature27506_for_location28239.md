To live in the Spine of Aethelgard is to exist in a state of permanent negotiation. Negotiation with the thin, metallic air that demands you breathe deep to survive; negotiation with the gravity that cannot be trusted to hold your footing; and negotiation with a landscape that refuses to stay solid long enough for you to call it home.

The dominant sentient inhabitants are a sparse, reclusive breed known to the few outsiders who glimpse them as the **Resonants**. They are not entirely human, or if they once were, they have been rewritten by the place. They are tall and excessively thin, their physiology evolved to maximize oxygen absorption in the rarefied atmosphere. Their skin possesses the same terrifyingly smooth texture as the fresh obsidian shards—pale, grey, and cool to the touch. They do not speak; the roar of the city makes vocalization futile. Instead, they communicate through a complex language of harmonic hums, vibrating the hollow bones of their elongated fingers against the iron railings, tapping into the city’s background thrum to send messages rippling through the metal itself.

The Resonants dress in layers of heavy, oilcloth dusters and leather aprons, salvaged from the ghost-city’s infinite surplus. Their faces are always obscured by complex, brass-filtering respirators that hiss with every exhalation, the minty taste of the air chemically scrubbed before it reaches their lungs. They are caretakers of a sort, though they do not repair the machines—how can one repair a machine that is un-building itself? Instead, they *shepherd* the decay. They walk the catwalks with long, hooked poles used to catch debris that falls from the folding reality, tossing it into the void before it can jam the gears of the world. They move with a slow, deliberate grace, knowing that to rush is to miss a step where gravity has momentarily taken a holiday. They look like lonely funeral directors marching at the head of a procession for a world that hasn't died yet.

Beneath the catwalks, clinging to the dripping underbelly of the city, dwell the **Slag-Eaters**. These are amphibious, urodelan-like creatures the size of draft horses, bloated and sluggish. They have evolved to digest the heavy metals and industrial runoff that coats everything. Their skin is a riotous, toxic kaleidoscope of colors—swirling greens and angry oranges—that serve as a warning. They feed on the rust that flakes off the ancient iron beams and the sludge that pools in the factory gutters. Where they walk, they leave trails of gleaming, polished metal, their acidic tongues scraping the decay clean. They are largely ignored by the Resonants, viewed as a necessary sanitation system. The Slag-Eaters possess a unique, biological adaptation to the weirdness of Aethelgard: they exist slightly out of phase. You can see through their translucent bellies to the gears behind them, and bullets—or physical force—often pass harmlessly through their gelatinous forms as if they were merely a rumor of a creature.

High above, in the vertical spaces where the clouds scrape the chimneys, the **Rust-Moths** rule the air currents. They are not merely insects; they are ecosystem engineers. The iron filings they gather aren't just for armor; they weave them into vast, hanging nests that look like conglomerations of shrapnel and wire suspended over the void. These nests conduct electricity. When the Tesla coils atop the transmission towers arc, the energy grounds itself through the moth nests, incubating their eggs with a slow, electro-static charge. The moths themselves are beautiful and terrifying, their wings jagged serrations of scrap metal that shimmer in the furnace-light. When they die, they do not fall; they simply rust away into a fine powder that feeds the copper-leafed ivy below.

Most elusive of all are the entities that inhabit the "folds" of reality—the **Glitch-Stalkers**. No one is sure if they are flesh or phantom. They appear only in the places where the geometry of the city fails, where a road folds back on itself or a wall blinks out of existence. They look like geometric abstractions, quadrupeds made of sharp angles and impossible vectors. They seem to feed on the spatial distortion itself, hunting along the edges of the non-Euclidean cliffs. To watch a Glitch-Stalker move is to feel nausea; they do not walk from point A to point B, but rather slide instantly between coordinates, flickering like a bad film reel. The Resonants give them a wide berth, fearing that to touch one is to be erased from the narrative of the world.

And finally, in the freezing crystal pools that collect in the grooves of the raw stone, there is life of a quieter sort. Here swim the **Glass-Skinned Minnows**. Their bodies are completely transparent, save for a bright, neon-blue strip of nerves running down their spines—the same color as the coolant in the factories. They are sightless, navigating by the vibration of the thrumming machinery. They have adapted to the erratic gravity; when the water flow reverses and climbs toward the sky, the minnows simply rotate their internal air bladders and swim upward along the vertical waterfall, treating the sky as just another direction of the sea.

In Aethelgard, everything is hungry. The iron eats the stone, the moss eats the iron, the Slag-Eaters eat the rust, and the reality itself seems to wait, patient and inhaling, ready to consume the slow, stubborn lives that cling to its ribs. The inhabitants here do not dream of a future, because in a place that is actively becoming, the future is a shaky concept. They only maintain the present, breathing in the copper and the cold, watching the mountains grow, vibrating in time with the heavy, heartbeat roar of the world.
