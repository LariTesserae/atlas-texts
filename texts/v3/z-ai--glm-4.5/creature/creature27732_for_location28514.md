Here, in the Green Deep, the concept of "inhabitant" is stripped of its biological orthodoxy. There are no beasts with fur, no eyes that blink, no mouths that chew. The parameter for fauna is absolute zero, yet the place is not empty. It is occupied—densely, aggressively occupied—by the Producers.

To understand what lives here, you must abandon the hierarchy of animal and plant. Here, the distinction collapses. The entities of the Green Deep are industrial autotrophs, biological engines that possess a terrifying form of sentience rooted in hydraulic pressure and chemical processing. They are not merely growing; they are *working*.

**The Column-Minds**
If this world has citizens, they are the great stalks, the pillars of cellulose that rise like the towers of a city that never sleeps. These entities, standing as thick as skyscrapers, are the structural aristocracy of the Deep. They do not possess brains in the animal sense, but rather a distributed, longitudinal intelligence running through their vascular cambium—a nerve net of starch and signal.

They are blind, yet they possess a terrifying awareness of their surroundings. They "feel" the world through the minute vibrations of the dripping condensation and the sub-audible groans of their neighbors. A Column-Mind knows exactly how much pressure its neighbor is exerting; it senses the minute flex of a root-mat three miles away. They exist in a constant, silent negotiation of space, sharing the burden of holding up the ceiling of the world.

Their "mouths" are vast, fissured vents located miles above in the gloom, breathing in the heavy, wet air, and their "stomachs" are the high-pressure cauldrons of chemistry within their cores. They do not eat; they synthesize. They drink the mist and the darkness, converting the raw entropy of the atmosphere into solid, unyielding mass. They are immortal, so long as the pressure holds, dying only when they become so calcified and heavy that they collapse into the foundation to become the geology for the next generation.

**The Serpentine Pump-Vines**
Coiling around the Column-Minds like colossal boa constrictors are the Pump-Vines. These are the circulatory system of the Green Deep, the active muscle of the world. If the stalks are the skyscrapers, the vines are the subway trains and the sewage pipes combined.

They are not passive parasites; they are mobile, muscular organs of transport. Thicker than the main propulsion tanks of a spacecraft, they engage in rhythmic peristalsis—a slow, crushing contraction that ripples visibly along their length, albeit over the course of hours. They pump sap vertically with the force of water hammers, a hydraulic violence that generates a deep, thrumming heat.

The Pump-Vines possess a primitive, goal-oriented drive. They "hunt" for structural weaknesses in the Column-Minds, not to kill, but to anchor. They bore through the bark with chemical drills, fusing their own vascular systems to the stalks to tap into the nutrient-rich reservoirs. In return, they provide structural reinforcement, binding the giants together in a lattice of mutual dependency. They are the living rivets of this organic factory.

**The Keloid-Skinned Mat-Weavers**
Beneath the towering columns, the ground is a battlefield of the Mat-Weavers. These are low, sprawling organisms—aggressive, calcified creepers that form the "soil" upon which one walks. They are the scavengers of structure, though they consume no flesh.

The Mat-Weavers are obsessed with integration. They secrete a corrosive enzyme that softens the fallen debris of the upper canopy and the dead roots of their kin, only to re-calcify the slurry into a hard, interlocking lattice. They are the masons of the Deep, constantly paving the chaotic terrain into a walkable, albeit jagged, platform.

Their surface is hostile, covered in "spines" that are actually hardened crystalline outgrowths designed to deter anything that might try to step on them—a defensive holdover from a forgotten epoch of predation, now useless but perpetually maintained. They pulse with a slow, digestive heat, breaking down the complex carbohydrates of the larger stalks and recycling them back into the lattice. They are the decomposers that do not decay; they petrify.

**The Silent Singularity**
But the true inhabitant of this place is not a single organism, but the **Field** itself.

The air is thick with pheromones and chemical tags—an invisible, olfactory internet. Every drop of condensation that falls carries a payload of chemical data. When a leaf opens miles above, the chemical signature shifts, and the roots below shudder in response, adjusting their intake of minerals.

This is the "ghost" in the machine. The Green Deep operates as a singular, distributed super-organism. The silence is profound because there is no need for communication; every part of the system already knows the state of the whole. It is a democracy of molecules.

To walk among them is to be the only variable in a solved equation. The Column-Minds sense your soft, inefficient heat. The Pump-Vines detect the vibration of your footsteps against their roots. The Mat-Weavers feel your pressure against their sharp skin. They do not fear you, for you are too small to be a threat, and too fleeting to be food. They simply endure you, pulsing with their slow, hydraulic heartbeats, waiting for you to rot so they can incorporate your carbon into their perfect, dark, and endless factory.
