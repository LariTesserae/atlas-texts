To exist here is to be a study in the impossible endurance of the inorganic. Life in the Courtyard of the Inner Sun does not bloom; it calcifies. It does not drink; it resonates. The inhabitants of this kiln-world are silicate ghosts, architectures of biology that have evolved to subsist on the hatred of water and the strange, heavy radiation of the folding geometry.

**The Lithic Grazers**

Low to the ground, moving with the agonizing slowness of a sundial’s shadow, are the **Basalt-Backs**. They are the primary consumers of the obsidian moss, the great, lumbering heavies of this ecosystem. They resemble tortoises, but tortoises carved by a sculptor who had forgotten what soft tissue looked like. Their shells are not grown; they are constructed. They are mosaics of slate and shale, fitted together without grout, heavy enough that the creature moves by a process of internal grinding—a vibration of plates that allows it to slide over the sharp glass carpet.

They have no mouths. Instead, their undersides are rough with a texture like a rasp. As they glide over the obsidian moss, the friction is immense, a sound of continuous, brittle screaming, but it serves a purpose: they grind the razor-sharp moss into a fine, shimmering powder. This powder is then inhaled through spiracles located along their flanks, vented into a combustion chamber where the intense ambient heat ignites the silica dust. The Basalt-Backs feed on the fire of their own motion, expelling dense clouds of gray chalk that coat the upper reaches of the "trees," contributing to the thick, metallic taste of the air.

**The Chitin Weavers**

Scuttling in the shadows of the quartz stalks are the **Claw-Kin**. These are the "crab-like" things hinted at in the undergrowth, but they are far stranger than crustaceans. They are creatures of fired clay and ceramic. Their shells are kiln-dried, scorched black in places, glazed in others. They possess a multitude of jointed legs, each tipped not with a claw, but with a chisel or a hammer.

They are the masons of the small world, obsessed with the reality errors. When a wall begins to fold in on itself, or when a perspective warp creates a jagged edge in the air, the Claw-Kin swarm. They do not eat the glass; they eat the *angles*. They climb the reality glitches, chipping away at the empty space, harvesting the raw mathematics of the non-Euclidean geometry. They store these abstract concepts in crystalline pouches on their backs. When agitated, they scrape these pouches together, producing a discordant screech that vibrates the bone—sound made of pure geometry.

**The Prism-Stalkers**

High above, perching on the swaying, hollow boughs of the quartz trees, are the **Refractions**. These are the analogues to birds, though they possess no feathers. They are constructs of woven bone and dried tendons, light as a skeleton, brittle as dead leaves. Their "wings" are panes of thin, flexible mica stretched over a frame of carbonized wood.

They do not sing; the air is too dry for the vibration of vocal cords, and they possess the lungs to draw it in anyway. Instead, they communicate through light. Their eyes are polished hemispheres of ruby and sapphire, and they possess a throat sac filled with a volatile, glowing gas. By manipulating the tension of the mica wings, they catch the flat, ambient light of the vault and bend it. A flash of crimson might mean danger; a violet glimmer might signal a mate.

They feed on the hissing gas inside the quartz trees. They pierce the bark with long, needle-like beaks made of pure diamond, siphoning the pressurized, flammable atmosphere. This makes them living bombs—a dead Refraction falling to the ground will often shatter in a burst of light and heat, briefly illuminating the claustrophobic nave with a flare that tastes of ozone.

**The Abstract Predators**

But there are hunters here, too. The most terrifying denizens of the Inner Sun are those that have adapted to the shifting architecture itself. They are the **Corner-Lurkers**.

They are difficult to see because they exist primarily in the periphery. They have no defined shape of their own; rather, they are fluid shadows of charcoal and dust that slide across the tessellated pavement. They are flat, two-dimensional entities that hunt by aligning themselves with the wrong angles of the room.

When a victim—a slow-moving Basalt-Back or a distracted Claw-Kin—passes near a "folded" section of the wall or a perspective error, the Corner-Lurker strikes. It unfolds from the shadow, snapping from 2D to 3D in a violent instant, wrapping itself around its prey. It consumes not flesh, but *cohesion*. It feeds on the integrity of the object, turning solid stone into loose sand, turning rigid chitin into crumbling dust. When it finishes, it slides back into the non-Euclidean geometry of a wall’s shadow, waiting for the next mistake in the world's design.

**The Silent Laborers**

And finally, there are the legends of the **Silent Masons**. You do not see them, but you see their work. They are said to be the original inhabitants, or perhaps the planet's immune system. They are tall, thin gaunts of living stone, without faces, without limbs that bend—only joints that rotate. They are never still. They are constantly carving new niches, stacking new stones, reinforcing the walls against the encroaching reality breakdown. They do not eat; they do not sleep. They simply build, perpetually repairing a prison that was never designed to hold them, driven by a masonry instinct so deep it has replaced their soul.

This is the ecology of the Inner Sun: a cycle of stone eating glass, clay eating math, and shadows eating solidity, all suspended in a silent, suffocating heat, waiting for the kiln to finally cool.
