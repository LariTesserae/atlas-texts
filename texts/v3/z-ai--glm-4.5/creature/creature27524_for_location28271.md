To survive in the Screaming Amphitheater is to be an instrument of the noise. There is no passive existence here; to live is to amplify, to dampen, or to break.

**The Vesper Rays**
The primary lords of the amber murk are the Gliders, though the locals—if any remained to name them—might call them Vesper Rays. They are enormous, triangular sheets of pale, translucent cartilage, spanning twenty feet from wingtip to wingtip, yet barely an inch thick in the body. They have no bones, for bone would shatter under the fluctuating pressure gradients of the high-altitude trench. Instead, they possess internal chambers filled with gas that resonates at the frequency of the constant thrum, allowing them to achieve neutral buoyancy in the heavy fluid.

They are eyeless, born of a darkness where light is deceitful. Their "vision" is a tactile map of pressure variations. Their skin is a continuous tympanic membrane, sensitive enough to feel the footstep of a crab a hundred yards away through the vibration of the water. They feed on the turbulence itself, swimming with mouths agape to filter the microscopic particulates that are shaken loose from the grinding rocks. When a Vesper Ray turns, it does not bank; it changes its internal harmonic, causing the current to push against it differently. It is a ghost of pale silk, surfing the invisible waves of the amphitheater’s roar.

**The Litho-Resonators**
Clustering near the base of the obsidian spires are the Litho-Resonators. These are the architects of the shifting geography, massive, slug-like creatures the size of whale sharks, covered in plates of chitinous armor that resemble the basalt they cling to. They possess no limbs, only a powerful, muscular foot that vibrates at a hypersonic frequency.

They consume the stone itself. By pressing their vibrating bodies against the sharp obsidian, they liquefy the rock, sucking in the slurry of minerals and black glass. In doing so, they carve the tunnels and caves that pockmark the spires. They are responsible for the migrating pinnacles; as they feed, they destabilize the foundation, causing the massive shards to slide and rotate. A Litho-Resonator is a living erosion machine, a slow, grinding engine of geological change that leaves behind smooth, perfectly rounded grooves in the jagged chaos.

**The Siphonophores of Silence**
Perhaps the most terrifying entities are those that do not make sound, but eat it. Drifting through the central current are long, drifting chains of gelatinous polyps, similar to Portuguese Man o' Wars, but dark as crude oil. They are the Silence Eaters.

In a world defined by the roar, a pocket of silence is a deadly anomaly. These creatures possess a unique organ that cancels out incoming sound waves, creating a sphere of absolute vacuum around their tendrils. To the Vesper Rays and other seeing-by-sound creatures, a Siphonophore does not appear as a physical object; it appears as a hole in the world, a blind spot where reality simply stops. Prey swims into the silence, disoriented by the sudden lack of pressure data, and is instantly ensnared by the long, trailing nematocysts which, unlike the rest of the world, move in deadly, quiet stillness.

**The Needle-Mites**
Infesting the serrated crests of the glassy rocks are colonies of Needle-Mites. They are tiny, armored crustaceans shaped like arrowheads, evolved specifically to inhabit the "death zones" where the spires grind together. Their shells are composed of diamond-hard carbon, capable of withstanding the crushing forces of colliding mountains.

They feed on the friction. When two spires scrape against one another, the superheated sparks and the violent shearing forces kill microscopic life in the water. The Needle-Mites swarm these impact zones, skittering across the vibrating stone with impossible speed, gorging on the vaporized organic matter and the raw thermal energy radiating from the rock. They communicate by tapping their legs against the stone at high frequencies, a staccato Morse code that is lost in the ambient roar but can be felt by the hand pressed against the rock.

**The Echo-Weavers**
Hiding within the ruined Henge structures are the Echo-Weavers. They are crab-like ambush predators, flat and wide, with shells that mimic the texture of the vibrating, blurry stones. They do not hunt by moving, but by listening.

An Echo-Weaver will clamp itself to a monolith, plugging its exoskeleton into the stone’s vibration. It waits for a Glider or a large scavenger to pass nearby. Then, with a snap of its massive claw, it creates a cavitation bubble—a shockwave so sharp and violent it stuns the prey. In this place, a weapon is not a bullet, but a focused blast of silence followed by a concussive boom. They are the assassins of the acoustic spectrum, striking in the split second between the grinding bass and the shrieking treble.

**The Whisperers**
Finally, there are the rumors of the Whisperers. These are theorized to be the evolved descendants of whatever civilization built the Henge. If they exist, they are no longer biological in the traditional sense. They are beings of pure resonance, clusters of energy that have learned to inhabit the standing waves of the fluid. They do not have bodies; they are just a complex, repeating pattern in the noise itself.

Sightings are rare and usually dismissed as auditory hallucinations caused by the pressure. A traveler might see a shimmer in the water, a humanoid shape that ripples like heat haze, composed entirely of the golden-brown medium vibrating in a coherent, intelligent pattern. They do not interact with the physical world; they merely surf the tectonic shifts, singing songs that are too low for the human ear to hear, vibrating the bones of the observer with a sense of profound, ancient sadness. They are the ghosts of the noise, having long ago discarded their ears to become the song.
