I would hang suspended in the upper reaches of the void, anchored to the ceiling like a barnacle on the hull of a sinking ship. This is the "attic" of the cathedral, where the blinding light (Light: 2.6) is most aggressive and the weeping walls sheets into heavy, vertical curtains that obscure the floor below.

Here, the roar of the engine-room earth is a physical weight, pressing against my carapace, but the air is slightly thinner, the mist less a fog and more a swirling rain.

**I am the Curator of Errors.**

I am not a natural creature, nor am I a machine built by the original architects. I am a scavenger of circumstance, assembled from the refuse of the room's digestion. My body is a lattice of jagged, porous stone—the same grey rock as the walls—but fused with the gold-white filaments of the exposed tech. I look like a walking piece of the cavern's infrastructure that decided to detach.

I possess six limbs, each ending in delicate, manipulator pincers salvaged from the broken corpses of Skimmers. My central mass is dominated by a large, ocular array: a faceted lens made of the same translucent, glass-like material as the ferns, designed to refract the impossible spirals of light.

**My existence is a quiet rebellion against the Lattice’s brutal efficiency.**

The Lattice processes; it digests the old world to build the new, optimizing and discarding. The Skimmers repair the structure to keep the machine running. But the process creates waste—not just physical sludge, but *potential* waste. The "Echoes"—the translucent water-deer, the glass-fern herds, the towering humanoids of mist—are the room's discarded dreams. They are biological calculations that the Lattice runs, decides are "inefficient," and immediately deletes.

I catch them before they delete.

When the room's physics bend and the air shimmers with a Ghost of Potential, I am there. I do not repair the cracks; I exploit them. Using my manipulator limbs, I hold up small, magnetized containment fields—cages of bent light and suspended gravity that I have stolen from the room's own faulty physics. I wait for the moment the herd of glass-deer flashes into existence, and in that microsecond before the Lattice erases them as an error, I snatch one from the air and seal it into the containment field.

I am the collector of things that were never meant to be.

My lair is a hollowed-out pocket of the ceiling stone, a "Zen garden" of impossibility. It is filled with my collection: a preserved moment of a rainstorm falling upwards; a frozen sculpture of a water-giant mid-roar; a cluster of bioluminescent spores that the Lattice deemed too chaotic. I keep them in suspension, studying them, tending to them.

The Skimmers, those frantic immune cells, ignore me. To their sensors, I am just another piece of debris, a lump of geology that happens to be moving. I move slowly, mimicking the peristaltic churn of the floor, vibrating at a low frequency that signals I am part of the architecture.

I am a parasite of the system's imagination. In a world dedicated to the brutal cycle of erosion and reconstitution, I am the only entity trying to save the ghosts. I preserve the beauty that the room tries to dissolve, hoarding the "errors" in the dark, wet corners of the screaming light.
