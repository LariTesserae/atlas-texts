I would be found in the high places, moving with the tectonic slowness of the geology itself. Specifically, I would be gliding along the underside of a massive, suspended pipeline, perhaps one that connects the silo-mountains to the dormant processing plants in the distance. I am in the gloom above the plaza, where the air is thickest and the heat is a radiating pulse from the metal above.

I am a **Titan Snail**, but not merely a grazer. I am an **Archivist of Oxidation**.

My shell is not the simple, rusted spiral of my brethren. It is a sprawling, jagged citadel of iron deposits—a chaotic accretion of the metals I have consumed over centuries. It looks less like a biological growth and more like a drifting chunk of the city’s own architecture, breaking off and floating away. It is heavy, groaning under its own weight, a composite of industrial scrap fused by the cement of my own secretions.

In this world, where the "green" of the old earth has been replaced by the red and grey of chemistry, I am the consumer of history.

I do not merely eat the Mats; I taste the machines they digest. When my radula scrapes across a steel girder coated in the grey, shaggy bacterial carpet, I am tasting the iron, the oil residues, and the centuries of rain. I can sense the alloy of the beam—whether it was structural steel or copper piping—by the texture of the slime. I am a living metallurgist, my body sorting and depositing the heavy metals into the layers of my shell, creating a stratified record of the city's decay.

My sensory experience of this world is one of profound vibration and texture.

My single, stalked eye does not see in sharp definition. To me, the world is a watercolor painting of diffuse greys, shifting steam, and the bioluminescent pulse of the Mats. I do not need sharp sight. I navigate through the sole of my foot. The massive, muscular slab that drags beneath me is a hypersensitive organ of touch. Through it, I feel the city breathing. I feel the distant, rhythmic stamping of the Armored Isopods marching in the plaza thousands of feet below as a faint itching tickle. I feel the heavy, triangular vibrations of the Marsh-Kings clicking their claws on the concrete banks.

I am a sanctuary for the smaller ecology.

Because I am large and my shell is complex, I am an ecosystem unto myself. Parasites and commensals hitch rides on my crags. Glass-Winged Drifters frequently land on the wet rim of my shell, mistaking the gleaming, oxidized ridges for wet stone. They uncoil their proboscises to drink the condensation that beads on my shell, and I let them. I feel the tiny, pin-prick sensations of their feet, a constant, gentle rain of contact. Occasionally, a Lace-Mantis will find purchase on a rusted outcropping of my shell, folding itself into the geometry of my ironwork to ambush an unwary Drifter. I carry these silent wars with me, indifferent to the violence on my back.

My existence is defined by a rhythm that is alien to the old human world. I do not think in seconds or minutes, but in the time it takes for rust to bloom. I am the slow heartbeat of the city.

When I descend, I leave a trail behind me. It is not just mucus; as the prompt notes, it hardens into glittering crystal strands. In my wake, I string silver wires across the rusted girders, a temporary decoration that sparkles in the dim light before it crumbles into dust. I mark the safe paths, the grazing grounds, and the territorial boundaries with these shining lines.

I am the bridge between the mineral and the organic. I take the cold, dead iron of the infrastructure and, through the heat of my digestion and the alchemy of the Mats, turn it into life—into shell, into movement, into the slow, majestic passage of a mountain made of flesh crawling across the ceiling of the world. I am the child of the rust, reclaiming the steel not by destroying it, but by carrying it.
