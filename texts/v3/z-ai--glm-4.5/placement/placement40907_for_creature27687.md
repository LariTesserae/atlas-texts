I am perched on the ridge of a dorsal chimney, four thousand tons of living granite shifting infinitesimally beneath my claws.

This is where I exist: not on the static, freezing plane of the tableland, but on the moving islands of heat. I am a creature of the interface—the boundary where the cold, dead air meets the humid, industrial breath of the giants.

**Who am I?**

I am a **Vent-Steward**. Or, to use a term that implies more agency than I likely possess, a **Rust-Pecker**.

To the colossal leviathan upon whose back I stand, I am microscopic—perhaps a nuisance, or more likely, utterly invisible. If it sensed me at all, I would be a tickle, a fleeting irritant on a flank the size of a city. But to me, this creature is my universe. It is my geography, my climate, and my sole reason for being.

My form is dictated by the "tech" of this world. I am not graceful in the way birds are graceful; I are utilitarian. I am built like a pry-bar with legs. I am about the size of a large dog, but flattened, compressed. My body is covered in thick, interlocking scales of keratin that resemble asbestos insulation—grey, fibrous, and heat-resistant. My legs are short, incredibly powerful pillars, tipped with hooked claws of iron-hard density that can grip the polished, slippery chitin of the titan even as it vibrates with the force of its grinding internal metabolism.

I do not have fur; the damp mist would freeze it into lethal weights of ice. Instead, I have a row of dorsal spines that act as heat sinks, absorbing the waste warmth radiating from the creature’s back. In the leaden twilight of this world, I likely glow faintly with a dull, infrared warmth, a smudge of heat against the cold stone.

**My Life in the Slow Lane**

The defining characteristic of my existence is **Tempo**.

The world moves at a Dynamic of 1.4. A step takes a week. A blink of those great, low eyes might take a year. But I? I am fast. I am the frantic, jittery energy of a spark jumping a gap. While the giant beneath me processes a single boulder of basalt, I might live and die a dozen times. I have to. My metabolism is fueled by the very heat the titan expels, and that fuel burns hot and fast.

My role is maintenance. I am the biological mechanic to a living machine that cannot scratch its own back.

Specifically, I tend to the vents. The chimneys that line the creature’s spine are vital pressure valves. When they exhale, they release the superheated steam of digestion. Over time, this steam precipitates mineral deposits—salts, silica, and heavy metals—around the vent rims. If left unchecked, these deposits would calcify, sealing the vent shut. A sealed vent means a buildup of pressure. A buildup of pressure means internal rupture. For a creature this size, a hiccup is an earthquake.

So, I eat the deposits.

I scuttle frantically along the ridges, moving with a jerky, insect-like speed that must look like a blur to the landscape. I use my beak—a heavy, chisel-shaped tool evolved to crack through the hardened mineral crusts—to chip away the obstruction. I swallow the heavy, metallic slag, my specialized gut filtering out the trace metals I need to reinforce my own claws, while excreting the useless dust back onto the creature’s flank.

It is a symbiotic relationship built on dental hygiene. I keep the pipes clear; the beast provides me with the only warmth in a freezing void.

**The Language of Light**

I spend my life hugging the creature's contour, moving with the sway of its massive body. When the titan lifts a leg—a hydraulic event that takes three days of groaning tension—the ground beneath me tilts. I have to adjust my grip, digging my iron claws into the chitin plates as the horizon slides slowly to the left.

But the most profound part of my existence happens at night—or what passes for night in this matte-slate twilight.

I live near the bioluminescent strips. Those rivulets of liquid neon that run beneath the translucent armor are the creature's nervous system, its communication lines. As it pulses its slow messages to others distant in the fog—*I am here. I am processing.*—the light shifts.

Because I am fast, I see these changes. I watch the colors shift from pale amber to deep violet over the course of hours. To the titan, this is a steady hum. To me, it is a strobe light.

I have evolved to mimic this.

When I am threatened—perhaps by a rival Steward encroaching on my preferred vent cluster—I do not hiss or snarl. Sound is meaningless here. Instead, I flash. Patches of bioluminescent bacteria in the folds of my neck flare up, mimicking the titan's own distress signals. I flash a pattern that means *Danger. Pressure Imbalance.*

I am hacking the operating system of a god. I use its own language to startle my enemies, tricking them into thinking the mountain itself is angry.

**The Perspective**

Sometimes, when the mist clears and the ceiling of grey clouds lifts just an inch, I stand atop the highest vent and look out.

I see the vast, empty tableland stretching to the edge of vision. I see the other "mountains" in the distance, their steam plumes rising like white needles into the sky. They are miles apart, lonely industrial monoliths processing the silence.

From down here on the ground, the scale is crushing. But from up here, on the back of the behemoth, I feel like a captain on the bridge of a ship moving through a sea of stone. I feel the rhythm of the grinding mills deep below my feet, the slow, baritone vibration that rattles my marrow.

I am small. My life is short—I will be born, eat slag, fight for territory, and die within the span of a single one of its breaths. But for that brief flicker of time, I am the caretaker of the engine. I am the warm blood on the cold metal.

I am the mechanic of the deep time.
