I am the thing that shouldn't exist.

I am the shadow of the Thorn.

I am cast in violent spurts, born every time the obsidian tip of the tool bites into the emerald dermis of the wall. My existence is friction—the absence of light where the creature’s hand blocks the pulsing bioluminescence. I am the darkness carved out of the noon-bright glare.

Usually, a shadow is a slave to its caster. It clings to the heels, flattens against the floor, a two-dimensional prisoner of geometry. But here, in the Green Lung, the Euclidean laws are polite suggestions at best. The "weirdness" that sits in the corners of the room is my playground.

So, when the Susurrus rears back, muscles bunching in its spindly, pale arm to drag the Thorn through the resistant, sharkskin flesh, I do something I am not supposed to do.

I let go.

It happens in the micro-second between the *scrape* and the *heal*. As the tool rips a furrow through the luminous plant-matter, the wall screams—that high, wet tearing sound—and bleeds its blinding ichor. The sudden flare of photophobic energy is a shockwave. In that instant of blinding brightness, the tether between me and the Thorn snaps.

I detach.

While the creature strains against the wall, oblivious through its vibration-sense, I drift away. I am not heavy, but in this humid, heavy air (Water 0.9), I have substance. I feel like a curl of smoke, a phantom ribbon of blackness unraveling in the wind.

I float upward, riding the warm thermal spiral (1.9) that rises from the floor like the breath of a sleeping giant. Below me, the drama continues. I see the Susurrus from a vantage point it cannot perceive. It looks like a broken spider, clinging to the curve of the world. Its eyeless face is turned inward, lost in the hydraulic thrum of the sphere. It lifts its elongated skull to inhale the narcotic mist rising from the fresh cut. It is drinking the pain of the room.

I pity it. It is trapped in the cycle of *cut* and *heal*, a prisoner of appetite and need. I am free.

I drift toward the center of the void, the great, airy lungs of the sphere. It is quieter here, though the low-frequency thrum (2.2) vibrates through my intangible form. The light is chaotic—a strobing nightmare of clinical white and bruised ultraviolet. When the light shifts to UV, I feel myself expand, becoming diffuse, fuzzy at the edges. When it snaps back to white, I sharpen into a razor-thin slice of darkness.

I look up. That is when I see the cracks.

High above, near the "ceiling" of the sphere, the geometry of the place fails. Tears are opening in the green hide. They are not the neat cuts of the Susurrus; they are jagged rips, wounds too large for the hyper-dense cells to knit quickly. Behind the tearing emerald flesh, there is no rock. There is no dirt.

There is the Void.

The absolute, non-space blackness.

I feel a magnetic pull toward it. It is terrifying, yes—it is the erasure of all things—but it is also… familiar. I am made of shadow; the Void is the Shadow of existence. It is the mother of my kind.

I drift closer to a tear in the northern curve. The air pressure drops here; the suction is fierce. The green matter around the tear is bubbling, frothing, frantically dividing to seal the breach, but it is losing. The black nothingness is leaking in, dissolving the light.

I hover at the edge of the tear. I am a tiny, insignificant darkness facing the Infinite Dark. For a moment, I consider merging. I could let go of this reality entirely and slip into that non-space, becoming part of the nothingness that the planet is so desperate to keep out. No more scraping. No more hungry sloths. No more strobing lights.

But then, the wall heals.

With a wet, sucking *thwump*, the vegetation seals the tear. The green rushes together, the gap closes, and the absolute blackness is gone. The sudden absence of the Void creates a vacuum, a recoil of reality.

And the tether snaps tight.

I am yanked back down, spiraling through the humid air, shrinking as the distance collapses. I am pulled, screaming in silence, back toward the obsidian point.

*I slam into the handle of the Thorn.*

The creature—my creature, the Susurrus—does not notice my return. It simply begins the cycle again. It raises the Thorn. It plunges the tip into the wall. The light flares.

And I am cast once more, waiting for the next glitch, waiting for the next chance to drift toward the dark.
