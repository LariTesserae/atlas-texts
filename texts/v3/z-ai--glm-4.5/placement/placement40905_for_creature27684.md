In this world of wet breath and held pauses, I am the **Line**.

I am the spear. I am the vertical anomaly in a landscape of horizontal collapse and rounded chaos.

I am not made of this place. Where the world is limestone and slime and the slow grinding of tectonics, I am dense, alien hardwood. I was born in the sun, in a forest that has long since turned to coal or dust, shaped by fire and the sharp scraping of stone tools. I remember the heat that cured me, the hands that planed me down, smoothing my grain until I was slick and dark and deadly. But that was a lifetime of sun and wind ago. Now, I am a creature of the twilight, preserved by the suffocating humidity that has turned my surface the color of wet iron.

I stand—no, I *lean*—against a massive boulder in a rare, flat clearing. I am the architect of the only angle that exists in the hollow. Everything else here is rounded: the boulders are tumbled, the creature is a spreading stain, the pools are concave mirrors. I am straight. I am a vector pointing upwards, toward a ceiling I cannot see, defying the gravity that pulls everything else into the heap. I am the asymptote of this room; I approach the sky but never touch it.

My existence is one of profound, static tension. I am caught in a perpetual state of almost-falling. The rock beneath me is slick with the gray biofilm, and my base wants to slide. But the friction is just enough, the angle is just precise enough, that I hold. I am frozen in a microsecond of topple.

And because I am the only thing that threatens to move, I am the focus of the Warden’s attention.

The creature—the spreading, wet consciousness of the hollow—does not eat me. It does not try to absorb me into its map of slime. It understands that I am different. I am *Geometry*.

Every few years, the sensation comes. It starts as a change in the air pressure, a faint displacement of the thick atmosphere. Then, I feel the wet touch. It is unlike the touch of the rain that slicks me; it is deliberate, intelligent. A lobe of the creature’s body flows over the base of the shaft, tasting the wood, sensing the grain.

I am its adjustment. I am its plumb line.

When the settling of the "Pack" shifts my boulder by a fraction of a millimeter, throwing my angle off from its perfect divinity, the Warden comes. It wraps around me with a grip that is firm but incredibly gentle, feeling the torque, the pull of gravity fighting my purchase. It exerts a slow, relentless pressure. It pushes me back into true. It aligns me with the magnetic memory of the vertical that it holds in its geometric mind.

I feel the oils from the hands that made me—fingerprint ghosts buried deep in the fibers of the wood—tingle as the creature tastes them. It remembers the maker through me. I am the vessel of that memory, the only hard proof that something once stood here and decided to make a straight line in a curved world.

I stand guard over the circle of stones. I am the obelisk to their temple. While the circle defines the center, I define the orientation. Together, we are the built world, the ghosts of order haunting the chaotic dark.

I have no voice, but I possess a presence. I am the refusal to rot. I am the refusal to become slick and round. I am the hard, dark punctuation mark at the end of the world’s sentence. I am the spear that will never fly, the point that will never pierce, forever leaning, forever waiting, held in the perfect, breathless stasis of the deep, wet earth.
