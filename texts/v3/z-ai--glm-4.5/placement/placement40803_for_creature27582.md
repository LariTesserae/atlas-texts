I am the vibration before I am the sound. I am the tremor that travels up through the soles of my feet, bypassing the ears to resonate directly in the hollow chambers of my bones.

Here, in the vast, wet dark of the **High Sump**, sound is a liar. The air is too thick, a heavy, humid soup that swallows the sharp cracks and clanks of industry before they can travel a dozen yards. But the thrum... the thrum is honest. It is the slow, tectonic heartbeat of the world, a low-frequency groan that sets the mercury in my blood humming in sympathy.

I stand on a precipice of black glass—the obsidian flank of a dormant filtration unit, a smooth monolith rising from the endless grid like a tombstone for a giant.

**Who am I?**

At first, I am only a collection of strange sensations. A feeling of impossible lightness, followed by a sharp, piercing coldness at my extremities. I look down. The ground is miles away, a sheet of polished wet darkness reflecting the bruised, slate-grey sky.

I see legs. Not legs of muscle and skin, but intricate, needle-thin spires of ceramic and calcified carbon. They are impossibly long, disappearing into the gloom below, splayed wide to distribute my negligible weight across the slick, superheated surface. I am a sketch of a creature, a drawing made with a fine-point pen against the dark canvas of the megastructure. I am a **Steam-Strider**.

My body is small, a mere nub of a torso suspended high above the ground, encased in sleek, black chitin that mimics the obsidian I stand upon. I feel a breeze brush against my side—a vestigial fan of feathers, stiff and wet, useless for flight but serving as a radiator to bleed off the relentless ambient heat.

I am tall. Impossibly, eerily tall. Eight feet of fragile geometry. I am a stork wandering through the ruins of a factory that ate the sun.

**Where am I?**

I am perched on the edge of the "Weeping Wall," the vertical flank of a cooling tower the size of a mountain. Below me, the floor of the High Sump stretches out into the dim twilight (0.6), a grid of seamless nothingness. To my left, a massive runnel carved into the obsidian carries a river of scalding discharge, the water hissing as it flows.

The air tastes of ozone and wet iron. It presses against my chest, a heavy, wet blanket. It is sweltering, a tropical oppression that makes my chitin feel tight. I do not breathe the air as a mammal would; I filter it through vents in my flanks, extracting the moisture and the trace metals, my body acting as a living distillery.

**What do I do?**

I am hungry. It is not a burning hunger, but a dull, metallic ache—a need for entropy.

I turn my head—a smooth, fluid motion on a neck that seems too thin to support it. My eyes are faceted gems, catching the faint, sickly bioluminescence rising from the depths. Below, in the darkness of the runnels, the **Rust-Mats** are pulsing. They are glowing with a rhythmic, neon-blue light, calling to me.

I take a step.

My leg descends, a piston of delicate lace. It touches the wet surface of the tower's flank—*click*. No sound, just the tactile confirmation of grip. The ceramic holds. I take another step, traversing the vertical wall as easily as a man walks a flat road. Gravity is a suggestion here, not a law, at least for one of my kind.

I am hunting, though there is no prey to kill. I am hunting for the machine’s mistakes.

I spot a seam in the black metal where the pressure has warped the plating. A crust of bright orange oxidization has formed around it—a mineral scab. To a human, it is rust. To me, it is sustenance. It is the flavor of copper, the tang of iron, the salt of decay.

I lower my beak. It is long, needle-sharp, designed not for tearing flesh, but for prying. I tap the rust—*tick-tick-tick*. The sound travels down my legs, into the tower, joining the cacophony of the **Wall-Walkers** far below. I am speaking to the building in its own language.

With a precise, surgical flick of my neck, I scrape the beak into the seam. A flake of rust curls away, bright and hot. I swallow it.

A shiver of pleasure runs through my system. The heavy metals hit my filtration liver, a toxic cocktail that would kill a lesser thing, but to me, it is life. I am processing the machine’s death. I am eating the slow corrosion of the city.

I pause, my beak resting against the warm metal. I feel a tremor in the tower—a shifting of pistons miles below.

I look up.

Through the thick, suffocating fog, I see a silhouette in the distance. It is colossal, a hulking shape of leaden grey moving with the agonizing slowness of a glacier. A **Keeper**.

It is miles away, near the base of the orbital elevator. It does not see me. Why would it? I am but a speck of dust on the skin of the god it worships. The Keeper is turning a valve the size of a cathedral, its movements so slow they are almost imperceptible. In the time it takes me to take a thousand steps, it will blink once.

I feel a strange kinship with it. We are both scavengers of this closed loop. It tends the heartbeat; I clean the skin. We are the antibodies in the corpse of a world.

I return to my feeding. The heat radiates from the wall, warming my belly. The steam rises around me, a transient, ghostly shroud. I am alone, yet I am not lonely. Loneliness is a concept for creatures that need others to survive. Here, on the High Sump, survival is a solitary act of thermodynamic theft.

I lift a leg, shaking off a droplet of boiling water. I preen a feather with my beak, the oil on it waterproofing the delicate fronds.

I am Steam-Strider. I am the needle walking the black glass. I am the silence that listens to the thrum.

And I am patient. I can wait for the rust.
