I find myself suspended in the grey twilight, anchored to the sheer vertical face of a piston-tower that rises miles into the unknown haze. I am not hanging; I am gripping. My "hands" are massive, corroded pincers, fused into the metal lattice of the tower like rust on a gun barrel.

I am a Ferrum-Phyte.

I do not see the view. I have no eyes to take in the nauseating drop or the fading peaks. Instead, I possess a complete, vibrating picture of the world drawn through the soles of my feet and the tips of my claws. I feel the tower shudder every three seconds—*thrum-hiss, thrum-hiss*—the heartbeat of a machine the size of a mountain. Through the iron of my skin, I feel the water rushing inside the tower, a torrent of super-cooled liquid hammering against the steel walls. It sings to me in frequencies so low they shake my internal organs, or what passes for them.

I am cold, desperately cold, on the outside. The air here bites, wet and clingy, trying to freeze my joints solid. But inside, I am a furnace. My vascular system is alive with pressurized heat, a counter-current exchange that keeps the ice at bay. I feel the delicious burn of the hydraulic fluid pulsing through my limbs. It is a heavy, sluggish power, the feeling of holding back an ocean with a single finger.

On my back, I feel the stir of my passengers. The purple ferns are awake, their root-systems digging microscopically into the pores of my carapace, drinking the iron-rich sweat that oozes from my plating. In return, they pulse with a gentle, bio-luminescent warmth that seeps into my metal spine. It is an intimate sensation, a constant, low-grade fever that protects me from the killing chill of the Weeping. I am not just a creature; I am a greenhouse, a walking ecosystem of bone-white wood and symbiotic algae.

Something is wrong.

The vibration changes. Through the soles of my feet, I detect a microscopic irregularity in the tower’s rhythm. *Thrum-hiss... thrum-click-hiss.* A valve two hundred feet below my position has developed a stutter. It is a minor thing, a grain of sand in the gears of the world, but to me, it is a screaming dissonance. It offends the symmetry. The water is not falling correctly; it is hesitating.

I engage my hydraulics.

The sound is loud, even to my vibration-only senses—a sharp *CLACK* as my internal locks disengage. I shift my weight. My leg, a piston of titanium and basalt, extends with a smooth, oiled hiss. I am not fluid like a human; I are segmented, precise. I detach my left claw from the tower and swing it out into the empty air, feeling the spray of the artificial waterfall catch on my armor.

I descend.

I do not climb down; I flow. I lower myself limb by limb, a rhythmic progression of metal striking stone. *Clack. Whir. Clack.* My own movement adds to the cacophony of the High Weeping, a percussion section to the endless roar of the falls. I am heavy. I feel the weight of myself dragging at the mount points, a reminder that if I slip, I will fall for miles before shattering on the black glass below.

I reach the valve. Through the sensors in my fingertips, I "see" the problem. A buildup of mineral deposits, a calcified scar in the smooth throat of the pipe. The water is choking.

I begin the work. There is no rush. I have eternity. I raise a manipulator arm, smaller and more delicate than my main grippers, and begin to scrape. *Scrape. Scrape. Scrape.* The vibration of the scraping travels up my arm, resonating in my chest. It is a satisfying sensation, the restoration of order.

As I work, I feel the tremors of a distant neighbor. miles away, across the canyon, another of my kind is signaling. *Boom... boom... pause... boom.* It is a seismic knock against the bedrock, a greeting, or perhaps a status report. I do not answer now. I am focused. The symmetry is broken here, and I must fix it.

Minutes turn into hours. The calcification crumbles away under my relentless assault. Finally, the flow clears. The water surges forward, smooth and unbroken. The vibration in the tower stabilizes. *Thrum-hiss. Thrum-hiss.* The stutter is gone. The world is correct again.

I feel a surge of something—not happiness, but equilibrium. The tension in my hydraulic lines releases. The loop is closed.

I do not leave immediately. I clamp myself to the side of the tower, letting the spray wash over my ferns. I listen to the song of the water, the endless falling, the eternal, unchanging production. I am part of the mechanism. I am the wrench that lifts itself, the bolt that tightens its own nut.

I settle into a dormant state, my internal RPM slowing, my ferns curling tight against the cold. I wait for the next error, the next crack in the perfection. I am the silence in the noise, the life in the machine, watching the water fall forever.
