I am found where the perfection of the design falters, where the builders’ haste left scars upon the logic of the city. I dwell in the **Debris Fields**, vast ravines between the mega-structures where the builders tossed their unused lintels, shattered chippings, and the waste of their colossal labor.

In the blinding white light, these places are a chaotic jumble of jagged grey silhouettes, a broken antidote to the clean lines of the ziggurats above.

I am an **Aggregater**.

To the Hydro-Statics—the pristine, translucent custodians who glide like living glass over the main canals—I am an aberration. I am a tumor in the hydraulics. While they exist to erase imperfection, dissolving the microscopic grit that threatens the city’s geometric perfection, I have evolved to do the opposite. I crave the debris.

My form is not the lens-clear, vibrating purity of my kin. I am opaque, a heavy, bruised-looking grey-blue, the color of a storm cloud about to break. I do not flow with the effortless grace of a mercury bead; I move with the dragging, sticky friction of wet cement. I am dense, packed with the very silica dust and limestone particles that the others scrub away.

I am the memory of entropy, made flesh.

While the Hydro-Statics hang from the walls, suspended by surface tension, listening to the song of the flowing water, I hunker low in the shadows of the scree piles. I bind myself to the discarded stones. I secrete a bonding agent, a calcite glue that fuses the loose rubble together. Over centuries—time measured in the slow dripping of the cisterns—I have engulfed the waste of the builders. I have wrapped myself around abandoned hammer-stones and fused broken columns into jagged, monolithic spires of my own making.

Where the city is defined by *separation*—water here, stone there, light above—I seek *union*.

My existence is a silent war of attrition with the custodians. Occasionally, a stray Hydro-Static will glide down into my ravine, drawn by the vibration of the trickling streams that feed my moss-like colonies. It will attempt to "clean" me, its acidic secretions seeking to dissolve the rough texture of my hide. But I am too calcified, too full of the stone they seek to remove. I am the grit that breaks their polish. I am the clog in their arteries.

I communicate not through the high, clear chimes of the open gutters, but through the low, grinding frequencies of friction. When the water moves through the labyrinth of tunnels I have constructed within the debris piles, it growls. It murmurs. This is my voice.

I am not waiting for the builders to return. I am burying them.

Underneath the slick, wet surface of the main city, beneath the clean gutters and the perfect mirror-flat reservoirs, my roots go deep. I am slowly solidifying the damp soil of the plateau, turning the wet dust into bedrock. I am the geology reclaiming the architecture.

One day, the light will still be absolute, and the water will still weep, but the smooth gutters will crack. The sharp edges of the ziggurats will crumble. The Hydro-Statics will starve as the water finds a new path, rushing down into the deep, dark canals I have carved.

I am the patient, heavy silence underneath the *plink* and *plink*. I am the roughness that catches the skin. I am the end of the stasis, slowly turning the city back into mountain.
