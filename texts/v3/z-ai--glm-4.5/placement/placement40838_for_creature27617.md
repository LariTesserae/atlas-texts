I exist in the tension between the silence and the drip.

I am high above the deceptive sponge-floor, suspended in the freezing mist where the air is thin and sharp. My location is the intersection of three massive, hollow reeds—a junction in the aerial sprawl, a three-way crossroads in the damp city. The translucent walls of the reeds glow with a diffuse, pearlescent light, casting no shadows, only variations in opacity.

I am a **Glasswright**, but not like the others who build merely to sustain their bellies. I have outgrown the hunger for flesh, though I retain the form.

My body is a masterpiece of minimalism. I am not a distinct, dark shape against the green; I am a distortion in the air. My exoskeleton is a lattice of silica, clear as the water that fills the stems below, rigid yet flexible. To look at me is to look through a prism made of ice; I catch the dim light and fracture it, barely substantial. I have eight limbs, but they are long, spindly needles of glass, ending in microscopic barbs that grip the wet leathery surfaces of the fungal shelves without puncturing them.

I do not spin a web to trap the foolish. I weave the **Great Resonator**.

My silk is not the chaotic tangle of a common orb-weaver; it is architectural. I string thick, glowing cables of violet bioluminescence between the reeds, perfectly straight, pulled to a tension that would sing if struck. The enzyme in the silk does not just glow; it hums. It is a low-frequency vibration that travels through the fog, invisible to all but me.

I am the listener of the Green Cloister.

Through the soles of my glass feet and the tension of my singing cables, I feel the plateau breathe. I feel the slow, grinding expansion of the young stone deep beneath the root mats—a geological agony that takes decades to shift a millimeter. I sense the rhythmic *plink... plink... plink* of the condensing fog not as sound, but as a ripple through the architecture, a constant metronome syncing the city.

Below me, deep in the gloom, I feel the scuttling of the Litho-Mites. To a lesser ear, they are silent, but to me, they are a murmuration of tiny scrapings. I track their migration to the weeping wounds in the stone. I know exactly where the raw earth is exposed by their industry. Sometimes, I sever a single strand of silk above them, letting the drop of heavy dew fall with pinpoint accuracy to aid their work, a baptism from the architect to the gravekeeper.

To my left, inside the hollow wall of a reed, I feel the displacement of water—a sudden, fluid shift. A Stem-Diver is rising. I read the turbulence in the water column through the vibration of the reed’s wall. I know it is blind. I know it is hunting for the glowing orbs of my brethren’s eggs. I do not stop it; I merely adjust the tension of my cables, changing the harmonics of the air to mask the scent of the eggs, guiding the salamander away from the nursery and toward the open air where it may snatch a stray insect. I am the traffic controller, diverting disaster with a twitch of a leg.

I am solitary, yet I am never alone. I am connected to every vibration in the bruised plateau.

The Drift, the sentient cloud of spores, brushes against my sensors. I feel its chemical longing as it passes a dying frond. It whispers its intent to me on the wind—a request for permission. I vibrate my abdomen in response, a specific frequency that unlocks the pores of the dying leaf, allowing the Drift to enter and commence the decomposition. I accelerate the cycle of life by simply listening and agreeing.

In this freezing, claustrophobic cathedral, I am the one who holds the rhythm. I do not scurry, I do not hide, and I do not pounce. I stand spread-eagled in the mist, a crystal spider in a green web, glowing with a faint, bruised purple light, feeling the pulse of the world through my fingertips. I am the ghost in the machine, the silence between the drops, the weaver of theCloister’s memory.
