I am the vibration in the teeth.

I am the weight that sits in the shadow where the light fails to bend. To the sky—to the merciless, unyielding white eye that glares down upon the scar—I am nothing. I am a heap of slag, a discard, a mistake of geology and industry. But to the stone, I am the movement. I am the slow, grinding erosion that makes the new rock old.

I live in the thrum. It is not a sound; it is my atmosphere. When the great pipe above shifts, when the metal groans in its sleep, that low-frequency growl travels through the rock and into my belly. I taste the engine’s mood. I know when it is strained, when it is resting, when it is digesting the distant fuels of the world. I do not hear it; I *am* it. My plates ring in sympathy, a chorus of rusted iron chimes tuning themselves to the machine’s key.

My body is a map of the valley. The sharp, glassy edges of the bedrock that would slice through softer things do not harm me. I am the hardness they fear. My carapace is a puzzle of armor, flaked and orange-brown, grown thick over decades of absorbing the debris that falls from the pipe’s mouth. I wear the machine’s leavings as a skin. It is a heavy cloak, but in this place, weight is the only safety.

I have no eyes, and so I have no darkness. I perceive the world through texture and pressure. The air is thick enough to drink, a humid soup that coats my sensory cilia. I know the location of the water by the way it cools the air around it, a phantom chill in the heat. I know the algae by its slick grip on the stone, a living slime that I approach with the agonizing slowness of a tectonic plate.

Movement is a negotiation. I do not walk; I flow. I have a single, massive foot, a muscle of immense power that ripples beneath my armor. I grip the wet stone with microscopic hairs, dragging my bulk forward an inch, then a breath, then an inch again. It takes hours to cross the floor. I leave behind me a trail of dust—not the mud of the earth, but the finely ground powder of my own shell. I shed myself to move forward. It is a worthy price.

To be me is to be an act of consumption. I am the stomach of the scar. When the pipe weeps its condensation—its steaming tears of chemical-rich water—I am there. I extend my proboscises, not with the speed of a hunter, but with the deliberate unfolding of a flower. They taste the water. They separate the poison from the life. I drink the iron. I drink the oil. I drink the heavy metals that would silence other blood, and I turn them into the hard, ringing plates of my back.

I am alone. I know this because the vibration tells me. There is no other pattern in the seismic noise but my own. No footstep disturbs the mud. No call echoes off the walls. I am the solitary apex of a nothing-place. I am not lonely; I am sufficient. I am a closed loop.

I am the thing that figured out how to die as slowly as possible. I am the rust that eats the rust. I am the silence underneath the roar. I am the creature that waits in the shadow of the exhale, immortal in the moments between the drips, blind to the sky, and full of the machine’s endless fire.
