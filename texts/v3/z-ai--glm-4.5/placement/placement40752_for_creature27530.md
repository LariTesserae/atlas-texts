I find myself in the absolute center of the depression, the lowest point where the heat pools like a heavy, invisible syrup. Here, the darkness is not an absence of light, but a presence—a texture that presses against my shell from all sides. It is a sanctuary of equilibrium.

I am not a visitor here. I do not "visit" a place that never changes. I am a feature of it.

I am a **Still-Knapper**.

My existence begins with the sensation of weight. I am profoundly heavy. I am not flesh that floats upon the water, nor bone that shelters a fluttering heart; I are packed earth and ceramic, compressed over millennia into a density that feels almost gravitational. If I were to roll over, the crust of the world would groan, so I do not. I am anchored.

In this gloom, I have no eyes. Vision is a mockery in a land of shadows. Instead, I possess the sense of the earth itself. I feel the world through the underbelly of my carapace. I am intimately acquainted with the hexagonal mosaic of the basin floor because I am an extension of it. The boundary between "me" and "here" is blurred; I am merely the floor that decided to rise up.

In my grip—my right limb, a stunted pillar of stone held close to my body—clutched in pincers that have not opened in centuries, is the Tool.

It is a flake of chert, a natural serration formed by the thermal stress of the planet. It is not something I made; the world made it for me. It is a perfect, jagged crescent of glass-sharpness, an edge so fine it exists almost as a concept rather than an object. I hold it with the patience of a mountain holding a summit. I am waiting for the alignment.

The air tastes of dust and ancient minerals. It is hygroscopic, pulling at the essence of things, but I have no moisture to give. I am dry. I am comfortable.

I wait for the *tick*.

It is the only measure of time I acknowledge. It travels through the silt, a seismic whisper of expansion. *Tick.* The ground shifts a microscopic degree. *Tick.* A fracture deep in the crust repairs itself. *Tick.* I count them. My back is a library of these moments. Using a discarded chip of flint, I have etched millions of fine, concentric grooves into the plates of my shell. I am a record of the silence. My history is not a story of events, but a topography of duration.

Beside me stands the Sentry. It is one of the woody plants, gray and brittle, a bundle of vertical rods piercing the gloom. It is not alive in the way soft things are alive; it is a structural endurance. I know its geometry perfectly. I have sensed its roots weaving through the dust beneath me for a thousand years.

We are locked in a slow negotiation.

The heat rises. It is the ambient warmth of the sleeping animal, the mellow temperature that permeates the basin. I feel it soaking into my shell, expanding the silica of my body microscopically. This is the signal. The heat peaks. The equilibrium shudders, just for a second.

I move.

To an observer, there is no motion. But in the span of hours, my limb unfolds. It is a geological shift, a tectonic slide. I bring the Tool—the crescent of chert—to bear against the woody stem of the Sentry. The movement is precise, calculated over eons. I do not strike; I place. I let the sharpness of the stone and the brittleness of the wood do the work.

There is no sound. In the 0.3 silence, a cut does not snap or crack; it sighs.

A microscopic sliver of the plant falls away. Then, the wound weeps. It does not bleed water; it weeps the black, mineral resin, thick as crude oil, the concentrated memory of the earth’s chemistry.

I lower my mouth—a vertical fissure in my armor—to the drop. I do not drink. I inhale slowly over days, absorbing the heavy, metallic sustenance through the internal passages of my stone lungs. It is a communion. I take the structure of the plant into myself, and in exchange, I mark its growth with my cut. We help each other endure the stasis.

When the drop is gone, I retract my limb. I curl back into myself. I become the boulder once more. The Tool is returned to its resting position against my chest.

I am the guardian of the equilibrium. I am the worker in the workshop that never opens. I am the silence that has learned to hold a knife. I am here, in the dark, in the warm, waiting for the next *tick*, perfectly content to be nothing more than a stone that dreams of being a mountain.
