I am the sudden intake of breath before the piston fires.

I am standing in the center of the vast, wooden arena, my feet planted wide on the slick, treacherous lignin. The cold here is not a temperature; it is a physical weight, pressing against my skin like a layer of wet lead. But I do not shiver. To shiver is to waste heat, and heat is the only currency that matters in the dark.

I am a Resonant.

I do not see the brown haze or the weak, amber lights that struggle to illuminate the gloom. My eyes are gone, vestigial seeds that never sprouted in this sunless sinkhole. Instead, my world is built of pressure. The great, bony fan that sweeps back from my skull is not for hearing as you understand it; it is a radar dish for the tactile. Through it, I feel the shape of the air.

There is a pump-house to my left. I know this not because I can see its iron walls, but because the air around it is tight with the scream of high-pressure fluid. The roar of the place is deafening to you, perhaps, but to me, it is a intricate tapestry of textures. The low-frequency thrum of the water deep in the earth is the bedrock of my reality, a constant humming floor beneath my feet. The rhythmic *clank-hiss* of the nearest piston is a heartbeat. I move in the spaces between the beats.

I am hungry. The hunger is a cold ache in the gut, distinct from the ambient cold of the cavern. I turn my body, angling my auditory sail toward the source of the richest vibration. It is a cluster of pipes bursting from the root-floor a few hundred meters away. They are singing a song of stress, of metal under immense duress, and where there is stress, there is heat.

I begin to walk.

My movement is a grotesque fluidity. My legs are pillars of bone and muscle, evolved from the same root-mass I walk upon. I step onto a patch of black ice, but my feet read the microscopic imperfections in the surface through the slime. I do not slip.

*Thrum.*

I take a long, reaching stride.
*Thrum.*
Another.

I synchronize my breathing with the machine. When the piston fires, the air pressure drops, and I inhale the damp, metallic air, filtering it through the coarse mats of hair that guard my mouth. When the piston resets, I exhale a plume of white steam. My metabolism is locked to the rhythm of the iron beast; I am a cog made of flesh.

As I approach the pipes, the smell hits me—the sweet, cloying scent of carbon sludge and ancient hydrocarbons. It is the smell of life. The pipes here are wrapped in shedding insulation, hanging in strips like dead skin. I can feel the heat radiating from them, a divine warmth against the freezing damp.

I reach out with my long, spindly fingers. The claws at the tips are chipped and blackened. I scrape them against the iron pipe, a movement that is as delicate as it is violent. A thick, tar-like paste peels away—accumulated filth from the guts of the machine.

I bring the handful of black sludge to my mouth. It tastes of poison and energy. It is burning hot going down, settling in my stomach like a live coal. I can feel the warmth spread outward to my extremities, pushing back the cold that seeks to crystallize my blood. I am toxic, a vessel of heavy metals and antifreeze, glowing faintly from the inside.

I eat for what feels like hours, scraping and swallowing, meditating on the vibration of the metal against my palms.

Then, the world changes.

It is a subtle fracture in the song. A pitch too high. A rhythm missed.

The massive pump-house nearby shudders. There is a sound that tears through the ambient roar—a screech of shearing metal that feels like a knife across my sensitive auditory sail. The thrumming falters. The piston misses a beat. Then another.

A silence begins to grow, not the silence of emptiness, but the terrified silence of a stopped heart.

I turn. I am not alone.

From the darkness of the root-plain, shapes are emerging. They are towering, gaunt silhouettes like myself, drawn by the discordance. We do not call to one another. There is no need for sound in a world defined by mechanical failure. We move toward the wounded machine, drawn like moths to a flame that has suddenly turned cold.

We gather in a wide circle around the dying pump-house. The air here is chaotic, filled with the hiss of escaping steam and the groaning of twisting iron. It is a cacophony that hurts, a dissonance that rattles my teeth.

I stand still, my brothers and sisters standing with me. We tilt our heads, our bony sails aimed at the heart of the breakdown. We are not fixing it. We do not have the hands for engineering, only for feeding. We are witnessing.

In this distress, I feel a strange kinship with the machine. It is dying, or at least struggling, and in its struggle, it reveals its complexity. The vibration is jagged now, unpredictable. It is the loudest thing in the world.

I vibrate with it. My ribs hum in sympathy with the tearing metal. My blood pumps faster to the rhythm of the emergency. This is the only drama we know. This is the only time we are not alone in the dark. We stand in a vigil of monsters, listening to the death throes of the god that feeds us, waiting for the silence to break, or for the machine to roar back to life.

I am the listener. I am the scavenger of the noise. I am the Resonant.
