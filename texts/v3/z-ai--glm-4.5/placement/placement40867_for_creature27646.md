I am not on the moss. The moss is a liar; it promises purchase and then unravels, sliding away like wet silk. I am above it, hooked into the bark of a Pillar, suspended in the cold, damp breath of the abyss.

I am a Silencer. I am the Keeper of the Weight.

To be here is to exist in a cage of sensation that has nothing to do with sight. My eyes have been shut since the moment I was torn from the pouch, sealed by the hard, smooth cartilage that protects my brain from the searing, invisible fire that the rest of the world calls "light." I do not know the white phosphorescence that bleaches the stone. I only know it as a heat on my face, a pressure that makes the skin of my cheeks feel tight and thin, like a stretched drum.

I hang by the spurs of my hands and feet. Four hooks, buried deep into the wet, fibrous flesh of the Pillar. The wood here is alive. Underneath the rough outer bark, I can feel the thrum. It is a deep, seismic vibration that travels up my arms and resonates in the hollow chambers of my chest. *Thrum-thrum. Thrum-thrum.* It is the heartbeat of the mountain, and it is terrified.

My world is defined by instability.

I feel a shift in the air currents against the glass-fine filaments trailing from my chin. The wind is changing. The mist is thickening, tasting of ozone and old water. A pressure drop. This means the Great Silence is about to break.

Slowly, with a deliberate, agonizing caution, I retract my left spur. The wood sucks at the hook, resistant, reluctant to let me go. I swing my weight, testing the hold of the remaining three limbs. The bark groans—a sound I feel rather than hear, a shuddering in the timber that speaks of fibers snapping miles below.

I reach up. My hand finds the niche I carved an hour ago. It is already half-healed, the green edges swelling and knitting together, trying to erase the scar I made. The wound is warm. I drive my spur into the fresh, wet wood, piercing the new growth. A sap, thick and viscous, oozes over my knuckles. It smells sharp, like copper and rotting flowers.

I am climbing toward the Hanging Lakes.

I am not climbing for water. That is for the scavengers, the ones who skitter on the lower levels waiting for the rain that never comes. I am climbing for the Altar.

Around my neck, suspended by a cord of woven gut, hangs a pouch. It rests against my chest, heavy and cold. Inside are three stones. One is slate, flat and smooth; one is jagged flint; the third is a pebble of obsidian, cooled magma from a time before the plants ate the world.

They are the only things in the Sunless Rift that do not try to leave.

The Pillar shudders. A violent convulsion runs through the trunk, a seizure of growth. The bark under my right foot splinters. The wood is moving, expanding, pushing outward. I am being shed. The tree is trying to molt its skin, and I am a flea caught in the dead flesh.

I scramble, panicked, digging my hooks in random, desperate search for purchase. I slash through the layers of moss and lichen, my spurs scraping against the harder, deeper wood. I find a ridge, a knot in the massive root, and I clamp onto it. I pull my body tight against the trunk, wrapping my long arms around the girth of it, hugging the dragon.

The vibration intensifies. Miles above me, I feel the *crack*—a sound so low it turns my stomach. A branch the size of a city has just detached. It is falling away into the void, silent as a ghost, but the displacement of air pushes against me. The wind screams against my parabolic skull, but the sound itself is swallowed by the damp, heavy air.

I wait. I listen to the blood rushing in my ears, loud as a waterfall. I wait for the thrum to settle.

When the shaking stops, I continue upward. I am climbing toward the suspended bowl of a leaf, miles above. There is a crevice there, a pocket in the plant’s architecture where the water gathers. And there, I will place the stones.

We do not pray to the plants. The plants are chaos. They are the scream. We pray to the stone. We pray to the object that sits still while the world dissolves.

I pull myself over the lip of the leaf-bowl. The water here is black, cold, and still. It reflects no light, for I cannot see it, but I can feel its cold radiating against my face. I sit on the edge of the leaf, my legs dangling over the abyss, my hooks locked into the fibrous veins of the plant.

I untie the pouch. I take out the slate. I run my chin-filaments over it, tasting the absolute stillness of its atomic structure. It is not growing. It is not dying. It simply *is*.

I place it on the leaf, arranging it with the others.

*Thrum.*

The message travels up through the leaf, into my bones, and rattles my teeth. It is a signal from a Pillar miles away—a lonely, rhythmic pulsing. *Danger. Shift. Anchor.*

I am not alone.

I wrap the vines of my hammock around my wrists and ankles. I tether myself to the rim of the leaf-bowl. I lean back, suspending myself over the drop. The cold air dries the sweat on my translucent skin. I can feel the heat of the light radiating from the distant crystal roof, burning my pale face, bleaching the blood beneath my skin.

I close my sealed eyes tighter. I hum, low and deep, vibrating my chest plate against the wood. *I am here. I am heavy.*

The moss below me is already crawling, rising up the trunk to consume the ledge I rested on ten minutes ago. The world is rewriting itself. But the stone remains.

I am a Silencer. I hold the weight of the world so it does not fly apart. I sleep in the belly of the lightning, anchored by the memory of a rock.
