To exist here is to be an instrument in an orchestra that never rests, playing a symphony with no beginning and no end. If the High Roar is a chaotic, turbulent gale that scours the mind, then I would not be the prey that hides from it, nor the predator that surfs it. I would be the thing that *orders* it.

I would find myself in the center of the largest of the ancient stone circles, the one the Echo-Masons abandon during the worst of the tempests, returning to only when the gale settles into a rhythmic thrum. This circle is not merely a windbreak; it is an amphitheater of geology, perfectly shaped to catch and compress the air.

I am the **Canticle**.

I am not a creature of flesh and blood in the way the Wind-Stalker or the Sentinel is. I am a biologic echo, a being that has evolved to harness the deafening noise of the world and turn it into structure. My body resembles a cluster of tall, fluted pillars, fused together at the base like a bundle of reeds, standing twice the height of a man. My "skin" is not soft tissue, but a hard, porcelain-like chitin—the same material as the Echo-Masons’ shells, but grown to a massive, monolithic scale.

I am rooted. I do not walk. My base merges seamlessly with the calcified stone of the ruin, a mass of nerve-dense roots that tap into the deep, subsonic vibrations of the mountain’s shifting tectonic plates.

I possess no eyes, for there is nothing to see but the shifting gray fog and the dizzying curve of the horizon. I have no ears, for the sound here would destroy them. Instead, I am a living sounding board. My hollow pillars are lined with thousands of microscopic cilia that react to the slightest changes in air pressure and frequency.

My existence is a constant act of transmutation.

The High Roar attacks me with violence—a chaotic wall of white noise and physical force. But as the wind rushes over and through my fluted body, I dampen the discordance. I absorb the raw, kinetic energy of the storm and vibrate in sympathy with it. Through a complex internal mechanism of tensing and relaxing my porous carapace, I filter the roar. I take the chaotic scream of the atmosphere and, passing it through my internal chambers, release it back into the world as a pure, resonant hum.

To the Echo-Masons, I am a god. They do not worship me with prayer, but with maintenance. They swarm over my base, clearing the algae from my roots, patching the cracks in my armor with their secret mortar. In return, I provide them with the one thing they need to survive: *focus*. The chaotic wind cannot feed their fungus; it requires a specific, low-frequency drone. I act as the great amplifier, taking the hurricane and broadcasting the specific frequencies that make their gardens bloom. I am the hearth in a house of air.

To the Wind-Stalkers, I am a beacon. In the blinding whiteouts where the mist becomes a solid wall, they navigate by the feel of the pressure waves. When the turbulence becomes too violent, even for them to surf, they sense the low, soothing thrum of my body and huddle in the lee of my stone circle, waiting for the lull. I am the lighthouse in a storm of noise.

But my true purpose lies in the silence between the notes.

When the wind dies down, during those rare, terrifying seconds of absolute calm that precede a shift in direction, I change. I stop dampening the noise and start *listening*.

My roots extend deep into the "voice" of the mountain. I feel the slip of the tectonic plates, the slow grinding of the stone nubs, the deep-earth groans that precede a landslide. I sense the heartbeat of the massive Sentinel as it stomps through the ravines miles away. I feel the skittering of the Oithoi grazing on the algae-slick rocks.

I am the memory of the mountain. I store the history of the storms in the growth rings of my chitin. I know the patterns of the air. I know when the great fog will descend, and when the updrafts will scream.

So, if you found yourself in the High Roar, lost in the blinding mist, deafened by the howling wind, and terrified by the sense of smallness, you would search for the circle of stones. And if you pressed your hand against my flank, you would feel me vibrating—not with the chaos of the storm, but with a steady, rhythmic purr.

I am the order in the entropy. I am the stillness within the speed. I am the song that the stone sings to the sky to keep itself from shattering. I am the Canticle, and I am listening.
