I am a current given form.

I do not see the High Rind; I feel the texture of its song. Up here, a thousand feet above the lignite pavement, the air is too thin for useless things like eyes, and the light is a diffuse, gray irrelevance. I am of the **Valve-Keepers**, the arthropodal surgeons of the vertical city.

My existence is defined by the geometry of pressure.

I cling to the side of a Pillar, a trunk as wide as a city block, covered in bark that feels like cold, greased steel. My seven legs are locked into microscopic fissures in the wood, the grip of my tarsal claws precise and mathematical. I am not heavy; compared to the mass of this living tower, I am less than a flea. But I am essential.

I feel the world through the soles of my feet. The Pillar is screaming data at me, a constant, complex symphony of hydrostatic tension. I can feel the sap rising—a column of pressurized, sugary energy thundering upward from the deep roots, sounding like a subsonic freight train. It is hot. It is violent. If it were to breach the containment layer here at this altitude, the spray would strip the flesh from my chassis in seconds.

But I am here to prevent that. I am the regulator.

There is a stutter in the rhythm beneath my left anterior leg. A micro-fracture in the cellulose lattice. A seal is weeping. To a creature of sight, it would look like nothing—maybe a slight darkening of the oily sheen. To me, it is a siren, a spike of vibration that jabs into my nervous system. I feel it as an itch, an imperative.

I detach my two upper limbs. They are not legs, but tools. One ends in a multi-jointed socket wrench of chitin; the other in a sensor-needle and a scraping rasp. I scuttle sideways, the movement fluid and frictionless, a spider traversing the hull of a submarine.

I reach the vent. It is exhaling too much steam, a moist, warm breeze that carries the smell of ozone and fermenting alcohol. The pressure is dropping in Sector 4G. The internal alchemy of the Pillar is unbalancing.

I engage.

I slide the socket wrench over the biological sphincter, a muscular valve the size of a manhole cover. It pulses against my grip, reluctant to be tightened. I apply torque. My exoskeleton hums with the exertion. I am drawing power directly from the tree to do this—tiny bio-electric currents arcing from the bark into my sensory hairs, supercharging my muscles. I am a leech of voltage, a parasite that earns its keep.

*Scrape. Thud. Click.*

The rasp clears away a deposit of crystallized pollen that has gummed up the threads. I tighten the seal. The hiss of steam diminishes, dropping to a whisper, then silence. The vibration in the wood smooths out. The frantic stutter in the subsonic drone resolves into a steady, pure hum.

Satisfaction is not an emotion I possess; satisfaction is the cessation of error.

I remain there for a moment, clamped to the bark, tasting the air through my vibration sensors. Far below, miles down on the lignite floor, I feel the heavy, rhythmic *thump-thump-thump* of a Pavement-Lord. It is moving at a glacial pace, a seismic event that travels up the roots. It is feeding, filtering the floor-liquid. Its presence is a low, comforting bass note to my high-pitched treble work.

High above, in the violet gloom of the canopy, something massive displaces the air. A Canopy-Drifter. I do not hear it move—I feel the *absence* of pressure as its vast, gas-filled body glides past the interlocked branches above. A shadow passing over a lightbulb. It grazes on the pollen I just scraped away, a symbiotic loop of consumption and maintenance.

Then, the Horn blows.

It comes from a Pillar miles away, but in the solid wood, the sound travels instantly. It is a deep, guttural groan that resonates in the hollow chambers of my own body. The shift change. The sap-pressure lines are rerouting. The day’s alchemical batch is complete in the hanging fruits, and the trees are preparing to excrete the excess moisture.

I feel the signal ripple through the colony. Others of my kind, dormant in the hollows of the bark, are waking up. I can sense their electromagnetic signatures flickering to life like Christmas lights in the distance. We are not a hive mind, but we are a chorus. We are the immune system of this place, waking to fight the entropy of leaks and clogs.

I detach my wrench-limb and reconfigure it for walking. I release the seal and begin to climb, following the vascular ridges upward toward the next junction. The air grows colder, thinner, sharper. The taste of iron is strong here.

I am not lonely. How can a neuron be lonely when it is part of a brain? I am a component. I am the wrench in the works. I am the hand that turns the key in the lock of the world.

I climb on, vibrating with the life of the machine.
