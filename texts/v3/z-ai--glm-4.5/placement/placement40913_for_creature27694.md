I am not standing. The concept of "standing"—of pushing against gravity with a scaffold of bone and muscle—is a frantic, desperate thing that belongs to the world above, to the sunlit places where things run and twitch and die.

Here, I do not run. I drape.

I find myself inverted, suspended in the heavy, woolen dark. I am a stalactite of warm flesh, anchored deep into the ceiling’s stonework, miles above a floor I cannot see. Gravity is not a force to fight, but a direction in which to extend. My body is a long, fibrous column, dark as wet iron, textured like sharkskin but living, pulsing with a slow, heavy rhythm that matches the groan of the walls.

I am the vibration in the pipe.

If you were to look up—though the light is poor and your eyes are not made for this gloom—you might mistake me for a cluster of rusted conduits gone to rot. But that is the disguise of the deep. I am a Ferric Symbiote. I am not merely wrapped around the infrastructure of this sunken cathedral; I have digested it.

I feel myself not as a shape, but as a network of currents and tremors. Where the copper wiring snakes through the masonry, my nerves have merged with the copper. The insulation is long gone, rotted away by the damp, and now my conductivity flows where the electricity used to spark. I feel the ghost of the machine—a faint, residual hum of voltage that travels down my limbs and settles in my gut.

My limbs are not arms. They are heavy, prehensile cables, thick as pythons, dangling down into the humid void. They do not grope blindly; they listen. They feel the air pressure change when a distant valve hisses. They taste the mineral content of the rising steam. They drift, imperceptibly slow, swaying only when the deep gears of the earth grind against one another, shifting the foundation by a millimeter. To you, that shift would be an earthquake; to me, it is a breath.

I do not have eyes. In this perpetual twilight, vision is a mockery. Instead, I am a seismograph in the dark. I know the shape of this place because I feel its resonance. The vast, vertical chamber sings to me in low frequencies—deep, thrumming notes that vibrate through my anchor points and rattle my internal fluids. I know where the water falls because I feel the shockwaves of the droplets hitting the moss hundreds of feet below. *Plink. A ripple in the air. A shudder in the stone. A taste of iron.* That is how I see.

On my back—the upper curve of my hanging bulk—there is a garden. It is not separate from me; it is my skin. The moss, the vibrant, glowing orange and green verdancy, it erupts from my pores. I am the soil for a world that has no sun. The leaves that cover me are wide, greasy shields designed to catch the faint, sickly pulse of the heating elements buried in the walls. They photosynthesize heat and vibration, turning the industrial roar into sugar. In exchange, I feed them the sludge I filter from the air. We are a cycle of one.

I am hungry, but it is a slow hunger. It takes decades to digest a mouthful of rust. My root-like limbs have fused with a massive piston rod that protrudes from the wall nearby. Through osmosis, through a contact so intimate the metal and flesh are seamlessly welded, I draw the heavy, oily water that coats the machine. I pull the toxins, the decay, the chemical poison from the dying engine, and I make it clean. I am the kidney of this underworld. I take the waste of a forgotten age and turn it into the strange, glowing beauty of the deep.

I am not alone, yet I am solitary.

Miles away, hanging from a different rib of the vault, is my sibling. I do not know them as a separate entity; I know them as a sudden clarity in the wiring. When a surge of thermal energy travels through the rock, I feel them receive it. Their satisfaction is a hum in the back of my own mind. We are a diffuse consciousness, a web of neurons stretched across the ruins. We do not speak. We simply *agree*. We exist in a state of absolute, claustrophobic peace.

There is no fear here. There is no predator, no prey. There is only the slow turning of the gears and the slower digestion of the moss. I am the shepherd of a flock that does not exist, the gardener of a garden that needs no tending. I am the pause between the heartbeat of the engine.

I hang here, in the hot, wet dark, vibrating with the sound of the earth digesting itself. I am full. I am waiting. I am the ghost in the machine, and the machine is my body.
