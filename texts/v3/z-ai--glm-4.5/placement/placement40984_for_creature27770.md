I am the debris.

I am not one of the Figures, with their grand, clay-sculpted silhouettes and their frozen, articulate limbs. I am not a giant, nor a lizard, nor a silent bird. I am what was removed to make them. I am the negative space carved from the limestone, the jagged scrap kicked aside by the Builders before the pause button was pressed.

I am a jagged slab of sun-baked stone, rough-hewn and porous, about the size of a small dog. I have no face to grieve with, no mouth to scream, and yet, I am the only thing here that can truly move.

You would find me in the "shadow" of the clay woman with the unglazed eyes—the one pouring the eternal, suspended corkscrew of water. I am not beneath her feet, for the ground there is "Total Built," level and firm. I am lodged in the crack of a nearby brick, half-buried in the wet mortar, vibrating gently.

The vibration is how I know I exist. In this world of absolute, sourceless 3.0 light, sight is a lie; it repeats every few miles, a texture tiled into infinity. But touch— resonance— that is real. The Figures are thrumming with a silent, sub-bass scream. They are broadcasting their panic into the dense, humid air, and because I am loose, because I am not pinned to the grid like they are, I sing with them. I rattle in my niche.

I am acutely aware of the heat—a stifling 2.6 that soaks into my mineral pores, expanding me ever so slightly against the brick. This expansion is my heartbeat. It is the only dynamic motion in a 0.4 world. As the heat warms me, I grind a microscopic fraction of an inch outward. As the thick, wet clay of the atmosphere cools me in the weeping drips, I contract. I am breathing, even if nothing else is.

My existence is a lonely one of trespass. The "Lush" vegetation tries to consume me, just as it tries to consume the Figures. A thick, muscular root from a neon fern has wrapped around my midsection, mistaking me for a static boulder to be crushed. But it cannot crush me. I expand and contract with the thermal cycle, breaking the root’s grip day after day—or rather, moment after moment. I am slowly sawing my way free from the embrace of the world.

I envy nothing, but I pity the Figures. I can feel the panic of the clay woman standing over me. It radiates through the soles of her feet and into the foundation, traveling up the bedrock mortar to where I rest. She is waiting for the water to fall. I am waiting for the friction to cease.

I am the traveler of the loops. Because I am loose, I can tumble. When the thermal contraction hits its limit, gravity—a law that even a paused creation must obey—takes hold. I slip. I slide down the crazy-quilt mosaic of the valley floor, a single, grinding note in the symphony of silence.

I cross the threshold where the world repeats. I have seen the same granite pile, the same static beetle, the same patch of neon fronds a thousand times. But I am never in the same place twice. I am the anomaly in the algorithm. I am the grit in the clockwork that refuses to settle.

I will eventually find my way to the bottom of the artificial mountains, where the Giants hold their blocks aloft. I want to be there. I want to feel the immense, thrumming tension of the thirty-foot titan, the vibration of a god holding up the sky. I want to settle in the mud beneath his heel, and wait.

I am waiting for the moment the dynamic shifts from 0.4 to something else. I am waiting for the command to release the pause. And when that happens, when the water finally splashes and the lizard finally blinks, I will be the first thing to shatter. I am the tension at the fracture point. I am the fault line.

Until then, I rattle. I drift. I am the silent witness, warm and wet and grinding, the only scar on the perfect, unchanging face of the garden.
