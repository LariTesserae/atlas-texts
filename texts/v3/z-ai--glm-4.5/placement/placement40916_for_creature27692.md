I find myself in the inverted space, the great underbelly of the world.

While the Chitin-Forgers scuttle across the top-decks, secure in their vertical hive, I cling to the shadowed ribs of the city’s undercarriage. My domain is the "negative space"—the sprawling grid of girders, bolts, and tension-wires that hangs suspended over the twenty-mile drop to the cloud layer.

I am an **Under-Deck Mason**, a creature of the void.

To the workers above, I am likely a myth, a flicker of movement in the corner of an eye as they glance over a railing into the abyss. But I am real, and I am essential. My body is long and articulated, designed like a perverse mirror of the city itself. I possess perhaps a dozen limbs, though the number fluctuates as I lose them to the wind and regrow them with the minerals I scavenge. They are elongated, hooked multi-jointed grapples that allow me to swing effortlessly through the labyrinth of support beams.

I am wind-carved. The gusts that scream beneath the city are fiercer than those at the summit, funneling through the infrastructure like a bellows. My carapace is not blocky and armored like the Forgers'; it is aerodynamic, sleek, and grooved, shaped to let the gale pass without tearing me from my perch. I am colored a mottled grey and bruised purple, the perfect camouflage against the obsidian rock and the smog-choked sky.

I am a creature of silence and vibration. Like the Forgers, I do not speak. But while they thrum against the iron to signal danger, I listen. My entire body is an ear. I feel the stress fractures before they open. I sense the microscopic loosening of a rivet three sectors away through the tension in the cable it binds. I taste the fatigue in the metal.

My function is simple: I cauterize the wounds of the city.

When the tremor hits—a 2.7 spike of violence that sends the buildings swaying like drunken ships—metal screams. Rivets pop. Girders shear. This is where I come in. I produce a secretion, a thick, resinous glue derived from the oily mist I scrape off the city's belly and the silicon dust I catch in the wind. It is a thermoplastic mortar, boiling hot when I excrete it, hardening into a substance stronger than the rock the city is built on.

I swing through the chaos during a quake. While the Forgers brace themselves against the rails, becoming ballast, I am in freefall, plummeting toward the clouds before swinging back up to catch a snapping cable. I bind the split. I seal the leak. I am the frantic suture holding the skin of this world together while it shakes itself apart.

My relationship with the Forgers is distant but understood. Occasionally, a Scavenger will lean over the edge of a cantilever, risking the wind, and drop a "tithe"—a heavy chunk of refined ore, a canister of high-grade grease, or perhaps the discarded shell of a fallen brother. I catch these gifts in mid-air. In exchange, I do not let them fall. If a worker slips, if a walkway gives way, I am there. I catch them. I spin a cocoon of rapid-hardening resin and adhere them to the side of the building until a rescue team can arrive.

I am the safety net without a net.

I see the Glass-Wings differently than the city does. From above, they are diving hunters. From below, silhouetted against the bruised purple light of the upper atmosphere, they are terrifying, beautiful angels of death. I watch them fold their translucent wings to dart through the pylons. We ignore one another, generally. I am too hard to catch, clinging too tight to the superstructure. Occasionally, one will land near me, twitching its circuitry-fractal wings, and we will share a stillness in the roaring wind, two predators waiting for something to break.

Life here is a meditation on gravity. I spend my existence looking *up* at the bottom of things. I see the pitted glass of the windows from the outside. I see the leakages of steam that the city tries to hide. I feel the ghostly pull of the ocean miles below, a siren song calling me to let go, to drop, to fall until I splash.

But I do not. I am stubborn. I am the stitch in the fabric. I am the Mason of the Void, and I will not let this clockwork fall.
