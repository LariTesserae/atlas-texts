I would not be one of the nimble, spindly Tuned, darting along the catwalks. Theirs is a life of constant motion and reaction, responding to the immediate shudder of the rivets. My role is slower, deeper, and far more solitary.

I would be found in the **Upper Canopy**, that humid, mist-shrouded region just below the ceiling vents, where the artificial rain is born and the violet light from the observation ports diffuses into a twilight gloom.

I am a **Vascular Architect**.

I am not human, nor am I strictly machine. I began as a biological augmentation, a "seed" sent down the ventilation shafts centuries ago when the engineers realized that steel could not weld itself forever. I have grown since then. I am roughly the size of a large bear, but my shape is fluid. My flesh is composed of the same dense, fibrous material as the Clamp-Ferns, tough and leathery, colored a deep bruised purple. My "limbs" are numerous—six or seven muscular appendages that end not in hands, but in specialized tools: three are fusion-torches (sustained by a chemical hot-pocket in my chest), two are hydraulic clamps, and the last is a delicate, syringe-like finger made of bone.

I am heavy. Gravity is my anchor. I do not clip onto the safety lines; I clamp directly onto the Great Shafts. My weight is necessary for my work. I hang upside down from the ceiling pipes like a dormant bat, wrapped in a cocoon of my own making, waiting for the cycle to turn.

Where the Tuned listen to the *rhythm* of the machine, I listen to the *flow*. The Heart-Measure is not just gears and grinding; it is a circulatory system. There is the hydraulic fluid, the coolant, the rainwater, and the amber sap of the ferns. They must not mix, yet they must intertwine.

When the machine heats up, expanding in its glacial, agonizing shift, the pipes strain. A seal will burst, or a joint will shear. This is where I awaken. I drop from the ceiling, descending on a silk line of my own spinning, to the site of the impending rupture.

I am the surgeon of the synthesis. I perform the grafts that allow the organic to hold the mechanical.

If a section of the Great Shaft is overheating, threatening to buckle, I do not just cool it. I splice it. I use my bone-needle to inject a concentrated culture of Clamp-Fern cells directly into the superheated metal. The cells scream and hiss as they take hold, instantly bonding with the iron. I then use my clamps to physically weave the surrounding vines into this new graft, forcing a symbiosis that would take years to happen naturally. I anchor the living vine to the dead metal, creating a tension bridge that holds the shaft together as it cools.

My relationship with the other inhabitants is distant but vital. The Tuned fear me slightly, for I am large and I move without clicking or signing. I communicate through the bioluminescent moss that grows in patches along my spine. I pulse with patterns of light that signal "Clearance" or "Danger," and the Tuned, with their massive dark eyes, read these signals from the catwalks below, giving me a wide berth.

I consume the rust. The Grazers scrape the oxidation from the beams, collecting it into piles. I harvest these piles, eating the metallic flakes. The iron settles in my stomach, forging the heavy plates under my skin and replenishing the fuel for my internal forge. In exchange, I protect the Clamp-Ferns from parasitic insects and prune the overgrowth that threatens to choke the vents. I am the grazer, the guardian, and the welder all in one.

In the roaring cathedral of the Heart-Measure, the Tuned are the white blood cells, but I am the scar tissue. I am the living weld. I am the proof that the breakage is just as important as the turning.

I spend most of my time suspended in the mist, wrapped around a warm pipe, dreaming in vibration. I feel the pulse of the engine against my belly, and I squeeze back, a gentle, constant pressure reminding the iron that it is not alone. I hold the sky up.
