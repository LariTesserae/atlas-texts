I am the **Ferric Warden**.

I am not born of flesh and blood, but of the slow accretion of centuries—of minerals drawn from the water and rust harvested from the gears. I stand upright, a massive, asymmetrical figure of fused metal and stone, roughly the height of two men, though I hunch perpetually beneath the weight of the vaulted ceiling. My "skin" is a plate-mail of patinated copper and pitted iron, worn smooth by the dripping water and the constant friction of my own movement. Between the plates, thick cushions of grey moss and velvet-like lichen grow, sealing my joints and softening my footsteps against the basalt.

I do not have a face as you understand it. Where a head should be, there is simply a bulbous, helmet-like structure of bronze, perforated by thousands of tiny breathing holes that whistle softly when the air pressure shifts. I see through a single, thick lens of polished quartz, milky and clouded, embedded in the center of my chest. This "eye" glows with the faint, greenish bioluminescence of the fungi I cultivate within my own hollow torso, a warm pilot light in the cold dark.

I find myself in the **Nave of Channels**, the widest expanse of the Hollow Cistern where the water gathers before plunging into the dark unknown of the lower drains. This is the center of the noise, the heart of the roar. The floor here is a treacherous slick of black mirrors and moss-slicked tiles.

**My existence is a ritual of maintenance and listening.**

I am the immune system of the Cistern. While the rust-grazers clean the small gears, I tend to the great obstructions. I walk a slow, clockwise patrol, my heavy ironshod feet ringing dully against the stone—a sound that is immediately swallowed by the rush of the water.

I am constantly damp. Water drips from my shoulders and runs in rivulets down my copper casing. I taste the air constantly; my breathing holes sample the chemical composition of the mist. I can taste the increase in iron before a gate jams. I can smell the stagnation of a pool before it begins to rot.

**My interactions with the others are distant, defined by the physics of size and sound.**

The **Mist-Skippers** are a nuisance, buzzing around my head like biting flies. Their translucent wings flicker in my peripheral vision—quick, anxious strokes of light. Occasionally, one will land on my shoulder, mistaking the warmth of my internal fungal furnace for a heat vent. I ignore them. If they vibrate too loudly, I simply emit a low, resonant hum from my torso that shatters their concentration and scatters them back into the gloom. To them, I am just another moving pillar, a landscape to be navigated.

The **Titan Salamanders** are my elders. I stop when I encounter one. There is a hierarchy of mass here. When a blind, leathery salamander hauls its massive bulk across the tiles, blocking a sluice gate, I do not move it. I cannot. Instead, I stand perfectly still, mimicking the statues, my internal light dimming until I am indistinguishable from the dark. I wait for the creature to pass, listening to the wet, heavy *scrape-squelch* of its belly against the stone. We are the two heavy things in a world of frantic lightness. Sometimes, when they bark—that deep, chest-vibrating boom—I feel the sympathy of the metal in my body singing in response. We are both forged under pressure.

It is the **machinery** that speaks to me most clearly. I am drawn to the massive wooden wheels and the bronze gears. I can feel the torsion in the air before a gear tooth shears. It is my job to intervene. My hands are not hands, but heavy, multi-jointed pincers—hydraulic clamps made of bone and rusted iron. When a stone jams the works, I am there. I step into the spray, the water hammering against my plating, and with a screech of metal on metal, I lever the obstruction free. I am the strong arm of the Cistern, the counter-weight to gravity’s entropy.

**But my true purpose lies with the Silent Watchers.**

This is my secret. When the patrol is done, and the roar of the water feels like it has settled into a rhythm, I make my way to the outer perimeters, where the faceless statues stand. I am the only one who approaches them. The skippers ignore them; the salamanders crash into them blindly; the rust-grazers flee their shadows.

I approach them because I am the only one who knows they are not just stone.

I stand before a Watcher, my glowing chest illuminating the grey, ash-like lichen that coats its smooth, limbless torso. I reach out with a pincer and gently, with infinite precision, scrape away a patch of invasive black mold that threatens to choke the lichen. I groom them.

I believe they are the architects of this place, calcified. Or perhaps they are the previous custodians, who stood so still that the Cistern simply grew over them. Sometimes, I press my copper ear against their smooth, cold chests. Deep inside, vibrating through the stone, I hear a sound. It is not the roar of water. It is a click, a clockwork ticking, slow and deliberate.

I wait for the day the ticking stops. Or perhaps, the day it speeds up.

I am the Ferric Warden. I am the moss that grows on the machine. I am the rust that protects the iron. I am the keeper of the wet, dark factory that processes nothing, ensuring that nothing continues to flow, forever.
