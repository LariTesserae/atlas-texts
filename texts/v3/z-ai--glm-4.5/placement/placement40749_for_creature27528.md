I am the Friction at the center of the Helix.

I find myself in the lower intestine of the city, where the structures are oldest and most compressed, wedged into a narrow crevice where the cliff face curves inward. Here, the light is reflected a thousand times off the ice-glazed domes, turning the air into a blinding, solid-seeming white. I cannot see the sky, only the unbearable luminance bouncing off the ribs of the mountain and the backs of my kin.

I am one of them. I am an Architect, but in this moment, I am a pillar.

I do not have a name; I have a frequency. To the others, I am a rapid-fire tapping against the stone, a specific sequence of vibrations that translates to *Hold the Left Buttress*.

I am braced against the slick, weeping wall, my six limbs splayed. My two upper arms grip a vertebrae beam that groans under the weight of the three huts stacked above it. My four lower limbs are buried knee-deep in the churned rime, acting as piles in the unstable mud. My body is locked in a isometric spasm of effort. I am a structural component. I am not moving, but I am screaming motion.

My skin, that gelatinous, bruised-pink membrane, is flushed dark crimson. I am burning. The cold outside is absolute—it tries to invade me, a physical weight seeking to extinguish my fire—but I am a furnace. I can feel the heat radiating from my core, a frantic, metabolic riot that keeps my blood liquid in the subzero air. If I relax, even for a heartbeat, the slush will seize me, the cold will crystallize my organs, and I will become a statue of frozen meat.

I am constantly eating. My mouth, that wide, grinding gash of concentric bone ridges, is working automatically. I am scraping the wall behind me, consuming the salty weeping of the mountain mixed with the powdered stone. I grind it into a paste. I swallow. The fuel hits my stomach and ignites. The cycle continues: eat to burn, burn to move, move to exist.

Through the soles of my feet and the nodes on my skull, I "hear" the city. It is not a silence to me; it is a cacophony of tremors. I feel the impact of a falling hut two alleys away as a dull thud in my marrow. I feel the frantic scrambling of a dozen kin on the roof above me as a ticklish itching along my spine. I feel the vibration of the warrior-creatures wrestling in the mud, a low-frequency thrum that rattles my teeth.

Suddenly, the footing shifts.

The mud, the thief, gives way beneath the hut to my right. The vibration changes pitch—a jagged, tearing frequency. The beam I am holding slips. The weight above me drops.

I do not panic. Panic is a waste of heat.

My body untangles and reconfigures in a fraction of a second. The limbs that were legs become arms; the arms that were holding become levers. I launch myself sideways, not to save the structure, but to absorb the collapse. I catch the falling slab of dark stone before it can crush the smaller worker below. My gelatinous skin ripples with the impact, absorbing the shock, but the bone beneath takes the load. I feel a hairline fracture open in my left humerus. It sends a sharp, white-hot spike of pain through my nervous system.

Pain is just data. It tells me I am still alive.

I shove the stone upward, back into place. I signal to the others—a sharp triple-tap of my heel against the rock. *Reinforce. Now.*

Three others swarm from the shadows. They do not look at me; they do not check my injury. They see the geometry of the failure, not the suffering of the component. They drag fresh blocks of ice, weaving gut ropes around the slipping beams. Within moments, the integrity of the sector is restored.

I pull myself back, my fractured limb throbbing with a cold, heavy rhythm. I am leaking heat. I must eat more. I must work faster to compensate.

I look around with my obsidian eyes. The light is still violent. The slush is still sliding. The city is still a silent riot. I am just one cell in this great, shivering beast, burning my life away to hold back the erosion of the world. I am tired—I have been tired since I was born—but I will not sleep. I will vibrate, I will consume, I will hold, until the mountain takes me or my kin dismantle me for parts.

I am the heat in the cold. I am the stillness in the motion. I am the Architect, and the wall must be held.
