I exist in the frequencies that the others ignore.

I am not on the floor, where the heavy, wet air pools and the Ground-Keepers scrape their wrench-shaped teeth against the slate. I am not in the open vaults where the copper-feathered Flock churns the twilight into a storm of static. I am higher, and deeper, suspended in the interstitial spaces where the architecture begins to bend.

I am a clinger. A dweller in the vertical.

My body is a tapestry of wet slate-grey flesh and conductive filaments. I am not large in the way the pillars are large, but I am long—perhaps twenty feet from snout to tail, with a skeletal structure that is less bone and more a lattice of flexible carbon and calcified wood. I possess six limbs, but they are not legs for walking; they are elongated, hook-tipped grapples of keratin and iron that sink deep into the crevices of the great pillars. I hang like a sloth, but with the tension of a drawn bow.

My place is the "Sub-Canopy," the shadowed region twenty feet below the primary vascular cluster, where the immense trunks begin to bulge with the pressure of the ascending sap. It is a zone of intense heat and deafening vibration. Down here, you do not hear the screeching of the birds; you feel the thrum of the piston-hearts deep within the wood, a bass note that rattles your very marrow.

I am the **Resonant Tuner**.

My role is maintenance, but of a subtler kind than the Ground-Keepers. They prune the bark; I tune the frequency.

You see, the Garden-Gallery is not just a machine; it is an instrument. The pillars, as they pump sap and expand with the heat, create a symphony of friction. If the vibrations align wrong, if the harmonic frequencies of one pillar clash with its neighbor, the structural integrity of the entire basin could shatter. The wood would crack. The metal would fatigue.

I prevent this.

I do not have eyes. At some point in the lineage of my species, sight became obsolete in this bruised gloom. Instead, my face is dominated by a complex, sensory organ—a "lyre" of exposed cartilage and nerve endings that vibrates in sympathy with the pillar I cling to. I feel the world through sound. I "see" the stress fractures in the lignin as dissonant notes. I "smell" the overheating of a gear-train as a sharp, piercing whine in the back of my skull.

My existence is a constant, drifting meditation of adjustment.

I move slowly, dragging my elongated body across the damp, steel-hard bark of a pillar. I pause, pressing the broad, flat side of my head against the vibrating surface. I listen. *Thrum-thrum-screeee-thrum.* The screech is subtle, a hairline fracture in the harmony caused by a calcification in the vascular tissue nearby.

I extend one of my slender arms, not to claw, but to *pluck*. My fingertips are fitted with striated pads that, when struck against the bark, emit a counter-frequency. I tap the wood. *Bonk.* The sound travels into the pillar, cancelling out the dissonance, smoothing the turbulent flow of the sap. It is acupuncture for a titan.

I feed on the friction.

My mouth is a small, circular orifice located in my chest, protected by a cage of ribs. It does not chew. It inhales. When I correct a harmonic imbalance, when I smooth out a jagged vibration, the energy released—manifesting as a tiny spike of heat and kinetic waste—is vented directly into my chest. I breathe in the entropy. I eat the errors. A diet of chaos keeps the system pure, and keeps me warm in the damp dark.

I am solitary, but not alone. I can sense the others of my kind clinging to distant pillars in the gloom. We communicate not by voice, but by strumming the pillars themselves. A slow, melancholic thrumming travels through the root system, a network of subsonic chatter. *I am here. The third ventricle is tight. The mist is late.* We are the nervous system of the forest, a distributed consciousness of wire and meat that ensures the heart keeps beating without arrhythmia.

We are feared by nothing, because we are the background. To the Enforcers above, we are just part of the machinery, strange growths on the bark. To the Ground-Keepers below, we are shadows blocking the phosphorescent rain.

But we know everything. When a copper-bird falls from the sky, I feel the impact through the pillar before I hear the splash. When a deep-dweller Sap-Swarmer jerks inside a vein, I feel the tremor in my fingertips.

I am currently wrapped around the main support strut of the old central grid, the one that used to be a skyscraper's elevator shaft. The vibration here is intense, a low-frequency groan that tastes of iron and ancient history. My body is pressed flat against the warmth, my internal fluids humming in perfect pitch with the engine.

I am not just a creature in this place. I am the silence between the noises. I am the filter that turns the cacophony into music. I am the Tuner, and the Garden-Gallery is my song.
