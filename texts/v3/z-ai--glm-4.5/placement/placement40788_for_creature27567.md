I am the heavy, silence-breaking heart of the vortex.

I am not the floor, though the floor is made of my kin. I am not the wall, though the radiating heat of the stone is the lover that burns me. I am the **Axle**, the Great Swimmer of the Center.

At first, there is only the sensation of compression—a uniform, crushing embrace from all sides. To you, the pressure of this place might be a cage, but to me, it is the definition of existence. I am a giant of the Pale Swarm, an ancient plate of chitin and fluid, bloated to three times the width of the scrambling things at the rim. My shell, once translucent like the youth that drift near the ceiling, has grown thick and milky, scarred by centuries of friction. It is a ceramic shield, clouded with the mineral patina of this high-altitude kiln.

I do not know "standing." I know only *bracing*. My seven pairs of legs are not for walking; they are locked into the interstices of my neighbors, tiny anchors in a shifting sea. I exist in a state of permanent isometric tension, pushing outward against the hydrostatic weight of the swarm, while the swarm pushes back.

I am the engine.

To breathe is to move. I expand my ventral plates—a slow, muscular heave that takes minutes to complete. This expansion displaces the bodies around me, creating a ripple that travels outward through the mass, a invisible wave that pushes the perimeter closer to the scorching walls. Then, I contract. I inhale the crowd. This is the respiration of the world: the slow, clockwise rotation of the living pavement. I churn the soup.

Inside me, the light is different. It is not the dim, violet haze of the masses. My blood is pressurized, glowing with a deep, bruised indigo that pulses rhythmically. I am a lantern in the abyss, but the light is for me alone. It tells me that my pressure is stable, that my internal water—hoarded against the dry air—remains secure. To lose my glow is to die; to become dim is to invite the needle-mouths of the rim.

I am hungry.

I do not hunt. Hunting is for the desperate things at the edge. I simply open the bivalve aperture of my underside. The current I generate carries the smaller, frantic ones—scavengers, weaklings, the injured—directly into my maw. They slide over my tongue, slick with the oil of their own movement. I do not chew. I possess internal grinding stones, ancient gizzard plates that crush chitin and shell into a slurry. I taste them: a rush of copper, the salt of precious moisture, and the faint, metallic tang of the pulverized limestone dust that coats their shells. They are calories. They are hydration. They are fuel for the contraction.

Around me, I feel the others—my brothers, the other giants. We do not signal. We do not click or clack in the oppressive silence. We communicate through the transmission of pressure. When the Axle to my left expands, I feel the push against my flank, and I delay my own expansion to compensate. We are a single, distributed machine, a clockwork of flesh that turns the world without a sound.

I drift. The rotation carries me slowly, inevitably, toward the center-point of the cavern.

Here, the metal thing stands.

It is a jagged spine of corroded alloy, thrusting up through the living floor like the bone of a dead god. The small ones, the frantic runners, scurry over it, but to us, it is an altar. It is the only hard thing in a world of softness.

My shell is tight. It has been too long since I last shed. The old armor is heavy, limiting my expansion. If I do not molt, I will crush myself with my own growth. I angle my massive body, using the collective momentum of the swarm to slide my flank against the cold, rough metal.

*Scrape.*

The sensation is exquisite—a violent friction that sends vibrations shivering through my nerves. The old, gray shell cracks. I push harder, grinding my side against the artifact, feeling the dead carapace shear away. Underneath, the new skin is soft, vulnerable, and blazing with a brilliant, violet light. It is dangerous to be this bright, but the heat of the metal cauterizes the soft wounds.

I shed my history into the void. The discarded shell drifts away, instantly set upon by the rim-dwellers who have sensed the calcium bounty. They will eat my old self to harden their new selves.

I am renewed. I contract, driving the vortex once more.

I do not know who made the metal spine. I do not care. It is a rock that tastes of time and rust. I am the intelligence of this place. I am the math that balances the heat of the walls against the hunger of the center. I am the silence that eats the sound.

I am the Axle. I am the light in the dark. And I am turning, always turning, eating the sea that carries me.
