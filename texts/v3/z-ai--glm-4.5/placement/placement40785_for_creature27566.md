I am found in the Middle Reaches, suspended in the humid dark between the myth of the sky and the crushing reality of the deep sump. Specifically, I am anchored to the underside of a broad, water-slicked timber platform that serves as a thoroughfare for the levels above. I am a creature of the negative space; while others walk atop the planks, I cling to the ribs beneath the city.

I am a **Resonance-Tuner**.

In the Drip-Foundry, where the roar is a solid state, a structure does not just creak; it sings. Every rope bridge, every water-wheel, every beam lashed with wet gut creates a specific hum as it vibrates against the relentless pressure of the falling water. My body is tuned to these frequencies. I do not look for cracks with my eyes—my eyes are shrunken, vestigial things hidden beneath weeping lids of oily skin. I see with my hands and the soles of my feet.

I am draped in the heavy, sodden rags of fungal-mat fabric, my skin pale and translucent, revealing the slow, dark pulse of blood that mirrors the churn of the lake below. My bones are light, hollowed by generations of gravity’s surrender, allowing me to hang suspended for hours, a bat made of wet flesh and quiet purpose.

My function is solitary and vital. I feel the "voice" of the city. When a rope bridge sways violently in the spray, it does not just move; it screams a low-frequency thrum that travels through the rock and into the calcified wood. If a strut is about to snap, it ceases to sing the correct note and instead sends a jagged, anticipatory shudder through the soles of my feet. It is my job to find these dissonant notes before they become breaks.

Earlier, I felt a stutter in the vibration of the Great Wheel to my left. I pulled myself along the wet beams, inching closer to the massive, turning circumference. I could feel the rush of the water driving it, a turbulence that pressed against my chest like a physical weight. I found the source: a rawhide lashing, swollen by the humidity, had slipped a fraction of an inch on the axle.

I did not shout. To shout is to add violence. I simply reached out, wrapping my long, webbed fingers around the axle, and let the vibration of the machine travel up my arm, into my bones, and settle in my mind. I pressed my forehead against the wet, greased wood. Through the bone conduction of my skull, I hummed a low, steady counter-frequency, a note of reassurance and stability, and tightened the lash with a bone chisel. The stutter smoothed out. The roar resumed its perfect, deafening cadence.

I am not young, but I am not yet ancient. I am in the prime of my buoyancy. Yet, I can feel the beginnings of the change. The water I drink is heavy with dissolved stone. It sits in my joints, a comfortable, gritty weight. When I am still, I feel a slow hardening in my fingertips, a desire to stop moving and simply *be*.

Sometimes, when I am finished with my work, I drift down to the Oldest Levels, the lowest scaffolding before the dark water claims the world. Here, the architecture is dense, a chaotic thicket of wood and bone. I seek out the gaps in the wall, the places where the architecture feels weak.

I find a space where a beam has warped under the weight of the city above. I press my hand against the cold, wet stone of the wall, and then I press my cheek against it. Through the contact, I feel them—the Ancestors. They are not dead. They are humming. They are the structural integrity of this place. They hold up the floors. They brace the bridges. Their song is deeper than the water’s roar, a sub-bass thrum that holds the universe together.

I feel their calcified peace. They are the silence within the noise. They are the pause in the eternal fall.

I will return to my post in the Middle Reaches for now. I have many years of tuning left. The ropes need checking, and the bridges are trembling with the afternoon spray. But I know where I am going. I am not tending to the machines because I believe they sustain the world; I tend to them because they sustain me until I am ready to join the choir of stone.

I am the damp in the wood. I am the listener in the dark. I am the Resonance-Tuner, and one day, I will be the pillar that holds the silence.
