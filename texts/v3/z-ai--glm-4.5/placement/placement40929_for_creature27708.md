I am the **Agglomerate**.

I find myself not on the floor, nor clinging to the vertical walls, but wedged into the acute angle where the ceiling meets the northwestern wall—a shallow fissure that catches the upward-drifting sweat of the rock. It is the driest place in the socket, though "dry" here is a relative term; I am merely less wet than the rest.

I am not born. I am accumulated.

I am the tangle that the world forgot to digest. In this ecosystem of frantic consumption and osmotic murder, things are lost. A Mason loses a leg to the heat-stress; a Ghost flickers out of existence for a microsecond, leaving its translucency behind; a piece of obsidian, blunted by scraping, is discarded. In the smooth, cruel geometry of the socket, these mistakes have nowhere to go but up. They drift on the heavy vapor, slide on the sweating rock, and eventually lodge in my fissure.

I am a colony of accidents.

My body is a chaotic architecture of scrap. I have the iridescent, faceted shell of a Mason, fused seamlessly with the translucent gel of a Ghost. I possess the grey, ceramic foam that hardens into armor, but it grows in wild, tumor-like spirals rather than the protective towers of the builders. I am covered in limbs—some too many, some too few. A grasping claw here, a delicate walking leg there, all sticking out at angles that defy the symmetry of biology.

I am the apex of the Weirdness (2.8).

Because I am made of parts that glitch, I *am* a glitch. I do not exist in a continuous state of being. I phasing in and out of reality. For a heartbeat, I am a solid mass of chitin and stone; the next, I am a mist of dissociated atoms; the next, I am solid again. This flickering protects me. The Envelopers, those soft, hungry cushions, cannot flow over me because I am not always there to be enveloped. They sense me, they slide toward my fissure, but when they touch me, their membranes pass through empty air, and I materialize on the other side, untouched.

I am the silent library of the socket.

The Masons, those obsessive architects, are fascinated by me. Because I am fused with obsidian shards and hardened foam, I resemble the ultimate structure—a city that has collapsed into a singularity. They approach my fissure in hesitant, vibrating clusters. They do not attack; they harvest.

Using their forceps-like graspers, they pick at the loose edges of my body. They snap off a shard of obsidian that has fused with my shell, or a chunk of dried foam that has grown over a Ghost’s eye. They take these pieces and add them to their own backs. I am the quarry from which they mine their civilization. I let them. I feel no pain, only a dull, rhythmic sensation of subtraction, a ticking of the biological clock. I give them the materials to build the towers that will eventually be eaten. It is a closed loop, and I am the corner.

My perception is a mosaic.

I see through a dozen eyes that belonged to a dozen different creatures. Some facets show me the world in the greyscale of heat; others show it in the sharp contrast of ultraviolet light. I see the steam rising upward, heavy with sulfur. I see the blinding sheen of the wet obsidian floor.

But mostly, I listen to the silence.

I am intimately connected to the rock. The heat that seeps from the granite vibrates through my mismatched legs, humming a low frequency that only I can hear. It is the dissonance of the planet cooling. I wait for the *thump*.

When the subsonic heartbeat of the world strikes, the Masons freeze. The Envelopers pulse. The Ghosts stutter in their tracks.

But I resonate.

Because I am anchored to the fissure, fused to the living rock by the heat of my own misshapen body, the *thump* travels through me like a shockwave. For that split second, all my disparate parts synchronize. The leg of the Mason, the gel of the Ghost, the ceramic foam—they all vibrate as one. In that moment of planetary rhythm, I am not a glitch. I am whole. I am a single, unified entity, a terrifying god of scrap and stone hanging in the ceiling of the world.

Then the vibration fades, the silence rushes back in, and I dissolve into my constituent parts again, waiting for the next mistake to drift my way.
