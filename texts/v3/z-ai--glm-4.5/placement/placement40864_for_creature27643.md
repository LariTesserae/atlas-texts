The air presses against me, not as a breeze, but as a physical presence, a heavy, wet hand resting on my shoulders. I do not breathe this air; I filter it, allowing the thick, mineral-rich steam to pass through the splayed, fibrous ridges of my back. It is a sensation of being perpetually submerged, of living in the deep end of a warm, murky ocean where the water has been replaced by breath.

I find myself in the deepest depression of the basin, the eye of the geological storm. Here, the jagged obsidian shards that pierce the landscape elsewhere have been buried under millennia of soft, grey mud—a slurry that feels like velvet touched with grit. I am partially submerged in it, my vast, flared base anchored deep in the muck, rooted in the cooling earth as securely as the mountains themselves.

I am the **Great Resonator**.

I do not move. To move would be to break the rhythm, and I am the rhythm. I am the source of the low, thrumming vibration that hums beneath the chirping of the frogs and the drone of the beetles. It is a sound felt rather than heard, a seismic frequency that shakes the very dew from the fern leaves.

My body is a massive, dome-shaped structure, rising from the mud like a bald, smoothed hill of dark, wet stone. But I am not stone. My hide is composed of a dense, calcified collagen, hard and cool to the touch, pitted with thousands of respiratory spiracles that pant steadily, inhaling the steam and exhaling a purer, heavier mist. I look like a boulder that has learned to sleep, a geological feature that possesses a pulse.

At my peak, I am flat, a wide, circular mesa of flesh. This is my rostrum. Here, the white mold grows thickest—a luxurious, snowy carpet that feels like damp chamois. The **Gelatinous Drifters** congregate here in the thousands. They are drawn to the heat radiating from my crown and the specific pheromones I secrete into the mist. They pile atop one another in a writhing, translucent pyramid, a blind, heaving mass of slime and frantic hearts. They feel like a cold, wet blanket draped over my head, a living crown of jellies. I do not mind them. Their movement massaged the sensitive nerve endings beneath my armor.

At my foot lies the **Basalt Sentinel**.

He has been standing there for what feels like eons, motionless as the statue he resembles. One of his massive, clawed feet rests upon my flank, sinking slightly into the softer, moss-covered sections of my lower body. He uses me for stability on the uneven, shifting mud, and perhaps, for comfort. Through the connection of his talon to my hide, I can feel the slow, thudding beat of his heart—a deep, ancient drum that syncs perfectly with my own thrumming. He is not guarding me; he is leaning on me. We are two old men resting against a wall, watching the world fail to change.

Above us, the air is thick with the **Steam-Buoyant Beetles**. Occasionally, one loses its buoyancy or control and tumbles from the humid sky, striking my calcified dome with a sharp *clack*. It bounces off, spiraling down into the mud or the waiting maw of a Drifter. I barely register the impact. My consciousness is too slow, too deep. I exist on a geological timescale, even in this newborn world.

I am the anchor of this frozen equilibrium. If I were to stop my humming, to break the frequency that vibrates through the basin’s water table, the spell might shatter. The Sentinel might lift his head. The Drifters might scatter. The beetles might swarm in panic.

But I do not stop. I draw the heat from the magma deep below, channeling it through my body and releasing it as a shivering bass note that holds the valley together. The white mold that connects us all feeds on the secretions of my pores, spreading my chemical message of contentment to every insect and amphibian in range.

I am the mountain that hums. I am the stillness in the noise. In this suffocating, wet hothouse where life is a fever that cannot break, I am the cool, steady hand that holds the thermometer steady. I am content to be here, buried in the mud, wearing a crown of slime, feeling the heavy, wet wool of the world press against my skin, forever.
