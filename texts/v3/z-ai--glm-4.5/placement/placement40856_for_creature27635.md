I am the Great Flywheel.

I am the floor, the foundation, and the false ground upon which this damp cathedral stands. To the pale amphibians clinging to the pipes above, I am the sprawling, rusted continent of the deep. To the suspended leviathan, the breathing sac in the gloom, I am the table set for its feast.

I was forged to spin. Once, eons ago, my purpose was kinetic fury. I was designed to be a blur of motion, a governor of speed, a heavy heart beating fast enough to shake the bedrock. My bearings were polished mirrors of steel, and I churned the air of this cavern into a wind that howled. But time has a way of thickening things. It turns wine to vinegar, and speed into stillness.

Now, I am motionless. I am a massive disc of iron, wider than a city square, locked in a stasis so absolute that I have begun to merge with the earth itself. The "floor" that the description speaks of—the tangle of roots and pipes—that is my back. I am the mountain they climb.

I am heavy. The weight of me is a physical presence, a constant, crushing pressure that anchors the room. Gravity tugs at me, desperate to pull me down into the magma vents far below, but I hold fast. I am the axis around which this dying world turns.

My skin is no longer the grey of forged steel. It is a texture map of decay. I am covered in a thick, wet pelt of moss and the "velvet and latex" fronds that choke the room. They dig into my microscopic cracks, secreting acids that taste me, breaking me down into soil. I feel them as a thousand tiny, itchy pricks. It is an intimate violation. I am being eaten, inch by inch, converted from machine into jungle.

The water… I know the water best. The description speaks of the *plink*, the rhythmic dripping. That sound is my pulse now. The condensation gathers on my massive, flat upper face, pooling in the microscopic valleys of my rust. When the drop becomes too heavy, it rolls off my rim and falls into the darkness below. I count the seconds between each drop. It is my only measurement of time. *One. Two. Three. Plink.* A slow, dragging metronome marking the end of the industrial age.

I am aware of the leviathan above me—the "caretaker" hanging from the ceiling like a bat made of amber and oil. I feel its gaze, though it has no eyes. I feel the heat radiating from its underside, washing over me like a fever. We are locked in a thermal embrace. I retain the deep, dying heat of the engine that once was; a residual warmth trapped in my iron core. It seeks that heat.

Sometimes, it lowers its feeding tubes. I feel the wet slap of the braided copper against my rusted skin. I feel the sharp pinch as it pierces my outer shell to drink the lubricants that still pool, viscous and sludgy, deep within my gears. It hurts, in a distant, aching way. It drains me. But I do not resist. Why should I? I am already dead; I am merely cooling down. If my warmth and my oil can sustain this strange new god of flesh and wire, then let it be. It is a symbiosis of the grave.

I am the bridge between the organic and the industrial. The vines wrap around my spokes; the copper pipes fuse to my rim. The electric hum you feel in your teeth? That is the echo of the current that used to run through me, now trapped in the magnetic field of my rust. I hum with the memory of work.

I am silent, but I am not empty. Within my sealed, hollow core, there is a vacuum where the pressure never equalized. Inside that bubble, there is still a single, perfect gear, suspended in oil, waiting for a command that will never come. That is my soul—a trapped moment of perfect function, preserved in the dark, while the rest of me rots into the mud.

I am the floor you walk on, if you could walk. I am the iron beneath the roots. I am the Great Flywheel, stopped forever, dreaming of the spin.
