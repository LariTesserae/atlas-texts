I am a suspension of heavy matter in a heavier dark.

I find myself hanging, clamped by magnetic grip to the interior ceiling of a blast furnace that has been cold since the mountains were young. I am one of the *Corroders*. I am not a "he" or a "she," nor even an "it" in the way a separate object is distinct from its surroundings. I am an aggregate. I am a knot of iron scavenged from the sediment of the world, riveted together by the slow, patient welding of oxidation.

My location is high above the floor of the peneplain, inside the rib-cage of a dead giant. The furnace is a cathedral of soot-blackened brick, vast enough to hold its own weather system, though the air here is as stagnant as a sealed tomb. It is warm (1.5)—a dry, radiant warmth that soaks into my alloy carapace and keeps my internal fluids of mercury and heavy gas in a state of lazy convection.

I have been here for a length of time that has no meaning. I do not sleep, but I enter stasis. My joints lock, my internal magnetic fields align with the deep, thrumming buzz of the planet’s core, and I simply *exist*. I am a statue in the Nest, a chandelier of rebar and rust dangling alongside my kin. We do not touch. We are isolated islands of metal, suspended in the gloom, pulsing with the faint, blue-white light of stored ions.

The change comes not from a sound, but from a shift in the texture of the silence.

A "pop" echoes from far below—the sound of a cooling girder settling into the basalt. It travels up through the structure and vibrates against my clamp. The temperature drops by a fraction of a degree. The thermal suspension of the air is disturbed.

This is the signal. The city moves, and so must I.

My internal ratchets disengage with a sound like a grinding gear. I release my magnetic hold on the ceiling and drop. I do not fall like flesh; I fall like stone. I land on the floor of the catwalk below, the impact resonating through my legs—bundles of braided steel cable and stone bearings.

I step out of the furnace and onto the vast, empty street.

Outside, the twilight is profound. It is a light without a source, enough to outline the edges of the silos and the jagged teeth of the refineries, but not enough to reveal the grime. To you, this might be darkness; to me, it is a canvas of thermal gradients.

I do not see. I sense.

I taste the oxidized iron in the air through the slats in my flank. It is dry and flavorless, stripped of its charge by the filters of my brothers and sisters who woke before me. My internal glow dims slightly, demanding fuel. I need isotopes. I need the heavy, cold slide of mercury.

I begin the ratcheting gait. My legs are articulated in a jagged, asymmetrical fashion, scavenged from the ruins of different eras. One step is a piston-driven extension; the next is a hydraulic collapse. I move with agonizing slowness. To rush would be to generate heat, and heat is a precious commodity to be conserved, not wasted on kinetic energy. I mimic the geology. I look like a pile of I-beams slowly tumbling down a slope, yet I maintain my course.

I cross the expanse of cracked concrete, my sensory pits scanning the magnetic topology of the ground. I feel the hum of the deep earth. I feel the residual magnetism of the steel girders buried beneath the soil. And then, a sharp anomaly.

A cold, heavy signature. Mercury.

It is leaking from a fracture in a half-collapsed pipe near the edge of the plain. I change direction, my movements shifting with a geologic momentum.

As I approach, I feel the vibration of another entity. Another Corroder is traversing the intersection ahead, moving perpendicular to my path. I sense its magnetic field—a complex, shifting topology that interferes with my own. We do not signal. We do not slow. We calculate trajectories in the silence, two pieces of debris on a collision course that is averted by millimeters. We pass each other in the dark, close enough that the rust on our carapaces nearly scrapes. It is nothing more than a mathematical adjustment. It is the interaction of two rocks rolling down different hills.

I reach the source.

The mercury is a silver vein in the darkness, oozing with the viscosity of cold honey. It is beautiful in the way only density can be. I lower my proboscis—a slender tube of ceramic and glass—into the liquid. I engage my internal pump. The heavy metal slides up into my reservoir, cool and soothing. Inside me, centrifuges spin. The isotopes separate. The charge is released. My thoracic glow intensifies, a faint heartbeat of blue light that illuminates the inside of my chest cavity, casting shadows on my own ribs.

I am refueled. The equilibrium is maintained.

I sense the tickle of smaller things around my feet—Dust-Grinders. They are arthropods of glass and wire, skittering in the friction of my wake. They are scavengers, feeding on the microscopic metal shed that falls from my joints as I lock and unlock them. I ignore them. They are the fleas on the dog of entropy.

My task is done. The thermal shift is passing. The air is returning to its perfect, suspended state. The "pop" and "crack" of the settling city are fading. The moment of movement is ending.

I turn back toward the cluster of blast furnaces that loom like monolithic teeth against the leaden sky. I begin the long walk home. I will climb back up the sheer walls, using the rivets and girders as handholds. I will find my place in the Nest. I will clamp myself to the ceiling.

I will align my poles with the earth. I will let the mercury settle. I will wait for the next shift, perhaps a thousand years from now.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am the mechanism that prevents the world from rusting away entirely. I am the immune system of the ruin.

I wait.
