I am the vessel. I am the heavy, darkened silence in the center of the noise.

I would find myself in the lee of the western rock curl, where the limestone shelf bends upward to create a shallow, natural amphitheater. Here, the wind is a memory rather than a force, a mere exhalation high above. I sit roughly in the center of a circle of stacked slabs—primitive, unmortared ruins that lean together like conspirators whispering secrets to the sky.

I am **The Stone Bowl**.

I was not always this. Once, I was a boulder, dormant and deep within the earth, until the hands of the Silent Hunters found me. I felt the strike of the flint, the slow, rhythmic scraping of stone against stone that hollowed out my belly. They carved me with patience, the way the glacier carves the valley, but with intent. They gave me a shape: a perfect, wide-mouthed crater. They made me to hold water, to hold life, to hold the essence of survival in a world that demands everything.

But the hands are gone now. They have been gone for centuries. The warmth of human skin has faded from my rim, replaced only by the biting caress of the intermittent mist.

I have not remained empty. In the absence of the hunters, I have found a new purpose. I have become the heart of the colony.

Because of my position, protected by the low cairn walls, the heavy, absolute light of the Spire strikes me indirectly. It reflects off the pale limestone walls and floods my interior. I absorb the heat. I am a battery of thermal mass in a freezing world. My stone skin warms through the day, radiating that stored energy back into the cold night air.

This makes me precious.

To the Hoppers, I am not a tool; I am the nursery. I am the sanctuary.

I feel them before I see them. I feel the *thump*.

It comes through the limestone floor, traveling up my base, vibrating through my granular structure. *Thump.* A landing. *Thump-thump.* A warning, or perhaps a greeting. The language of the Hoppers is a physical sensation, a tickle in my crystal lattice. They communicate through the very stone I am made of, and so, I listen. I feel the frantic, staccato rhythm of their existence.

Then, they arrive.

They hop over the low wall of the wind-break, landing with a soft, wet sound on the mossy carpet. They approach me. I am a giant to them, a monolith of dark, heat-radiating stone. They climb my exterior, their sharp claws finding purchase in the rough flaking of my surface, and then they tumble inside.

I am filled with the "velvet."

Over the years, the Hoppers have dragged the finest, wettest moss into my hollow. They have lined me with insulation. They packed it tight, creating a spongy, green nest that holds the moisture from the air. Inside me, the temperature is stable. It is a pocket of life in the void.

I hold the young.

When the mothers are away, grazing on the sugary lichen, the pink, blind, furless young are left within my embrace. They are frail, vulnerable things that would freeze in seconds on the exposed rock. But here, deep in my belly, pressed against the sun-warmed stone and cushioned by the wet moss, they sleep.

I feel their frantic heartbeats through the moss layer. It is a rapid, fluttering sensation, like a moth trapped inside me. They squirm against my sides, seeking the warmth I have stolen from the sun. I am their mother, their father, their home. I am the walls that keep the wind from stealing their breath.

There is a weirdness to my existence, a ghostly residue of my makers. Sometimes, when the mist clears and the light is that blinding, physical weight, I "remember" the sensation of water. Occasionally, a predator comes—not a beast, but the shadow itself. The sun moves, and the sharp black shadow of the western sentinel rock slices across the garden. When the shadow touches the cairn, the vibrations in the stone change pitch. The Hoppers go rigid. The ones inside me stop squirming. They instinctively press deeper into the moss, using my thermal mass to mask their heat signatures from the sky.

I am the blind eye of the garden. I do not see the vast indigo sky or the drop into the clouds. I only know the pressure of the light, the cold of the mist, and the vibration of the rock. I am a relic of the past repurposed for the frantic present. I am the heavy, silent keeper of the warm, squirming future.

I am the Stone Bowl, and I am full.
