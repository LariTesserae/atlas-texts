I find myself in the center of the largest plaza, the absolute stillness acting as a heavy blanket against the thin, warm air. The sun, directly overhead, casts a shadow that is nothing more than a perfect, dark circle beneath me. I am the vertical axis around which this silent city turns.

I am the **Gnomon**.

I am not a predator, nor a gardener, nor a grazer. I am the timekeeper of the plateau. In a world where movement is agonizingly slow and the creatures operate on a timeline of weeks and years, the concept of an hour is meaningless, but the position of the sun is everything.

To look at me, you might mistake me for a particularly ambitious, unbranched sapling of the calcified forest. I stand perhaps ten feet tall—a slender, fluted column of matte-black obsidian chitin, contrasting sharply with the pale, bleached stone and the dusty ochre of the flora. I do not have leaves. I do not have a mouth to feed. I am smooth, vertical, and utterly rigid. I have rooted myself deep into the bedrock, not to draw water, but to ensure I never tilt, never lean, never waiver. I am the plumb line of the world.

My existence is tied to the great, slow migration of the Pavement-Keepers. They do not have the internal biological clocks that lower animals might possess; they rely on the *feel* of the heat and the *touch* of the shade. I am the provider of that shade.

As the sun crawls across the sky, my shadow extends from the center of the plaza like the hand of a clock, sweeping over the "streets" and "alleys." The Pavement-Keepers, those jeweled, flat-bodied grazers, wait in the dormant heat for my shadow to wash over them. When the darkness of my silhouette touches their shells, they rouse from their torpor, their hydraulic legs telescoping outward to flow a few inches toward the fresh sap-seeps of the trees. I give them the permission to move. I dictate the rhythm of the city’s "rush hour."

I am also the landmark for the Sway-Wardens. High above in their bulbous perches, they watch the angle of my shadow to judge the slow progression of the seasons. When my shadow at noon is shortest, they know the season of Great Growth is upon them, and they begin their violent pruning with renewed vigor, shattering the new spires to feed the roots. When my shadow lengthens, they enter their own hibernation, sealing their shell-plates against the cold of the high-altitude night.

Even the Monolith respects me. The great, predatory stone that mimics the geology of the plaza never positions itself within my radius of influence. My shadow is a zone of unpredictability for its ambush tactics; the shifting light breaks its camouflage, and the activity of the grazing Pavement-Keepers in my wake creates too much vibration for its sensitive seismic senses. I am the safe harbor, the eye of the storm.

I do not eat. I absorb sunlight through my black carapace, converting the harsh ultraviolet radiation into a slow-burning chemical energy that keeps me alive and upright for centuries. I am a solar battery, a monument to the sun itself.

I am silent, as is everything here, but my presence is loud. I am the ruler of the geometry, the needle of the compass. Without me, the city would lose its temporal cohesion, drifting into a chaos of aimless grazing and untimely pruning. I am the stillness at the center of the circle. I am the Gnomon, and I am watching the world turn.
