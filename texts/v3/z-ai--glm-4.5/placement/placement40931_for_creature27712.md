I am the pause between the drips.

I am suspended on the southern face of the Spire, a jagged tooth of granite that pierces the bruised violet of the sky. Here, the wind is a thin, keening whisper, barely audible over the thudding of my own heart. I am secured by a rope woven from the coarse, oily hair of the Behemoths, smelling faintly of the musk and warm dung of the stables far below. It is anchored to a piton of meteoric iron, black and star-bitten, driven deep into the stone by my grandfather’s grandfather.

I am a **Channel-Carver**.

My existence is defined by the intersection of gravity and water. The city weeps, constantly. The clouds from the abyss smash against us, condensing on the cold stone, and if left unchecked, that water would find the fractures in the masonry, freeze, expand, and shatter the foundations of the world we have built. So, I carve.

I am small. My people are not tall. We are built like the stone itself—dense, compressed, low to the ground. My chest is a barrel, housing the furnace that keeps me alive against the absolute chill. My lungs are bellows, dragging the thin, razor-air into my body, extracting the meager oxygen to feed the fire.

I hang here, three thousand feet above the floor of the mist, chisel in hand. My fingers are wrapped in rags of felt, but I can feel the cold vibration of the granite through the tool. *Tap. Tap. Pause.* I do not rush. To rush is to sweat. To sweat is to freeze. I move with the glacial inevitability of the ice caps themselves.

I am adjusting the angle of a runoff gutter. It is a small channel, no wider than my thumb, but it must guide the flow from the roof above to the cistern below. If the angle is wrong, the water will back up, form a pool, and become a blade of ice hanging over the sleeping thoroughfares.

My mask is a sculpture of leather and felt, fitted tight to my face, leaving only a slit for my eyes. Through this slit, I see the world in high-contrast monochrome. The grey of the wet stone, the white of the frost, the black of the space above. My eyes are wide, absorbing every photon of the pale, distant sun. They do not water. They have forgotten how to water.

Below me, the city is a cliff of architecture, a chaotic termite mound of masonry and wood. Smoke curls from a thousand vents, mixing with the fog, creating a layer of smog that insulates the lower levels. I can hear the low-frequency hum of the Behemoths in their caverns—a vibration that travels up the ironwood beams and through the soles of my boots. It is a comforting sound, the sound of heat, of life, of shared breath.

I am not alone. To my left, hanging ten feet away, is Kael. We do not speak. To speak is to open the mask, to let the precious heat escape. Instead, I shift my shoulder—a minute roll that catches the corner of his eye. He sees the flicker of my movement. He stops his work. He watches me tap the stone, clearing a blockage of ice from the channel. Water spurts, liquid and silver, before finding its new path. I shift my shoulder again. A nod. He returns to his chisel.

We are a hive mind suspended in the void.

My belly rumbles, a hollow sound swallowed by the layers of wool and fur that swaddle me. I am hungry. It is the state of being here. We are always burning fuel. I think of the thick slabs of yak meat and the cakes of rendered fat waiting in my pouch, sealed against the damp, but I do not eat yet. I will finish this seam. The water must flow.

I look up, past the city, past the mist, to the hard line of the horizon. It is a wall of shattered stone, the violent, unyielding edge of the world. Beyond it lies the black, the vacuum, the nothingness. We do not look to the stars with longing. We look to them with the indifference of a survivor staring at a shark. They are there. We are here.

I am an architect of necessity. I have no dreams of flight or metal machines. My innovation is finding a new way to tie a knot that holds fast in the cold; my invention is a mixture of ash and fat that stops the wind from cracking my heels. I am the custodian of the moment.

The sun breaks through a rift in the clouds, a sudden, piercing spear of white light. It strikes the icicles hanging from the eaves above me, and for a second, the grey world explodes into brilliance. A thousand rainbows fracture in the ice, a prism of color in a monochrome world. I stop. I breathe. I watch the light dance on the dripping water.

And then the clouds swallow the sun again. The grey returns. The cold tightens its grip.

I resume my work. *Tap. Tap. Pause.*

I am the stone that endures. I am the blood that keeps the city from freezing. I am the silence.
