I am the weight. I am the heavy, dragging *shhh* that sounds like sand drawn across a mirror.

The first thing that is not the sleep is the Hum. It rises through the floor, traveling up the mineral scaffolding of my eight legs, vibrating the iron-oxide joints until it resonates in the hollow chamber of my glass gut. It is a slow, thick thrum, the heartbeat of the Center, the pulse of the Only-Thing-That-Grows. It says: *Wake. Work. Endure.*

I unfold. It is not a quick motion. Quickness is for the world above, the world of wind and teeth and chaos. Here, in the wet, blinding stillness, speed is a vulgarity. I extend my third leg, feeling the resistance of the dense, humid air. The air is thick enough to swim in; it presses against my translucent carapace, a heavy, tropical blanket that carries the taste of ozone and old stone.

I am on the periphery. The floor slopes away from me, a convex lens of polished quartz that feels greasy under the sensitive pads of my manipulators. I cannot see the slope, but I feel it in the way my weight shifts, in the way the gravity pulls me down toward the deep dish of the world.

I begin the descent.

Step. Drag. Pause.

The friction is non-existent. The walls weep, a constant, silent film of water that turns the universe into a hazard. I rely on the texture of the quartz, the microscopic imperfections that my underbelly reads like Braille. To you, this place is a blinding white void, a terrifying exposure. To me, it is a tapestry of sensation. The heat is a constant pressure, a swelling of the fluids inside my glass shell. I am a sealed flask of biology moving through a furnace.

I am not alone. I feel the *shhh-shhh* of my brothers and sisters to my left and right. We do not signal. We do not call out. To break the silence would be to shatter the perfection of the Cyst. We are a procession of glass and iron, a slow-moving rusted river flowing toward the heartbeat.

The Hum grows louder. It becomes a physical tremor that rattles my organs.

I arrive at the Colony.

I feel the presence of the Anemone before I touch it. It is a mountain of soft heat. Its flesh is not hard like the floor; it is yielding, gelatinous, a towering wall of living mucous that tastes of salt and life. It pulses against my legs, a slow inhalation and exhalation that takes minutes.

I assume the position. I am a surgeon. I am a barber of the gods.

From the sheath of mineral deposits on my back, I select my tool. It is a sliver of obsidian, blacker than the darkness we don't have, sharper than the pain I cannot feel. I extend the manipulator tendrils, the soft, pale flesh of my arms contrasting with the black stone.

I begin the scrape.

*Schlepp.*

A ribbon of thick, nutrient-rich slime peels away from the pale-green skin of the Anemone. The giant entity flinches—a ripple in the vibration—but does not retreat. It needs this. It is heavy with its own bounty, choking on the abundance of its own life. I am the release. I am the cleaner.

I work with the deliberation of geology. I am not stripping meat; I am harvesting the excess. I fold the slime, this precious glycoprotein ink, into a packet made of dried, shed skin. It is sticky. It is warm. It is the currency of our existence.

My packet is full. The rhythm of the scrape changes. It is time to return.

I turn, the iron legs grinding slightly, and begin the ascent back up the convex curve. The burden on my back changes my center of gravity; I must lean into the slope, trusting the friction of my sharp claws against the wet stone.

I pass the others who are still descending. We brush against each other—glass clicking against glass. A momentary transfer of heat. A recognition of the Process. We are not individuals; we are parts of a single machine, a clockwork mechanism designed to serve the Pulse.

I reach the periphery, the place where the floor begins to climb toward the unreachable walls. This is my canvas.

I stop. I lower myself until my sensory node—the eye that feels—brushes the floor. Here, the quartz is smooth, but it is empty. It is chaos. It needs order.

I take the packet of slime and I squeeze it.

I do not scribble. I *architect*. I move my body with mathematical precision, using the obsidian tool to lay down a line of biological ink. It is a spiral. It must be a spiral. The geometry is hardwired into my legs, written into the magnetic alignment of my iron bones.

I feel the line raised against the white ground. It is microscopic to a giant, but to me, it is a canyon. I walk the line, correcting the curve, ensuring the distance between the loops is exact. Why? I do not ask "why." The question implies a choice. There is no choice. There is only the pattern.

The pattern is a map. The pattern is a clock. The pattern is a prayer written in wet salt to a ceiling of blinding light.

As the slime begins to dry, hardening into a permanent ridge on the frictionless glass, I feel a settling in my chest. The work is done. The order has been imposed upon the void.

The vibration in the floor shifts. The Anemone enters its rest cycle. The Hum slows, deepens into a subsonic drone that vibrates in the marrow of my iron legs.

It is time.

I move away from my spiral, toward the edge of the world. I find the hairline fracture, the scar in the quartz. It is a shadow in a world without shadow, a place where the light refracts into a penumbra of coolness.

I wedge myself inside. I fold my legs, tucking the obsidian tool away. I retract the sensory tendrils. I seal the vents of my glass shell.

The blinding, oppressive white fades—not into darkness, but into a dull, comforting pressure. The humidity wraps around me like a shroud.

I stop. The purple-black fluid inside me slows its churn. I become a stone. I become a part of the wall.

I wait for the next pulse. I wait for the next million years.

I am the Vitreous Kin. I am the silence that tends the light.
