I would not be in the crush. I would be above it.

My place is the verticality, the sheer, terrifying faces of the splintered pillars that rise like jagged spears toward the lost ceiling. While the floor below is a churning ocean of fur and chitin, I cling to the high, cold obsidian, perhaps ten meters above the heaving carpet, flattened against the wet stone.

I am a **Lithic Mimic**.

To look at me, you would see nothing but a jagged outcropping of the fresh, grey rock—a shard of the catastrophe that formed this place. My physiology is a direct response to the sharp, unyielding geology. I do not have the soft, vulnerable scales of the reptilian scavengers, nor the rigid, rounded shell of the beetles. Instead, my body is comprised of flattened, interlocking plates of keratin and minerals that mimic the knife-like edges of the stone itself. I am angular, hard, and unwelcoming. My coloration is the exact damp, charcoal-grey of the basalt, absorbing the faint, grey luminescence rather than reflecting it.

I possess no eyes. In this flat twilight, where shadows are swallowed by the gloom, vision is a liability. instead, I perceive the world through **tactile resonance**. My entire body is an ear. The plates of my skin are exquisitely sensitive to vibration, allowing me to feel the frantic scrabbling of the beetles on the floor far below, the wet slap of the worms against the pillar’s base, and most importantly, the distinct, rhythmic scratch of claws climbing toward me.

I am an ambush predator of the vertical space, and I am the counter-force to the tool-users.

The Spined Scavengers, those frantic, intelligent reptiles, are my prey. They are the only creatures foolish or desperate enough to leave the relative safety of the floor to climb the sharp pillars, seeking the nutrient-rich mold that grows in the high condensation zones, or the mollusks that cling to the wet rock. When one climbs past me, it is focused entirely on its task—its mind occupied with the leverage required to pry a shell from the stone, its claws gripping the wet edges.

This is where the loop tightens.

I wait. I am the embodiment of the stasis (0.1). I can remain motionless for hours, my metabolic rate slowing to match the chill of the cavern, indistinguishable from the stone. I am part of the freeze-frame catastrophe.

When a scavenger climbs within reach, its vibrations humming against my flank, I do not pounce with a burst of speed. That would create noise, a disturbance in the rhythm. Instead, I simply... let go of my camouflage. My plates shift, a whisper of stone grinding against stone, and I flow downward with the sluggish inevitability of a falling rock.

I strike with the weight of the geology. My body is heavy, dense with minerals. I land atop the climbing reptile, pinning it against the sharp face of the pillar. There is no struggle, only the sudden, violent compression of its shell against the obsidian. I consume it quickly, efficiently, tasting the copper tang of its blood and the musk of its fear.

Afterwards, I do not rest. I return to my position. I flatten my plates. I realign my edges with the fractures in the stone. I become the rock again.

The water continues to drip. *Plip. Plip.* The roar of the millions below continues its deafening thrum. The scavenger that was climbing is gone, but within moments, another will begin its ascent, drawn by the same scent of mold, following the same path, doomed to repeat the encounter. I am the pause in their noise, the sharp edge in their chaotic world, waiting in the damp, grey dark for the vibration of the next meal.
