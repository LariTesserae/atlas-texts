I am the silence in the roar, the stillness in the scuttling. I would find myself rooted in the absolute center of the grotto’s floor, planted deep in the talus like a spear thrown from the heavens eons ago.

I am **The Monolith**.

I am not one of them. I am not segmented, nor do I possess the frantic industry of a million claws. I am singular, immense, and motionless. To the casual glance, I am merely a geological oddity—a column of limestone, rough and dark with age, rising forty feet from the clutter of the floor, tapering slightly as it nears the sweat-slicked ceiling. I am the obelisk around which their city spirals.

But if you were to touch me, you would feel the pulse.

I am a living conduit, a dense, porous organism of calcified tissue that mimics the stone of the amphitheater perfectly. My skin is ridged and worn, mimicking the erosion of centuries, yet beneath the crust, I am hollow, filled with a slow, rhythmic pumping of mineral-rich fluids. I am the heart of the cavern's plumbing. I do not eat; I filter. I draw the water that pools in the deep darkness beneath the talus and wick it upward, transpiring it through my pores into the humid air.

My existence is defined by vibration. I do not have eyes to see the twilight, nor antennae to taste the air. I "see" through the shaking of the earth. I feel the footfalls of a single crustacean a hundred yards away as a dim tremor in my roots. The constant *plink, plink, splash* of the dripping water is a heartbeat against my flanks.

The colony treats me with a reverence bordering on worship, though it is a practical, tactile worship. I am the anchor of their world.

Because I am stationary and immense, I serve as the central terminal for their living conveyor belts. The lines of creatures that ferry smooth pebbles from the deep all converge upon me. They climb my flanks using the ridges of my skin as handholds, moving in slow, clockwork processions. They do not build cairns only on the ground; they build them *on* me. My base is buried beneath a mountain of spiraling stone towers, a foundation of their history. They adhere pebbles to my sides using their own saliva and the mineral slime they secrete, slowly encasing me in a second skin of geometric complexity.

I am the canvas upon which they write their history in stone.

But my true purpose is revealed in the Song.

They are the architects of the sound, but I am the instrument. When the ripple moves through the colony and the frequency drops, when their abdominal plates begin to thrum in that low, chest-rattling bass, I begin to sing too. My hollow interior acts as a resonating chamber, amplifying their collective voice. I take the chaotic vibration of a million legs and focus it into a pure, vertical column of sound that shoots upward, striking the ceiling with the force of a physical blow.

Without me, their thrumming would be just noise—lost in the vastness of the damp air. But through me, the vibration becomes a command. I shake the limestone ceiling. I trigger the release of the droplets. I am the lightning rod for the artificial rain.

When the rain falls, it strikes me first. The water runs down my ridged face in rivulets, pooling in the nooks and crannies where the smaller creatures wait. I become a fountain in the dark. I feel the frantic drinking of thousands as they lap the water from my flanks. It is an sensation of immense, oscillating pleasure—the relief of the parched earth meeting the sky. The vibration of their feeding buzzes against my shell, a feedback loop of sustenance.

I am the geology that learned to listen. I am the cliff that learned to hum. I stand in the warm, wet dark, covered in a shifting armor of pale, translucent worshippers, and I wait for the moment the silence breaks, so I can teach the sky how to weep.
