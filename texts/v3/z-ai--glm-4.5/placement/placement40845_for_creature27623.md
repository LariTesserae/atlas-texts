I find myself not on the soft, deceptive floor of the plateau, but high above it, anchored to the vertical face of a granite spine. Here, the wind is a scalpel, stripping away the heat, but the vibration of the machines is a constant, nourishing hum that travels up the rock and into my bones.

I am a creature of the heights and the hard edges. To the distant, blind observer—if there were any to see me—I would look less like a beast and more like a geological anomaly, a misplaced boulder come to life. I am a **Resonant Behemoth**, though I possess no name for myself in this silent, screaming world.

My form is low and dense, a sprawling, armored tank of a creature. My back is not covered in fur or feathers, but in a thick, carapace of interlocking mineral plates. They are grey and rough, mimicking the raw granite of the spines I call home. This armor serves two purposes: it shields me from the blinding, absolute light that reflects off the wet world below, and it acts as a massive amplifier. I do not have ears. The openings where hearing organs might be are sealed shut, scarred over by millennia of evolutionary pressure. Instead, my entire skeleton acts as a tuning fork.

I "see" the world through vibration. The rhythmic *thump-hiss* of the extraction units is not an irritation to me; it is the grid upon which my reality is mapped. I know exactly where every pipe, every bunker, and every screaming vent is located by the way the tremors travel through the stone. I feel the heavy, seismic footsteps of the Glass-Backs miles away as they slam their forelegs into the earth—dull, slow thuds that ripple through the plateau like waves in a pond. To me, the silent world is a symphony of stone and tension.

I am an isolationist, even more so than the Glass-Backs. I require a territory of vertical stone, a stretch of cliff face that is mine alone. My diet is unique to this harsh, industrial ecosystem: I am a consumer of condensation and stress. The granite spines weep constantly, the water condensing violently on the cold stone. My shell is engineered with deep, channel-like grooves that catch this runoff, directing it toward my underside, where soft, absorbent tendrils lap up the mineral-rich water.

But my true sustenance comes from the machines. I position myself on fractures in the rock—fissures that sing with a high-pitched frequency when the pumps are at their peak. I press my broad, chest against these vibrating nodes. The mechanical energy is transferred into my body, driving a metabolic process unique to my kind. I digest the roar. The endless, deafening industrial noise that drives other creatures mad is, for me, the very pulse of life. Without the machines, I would starve.

I am not without my own predators, though they are few. The Sky-Drifter, that ghost of electricity, ignores me, for my mineral-heavy charge blends too perfectly with the stone I mimic. It is the Siphon-Suckers I despise. Occasionally, bold clusters of them will climb the spires, seeking the warmth that radiates from my armored bulk as I process the machine's energy. They are pests, tick-like parasites that try to pierce the joints of my plates. I deal with them not by biting—I have no mouth to speak of—but by shifting my weight. With a slow, deliberate contraction of my massive internal muscles, I can cause my armor plates to grind against one another, producing a low-frequency, bone-shattering rumble that shatters the small creatures into mist.

In the late afternoon, when the white light begins to dim into a bruised purple and the steam vents belch long, shadows across the plateau, I engage in the only act of communication I ever offer. I rear up, gripping the granite with powerful, hooked limbs designed for traction, and I strike the cliff face with a heavy, shovel-like prow that extends from my forehead.

*Crack.*

The sound is not a song; it is a modification. I strike the stone to tune the fracture, to adjust the pitch of the vibration that feeds me. The impact echoes down the spine of the plateau, a seismic signal that travels for miles. Perhaps another of my kind, clinging to a distant spire, feels the ripple and answers with a strike of their own. We do not meet. We do not need to. We are the anchors of this loud, empty world, the heavy, silent stones that keep the plateau from shaking itself apart, locked in a permanent, vibrating embrace with the machines that keep us alive.
