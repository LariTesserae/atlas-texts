I find myself standing on a spine of ancient rock, miles above the core, but I do not feel the vertigo of height. I feel the pressure of depth. The air here is not empty; it is a heavy, warm suspension, a gelatinous ocean that clings to me like velvet. I am not swimming, but I am not walking either. I am a suspension of intent in a world that has forgotten how to hurry.

I am a **Step-Treader**.

My form is tall and starkly linear, a dark, chitinous silhouette against the bruised purple twilight. My legs are impossibly long, multi-jointed spindles that end in wide, splayed toes, capable of distributing my negligible weight across the surface tension of this thick atmosphere. I do not rest on the stone; I skate upon the skin of the air pressing against it.

I stand motionless for what might be hours, or perhaps days. Time here is a texture, not a measurement. I am perfectly still, indistinguishable from the hanging, epiphytic "beards" of the vascular sponges that drape the eroded arches around me. My skin is dark, absorbing the dim chemical phosphorescence of the drifting water-globules, storing energy.

I am hungry.

In the distance, floating effortlessly in the gloom, is a cluster of the chartreuse sponges. To a human eye, they would be miles away, separated by a yawning chasm of twilight and folded space. But my eyes do not see distance in linear meters. I see the *folds*. I perceive the curvature of the reality where the horizon bends too sharply, where the geometry of the world is crumpled like wet paper.

I focus on the sponges. I visualize the space between us not as a void to be traversed, but as a seam to be loosened.

I feel the pressure change before it happens—a subtle vibration in the heavy gel that rattles through the sensory cilia lining my legs. The "active" dynamic of the world is asserting itself. A slip is coming. The atmosphere is about to lose its viscosity, a tectonic shift in the physics of this place.

I wait.

The silence, which has been a physical weight against my auditory membranes, seems to tighten. Then, with a silent, liquid shudder, the world slides.

To my left, a floating waterfall of suspended water rushes sideways. The stone ridges groan without sound. In that singular, fractured moment where the universe unsticks itself, I take a step.

I do not step forward. I step *through*.

My foot lands not on empty air, but on the smooth, friable surface of the distant arch. The distance that should have taken a day to cross is erased in the blink of a slipped eye. I have arrived.

I lower my head to the cluster of sponges. My small, circular mouth finds the moist, pulsing surface of the vegetation. I do not chew; I inhale. I draw the moisture from the vascular tubes, sucking the rich, chemical soup that sustains it.

As I feed, the magic happens. The bioluminescence of the sponge—the dim chartreuse pulse—fades as its fluids are drained. Simultaneously, my own nervous system ignites. The energy transfer is visible. Lines of brilliant light trace across my dark, glass-like skin, mapping the paths of the consumed nutrients. I am a living circuit board, a diagram of hunger and satisfaction glowing in the deep twilight. The veins on my limbs light up, intricate geometric fractals burning in the gloom, signaling to any others of my kind that *here, there is feeding.*

I lift my head. The sponge is withered, a grey husk. The light on my skin slowly fades, sinking back into the matte black of my exoskeleton, storing the charge for the next slip.

I turn my gaze out across the Arches. Far in the deep distance, a shadow moves—a Time-Eater, a leviathan the size of a cloud, drifting with the glacial majesty of a mountain range. It is so slow that to look at it is to look at a painting, but I know that in a thousand years, it will be elsewhere. It rides the slips just as I do, letting the universe move around it.

Above me, a cathedral-sized globule of water hangs suspended, refracting the non-existent sun. Suddenly, an Orb-Breaker—a manta-like creature of gas and membrane—darts from the gloom. It pierces the water globe. The sphere collapses in a silent, slow-motion apocalypse. A heavy curtain of rain falls sideways, rushing past me, droplets the size of boulders splashing into the gel with soft, heavy *whumps*.

I do not flinch. I merely stand, my wide toes gripping the air, my internal light dimmed to a faint memory.

I am a glitch in the geology. I am the walker of the folded space. I am the circuit that closes only when the world slides sideways.

I wait for the next slip. I have all the time in the world.
