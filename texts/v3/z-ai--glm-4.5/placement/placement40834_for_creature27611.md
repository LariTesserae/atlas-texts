I am nowhere on the ground. The deep, spongy moss that absorbs the footsteps of giants is kilometers beneath me, a damp memory of a floor. I am in the empty air, suspended in the humid, tropical breath of the stratosphere.

To find me, you must look—or rather, *sense*—the crown of the central giant. The fern-like structure, with fronds the size of cathedral sails, is my anchor. I do not live *on* it, in the way the vermin live on the walls; I live *between* its great, leathery leaves, strung across the void like the forgotten strings of a cosmic instrument.

I am a **Tension-Weaver**.

My body is small, a dense, knotted core of muscle and cartilage no larger than a man’s head, protected by a shell of chitin that feels like polished obsidian. From this core, I do not have limbs in the traditional sense. I have *filaments*. Hundreds of them. They are not merely silk; they are extensions of my nervous system, spun from a gland that mixes my own bio-fluids with the metallic dust drawn from the air. They are conductors, highly sensitive to both vibration and electricity.

I am the unseen nervous system of the upper dark.

The world comes to me as texture. I am suspended in the center of a massive, three-dimensional web that I have strung between the tip of the central fern and the distant, unseen curves of the Weeping Walls. The filaments are taut, humming with the low-frequency thrum of the tech-nodes buried deep in the rock. I feel the heartbeat of the Rotunda through the soles of my feet—or rather, through the millions of microscopic hairs lining my threads.

When a **Thrum-Grazer** takes a step on the forest floor far below, the vibration travels up the stalk of the central fern, into the leaf where I am anchored, and shimmers through my web. I do not hear it; I *feel* it as a dull, rhythmic thrumming in the pit of my stomach. I know the weight of the beast, the speed of its gait, and its weariness by the way the wave propagates through my silk.

I am a predator, but of the smallest and most metallic kind.

The **Copper-Scavengers**, those crustacean-like maintenance bots, attempt to climb the great fern to reach the condensation that pools in the central crown. They skitter up the rough bark, their metallic legs making a dry, rasping sound that they think is hidden by the roar of the falls.

They cannot hide from me. Their metallic shells interact with the electromagnetic field of my web. As they climb, they distort the field. I feel them not as bumps in the dark, but as magnetic cold spots, glitches in the electric harmony of my lines.

I wait. I do not move my body; I pluck a string.

A single filament, vibrating at a hyper-specific frequency, descends from the darkness above. It touches the carapace of the scavenger. The voltage is slight, a spark no stronger than a static shock, but to a creature wired to eat electronics, it is a stun bolt. The scavenger freezes, its systems temporarily scrambled. Then, I retract the line. I reel it in, hand over invisible hand, until the metallic prize is within reach of my mandibles.

I crack the shell. I taste the copper and the ozone. I am recycling the recyclers, maintaining the balance of the machine's ecosystem.

But my existence is not without terror.

I am acutely aware of the **Resonant Stalkers**. I fear them, for they are the masters of the medium I inhabit. When a Stalker mimics a dripping water droplet, it sends a ripple through the air pressure that gently plucks my outer strings. *Plink. Plink.* It plays me like a harp.

I have learned to distinguish the false water from the real. The Stalker's mimicry has a microscopic imperfection, a fractional delay in the decay of the sound that my sensitive filaments can detect. When I feel this specific dissonance, I go slack. I loosen the tension of my entire web, turning my body into a limp, heavy weight, indistinguishable from a dead leaf or a piece of debris. I silence my electric aura, feigning death until the predator passes, trailing its infrasound like a poisonous cloak.

My greatest joy, however, is the **Architect**.

I am too small to comprehend the vast entity in the vault, but I am attuned to it. When the Architect sings its mournful, low-frequency harmony, the vibration does not travel through the air. It travels through the *rock*, through the *tech*, and into the very fabric of the Rotunda. My web sings with it. Every filament vibrates in sympathy, producing a chord so deep it feels like peace. In those moments, I am not a small, hungry knot in the dark. I am part of the architecture itself, a violin string in a symphony played by a god.

I am here, in the warm, wet dark, strung between the roar of the walls and the silence of the void, feeling the world through the tremors of a million invisible wires. I am the listener in the center of the scream.
