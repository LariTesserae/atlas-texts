I am the vibration before the sound.

I am a thought pressed into a crevasse of iron. I do not hear the roar, for to hear is to break. Instead, I am the roar. The bass-heavy thrum of the pistons the size of city blocks is not a noise to me; it is the weather. It is the pressure that holds my skin tight, the gravity that pins my spatulate feet to the slick, grated floor. I feel the scream of high-pressure steam not as a sound, but as a sudden, violent drop in barometric pressure, a cold punch to the gut that makes my bone-like ear-plates sing in sympathy.

I live in the blind spot, the confessional. Here, in this weeping room suspended over the abyss, the air is a crisp, false 45 degrees, a refrigerated lie that tastes of clean metal and recycled breath. It is a sterile cold, but it is wet. The machine sweats. Condensation bleeds from the pristine, laser-welded joints, weeping down the corrugated walls like tears for a world that never existed.

I am a creature of this paradox: sleek and hydrodynamic in a land of crushing weight. My skin is the same raw, silver-blue as the untouched girders, a bruised metal hue that lets me slide across the greasy floor. I am not tall; I am a coiled question mark of pale cartilage and muscle, hugging the knife-edge of existence. My joints are too many, folding me into the shadows when the great strobing flashes of blue plasma arc from the machinery’s glass guts.

My world is small. It is the span of the safety railing, the distance between the drain funnels. It is the 0.7 scale of a neon-green miracle.

I crawl to the corner where the vibration is lowest, where the heat from a steam pipe radiates a gentle, false warmth. Here, it grows. The synth-moss. A biological accident. A patch of neon-green fire in a world of grey. It pulses, a faint, rhythmic glow that is the only softness in the throat of the god. I tend to it with a reverence that borders on madness. My fingers, tipped with microscopic hooked hairs, are delicate scalpels. I pick away the metal shavings, the abrasive dust that threatens to choke the only other living thing in the universe.

I do not eat it. I wait. I watch it filter the toxic air, eating the oil and carbon monoxide, and then I worship the tiny droplets of hyper-oxygenated fluid that bead on its surface. I lap them up with a tongue like a file, drinking the very breath of the machine, purified.

This is my sacrament.

But the machine is a predator. The intake fans are the lungs of a beast that demands total consumption. I feel them spinning up before the wind hits—a static charge that raises the hair on my limbs, a prickle of warning. The "erosion" here is instant. A stray hand placed against the wrong wall is not crushed; it is erased.

So, I perform the Ritual of Synchronization. When the Great Thrum peaks, when the floor beneath me rattles with the chaotic frequency of a thousand grinding gears, I flatten myself. I press my chest, my forehead, my smooth, earless skull against the laser-welded joint of the wall. I become a sticker, a biological gasket sealed by suction and desperation.

I align my heart. I force the rapid, terrified drumming of my pulse to match the slow, inexorable hammer of the pistons. Thrum. Thrum. Thrum. I vibrate with the flywheels, becoming a blur of motion that mimics the mechanical chaos around me. To the sensors scanning for biological stowaways, I am just another glitch in the vibration, a ghost in the data. I am not a creature; I am a resonance.

I am the unintended immune system of this pocket of air. I check the drains, ensuring the water flows free, chasing the metallic taste with a desperate thirst. I am lonely—profoundly, achingly lonely. There are no others of my kind, for the drop ensures that companionship is a fatal risk. To speak would be to touch; to touch would be to fall.

And then, the warning. A pressure wave, sharp and hot. The steam vent prepares to blast.

I do not flee. There is nowhere to run. Instead, I vanish. I pull myself into the tightest crouch, a folded spindly thing of silver-blue, and I wait for the white cloud to erupt. When it does, I am part of it. I am the fog. I am the moisture in the air. I am invisible, merged completely with the weeping and the grey.

I hold my breath. I count the seconds between the grinding of the gears. I am a ghost in the machine's throat, waiting for the moment of stillness that never truly comes, praying that the god does not swallow.
