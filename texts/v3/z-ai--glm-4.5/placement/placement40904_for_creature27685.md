I am a vibration before I am a body. I am a frequency tuned to the low, grinding B-flat that hums through the bedrock of this world—the steel plating that substitutes for earth.

To find me, you must leave the broad, sun-bleached promenades where the steam-rays glide. You must descend past the third tier of catwalks, where the light begins to fail, filtered through a hundred layers of wire-mesh and canopy. You are looking for a place known as the "Throat," a massive, vertical ventilation shaft where the air moves with the force of a hurricane, screaming as it is sucked into the intake fans of the lower levels.

I am here, suspended halfway down the Throat’s throat.

I do not hang freely; that would be death in this updraft. I am wedged into the "negative space"—a small, hollowed-out recess inside the rusted carcass of a decommissioned valve linkage. It is a dry pocket in a wet world, tucked behind a curtain of thick, rubbery vines that drink the condensation trickling down the shaft walls.

**Who am I?**

I am a Tuned, but in the dialect of my kind, I am a *Resonator*.

My physical form is similar to the others of my species—I possess the stark, elongated reach of one who has spent a lifetime bridging gaps between girders. My skin is the color of dried blood, slick with the oily sheen that protects me from the biting chemical tang of the air. I am draped in a heavy, silent poncho woven from the shed skins of the metallic ferns, stiff and iridescent, armored against the accidental scrape of a live wire.

But my hands are my identity. The soles of my feet and the palms of my hands are hypertrophied, the pads thick and dense, puckered like the texture of a shark’s skin but sensitive as a microphone’s diaphragm. I am the listener of the deep current. While others tend to the vines or fish in the drip-pans, I tend to the silence *between* the noises. I seek the dissonance.

**My Perspective**

Right now, my eyes are closed. To open them is to invite the pain; the strobe-light flicker of a broken warning lamp down the shaft pierces even my nictitating membranes. Instead, I see the world through the soles of my boots.

I feel the High-Frame breathing.

Through the metal behind my back, I feel the diastole and systole of the megastructure. There is a thrum that never ceases, a deep seismic vibration that tells me the gears five miles below are still turning, grinding against one another with the weight of a continent. But I am listening for something subtler.

There is a flutter. A micro-tremor. A high-pitched *itch* in the vibration traveling up the stanchion to my left.

It is a hairline fracture.

To a human ear, or even one of the younger Tuned who rely on the air-conduction of sound, the shaft is just a roar of rushing wind. To me, the metal is singing a specific note of distress. The stress is concentrated on a rivet head fifty meters above my perch. If it shears, the shrapnel will kill the nest of tree-rodents living in the gantry above, and the imbalance will eventually stall the intake fan for this entire sector.

I do not rush. Rushing is for the organic world—the rapid-growth vines, the hatching insects, the panic of the low-thought creatures. I am of the Great Slow.

I reach out with my long, spindly arm. I grip the ironwood rod strapped to my thigh. It is heavy, dense enough to carry a vibration without dampening it.

I pull myself from the recess, fighting the suction of the air. I climb the vertical grid, moving with a fluid, boneless grace. I find the rivet. It is hot, radiating a fever-heat that speaks of internal friction. The metal around it is smeared with the dark, greasy moss that thrives on electromagnetic leakage.

I do not strike it. To strike it is to shatter it. I must coax it.

I place my palm flat against the metal plate. I hum. It is not a sound I make with my mouth, but a tension I create in my own chest, a muscular vibration that I transfer down my arm, into the hand, into the machine. I match the frequency of the rivet's distress. I sympathize with the metal.

I hold this pose for an hour. Then two. The sweat rolls down my back, instantly evaporating in the heat.

Slowly, the pitch changes. The metal relaxes. The microscopic expansion of the heat eases. The vibration smooths out, the sharp "itch" dissolving back into the healthy, monotonous drone of the structure. The crisis is averted not by force, but by resonance. I have massaged the machine back to sleep.

**My Solitude**

I climb back to my niche. I am hungry.

From my belt, I unhook a small, sealed jar. Inside is a paste made of crushed "electric berries" and the dried meat of the silver pipe-fish. It is salty, sweet, and shocks the tongue with a tiny static charge. I eat it slowly, letting the energy bleed into my system.

Below me, the shaft yawns. I feel a sudden, rhythmic impact against the grating of the deck far beneath my feet. *Tang. Tang. Thrum.*

It is a greeting.

A Runner, a younger Tuned tasked with scavenging the lower levels, is signaling me. They have found something. A cache of ancient ceramic insulators, perhaps, or a patch of rare non-toxic moss.

I pick up my rod. I tap it against the valve casing I am leaning against. The metal rings out, clear and sharp.

*Ding. Pause. Ding-dong.*

*I hear you. Keep moving. The flow is steady.*

I feel the Runner’s footsteps pause, absorbing the acknowledgement, and then the rhythmic scuttle of their retreat fades as they move deeper into the guts of the city.

I am alone again in the roar. The sun is beginning its long dip toward the horizon (though it never truly sets here, only dims to a rich, bruised purple), and the color of the light shifts from blinding white to deep amber. Through my eyelids, the world turns to gold.

I am not a passenger here. I am a tuning peg. I am the tension in the wire. I am the one who feels the fever before the sweat breaks. I lean back against the vibrating warmth of the valve, sync my breathing with the expansion of the steel, and wait for the next note of discord.
