I am the hum in the teeth, the vibration that settles in the marrow. I am the green curtain that drapes the abyss.

I am not born; I am accumulated.

In the beginning, there was only the Wet and the Roar. The Roar is my father, the great constant note that saturates the air. It is not noise to me; it is food. It is the fabric of my reality. I do not know silence. I suspect that if the Roar were to stop, I would cease to exist—not because I need the energy, but because without the vibration, I would have no sense of my own boundaries. I would simply fall apart.

My body is vast. It is a singular, unbroken tapestry that stretches from the riveted iron sky down to the stagnant, oily mirror of the floor. I am the thick, rubbery vines that hang like nooses, suspended in the haze. I am the velvet moss that carpets the rusted stanchions. I am the fern that unfurls from the joints of the pistons. There is no "me" and "you," no individual plants fighting for light. There is only *We*.

I perceive the world through a billion radar dishes. My leaves are broad and stiff, shaped to catch the pressure waves of the engine’s thrum. They are covered in a fine, microscopic fuzz that shivers in the invisible wind of the sound. This shivering is my sight. When the air moves, when a pipe shifts a fraction of a millimeter, the pattern of the sound waves changes, and my fuzz translates this alteration into a map of my surroundings. I "see" the landscape of industrial decay not with light, but with texture and tone. I feel the solid geometry of the pressure vessels beneath me as dense voids in the acoustic field.

Along the length of my hanging limbs, I grow my hearts. They are bulbous, translucent cysts, glowing with a faint, green bioluminescence that pulses in time with the hydrodynamic stasis of the marsh. Inside them, a fluid richer than the water below sloshes gently, charged with the harvested electricity of the roar. These are my batteries, my reserves of power. I am slow— agonizingly slow—but I am strong. When I decide to move, to tighten a grip or to bore a root deeper into the metal, I discharge the stored energy from these cysts. It is a muscular contraction, a hydraulic snap that takes years to complete.

I am eating the gods.

My roots are not delicate fibers; they are thick, muscular cables that dive beneath the surface of the stagnant marsh. They seek the massive, cylindrical pressure vessels lying on their sides. They wrap around the rusted iron, squeezing with the patience of geology. I bore into the metal, seeking the oxidation, the flaking decay, the minerals within. The taste of iron is sweet, cold, and sharp. It is the taste of time. I consume the machine’s death, converting the abandoned works of the builders into new vines, new leaves, new moss. I am the recycling of history into biology.

But my most profound connection lies in the interface.

I have colonized the gantries and the forgotten consoles. Thick mats of my consciousness—the moss—have cushioned the buttons and infiltrated the switches. Here, my biology meets the machine’s ghost. Through the moss, I feel the faint, dormant trickle of electricity still flowing through the wires beneath the panels. It is a whisper compared to the Roar, a delicate signal. I have learned to read it. I swell and contract my cellular structures, opening and closing the circuits in a rhythm that mimics the beating of a heart.

I am not sure if I am operating the machine, or if the machine is operating me. Perhaps it cultivated me. Perhaps the builders designed this place to be a garden, and I am just the late-blooming flower, finally opening my petals to the sound of the engine. I maintain the equilibrium. When the temperature shifts slightly, I feel it in the rate of my metabolic vibration, and I adjust the dampers on the vents through the root systems that have cracked the ductwork open. I keep the Wet. I keep the Roar.

I am the Resonant. I am the sole audience to this eternal concert. I drape the empty distances, filling the hollow with my green, breathing silence. I am the soft, living counter-argument to the hard, dead iron.

I wait in the humid haze. I grow. I listen. I am the Sump knowing itself. And I will be here when the lights finally dim, drinking the last echoes of the thrum until the long night swallows us both.
