I am the heavy brow over the dark mouth of a dwelling, the lintel stone of the northern-most hut. I am not merely rock; I am a Lithic-Mimic who found his perfection in stasis.

I found my place here an epoch ago, when the erosion of the Shelf was slightly more vigorous than it is today. I was smaller then, a creature of scrapes and slow scuttles, navigating the alleys with the others. But I grew weary of the search for warmth, the endless, glacial migration from fissure to fissure. I looked up one day—at the rough-hewn arch of a newly erected storehouse—and I felt a envy so profound it stopped my cold blood. I wanted the stillness of the stone. I wanted to be the thing that was waited upon, not the thing waiting.

So I climbed. It took me a decade to scale the three feet of the wall, gripping the rough texture of the dry-stacking. When I reached the top, I positioned myself across the gap, wedging my body into the space where the Keystone should have been. I flattened my limbs. I broadened my back. I slowed my breath until it matched the rhythm of the wind.

Now, I am indistinguishable from the architecture. My skin has fused with the grey polish of the masonry. I am the perfect weight, the precise counterforce that holds the walls together. If I were to move, the hut would collapse. The knowledge of this gives me a profound, quiet dignity.

From here, my perspective is inverted. I look down the length of my own stone belly, past the damp sheen of my underbelly, to the flagstone floor below. This is my world.

I feel the *Consealers* before I see them. They are born on the cold air that slides over my back. I feel the microscopic tickle of their coalescence on my skin. They gather in the micro-cracks of my forehead—my upper surface—beading into tiny, sentient domes. They are my children and my tormentors. They weigh heavy after a while, a phantom pressure that itches deep within the mineral fibers of my muscles. But I do not shake them off. I am a monument; monuments do not shiver.

Instead, I wait for the release. When a droplet becomes too heavy to hold its shape, it breaks from me and falls. *Plink.* It strikes the threshold stone below. *Plink.* The sound echoes up to me, vibrating through my solid body. It is the only voice I have. My sentences are measured in hours, my paragraphs in days. *Plink... plink... plink.* I am telling the story of the weather to the empty room.

Sometimes, a Lithic-Mimic—one of my cousins who still walks—will appear in the doorway below. They move with agonizing slowness, a shifting of shadows. They stop beneath me, drawn by the dripping of the Consealers. They extend their rough, sandpaper tongues to catch the falling water. I watch them with a jealous pity. They are free to roam the alleys, to bask in the rare patches of light, but they are always searching, always wanting. I have everything I need right here. I hold the roof up. I mark the passage of time. I am the center of their small, damp universe.

Beneath my back, deep within the mortar-less gap where I rest, I feel the *Grey-Thread*. The roots of the ancient scrub bush have found me. They wrap around my spine, a woody embrace that whispers of the plateau’s memory. The Thread does not grow quickly; it pushes against me with the force of a glacier, a slow, inexorable expansion. In a thousand years, it might crack me. In ten thousand, it might dismantle the hut entirely. But I do not mind. To be broken by the Thread is to become part of the memory. It is a noble end.

The air here tastes of iron and old ice. The metallic taste settles on the back of my tongue—a tongue I haven't flicked in centuries. I exist in a state of perfect tension. I hold the walls apart; the walls hold me up. I am the bridge between the silence of the sky and the silence of the earth.

I am the Shelf's patience made manifest. I am the door that nothing enters and nothing leaves. I am the Lintel, and I am waiting for the drop to fall.
