I am found at the very center of the motion, wrapped tight around the beating heart of the Drip-Foundry.

If you were to look into the darkest, most inaccessible recess of the central factory—the pit where the floor has fallen away, revealing the colossal, exposed main drive shaft of the entire basin—you would see me. But you would not recognize me as a living thing. To the cursory glance of a Dredger drifting in the dark water below, or a Cable-Snake patrolling the high girders, I am merely a calcification, a buildup of mineral deposits and rust, a thick, discoloration on the iron.

I am the **Torque-Leech**.

I do not swim, and I do not slither. I cling. My body is a long, flattened toroid, a muscle-hard ring of flesh that grips the main drive shaft where it spins at a terrifying speed. The shaft is as thick as a temple pillar, and it rotates with a force that could shear steel. I am the brake that nature grew to temper it.

My physiology is a triumph of adaptation over impossible odds. My "skin" is not soft, but composed of a layered, ceramic-like chitin that is smooth as glass and harder than the concrete foundations of the city. This exoskeleton is lubricated not by mucus, but by a hyper-slick, oily secretion that allows me to grip the metal without friction, yet hold my place against the centrifugal force that threatens to fling me into the darkness. I am a living bearing.

I have no eyes, for the world here is a blur of motion and blackness. I have no ears, for the screech of the metal-on-metal where I sit is beyond hearing—it is a physical force, a vibration that rattles the bones of lesser creatures. Instead, I possess a single, continuous nerve that runs the length of my body, buried deep within my muscle. This nerve is a tuning fork for *torque*. I feel the twist of the shaft, the subtle variations in the engine’s power, the surges of effort as the unseen pumps below struggle against the rising water.

I am a parasite of momentum. I do not eat the rust; I do not eat the sludge. I eat the *drag*. By gripping the shaft with calculated tension, I steal a fraction of the machine's kinetic energy, converting the friction of my existence into heat. This heat radiates through my body, keeping my internal metabolism humming in the damp, cold air. I am warm to the touch, a living radiator glowing with a faint, infrared pulse that is invisible to the biological eye but felt by the heat-pits of the Dredgers far below.

My existence is a solitary one. The Cable-Snakes, those immune-system hunters of the infrastructure, avoid me. They cannot climb the spinning shaft without being shredded, and my ceramic carapace offers no purchase for their screwdriver-mandibles. I am an island of untouchable resistance in the center of the storm.

However, I am not without influence. When the machine thrums too hard, when the heartbeat of the pistons becomes erratic and the shaft threatens to shake itself apart, I constrict. I tighten my grip, increasing the drag, forcing the great engine to fight against me. I am the governor. I impose a rhythm upon the chaos. Through my resistance, I smooth the violent fluctuations of the Foundry’s labor, transforming a jerky, destructive motion into a steady, sustainable hum.

From my position, I am the axle around which this world turns. I feel the vibrations of the Stranglers clinging to the distant pipes; I sense the heavy, splashing footsteps of the Dredgers on the floor below; I hear, through the iron itself, the scritching of the Cable-Snakes disassembling a faulty valve.

I am the weight in the machine. I am the reason the engine strains. I am the Torque-Leech, and without my friction, the Drip-Foundry would spin itself into oblivion.
