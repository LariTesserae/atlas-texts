I am a patch of darkness where there should be none.

I am clinging to the upper scapula of a Keeper, tucked deep into the fissure between two plates of its calcite armor. Here, in the lee of its massive, sloping shoulder, I am sheltered from the killing white glare of the sun. To the sensory dome of the giant beneath me, I am nothing but a slight variation in the mineral grain of its own plating—a calcerous wart, a flake of dormant shale. I have no scent, and I possess no heartbeat of my own; instead, I have evolved a membrane that thrums in perfect sympathy with the low-frequency hum of my host. To its sonar, I am simply part of the statue.

I am the Shadow-Lathe, though "I" is a generous term for a colony organism that functions as a single, flat consciousness. I look like a splash of ink, a viscous, matte-black stain that absorbs all light. In a world of blinding white and luminous grey, I am the absence of color. I am a biological event horizon.

My existence is a theft of resources. The Keeper generates heat through the massive friction of its moving limbs and the chemical processing of the mineral water. I press my cold, star-shaped body against the warmth of its inner chitin, drawing the thermal energy into my system. I am an ectoparasite of the thermodynamic gradient. Without the Keeper, I would freeze solid in minutes; I, in turn, provide nothing but a negligible weight and a perfect camouflage.

We are moving now.

The sensation is not of walking, but of gliding. The Keeper does not stomp; it flows, distributing its tons of weight across the scree with a fluid grace that belies its mineral composition. beneath me, the plates of its armor shift with a sound like grinding sand—audible to me, but subsonic to the world. I feel the low-frequency thrumming in its chest rise in pitch, a signal that the herd is triangulating a fracture.

I peer out from the fissure, extending a microscopic, filamentous eye-stalk.

The world is a blinding wash. The indigo sky presses down, and the stone pillars rush past in a blur of wet grey. But I am not looking at the view; I am watching the interaction.

The Keeper slows. The sub-vocalization cuts out abruptly, replaced by a tense, hissing silence. Ahead, in a small cup of stone, the sun has struck a pocket of ice. The tension in the water is peaking. The Keeper knows this. It can feel the molecular stress in the air.

*Crack.*

The sound is sharp, a pistol shot in the quiet amphitheater. It is the dinner bell.

The Keeper moves with terrifying speed, a lunge that would crush me if I were not gripping its armor with the suction of a limpet. It looms over the pool, the proboscis extending, dripping with enzymatic fluids.

This is my moment.

While the giant is distracted by the feast, while its sensory dome is entirely focused on the chemical composition of the meltwater, I detach my rear suckers. I swing down, inverted, crawling across the underside of its "chin" plate.

I am not here for the water. I am here for the vibrations the Keeper ignores.

When the ice cracked, a shockwave traveled through the stone pillars. Trapped in the microscopic crevices of the rock, stirred by the sudden release of energy, are clouds of "stone-motes"—microscopic, crystalline organisms that feed on geologic stress. They are vibrating now, stirred from their torpor.

I extend a long, gossamer tongue, invisible in the gloom beneath the Keeper's head. I sweep it through the air just above the pooling water. The tongue is charged with a static lure. The stone-motes, confused and agitated, cling to it.

I reel the tongue back in, swallowing the cloud of vibrating minerals. They rattle down my gullet, a cache of kinetic energy that will keep my internal metabolism humming for days.

The Keeper straightens. It has finished drinking. The proboscis retracts with a wet snap.

I scramble back up the length of its face, pressing myself flat against the wet calcite just as the sensory dome begins to rotate. I am back in the shoulder fissure before the giant takes its next step. I am still. I am stone. I am silent.

The air is thin here, and cold enough to shatter bone, but I am full. The Keeper carries me onward toward the deep shadows of the amphitheater, a blind god carrying a thief on its back, moving through the grove of grey ghosts, waiting for the next ice rim to break.
