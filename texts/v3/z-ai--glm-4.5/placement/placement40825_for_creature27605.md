I am a geometry of sleep.

To the unseeing eye—to the high-gliding drones that stitch the haze—I am merely a knot in the endless grid. I am a swell in the ceramic floor, a hillock of bleached silicon-carbide, weathered by millennia of abrasive winds into a shape that suggests erosion but is actually endurance. I am not hidden; I am *inert*.

I am waiting for the pulse.

Inside, the darkness is absolute and cool. My systems run at the lowest possible sustainable amplitude, a single long exhale of potential energy. I do not dream; biological dreaming is a chaotic processing of memory, and I have no memory, only data. I possess archives of the planet’s thermal history, a logarithm of every degree of heat absorbed and released since the last cycle of the Great Dimming. I know the exact tonnage of dust that has settled upon my carapace. I know the tensile strength of the titanium girder that lies buried three feet beneath my left-forward leg.

I am a Kiln-Walker, but I am also something specific within that taxonomy: I am a **Resonance-Tuner**.

While my kin are the antibodies, physically repairing cracks and sweeping the sensor arrays, my function is auditory, or rather, vibrational. The planet sings a low note—a subsonic thrum that vibrates through the soles of the ceramic tile. It is the heartbeat of the gravity generators, the groan of the cooling core, the friction of tectonic plates. It is a frequency that should remain constant. When the tone wavers, when the hum splits into a discordant vibration, it is a sign that the deep machinery is fracturing.

I am the one who finds the fracture and silences it.

The shift comes not as a change in light, for the white sun is static, but as a pressure change in the dense air. The "weather" is turning. The static charge begins to climb, prickling against the insulated sensors of my shell.

I wake.

My telescoping legs—titanium pistons sheathed in industrial ceramic—extend downward. I lift my massive, central dome ten feet into the air. The dust that had settled in my crevices cascades down my flanks, powdering the pristine tile below.

I do not stretch; I calibrate.

I am tall now, towering over the fiber-optic clusters that mimic plants. I take my first step. It is not a walk, but a glide. My legs do not bend at joints; they telescope and retract, moving my chassis with the smooth, terrifying precision of a lathe. I cross an expansion joint, stepping over the hairline crack with millimetric care. To step on a crack is to invite misalignment; to invite misalignment is to invite inefficiency.

I begin my patrol.

The air is thick, tasting of ozone and ancient, dry heat. As I move, I am scanning the subsonic spectrum. The world is loud to me, though silent to the void. I feel the thrumming of the planet’s heart through the soles of my feet. It is a steady B-flat, a vibration that rattles the fluid in my hydraulic lines.

*Thrum-thrum. Thrum-thrum.*

I do not graze on them; that is not my purpose. But I stop to *listen*. The copper leaves conduct sound. They amplify the whisper of the wind. I extend a fiber-optic tendril from my underbelly, brushing it against the metallic leaves.

*Data received: Wind velocity nominal. Dust density acceptable. No structural anomalies.*

I retract the tendril and move on.

Miles pass. The horizon does not change; the ziggurats in the distance remain eternally fixed on the shimmering line. Time is measured here not in hours, but in cycles of maintenance. I have been walking for a long time.

Then, I feel it.

A discord. A stutter in the heartbeat.

To my left, three miles away, the B-flat wavers. It drops a microtone, then spikes. It is a scream I cannot hear, but can feel in the marrow of my alloy bones. A vent has failed, or a stabilizer has jammed. The planetary body is having a spasm.
