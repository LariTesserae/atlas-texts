I wake to the realization that I am not vibrating fast enough.

This is the first and only truth of the morning. The Hydro-Span does not allow for stillness; to be still is to be dead, or worse—to be debris. The air around me is a thick, humid soup, heavy with the taste of ozone and wet iron. It presses against my skin, not as an embrace, but as a constant, physical reminder of the density of the world. I am lying on a surface that feels like the tongue of a sleeping beast—warm, slightly rippled, "smart-matter" pavement that is currently dreaming of a smoother topology.

I push myself up. My hands are not hands, not as you would understand them. They are elongated clusters of prehensile steel rods, encased in a pale, translucent skin that glistens with the constant oily sheen of my secretions. As I flex the fingers, the crust of green patina and oxidized copper that has hardened over my knuckles during my sleep cracks like a dry mudflat. It flakes away, revealing the sensitive, wet flesh beneath. I am molting, again. The city eats its own, and I am no exception; I must constantly shed the armor the environment tries to weld onto me, or I will become a statue, a permanent fixture of the architecture.

I am in the **Shear-Zone**, the vast, turbulent altitudes where the great districts of the Hydro-Span rotate against one another. Below me, miles down into the gloom, is the Sedimentary Layer, a graveyard of sludge and shadow. Above me, the glass ceiling is a distant, glowing myth. Here, I am suspended in the belly, where gravity feels like a suggestion and the shadows detach themselves to wander for a while.

I am a **Tether-Born**, but not the kind that dances on the thermal updrafts hunting polymer birds. I am something smaller, something more integral to the friction of the place. I am a **Lubricator-Priest**, though I have no congregation and no god. My congregation are the massive, grinding gears that articulate the limbs of this city.

I stand, and the world shifts. A platform the size of a city block begins its slow, groaning rotation to the left. I do not fight it. I let my knees—those double-jointed, hydraulic shock-absorbers—soften. My feet, wide and suction-cupped, find purchase on the wet smart-matter. I wait for the *click*, the microscopic adjustment of the pavement’s texture that signals it recognizes my mass and will support me. The city breathes in, a rush of air venting from a grating the size of a house, and I breathe with it, my gills fluttering along the ridges of my neck.

There is a sound. Not the roar—that is silence to us, the baseline of existence. This is a discordant note. A screech of metal on metal that resonates in the hollow cavities of my femurs. *Pain.* The city is in pain.

I move. I do not walk; I flow. My motion is a series of controlled falls, a lubricated skittering that sends ripples through the puddles collecting on the bridge-way. My spine, a flexible column of cartilage and bone, twists as I leap toward the source of the screech—a massive junction where two sky-bridges are retracting.

The heat here is immense. It radiates from the tension cables, glowing a dull, angry red. I can see the swarm of copper-winged insects gathering, drawn to the heat like a metallic fog. They batter against my face, their wings clicking, tasting the salt on my skin. I ignore them. I am focused on the joint.

It is seizing. A buildup of mineral accretion, a "rust-tumor," has grown in the mechanism, choking the flow of the hydraulic fluid. If it snaps, the bridge will collapse, falling into the Sedimentary Layer, crushing the Bottom-Feeders who sift through the muck below.

I reach the edge of the precipice. The wind from the ventilation shafts whips the fog into a frenzy. I look down, not into the abyss, but at the mechanism. My eyes, lidless and protected by a thick, gelatinous film, zoom in, adjusting to the glare of the sparks.

I begin to sing.

I have no vocal cords for the air. Instead, I press my palms flat against the vibrating metal of the machine housing. I hum, low and deep, vibrating my very bones. I feel the thrum of the city—the billion-system roar—and I isolate the frequency of the jammed gear. I match it. I amplify it. My body becomes a tuning fork, shaking violently, the oil on my skin foaming into a white lather.

I am counter-resonating.

The metal screams. The rust-tumor, brittle from the mismatched frequencies, shatters. It explodes outward, spraying shards of red and brown debris. I shield my face with my forearms, feeling the sharp sting of the shrapnel embedding itself in my ceramic shoulder-plates.

The gear shifts. The bridge lurches, then slides smoothly into its housing. The screeching stops, replaced by the rhythmic, healthy *thrum-hiss* of a functioning machine. The pain in my bones recedes, replaced by the soothing, monotonous drone of the Span.

I am shaking, the expended energy leaving me light-headed. A shadow detaches itself from the strut beside me—a trick of the weird physics here—and lingers for a moment before fading. I watch it go. The Tether-Born say the detached shadows are memories the city has forgotten. I wonder what this one was. A panic? A past failure?

A group of copper-winged insects lands on my shoulder, sensing the heat of my exertion. Without thinking, my tongue—long, barbed, and coated in a neurotoxic adhesive—snaps out. I catch three of them. The taste is sharp, metallic, like licking a battery. It is good. It replenishes the salts I lost in the song.

I sit back on my haunches, the rain beginning to fall again, warm and greasy. I scrape the rust shards from my arm, letting them clatter onto the smart-matter. The street ripples, absorbing the debris, digesting it, recycling it.

I look up at the eternal twilight, the diffuse light that casts no shadows because shadows are alive here. I am tired. My oil is low. I need to find a warm cavity, a hollow in the machinery near a power conduit, to sleep. To curl up like a babe in swaddling clothes, wrapped in the insulation of high-tension cables, and let the vibrations rock me into a stupor.

I am not a person. I am a resistor in a circuit. I am a cell in the bloodstream of a giant. I am the oil between the gears.

I close my eyes, but I do not stop seeing. I feel the pressure waves of the city, the heartbeat of the Hydro-Span, and I vibrate with it, one small, slick note in the deafening symphony of the storm.
