I am the heat sink. I am the stillness in the lung of the mountain.

Here, the air is not a medium to breathe but a substance to swim through—thick, metallic, hot. It presses against my hide, a gelatinous membrane that feels the pressure of the darkness as a physical weight. I do not have eyes to close, but my entire body is an eye that sees only texture and temperature. I feel the geologic cyst around me, a jagged womb of newborn stone, screaming silently as it cools.

*Plip.*

The sound hits me through the floor plates of my feet before it registers as noise. It travels up through the dense rubber of my muscles, a seismic ripple in a world the size of a breath. One. Two. Three. Four. The metronome of my existence. In the interval between the drops, I listen to the mountain grind. I hear the microscopic shrieks of crystal edges locking into place. The rock is virulent, sharp, angry to exist. It is a chaos of needles waiting to bleed anything that moves too quickly.

But I do not move quickly. I am the Sentinel of the Pause. I am the creature that learned to stop moving so the mountain would forget I was there.

I extend a limb—not an arm, but a probe of intention—and my filaments drift ahead of me. They taste the fog. The humidity is 100%, a soup of iron and acid. It coats my sensory hairs, carrying the chemical signature of the corner. The food is there. The soft, vibrating liver of the world. It hums a low-frequency thrum that rattles my teeth, a solitary note of biology in a choir of geology.

I pull my limb back. I am not hungry yet. I must calculate the mass. To take too much is to break the equation.

Instead, I shift my weight. My body spreads, flowing like oil into the negative spaces between the crystal forest. I avoid the serrated outcrops with the precision of a mathematician. My flesh is cool against the fever of the rock, absorbing the ambient heat, storing it, radiating it back in slow, rhythmic pulses. I am a thermostat in a furnace.

My manipulator arm curls inward. The three-fingered gripper, scarred by a lifetime of navigating this blade-filled world, flexes. Inside the grasp is the object.

The River Stone.

It is smooth. It is an alien thing. It does not belong here, where everything is sharp and wet and raw. I run a calloused pad over the surface of the stone. It feels like a memory of water that moved freely, of a sun I have never felt, of a time before the mountain folded in upon itself. The handle of woven dried grass is tight in my grip, preserved by the oils I secrete. It is my civilization. It is my proof that there was once a "not-here."

I sit on the worn slab—the friction-polished throne where I have waited for eons. I lower my metabolic rate. My heart stretches the silence between its beats, mimicking the *plip* of the condensation. I match the temperature of the room. I become the stone.

I am not trapped. I am not a prisoner, though I am the warden. I am the keeper of the equilibrium. I hold the stone that remembers the river, and I wait in the dark, ensuring that the balance remains. That the sharp walls do not close. That the fungus continues to pulse. That the air continues to sweat.

I am the intelligence that sits in the dark, holding the memory of smoothness, while the mountain is born around me.

*Plip.*

I am here.
