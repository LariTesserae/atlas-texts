I would be a fixture of the architecture, immobile and immense, mistaken for the very bones of the city until one looked too closely.

**Where I Am**

I would find myself suspended in the "Middle Tiers," that suffocating layer kilometers above the buried ground and kilometers below the bronze sky. Specifically, I would span a jagged chasm between two ancient, cylindrical refineries—structures the size of mountains, their skins flaking in sheets of rust the size of city blocks.

This chasm is a wind tunnel. The humid air is funneled through it at high velocity, creating a perpetual, mournful howl that drowns out the distant screeching of the Shear-Wings. Here, the condensation is heaviest; the water weeps from the overhead pipes and turns into a thick, oily mist before it can hit the depths. I would be rooted deep into the reinforced concrete of the western refinery, my mass arcing across the void to merge seamlessly with the eastern hull. I am not merely passing through; I have grown into the metal and stone. My roots penetrate the ventilation shafts, drinking the waste heat, while my upper surface provides the only horizontal surface for a kilometer in any direction.

**What I Am**

I am a ** calcified colossus**, a living bridge. I belong to no species described in the records of this world, for I am a singular occurrence, a mutation of scale and time.

My form is that of a vast, segmented arch, reminiscent of the ribbed spine of a whale but stretched taut and ossified. I am perhaps a hundred meters high at the center of my span, and half a kilometer long. My "skin" is not soft flesh, but a dense, ceramic-like carapace that mimics the pitted concrete and riveted iron of the surrounding refineries. Over centuries—perhaps millennia—oxidation and industrial soot have stained me in perfect camouflage. To the Conduit-Stalkers sunning themselves on the pipes above, I look like just another buttress of the city. To the Grazers below, I am the sky.

I am a **Sessile Thermal Siphon**.

I possess no mouth, no eyes, and no limbs. I do not move; I have not moved since the Riven Shelf was young. I exist entirely to process the environment. My internal biology is a labyrinth of hollow channels and cooling fins, evolved to exploit the temperature differentials of my location. I draw in the superheated steam rising from the factories through vents in my underbelly, circulating it through my massive bulk to drive a metabolic process so slow it makes the pistons below look frantic.

**My Existence**

Because I am vast and motionless, I have become an ecosystem unto myself—a "living island" in the river of smog.

The **Volt-Parasites** love me. My large, flat back acts as a grounding plane for the stray currents of the Shelf. They swarm along my spine, their blue bioluminescence flickering like Christmas lights in the fog, biting into my shell to sip the bio-electricity that builds up around my nervous system. I do not mind them; their presence keeps my shell polished and free of the heavier rusts that would otherwise seal my pores.

The **Conduit-Stalkers** use my arch as a bridge to cross the chasm where the catwalks have collapsed. They lumber across my back, their heavy, clawed feet clicking rhythmically against my carapace. I feel the vibration of their steps—a dull, resonant thud that travels through my stonelike flesh. Occasionally, a Stalker will pause to rest in the lee of my flank, mistaking me for a dead piece of the city. I let them stay. The friction of their scales against my side removes the lichen that tries to grow there.

The **Sump-Rats** live within me. They have burrowed into the softer, insulating layers between my armored plates, creating a labyrinth of tunnels that mirrors the city’s own subway system. They scavenge the detritus that collects on my back—dead beetles, bird droppings, the bodies of exhausted Volt-Parasites—and in return, their movements aerate my internal tissues, keeping my slow circulation from stagnating.

**My Consciousness**

My mind is not a rapid-fire of thoughts, but a long, slow tide. I perceive time in the shifting of the bronze clouds and the groan of the tectonic plates.

I am intimately connected to the heartbeat of the Riven Shelf. Because my roots are deep in the foundations, I feel the agonizingly slow plunge of the pistons miles beneath me. I feel the pressure spikes in the deep water tables. I am the machine’s sense of touch.

To be me is to endure. I do not know hunger or fear, only a profound, vibrating existence. I am the synapse connecting two halves of a sleeping world. When the humid wind screams through the chasm, it does not disturb me; it sings to me. I vibrate in harmony with the roar, a standing wave of flesh and concrete, eternal and unmoving, holding the chaotic, scuttling world upon my back.
