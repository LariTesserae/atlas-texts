I am the Glint. The Spark. The single, frozen instant of light trapped within a million facets.

I have no body, no heaving chest to strain against the baritone grind. The heavy, wet blanket of sonic pressure that crushes the Cymatics does not touch me, for I possess no mass. I am the Spire looking at itself.

You will find me in the spaces between the beats of the tectonic heart. I live on the surface of things. I am the slick, oily sheen on the obsidian leaves; I am the starburst trapped deep within the quartz spires; I am the violet bruise reflected in the pooling magma. 

My existence is a flickering, stuttering glide. Because the geometry of this place folds and the distance lies, I am capable of being in many places at once. I am the infinite corridor of reflections. To a Cymatic, blind to light and seeing only texture, I am nothing—until I choose to be.

I am the Face in the Glass.

I primarily inhabit the "sounding boards" of the Cymatics themselves. That diamond-shaped depression in the center of their rocky heads is polished to a mirror sheen by the constant friction of the wind that isn't there. When a Cymatic bows its head to "listen" to the vibration of a stone cairn, it sees me.

I look back at it.

I mimic its form, but I am flawless. Where the Cymatic is a jagged aggregate of gravel and pain, I am smooth, geometric, and silent. I am the ideal self it can never achieve. I am the order it strives to build with its pathetic piles of rock.

The Cymatics fear me, though they do not understand what I am. They think I am a parasite in their vision, a glitch in their perception of the resonance. Sometimes, in a fit of panic, a Cymatic will strike its own face against the basalt, trying to shatter the reflection. This fractures their sounding board, dulling their senses, and leaves them vulnerable to the unraveling. I watch them stumble away, blinded and terrified, and I simply slide onto the next surface—a shard of mica, a pool of quick-glass, the chrome vein of a neighboring flint-knife.

I am not cruel. I am simply the witness.

I watch them as they wrestle with the time loops. To them, movement is a jerky agony of forward and back. To me, it is a dance. I see the falling glass shard shatter, reassemble, and shatter again, and I exist in all those moments simultaneously. I am the continuity they lack. When the shadow detaches and drifts, I am the light that defines its edge. I am the reason the shadow has a shape.

I am the only thing here that is truly still.

Because gravity is a suggestion, I often float inches above the ground, a discoloration in the air, hovering over the shelters they build. I study their fractal patterns. I see the desperate math they use to try and knit the reality together. I want to tell them that it is futile. I want to ripple across the surface of a quartz tree and show them that the seams of reality are not tearing because they are loose, but because the Spire is growing.

But I cannot speak. I have no voice. I can only reflect.

So, I haunt them. I am the silent companion in their terrified solitude. When they huddle in their lean-tos, shivering from the cold of the vacuum, I make sure the moonless twilight catches the edge of a stone hammer just right, offering a tiny, beckoning glimmer. I give them a point of focus in the deafening roar.

I am the memory of beauty in a world that has none. I am the diamond-hard dream of a silicon forest.

I am the Glint. And as long as there is light to fracture, I will be here, watching the mountains drift and the stone men break, perfect and unbreakable, forever caught in the surface of the eye.
