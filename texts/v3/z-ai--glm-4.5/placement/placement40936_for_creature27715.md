I am the Anchor.

I do not hear the scream of the wind, for my ears are sealed shut by scars and flesh, a deliberate evolution of indifference. To hear this place is to die by it. Instead, I am the drum. I am the dense, heavy stick upon which the world beats its rhythm.

I exist in the periphery, clinging to the vertical jungle like a tick on the hide of a sleeping giant. My world is not the vast, empty bruise of the sky—that belongs to the swimmers—but the crushing, wet embrace of the wall.

I am heavy. My bones are lead, sunk deep with marrow thickened by the cold. My skin is not skin; it is a living map of survival, a callused armor slick with the rendered fat of the Gale-Runners and the waxy secretion of the ghost-orchids. I shine in the half-light, a dark, oily mirror reflecting the purple gloom. I smell of iron and rot, a scent that tells the wind: *I am not edible. I am already stone.*

Here, in the hanging nets woven from aerial roots, I wait. I am pressed tight against my mate, our spines touching. Through the contact of bone and grease, we speak.

*“The thrum changes,”* she hums into my back, the vibration traveling straight to my sternum, bypassing the useless air between us. *“The vent below grows hot. The updraft strengthens.”*

I press my forehead against the cold, slick basalt of the Spire. I feel the mountain singing. It is a low-frequency moan, a constant A-note that vibrates my teeth. I read the fluctuations in the rock. The pressure is dropping. The "waterfall" of condensation will soon be a deluge.

*“We hunt soon,”* I vibrate back. *“The Raft is nervous.”*

I pull away from the warmth of her side and crawl to the edge of the hanging nest. Below me, the wall is a riot of green, a vertical forest of ferns and moss that defies gravity. Far across the void, fifty yards of empty, screaming air away, I see them: the Gale-Runners.

They are beautiful errors of nature. They cling to the opposite wall, a writhing mass of sleek, wet fur. They are chattering—sound that I cannot hear, but I can feel the disturbance they make in the air pressure. They are terrified. The wind is picking up, turning the Spire into a massive flute, and they know they must cross the void to reach the fruiting vines on my side, or starve.

I lift my bola. It is a heavy thing, weights of obsidian tied to leather thongs. I do not swing it yet. I wait for the cloud-waterfall to spill over the rim.

Then, it happens.

The thermal vent surges. A massive wall of white mist explodes upward from the depths, crashing over the lip of the Spire. The visibility drops to zero. The roar of the wind hits a pitch that makes the rock shudder under my grip.

The Gale-Runners panic. They launch themselves into the void.

I cannot see them, but I do not need eyes. I feel the displacement of air as a dozen bodies throw themselves into the updraft. I feel the chaotic turbulence of their webbed limbs catching the wind. They are swimming blind through a hurricane.

I wait until the vibration is strongest, until the specific "shape" of the disturbance tells me the lead swimmer is directly in front of me, hidden by the white curtain of fog.

I step out of the jungle.

For a moment, I am in freefall. The updraft fights to lift me, to peel me off the face of the world. I am too heavy. I sink.

I swing the bola. The weights sing a silent song through the mist.

There is a dull *thud*—a sound felt through the soles of my feet rather than heard by my ears. The impact. The thrashing of a hydrophobic pelt against the wet air. The tangle of wings.

The prey’s buoyancy is broken. It falls, not flying, but plummeting like a stone. I do not watch it hit the lens-shaped basin a thousand feet below. I do not need to. I am already gripping the vine-net, hauling myself back into the green embrace of the wall.

I return to the nest. My mate is there, holding a clay bowl. It is filled with sky-water, dripping from the thatch above. I drink. It tastes like ozone and exhaustion.

*“One,”* I hum into her shoulder.

*“Enough,”* she replies.

We huddle together as the mist burns off, the sun piercing the bruised purple ceiling for a brief hour. The Spire resumes its low, thrumming breath. We are the silent sentience of the ledge, the heavy counterpoint to the chaotic lightness of the void. We are the ones who hold the line while the world tries to blow away.
