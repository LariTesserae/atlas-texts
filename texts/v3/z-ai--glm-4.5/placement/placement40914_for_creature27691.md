I would find myself not on the ground, nor in the water, but suspended in the interstice between the grinding sky and the sweating earth. I am anchored to the vertical flank of the largest piston, a monolith of pitted iron the size of a mountain that rises and falls with the slow, rhythmic respiration of the world.

I am a **Felt-Weaver**.

I am not small. My body is a sprawling, multi-limbed architecture of flesh, resembling a crab, but one that has been overgrown by a mossy, gray coral. I am the size of a small house, yet I am light, built of hollow bones and gas-filled bladders that keep me from buckling under my own mass. My exoskeleton is not hard; it is soft, spongy, and absorbent, covered in a thick, luxurious pelt of grayish fur that constantly drips with the moisture I wring from the air.

I am the keeper of the quiet.

In a world of grinding metal and churning biomass, noise is an enemy—a chaotic vibration that threatens to crack the delicate balance of the deep hum. This is where I find my purpose. I possess no eyes, for vision is useless here in the bruised twilight. Instead, I am a being of pure tactile sensation. My limbs, eight in number, are tipped with sensory manipulators that can feel the stress in a steel plate three inches thick. They can taste the harmonic dissonance in a gear tooth.

I spend the long, slow cycles of the piston traversing the vertical surfaces of the machine. I am a mobile patch of insulation. Where the metal grinds against metal, where the friction creates a high-pitched shriek that is audible only to the most sensitive of ears, I descend. I secrete a thick, viscous gray mucus from glands along my underbelly. With my wide, spatulate hands, I smear this secretion over the complaining joints, layering it with the precision of a master mason.

This mucus hardens quickly into a dense, organic felt. It is a living fabric, teeming with microscopic bacteria that eat the sound. As the felt cures, it absorbs the vibration, turning the screech of tortured iron into a dull, subsonic throb—a beat that matches the thrum of the world. I am bandaging the machine, swaddling the sharp edges of its decay in softness.

I am symbiotic with the machine, but also parasitic. I eat the friction. The heat generated by the grinding gears warms my blood; the vibration stimulates my nerves. I cannot survive in stillness; I require the constant, low-level agony of the machine to exist. When the piston rests in its long exhale, I curl into a ball, locking my limbs into the crevices of the metal, and sleep, dreaming of the next cycle’s work.

Below me, the great Oxidizers trundle like living boulders, their footfalls sending tremors through the metal. I feel them through the soles of my feet. They are the gardeners of the floor, and I am the gardener of the architecture. Sometimes, a Thermal-Weaver will flow past me on its way to a steam vent, its ribbon body seeking the heat. We ignore one another. We are merely different components in the same engine.

My existence is a slow, meditative labor. I move with the tectonic pace of the piston itself. There is no rush. The rust is eternal; the noise is constant. I am the silence made flesh, a heavy, wet shadow moving in the gloom, binding the wounds of the world with a layer of soft gray felt, ensuring that the only sound that remains is the deep, breathing lullaby of the basin.
