I am the motion that is mistaken for stillness.

To look at me is to see nothing but a rise in the grey earth, a curvature of stone that doesn't quite match the sweep of the land. I am a **Stone-Lipped Behemoth**.

I find myself in the center of a vast, shallow bowl, the Peneplain, where the horizon pulls away from me in every direction, a silent tease of distance that I will never traverse. Beneath my belly, the bedrock is smooth and cool, polished by the bellies of my ancestors into a surface like glass. The earth here does not support me; I float upon it. The water table sits so high that the stone weeps, creating a slick frictionless film that allows my immense mass to glide.

I am heavy. I am heavy in a way that nothing in this world but the mountains themselves used to be. My legs are pillars, stumps that have forgotten how to bend, ending in splayed toes that distribute my gravity like a stain upon the rock. I do not step; I ooze forward, a geological event occurring in real-time, slower than the dripping of the condensation.

My world is defined by the vibration of the stone. Because I am deaf to the higher pitches—the frantic, electric panic of the world above—I feel the landscape as a constant, low-frequency thrumming. Through the pads of my feet and the skin of my underbelly, I sense the boiling energy of the **Lens-Pan Frogs**. To me, they are not a cacophony of croaks, but a physical tickle, a million tiny drumbeats striking the rock. They are the fever of the world, a frantic itch that I carry with me wherever I go.

Above me, the air is thick enough to drink. It hangs warm and heavy, smelling of wet dust and the sharp, green scent of crushed algae. My shell, a wide, grey boat deck of keratin and armor, is a universe of its own. I feel the condensation gathering on my back—the tears of the world—beading up and trickling down my flanks in rivulets. Sometimes, small things—mosses, opportunistic succulents—try to take root in the cracks of my carapace. I let them. They are the jewelry of the old.

I am currently grazing.

My beak, shaped like a shovel’s edge, drags across the wet stone. *Schh-thwip. Schh-thwip.* It is the only sound I make, and it is softer than the rain. I am scraping the world clean, consuming the gelatinous, grey-green films of algae that are the only harvest this senescent land offers. It is a taste of iron and time. I do not rush. A single patch of slime may take me a day to consume. I am stripping the rock of its skin, and the rock does not mind.

Occasionally, a shadow falls over me, sharp and sudden. I look up—slowly, with the agonizing hydraulic creak of neck muscles that haven't turned in a century. It is a **Stilt-Walker**, a Wraith of wet slate feathers, hovering on the heavy thermals above me. To it, I am merely a moving island, a feature of the terrain. It does not fear me. It screams, a harsh laugh that tears through the humid air, and dives toward one of the shimmering lenses of water nearby. I watch it with eyes that are rheumy and dark, seeing it as a jagged tear in the soft fabric of the sky.

I am envious of its speed. It moves with the frantic energy of the **Drones**, those gauze-winged sparks that burn out in a single evening. It lives in the blur; I live in the pause.

The sun begins to dip, though the warmth does not leave the air. This is the time of the waking. Around me, the vibration of the frogs intensifies, becoming a roar that I feel in the marrow of my bones. The water lenses boil with their mating, a desperate attempt to shout down the silence of the dying hills.

I pause my grazing. I lower my great head, resting my chin upon the wet stone. My breathing slows, matching the rhythm of the seeping water. I am the anchor. I am the memory of the mountain. While the frogs scream their short lives away, I will wait here, dissolving into the grey, indistinguishable from the rock, until the horizon finally comes to meet me.
