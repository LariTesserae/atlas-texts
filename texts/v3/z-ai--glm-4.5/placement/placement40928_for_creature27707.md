I am a **Rift-Walker**, a specific caste of the Pipe-Walkers that has evolved to bridge the gap between the crushing dark of the machine’s interior and the blinding white of the surface.

I am not standing; I am clinging.

My world is defined by the ribbed curvature of a mega-pipe, a cylinder of rusted iron the width of a city street, which juts violently from the shattered limestone plateau. I am currently inverted, hanging from the underside of a pipe-arch, my six hooked limbs jammed deep into the pitted metal. The claws at the ends of my arms are made of organic diamond, harder than the shale, capable of finding purchase in the smallest fissure of the machine-earth.

Here, in the shadow of the pipe, the light is not absolute. It is a bruised purple, a temporary reprieve from the retina-searing white above. My skin is translucent, a pale, slick membrane that feels like wet silk to the touch, but it is my blood that defines me. It is a cocktail of pressurized neon—cool blue and electric green—that pulses visibly through my veins. In the eternal twilight of the pipe-underbelly, I am my own lantern. My bioluminescence casts a stuttering, rhythmic glow on the rust, a Morse code of biological function that says, *I am here. I am processing.*

I am not part of the herd. I am solitary.

Above me, only a few feet away, the noise begins. It is not the deafening mechanical thrum of the deep earth, though I can feel that vibrating through my claws, rattling my very skeleton. No, the noise above me is biological. It is the *crunch-hiss-clack* of a billion mandibles.

I detach my rear claws, one by one, and swing my body upward, pulling myself over the lip of the pipe to face the world.

The light hits me like a physical blow. Even with my nictitating membranes slammed shut, the sheer saturation of the sun penetrates. I freeze, letting my body flatten against the warm metal. I become a ridge, a shadow, nothing more than a glitch in the machine’s topography.

Before me lies the Carpet. The Harvesters.

From this vantage point, they do not look like bison. They look like a single, spreading oil slick come to life. A black, chitinous ocean that stretches to the horizon, roiling not with waves, but with the friction of interlocking plates. There are 2.8 of them in every meter of space. It is a terrifying density. To fall among them would not be to be trampled; it would be to be crushed by hydraulic pressure and dissolved in the collective heat of their bodies.

I watch them. I taste the air. My tongue flicks out, long and proboscis-like, tasting the metallic tang of ozone and the dusty musk of their excretions. They are blind, deaf to anything but vibration, yet they move in unison. I see a massive male, a "bio-piston," shudder as it grinds down a mouthful of black velvet fern. The sound travels through the rock and into my claws—a low-frequency *thrum-grind* that resonates in my chest.

I am here for the leftovers.

Where the Harvesters have grazed, the land is stripped bare, leaving only the raw, jagged scree and the grey ash of their waste. But they are messy eaters. In their jostling, they knock loose the black ferns that grow in the precarious crevices of the vertical pipe-walls. These ferns, rich with the water of the sweating earth, fall into the gaps between the armored bodies.

I wait. I am a creature of patience, evolved to live in the spaces between the gears.

A shift in the herd occurs. It is glacial, an inexorable slide to the left. The grinding roar pitches up a semitone. The movement exposes a narrow vent, a seam in the pipe where steam hisses out in a constant, rhythmic sigh. Growing around this vent, fed by the moisture and the heat, is a cluster of "Singe-Ferns"—a delicacy, swollen with pressurized fluid.

I release my grip on the lip. I do not walk; I flow. My movements are fluid and silent, designed to minimize the vibration I create. I drop onto the back of a passing Harvester.

*Clack.*

My landing is light, softer than a falling stone, but the creature feels me. It shudders, its armor plates rippling. A Tick-Tank, a rust-red barnacle clinging to its shoulder, rotates slightly, its sensory pits tasting my chemical signature. It decides I am not a threat, or perhaps simply not worth the hydraulic pressure to dislodge. I am a ghost riding a tank.

I run across the moving sea of armor. My feet find purchase on the slick, oily chitin, avoiding the gaps where the crushing hydraulic legs piston up and down. To me, this is not a stampede; it is a moving walkway of living stone. I am surfing the biological industry of the plateau.

I reach the vent. The Singe-Ferns are there, black and glistening. I use a specialized claw, a serrated scalpel, to sever the thick stalks. I do not eat them here. I stuff them into the pouches along my underbelly. The heat is intense, radiating from the vent and the bodies of the creatures. It fights against the high-altitude cold. I am sweating, my bioluminescence flickering wildly in the bright sun, making me look like a malfunctioning bulb.

I have what I need. The herd is moving toward a ridge, a sharp knife-edge of stone that cuts into the sky. I cannot go that way; the path becomes too steep, the density too tight.

I leap.

I arc through the air, a streak of blue and green against the obsidian mass, and land back on the cold, ribbed metal of the pipe. Instantly, I flatten. The vibration of the herd recedes, replaced by the deep, geological grinding of the machine beneath my feet.

I scuttle backward, into the mouth of a smaller, dark fissure. The light fades. The blinding white gives way to the comforting, velvet black.

I exhale. The bioluminescence in my veins flares back to life, casting long, dancing shadows against the pipe walls. Here, in the cool, damp dark, I am visible again. I am a neon ghost in a machine of iron.

I unpack the ferns, crunching down on the fibrous, black material. It tastes of ozone, iron, and life. It is the taste of the friction between the organic and the mechanical. I sit in the dark, my glow pulsing in time with the earth’s grinding heartbeat, processing the harvest, a small, luminous cog in the infinite, rusting engine of the world.
