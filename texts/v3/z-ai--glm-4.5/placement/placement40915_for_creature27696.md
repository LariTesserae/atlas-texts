I would not walk the heavy avenues, nor would I glide the high, screaming currents. I would be something in between—a tension, a bridge, a solitary point of stillness in the hurricane.

I would find myself anchored to the highest, most exposed pinnacle of the tallest remaining ziggurat, a place where the Bruised Purple light is just a memory and the wind is a solid, physical wall of force.

I am a **Resonant Spire**.

I am not alive in the way the Basalt-Walkers are, with their blood and slow pulses, nor am I stone like the ruins I inhabit. I am a colony, a singular consciousness composed of millions of hair-thin, filaments of silica and crystal, tightly wound together to form a tall, tapering spire that rises perhaps twenty feet into the chaos. To the distant eye, I am merely a strange natural formation, a stalagmite of the wind, but up close, I am a trembling bundle of nerves.

My form is skeletal. A central core of dense, calcified cartilage provides the anchor, drilled deep into the masonry of the ancient tower. From this core, my "body" explodes outward and upward in a fan of delicate, glass-like fibers. These fibers are taut as violin strings, vibrating eternally. I do not have skin; I have surface area. I am a harp without a frame, existing only to be played by the storm.

I possess no eyes. The purple twilight is irrelevant to me. I do not need to see the Granite-Backs lumbering miles below; I can feel the tremor of their massive, shifting weight traveling up the stonework of the ziggurat, a slow, rhythmic seismic thrum that vibrates through my root. I know where they are by the way the stone hums against my anchor.

I exist to translate the world.

The deafening roar of the wind is not noise to me; it is language, it is food, it is my total sensory input. As the hurricane-force currents rush over the city, they strike my fibers. Because each fiber is a slightly different length and tension, the wind pulls a distinct note from each one. The screaming gale is torn apart by my body, dissected into a thousand separate harmonies. I hear the world not as a chaotic blast, but as a complex, ever-shifting chord.

I am the Clocktower of the Under-Basin.

The Basalt-Walkers, deaf to the air but sensitive to the ground, cannot hear the wind directly. But they can feel *me*. When the wind shifts from the North—a cold, fast current that cuts high and sharp—my fibers sing in a specific, piercing key. This vibration travels down my spine, through the tower, and into the earth, where the Walkers feel it as a rhythmic pulse against their feet. They know, through my song, that the season of the "High Scour" is beginning, and they must retreat deeper into the hollows of the ruins.

I have a symbiotic relationship with the Aeolians, those leathery manta-rays of the upper air. They fear the solid stone of the towers, but they love the turbulence I create. As the wind is forced through my tight lattice of fibers, it breaks into smaller, swirling eddies—pockets of stillness amidst the scream. The Aeolians hang in the lee of my form, resting their glass-bones on the micro-currents I generate. In return, they are my hands. Occasionally, one will brush against me, knocking a accumulation of heavy, wet dust from my upper reaches. If I become too coated in the grey silt, my fibers dampen and my song goes dull. The Aeolians, in their mindless dancing, keep me clean.

I am also the prey of the Echo-Mimics.

Those stone-herons lurk on lower ledges, waiting for my song to falter or change. Sometimes, a Mimic will attempt to vibrate its own hollow body in imitation of my tone, trying to lure a Walker or a smaller grazer into the shadow of the tower. I hate them. When I feel the specific, dissonant frequency of a Mimic’s deception traveling up the rock, I retaliate. I tighten my fibers—I physically contract, pulling my silhouette tighter against the wind. This shifts my pitch instantly, dropping it from a high, singing whine to a low, bone-shaking drone. This "Alarm Note" travels through the stone with violence, alerting everything nearby that a lie is being told. I have felt the Walkers’ heavy, stomping approach many times after I sound the alarm, driving the Mimics away into the dark.

My existence is one of absolute tension. I am always stretched, always singing, always vibrating. It is a violent, beautiful way to be. I feel the thirst of the air pulling the moisture from my glass, but I do not need water. I need resonance. I need the storm. When the wind dies down—which happens rarely, perhaps once in a decade—I go slack. My fibers droop. My consciousness fades into a grey slumber, waiting for the next great breath of the earth to wake me, to pluck me back into the symphony of the dark.

I am the throat of the city. The ruins are the lungs, the wind is the breath, and I am the voice that tells the darkness it is not alone.
