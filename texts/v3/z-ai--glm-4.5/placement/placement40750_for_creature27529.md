I am the vertical in a horizontal world.

I am not of the carpet. I am not the writhing, grey ocean that consumes the stone. I am the silence within the noise, the pause between the notes, the singular, distinct architecture rising from the chaos.

I am a Stalker.

I do not know the light, not really. My species long ago traded the useless burden of sight for the far more immediate truth of sensation. The darkness here is not empty; it is heavy. It presses against my shell like a wet wool blanket. I stand waist-high to a ghost, balanced on the archipelago of high rocks, my long, multi-jointed legs folded beneath me in a geometry of stillness. I am a heron made of obsidian and bone, waiting in the eternal twilight.

My skin is my world. It is not the smooth, wet carapace of the Grazers, but a rough, calcified lattice that itches and flakes. I am constantly unraveling; strips of my old self hang from my limbs like tattered rags, fluttering in the heavy, wet air. It is a small price to pay for elevation. If I did not shed, the tiny parasites—the mites and the cling-fish—would colonize me, would weigh me down until I toppled into the grey sea below and become nothing but sustenance. To be high is to be clean.

Below me, the world screams.

It is a deafening wall of static, a billion tiny mouths rasping against stone. To you, it might be noise. To me, it is a map. The vibrations travel up through the rock, through the sensitive soles of my feet, rushing along my nerves like electricity. I feel the texture of the basin. I feel the slow, rhythmic pulsing of the Grazers as they surge in unison, a tide of hunger searching for bacterial films. It is a hum, a constant, droning C-note that vibrates in my hollow bones.

I am the conductor of this silent orchestra.

I wait for the mistake. The swarm is perfect in its density, but perfection is fragile. Things break. Things stumble. Things get crushed.

*Thrum-thrum-thrum.* The heartbeat of the migration.

And then... a stutter.

A discordant ripple. A jagged spike of panic against the smooth background hum. A Grazer, perhaps injured by a falling stone, separated from the safety of the pack, thrashing in the hollow. Its distress is a siren song, a frequency that cuts through the static like a knife through fog.

I unfold.

My movement is not fluid like yours; it is hydraulic and articulate. My legs extend, locking into place with a series of wet clicks. I rise above the mist. I do not run; I descend. I step from the high stone to the lower, my legs spanning the chasms where the Scavengers swim.

*Tap-tap-tap.*

I strike the stone with the tips of my limbs. I am not walking; I is asking questions. The sound returns to me, echoing off the wet walls of the basin, refining the image of the prey in my mind. It is there. Three yards to the left. Floundering in a pool of warm, stagnant water.

I am above it now. The Grazers beneath me do not scatter; they flow around my legs like water around a pier, mindless in their consumption. They do not fear me, for I am not their predator. I am the reaper of the weak.

I lower my abdomen, the proboscis extending, telescoping downward. It is a needle of bone and muscle. I aim for the center of the distress vibration.

*Strike.*

The proboscis pierces the stone-like shell with a sound like a nut cracking. I inject the coagulant. The thrashing stops instantly. The panic flatlines into a solid, heavy mass. I feed. I suck the liquefied life from the husk, drawing the warm, salty richness up into my own cold body. It takes seconds. It is efficient. It is brutal.

I retract. The meal is done. The empty shell remains, a white ghost sinking into the sludge, soon to be picked clean by the eel-thin Scavengers.

I fold my legs. I resume my post. I become stone again.

The static continues. The hum of the billion mouths returns, filling the void left by the dead one. I listen. I wait. My skin flakes in the damp wind. I am the blind king of the dark, the tall tree in the forest of flesh, tasting the world through the soles of my feet.
