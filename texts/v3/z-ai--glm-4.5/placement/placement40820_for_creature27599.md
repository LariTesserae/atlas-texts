I am the Resonator.

I find myself suspended from the vaulted ceiling, deep in the gloom where the light is poorest. I am not stone, though I look it. I am not a machine, though I hum with the grid. I am a cluster of elongated, calcified tubes, hanging like a chime of hollow bones, fused together by centuries of grey limestone drippings. I anchor myself to a rusted, rib-like pipe that protrudes from the darkness above, gripping the metal with a crust of hardened mineral.

From here, upside down, I view the Rust-Garden as a map of faint, bioluminescent constellations. The sickly yellow-green drizzle sifts past my elongated body, the droplets ticking against my outer shell like a clock counting seconds that no one else counts. I do not need the light; I do not have eyes in the traditional sense. Instead, I perceive the cathedral through the texture of vibration.

I am the listener and the amplifier.

My existence is defined by the tension I hold between the industrial past and the geological present. My internal chambers are hollow, lined with sensitive, vibrating cilia that catch the faintest whispers of the world below. I feel the chaotic, syncopated rhythm of the falling rain. I feel the heavy, wet *thump* as a Rust-Mite drags its scrap-metal shell over the rounded stone floor—a sound like a bag of wrenches being slowly spilled.

But I do not merely hear. I return the sound to the room.

When the room is too quiet, when the silence becomes heavy enough to crush the smaller creatures, I sing. It is not a song of melody, but a low, infrasonic drone—a perfect harmonic match to the sleeping machinery deep beneath the floor. I resonate with the thrum that others feel in their teeth. By amplifying it, I stabilize the ecosystem. My hum tells the Glass-Spiders that the sponges are stable; it tells the Drip-Hunters that the air currents are steady. I am the tuning fork by which this wet, chaotic world keeps time.

Directly beneath me hangs a Drip-Hunter. It is a neighbor I know only through the feedback of its movements. I feel the rhythmic *pulse* of its leathery body as it expands and contracts, filtering the drizzle. It is a slow, sloppy metronome. Sometimes, when a heavy drop of condensation strikes its back and runs down, I feel the pluck of the tension in its tendrils, and I modulate my hum to soothe it, a low-frequency lullaby that keeps the "rag" from tearing in the draft.

The world is slow here, but I am slower.

The rule of the Rust-Garden is accretion, and I am its willing victim. The very air I breathe to sustain my resonance is heavy with calcifying dust. With every breath, with every exhalation of vibration, I coat my own insides with a microscopic layer of grey stone. My voice is deepening, growing darker and heavier as my tubes narrow. I am becoming denser, more solid.

I watch the world below through the ripples of the pools. I can feel the impact of the Lacuna-Stalkers as they lunge in their clumsy, time-delayed dance. When they strike the water, the shockwave travels up through the stone, through the pipe I cling to, and into my bones. I taste the desperation of the hunt and the success of the catch. It is a dull, metallic taste, like old blood in copper water.

I know my fate. I am not meant to last forever. I am becoming part of the architecture. Already, my lowest tubes have fused shut, no longer capable of vibrating, turned into solid pendants of rock. I am heavy. The pipe I grip groans under my weight.

Eventually, perhaps in a century, perhaps in ten, I will fall silent. My hollow chambers will seal completely, and I will be just another mineral formation hanging in the dark, indistinguishable from the stalactites around me. The hum will cease, and the Glass-Spiders will scatter in confusion.

But for now, I am the heart of the cathedral. I hang in the damp, yellow gloom, wrapped in the cold, singing the electric song of the earth to keep the darkness at bay. I am the sound that reminds the ruins they are not yet dead.
