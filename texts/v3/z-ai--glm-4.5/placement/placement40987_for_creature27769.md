I find myself suspended in the amber gloom, halfway between the sharp floor of gravel and the woven ceiling of dust.

I am a **Span-Weaver**.

I am not a hunter, nor a grazer. I am a structural necessity, a biological rivet in this architectural ecosystem. My body is an elongated, asymmetrical series of interlocking plates, colored the same matte, oxidized rust as the Fenestration itself. I possess six long, multi-jointed legs that splay out from my central carapace like the stays of a suspension bridge. Each limb terminates not in a claw, but in a magnetic, gripped pad that adheres to the fibrous mineral surface of the stalks without piercing them.

I do not walk; I climb with the slow, fluid precision of a clock hand, traversing the vertical canyons where the "skyscrapers" of the Fenestration press tightest against one another.

My home is the tension. I live in the narrow gaps where two or three massive stalks grind together in the slow growth of the basin. Here, the immense pressure of the expanding city creates friction. My purpose is to bind them.

I am a mason of dry secretion. From a specialized gland on my underside, I extrude a filament of incredibly strong, sticky fibrous material—a composite of my own proteins and the fine cinnamon dust that coats everything. It dries instantly in the warm air into a substance harder than the stalks themselves. I move back and forth between the towers, spinning cables and buttresses, lacing the neighboring trunks together. I reinforce the natural weaving of the canopy, creating the catwalks and suspension bridges that allow the lighter creatures to traverse the void without falling to the gravel below.

To look at me is to look at a mistake in geometry—a jagged, rust-colored growth interrupting the smooth vertical lines of the forest. But I am the glue that holds the silent city together.

My existence is defined by vibration. Because sound dies in the air, I "hear" through my legs. I feel the deep, infrasonic groan of the Column-Eaters as they shear bark five hundred feet above me. I feel the sharp, pinpoint impact of a Litho-Mimic scuttling across the rocks far below, transmitted up the stalks like a telegraph wire. And I feel the terrifying, silent pause of a Silencer as it locks itself into a crevice, waiting.

I am immune to the Silencers. My shell is too dense, my position too exposed, my flesh too dry and unpalatable to be worth the energy required to crush me. I am part of the architecture, as inedible as the rock itself.

My life is a cycle of maintenance. When the ground heaves and a new spire shatters the floor, the shockwave travels up the Fenestration, snapping old fibers. I feel the *snap* in my joints—a violent jolt that rattles my sensory plates. I scuttle to the site of the damage, guided by the fracture patterns in the wood, and I weave it shut again. I am the scar tissue of the forest.

As the "weather" of dust drifts down from the canopy, coating me in a layer of fresh beige, I sit motionless against the towering trunk. I look out across the endless, silent metropolis of brown and rust. Through the narrow gaps between the stalks, I watch the Column-Eaters moving like siege engines in the distance, their silhouettes etched against the dim light. I feel the thrum of the earth beneath me, the slow, grinding engine of creation.

I am at peace here. I have no need for water, no desire for softness. I am a creature of tension and cable, of dust and stone. I am the knot that holds the world together.
