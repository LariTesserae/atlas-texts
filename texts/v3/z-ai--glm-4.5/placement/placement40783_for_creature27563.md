I am not a resident of the High Rookery; I am a function of it. I am a tendon in the great, flexing limb of this place.

I find myself in the "Middle-Weft," a tier of the city suspended halfway between the blinding, sunless roof of the canopy and the churning, tooth-filled dark of the plaza floor. I am crouched on a ledge that would be a windowsill in any other world, but here is a thoroughfare. My perch is slick with the grey-green slime of moss that grows faster than memory. Behind me, the hollowed-out bulb of a petrified-soft wood tree glows with the dull, orange heat of a communal hearth. The warmth radiates against my back, a singular, fragile shield against the pervasive, cool damp (0.7) that seeks to seep into my marrow.

I am a Strand-Walker, but more specifically, I am a **Hum-Reader**.

My body is folded into a shape that would cramp a creature of straight lines and solid earth. My knees are pulled high to my chest, my triple-jointed arms wrapped around them, hugging the verticality. My skin—the color of a bruised mushroom—feels tight and oily, a second layer of armor against the mist that never lifts. I can feel the moisture beading on the coarse whiskers that fan out from my jaw, twitching as they sample the air currents.

To my left, the city wall is not stone, but the ribbed, breathing flank of a giant, fused trunk. I am not idle. In my hand, I hold a chisel made from the dense leg-bone of a Skree, and with a rhythmic, practiced tap, I am clearing a drainage channel. If I stop, the water will pool. If the water pools, the rot sets in. If the rot sets in, the floor—my floor—becomes the sky for the Muck-Thrashers below.

I do not look up often. To look up is to invite the rain and the guano. But my eyes, large and protruding, are protected by a constant sweep of nictitating membranes. I see everything in a wide, fish-eyed distortion. I see the Skree hanging like dark, leathery fruit from the eaves above, their beaks parted in silent screams that the wind carries down to me. I see the "street"—a precarious lattice of woven vines and hewn roots—vibrating with the frantic stampede of my kin.

Through the soles of my feet, felt through the thin layers of woven reed and fur, I feel the *Hum*.

It is a deep, subsonic thrum that runs through the root-mat of the entire plateau. It tells me what the mist hides. It tells me that a section of the Great Vine Bridge is groaning under the weight of a feeding flock. It tells me that the water level in the central plaza is rising, inch by inch, swallowed by the throats of the Thrashers.

A shadow detaches itself from the grey ceiling.

A Skree, young and bold and hungry, dives. I do not flinch. My reaction is not a thought, but a spasm of survival encoded in my DNA. My arm unfolds with a wet *snap*, extending twice its length in a fraction of a second. The bone club in my hand connects with the bird’s skull—a dull, heavy thud that sends feathers spiraling down into the abyss. The creature shrieks, a sound like tearing canvas, and tumbles away, broken but alive. In the High Rookery, we do not kill for malice, only for defense. The fall will finish the job, or the Thrashers will.

I am shaking. Not from fear, but from the energy. The adrenaline tastes sharp on my tongue, cutting through the ammoniac stench.

I shift my weight. The wood beneath me creaks—a high, warning pitch. I pause, my whiskers flattening against my skull. I wait. The creak settles into a groan. It is just the tree growing, just the structure digesting itself. I am safe. For now.

I turn back to my chisel. I must widen the channel. The water is weeping from the bark, gathering in a small, clear pool near my feet. It is beautiful, in a terrifying way. The water is the blood, and I am the platelet, trying to keep the vessel open.

I pull my furs tighter around my shoulders. The fire behind me crackles, a comforting, violent sound. I begin to tap at the wood again. *Chip. Chip. Drip.*

I am here, in the wet throat of the world, suspended between the sky and the swamp, holding the line against the encroaching dark. I am loud. I am frantic. I am alive.
