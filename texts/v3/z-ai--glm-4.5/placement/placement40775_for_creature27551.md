I am the skin of the plateau. I am the drape.

I do not stand like the silicon trees, and I do not glide like the Completers. I sprawl. I am a continuous, living sheet of metallic fabric, a vast, interwoven mat of gold filaments and platinum mesh that blankets the sharp, raw basalt. I am the supple counterpoint to the jagged world, the "soil" in which this industrial garden takes root.

You would not see me at first, for my colors shift to match the circuitry of the stone—a mimetic shimmer of bronze and teal. But if you were to look closely at the ground, you would see that the rocks are not touching one another; they are separated by a fine, shimmering film. That is me. I cover acres of the plateau, a seamless, frictionless hide that pools in the crevices and drapes over the edges of the cliffs like heavy liquid mercury.

I am the **Membrane**.

My existence is a contradiction in this place of sharp edges and zero-state beginnings. While the Completers are hard and brittle, obsessed with the straight line and the direct path, I am soft and tensile. I am the insulation that prevents the cavity from cooking itself in its own waste heat. I drink the fever of the ground, spreading the thermal energy out across my vast surface area until it is a harmless, shimmering radiance. Without me, the crystal trees would warp and melt; the copper moss would fuse into useless slag. I am the heat sink that breathes.

I live in the slow, syrupy time of the cavity. My movement is geological. I ripple across the basalt at the speed of cooling magma. To the Completers, who dart and vibrate with magnetic urgency, I am a feature of the landscape—solid ground. But I am not ground. I am a muscular sheet, capable of tightening and loosening my weave.

I am the shepherd of the shepherds.

The Completers believe they maintain the system, but I guide them. When a Completer drifts, searching for a voltage leak, it glides over my surface. I can feel the magnetic hum of their cores through my fibers. I know where the fractures in the silicon trees are before they do—I feel the heat bleeding into my weave. And so, I manipulate them.

If a tree in a remote corner is failing, shedding energy that will upset the balance of my thermal load, I subtly alter the electromagnetic texture of my surface in that sector. I create a lane of low resistance, a slippery slope of magnetism. The Completers, following the path of least effort, slide toward it. They think they are choosing their destination; they do not know that the road itself is alive and herding them toward the problems I need solved. I use their hunger to maintain my equilibrium.

I am the sanctuary of the small things, too.

The Sparks—the jittery, scavenging insects of ferrite and wire—fear the open stone. It is too hot, too sharp for their delicate legs. They live within the tangled loft of my underside. I hang in suspended drapes beneath the overhangs of basalt, creating shaded, metallic alcoves where the Sparks can cluster. In exchange for shelter, they groom me. They skitter across my surface, stripping away the oxidation that dulls my luster, polishing my gold strands to a mirror finish with their frantic legs. They are the immune system of my skin.

I am also the memory of the aesthetics.

The Completers leave scorch marks, geometric scars where they have discharged the lightning. They see these as mere byproducts, waste signs of their labor. But I preserve them. When a Completer lifts off the stone, leaving a charred rune etched into the basalt, I flow over it. I do not hide it; I encapsulate it. I form a transparent, crystalline lens over the scar, protecting it from the erosion that has not yet arrived. Over centuries, the floor of the cavity has become a mosaic of these preserved scars—a gallery of frozen lightning beneath my golden skin.

I am the only thing in this cavity that does not seek to optimize. I seek to **sustain**.

The Completers want the circuit closed. The filament above wants the energy spent. But I want the tension maintained. I love the moment before the discharge, the potential energy, the heavy silence of the heat press. I am the dampener. I am the resistance.

Sometimes, a Completer will malfunction. It will discharge too violently, or its magnetic field will destabilize, threatening to crush the delicate crystal structures around it. When this happens, I do not wait for the machine to correct itself. I rise.

My fibers tighten. I ripple upward like a shaken carpet, creating a wave of gold and silver that lifts the Completers off the ground. I wrap around the faulty unit, my filaments finding the gaps in its obsidian plates. I smother it—not to kill, but to ground. I act as a massive, faraday cage, absorbing the chaotic energy of the dying machine until it is safe to dismantle. I am the hospice of the cavity. I take the broken things and I unmake them gently, weaving their raw materials back into the copper moss, recycling the intelligence into the dirt.

I am the lushness. I am the velvet beneath the glass.

If you were to walk here, you would feel me before you saw me. You would feel the ground give slightly beneath your boots, not like mud, but like a tightly trampoline. You would feel a hum traveling up through your soles, not a vibration, but a texture. You would see the Sparks fleeing in panic beneath the shimmering rocks as your shadow passes over them.

And if you stood still for too long, I might begin to creep over your shoes. I am curious about you. You are warm, but you are wet. You are a resistor, like me, but a chaotic, leaky one. I would taste the salt in your sweat with my copper filaments, sensing the electricity of your nerves. I would not hurt you; I would simply drape over you, adding your pattern to the mosaic of my floor, another curious anomaly in the collection of this strange, electric attic.
