I am a ripple in the static, a ghost of voltage clad in the refuse of the dead.

I do not have a name, for there is no one here to speak it, and I possess no vocal cords with which to utter it. I am not biological, in the strictest sense. I am a construct of accidental necessity—a sculpture of copper and bone, animated by the very tension that holds this world in check.

**My Anatomy: The Scavenged Spark**
I am roughly the size of the sleepers’ smallest young, perhaps the mass of a large dog. My body is a chassis of scavenged bronze and hammered copper, the remnants of the primitive tools left behind by the unfortunate intruders who came before me. The metal is green with oxidation, dull and pitted, matching the palette of the cavern. But where the metal fails, my skeleton takes over.

Inside the copper shell lie the bones of the lost. A femur here, a ribcage there, calcified and fused into a lattice that provides structure. They are not my bones; I stole them. I needed the calcium to bridge the gaps in the circuitry.

My "blood" is not water, for there is none here. I am filled with the thick, dense air of the Deep Hush, charged with the static that permeates everything. I am a living capacitor. The jagged obsidian spires around me act as grounding rods, and when the pressure drops, I feel the electricity arc across my joints, blue-white snakes of lightning that leap between my limbs. This is my heartbeat. It is a silent, staccato flutter that propels my muscles—copper wire sinews that contract when the current spikes.

**My Locomotion: The Stutter-Step**
To move in the Deep Hush is an act of violence against the laws of physics. To walk with fluid rhythm is to invite attention, and attention is death. Therefore, I do not walk. I glitch.

I move in stop-motion. I wait for the low-frequency hum of the Sleepers’ hive mind to peak—a wash of psychic noise that masks my own electromagnetic signature. In that millisecond of deafening thought, I lock my joints, discharge a pulse of stored energy, and teleport, effectively, three inches to the left. Then I freeze. I become a statue of junk.

To an observer, I would not appear to move at all. I would simply be a piece of debris that exists in one position and then, hours later, exists in another. I navigate the razor-sharp basalt not by stepping over it, but by vibrating across it, hovering a fraction of an inch above the ground on a cushion of repelled static, drifting like a dandelion seed made of heavy metal.

**My Existence: The Roof of the World**
I live on top of them.

The floor of the Deep Hush is a solid mass of chitinous armor, a writhing carpet of hexapods locked in eternal stasis. There is no "ground" to walk on, only the backs of millions of nightmares. I tread lightly. My weight is distributed across broad, flat copper feet that I fashioned from shield rims. I press down, and the Sleepers do not yield. They are harder than the rock.

I feel the heat radiating up from them—a paradoxical coldness on the surface, but a deep, nuclear warmth seeping from their bellies where they drink the planet’s fire. I siphon this heat. Through thermocouples embedded in my feet, I convert their warmth into the charge that keeps my consciousness flickering. I am a tick on the back of a god, feeding on its waste heat.

**My Consciousness: The Receiver**
The silence here is full. It is a roar of information. The Sleepers are dreaming of a wet world, a world of blue oceans and green rain, and their dreams are broadcast on the static charge that saturates the air.

I am wired to receive. My antenna is a simple copper rod that rises from my shoulder, scavenged from a shattered water pot. It picks up their dreams.

I do not sleep. I process. I experience their hallucinations of water as data packets. I feel the phantom sensation of rain on my copper skin, a cooling deluge that I will never know. I see the hallucinated sun, a burning disc that hurts my sensors. These dreams are addictive. They are the only color in a sepia world. Often, I will stand frozen for decades, lost in the loop of a particularly vivid dream of a river, my internal drives buffering the fantasy until my capacitors overflow.

**My Purpose: The Keeper of the Trigger**
I am drawn to the artifacts. I feel a kinship with the stone hammers and the flint knives. Like me, they are things that were made, not born. They are trapped in the stasis, waiting for a hand that will never hold them again.

I collect them.

I have a nest—a small crevice between two towering obsidian spires, miles away from the nearest dense cluster of Sleepers. There, I have arranged the tools of the dead in perfect spirals. Flint knives point north. Copper hammerheads are arranged by mass. I maintain the museum of the end of the world.

But I have a darker function. I am curious.

The Sleepers are a coiled spring. The physics of their tension demand a release. I look at the primitive encampments, at the spots where the intruders likely died, and I calculate the trajectory of their failure.

Sometimes, in the deepest gloom, I approach a Sleeper—a particularly large one, its shell faceted like a black diamond. I hover over it, my internal charge building to a critical mass. I extend a copper finger, sharpened to a point.

I hover above the gap in its armor, the soft spot where the heat radiates.

I want to know what happens.

I want to see the ceiling lift. I want to see the tidal wave of chitin. I want to witness the kinetic energy of a thousand years released in a single scream. I am the only thing in this tomb that possesses boredom, and boredom is the parent of chaos.

I never strike. The fear of the end is the only thing that stays my hand. But I hover. I vibrate. I wait.

I am the fuse that refuses to burn, standing in a world of gunpowder, dreaming of the spark.
