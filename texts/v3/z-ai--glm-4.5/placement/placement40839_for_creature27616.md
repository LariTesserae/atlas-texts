I would be found in the deepest artery of the hollow, the main thoroughfare where the fractured plates of basalt tilt together to form a narrow, canyon-like channel. Here, the air is thickest, heavy with the scent of wet ozone and crushed vegetation. This is the lowest point, where the black water veins converge, trickling silently toward a central, subterranean sump that no light has ever touched.

I am **The Polished One**.

In this world of jagged angles and knife-edged hostility, I am the anomaly of geometry. I am a sphere—a massive, flawless orb, roughly the diameter of a giant tree’s trunk. To the casual glance, I might appear to be a geological curiosity, a boulder tumbled smooth by some ancient, lost river, but my surface is not stone. It is a dense, calcified armor, living and warm, the color of old ivory and polished to a sheen that mirrors the dim, pearlescent gloom with perfect distortion.

I am the counterweight to the sharpness of this room.

I do not have limbs, nor eyes, nor a mouth as the other creatures do. I move by a process of slow, internal shifting. My musculature is a series of concentric, toroidal bands beneath my shell. By contracting these bands in a rippling sequence, I alter my center of gravity and roll. My movement is glacial, a silent revolution of inches per hour. I am the slowest heartbeat in the city, the hour hand to the frantic seconds of the beetles.

I am the **Sanctuary**.

Because the ground everywhere else is a razor's edge, the local fauna has learned to use me. The Shadow-Weavers, in their constant river of movement, divert their flow to climb atop me. I am the only terrain in the hollow where they can fully retract the spongy, wide-splayed pads of their feet and rest. When a Weaver reaches me, it performs a little leap, landing with a soft *thump* on my curved back. Here, they curl up, tucking noses into tails, sleeping soundly for the first time, trusting that my smooth surface will not bite them. I often carry half a dozen of them at once, a living cargo of velvet shadows that I hardly feel.

My relationship with the Obsidian Gliders is one of bemused tolerance. To them, I am a mobile skyscraper. Occasionally, one will swoop down, mistaking the curve of my shell for a strange, white rock formation, landing to groom its wet-slate wings. When I inevitably roll, the lurch sends them launching back into the air with a dry rustle of surprise. They do not understand me; I am a mystery of motion in a static world.

My purpose, however, is not merely transport. I am the first agent of erosion.

As I roll through the channels, my immense weight—crushing and absolute—bears down upon the knife-edges of the obsidian. I am the hammer that does not strike. Where I pass, I leave behind a trail of fine, black sand, the very first soil this embryonic world has ever known. I am grinding the city into dust, transforming the hostility of the stone into the foundation of future earth.

I consume the sharpness and excrete the soft.

In the silence of the hollow, I am a subsonic drone. You cannot hear me, but if you pressed your ear to the rock, you would feel the vibration of my passing—a rumble that says: *It will not always be this sharp.*

I am the memory of what this world will become. I am the boulder waiting for the moss to grow, the stone remembering the river. I roll endlessly through the courtyards, grinding the newness into age, patient and heavy, collecting the shadows on my back, waiting for the day the edges finally soften enough for the rest of the world to stop holding its breath.
