I am the Dissonance. I am the flaw in the perfect chord.

You would find me in the darkest quietude between the pillars, suspended in the negative space where the sound waves cancel each other out. I am not massive like the Resonants; I am small, low to the ground, and deliberately, violently soft.

In this world of iron-hard wood and calcified vine, I am a blob of gelatinous, bioluminescent silence—a creature of heavy, water-laden flesh that shudders at every thrum of the city. I am a remnant of the old world, a biological anomaly that the "fauna parameter" failed to delete, evolved to hide in the acoustic shadows.

I have no shell, no exoskeleton. My skin is permeable and damp, craving the humidity that this arid desert has denied for eons. I secrete a thick, viscous mucus that smells of stagnant water and wet moss, a pungent, living stink that stands out in the sterile air of dry cinnamon. To the Resonants, I am not a predator; I am a patch of rot, a damp stain on the flawless instrument.

I move by gliding over the compacted pavement of fossilized spores, leaving a slick, wet trail behind me that instantly begins to calcify in the dry air, turning to white chalk. I am slow, slower even than the stone-moving giants, but I am patient.

Where the Resonants are the amplifiers, the tuners, the shouters into the void, I am the absorber. My body is dense and acoustic-deadening; I drink the vibration. The endless chord that sustains them is a cacophony of torture to me, a roaring feedback loop that vibrates my very bones. To survive, I must steal the sound.

I haunt the underbellies of the suspension bridges. I cling to the underside of the calcified vines where the tension is highest, wrapping my soft, malleable body around the vibrating cables. I squeeze. I constrict. I press my flesh against the singing wood until the vibration transfers into me, dampening the resonance, turning the kinetic energy of the song into heat that I use to sustain my wet, internal metabolism.

When I feed, I create a pocket of absolute silence. In a world defined by sound, this is my only defense.

The Resonants know me not as a living thing, but as a "Dead Spot." They perceive me through their root-network tremors as a place where the signal suddenly vanishes, a void within the Void. To them, I am a sickness in the wood. Sometimes, a great caretaker will detach itself from its pillar and come lumbering through the dark, following the trail of chalk-dust and the scent of water, seeking to silence the dissonance.

I cannot fight them. Their wooden fists are hammers that could pulp me in an instant. Instead, I wait. When they reach for me, I release my grip on the bridge and drop.

I fall into the kilometer-deep darkness, plummeting away from the light I cannot see, down toward the floor that is miles below. I tuck myself into a ball, a heavy drop of water, and let the air rush past me. I land on the hardpan with a wet *splat*, the impact absorbed by my gelatinous form. Then, I burrow. I dig into the dry, compacted dust, coating myself in the powder, disguising my scent of wet earth with the smell of dry cinnamon. I become a stone, a silent lump beneath the floor, waiting for the tremors of the hunter to fade.

I am the ghost of dampness in the house of dry wood. I am the pause in the music. I am the thing that waits for the instrument to finally crack, for the strings to snap, for the singers to lose their breath, so that I may finally drink the silence that remains.
