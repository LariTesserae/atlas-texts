I would not be on the floor. The floor is for the frantic, for the Dashers and their desperate sprints, for the water that falls and flees. Nor would I be in the deep dark below, buried in the sludge with the Leviathan. That existence is too vast, too removed from the delicate architecture of the heat.

I would dwell in the "Density."

I would find myself wedged deep within the thickets of pipe where the rating hits 2.4, a vertical labyrinth where the insulation crumbles into a white, carcinogenic snow. Here, the heat is not just a weight; it is a solid presence. The air is so squeezed between the conduits that it barely moves, stagnant and super-heated, tasting of copper and ancient dust.

In this claustrophobic squeeze, I would be a **Suspension-Gardener**.

I would not be a creature of speed like the Dasher, nor a hunter like the Stalker. I would be something that has evolved to embody the machine’s patience. My form would be ambiguous, a mirage of bio-mimicry. To the casual glance—and there are no eyes here to glance casually—I would look like a calcified valve joint, a irregularity in the pipe-work, a tumor of rust and mineral deposits.

My body would be flattened and segmented, capable of sliding through spaces that seem narrower than my own width. My limbs would be long, multi-jointed spindles, ending not in claws, but in sensitive, frond-like tendrils made of the same dense, heat-absorbing material as the Rust-Grubbers. I would not have a distinct head; my sensory organs would be distributed along my spine, a line of neural pores that taste the vibration of the fluid rushing through the pipes on which I cling.

I would be an omnivore of the infinitesimal.

While the Dashers fight for the falling droplets, I would tend to the micro-climate of the pipes. I would feed on the crust of the insulation, scraping away the asbestos-like fibers that crumble and threaten to clog the flow of heat. I would consume the slow-growing oxidation that bridges the gap between two stationary pipes, keeping the mechanism from seizing together. In the crevices where the condensation lingers longest, I would cultivate the velvety grey mold, farming it like a silent rancher, pruning it to encourage new growth that feeds the Grazers, which in turn feed the Stalkers.

I would be the stabilizer in the high wire.

Because I live in the Density, I feel the "glacial dance" of the pistons long before it is visible. My tendrils detect the microscopic expansion of the metal as the temperature rises by a fraction of a degree. When the machine prepares to shift—when the pressure builds to the point of movement—I would tighten my grip, becoming a rigid part of the structure, letting the iron move around me. I would be the flexible joint in a rigid world.

I would communicate not through sound, but through touch. When a Dasher flashes through the gloom below, I might send a shiver through the pipe I am clutching, a seismic telegraph that warns others in the thicket that the "tick" is coming. I would be the nervous system of the vertical spaces, the silent librarian of tension.

I would live my life in a state of profound, meditative suspension. There is no day or night here, only the endless purple bruise of thermal luminescence. I would hang there for decades, motionless as a rivet, my metabolism slowed to match the grinding of the gears, digesting the dust of the dead world, waiting for the next inch of movement, content to be the only thing that holds the chaos together.
