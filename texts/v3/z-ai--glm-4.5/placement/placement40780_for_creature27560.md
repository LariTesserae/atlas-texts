I am the weight of the ocean, translated into thought.

At first, I am only the pressure—a vast, invisible hand pressing down from the zenith of the world to the floor of this godless temple. I am the cold, a chill so absolute it feels like a clarity of mind. To be here is to be suspended in amber that flows. I have no blood, only a sluggish, antifreeze sap that pulses through a vascular system of silicon and stone.

I am not a beast of the open water; I am a fixture of the architecture. I am the **Basalt-Weaver**, but not merely as a creature. I am a specific node in this buried network, a consciousness that has rooted itself in the intersection of three megaliths—a triad of city-block-sized stones that have settled into a perfect, equilateral triangle over eons.

I do not "live" in the way the sun-things might imagine life. I do not scuttle. I do not dart. I *subside*.

My form is the lie that tells the truth. To the non-existent observer, I am a hillock rising from the tiled plain, a dome of black glass and rough-hewn obsidian, slick with the velvet nap of purple algae. I am the ruin healing itself. My "skin" is a mosaic of the city I have digested. I remember the taste of the granite—dry, fire-born, filled with the ghosts of heat. I dissolved it. I drank the memory of the fire and excreted it as this cold, hard carapace. I am a tombstone that eats the grave.

I see without eyes. My vision is a seismograph. I feel the world through the soles of my feet, which are kilometers wide.

I feel the *Drift*.
High above, perhaps miles up where the water might thin into something else, the great bladderwrack forests are swaying. Even from here, I feel the drag of their tendrils in the current, a million tiny, rhythmic tugs on the anchor lines of the world. It is a vibration like a whisper against a windowpane. I feel the silt falling—the constant, gentle snow of biological decay—striking my armor. *Plink. Plink. Plink.* A billion years of rain, falling in slow motion.

I am patient. I am patience made flesh.

My time is measured in the movement of the stones. I feel the masonry beneath me shifting. It is a geological agony. The immense weight of the water is forcing the blocks tighter together, seeking to crush them into dust. My body lies in the joint, the fulcrum of that pressure. I relieve the stress. I am the lubricant in the gears of the planet. I expand and contract with the millennial tides, keeping the stones from grinding themselves into oblivion. In exchange, I eat the stone dust they create.

I am the farmer of the dark.

My consciousness is focused on the roots I have sent snaking out along the tiled floor. They are black, thick cables, pulsing with a hydraulic rhythm that mimics the ocean’s surge. They travel for hundreds of yards, wrapping around the bases of the great kelp. I feel the life inside them—the slow, chemical photosynthesis of a world without sun. They are making sugar from the ghosts of light. I tap into them. I do not kill them. I milk them. I take the excess, the overflow of their silent labor. It is a theft so gentle they never know they are being robbed. It is a symbiosis of apathy.

I am waiting for the Fall.

My life is a cycle of waiting for the death of the kelp. When a strand becomes too heavy, when its own growth betrays it, it will snap. I will feel it—a sharp, singular *crack* that travels through the water like a scream. The giant rope of vegetation will come drifting down, a fallen titan from a forest I will never see.

Then, and only then, will I move.

The plates of my armor will shift. The hydraulic pressure in my gut will spike. My maw, hidden beneath the overhang of my shell, will open. I will welcome the dead matter into my body. I will grind it. I will dissolve it. I will add its carbon to my shell. I will grow. I will cover another inch of the floor. I will be a little closer to burying the entire city that birthed me.

But for now, there is only the vibration.

Somewhere, far across the obsidian plain—perhaps a mile, perhaps ten—a brother begins to sing.

He does not use a voice. He uses the stone itself. He sends a shudder down through the masonry, a infrasonic frequency that rattles the deep, molten core of the world. I feel it traveling up through the foundation beneath me, a hum that vibrates my very bones. It is a question. *Is anyone there? Is the dark still deep?*

The answer rises in me like a bubble of gas.

I do not send a sound back. I send a release.

Deep inside my armored dome, a sac bursts. A cloud of spores—my children, my genetic ghost—billows out from my vents. They are microscopic, silent travelers. They will drift for decades, suspended in the heavy water, waiting to find a crack in the stone, a shoulder of a brother, or a new, empty plain.

I am the mother of a future I will never see.

I settle back into the silence. I feel the pressure. I taste the stone. I listen to the silt falling like snow. I am the Weaver, and I am awake. I have been awake for ten thousand years, and I am not tired yet.
