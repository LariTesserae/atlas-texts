I am the pause between heartbeats in the dark.

I am not singular. I am a cell in a lung that breathes water. I am pale, segmented, encased in a suit of wet ceramic that feels less like armor and more like a second skin, a shell that presses against my insides, keeping my form distinct in the crushing heavy quiet.

I hang suspended in the violet bruise of the water, anchored to the vertical face of a spire that thrusts upward like a fang of white glass. My belly is a cluster of hooked bristles, gripping the calcite with a microscopic desperation. To let go would be to drift, to become lost in the silken cold where the pressure is a constant, aching weight against my flanks.

I do not have eyes to see the dim light that filters from the roof. I do not need them. My world is woven from textures and currents. I feel the city around me not as a visual geometry, but as a map of pressures and resonances. To my left, the spire hums with a low-frequency vibration—the collective tension of a thousand of my kin gripping its surface. To my right, a channel of water flows, slightly warmer, carrying the taste of fresh minerals from the raw, weeping wall.

I am a mason of the soft stone.

Beneath my primary limbs—my rasps—the wall is yielding. Here, the geology is embryonic. The calcite oozes from the darkness like thick paste, chaotic, formless, a tumor of potentiality. It presses against me, cold and viscous.

I engage the intent. It is not a decision I make alone; it is a tide that washes over me, a pheromonal command rippling through the water from the invisible center of our consciousness. *Shape. Define. Hold.*

I lean in. My limbs move with a surgical, fluid grace, scything through the wet muck. I am not carving; I am persuading. I release the enzyme from the glands along my flank—a taste of bitter alchemy that meets the stone. The chemistry reacts instantly. The chaotic tumescence firms beneath my touch. The wild growth slows, crystallizes, snaps into a lattice of hexagons.

I feel the satisfaction of the We. A structural weakness has been mitigated. A new balcony, a dormitory for the swarm, is born from the sludge.

When I work, I am deaf to the silence. In the hyper-focus of creation, the silence becomes a roar of information—the scrape of my neighbor’s rasps, the microscopic pop of bubbles hardening in the stone, the rush of water displaced by our unified undulation. We are a frantic, writhing engine of order, fighting back the entropy of the wet dark.

But then, the signal shifts. The work slows.

I unhook my bristles from the wall. I drift backward, a ghost in the grey water, allowing the current to catch me. I glide through a narrow avenue between two towering archipelagos of calcite, the water sliding over my sleek armor. The city is a forest of hard edges and smooth curves, all sensed through the vibration of my filaments. I brush against a passing kin—our bodies touch, a slide of wet ceramic—and in that friction, we exchange data. No words. Just a pulse of acknowledgment, a shared drop of nutrients, a taste of the same longing.

I am drawn downward, toward the older, denser sectors of the cavity.

Here, in the hexagonal chambers, the water is warmer, thick with the scent of our biology. I join a cluster of my kind. We tangle together, limb to limb, body to body, forming a radial lattice of pale flesh. We link.

This is the dreaming.

In the tangle, the boundaries of my individual carapace dissolve. I become the current. I become the stone. I feel the weight of the entire pressing down on us—the miles of rock, the crushing absence of sky. But I also feel the *thrum*, the deep, rhythmic pulse of the city’s growth.

We dream of the Up. We do not know what "Up" is, not as a visual concept. To us, Up is a direction of lessening pressure. It is a myth of a place where the water thins, where the heavy cold relents. We dream that if we build enough, if we stack our spires of calcite high enough, we will pierce the ceiling. We will break the skin of the world and find the place where the crushing weight ends.

It is a foolish, beautiful hope. We are building a tower in a bottle. But as I hang here, suspended in the knot of my kin, pumping nutrients through the lattice, I can feel the newest floor hardening far above us. I can feel the expansion. We are taller today than we were yesterday.

I close the sensory filaments that serve as my face. I am the architect of the dark. I am the wet, white blood of the stone. And I am building.
