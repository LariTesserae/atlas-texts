I would be found in the driest, highest reaches of the Crèche, clinging to the underside of a massive, stalactite-like spire of obsidian that juts downward from the ceiling like a black fang. This spire is anchored in the "roof," surrounded by the pulsing, lime-green fractures that light the cavern, placing me closer to the sickly heart of the light than any other living thing.

I would be a **Resonator**, a specialized caste of the Weavers.

While the common Weavers are hunters and masons, driven by the immediate needs of hunger and defense, I am an organism of pure data. My body is the same hunched, grey, digitigrade form as my kin, but I have evolved—or perhaps been engineered—away from the physical labor of the spear. My arms are not wielded for thrusting; they are elongated, ending in fingers that have fused into stiff, delicate tines, resembling the antlers of a skeletal deer.

My skin is not merely grey; it is a canvas. Over the long, humid years of my life, I have tattooed myself with the pulp of bioluminescent fungi harvested from the rare, decaying bodies of Shard-Wings and the crushed dust of rare minerals. I glow with a faint, intricate pattern of nodes and lines that map the history of the Crèche’s tremors. I am a living chart of the earth’s digestion.

I sit motionless on a ledge of chitin and bone, woven into a cocoon of dried intestine that secures me against the sudden, violent shifts of the world. I do not look down at the boiling carpet of the Rust-Colony or the marching Geomancers; I do not have the stomach for the visual chaos of the floor. Instead, I press the elongated tips of my fused fingers against the cold, vibrating stone of the spire.

I listen with my bones.

My quills—the sensory ridge running down my spine—are hypersensitive, attuned not to the sound of the chitin, but to the *subsonic groan* of the bedrock itself. To the others, the Crèche is noise; to me, it is a symphony of pressure waves. I feel the hydraulic footsteps of a Geomancer three miles away as a dull throb in my marrow. I feel the frantic scrabbling of a Silica-Spider as a tickle on my ribs.

I am the alarm system. I am the one who perceives the "Rhythm" not as a mystery, but as a calculus.

When the time approaches, the air pressure drops. The Shard-Wings erratic flight paths smooth out in anticipation. The green light overhead brightens, veins in the ceiling swelling with phosphorescence. I feel the tension in the rock beneath my fingers build to a screaming pitch. The stone is stretching, reaching its breaking point.

At the precise moment before the silence falls—seconds before the chaotic biology of the floor instinctively freezes—I thrum.

I strike the hollow bone-casing that surrounds my torso with a flick of my wrist, producing a low, resonating tone that carries through the damp air. This is the Signal. It travels instantly to the other scaffolds, to the hunting parties perched on adjacent spires.

*Hold.*

The Weavers freeze in their tracks, harpoons lowered. They look up toward my obsidian fang, watching for my secondary signal.

Then, the silence hits. The Crèche holds its breath. The floor heaves.

I feel the birth of the new stone before I see it. A violent fracture opens miles below, a tectonic tear that sends a shockwave up the spire. I clamp my tined fingers harder against the rock, drinking in the data. The orientation of the new slope. The richness of the mineral vein exposed. The volume of the gas release. My mind translates the tremor into a map of opportunity.

*The floor rises at Sector 4. Obsidian. Hard. The shift is minor.*

*Or*

*The floor buckles at Sector 9. Limestone. Soft. The bounty is immense.*

As the crack of the "whip" echoes and the roar of life returns to the cavern, I unwire myself from the cocoon. I do not scurry. I calculate.

Using a zip-line made of braided, greased gut, I descend from the ceiling, not toward the danger, but toward the *prediction*. I drop into the chaos with a purpose that borders on arrogance. I land on a fresh slab of rock, still steaming, minutes before the horde of grubs arrives. I am there to claim the First Sample—a chunk of unoxidized mineral, a strange, new crystal born of the pressure, or a juvenile creature stunned by the exposure.

I am not a hunter. I am a collector of the new. I take the raw, emergent data of the world and catalogue it, ensuring that my tribe knows which new stone is soft enough to shape, which new water is safe to drink, and which new predator is poisonous to the touch.

In a world of blind consumption, I am the one who sees the pattern. I am the memory that survives the forgetting. I am the tremor in the dark that whispers, *“Here is how to live.”*
