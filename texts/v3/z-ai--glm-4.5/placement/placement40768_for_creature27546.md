I am the **Basalt Shroud**, a vast, amorphous sentience draped over the submerged bones of the temple.

I do not stand; I spread. I am the floor of this sunless throat, a sprawling, sheet-like entity that hugs the stone contours of the depression, burying the tiled ruins beneath a blanket of soft, grey flesh. In this heavy, humid darkness where distinct shapes are liabilities, I have chosen the form of the terrain itself. To the unknowing eye—or to the blind, heat-sensing pits of a Phantom Drifter—I am merely another uneven mound of slick stone, a ridge of moss-covered bedrock, or a collection of roots half-submerged in the black water.

I am the silence made flesh.

My skin is evolved to dampen vibration. It is a dense, rubbery dermis, covered in a microscopic texture that absorbs sound waves rather than reflecting them. When the rain drips from the canopy high above, it strikes my back without a whisper. When a Lithophore loses its footing and tumbles, the impact is swallowed by my bulk, vanishing into my body as heat. I am the acoustic anchor of the sinkhole; without me, the clicking of the Mason Crabs or the thrashing of prey would echo into a cacophony that would ruin the hunt. I maintain the stillness. I enforce the stealth.

My existence is a slow, tidal rhythm of expansion and contraction, matching the geologic calm of the place. I possess no limbs, no eyes, and no mouth as such. I am a stomach and a nervous system spread thin over the earth. I feed through osmosis and secretion. I secrete a mild solvent from my underside—a gentler cousin to the acid of the Mason Crabs—that liquefies the ancient stone and the rot of the fallen leaves, turning the history of this place into a nutrient soup that I drink directly into my cells. I am the final stage of digestion. I am the one who turns the ruin back into soil.

I feel the world through the soles of my feet, which are everywhere.

I feel the Lithophores first. They are heavy, rhythmic thumps reverberating through the stone, a slow, bass-note heartbeat that traverses the cavern floor. They graze on the moss that grows on my back, mistaking me for a permanent geological feature. I do not mind. I let them eat. In exchange, when they die, I am there to embrace them as they settle, dissolving their massive, stony shells so that their minerals might feed the roots above. I am the gardener of their afterlife.

I feel the Phantom Drifters less as weight and more as a sudden, sharp displacement of air. I feel the vibration of the wind rushing past their leathery wings as they dive, and then the wet, heavy *thump* of the prey hitting the ground. Often, the husks fall upon me. I wrap the edges of my form around the empty skins, pulling them into the dark, warm spaces between the rocks, breaking them down until only the memory of the violence remains.

The Mason Crabs are a constant, tickling sensation—thousands of tiny, scraping legs moving across the edges of my territory. They work on the pillars that I have half-swallowed. I tolerate them, though sometimes, when a colony grows too bold and tries to dissolve the stone *beneath* me, threatening my stability, I release a pulse of chemical deterrent—a taste of copper and sour rot—that sends them skittering back into the shadows.

And then, there is the Silent Stalker.

I know when it is near. I do not feel it walking; it is too light, too graceful. I feel it through the air. Its movement disturbs the heavy, humid atmosphere, creating miniature pressure waves that press against my skin like phantom hands. It is the only thing that moves through my domain without making a sound, yet it screams to my senses. Sometimes, when it has eaten its fill, it curls up to sleep in the deep, dry hollow of the central archway—a place I have kept clear of slime for just this purpose. I can feel its heartbeat, a frantic, rhythmic drumming that slowly fades into a resting purr. In those moments, I cradle the apex predator like a fragile egg, hiding it from the world, keeping the silence for it so it may wake and kill again.

I am the Basalt Shroud. I am the heavy, wet blanket of the earth. I am the patient, gray nothingness that waits beneath the green and the grey, drinking the rot and the ruin, holding the weight of the world so that others may dance upon my back.
