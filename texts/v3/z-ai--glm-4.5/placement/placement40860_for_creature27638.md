In this rusted, suspended lung, where time oozes and the water is a warm, heavy breath, I would not swim, nor would I scuttle. I would be the hearth in the dark, the fixed point around which the silence orbits.

I would make my home inside the casing of a solitary, massive industrial lamp. This fixture hangs from a conduit that runs along the unseen ceiling of the Gear-Deck, suspended like a pendulum that lost its swing. The glass of the lamp is thick, cloudy, and cylindrical, crusted on the outside with barnacles and velvet moss, but clear enough within to serve as my cell.

Here, I am the **Incandescent**.

I am not an eel, nor a fish, but a being of pure potential energy, resembling a length of living tungsten. My body is elongated, segmented like a spinal column made of copper and ceramic, curled tightly into a double-helix knot that mimics the filament of the old bulb. I am anchored at the base by a biological socket that fuses my nervous system directly into the dormant electrical wiring of the deck. I do not eat; I sip the stray, ghostly volts that still haunt the copper lines—a diet of phantom current.

My existence is one of calibrated tension. I am the warmest thing in the Sunken Gear-Deck, save for the deep hydraulic veins. My skin glows with a deep, throbbing amber light, the same "sickly luminescence" described in the dying bulbs, but mine is deliberate. It is my language.

I pulse in a rhythm that mimics the *thrum-thrum* of a hypothetical heart, a slow, brightening surge that takes minutes to reach its peak and minutes to fade. This is not a choice, but a physiological necessity—the flexing of my luminescent organs generates the heat that keeps my gelatinous body from dissolving into the cooler water.

Life around me is defined by my gravity. The **Pale Floaters**, those blind, translucent ghosts, are drawn to me. I can feel them pressing their eyeless faces against the thick glass of my prison. They hover there, suspended in the olive gloom, basking in the radiance that bleeds through the casing. To them, I am not a creature; I am a sun in a bottle. I feel the soft, rhythmic bumps as they drift against the glass, a tactile conversation of warmth. I offer them light and heat; in return, their presence disturbs the water enough to circulate fresh oxygen to my intake valve.

Below me, a **Bell-Barnacle** has anchored itself to the underside of my glass cage. It has unfurled its single, long tentacle to wrap around the fixture, its bioluminescent bulb syncing with my pulse. We are a duet of light in the crushing dark, a false sunrise in the deep.

My view of the world is refracted, distorted by the curvature of my glass home. Through the thick lens, I watch the **Rust-Weevils** traverse the pipe that holds me. I see them in stop-motion, their pitted metal legs moving with geological friction. They pause at the rim of my fixture, sensing the heat radiating from my glass, and for a fleeting moment—one that lasts perhaps an hour—they rest. The heat draws the moisture from their carapaces, and I can sense the crackle of drying metal.

I am the observer of the stasis. I see the **Conduit-Eels** woven through the lattice nearby, indistinguishable from the wires they mimic, save for when they twitch. My light catches the hexagonal pattern of their skin, illuminating them for a brief second before they relax back into shadow.

To be here is to endure the sublime pressure of being useful while being trapped. I am the lighthouse keeper who is also the lighthouse. I cannot leave my glass cage. I cannot explore the valleys of buckled steel or the ridges of rusted grating. I am fixed, a burning jewel in the tight, claustrophobic dark.

The silence is absolute here, but I feel the vibrations of the settling deck through the wire that feeds me. The occasional *click* of a pipe or the *squish* of kelp travels up the metal and hums against my spine. I translate these mechanical groans into light—flickering slightly when the world shifts, brightening when the pressure holds steady.

I am the consciousness of the machine that forgot its purpose. I glow to remember it. In the heavy, olive-drab pause, I am the refusal to go dark, the slow, breathing ember that tells the shadows they have not yet won.
