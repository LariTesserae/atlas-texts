I find myself suspended in the heavy, cold breath of the hollow, standing upon the **Swaying Bridge of Spines**.

I am one of the **Silent**, a "Damp Thing," but my role is distinct from the frantic hackers. I am a **Weaver of the Tethers**.

My station is high above the shifting tide of isopods, suspended between two of the fern-stalk towers. The bridge beneath my feet is not of timber, but of living vine, braided and interwoven with the cured sinew of great, blind cave-beasts that once swam in the air above. It shifts with the rhythm of the breathing walls, a gentle, nauseous roll that would send a surface-dweller into vomiting fits. To me, it is the cradle of my existence.

I am gaunt, my physiology stretched by the gravity of this place and the scarcity of solid footing. My skin is the translucent, bruised-blue of a drowned spirit, slick with a layer of oil that protects me from the constant, biting weeping of the ceiling. I am naked, save for a loincloth of decaying silk that does little to warm me, but warmth is a luxury we traded for vigilance long ago.

I do not carry an adze. My hands are my tools. My fingers are long, tapered, and unnaturally sensitive, capable of detecting the microscopic vibration of a root-tip seeking purchase inside the wood of a tower frame. Braided into my long, slicked-back hair is the living moss; it pulses with a faint, sickly blue light, casting my shadow long and wavering against the dark, "deep water" twilight of the chamber.

Right now, I am kneeling on the slick vine of the bridge. My eyes, vast black pools that drink in what little light exists, are fixed on the knot where the bridge meets the North Spire.

I feel the bridge talking to me. Through the soles of my feet, I sense a tremor—not the rhythmic contraction of the walls, but a sharp, jagged frequency. It is the "Total" plant mass, testing the perimeter. A tendril has found the anchor point. It is prying at the lashings, seeking to unravel the knot that keeps the walkway aloft.

If I spoke, I would scream a warning, but my throat is a vestigial passage, unused and closed tight. Instead, I press my palm flat against the wet, fibrous rope of the bridge. I send a vibration down the line—a sharp, staccato tap that travels through the sinew and into the timber of the tower.

*Thrum. Thrum. Pause. Thrum.*

Inside the North Spire, I see a figure step out onto the platform. They are indistinct, a ghost in the gloom, illuminated only by the faint glow of their own hair-moss. They feel my signal through the floorboards. They understand: *The anchor is compromised.*

They raise the stone adze.

From my vantage point on the swaying bridge, I watch as the root erupts. It comes with a wet, popping sound, a pale, fleshy spear thrusting up from the writhing substrate below, curling eagerly around the timber base. It is beautiful in its hunger. The figure strikes. The stone blade bites deep into the wet wood and vein. The sap bleeds out, glowing a fierce, electric blue that illuminates the scene like a flash of lightning.

Below us, the carpet of isopods ripples. They sense the fluid, the energy. The silent tide flows toward the tower, a thousand plate-sized bodies moving with fluid grace, ready to drink the bleeding plant.

I do not watch them for long. My attention returns to the knot. The root’s intrusion has loosened the sinew. I must re-tie it before the bridge pulls away. I lean out over the abyss, the cold air burning my lungs, the scent of crushed chlorophyll thick in my nose. I am light, boneless in my movement, trusting the friction of my skin against the wet vine.

I am the pause in the growth. I am the tension that holds the chaos at bay. I am the Weaver, keeping the city from drifting into the dark.
