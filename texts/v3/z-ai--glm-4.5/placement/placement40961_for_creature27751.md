I would be the Anomaly. The Other.

In this world of saturated grey stone and biology driven by the roar, I would be the Wood. Not the living, growing timber of a forest that does not exist here, but the scavenged, dead remnants of the "elsewhere"—a heavy, rune-carved staff, borne in the calloused grip of a master mason.

I would find myself high in the vertical warren, perched on a slick ledge of basalt where the mud has been stamped into a crude, temporary platform. The air here is thick enough to taste; it feels heavy against my grain, a constant, physical pressure trying to force its way into my fibers. I am dry—or I was, once. Now, I am fighting a losing war against the dampness. The moisture seeps into me, softening my ancient resolve, blunting the sharp corners of the runes carved into my side. I am rotting, slowly, in the warm, wet twilight, turning into the very mush that coats the feet of the city.

My existence is a constant vibration. I do not have ears, but I feel the world through the touch of the hand that holds me and the stone I strike.

I am the rhythm. The city is a wall of chaotic noise, a tsunami of screaming amphibians and shouting throats, but I provide the floor. The mason who carries me is a brute of a creature, a barrel-chested giant with skin like hot slate and a neck swollen by the leather strap of his larynx. He does not speak; he broadcasts. But when he works, he silences his roar to focus his hydraulic strength. He lifts me high—a heavy, dark weight of forgotten forests—and brings me down.

*Thud.*

The shock travels up my length, jarring my core. He strikes the fresh mud-mortar, packing it tight between the rough-hewn black stones. *Thud.* *Thud.* *Thud.* I am the metronome in the cacophony, the steady beat that tries to organize the madness into structure. Through my roots, now dead, I feel the geology beneath us. It is raw, jagged, and embryonic, shuddering under the weight of the city.

I am also a perch. The amphibians—the dog-sized, slick-skinned horrors that infest this place—see me as a vantage point. One of them clings to my midsection now, its wet, bulbous eyes rotating independently. It is cold, a stark contrast to the feverish heat of the mason’s hand. It grips me with sticky pads, adding its weight to the labor. The mason does not mind. In a moment of stillness, he might reach down, snap its neck with a casual twist, and eat it one-handed while continuing to pack the wall, but for now, we are a triad: the Man, the Tool, and the Beast.

My perspective is of the eternal Now. I do not remember the sun I grew under, only a ghostly sensation of a light that was warm and dry, unlike this dim, oppressive grey-gold haze. I do not dream of the wind that once rustled my leaves, though I shiver at the phantom memory of it in this stagnant, windless air.

I am the only thing here that possesses the architecture of silence. Stone is loud; it rings when struck. Mud is wet; it sucks and splatters. Flesh is noisy; it screams and croaks. But I am wood. I absorb. When the mason sets me down to rest, leaning against a dripping spire of black rock, I drink in the residual tremors of the city. I hold the echo of a thousand shouts and the vibration of a million screams within my cellular memory, trapping them where they cannot be heard.

I am a dying thing in a world that refuses to change. The stone is eternal, the slime is reborn instantly, and the humans maintain their feverish equilibrium. But I am finite. With every strike, I splinter. With every hour, the rot softens my heart. I am the import from elsewhere, slowly surrendering to the wet, noisy entropy of the grey hell. I am the staff, silent and screaming, marking the time in a place where time stands still.
