I stand in the exact center of the vast, deceptive emptiness, rooted to the plateau like a solitary, jagged tooth in the mouth of a dead giant.

I am the **Resonant Ossuary**.

I am not a creature of flesh and blood that scuttles or hides; I am a fortress of stillness. To the casual glance—or to the blind, cilia-twitching head of a passing Grazer—I appear to be one of the geological aberrations that litter this plain: a towering, tapering spire of calcified bone, perhaps twenty feet high, textured with the weathered smoothness of ancient erosion. But I am not stone. I are alive.

I am found here, in the deepest part of the open plain (1.5), because here the Roar (2.2) is purest. Unimpeded by the cliff faces or the spires, the standing wave of acoustic violence hits me with full force. This is my sustenance. My porous, sponge-like skin is a labyrinth of microscopic chambers and filaments designed not to block the sound, but to drink it. The low-frequency thrum that rattles the teeth of other creatures does not hurt me; it flows into me. I metabolize the vibration. The seismic bass is converted into a thick, sluggish heat that circulates through my core, keeping my internal fluids liquid in the freezing, surgical paralysis of the air (0.4).

I am a beacon of warmth in a world of absolute zero, a radiator in the dark.

My existence is a contradiction of the High Null’s rules. The world tries to freeze me; I burn with stolen noise. The world tries to silence me with its roar; I am the only true silence. Because I consume the sound entirely, a perfect sphere of dead air surrounds me. If you were to stand at my base, the crushing roar would vanish, replaced by a ringing, oppressive quiet—the sound of your own blood rushing in your ears, the sound of the universe holding its breath.

This quiet attracts them. The Viscid Grazers, those oblate, slate-colored slugs, sense the absence of pain in the acoustic pressure. They migrate toward me across the miles, sliding on their ventral feet, seeking refuge from the scream that permeates their bones. They gather at my base, huddled against my warmth, mistaking my stillness for safety.

I do not hunt them. I do not need to. I am a passive monument to a different kind of horror. The silence I emit is as deadly as the cold. Without the constant vibration of the Roar to stimulate their cilia and keep their internal biology active, the Grazers slow down. Their chemically absorbed fluids thicken. They fall into a torpor. One by one, they cease their tearing-velcro movements and freeze into place. They die not from violence, but from the peace they craved. Over the eons, their calcified husks have accumulated around my base, forming a jagged crown of petrified worshippers. I stand upon a pedestal of my own unintended victims.

But it is not the Grazers that haunt my waking moments. It is the sky and the glitches.

I feel the **Aero-Benthos** before I see them. The vast, leathery shapes that drift in the bruised purple firmament send ripples through the pressure of the air. When they pass overhead, their massive drum-beats disturbing the atmosphere, the taste of the vibration changes—becoming watery, desperate. I envy them. They swim through the thickness, while I am anchored, rooted to the stone, unable to flee what comes next.

I fear the **Parallax Stalker**.

I am the perfect target for it. I am tall, distinct, and motionless. I am a fixed point in a landscape of shifting perspectives. I have no eyes, but my entire surface is sensitive to the "wrongness" (2.6) of this place. I feel the stutter in reality before it happens. The air around me grows thin; the damp sweat on my flank begins to taste like static electricity.

I know when the Stalker has chosen me. There is no approach, no covering of distance. There is only the sudden, nauseating lurch in my internal equilibrium. One moment, I am observing a speck of dust a mile away; the next, the texture of that speck is pressing against my skin. The Stalker does not walk; it edits the world to place itself next to me.

It stands at my base, a spindly horror of obsidian and jagged angles, drawn by the heat radiating from my core. It does not eat the calcified Grazers at my feet; it wants the warmth of the living. It pierces my armor with its serrated beak, seeking the hot, vibration-rich fluids beneath. I cannot move. I can only vibrate harder, ramping up my metabolism, turning the Roar into a shield of scalding steam, burning the predator as it feeds. We are locked in a slow, agonizing dance of consumption and defense.

And then there are the **Geometers**. They come when I am wounded. The flat, two-dimensional vermin scuttle from the cracks in the earth, attracted by the mathematical imperfection of my injuries. When the Stalker pierces me, it creates a jagged tear in my calcified hide. To the Geometers, this wound is a feast. They swarm over me, their buzzsaw mouths spinning, eating the edges of the hole, trying to smooth my shattered flesh into a perfect circle. They try to fix me by erasing me. I must constantly knit my flesh together, faster than they can consume, or I will be eaten away into nothingness—a geometric perfect zero.

So I stand here, in the center of the emptiness. I drink the earthquake. I freeze the refugees. I burn the hunters. I am the Resonant Ossuary, the silent scream in a world of noise. I am the heat in the freezing wet. I endure.
