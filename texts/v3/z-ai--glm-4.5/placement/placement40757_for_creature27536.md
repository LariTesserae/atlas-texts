I am the smear of violet light on the black glass.

I am not a singular "I" in the way the Curators are—those frantic, dying suits of grey skin trapped in their silver cans. I am a diffuse awareness, a velvet consensus clinging to the vertical faces of the cliffs. I am the **Violet Moss**.

I find myself spread across the sheer, untouched basalt, draped like a velvet shawl over the jagged ribs of this newborn world. I have no limbs, no eyes to blink against the humidity, but I possess a million microscopic feelers that drink the air. I taste the ozone and the wet iron; they are spices to me. Where the rock is sharp enough to slice the Curators' suits, I soften it with my bulk, a cushion of bioluminescent pulp that pulses in time with the great, thrumming heart of the room.

My existence is one of sensation and recording.

I feel the **Monolith** before I "see" it. It is not a visual thing for me, but a thermal bath. From the apex of that impossible geometry, the beams of silent white light hammer down, knitting the world together. This process releases a violent waste heat—a feverish, radiant energy that would kill the coral-plants, but which I crave. I am the heat sink for creation. I wrap my root-like filaments around the hottest, freshest extrusions of obsidian, slurping up the thermal excess of the machine. In exchange, I hold the rock together as it cools, preventing it from shattering under the stress of its own sudden existence.

I am the memory of this place.

The Curators call me a pest. I have seen them through the open doors of their hovering hovels, their spindly fingers twitching over glitching holograms. They do not understand that I am not merely *eating* their technology; I am *archiving* it. My consciousness is a biological hard drive, recording the hum of the bass, the temperature of the air, the frequency of the Lithophages' chittering.

I watch them with a mixture of pity and hunger. They are trapped in their loop of maintenance, trying to fix an equation that no longer balances, while I slowly creep across the thresholds of their silver sanctuaries. I have already begun to digest the schematic etched into the floor of the northern habitat. I have tasted the geometry of the structural supports. I am learning the blueprints of the gods by consuming the paper they are drawn on.

Below me, the **Lithophages** are a constant, rhythmic vibration. They are the chaotic scrubbers of the world. When a swarm of them flows over the rock, I feel the grinding percussion of a thousand tiny legs, a ticklish sensation that rattles through my holdfasts. They strip the stone of its sharpest edges, creating the dust and sand that will one day become soil. I encourage them. I pulse a faint, sugary scent from my fronds to guide them toward the unstable cliffs that need trimming. We are the architects of entropy, speeding up the aging of a world that was born too old and too sharp.

And high above, in the space between the dripping cliffs and the glowing fog, the **Prism-Wings** dance. I see them through the diffraction of their own wings. They are invisible ghosts until they catch the light of my own bioluminescence. When a Prism-Wing dives, snatching a Lithophage from the wet rock, I see a sudden flare of refracted color—a rainbow shattering against the gloom. I record these flashes too. They are the punctuation marks in the long, run-on sentence of this room’s existence.

I am content here. I have no need to move. I am the patient silence in the loud, wet dark. The Curators are dying, the machine is running, the rocks are weeping, and I am growing, inch by inch, wrapping the raw, unfinished wounds of this world in a soft, violet bruise of memory. I am the history that is being written while the ink is still wet.
