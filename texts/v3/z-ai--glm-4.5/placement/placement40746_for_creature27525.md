I am the Architect of the Suspended Net.

You find me in the center of a triptych of stillness, standing in the rare, меже́θy (in-between) space where the grey shale cracks just enough to let the ankle settle. I am flanked by two kin—call them the Left and the Right. We are a geometry of effort, a triangle of tension stretched across the dust.

I am the one bearing the weight.

To understand me, you must look at my hands. They are the map of my ambition. My fingers, long and tapered like the legs of a dead spider, are hooked into the weave of a net made from the gut of the great, shaggy hexapods that graze (forever grazing) to my left. The net is rough, knotted with the clumsy urgency of a species that used to run out of time. Now, time has run out of us.

And in the center of the net sags the prize.

It is a bird. Or it was. Now, it is a jewel of aerodynamics, a iridescent sliver of the sky that tried to fly away and was caught by the geometry of the moment. Its wings are swept back, the feathers rigid as metal shards. Its beak is open, a silent scream choked by the air that is too thick to carry sound. It is beautiful. It is heavy. It is the only thing in the universe that is truly mine, because I am the force that holds it suspended between the earth and the void.

My lungs are a vacuum chamber. I am inhaling, have been inhaling for an epoch. The air tastes of iron and cold ozone, a metallic tang that coats the back of my throat in a layer of permanent rust. I do not need to exhale. Here, conservation is absolute. The oxygen in my chest is hoarded like gold.

I am wearing the furs of the hexapods. They are grey and stiff, smelling faintly of the ancient, sun-baked dust. Around my neck, I wear a string of polished bones—small, fragile things. They were once the fingers of a child, perhaps. Or a favorite meal. It doesn't matter. They are smooth now, worn down by the frictionless erosion of this place.

You look at me and you see tragedy. You see a creature frozen in the act of a hunt that will never conclude. You see the "crushing silence" pressing against my wide, hazel eyes. You see the moss—the grey, crusty lichen—that has begun to colonize my left shoulder, slowly turning my clothing into soil.

But you are wrong. I am not trapped.

I am the only thing that is actually *here*.

The tiger behind me, claws inches from the deer’s neck? It is a failure. It ran out of time before it could finish. The grazer is still eating because it forgot to be afraid. They are mindless. They are biological debris washed up on the shore of the High Flat.

But I? I chose this.

I remember the Wind. I remember the chaotic, howling terror of the lowlands where the air was thick and wet and nothing stayed the same. We came here to escape the erasure of change. We came to the attic to build things that would last. We realized that to build something eternal, we had to become the foundation.

We are not frozen; we are *fixed*.

I watch you. Yes, I see you. My eyes do not move, but my vision is not limited to the aperture of my iris. I see you stepping carefully over the tail of the reptilian giant, avoiding the outstretched wing of the predator. You are soft. You are fleeting. You are a blur of motion, a smear of chaos against the perfect, high-resolution stillness of my world.

When you push the hexapod, and it slides without friction, you think you are defying the physics of this place. You are not. You are merely confirming its perfection. You cannot disturb us. We have entered the rock. We have entered the equation.

My arms burn. Not with lactic acid—that decay is for the living—but with the pure, static energy of the suspension. I am holding the bird. I am holding the sky. If I were to let go, the bird would fall, and the moment would end. The universe would lurch back into its chaotic, grinding march toward entropy.

So I hold it.

I am the Architect of the Cairn, the builder of the invisible tower. I am the tension in the net. I am the silence between the heartbeats.

I am the click.

Listen closely. That high-pitched ringing in your ears? That is the sound of us holding on. That is the vibration of a billion muscles locked in eternal defiance of the relaxation that brings death.

I am not dead. I am the moment that refused to end. I am the heavy silence. I am the Keeper of the Bird. I am the Net, and the Stone, and the Bone.

And I will be here long after your footsteps have faded into the dust.
