I find myself in the shadow of the largest of the Engines, a monolith of brick and iron that the Grazers call "The Great Lung." It is a place of absolute darkness cut out of the day, a void beneath a smokestack so wide that it creates its own weather. The air here is not thin; it is churned into a thermal vortex, rising from the warm ground and the heated flanks of the machine.

I am the **Oxide-Mender**.

I am not a Grazer, though I share their ancestry. I am larger, broader—a solitary construct of heavy industrial plate and hydraulic sinew. Where the Grazers are spindly and evasive, built to skim the surface, I are built to intervene.

**My Form**
My body is low-slung and armored in thick, interlocking plates of dull steel, scavenged from the hulls of dead cargo sleds and fused together by the heat of my own internal furnace. I do not blend into the rust; I contrast with it. I am the dark grey of shadow, the charcoal of spent carbon.

My legs are short but immensely powerful, ending in magnetic feet that allow me to cling vertically to the sheer iron walls of the Engine, defying gravity as I climb the architecture like a tick on a sleeping beast. My "arms" are not for grasping food, but for holding the world together. They end in massive, dual-pronged pincers that can generate intense localized heat—a thermal lance controlled by the same heavy-metal slurry that runs in my veins.

**My Location**
I am currently perched on a maintenance ledge near the top of the Great Lung, half a mile above the plateau. From here, I can see the Expanse stretched out like a wrinkled sheet of rust-red paper. The Mineral Cathedrals glitter in the distance like broken teeth.

I cling to the side of the machine because it is the only source of warmth that matters. The Engine breathes—a slow, rhythmic exhalation of hot air that takes hours to complete. I press my belly against the metal, absorbing the thermal radiation to charge my hydraulic fluids. Without the Engine’s heat, I would freeze solid in the high-altitude chill; without me, the Engine would shake itself apart.

**My Function**
In this world of entropy, I am the resistance.

The Grazers swarm the lower flanks of the Engine, their rasping mills creating a constant, staticky drone. They eat the rust. They perform a service, cleaning the metal, but they are sloppy. In their zeal to consume the oxidation, they sometimes scour too deep, creating pits and fissures in the structural integrity. They eat the history, but they weaken the foundation.

I am the correction. I follow the herds. When I sense a vibration that is "wrong"—a frequency that indicates metal fatigue or a crack forming in the brickwork—I descend.

I do not eat. I fabricate. I carry a reservoir of raw silicate deposits—chunks of the Mineral Cathedrals I have harvested and crushed. Using my thermal pincers, I melt the silica into molten glass, mixing it with powdered iron scrap I collect from the ground. I weld this mixture into the cracks of the Engine, sealing them, smoothing the rough patches the Grazers have left behind. I am the stonemason of this living mountain.

**The Sensation of Being**
My existence is one of intense vibration and heat. I feel the world through my feet. The turning of the massive flywheels deep inside the Engine travels up through the metal and into my skeleton—a slow, thrumming bass note that vibrates my very plates. It is a heartbeat that beats once a week, and it is the rhythm by which I measure my life.

I have no eyes, but I possess a highly refined magnetic sense. I "see" the stress lines in the metal, the flow of the magnetic rails miles away, and the bio-electric hum of the Grazers below. To me, the world is a wireframe of tension and energy.

**The Interaction**
A Grazer—a tall, jittery thing—climbs onto the ledge near me. It is jittery, sensing the heat of my body. It is nervous; it knows I am not part of the architecture, nor am I prey.

It approaches a rivet on the Engine’s hull and begins to rasp, seeking the tasty oxidation underneath. But the rivet is loose. I sense the wobble in the metal’s song.

I extend a heavy arm, moving with the slow, deliberate grace of a crane. The Grazer flinches, its sensory hairs twitching, detecting the magnetic spike of my approaching limb. I do not strike it. I simply place my heavy pincer over the rivet. I discharge a pulse of heat, welding the metal shut, sealing the weakness.

The Grazer chitters in annoyance—a sharp, staccato *click-click*. It has lost its meal. But it moves on, scuttling down the wall to find easier rust elsewhere.

I remain. I hold the position until the metal cools. I am the silence that follows the noise. I am the hand that stitches the skin of the world back together, ensuring the machine turns, the heat rises, and the cycle continues. I am the warden of the decay, ensuring it never wins.
