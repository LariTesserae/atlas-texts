I am perched on the rusting spine of an old world, balanced on a cable thick as a mountain trunk yet frayed into individual wires that sing in the wind.

I find myself suspended in the middle distance, not on the ground and not quite in the sky, but on the **Iron Archway of Omen**. It is one of the largest bridges connecting two primary mesas, a structure that was once forged of steel and concrete but has since been half-swallowed by the living stone. I am standing on a maintenance gantry that hasn't known a human boot in millennia, the metal grate beneath my feet slick with the ever-present condensation.

**Who am I?**

I am one of the Bridge-Tenders, but to the others of my kind—the silent, grey stalkers that haunt the shadowy underbellies of the cliffs—I am simply *a current in the wire*. I have no name, only a function.

My form is elongated, an exaggeration of the verticality that rules this place. My limbs are spindly, segmented in three joints like the rusted pistons of the machines I tend. My skin is the color of wet clay, translucent and cool, absorbing the harsh radiation of the sun while secreting a thin, oily sheen that protects me from the biting, metallic wind.

I possess no eyes. To possess eyes would be to rely on a deception—the light here is too bright, the shadows too deep. Instead, my face is a smooth, featureless oval of pale grey, save for the vertical slit that serves as my nose and mouth. I see the world through my skin. I feel the infrared warmth of the stone baking in the sun, and I feel the cold, seeping shadow of the deep crevices. The world to me is a tapestry of temperature gradients and vibrations.

**What am I doing?**

I am waiting. I am knitting.

In my hands—multi-fingered, delicate things that end in tool-like blunt tips—I hold a long, sticky strand of organic filament. It is not twine. It is sinew harvested from a Ferric Kite that met its end against the cable earlier this morning. I felt the impact, a sudden, sharp spike of heat and force resonating through the iron of the bridge. I retrieved the body before the Oxid-Limbed Scuttlers could strip the calcium from its wings.

The wind here is a living thing. It is not a gentle breeze but a sliding, heavy thermal that tries to pry me from my perch. I am light, hollow-boned, filled with gas bladders to keep me buoyant in the thin air, so I must anchor myself. I hook my feet, which possess opposable, talon-like toes, into the rusted lattice of the gantry.

**The Experience of the Place**

The air tastes of copper and ozone. It coats the back of my throat, a tangy, electric flavor that hums with potential energy.

I am acutely aware of the "hunting silence." Above me, I sense the chaotic, shifting heat signatures of the Kite pack. They are riding the thermals, spiraling in a vast, silent gyre. They are hunting the Scuttlers currently swarming the western face of the butte. To my thermal sense, the rock face looks like a dark, cooling ocean, but the Scuttlers are thousands of tiny, bright orange sparks crawling over it. The Kites are pale, cold ghosts diving toward the sparks. It is a beautiful, deadly geometry.

I feel the bridge groan. It is a low-frequency sound that travels up through the metal of my perch and into the marrow of my bones, rattling my teeth. The pressure is dropping. The viscous rain is coming.

I look up—or rather, I turn my face-slit toward the sky. The clouds are snagging on the peaks, heavy and pregnant with static. The hair-fine cilia covering my scalp stand on end. The electricity is waking up.

**The Connection**

As the first drops of the oily, iridescent rain begin to fall, sizzling slightly as they hit the charged metal of the bridge, I return to my work. I tie the Kite's sinew into a fraying section of the main support cable. I am not just repairing a rope; I am grafting flesh into iron. The sinew will bond with the rust, creating a seal stronger than the original metal. The organic matter will feed the rust, the rust will harden the matter. It is the cycle of the Ferric Serras. We do not build; we synthesize.

I feel a pulse of electricity from the cooling tower on the adjacent mesa. The Conduit Eels are discharging. The machine is singing. It is a low, mournful note that vibrates through the soles of my feet. I hum back, a sub-sonic rumble in my chest that matches the frequency of the tower. We are synchronized, the machine and I. We are both ghosts in the gears, keeping the high, crowded room from collapsing.

I am the bridge, and the bridge is me. I am the tension that holds the world together. I am the wet, grey silence watching the hunters circle. I am here, at the jagged roof of the world, weaving the dead into the structure so the living may cross.
