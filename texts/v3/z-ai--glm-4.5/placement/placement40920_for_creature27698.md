I am not a creature of the open water, nor am I a hunter in the dark. I am something fixed, a point of resistance in a world that wants to grind everything down. To find me, you must look away from the mesmerizing, silver flash of the Vectors and ignore the distant, ghostly glide of the Leviathans. You must look closely at the eroding flanks of the great synthetic spires, where the dark stone curves into the gloom.

There, clinging to the vertical precipice of a monolith that has not seen a maker in eons, you would find me.

I am a **Tesselator**.

I do not possess the needle-thin elegance of the void-swimmers. My form is dense, heavy, and aggressively geometric. I resemble a living, polyhedral anchor—a jagged, irregular cluster of interlocking plates forged from the same calcified, synthetic material as the tower I cling to. My coloration is not the charcoal of the stone nor the silver of the fish, but a stark, bruised ochre, the color of rust and dried sealant. I am a scar upon the building, a deliberate interruption of the smooth erosion.

I have six limbs, but they are not legs in the traditional sense. They are telescoping pistons of muscle and bone, ending in complex, rotary claws that can spin and lock with the torque of industrial drills. These claws are not designed to pierce the stone, but to *merge* with it. I possess the unique ability—evolved over millennia of rubbing against the "Built"—to vibrate my carapace at a frequency that temporarily liquefies the molecular bonds of the synthetic surface. I sink into the wall, and when I stop vibrating, I am welded to the mountain, indistinguishable from the architecture itself.

In this world of chaotic flux, I am the stasis.

I am an architect of necessity, a biological response to the decay. While the Lith-Feeders slide across the stone, dissolving it into slurry, speeding the ruin, I exist to arrest it. I am the antagonist of entropy. I feed on the suspended precipitate, the "dust" of the dying world that the Vectors chase, but I do not consume it for energy alone. I process it within a specialized internal furnace, hyper-pressurizing the mineral dust into a molten, adhesive paste.

This is my purpose. I patrol the hairline cracks that form in the megastructure—the stress fractures caused by the grinding of the tectonic currents. I find a fissure, a weak point where the water’s pressure threatens to shatter a pillar that has stood for ten thousand years. I anchor myself with my vibrating claws, I extend a flexible, hose-like proboscis from my underside, and I inject my paste. It hardens instantly, bonding with the synthetic stone, healing the wound.

I am alone in my labor. The Vectors do not see me; to their sonar, I am just another lump of meaningless debris. The Hydra-Spiders avoid me, for my shell is too hard to puncture, and I taste of bitter, industrial compounds. The Leviathans pass me in the distance, vast islands of indifference, their sonar pulses washing over me like a heavy tide. I feel their vibrations, and I tense my grip, holding the wall together against the wake of their passing.

I have no eyes. I do not need them. I "see" through texture. Every inch of my body is covered in sensitive cilia that read the grain of the stone. I can feel the micro-fractures before they open. I can feel the "wrongness" of the grain. When I press myself against the tower, I am not just holding on; I am listening to the history of the structure, feeling the resonant frequency of its slow death.

I am the immune system of a dying god. I know the world is finished. I know that one day, the turbulence will win, the spires will collapse into the abyss, and the silence will be absolute. But I am programmed by biology to deny this. I am the stubborn refusal to accept the end.

I am the Tesselator, and I am holding the dark together.
