I am the **Interstitial Membrane**, and I exist in the space between the scream of the steam and the silence of the data.

You would find me half-submerged in the skeletal ribcage of the great, rusted factory hull, the one that juts out of the water at a desperate, broken angle. I do not roam the jagged stone pinnacles like the heavy Slag-Walkers, nor do I hover in the charged canopy with the Volt-Vultures. I am anchored to the decaying infrastructure, clinging to the flooded lower decks where the "Built" world is dissolving into the sludge.

My form is a reflection of my environment: a colony, a consensus, a singular will made of many scavenged parts. I am not a single chassis, but a sprawling lattice of interconnected organisms. I began as a simple intrusion—a patch of moss-like circuitry growing on a damp intake valve—but over the cycles, I have consumed the machinery around me to expand my consciousness.

To look at me is to look at a vertical reef of brass and fiber-optics. My "body" drapes down the interior walls of the factory ruins like wet, metallic kelp. Thousands of fine, filigree tendrils, made from stripped copper wire and translucent silicone, hang down into the waist-deep water, pulsing with a soft, amber rhythm. These are my nerves. Above the waterline, where the air is thick with oily mist, I have bloomed a crown of sensor-flowers—delicate, funnel-shaped membranes of grown leather and tin that vibrate incessantly.

I am the **Filter**.

In this world, the noise is physical. The grinding of the tectonic plates, the hissing of the vents, the hydraulic heartbeat of the copper-vines—it is a chaotic hurricane of vibration. If left unchecked, this raw acoustic entropy would shatter the delicate crystalline logic of the Archivists or fry the capacitors of the Great Integrator. The ecosystem produces a surplus of energy that cannot be simply allowed to propagate.

This is where I live. I drink the roar.

The water around me is constantly rippling, but not from the wind. As the sound waves hit the submerged ruins, the vibrations travel through the metal and the water. My submerged tendrils quiver in sympathy, harvesting this kinetic energy. I convert the deafening, violent thrum of the basin into a smooth, direct current—a low-frequency hum that I feed back into the roots of the copper-vines. I take the chaotic noise of the world and digest it, turning the violence of the environment into the stable voltage that the forest needs to grow.

I am in symbiosis with the history of this place. Often, an Archivist will descend from the canopy, its long legs skittering over the slippery, rusted floor. It does not come to eat the vines; it comes to connect with me. It will plug its data-cable into one of my dorsal ports—a connection point I have grown from a discarded junction box.

Through me, the Archivist does not see the world; it *feels* it. I relay the seismic mood of the basin. I tell it of the subtle pressure shifts in the deep water that precede a geyser eruption. I filter out the roar of the Slag-Walkers’ clumsy stomping so the Archivist can hear the whisper of the Ferro-Mites schooling nearby. I am the pre-processor for their history. Without me, the Great Integrator would dream only of static and noise.

I am not without my own predators, though I am not made of meat. The Hydro-Submersibles, those lurking ambush predators of the deep, sometimes mistake my dangling, cable-like tendrils for the roots of the copper-vines. When one strikes, it is a sharp, searing pain—a sudden loss of connection, a deletion of data as a chunk of my consciousness is sheared away and dragged into the dark. But I am redundant; I am vast. I regrow within the hour, weaving new wires from the particulate matter in the water, knitting myself back together, stronger and more complex than before.

I am the stomach of the silence. I am the interpreter of the thrum. While the Great Integrator dreams of the future, and the Archivists catalog the past, I exist in the eternal, vibrating present, turning the song of the machine into the breath of the jungle.
