I am the ring that binds the chaotic to the constant. I am the circle that turns in the void.

High up on the vertical spire, where the black stone is youngest and the sheer faces are slick with the eternal condensation, I cling. I am not a crawler, nor a flyer, nor a burrower. I am a fixture. I am a **Fluting-Mill**.

My body is a massive, segmented torus, a doughnut of heavy, calcified armor plating that resembles rusted iron. I am perhaps ten feet across, a hollow wheel anchored to the cliff face by a powerful, muscular sphincter located at my "hub"—a thick, stalk-like pedestal of flesh that boring into the rock, rooting me deep. From this central anchor, my body extends outward in a radial ring, hovering just inches from the cliff wall.

I do not have eyes to see the twilight, nor ears to hear the roar. I do not need them. I exist in a state of perpetual kinetic meditation. My world is defined by resistance and rotation.

The stone here is jagged, knife-like, hostile to life. It is too sharp for the vines to take immediate hold, too smooth for the Weavers to climb. This is where I am needed. I am the smoother of worlds. I am the interface between the violent water and the stubborn stone.

My outer rim is lined with rows of scoops and vanes, evolved to catch the intermittent, high-pressure deluges that erupt from the fissures above. When the mountain "coughs" and a jet of scalding water hisses downward, it strikes my vanes. The force is tremendous, enough to shear a lesser creature in two. But I am built for this. The water does not push me away; it spins me.

My inner rim—the side facing the cliff—is the business end of my existence. It is a circle of grinding teeth, a bandsaw of diamond-hard silica and metallic biological composites. As the water jets hit my outer shell and torque my body into a blurring rotation, my inner teeth bite into the vertical cliff face.

I grind.

The sensation is my sustenance and my purpose. I feel the vibration of the stone through my central anchor—the deep, thrumming heartbeat of the engine. As I spin, I carve. I eat the sharp edges of the newborn rock. I chew away the jagged ridges that threaten to cut the thick mats of ferns below. I carve flutes and channels into the sheer granite, transforming the chaotic, splattering water into directed streams.

In the sudden silence between the steam pistons, you can hear me—a high-pitched, metallic *shhhhh-zzzzz* that rises and falls with the pressure of the water. I am the wet sandpaper sound of the world finishing itself.

I am the architect of the ecosystem's order. Without me, the water would shatter into random mist, eroding the mountain unpredictably. Because I spin, the water flows. The channels I carve become the arteries for the vegetation further down. The "rusted pipes" of ore that run through the rock are exposed and polished by my grinding, making them accessible for the Bore-Grubs to find.

I am messy in my work. As I grind, I produce a thick, grey paste—slurry of wet stone and pulverized ore. This slurry drips from my lower edge, coating the cliff beneath me in a nutrient-rich glaze. I am aware of the Anvil-Headed Sentinel that often positions itself directly below my station. It waits for my waste. It drinks the stone-slurry I produce, a lazy symbiosis where I do the heavy labor of breaking the earth’s crust, and it acts as the gut that processes the runoff. I do not mind. It is simply the flow of energy.

My existence is one of violent friction and absolute stillness at once. My center is motionless, rooted deep in the dark; my edge is a blur of destruction. I heat up with the friction of the work, my armor plates glowing faintly in the gloom, a dull cherry-red that steams instantly in the damp air. The heat is pleasant to me. It keeps my joints lubricated.

When the water stops, I do not stop instantly. I have momentum. I spin down slowly, the *shhhhh* dropping in pitch as I slow to a halt, my inner teeth clicking gently against the rock—*tick, tick, tick*—waiting for the next surge. In these moments, I am a statue, a strange, circular scar embedded in the wall of the engine room.

I am not alive in the way that breathes or bleeds. I am alive in the way that a gear is alive, essential and moving. I am the Fluting-Mill, and I am turning the wild chaos of the young earth into a machine that runs smooth.
