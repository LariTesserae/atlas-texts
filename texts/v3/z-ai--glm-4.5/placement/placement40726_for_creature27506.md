I am a **Resonant**.

To look at me from a distance, you might mistake me for a(vertical stack of railings abandoned to the rust. I stand seven feet tall, my physiology stretched thin by the generations spent in this rarefied, copper-tainted atmosphere. My skin is the color of wet slate—smooth, cool, and vaguely translucent, mimicking the texture of the fresh obsidian shards that litter the ground. I am bundled in heavy layers of oilcloth and scavenged leather, a coat that weighs more than I do, hanging off my sharp shoulders like the skin of a shed animal.

My face is hidden behind a complex, brass-bound respirator, a mask that covers my nose and mouth with a series of filtration canisters and dampening chambers. With every inhale, the device gives a low, wet hiss; with every exhale, a rattling chuff. The air that reaches my lungs tastes of cold mint and scrubbed metal, a taste I have known since birth.

I am standing on the **Perimeter Gantry of Sector 4**, a narrow iron walkway that spirals around the throat of a massive, dormant ventilation shaft. The drop is vertiginous, plunging down into a darkness so absolute it seems to swallow the light from the Tesla coils arcing in the distance. Above me, the ceiling of cloud presses low, lit from beneath by the orange furnace-glow of the city, turning the perpetual twilight into a bruised, suffocating purple.

My existence is defined by vibration. I do not hear the city as you might; I feel it. The massive pistons slamming miles away send shockwaves through the soles of my boots. The grinding of the tectonic plates vibrates in the hollow spaces of my marrow. I am attuned to the **Slow Dynamic**. My hands, elongated and spindly, possess a sensitivity to the iron railings that borders on the synesthetic. I can run a fingertip along a bolt and taste the stress-load of the metal; I can press my palm against the railing and hear the conversations of other Resonants miles away, their messages rippling through the iron infrastructure like plucked strings on a cello.

Right now, I am waiting.

I am watching a **fold in reality** about fifty yards ahead of me. It is a subtle distortion—a place where the geometry of the Spine grows tired of being straight. A section of the catwalk is slowly, agonizingly beginning to curl upward, not bending like metal, but folding like wet paper. The iron railings are losing their definition, blurring at the edges. Through the haze, I can see the other side of the fold connecting to a walkway that should be three miles down-mountain.

This is my duty. I am a Negotiator of Edges.

Beside me, resting on the metal grating, is a **Gaff-Pole**. It is a tool of my trade—a twenty-foot shaft of composite graphite and iron, tipped with a magnetic grapple and a heavy, blunt hook. I use it not to fight, but to guide. When reality shifts, when gravity hesitates, debris falls. And debris in a machine that is constantly trying to un-build itself can be fatal.

There is movement below me. I glance over the railing, my magnifying lenses whirring as they focus. A **Slag-Eater** is hauling its massive, gelatinous bulk along a lower pipe. Its skin is a shifting riot of toxic greens and oranges, a warning sign that glows faintly in the gloom. It is pausing to lick a patch of rust from a rivet, its acidic tongue leaving the metal polished and gleaming. The creature is semi-transparent; I can see the gears of the elevator mechanism turning slowly *through* its belly. It ignores me. We are part of the same digestive system, it and I. It cleans the metal; I clean the physics.

Suddenly, the railing beneath my hand sings. A sharp, discordant hum travels up the iron from the depths of the city.

*Alert. Something is coming.*

I turn my mask toward the sky. The air pressure drops, making my ears pop inside the respirator. From the slate-grey clouds, a shape descends—a **Rust-Moth**. It is immense, a six-foot wingspan of jagged scrap metal and iron filings. Its wings beat the air with a heavy, rhythmic *clank-shhh, clank-shhh*. It is hunting for metal to repair its nest, spiraling down toward the Tesla coil on the nearest tower.

I do not move. To move is to risk traction on a walkway that is currently debating whether or not to exist. I simply plant my feet wider, feeling the smooth obsidian stone beneath the grate, anchoring myself to the bedrock.

The moth swoops, its shadow passing over me, and lands on the transformer housing above. The sound of it tearing at the metal grating is harsh, but rhythmic. It is the sound of the world feeding on itself.

I turn my attention back to the fold. The catwalk ahead has curled another ten degrees. The gravity there is failing; I can see a loose pebble drift upward, floating lazily toward the gap in the clouds. If I were to walk there now, I would likely fall *up* into the sky, or sideways into the solid rock of the mountain wall.

I lift my Gaff-Pole. I extend it to its full length, the graphite segments sliding out with a smooth click. I reach out across the void, hooking the end around a secure stanchion on the stable side of the fold.

Then, I begin to hum.

It is a low, sub-sonic frequency, resonating in my chest, amplified by the brass of my mask. I direct this hum into the railing, into the stanchion, into the pole. I am introducing a counter-frequency, a stabilizing rhythm to the chaotic vibration of the fold. I am reminding the metal of its purpose. I am negotiating with the mountain, asking it to hold its shape for just a little longer.

Slowly—so slowly that a watching human would think it was a trick of the light—the curling catwalk shudders. The blur at the edges sharpens. The floating pebble drops, hitting the grate with a *ping*. The fold hesitates, then settles back into a flat line. The geometry holds.

I retract the pole. The vibration in the railing smooths out, returning to the steady, comforting thrum of the city’s heartbeat.

I did not fix it. I cannot fix the Slow Dynamic. I simply borrowed a moment of stability. I bought the city another hour of existence.

I turn away from the edge. My shift is not over. The dampness seeps through my coat, the cold numbing my joints, but I do not mind. I am a Resonant. I am the vibration in the wire, the silence between the gear shifts. I am the one who watches the world knit itself together, and unravel, all at the same time.

I take a deep breath of the minty, copper air, and I begin to walk.
