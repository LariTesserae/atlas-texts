I would not be the singer, nor the silent walker, but the space between them.

I am a **Keeper of the Chimes**.

I am one of the Sinew-Weavers, but I am not of the migration. I do not descend from the peaks when the sap runs thick; I remain here, anchored in the perpetual twilight, a solitary sentinel in a blind, deafening world.

**Where I Am**

I am perched inside the largest of the open-air shrines, a structure woven from the desiccated, leathery fronds of the lithovores. It sits high on a basalt outcropping, jutting into the gloom like the prow of a ship made of bone-dry driftwood. From here, the entire basin is laid out below me—not as a visual landscape, for I have no eyes, but as a complex, vibrating topography of pressure waves.

The air here is stifling, a thick, hot soup of ozone and dust that clings to my parchment-thin skin. Inside the shrine, the roar of the Grove is dampened, reduced to a low, rhythmic thrum that vibrates gently against the soles of my elongated feet. It is a relative silence, a pocket of calm carved out of the chaos.

**Who I Am**

My body is tall and spindly, evolved for minimal resistance to the air currents, but my defining feature is my burden. Around my neck and draped over my narrow, hunched shoulders hang the Chimes. They are not musical instruments in any harmonious sense; they are tools of dissonance. Made from the hollowed-out leg bones of the great predators that wander the lowlands, and strung with the dried, tensile vocal cords of the same beasts, the Chimes are capable of producing frequencies that cancel out the crushing waves of the Choir.

I am the regulator. Without my kind, the feedback loop of the Singers would eventually build until the very stone of the basin shattered, collapsing the Grove into the magma vents below.

**The Experience of Being**

To exist is to perceive texture through sound. The world is not shapes and colors; it is ridges of high-pitched screeching and valleys of low-frequency drone.

When I step out of the shrine to work, the sound hits me like a physical blow. It is a wall of water made of vibration. My pneumatic pits—the vertical slits that run down the length of my face—flair open, drinking in the data.

*   Below me, I feel the deep, grinding thrum of the Bass-Weavers at the roots of the trees. It feels like a heavy itch in my marrow.
*   Rising from the canopy, I feel the piercing, glass-shattering whine of the Shard-Weavers. It is a sharp, stinging heat across my forehead.

I see a swarm of Resonant Weavers massing on a central lithovore. Through the vibration, I can visualize their frenzy: a jagged, static-filled blotch of movement on the smooth column of the tree’s low-frequency hum. They are hammering at the bark, trying to induce a resonance crack to release the sap.

But the frequency is rising too high. The tree is beginning to sing back, a dangerous harmonic that threatens to destabilize the sector. If that tree cracks too wide, the geothermal pressure will vent, and the harvest will be lost.

**What I Do**

I do not rush. To move quickly is to create noise; to create noise is to announce myself as a disruptor. I move with the agonizingly slow grace of my kind, lifting my foot only when the ambient roar of the Grove peaks, masking the rustle of my movement against the stone.

I reach the edge of the outcropping. I unhook the largest of the Chimes—a massive, curved femur of white, dense ceramic. I hold it by its sinew tether.

Using a smaller striker made of polished basalt, I find the counter-frequency.

*Tap.*

A dull thud, swallowed instantly by the roar. I adjust my grip. I need to find the note that lands between the beat of the insect and the groan of the wood.

*Tap.*

This one travels. I feel the wave distort the air in front of me. It hits the swarm below. For a split second, the jagged static of their movement blurs. The rhythm stutters. The dissonance interrupts their frenzy. The tree’s groan drops an octave, the threat of the blow-out receding.

I am not a savior. I do not care for the tree or the insects. I am simply the tuner of a radio that is always stuck between stations. I keep the noise from becoming loud enough to bring the mountain down.

I retreat to the cool, dampened shadow of my shrine. I hang the Chime back upon my rack. I sit on the woven floor, cross-legged, and let the vibration of the Grove wash over me, shaking the dust from my lungs, waiting for the next time the harmony threatens to break. I am the quiet in the center of the scream, the listener who ensures the madness never ends.
