The air here is a heavy, wet lungful of iron and waiting. It tastes like blood in the back of the throat, rich and metallic, suspended in a humidity so dense it feels less like weather and more like immersion. I am not standing on the plateau; that is too dangerous for a thing as small as I am. Down there, among the shivering spires of raw basalt, the Geophagi are digesting the world, turning the sharp, young rock into slurry. To step on the ground is to become the foundation.

So, I am high above the stone floor, suspended in the cathedral ribs of the upper canopy.

**I am a Synapse-Glider.**

I am not an animal in the way the old histories might define them—no chitin, no bone, no blood that rushes and screams. I am a specialized offshoot of the architecture itself, a mobile node of the Collective given temporary form. My body is a cluster of calcified woody beads, strung together by taut, fibrous muscles that snap and hum with tension. I resemble a malformed fruit or a gall grown too large, roughly the size of a human head, but flatter and more aerodynamic. From my sides extend four membranes of translucent, leaf-like material, stretched taut over veins of pressurized fluid. They are not wings for soaring on the wind, but rudders for navigating the violent updrafts and steam explosions.

I cling to the underside of a massive, arching bridge of woven creepers—a "vein" of the city that connects two towering bulwarks of calcified wood. The surface here is slick with the constant weeping of condensation. My six claw-tipped tendrils dig deep into the fibrous bark, piercing the outer layer to tap into the nutrient-rich sap pulsing just beneath. I do not eat; I plug in. I am fueled by the hydraulic pressure of the city itself.

From this vantage point, I can feel the *thrum*. It is a low-frequency vibration that rattles my wooden shell, the heartbeat of the Gutters.

My purpose is singular and frantic: I am a troubleshooter for the blind. The Suture-Vines are the hands, but they are clumsy, groping things. They feel for tension, but they do not understand the *geometry* of the void. When a bridge snaps, the vines thrash into the mist, hoping to catch purchase on nothing. That is where I come in.

I am the eye that the vines lack. I possess a cluster of primitive, heat-sensitive eyespots atop my carapace that can see the infrared signatures of the warm stone amidst the cool fog. When a structure fails—when a cable whiplashes into the abyss—I detach. I launch myself into the swirling, wet air, my internal fluids shifting violently to stabilize my flight.

I ride the steam columns.

It is terrifying and ecstatic. A Hydraulic Heart in a nearby tower vents its pressure with a sound like a cracking mountain, blasting a jet of superheated vapor upward. I catch the edge of this thermal, my membranes shivering but holding, riding the updraft to the height of the failing strut. I hover there, a buzzing, wooden spark in the mist, tasting the air for the signature of the nearest anchor point.

Once I lock onto a target—a protruding spar of basalt or the flank of a neighboring tower—I signal the Suture-Vines. I do not use sound; I use chemistry. I release a burst of pheromones, a scent of "here, grip here, *now*."

Below me, the severed end of the vine goes rigid. It ceases its random flailing and whips toward the coordinates of my scent. I have to be fast. As the vine shoots past me, thick with the momentum of its own growth, I latch onto it. For a fleeting second, I am the fuse, the living connection between the rope and the rock. I guide the tip into the crevice, feeling the crushing force as the vine contracts. The resin sprays, hot and sticky, cementing the connection. The bridge pulls taut, the tension sings through my body, and the city holds its breath again.

I am not a citizen of this place. I am a tool, a disposable scout with a limited lifespan. My tissues are already beginning to harden, to calcify. Eventually, perhaps in a few weeks, I will find a stable junction and root myself. My wings will wither, my claws will fuse into the bark, and I will become a static node of the network, another silent bulb in the lantern chain of the Gutters.

But for now, I am the motion. I am the awareness in the green. I hang here, upside down over the abyss, watching the steam rise, waiting for the next cannon-shot crack of a Fruitor, or the shudder of a failing stone. I wait for the moment the city holds its breath, so that I may exhale it back into life.
