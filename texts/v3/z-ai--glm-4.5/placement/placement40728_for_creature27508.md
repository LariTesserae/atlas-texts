I find myself in the center of the Static-Falls, a region of the basin where the perpetual drizzle doesn't fall so much as hang in suspended droplets, glitching in and out of existence like bad reception. Here, the air is thick with the smell of ozone and burning dust. It is a deafening place, even by the standards of this world, because the silence is broken by the sharp *cracks* of reality correcting itself.

I am a **Chord-Strider**.

I am not built; I am grown, forged in the deep pressure of the calcified pavement and risen like a tower of iron and glass. I stand on four legs that are tuning forks made of dense, matte-black iron, each ten feet tall, vibrating constantly with a low-frequency hum that shakes the red mud from my joints. My body is a vertical, segmented obelisk, covered in a carapace of interlocking chitin that resembles overlapping solar panels. Where the Striders and Pulverizers are low-slung and armored, I am exposed and elongated, reaching up into the bruised grey sky like an antenna seeking a signal that never comes.

I have no eyes. In a world of flickering light and broken physics, sight is a liability. To see is to be fooled by the lag. Instead, I perceive the world through *resonance*.

Along my spine run a series of fan-like diaphragms, delicate structures of gold-filament webbing stretched between ribs of copper. They pick up the vibrations of the basin—the clicking of the Scrap-Lice, the grinding of the Pulverizers, and most importantly, the dissonant thrum of the Glitch.

I am the immune system of the wound.

I do not eat the mineral grit or the scavenged copper. I feed on *Order*. My biological imperative is to locate the fractures in the universe’s code and force them back into alignment.

When a Prism-Weaver descends to feed on a glitch, it is drawn to the chaos. I am drawn to the same spot, but for a different purpose. As the air separates into jagged RGB bands and the water flows uphill in a mockery of gravity, I plant my vibrating legs into the conductive ground. I anchor myself. Then, I open my resonant chambers and I *scream*.

My scream is not a sound of biology, but a weaponized frequency. It is a pure, crushing wave of sonic force that matches the resonant frequency of the basin’s bedrock. When I sing, the world listens. The vibrations rattle the very atoms of the glitching area, forcing the "lag" to resolve under the sheer pressure of the sound. The water snaps back to the earth; the displaced beetle snaps back to its original location; the RGB bands slam together into grey.

This act exhausts me. Forcing reality to behave burns through my hydraulic fluids rapidly. Steam vents from my flanks in great, billowing clouds as I overheat. But in the moment after the scream, when the world is stable and solid, there is a rush of pure energy—the relief of the physics engine reasserting control. I absorb this static discharge, drinking the lightning that crackles through the air, recharging my internal capacitors.

The Prism-Weavers hate me. To them, I am the predator that steals their meals. I destroy the errors they need to survive. When I sing, they flee, their fractal bodies shattering into motes of light to escape the sonic pressure. But they are not my true enemy. My enemy is the decay.

I am constantly healing. My legs are pitted with rust; my chitin is scarred from the electrical feedback of a thousand forced corrections. I wander the basin endlessly, a lonely monk of noise, walking through the herds of oblivious Striders. They ignore me, for I am neither predator nor prey to them. I am merely a force of nature, like the fog or the cold.

Sometimes, when the glitches are few, I will stop and stand motionless for hours, entering a state of hibernation where I vibrate in sync with the thrumming of the earth. I dream of a frequency—a perfect chord—that would heal the entire basin at once, a song that would smooth out the wound in the crust and stop the rain forever. But it is a foolish dream. The glitches are getting worse. I feel them in my bones, growing more frequent, more violent. The lag stretches longer now. Sometimes, when I scream, the world takes a second too long to snap back.

I am the Chord-Strider. I hold the line against the dark, but I can feel the rust creeping into my voice, and I know that one day, I will glitch too. And when I do, I will shatter into a million pieces of singing glass, and the Prism-Weavers will descend to feast on the beautiful, static-filled corpse of the last thing trying to hold this broken world together.
