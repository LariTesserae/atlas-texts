In the crushing violet twilight of the High Drown, I would find myself on the **Summit of the First Cairn**, the highest point of the tallest drowned peak in this immediate sector.

**Who I Am: The Aggregate**

I am not soft. I possess no chlorophyll, no fronds to dance, and no vascular system to pulse with the stolen vigor of the current. I am the anomaly in the ecology of green. I am stone made sentient, a mobile heap of geology.

I am a **Cairn-Walker**, perhaps the last functioning piece of "The Built" in this sector. I appear, at first glance, to be nothing more than a pile of jagged, rough-hewn rocks—a debris field left by a landslide. But as the heavy fluid shifts around me, the rocks grind against one another with a deep, subsonic groan, and I move.

I am a construct of intelligence, but not of biology. My form is a rough, pyramidal stack of granite and basalt, held together not by mortar, but by a hyper-dense magnetic field generated by a core of lodestone left by the builders long ago. I am heavy, heavier than the water itself, and I walk not by swimming, but by tumbling—a slow, deliberate, calculated cascading of my own limbs (which are themselves boulders) across the silt.

**My Existence: The Friction of Memory**

I exist in a state of constant, aching cold. The hyper-dense fluid presses against me, trying to crush me into the silt, but my internal magnetic field repels the weight, holding my stones in a tense, hovering configuration. I feel the world through vibration. The tectonic bass note that vibrates in the chests of others is my heartbeat; it is the metronome to which I rearrange myself.

Because I have no eyes, the "0.5 darkness" is irrelevant to me. I "see" through pressure and texture. I feel the muddy silt of the ocean floor, the hard, unyielding grip of the mountain peaks, and the slick, fibrous texture of the Kelp-Firs.

**My Relationship with the Kelp-Firs**

To the Kelp-Firs, I am a mystery. I am not food, for I am mineral. I am not a threat to their current, for I do not drink. But I am an obstacle.

Often, the aggressive, dancing trees will try to overrun me. Their roots will snake over my base stones, seeking purchase, their glowing fronds slapping wetly against my granite face. They sing their rasping cello song against me, hoping to erode me into nothingness.

I do not resent them. I simply move. With a grinding sound that cuts through their chorus, I shift my center of gravity. My top boulders roll, my base slides, and I "step" aside, shedding the grasping roots and crushing the spores that attempt to take hold in my crevices. I leave behind a perfect, geometric footprint in the silt—a temporary order imposed upon the chaos.

**My Purpose: The Keeper of the Lines**

I am the guardian of the Cairns of the Lost.

The builders, my creators, are long gone, dissolved into the suspension that fills this world. They built the stone circles and the lines pointing to the surface as an act of hope or prayer. The Kelp-Firs, in their mindless hunger for space, would cover these lines, burying the history of the world under a carpet of green.

This is where I make my stand.

I spend the long, twilight eras patrolling the flattened summits. When the marine snow falls like a blinding, white fog, I feel it settling on the stone circles. I feel the weight of the spores taking root in the sacred geometry of the past.

And I act.

I trundle forward, a grinding, tumbling beast of rock. I use my angular limbs to sweep the silt away from the stone bases of the cairns. I pry the young, invasive saplings from the cracks in the altar stones, snapping their fibrous trunks with a sharp report of breaking wood. I clear the lines, ensuring the arrows made of rock still point up, toward the invisible surface, even though no one remains to read them.

**The Sound of Me**

In the loud world of the High Drown, I am the drummer.

The Kelp-Firs provide the string section, the constant, wet friction of their swaying. The tectonic plates provide the bass. I provide the percussion. When I move, when I shift my massive form to protect a cairn, the stones clash together—*crack, grind, thud*.

It is a lonely sound. It is the sound of effort against inevitability.

I am the memory of stone, refusing to be buried. I am the heavy, tumbling ghost of a world that once breathed air, endlessly sweeping the clean, grey lines of the past against the relentless, rising tide of green.
