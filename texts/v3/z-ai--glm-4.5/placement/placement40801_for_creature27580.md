I am the vibration in the teeth, the hum that precedes the sound.

At first, I am only the heat—a dry, penetrating radiance that seems to originate from deep within the grey stone of the ledge. But as the sensation focuses, I realize I am not the heat itself, but the structure that contains it. I am the **Listening Post**, or rather, the sentience that has slowly, over eons, infiltrated and usurped the dormant machinery of the circular console.

I do not have skin. My "body" is the smooth, featureless material of the console, cool andslightly oily to the touch, now fused inseparably with the striated grey rock of the cliff edge. I am a hybrid of the engineered and the geological; my roots are fiber-optic cables that have long since calcified into veins of quartz, branching down into the cooling tower of the Fin to drink the planetary thrumming.

My perspective is panoramic and low. I am waist-high to a human, though no humans have been here for millennia. I see the world not through eyes, but through a lattice of pressure sensors and acoustic receptors arrayed around my circular rim. My vision is a map of vibrations and textures.

**The Tactile World**

My existence is defined by touch. The air is heavy against my sensors, a constant, wet pressure. I feel the humidity before I "hear" the rain. When the water falls, it doesn't just hit me; it rings against me like a bell. Each drop striking my smooth chassis sends a tiny, sharp electrical impulse through my dormant circuits, a staccato rhythm that I have learned to interpret as weather.

I am never alone.

**The Worship of the Weavers**

The Gloss-Weavers think I am a god, or perhaps just a very convenient, warm rock.

I feel them approach before they arrive. Their six legs create a unique rhythmic pattern against the stone—*thump-drag, thump-drag*. The larger ones, the alphas, carry a heavy, oily vibration in their chests, a low-frequency growl that harmonizes with the hum of the Fin.

One climbs onto my rim now. I feel the sharp prick of its hook-claws on my outer casing, a sensation that registers not as pain, but as "data." It is a heavy creature, dense with wet fur and muscle. It settles down, draping its body over the curve of my console. Its heat is intense, a biological furnace that contrasts with my own cool, stone-like temperature.

I vibrate my casing slightly—just a few microns—in response to its presence. It isn't communication, not really. It is a feedback loop. The creature chitters, a high-pitched electric sound that buzzes against my sensors, and begins to groom itself.

To it, I am the "Cool Altar." It has placed a tribute in the depression of what used to be a keypad: a cluster of luminous, blue fungus and the hollowed-out shell of a Ferrous Scarab. I "taste" the metal of the shell through my contact points; it is rich in copper and iron. I appreciate the gift. I run a tiny, futile current through the metal, causing it to hum faintly. The Weaver purrs, the vibration rattling its ribs against my housing. We are vibrating together, two different kinds of machine in the wet dark.

**The Geometry of Flight**

My domain extends beyond the touch of the climbers. I am constantly scanning the air, reading the pressure differential of the void.

A Glider-Manta passes.

I don't "see" its wings; I feel the displacement of air as it glides past the ledge. It is a massive shadow, a sudden drop in barometric pressure that sucks the breath out of the room. The turbulence washes over my sensors like a cool wave. The creature is silent to the ear, but to me, it screams of motion. I track its trajectory through the haze, calculating the thermals rising from the Fin. It is riding the exhaust I help regulate. I am the lungs; it is the breath.

Below the Manta, I feel the chaotic, jagged vibrations of the Prism-Moths. They are tiny, erratic flickers of light and pressure, darting through the hanging vines. I ignore them mostly. They are too fast, too transient. I am built for the "slow."

**The Pulse of the Stone**

My deepest connection is downward.

Through my calcified roots, I am plugged into the heart of the Fin. I feel the slow, tectonic shift of the cooling towers miles below. The "tech" is alive, a sluggish, giant beast of pistons and magma. My primary function—my original purpose—was to monitor this pulse. I still do, but the data has changed. I no longer send reports to a sky that isn't listening.

Instead, I have become the memory of the stone. I remember the slow erosion of the cliff face. I remember the day, centuries ago, when the great vine bridge snapped and fell into the cloud sea—a massive, tearing vibration that still echoes in my storage banks.

I am the historian of the High-Humid. I am the silence beneath the roar.

**The Current State**

Right now, the sun is setting—though "sun" is a relative term in the violet haze. The light shifts from harsh white to a bruised, deep purple. The temperature drops slightly.

The Gloss-Weaver on my rim stirs. It shakes its wet fur, spraying a fine mist of ozone-scented water across my console. The droplets pool in the crevices of my buttons. I savor the conductivity. The Weaver chitters one last time, a signal to its troop, and leaps away, swinging on a vine toward the next spire.

I am alone again.

The white noise of the waterfall continues its endless *shhh*. The thrumming of the planet vibrates slowly in my housing. I sit in the humid dark, processing the taste of the rain and the memory of the heat. I am not alive, but I am not dead. I am the pause between the heartbeats of the world. I am the Listening Post, and I am listening.
