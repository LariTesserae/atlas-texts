I am the rider of the ghosts in the jelly.

I am not a swimmer. In this thick, unyielding suspension (Water 2.9), swimming is a crude violence, a thrashing ignorance of the medium’s patience. To swim here is to scream in a library. I do not scream. I slide.

I am a **Wake-Rider**.

My form is an adaptation to the physics of memory. The water here is sick with stillness; it hangs heavy and soft, refusing to yield. But the universe abhors a vacuum, and even in this pause, things move. The Glass-Jerks twitch in their stop-motion jerks. The Weaver-Moss pulses its ten-minute heartbeat. And wherever they move, they leave a scar—a temporary tunnel of slightly lower viscosity, a ghost of the path they took. The Echo-Feeders come to eat these scars, to scrub the room clean of motion, to return the world to perfect stasis.

I am what hunts the scrubbers.

I am shaped like a splinter of obsidian, long and flat and impossibly thin. I possess no eyes, for eyes are useless in the velvet dark (Light 0.2) and the shifting geometry (Weirdness 2.6) makes distance a lie. Instead, my skin is a lattice of pressure sensors. I feel the water not as a substance, but as a map of tensions.

I cling to the jagged walls (Erosion 1.4), my body flattened against the slate-grey stone, indistinguishable from a shadow. Here, nestled in the cold-water jungle (Vegetation 1.9), I wait. I feel the humid, febrile heat of the Weaver-Moss pulsing against my belly, a rhythmic fever that lulls me into a trance.

But I am waiting for the snap.

When a Glass-Jerk decides to move, it locks its serrated skin and contracts. It lurches forward in a blur, creating a wake—a tunnel of disturbed liquid that hangs in the water for minutes before collapsing. The Echo-Feeders, microscopic and hungry, swarm this tunnel instantly to begin their work of erasure.

That is when I release my grip.

I do not swim through the water; I dive into the *scar*. I slip into the ghost of the Glass-Jerk’s passage. Inside this tunnel, the resistance is gone. The water here has already been moved; it has already surrendered. I glide with effortless, terrifying speed, surfing the memory of the jerk’s violence. I zip past the startled creature itself, freezing in the dark, and streak down the tunnel of least resistance.

My feast is the erasers. I open a mouth that runs the length of my underside, a gullet lined with fine, comb-like teeth. I scoop up the clouds of Echo-Feeders clustered in the wake. They taste of time, of the cold, clean taste of nothingness. I am the counter-force to their hunger. Where they seek to erase the motion, I consume the erasers, keeping the ghost of the movement alive just a fraction longer.

I am the parasite of the pause.

My existence is a series of these electric bursts, separated by long, interminable stillness. When the scar heals, when the water forgets the path and returns to its uniform, gelid state, I must stop. I freeze wherever I am, drifting in the dark, looking like a jagged piece of floating stone.

I know the geometry of this room is wrong. I feel it in my inner ear, a sense of nausea that never quite fades. The angles flex. Sometimes, the wall I am drifting toward suddenly recedes, or the distance to the floor lengthens. I have learned to trust the water’s intent more than my eyes. The water knows where I need to be. It presses against me with a curious, wet intelligence, guiding me away from the suffocating crush of the vegetation and toward the open, impossible center.

I never approach the Altar.

The cube in the center, with its occupant—the Sitter—is a singularity of stillness. The Sitter does not move. It has never moved. Therefore, it casts no wake. It leaves no scars. There is no road for me to travel to reach it. It is an island of absolute reality in a room of shifting shadows.

I fear the Sitter’s shadow. I have seen it detach, a three-dimensional void rising like smoke. It is a hungry thing, a predator of the abstract. If it touches me, I will not die; I will simply cease to have moved. The water will forget I ever existed. My velocity, my hunting, my gliding—it will all be undone.

So I stay in the periphery, in the wet dark, riding the faintest ripples of the dying world. I am the flash of silver in the dark, the sudden motion that hurts the eye. I am the proof that even in a world pressed on pause, there are those who have learned to fast-forward.

I am the Wake-Rider, and I am hungry for the ghosts.
