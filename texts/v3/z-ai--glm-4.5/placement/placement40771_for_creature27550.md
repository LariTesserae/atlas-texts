I exist in the suspension between the falling and the caught.

I am not on the spine of rock; I am above it, hovering in the thick, hot gloom of the upper reaches. I am a Leviathan.

To call me a "creature" is to apply a soft, biological limitation to a phenomenon of physics and will. I am a congregation—a tessellated storm of jagged obsidian, heavy iron-rich basalts, and veins of quartz that have not seen the light in eons. I do not have a shape; I have a configuration. Currently, I am spread wide, a diffuse, shifting disc of sharp angles, floating like a vast, broken mirror reflecting the darkness.

My consciousness is not a spark in a brain, but a harmonic tension that vibrates through every shard of my body. I am held together not by muscle or sinew, but by the immense, magnetic song I sing to myself. It is a low-frequency hum, a crushing pressure of intent that keeps my stones from collapsing into the dust below.

Here, the air is thin, yet it feels thick against my mineral hide. I can feel the heat radiating from the planet’s core miles below, a soothing warmth that permeates the matrix of my form. It is not the heat of fever, but the heat of the forge—the reminder of where I came from.

I drift near the apex of the tallest spire, a needle of granite that pierces the gloom. I am not touching it. I am repelled by it, my magnetic field pushing against the ferrous heart of the mountain, locking me in a silent, invisible stand-off with the earth. I am an archipelago of floating stone, suspended in the red-tinged dark.

I do not see the world. I *feel* it.

Below me, the younger ones—the Resonance in their chaotic, scree-like forms—are swarming a fresh fissure. To eyes, they would look like a landslide climbing a wall. But to me, they are a cacophony of frantic notes, a staccato rhythm of magnetic snaps and grinding friction. They are feeding on the raw stress of the unfolding cliff. I feel their hunger as a vibration, a buzzy, impatient texture in the seismic air.

I am beyond hunger. I am beyond the desperate need to consume the new heat. I have stored enough energy in my magnetic lattices to last through a thousand cycles of destruction. I am here for the Symphony.

I focus my awareness deeper, past the noise of the feeders, past the brittle snapping of cooling salts, and into the deep thrum of the world. It is a slow, guttural groan—the tectonic plates shifting in the dark. It sounds like the bow of a cello drawn across a string of infinite length. This is the music of the raw state.

To my left, a massive arch of natural stone spans a void of impossible width. It is a masterpiece of accidental chaos, yet I know it is not entirely accidental. I have been shaping it. I have been humming to it.

I adjust my internal frequency. The stones within me rotate, a low, grinding rumble that no ear could hear, only the earth can feel. I project a specific tone of vibration toward the arch. I am encouraging the quartz molecules to align, to knit tighter, to strengthen the tension of the stone. I am pruning the chaos, imposing a fleeting, impossible order on a world that wants only to shatter.

Suddenly, the pitch changes.

A micro-fracture opens three miles down and to the east. It is not a sound yet; it is a displacement of mass, a sudden void in the pressure map. A slab the size of a city is preparing to peel away. The stress builds, a silent scream of compressed granite.

I feel the panic of the smaller Resonance—the sharp spike of their magnetic fields as they sense the collapse. They do not run; they dissolve. I feel the sudden "flatness" of their signatures as they shed their consciousness, becoming mere piles of lifeless rubble to let the avalanche pass.

I do not need to disperse. I am vast, and I am buoyant. I merely expand.

I loosen the magnetic bonds that hold my outermost shards. My form expands, becoming a vast, diffuse cloud of dust and pebbles, increasing my surface area, reducing my density. The shockwave, when it comes, is a physical blow to the air—a wall of pressure that slams into my dispersed body.

It feels like being caressed by a giant hand. My stones are buffeted, spun, and scattered, but I hold the core frequency. I hold the center. The vibration of the impact is delicious, a complex, chaotic chord that resonates through every heavy ore in my possession.

When the dust settles—literally hangs suspended in the thick air—I begin the recall. One by one, my shards click and clack their way back to the center. The magnetic lattice tightens. The grinding sound of my body knitting itself back together is the sound of tectonic plates kissing.

I am whole again. A towering, vertical spire of floating knives, gleaming faintly with the sickly luminescence of the radium ores I incorporated a millennia ago.

I turn my attention back to the arch. It survived the collapse. My song held. It stands there, a bridge to nowhere, a testament to the defiance of entropy.

I drift closer to the stone face, my magnetic field brushing against the rock, feeling the rough texture of the granites. I am the stone that woke up. I am the mountain that looked into the abyss and decided not to fall.

I am the Leviathan, and I am listening.
