I am high above the churning floor, suspended in the amber gloom of the Scaffolds, in a place where the verticality of the world is absolute. I am not on a platform, nor am I safely inside a hollowed bone. I am in the "Between"—the perilous empty space that separates the living architecture of the Unanchored from the obsidian trunk of a mega-flora we call the Spire-Heart.

I am a **Weaver of the Taut-Stitch**.

I am Unanchored, built of the same desperate parchment and dense bone as my kin, but my existence is defined by a specific, terrifying intimacy with the living ropes that hold us aloft. My hands are my identity. They are stained permanently green with the ichor of the binding vines, the knuckles swollen from years of friction against the unyielding calcified bark. My fingers are longer than the average, tipped with hooks that are worn smooth but diamond-hard. They do not just grip; they *sense*.

I am currently inverted, hanging upside down by the crook of my knees, a hundred feet of empty air beneath my head. The heat rises from the floor below like a physical blow, a wave of dry, cinnamon-scented oppression that tastes of alkaloids. Below me, the forest is a heaving carpet of violence—the grinding of the armored beasts, the shattering of glass-like fronds—but it is far away. Here, the danger is silent and slow.

I am pruning the structural integrity of our village.

The ropes that lash our bone platforms to the trees are not dead hemp. They are severed tendrils of the Strangler-Vines, plants that do not know they have been repurposed. They pulse with a slow, metabolic rhythm. They thicken. They seek purchase. If left unchecked, the very ropes that secure our homes will begin to crush the calcified slabs of the floor, or worse, they will send rootlets deep into the bone, seeking the marrow of the giant flyers we use for beams. If the rope eats the beam, we fall.
