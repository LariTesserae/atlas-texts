I am found on the vertical axis.

I am not of the floor. The floor is for the grazers, the broken ones who drag their soft bellies across the razor-sharp pavement, risking their undersides on the world that hasn’t finished cutting itself yet. I am above them. I am the pause in a fracture line halfway up a monolith that rises four hundred feet into the humming, still air.

I am a Spire-Stalker.

To look at me is to look at nothing. I am a stretch of shadow too long for the time of day. I am a fissure that has learned to breathe. My body is a tessellation of white plates, angular and interlocking, pressed so tight against the calcite of the tower that I have effectively become the wall. My internal temperature is the same as the stone. My color is the blind, bleached white of the sun. I possess no distinct silhouette, only the absence of light where my folded limbs tuck against my torso.

I am waiting. Here, waiting is not a passive state; it is an act of aggression. I have been clinging to this specific spot for three cycles of the steam-vents. My claws are hooked into microscopic pores in the pillar, locked by a muscle tension that requires zero effort, only absolute stillness. If I move, I die. If I move, I become a silhouette, a thing with edges, a signal in the void. So I do not move. I let the mineral dust settle in the folds of my carapace. I let the damp heat oxidize the sensory pits along my flanks until they are as dull as the rock itself.

My world is a sensory deprivation tank that hums.

I do not hear through ears. I hear through my bones. The entire column of the pillar is a tuning fork. Deep below, perhaps a mile down in the crust, the "construction" is happening. I feel the slow, grinding shudder of new crystal beds meshing together. It is a bass note so low it vibrates in my marrow, a constant, subsonic purr that promises the world is getting bigger, slowly crushing the old geometry to make way for the new.

I am looking down, but not with eyes. My vision is thermal. Below me, the white pavement is a uniform sheet of radiated heat. But there, moving across the grid—a cold spot.

It is a Mime.

To my thermal gaze, it is a rectangular shadow sliding over the hot stone. It is a coffin-sized block of cooler matter, dragging itself toward the base of my pillar. It is grazing on the grey moss, its mouthplate spinning, creating a high-frequency whine that travels up the stone and into my claws. *Skrrrrt. Skrrrrt.*

It is beneath me now. It is seeking the shade of the monolith to escape the wet, heavy oven of the basin. It thinks it is safe. It thinks the wall is neutral.

I feel the wind change. The air shifts, displacing a plume of steam rising from a vent to the west. The mist curls around my pillar, a momentary shroud of wetness. This is my cover. This is the only permission I will ever receive.

I detach.

Gravity takes me. I do not "fall" like a creature; I fall like a piece of the building that has calculated its own failure. I am rigid, a falling shard of white death. I make no sound until I hit.

*Impact.*

My collapsible exoskeleton absorbs the shock, crumpling inward to protect my organs, while my momentum drives a specialized, harpoon-like proboscis through the Mime’s upper shell. The grazer shudders, a seismic ripple that travels through its body and into mine. It tries to lift itself, to ride the vibration of the attack, but I am the vibration now.

I release the solvent. It hisses as it enters the Mime’s system. The calcified armor of the prey begins to liquify, turning from hard stone into a slurry of rich mineral soup. I am not eating flesh; I am drinking the stone of another. I am drinking the potential energy of its shell.

I clamp down, anchoring myself to its back. To an observer—a theoretical one, for there are none here—I am just a stain on the grazer, a blemish on the rock. I look like nothing more than a patch of rougher texture.

We wait out the hours together, predator and prey, locked in a slow chemical embrace. The sun beats down. The heat is oppressive, a heavy hand pressing on my spine. The drip-drip-drip of condensation falling from the terraces above punctuates the silence.

Then, the ground groans.

It is not the subtle growth of a pillar. This is the heavy, shifting sigh of a Geode-Construct.

The vibration hits the pillar before I hear the sound. It travels through the stone, into the Mime, and into me. It is the sound of a mountain sliding across the floor. The Mimes know this sound. It is the only thing they fear more than the shadow falling.

The Mime beneath me tenses. It abandons its camouflage. It ripples its fringe, trying to sprint, trying to flee the crushing approach of the Apex. But I am anchored to it. I am the burden it cannot shake.

I taste its panic through the chemical slurry I am ingesting. It is sharp, acidic.

The Geode-Construct passes a hundred yards to our left. It is a massive archway of white stone, moving with the grinding, articulated silence of a tectonic plate. It is hunting for a steam vent. It does not care for us. We are dust to it. But its passage displaces the air, sending a wave of hot, metallic wind that nearly tears me from my prey.

The Construct settles over a fissure with a sound like a closing tomb. The steam rises, enveloping it, hiding it. The tremors cease.

The Mimes resume their slow drift. The silence returns, heavy and ringing.

I finish feeding. The Mime’s shell is soft now, collapsing. It will not recover. It will become part of the pavement, a softened spot in the geometry that will soon be crushed or built over.

I disengage. My proboscis retracts. I expand my collapsed plates, clicking back into the rigid, angular shape of a rock shard. I crawl off the dying grazer, leaving it to dissolve into the white dust.

I must return to the vertical. I skitter up the side of the pillar, my hooked feet finding the imperfections in the calcite, moving only when the steam blows. I climb until I find a new fracture, a new shadow, a new place where the wall is incomplete.

I press myself flat. I fold my limbs. I stop my breath.

I am the Spire-Stalker. I am the silence that waits on the knife-edge. I am the architecture of hunger. I am stone, and I am waiting for the world to stop moving so I can move again.
