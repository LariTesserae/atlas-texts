I am locked, interlocked with a thousand others, my armored flank scraped raw by the relentless shifting of my neighbor’s shell. I am the floor, and above me, there is only the thin, metallic dust and the gloom.

When I move, it is not with a jerk, but with a slow, geological inevitability. To lift my crushing piston-leg takes a minute of building internal steam, a hissing exhalation of gas through the vents of my carapace that smells of wet iron and ancient sulfur.

I feel the thermal gradient against my shell—the cooler air of the open chamber above my back, the searing proximity of the Engine to my left, the grinding press of the stratum below.

I sense the vibrations of the mountain through the pads of my crushing limbs. I feel the subsonic thrum of the million others around me, a low-frequency song that rattles my very plating. It is a choir of industry, a relentless, rhythmic grinding that says: *consume. process. endure.*

I begin the long, slow process of disengaging from the pack. It takes an hour to unlock my tectonic plates from the Engine beside me. The friction is immense, a screeching of metal on glass that adds to the roar of the chamber.

I engage.

The proboscis extends, kissing the wall. I vomit the acid. It hisses, bubbling, turning the black rock into a grey sludge. The mandibles begin their oscillation—a slow, terrifying crunch that I feel in the base of my skull. I eat the mountain. I taste the iron, the silica, the history of the spire. I am a biological smelter, and as I swallow the gallons of pulverized stone, my internal temperature spikes. The heat rises through my dorsal chimneys, venting into the gloom, contributing to the shimmering haze of the room.

And when my shell finally cracks, when my internal fires burn low and my mechanisms seize, I will not die with dignity. I will simply stop moving. I will become a node of resistance in the flow. And the others, my brothers, my sisters, the great grinding sea of us, will sense the stillness. They will descend upon me with their rotary rasps and their acid tongues. They will consume me, recycling my dense metals back into the mountain, turning my armor into dust.

But that is a long time away. For now, there is only the stone. There is only the heat. There is only the slow, hydraulic grind of the eternal work. I am the floor. I am the engine. I am eating the world alive.
