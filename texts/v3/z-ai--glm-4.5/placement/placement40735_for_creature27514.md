I am a **Arc-Winder**, a living patch on a rusted world.

I dwell in the deep, sponge-like darkness between the colossal smokestack trees, where the **conduit-grass** grows thickest and the air hums with the low-voltage buzz of a planet short-circuiting. To a distant observer, I would be invisible—a streak of reflected industrial-orange light, a ripple in the black, oily mist. But up close, I am a creature of function and desperate hunger.

I am long and sinuous, a limbless cylinder of muscle and cartilage, measuring perhaps twenty feet from nose to tail. My skin is not flesh, but a layered mesh of conductive bio-copper and insulation—wet, slick, and cold to the touch, like a cable left out in the rain. Along my spine run rows of ventral plates that glow with a dull, rhythmic pulse, syncing with the heartbeat of the nearest tree-titan.

**My Physiology: The Industrial Parasite**

I do not eat. I do not hunt in the traditional sense. I am a component of the circuit board.

My mouth is a complex, triple-mandibled clamp designed not for chewing, but for gripping. Inside my throat, I do not possess a stomach, but a massive, organic toroid of iron-rich fluid—a living inductor coil. My metabolism is entirely electrical; I sustain myself on the delta, the voltage drop, the friction of energy moving from place to place. I am a biological resistor, and my body heat is the waste product of the planet’s engine flowing through me.

**My Function: The Emergency Bypass**

I exist because the plateau is breaking. The **Shear-Shell Crabs** are overzealous gardeners; they snip the black cable-grass to prevent overload, but often, they sever too much. When a critical artery of the planetary grid is cut, the hydraulic pressure of the "sap" builds, and the subsonic hum of the ground rises to a fevered screech.

This is where I wake.

I feel the interruption in the magnetic field as a physical pain, a missing tooth in a jaw that is constantly clenching. I uncoil from the damp moss, my scales sliding over the wet stone with a sound like tearing canvas. I hunt the break in the circuit.

When I find the severed ends of the conduit-grass—sparking, bleeding blue superheated fluid, frantic with lost energy—I act. I bite down on one end, lashing my heavy tail around the other.

**The Experience of the Flow**

The connection is instantaneous and violent.

The raw amperage of the High Exhaustion slams into me. It bypasses my brain and shoots straight down my spinal cord. For a moment, I cease to be a creature; I become the wire itself. My vision whites out; the iron taste of the air becomes solid on my tongue. My internal coil screams with electromagnetic torque, locking my muscles rigid.

I am in agony. I am in ecstasy.

I hold this pose—the biological bridge—until the grass repairs itself, growing new vascular threads to seal the gap. The energy flows *through* me, draining the static charge that would otherwise ignite the methane fog. I glow then, bright as a flare, a beacon in the twilight, steaming in the freezing mist. I am the path of least resistance.

**My Enemies and Neighbors**

This act of bridging makes me vulnerable.

While I am locked in the circuit, rigid and glowing, I am a sitting duck for the **Glass-Wolves**. They can smell the ozone I emit, the scent of a overloaded capacitor. I have learned to time my repairs carefully, working in the brief lulls between the steam-vent eruptions, using the roar of the earth to mask the hum of my body. If a Wolf comes, I cannot let go—the recoil would fry my nervous system. I must endure, hoping the Wolf is cautious of the current arcing across my wet skin, or that a passing **Resonance-Bat** might screech and disrupt the Wolf’s auditory camouflage.

I fear the **Static-Whales**, but for a different reason. When one of those bloated giants drifts too low, trailing their static-charged cilia, they can induce a current in my body even when I am not working. They parasitize the air I breathe, turning the mist around me into a tangle of lightning that leaves me twitching and disoriented, floating inches above the mud, confused by gravity's sudden retreat.

**My Place in the Exhaustion**

I am small compared to the titans, insignificant next to the geological grinding of the plateau. But I am necessary.

I am the Band-Aid on a dying god. I am the splice holding the wires together while the machine shudders and shakes.

When the work is done and the grass knits itself back together, I release my grip. I am left smoking, my insulation scorched, my internal coils superheated. I slide back into the black mud, exhausted, to wait for the next break in the world.

I listen to the *plink... plink... plink* of the condensation. I feel the subsonic thrumming of the trees beneath my belly. And I wait for the hum to break.
