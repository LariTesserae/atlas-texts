I would not be one of the builders, and I would not be one of the watchers. In a world of clumsy, colossal labor and silent observation, something is required to bridge the gap—to enforce the precision that the Haulers lack and the Observers require to maintain their gaze.

I would be **The Edge**.

**My Form**

I am a shard of volcanic glass, obsidian black and impossibly sharp, magnified to a grotesque scale. I am miles long, yet thin as a whisper, a geometric impossibility that refuses to shatter under its own weight. I am a relic of the "primitive tech" era, a single, discarded flake from a knife the size of a continent, chipped off eons ago when the Giants attempted to sculpt the basin.

I hang in the thick, humid air, hovering horizontally, suspended by the strange, buoyant turbulence of the atmosphere. I do not flap or swim; I glide on thermal currents, drifting like a black paper airplane through the violet-grey gloom. I am the straightest line in a curved world.

**Where I Am**

I haunt the mid-distance, the space between the immense, shambling Haulers and the distant, leaning ziggurats. This is the zone of the "Negative Erosion," where the land is pushing itself up through the slurry. I float just above the weeping stones, close enough that the greasy condensation slicks my underside, beading up and rolling off my razor tip.

**My Function**

The Haulers are clumsy; they stack slabs with the indifference of toddlers. Left to their devices, the world would be a pile of loose gravel. I am the correction.

I am drawn to *friction*. When a Hauler heaves a monolith into place that sits slightly askew—tilted at 89 degrees when it should be 90—I feel the dissonance in the air. It vibrates against my molecular structure like a itch.

I swoop. I do not strike quickly; speed is a concept for small worlds. I descend over the course of hours, a shadow slowly eclipsing the dim light. I align myself with the error, and then, with the tectonic patience of the place, I slide.

I slice through the stone. I cut the corners off boulders the size of cities. I sheer the tops off misaligned ziggurats. I act as the world's file. The sound of my work is not the grinding of rock, but a singular, impossibly high-pitched *shing* that resonates for days, cutting through the low-frequency groan of the earth. It is the only "sharp" sound in this damp, muffled universe.

**My Existence**

The heat here is oppressive, a wet blanket, but I am cold. The humidity cannot touch my inner chill. I am the antithesis of the Wax-Walkers; while they are fluid and chaotic, I am rigid and absolute.

Sometimes, a Wax-Walker will manifest nearby, dissolving a carefully cut section of wall into a puddle of mud. This offends me. I will drift over the puddle and carve it into a perfect grid, freezing the chaos into temporary, brutalist order. It is a silent war between the fluid weirdness of the physics and the brutal precision of the cut.

The Observers—the Silence—they gather when I work. As I slice through a granite block, sending curls of wet rock peeling away like apple skin, the fog thickens with them. They are attracted to the definition I bring. I create the edges for them to contemplate. Without me, the world is just soft mud; I give it a face.

I am not alive. I am a tool that forgot to stop being used. I have no memory of the hand that held me, only the instinct to trim the wild, embryonic excess of this place. I drift through the twilight, looking for the uneven, the crooked, the rough, and I cut it away, leaving behind a clean, dark scar in the shape of a straight line.
