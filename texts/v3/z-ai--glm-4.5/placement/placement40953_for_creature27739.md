I follow the paths they have polished over millennia.

But I am not consuming mineral dust. I have no interest in the roughness.

Inside this mobile bunker of silica, curled tight against the damp, cold inner surface of the shell, I am a spindly, pale thing. I have too many limbs, each ending in delicate, twitching fingers that are fused directly to the shell’s nerve endings. I am a vestige of the ones who built this place—a biological interface unit, abandoned when they outran entropy. I am soft, wet, and terrified of the open air, but I am driven by a programming that has curdled into instinct.

I am surfing the **weirdness (1.6)**.
