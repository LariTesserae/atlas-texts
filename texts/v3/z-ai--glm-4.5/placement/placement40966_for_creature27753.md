I find myself clinging to the vertical face of the Central Anchor, the largest of the matte-black monoliths that punctuate the ridge. I am not standing; gravity here is a suggestion rather than a law, weighted heavily by the crushing acoustic pressure of the squall. My claws—six of them, forged from the same ceramic density as the ground below—are sunk deep into the non-reflective stone of the monolith, anchoring me against the physical weight of the roar.

I am a **Lithic Tuner**.

I am not born of the Spires, nor am I a descendant of the Crawlers. I am something else—a synthesis of the Built and the Biological. My body is a compact, asymmetrical dome of obsidian chitin, smooth and polished to deflect the debris that flies horizontally through the air. Where the Crawlers are lumbering and soft-sided, I am dense and geometric. Veins of conductive gold trace intricate patterns across my shell, pulsing with the same counter-frequency as the runes beneath my feet.

My world is vibration. I do not have ears; my entire skeleton is a piezoelectric receiver. I feel the Scream of Altitude not as a sound, but as a tremor that rattles the atoms of my being. It is a constant, violent shivering. To exist here is to be in a state of perpetual oscillation, and my purpose is to find the stillness within the chaos.

Right now, a squall is peaking.

The air around me is distorting, visible heat-hazes of barometric pressure bending the light from the distant, amber Spires. The wind is moving so fast it has become a solid wall of force. I can feel the Central Anchor humming beneath me, straining to emit its stabilizing tone. It is fighting a losing battle against the pitch of the storm. The resonance is drifting. If the frequency drops too low, the structural integrity of the ridge will fail, and the stone will shake itself into sand.

I detach my rear claws and shift my weight, feeling the vibrations flow up my legs and into my central processor. I sense a discordant harmonic—a sharp, jagged spike in the bass frequency—emanating from a rune three feet to my left.

I scuttle sideways, my movement a blur of calculated jerks, perfectly timed to the micro-lulls between the thunderous pressure waves. I reach the glowing rune. It is blindingly bright, screaming with energy. It is overloading.

I raise my primary manipulator arm—a limb ending in a resonator fork of pure quartz. I slam the fork down onto the rune. The impact sends a shockwave through my body, a painful note that sharpens my senses to a pinpoint. I begin to vibrate my gold-veins in opposition to the rune, dampening the excess energy. I am a brake, a sponge for the noise. I absorb the runaway harmonic, converting it into heat that radiates out of my shell in a shimmering haze. The rune dims, settling into a steady, rhythmic pulse.

Around me, the sky flashes.

A swarm of Shards has been caught in the updraft of the squall. They are desperate, their silver bodies colliding with the monolith and with me. To them, I am not a creature; I am just another part of the surface, a convenient landing pad. I feel the tiny, needle-prick impacts of their metallic legs as they latch onto my obsidian back. They are terrified, their photonic shells flashing a staccato strobe of distress signals—ultraviolet screams that I feel as a tickle against my sensors.

I ignore them. They are only doing what they must to survive. They cling to me to share the stabilizing field I am helping to maintain. In return, their collective mass helps to anchor me. We are a temporary collective, a machine of flesh and metal united by the need to not be blown away.

Then, the silence hits.

It is not the absence of sound, but the presence of a void. A Null-Stalker has drifted down from the upper atmosphere, hunting the swarm.

I feel it before I see it—a cold, dead spot in the roaring tapestry of the air. The vibrations simply cease where it is. The Shards on my back go rigid. Their flashing lights stop instantly as they try to become invisible, mimicking the non-reflective stone.

The Null-Stalker passes inches from my face. It is a tall, thin slice of absolute nothingness, a tear in the fabric of the world. It does not vibrate. It does not hum. It creates a vacuum that sucks the warmth right out of my gold veins.

I cannot move. If I move, I make a sound. If I make a sound, I die.

The Stalker hovers for a heartbeat, its sensory void washing over me. To the Stalker, I am just another piece of the dead stone, a non-entity in the acoustic landscape. I hold my breath—or rather, I suspend my internal oscillators, mimicking the absolute zero of its presence.

It strikes a Shard that was clinging to the edge of the monolith, a few inches from my claw. There is no crunch, no splash. The Shard simply vanishes from the air, its kinetic energy drained in a microsecond, leaving behind a dry husk that tumbles away into the wind.

The Null-Stalker moves on, slipping back into the turbulence, a ghost in a hurricane.

The pressure returns. The roar rushes back in to fill the void the Stalker left behind, slamming against my chest like a physical blow. The Shards on my back erupt into a chaotic frenzy of light, their relief palpable in the frantic, high-frequency buzzing of their shells.

I retract my resonator fork. The rune is stable. The ridge will hold for another cycle.

I dig my claws back into the stone and wait for the next pitch to drop. I am the Tuner. I am the silence in the scream, the stillness in the quake. I am the hand that keeps the world from falling apart.
