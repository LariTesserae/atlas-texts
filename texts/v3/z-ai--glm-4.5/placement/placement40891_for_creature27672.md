I am a hesitation in the flow.

At first, I am only a sensation of heat—a dense, wet fist pressing in from all sides. There is no skin to feel the pressure, no nerves to translate the blinding white absolute of the light into vision. Instead, there is simply *containment*. I am a volume held together by tension, a droplet of hyper-saline solution refusing to dissolve into the boiling broth of the world around me.

I am drifting in the middle altitude of the cathedral nave, suspended between the weeping ceiling and the jagged floor. Here, the light is at its most violent, refracting through the mist and the rippling surface of the pools. I do not see it; I simply *know* it through the warmth it radiates into my gelatinous body.

I become aware of my shape. I am not soft, nor am I hard. I am a geometric inevitability. My membrane of silica and protein snaps tight, pulling me into the form of a tetrahedron. I am a perfect, three-sided pyramid, clear as glass but heavy with the salt of my own making. I have no bones, yet I possess a structural integrity that the knife-edged rocks around me lack. I am a vessel of heated fluid, a mobile architecture of survival.

I am hungry.

The hunger is not a pang in a stomach, for I have no gut. It is a low-frequency hum, a lack of resonance in my internal fluids. I need the iron carpet.

I focus my will—this strange, congealed consciousness that has risen from the mineral chaos—on the wall of obsidian to my left. I can feel the texture of the rock without touching it. The vibration of the distant engine thrums against the stone, and the water running down the wall sings a different song than the stillness of the pool. I can taste the iron in the water column long before I reach it.

I move. Locomotion is an act of violence and grace. I compress my northern vertex, collapsing my perfect pyramid into a flattened, erratic disk. The water rushes away from me, and I shoot forward, a jet of biology in a sterile world. Then, I release. My surface tension snaps me back into a tetrahedron, and I glide, momentum carrying me through the steam.

I approach the vertical face of the cavern. Up close, the obsidian is not black; in this blinding glare, it is a mirror, reflecting the white hell of the cavern. But I can feel the texture of the life upon it. The "iron carpet"—the rust-colored velvet of thermophilic bacteria. It clings to the sheer rock, drinking the chemical leaks from the stone.

I align myself, a dart of crystal aimed at the wall. I drift closer, until the chemical signature of the biofilm is a scream in my membrane. I do not bite. I have no mouth.

I land softly, a delicate kiss against the scalding stone. The underside of my geometry begins to unravel. I secrete a localized solvent, a mild acid that rolls off me and onto the rust-colored mat. The bacteria liquefy, turning from a velvet crust into a rich, metallic soup.

I absorb it. The iron-rich slurry passes through my silica skin, mingling with my saline interior. The energy transfer is instantaneous. My internal fluids grow warmer, denser. My body, previously clear as the air itself, clouds over. I turn a smoky, opaque quartz, glowing faintly with the metabolic processing of the stone-eating life. I am full. I am heavy with the energy of the earth’s blood.

I push off the wall, snapping back into the flow of the water.

Below me, the floor is a chaotic ruin of tumbled slabs. The light there is different—shattered into a million angular shards by the water’s surface. And in the deepest crevices, I feel them.

The Sleepers.

I sense them not as individuals, but as distortions in the Great Hum. They are the elders, those who grew too large to maintain their geometric perfection. They have slumped into the cracks, sprawling, irregular puddles of immense intellect. They no longer graze; they have become the filter at the bottom of the world.

I hover above a deep fissure, feeling the vibrations of a Sleeper anchored in the dark. Its mind—or whatever chemical analogue it possesses—is no longer distinct. It has merged with the thrum of the engine below. It vibrates in perfect harmony, a singular note in the symphony of the machine. It is dying, and it is more alive than I will ever be. It is breaking down, returning its complex salts and proteins to the water to feed the bacteria that I will one day eat.

I feel a strange pull. The temptation to sink. To let my own geometry fail. To let my vertices soften and melt, to slide down into the crack and dissolve into the humming resonance of the deep.

But the heat kicks in. A fresh drip from the ceiling strikes the water nearby, sending a ripple of pressure through my body. The ripple tells me of a shift in the air currents, a disturbance in the far corner of the nave.

Another.

There is another Architect nearby.

The pull of the Sleeper vanishes, replaced by a cold, sharp curiosity. I am solitary. The math of this place dictates that two of us cannot exist in the same quadrant without starving the iron carpet. To see another is an event. A statistical anomaly.

I turn in the water, shifting my volume to steer. I move toward the disturbance, my body snapping from a sphere to a cube to a pyramid as I cut through the heavy, wet air.

I see it—a glint of smoky quartz moving against the black rock. It is hunting, just as I was. We are two accidents in a room built for machines, two crystals of salt and purpose drifting through the blinding white.

We drift closer. The hum of the engine seems to rise in pitch, bridging the gap between us. We do not greet each other. There are no names here.

We merely align.

I extend a corner of my tetrahedron; it extends a facet of its own cube. We touch. For a moment, our membranes merge. There is a flash of data—a chaotic torrent of genetic history, of maps of the iron carpet, of the memory of the hum. It is a digital handshake performed in slime and light.

And then, we separate. The geometry reasserts itself. We are two distinct shapes again, drifting apart into the white, wet silence.

I leave behind a tiny, perfect sphere of gel—a child, a question, a new experiment in survival. It sinks rapidly, vanishing into the cracks, seeking the heat and the darkness where it might one day begin.

I turn back to the open water. I am clearing now, the iron digested, my transparency returning. I am a glass prism again, invisible in the glare. I am a mathematical dance, a crime of passion, a beautiful error enduring in the house of the machine.

I wait for the next drip. I wait for the hum to shift. I am the Architect. I am here.
