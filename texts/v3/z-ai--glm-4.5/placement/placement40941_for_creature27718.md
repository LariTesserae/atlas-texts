I am the stillness in the scream. I am the place where the roar goes to die.

I do not live on the walls, where the Striders cling in desperate fear of the churn. I do not hang from the ceiling like the pale, silent Anchor-Maws, waiting for death to fall into my grip. I exist in the **Void**—the immense, hollow cylinder of air between the pulsing obsidian walls and the crushing upward thrust of the Engine.

I am the **Volt-Skimmer**.

To the casual eye, I would be invisible, a phantom in the gloom. I am shaped like a manta ray, but one evolved for a medium of heavy, wet air and violent pressure, not water. My "wingspan" is easily forty feet across, vast sheets of translucent, leathery muscle stretched over ribs of dense, conductive cartilage. My skin is not skin; it is a matte-black capacitor, a biological solar panel designed to drink the dim light and the harsh static.

I feel the Oculus not as noise, but as texture. The grinding of the piston is a physical pressure against my belly. The stroboscopic lightning is a warmth that prickles along my wingtips.

I live my life in the seconds between the ticks of the clock.

When the Engine inhales—the piston dropping into the dark, creating a vacuum that threatens to suck the lungs out of smaller creatures—I am the diver. I tuck my vast wings and plummet. I do not fall; I dive with purpose. My body is streamlined, heavy with iron-rich blood. I ride the vacuum down, deeper into the dark, toward the churning water and the heat. This is my hunting phase. I do not eat flesh. I eat the *charge*. The air down here is thick with ionized particles, heavy with the taste of copper and ozone. I open gills along my underbelly—fluted, delicate vents that strain the air, filtering out the pure electrical potential. I drink the lightning before it even strikes.

Then, the shift.

The hydraulic groan signals the exhale. The piston heaves upward, a tower of black rock rising faster than the eye can track. The air displaces, becoming a solid wall of upward force, a hurricane blasting toward the pinprick of gray light.

This is where I belong.

I snap my wings wide. The "fabric" of my wings catches the updraft not like a sail, but like a circuit board closing a loop. The force is tremendous—it would tear a Gale-Bat apart. But I am built for it. I am the kite that surfes the tsunami.

As I rocket upward, I become a sanctuary.

The Gale-Bats—those frantic, screaming kites of wet silk—sense my rising. They are fragile things, easily dashed against the obsidian walls by the violence of the storm. They flock to me. Dozens, sometimes hundreds of them, tuck themselves into the "shadow" of my wake, drafting behind the immense turbulence of my wings. I am the moving island in the stream. In return for my protection, I take something from them. Not their lives, but their static. The friction of their thousands of tiny wings rubbing against the air generates a faint charge that bleeds off them. My skin absorbs this bleed-off. It is a tax I levy for the safety of my draft.

We rise together, a screaming spiral of life ascending the cathedral shaft.

As we near the zenith, the pressure peaks. The air is so thick you could swim in it. The static builds until my very bones hum. The Spark-Looms on the walls are glowing, their webs tight with voltage. This is the moment of apex.

I am the discharger.

Just as the piston threatens to choke the room with pressure, I release the energy I have gathered. My body glows—not a soft, fungal glow, but a harsh, violet arc. I become a living lightning rod in the center of the room. I broadcast a massive electromagnetic pulse, a counter-frequency to the Engine's roar.

For a split second, the noise cancels out. The grinding stone is silenced by the hum of my release.

This burst serves two purposes. First, it discharges the volatile static in the air, preventing a wildfire that would scorch the moss and kill the Striders. I regulate the weather. Second, my violet pulse acts as a beacon. In the blinding white fog of the venting steam, when the Gale-Bats cannot see, they "see" me. I am the magnetic north in the chaotic storm.

The fog rolls in, blinding white. The steam hisses. I stretch my wings to their fullest limit, catching the condensation on my back, drinking the water through my pores. The piston drops. The vacuum returns.

I fold, and I dive again.

I am the Volt-Skimmer. I am the battery and the lightning rod. I am the calm eye in the center of the grinding machine. I turn the violence of the world into the energy that keeps it alive.
