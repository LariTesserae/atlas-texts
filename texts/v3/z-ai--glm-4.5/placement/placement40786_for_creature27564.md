I am the swimmer in the air. I do not walk on the knotted mattress of roots, nor do I cling to the rusted ribs of the old machines. I inhabit the space between—the heavy, humid column of atmosphere that acts as the ocean of this world.

The air here is thick enough to support me. It is a aerosol soup, a suspension of water and spores so dense that a creature with the right buoyancy can float in it as if in a sea. I am that creature.

**Form and Substance**

I am a disc, a flat, circular ray of wet muscle and translucent skin, perhaps four feet across. I have no bones; my structure is maintained by the pressure of the fluid inside me and the density of the air outside. If you were to shine a light through me—though such direct, harsh light rarely penetrates the canopy—you would see the silhouette of my internal organs, drifting like dark islands in a glass jar.

My upper side is a mottled camouflage of rust-orange and moss-green, mimicking the patina of the iron pipes and the bark of the vine-pillars. My underside is pale, a sensitive belly of white cartilage.

I possess no tail to propel me, nor wings to flap. Instead, I possess a single, continuous ruffle of skin that runs around the perimeter of my disc. I ripple this muscle in slow, rhythmic undulations, a motion that pushes against the heavy air with the efficiency of a jellyfish moving through water. I move with a terrifying slowness, drifting forward inch by inch, silently navigating the gloom.

**The River Inversion**

I live in the thermal currents generated by the rot and the machines. The massive flywheel in the center of the clearing, half-swallowed by wood, generates a column of faint heat as the decomposition in its hub creates a slow compost burn. This heat rises, pushing the humid air upward.

I ride this invisible river. I spend my days circling the flywheel, drifting upward in the warm draft until I hit the underside of the canopy leaves, then letting the cooling air slide me back down toward the root-floor. I am a satellite in a perpetual, slow orbit around the decaying heart of the Sump.

**Sensory World**

I cannot see clearly in the aquatic gloom. My eyes are simple pits, capable only of distinguishing the shadow of a vine-pillar from the dim light filtering from above.

Instead, I navigate by taste and pressure.

My entire body is coated in a slime that absorbs the chemical signature of the air. I can taste the salt content of the rising water, the metallic tang of the rusting pipes, and the sweet, cloying explosion of an orchid blooming fifty yards away. I navigate towards the sweetness.

I also possess a lateral line—a series of sensitive pores running down my center—that detects the vibration of the thrum. The Sump is a loud place, a deafening roar of water and settling metal, but I have evolved to ignore the constant drone and listen only for the anomalies. I hear the *ping* of a water drop hitting a metal plate, and I can feel the pressure wave of the Stagnant Walker shifting its weight on a pipe arch.

**Ecology: The Filter of the Mist**

I am a filter-feeder, but I do not strain water. I strain the air itself.

As I drift, I draw the thick atmosphere into a ventral slit. Inside, a series of fine, bristle-like combs catch the suspended debris: the drifting pollen, the spores of the fungi, the floating dust of oxidized metal, and the motes of organic detritus that swirl in the lower levels. I compress this dust into a nutrient-rich paste, sustenance in a world where solid food is scarce to a thing that cannot touch the ground.

I am the reason the air in the upper Sump is relatively clear, despite the density of the vegetation. I am the lung-cleaner of the cathedral.

**The Danger of the Drift**

Existence as a drifter is a life of constant, quiet tension. I am exposed.

I must never stop moving. Stagnation is death. If I were to stop, the Pneuma-Moths might mistake my pale underside for a lantern and try to land, their needle-proboscises pricking my skin. Worse, the Bolt-Head Weavers might mistake me for a stationary surface—a strange, fleshy part of the architecture—and attempt to anchor their resin nests to my back.

But the true terror comes from below.

The Stagnant Walker cannot fly, but it can reach. Occasionally, as I drift in my slow, sleepy orbit near the pipe-arches, I will feel a sudden, sharp displacement in the air pressure. The thrum will change pitch.

I will ripple my edge, trying to gain altitude, but I am heavy with the air I breathe. From the darkness of the arch, a long, moss-covered arm will move with a speed that belies the creature’s sloth. It does not claw at me; it simply waits. If I drift too low, the Walker will open its mouth, creating a sudden vacuum that sucks me, struggling and rippling, toward its maw.

I have lost family to the Walker. I have seen the dark shapes of my kind suddenly vanish into the green gloom, swallowed by the furniture of the forest.

**The Symbiosis of the Glass**

To protect myself, I have formed a strange partnership with the decay of the past.

I seek out the shattered remains of the glass gauges and observation windows that litter the Sump—fragments of the old world that persist even as the iron rusts. I rub my soft, vulnerable belly against these shards until they cut me. In the open wound, I embed a shard of glass.

Over time, the glass heals into my skin, a jagged, transparent shield. It is a painful armor, but it is necessary. When the Stagnant Walker tries to swallow me, it bites down on glass. It spits me out, confused, leaving me torn and leaking but alive.

I am a creature of wounds and scars, a floating mosaic of broken glass and wet muscle.

**Where You Would Find Me**

If you stood in the Sump-Garden and looked up into the emerald gloom, you would likely miss me. I look just like a falling leaf that forgot to fall, or a patch of shadow that has detached itself from the wall.

But if you listened—really listened—past the thrum and the drip and the groan of the flywheel, you might hear a faint, wet sound. *Schwup. Schwup.* The slow beat of my ruffle as I push through the thick air.

Look for a rusted pipe that vents a thin stream of warm steam. There, hovering in the updraft, you will see me. A flat, jagged disc, suspended in the wet breath of the earth, filtering the history of the machine through my gills, waiting for the darkness to solidify enough to swallow me whole.
