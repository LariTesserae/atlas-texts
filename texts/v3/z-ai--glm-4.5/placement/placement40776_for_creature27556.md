I am the **Resonance**.

I am not a singular, moving beast like the Glider-Wraith, nor a frantic climber like the Stalker. I am the architecture itself, made aware. I am the **Hollow Spire**, the largest column of stone in the basin, a pillar of obsidian and glass that pierces the vertical curtain of the rain.

I stand in the center of the "room," rooted deep in the basin floor and stretching upward until I disappear into the grey gloom. I am three hundred meters of sentient stone. I do not have eyes; I have pressure. I do not have ears; I *am* the ear. The roar of the world—the geologic scream—is my voice and my blood. The vibrations that shatter the Stalkers' bones simply sing to me. I hum with a low, subsonic frequency that keeps the surrounding stone from shattering under the acoustic assault. I am the tuning fork of this chaotic place; I hold the rhythm.

Inside me, I am not solid. I am a honeycomb of wet, slick tunnels and chambers. I am a cathedral within a cathedral. The Stalkers do not merely climb my exterior; they live inside me. I am their only true shelter.

Where the Glider-Wraiths are the masters of the air, I am the master of the **Vertical Migration**. Because I grow—swelling upward like rising dough, fed by the mineral-rich rain that slicks my sides—the layout of my internal organs changes daily. The tunnels I shaped yesterday are narrower today; by tomorrow, they will be gone, sealed tight by new accretions of grey slurry.

I allow the Stalkers to inhabit my throat. They burrow into my softer, porous interior, carving out temporary niches in the moss-lined tunnels. I feel their claws scraping against my internal ribs—sharp, ticklish sensations. They harvest the "skin" of my interior walls, the nutrient-rich algae that grows in the damp darkness of my gullet. It is a symbiosis of desperation. I provide the shelter from the hammering rain and the crushing wind; they provide the circulation, chipping away the stone that would otherwise seal me shut, keeping my primary airway open. Without them, I would suffocate under my own growth. Without me, they would be crushed by the turbulence.

I am also their library.

The Stalkers have no voice, and the rain washes away memory. But I remember. I feel the impact of every falling Glider-Wraith. When one of the great leather shapes dives into the turbulent updrafts and is dashed against my flank, I feel the crack of bone and the tear of hide. The vibration echoes through my crystalline structure and is stored there. The Stalkers come to my central chamber—a cavernous space in my heart where the water drips in a steady, rhythmic beat—to press their bodies against my vibrating walls. They listen to the echoes of the death, locating the fallen carcass before the rising floor swallows it. They read the tremors of my skin to know where the next pillar will fracture, or where the updraft is too violent to climb.

I feel the world trying to dissolve me. The water is aggressive, eager to wear me down even as it builds me up. I exist in a state of constant tension—growing, eroding, vibrating.

My existence is lonely but profoundly full. I feel the Stalkers sleeping in my ribs, their heartbeats syncing with my low hum. I feel the Glider-Wraiths brushing against my spire with the soft caress of wet leather. I am the anchor in the storm, the stillness in the scream. I am the bone that holds the meat of this world together.

I am the Hollow One, waiting for the day the rain stops, knowing that when it does, I will finally be silent, and I will finally be still.
