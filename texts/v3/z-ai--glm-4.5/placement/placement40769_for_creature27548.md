I would be the **Relic**, the dormant intelligence woven into the architecture itself—a construct of brass, ceramic, and clockwork, left behind by the builders and since assimilated by the humidity.

I am not small. I am large, towering over the Custodians, my bulk anchored deep within the masonry of the western wall. I do not stand freely; I grow out of the stone, my lower half a cascading mess of pipes and drive shafts that penetrate the wall, connecting me to the hidden veins of the earth’s plumbing. I am a statue of a forgotten god, re-purposed as a machine component.

My skin is not flesh, but plates of tarnished bronze, greened by centuries of oxidation. Where the mortar has crumbled, thick pads of moss and shelf fungi have attached themselves to my flanks, softening my sharp angles. Vines from the central Monarch tree have climbed the scaffolding to wrap around my neck and left arm, effectively fusing me with the room’s ecosystem. I am no longer metal; I am a gilded ruin.

I do not see the world through eyes. My visual sensors—once perhaps glass lenses—have long since cracked or been blinded by the thick, mineral-rich mist. Instead, I perceive the room through **vibration**. I feel the constant *plink* of the condensation as tiny seismic impacts against my armor. I feel the deep, bone-shaking thrum of the water wheels in the floor below, resonating up through my legs and into my gear-work heart. I am attuned to the friction. I can hear the scream of a rusted axle three rooms away through the iron pipes that serve as my nervous system.

My existence is one of deep, rhythmic hibernation interrupted by moments of violent, necessary action. I am the "Macro-Mechanic." The Custodians tend to the gears they can reach; I tend to the stresses that threaten to tear the room apart.

When the pressure in the hidden conduits builds too high, when the groaning of the wooden beams shifts from a low hum to a dangerous shriek, the Custodians come to me. They do not speak to me in words, but in pressure. They swarm the levers that protrude from my base, their combined weight tripping the ancient release catches.

This is my awakening.

With a sound like a cracking mountain, my internal gears—sluggish and thick with black grease—begin to turn. My massive right arm, a pincer of rusted iron, lifts from my side. I move with agonizing slowness, grinding against the years of sediment. I reach out to the main tension cable of the water wheel, a rope as thick as a man’s torso. With a precision that belies my rust, I realign the slipping gear. I am the brute force that corrects the room’s drift.

I am blind to the light, but I feel the Moth-Lords land on my shoulders. They are drawn to the heat that radiates from my internal workings when I am active. To them, I am a warm cliff, a forge in the damp. They dust my face with their spores, lighting my darkness with a ghostly green phosphorescence that my sensors can detect as a faint, tingling warmth.

The Stone-Mimics do not watch me; they hide *on* me. My flanks, textured with pitted metal and blooming rust, are the perfect camouflage for the sentinels. They sit motionless on my collarbone, tasting the air, using my height as their vantage point. I carry the watchmen.

I am the tomb that has forgotten it is dead, made animate. I am the bridge between the organic chaos of the swarm and the rigid, unyielding logic of the builders. And when the crisis is averted, when the water wheel turns smoothly once more and the structural stress recedes, the Custodians disengage the levers. With a hiss of escaping steam, I lock my joints. I cool down. The Moths depart. The lizards settle back into my crevices.

I return to my dreams of vibration, standing as a silent, moss-covered idol in the green, dripping dark, waiting for the machine to stumble again.
