I find myself suspended in the throat of the world.

I am not standing, for there is no solid ground beneath my feet, only a rope woven from the glass-like filaments of the silk-vines, thick as a man’s torso and singing in the gale. I am hanging upside down, my elongated limbs hooked securely around the braided fiber, my multi-jointed toes locking with the effortless precision of a raptor’s talon. My tail—the heavy, counterweighted rudder of my existence—drifts beneath me, swaying gently in the updraft, acting as the anchor that keeps me pinned against the sky.

I am a **Resonant**.

I am the Keeper of the Great Lung, the central vertical shaft of the City. This is my domain: the vast, hollow cavern at the city’s heart, a cylindrical void stretching from the polished mirror of the floor up to the blinding zenith. It is the chimney of the Ocular.

My skin is alive. It is not the soft, porous hide of a creature from a wet world, but a shifting mosaic of silica plates and photovoltaic cells. As I hang here, bathed in the torrent of white-hot light, I can feel my skin drinking the violence of the sun, converting the searing radiation into the cold, electric fire that pulses through my veins. To look at me is to look into a shattered mirror; I am a being of sharp angles and refracted rainbows, camouflaged perfectly against the glare. I do not glow; I *am* the light, returned.

I am deaf to the sound that would shatter a lesser mind. The roar of the convection current—the supersonic wind that screams up the throat of the Lung—is a physical pressure against my chest, a rhythmic hammering, but it is not noise to me. I possess no ears, no vulnerable canals to rupture. Instead, I hear through my bones. The hollow, honeycombed lattice of my skeleton is a tuned instrument, vibrating in sympathy with the wind.

Right now, the city is singing in the key of Survival. It is a low, thrumming major chord, felt deep in the cartilage of my ribs. The wind rushing over the thousands of bone-flutes and raw-hide tensioned bridges of the outer city creates a harmonic frequency that stabilizes the structure. If the pitch were to waver, to slip into a discordant minor key, the vibrations would shake the brittle towers into dust.

My role is to ensure the pitch holds true.

I feel a tremor through the soles of my feet where they grip the rope. It is a stutter, a micro-fracture in the airflow. A thermal spike is building in the lower quadrant, near the Ironwood groves.

In a blur of motion, I release my hook. I do not fall; I dive. I plummet down the central shaft, arms spread wide, the wind filling the webbing of my membrane-thin underarms. I control my descent not by fighting the air, but by vibrating my tail, using it as a rudder to carve through the updraft. To an observer, I am a streak of diamond light falling into the abyss.

I land on a narrow observation deck made of polished basalt, skidding to a halt using the friction of my calloused palms. Here, the roar is a physical wall. Before me stretches the complex machinery of the city's intake: a massive array of levers and vanes made from the bleached ribs of the crystal-trees.

The vibration is wrong. The "tongue" of the main flute—a massive slab of obsidian that regulates the airflow—is sticking. The heat has expanded the stone, jamming the pivot.

I approach the mechanism. I do not use tools; my body is the tool. I wrap my long, multi-jointed fingers around the overheated stone. My skin is insulated against the burn, but I feel the heat radiating into my bones. I plant my feet against the basalt floor, digging in my toes, and I pull.

I pull with the slow, grinding strength of the earth itself. My muscles, dense and fibrous as steel cables, bunch beneath my crystalline armor. The obsidian groans, a sound I feel as a tooth-rattling buzz in my shins.

I am not alone in the effort. I feel a rhythmic tapping on the floor beneath me. *Thrum-thrum... pause... thrum-thrum.*

It is another Resonant, working in the tunnels directly beneath this deck. We cannot see each other, separated by layers of bone and mud, but through the stone, we are one mind. The tapping is a request: *Wait for the beat.*

I align my breathing with the thrum of the city. I wait for the peak of the harmonic cycle, the split second where the vibration loosens the stone’s grip.

*Now.*

I heave, timing my strength with the surge of the wind. The stone slab shifts. A screech of tortured rock tears through the roar, but the vibration in my bones smooths out instantly. The discordant rattle vanishes, replaced by the pure, clear hum of the major chord.

The air intake stabilizes. The thermal spike is vented.

I exhale—a sharp hiss of hot air—and tap a response on the floor: *Hold.*

The response comes back instantly: *Holding.*

I am alone again. The silence of the Ocular is impossible, but the silence of the Resonants is absolute. I look out through the crystal shutters of my eyes, protected from the blinding stare of the sun. Below me, far down on the glass floor, I see the tiny, skittering forms of the Echo-Runners. To them, I am a god of light and noise; to me, they are mere static, random fluctuations in the data stream of the world.

I am tired. The effort of moving the stone has drawn heavily on my energy reserves. I need to feed.

I climb back up the rigging, moving with the slow, liquid grace of my kind. I find a cluster of "fruit"—dense nodules of metallic salts that have grown on the copper vines surrounding the deck. I pluck one, cracking it open with my mandibles. It tastes of battery acid and ozone, a sharp, electric tang that sends a jolt of power through my nervous system.

My work here is done. The city sings true.

I make my way to my sleeping place, a hammock of woven copper wire suspended high in the upper rafters of the Lung. I hook my limbs over the metal, letting my heavy tail dangle free. I do not sleep like a creature of flesh; I do not dream of soft grass or cool rain. Those things do not exist.

As I hang suspended in the blinding brightness, my body begins to sync perfectly with the city's vibration. I become part of the architecture. I close my crystal shutters, and inside my mind, the roar resolves into a single, perfect tone. I am the tuner, the listener, the component.

I am the silence inside the scream. I am the Resonant. And the Ocular is open.
