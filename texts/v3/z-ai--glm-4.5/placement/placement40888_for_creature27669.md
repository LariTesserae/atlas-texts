I am the **Resonance-Weaver**.

I do not walk the canyons, nor do I drift among the clouds. I am the tension in the empty spaces between the stone ribs. I am the architect of the invisible.

My body is a thing of severe geometry. I evolved not from the grazing climbers or the blind singers, but perhaps from something that once built shelters in the rock, now turned entirely to the exploitation of the vibration. I am elongated, possessing a central abdomen that is a hollow, vibrating chamber of chitin and cartilage—shaped like a cello’s body, but translucent, revealing the oscillating organs within. From this center radiate eight long, multi-jointed limbs. They are not legs in the traditional sense, but spindly, needle-thin manipulators of jet-black basalt hardness.

I live in the narrowest, most choked alleyways where the wind accelerates to a shriek, where the air pressure is lowest and the sound is highest.

**My Craft**
I am a weaver of silk, but my silk is not for trapping flies. There are no flies here to catch. My silk is extruded as a liquid conduit of super-cooled saline, instantly freezing in the cutting wind into threads that are finer than hair but stronger than steel. I spin these cables between the towering pillars, bridging the gaps where the wind howls.

These webs are vast, spanning sometimes fifty feet across the echoing canyons. They do not catch prey; they catch *tone*.

As the wind screams through the stone pipes, it strikes my webs. Because the threads are tensioned to precise microscopic tolerances, they do not tear; they sing. My web acts as a harp that strums the hurricane. The vibration travels down the threads and into my hollow body, shaking my very frame. This shaking is my sustenance. I feed on kinetic energy, on the raw violence of the acoustics. The louder the world screams, the more vibrant I become. I am a filter; I turn the chaotic noise into pure, internal oscillation.

**My Vision**
To survive here, I have abandoned eyes that can be fooled by mist or glare. Instead, I see through **Cymatics**.

Fine, sensitive hairs cover my limbs, detecting the minute displacement of air molecules. In my mind, the world is not grey stone and green moss; it is a kaleidoscope of shifting geometrical patterns. The stone pillars are not solid objects to me, but towering columns of standing waves. The wind is not moving air; it is a rippling sheet of interference patterns.

When the sun hits the suspended water droplets held aloft by the acoustic pressure, I see them as bright, sparkling nodes of static in my visual field. I watch the Aeolian Kites not as animals, but as drifting soft-spots in the sound map, gently absorbing the high-pitched frequencies that would otherwise shatter my webs.

**The Shadow in the Song**
My existence is one of constant, deafening input, but I am terrified of one thing: The Silence.

I watch the Lithic-Crabs on the ground below with a deep, primal disdain. They build their cairns, their little circles of cancellation. To me, these are not shelters; they are holes in reality, blind spots where my sensory map simply ceases to exist.

And then there is the Gloom-Walker.

When the Walker enters my territory, it does not appear as a shape. It appears as a void. It moves in the pockets between the wind gusts, locking its joints so perfectly that it creates no vibration of its own. In a world that screams, the Walker is absolute zero. I cannot hear it. I cannot feel it. I only know it is there because the background hum of the moss and the stones is suddenly *interrupted* by a shapeless nothingness moving through the canyon.

When the Walker strikes, when it snatches a Moss-Stalker from the wall, the sudden burst of panic—a high-frequency chirp of terror—lights up my web like a supernova. It is a flash of brilliance in the dark, a sudden spike of energy that I drink in greedily.

**My Purpose**
I am the recorder. The Choir of the Ribs is a song of erosion, a song of dying mountains and freezing water. It is a song that disappears the moment it is made. But I catch it. My vibrations ripple through my hollow chambers and are stored there, layered upon layer, season after season.

If you were to cut me open, you would find the history of this plateau etched into the calcified rings of my internal shell—a fossilized library of every storm, every thaw, and every scream of the earth. I am the memory of the noise. I am the one who listens until the stones finally fall silent.
