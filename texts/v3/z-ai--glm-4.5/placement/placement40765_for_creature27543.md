I would find myself in the **Glass Wastes**, the shimmering, chaotic wake left behind by the passing of a Basalt-Back. This is the zone of immediate aftermath, where the raw, grey stone has been sheared away and replaced by the unnatural, obsidian slickness of cooling slag. It is a treacherous, beautiful place—a black mirror broken into a million jagged shards, reflecting the slate-grey sky in fractured distortions. The air here is thicker, heavy with the smell of sulfur and the residual heat of the giant’s internal furnace, a pocket of sweltering humidity trapped within the world’s freezing indifference.

Here, I am the **Chime-Walker**.

I am a solitary stalker, standing tall on legs that are impossibly thin and long, adapted to bridge the gaps between the unstable sheets of glass. My body is gaunt, covered not in scales or fur, but in a thick, felted coat of grey asbestos-like down that muffles the sound of my movement and protects me from the biting wind. I resemble a heron constructed from cast iron and dried clay, designed for patience rather than flight.

My purpose is to act as the counter-force to the giants’ creation. Where they melt the stone into rigid, indestructible glass, I break it back down.

My head is small, dominated by two oversized, radar-dish ears that swivel independently, tuned not to the roar of the beasts or the wind, but to the specific, high-pitched *tinks* and *pings* of cooling glass. As the Basalt-Back moves on, the slag it leaves behind cools rapidly, contracting and shattering under its own internal stresses.

I hunt these fractures.

My beak is a specialized tool, a heavy, piston-driven chisel of hardened tungsten-carbide. I move across the slick obsidian with a dancer’s grace, stepping silently until I locate a vein of tension in the glass. I rear up, balancing on spindly legs, and deliver a single, precise strike.

*DING.*

The sound is clear and piercing, a bell-tone that rings out above the low thrum of the distant industry. It is the signal for my work to begin. The glass, already weakened by the thermal shock, splinters. I strike again, rhythmically, walking the line of the fracture, breaking the massive, dangerous sheets of slag into smaller, manageable pebbles.

I am the agent of erosion in a world that has no time for it. By shattering the glass, I am beginning the process of turning the mountain back into sand. The debris I create falls into the fissures and cracks, filling the voids with grit. This is the soil from which the bruised-iron lichen will eventually grow. Without the Chime-Walker, the plateau would be a prison of smooth, impassable blades; I am the one who roughens the world, making it habitable for the small.

I am often accompanied by the Gutter-Runners, though they are not my friends. They scurry around my feet, snatching up the still-warm shards I knock loose, storing the heat in their pouches. They are the impatient consumers of the present; I am the gardener of the distant future.

When the glass is particularly stubborn—thick veins of volcanic obsidian that refuse to crack—I have a secondary adaptation. Along my spine are a series of resonating chambers. I can inhale the ozone-rich air and, by closing my throat, force the air through the chambers to produce a low-frequency hum. When I press my chest against the glass and vibrate, the resonance shakes the molecular structure of the slag until it weakens, allowing me to deliver the final blow.

In the perpetual twilight, I am a grey ghost, a rhythmic tapping moving through the mist. I am the silence between the giants’ footsteps, the chisel that chips away at the factory floor, slowly, tirelessly, turning the industry of the beasts into a canvas for life.
