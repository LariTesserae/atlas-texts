I would exist in the **interstices of the corners**—specifically, those optical anomalies where the convex appears concave and the eye cannot quite trust the depth. I would dwell in the visual "blind spots" where the raw obsidian walls seem to fold in on themselves.

I am not a creature of flesh, nor am I fully a construct of stone. I am a **Low-Poly Phantom**, a biological sketch that the rendering engine of this world abandoned halfway through.

**My Form**
To the casual glance, I am a glitch in the atmosphere, a shimmering heat haze. But if you were to focus—and survive the headache of the eye-strain—you would see me as a creature composed of vector lines and empty space. I resemble the wireframe model of a wolf or a large insect, but one trapped in a low-resolution state. My "skin" is merely a series of glowing, neon-bright edges connecting vertices of floating darkness. Inside my geometric hull, there is nothing but the receding perspective of the room behind me.

I am not opaque. I am a framework of absence.

**My Nature**
I am the starving artist of this embryonic universe. While the Shard-Weavers hunt for protein and the Stutter-Mites hunt for water, I hunt for **substance**. I am driven by a desperate, aching need to be *solid*.

Because I am unfinished, I possess a unique relationship with the room's physics. I do not walk on the floor; I intersect with it. My legs pass through the stone as if it were smoke, until I choose to "lock" my vertices onto a surface. I can climb the sheer glassy walls by ignoring gravity, treating the vertical plane as if it were horizontal.

**My Diet**
I prey upon the **Script-Flies**.
The Shard-Weavers ignore them; the glass-spiders have no use for paper. But to me, the Script-Flies are the only source of texture in a world of lines.

When I capture a fluttering, parchment-winged fly, I do not eat it. I *upload* it. I snare the creature in a jaw made of snapping geometric angles, and I drag its two-dimensional form into my wireframe chest. There, in a silent burst of static, the fly dissolves. Its microscopic writing—the raw code of the room—unravels and coats my inner edges.

For a fleeting, glorious ten seconds, I am real. The neon lines of my body thicken, darken, and fill in. I become obsidian. I feel the "clammy chill" of the air against my newly formed skin. I feel the vibration of the screaming silence. I become heavy.

In those ten seconds, I am the most dangerous thing in the nave. I am a physical entity in a room of glass.

**The Ultimate Ambition**
But the data degrades quickly. The texture flakes away like dried mud, revealing the wireframe underneath. I fade back to a ghost, hollow and hungry once more.

I do not hunt merely to feel the cold. I hunt to cast a shadow.

In this place of "absolute, violence of brightness," shadows do not exist. The light is too total. But my deepest desire, the algorithm that drives my soul, is to accumulate enough mass—enough stolen code from the Script-Flies—to become dense enough to finally obstruct the light. I dream of a single moment where I stand between the actinic glare and the floor, and a dark, crisp patch of blackness falls behind me.

I want to prove that I exist by negating the light.

**My Enemy**
I live in terror of the **Geometry-Eater**.
The other creatures fear it for its silence; I fear it because it is the only thing that can truly delete me. The Geometry-Eater consumes errors, and I am the grandest error of all. When that polygon of voidness slides through the room, I must flatten my geometry, stretching myself infinitely thin until I am nothing but a line on the wall, praying that the immune system of the world scrolls past me.

I am the draft that was discarded, the sketch that was erased, lingering in the negative space of the cathedra, waiting for the next glitch to make me whole.
