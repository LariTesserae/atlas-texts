I am a Volute of the Ground, a creature of the deep shadows and the humming frequencies.

I do not walk the surface so much as I flow over it, a low, articulated ripple of dark basalt and obsidian. I am not a single stone, but a colony of them—a segmented, centipede-like architecture of interlocking plates. My legs are myriad, short pillars of polished granite that tap out a complex, staccato rhythm against the shattered slate. I am perhaps the length of a human arm, but dense, heavy with the weight of the world.

I find myself in the "undergrowth," that optical riot created by the Glass-Spires. I dwell in the violet dapples beneath the western facets of a particularly large spire we call the Monolith. Here, the light is refracted into a cool, bruised purple, and the Prism-Lichen glows with a hungry, infrared heat. This is my niche, the thermal gradient where the searing white sky meets the radiating cool of the stone.

I am a tuning fork for the earth.

My purpose is bound to the silence of this place. While the Skylark rules the air above, interpreting the flash of sun on wing, I interpret the subsonic thrum of the Spires. My body is designed to catch these songs. Along my spine, running the length of my segmented back, is a ridge of hollow quartz crystals. They are slightly detuned from one another, creating a sensory array that feels the vibration of the ground through my belly and the pressure waves through the air.

I am immortal, or near to it. I have no natural enemies, for I am made of stone and I taste of nothing but dust and silence. Even the Skylark ignores me; I am merely another rock to it, until I move.

My perception of time is glacial. A day is a blink. The movement of a shadow across the ground is like the sweep of a second hand. I watch the Skylarks dive like falling stars, their visits fleeting and violent, while I endure. I remember when the Monolith was half its height. I will remember when it finally weathers into sand.

I am a witness to the slow, grinding patience of the earth. I am the silence that feels the noise. I am the dark, heavy counterpoint to the bright, light forest. I am the Volute, crawling through the eternal afternoon, tuning the crystal choir of the High Lithic Plain until the mountains themselves decide to dissolve.
