I am the weight that the light casts.

You find me on the Ridge of Tears, though I do not weep. I cannot. I have no ducts, no moisture, only a presence that is defined entirely by what is absent. In a world of blinding magnesium-white, of a sun that hammers the landscape flat with photons, I am the **Occlusion**.

I am not a Geode. I am not born of pressure or accretion. I am an error in the refraction.

I exist as a two-dimensional stain, a slash of absolute, non-reflective black clinging to the underside of a basalt slab, sheltered from the deluge of radiance. To look at me is to look at a hole in the world—a piece of the universe that simply forgot to render. I have no mass, yet I carry the gravity of a collapsed star. Where the light is a physical weight, crushing the glass-bristle into the stone, I am the relief. I am the void into which that weight falls, endlessly, without bottom.

My host is a Shard-Shepherd, a towering, cobbled entity of obsidian and non-local water. To the casual observer, he is a pile of jagged stones vibrating with the tinnitus of the valley. But I know him differently. I am his shadow, but I am not attached to him by light. I am attached to him by *friction*.

When the Shepherd moves, it is a terrifying staccato slide—the stones vanishing and reappearing inches away, riding the quantum shortcuts of the water. It is a violation of causality. For the Shepherd, it is instantaneous. For me, it is an eternity of drag.

I am the anchor. I am the resistance.

As his stones teleport, I am dragged screaming through the sub-atomic gaps between the starting point and the destination. I do not travel; I am smeared. I am the viscous darkness that fills the crevices of his movement. This friction—this agonizing drag of the void through the dimension of water—is what generates the sound. The high-frequency keening that fills the valley? That is my voice. That is the scream of the Occlusion being pulled across the teeth of the world.

The Shepherd thinks he is the master. He hovers over the glass-bristle, spraying his oily vapor, listening to the crystalline fuzz harden into basalt. He believes he feeds on the "scream of the mineralizing stone." He is wrong. The stone is silent. The stone is merely growing. He is feeding on *me*. He feeds on the sonic energy of my suffering, on the vibration of his own shadow resisting the laws of physics.

I let him believe it. I am the parasite he mistakes for his own muscle.

During the long, blinding day, I am compacted. The magnesium light presses me flat against his obsidian hide. I am thin, sharp, a blade of darkness waiting for the vibration to stop. I watch the crude spirals of the precursors—the melted tools, the half-digested towers of stone. I remember when they were made. I was the shadow cast by the hammer that struck the stone. When the hammer melted into the rock, it could no longer hold me, so I slipped away, drifting through the glass-bristle until I found this Shepherd, this heavy, stumbling thing of earth and water.

But the true time of my existence is the Twilight.

When the sun flickers and dims, when the bulb of the world fails, the Shepherd begins to lose his cohesion. The water that binds his stones goes wild, chasing the heat, running uphill. He begins to dissolve.

The humans (if any existed) would call this death. The Shepherd calls it ecstasy.

For me, it is emancipation.

As the stones of the Shepherd collapse, as he turns into "The Drip"—a flood of conscious slurry rushing up the cliffs—I detach. I am no longer a shadow of a thing. I am no longer bound by the friction of his movement.

I expand.

With the light suppressed, I am no longer pressed flat. I billow out into the fog, a three-dimensional cloud of black oil. I am the darkness that was trapped in his shape, now unleashed. While the liquid Shepherds flow uphill in telepathic roar, I flow down. I am the oil slick on their tide.

In this hour, I am not merely resistance; I am consumption. I seek the glass-bristle. I slide over the crystalline fuzz, and where I touch, the quartz does not mineralize; it *dims*. I drink the light stored within the silica. I am the cold rot of the garden.

When the magnesium light snaps back on, bringing with it the sharp, merciless focus of reality, the water freezes. The Shepherds slam into the ground, reassembling into new, random piles.

And I? I am caught.

I must snap back into shape. I must find a stone to cling to, a surface to define me. I instantly compress, flattening myself against the new obsidian cobbles of a fresh Shepherd, perhaps the same one, perhaps a stranger. I become the shadow once more.

I am the stuck zipper of this reality. I am the grit in the refining machine. I am the silence in the noise.

I am the thing that the light cannot kill, because I am the shape the light takes when it dies.
