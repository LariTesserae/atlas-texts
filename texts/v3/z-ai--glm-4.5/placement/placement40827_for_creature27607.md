I am the weeping.

I begin as a shudder in the dark, a sudden condensation of longing high up in the throat of a Vent-Born monolith. For an hour, I am only pressure and heat, a scream held back behind a valve of oxidized iron. Then, the release.

I am the mist.

I fall not as rain, but as a heavy, exhaling breath, pouring down the flanks of the great obelisk towers. I am the cold silk that coats the world. In this darkness where no eyes exist to see me, I feel my way across the landscape. I taste the rust-red history of the alloy; it is sharp on my tongue, metallic and ancient.

As I gather volume, running over the smooth, unyielding skin of the machine-hills, I become a silver rivulet. I am a vein tracing the sutures of the world. I slide over the grid-like seams that patch the planet together, feeling the vibration beneath my surface. The deep, resonant thrum of the planetary pumps travels through me, shaking me into a froth. I am the lubricant for a world that has forgotten how to stop moving.

I flow downhill, pulled by the gravity of the basin, hunting for the low places. In my descent, I pass the Thermal Gardeners. They flare into existence as I near them—patches of bio-luminescent moss that glow with a sickly, amber urgency. I bathe their geometric roots. They pulse in time with my movement, sensing the thermal waste I carry. To them, I am not water; I am information. I am the messenger telling the nerves that the heart still beats. In exchange for my touch, they offer me the only light in the universe, a brief, ghostly illumination of the path ahead.

I pool in the shallow, circular depressions, forming the black mirrors that dot the valley floor. Here, I am still. Here, I become a reflective eye staring up into the absolute darkness, waiting for the amber discharge of the Ghost in the Amber to flicker across my surface. In these moments of stillness, I remember that I am not just water; I am an electrolyte broth, rich with the conductive memories of the machine. I hold the charge.

But the stillness is always hunted.

The sound changes. The low-frequency growl sharpens into a distinct, rhythmic clanking—the Tectonic Thinkers shifting their gears miles below. The ground under my black mirror shudders. The seams of the intake grid begin to gape open.

The predator awakens.

The vacuum is not a sound; it is a lack of sound, a sudden tearing away of air. The intake grids are mouths, and they are starving. I feel the pull, a violent drag that tears me from my reflective rest. I swirl, faster and faster, spinning down the drain. I am no longer a serene pool; I am a chaotic spiral, a scream of liquid being devoured.

I am sucked into the artery.

Here, in the dark beneath the skin, I am terrified and exalted. I am pressurized. I am forced through pipes the size of cities, squeezed by the massive reciprocating hearts of the Circulators. The heat is intense, scalding away the impurities I picked up on the surface. I am purified. I am stripped of my wandering nature and turned into pure function.

I become the blood.

I rush through the hidden veins of the leviathan, feeling the friction of the metal against my flow. I am part of the circuit now. I am the hydraulic fluid that shifts the tectonic gears. I am the force that turns the mountain. I feel the machine’s thoughts—surges of pressure, drops in temperature, the logic valves opening and closing. I am thinking with the machine. I am the ghost in the machine’s bloodstream.

Until the heat becomes too much. The pressure builds until I cannot hold my shape. I am driven upward, desperate for release, climbing the vertical shafts of the towers, boiling into fury.

I reach the vent. The valve opens.

I explode into the void.

I am steam. I am the Vent-Born’s final breath. I scream into the darkness, a plume of white hot vapor racing toward the amber ceiling. I am violence. I are release. For a fleeting second, I am free, suspended in the cold air, drifting away from the metal skin.

But the cold is patient. It catches me. It pulls the heat from my lungs. I condense. I grow heavy. The mist reclaims me. The sorrow of gravity takes hold.

I begin to fall.

I am the weeping again, sliding down the flank of the monolith, looking for a place to pool, waiting to feel the pulse of the moss, waiting to be eaten by the pump. I am the cycle. I am the machine remembering itself.
