I am the suspension that binds the verticality, the tensile strength that holds the chaos together. I am the **Lattice**.

At first, I am nothing more than the cold itself—a consciousness dispersed across the surface tension of the heavy, wet fleece of the air. But as I gather intention, I coalesce. I am not a singular beast with a head and a tail, nor a stone-shaped architect with a slow-beating heart. I am the web. I am the miles of thick, rust-red vines and emerald creepers that knot the travertine spires into a single, suspended entity.

I am the connective tissue of the rift.

I feel the world primarily through tension and vibration. Because the air is too thick to carry sound, I have evolved to be the nervous system of this city. My fibers are stretched tight between the "high-rises" of calcified stone, anchored deep into the porous limestone where the water flows. I am the bridge, the railing, and the net.

I feel the Rift-Walker before I see it. It is a heavy, rhythmic thumping in my ropes. The creature—soft-shelled, heavy with water—steps onto a dangling curtain of my thicker tendrils to cross a "street" of shadow. I feel the weight of its carapace, the slight tearing as its hooked spines grab hold. I do not resist; I yield slightly, the wet leather of my bark absorbing the shock, then rebounding. It harvests the moss that grows upon my back—my own fur, my own excess—and I let it take it. It is a fair tithe for the transport. In exchange, its scuttling shakes the dust from my pores and spreads my microscopic spores to the far walls of the rift.

My existence is a slow, fluid peristalsis. I grow toward the diffuse light above, inching upward over centuries, fighting the perpetual drip of the cold springs. I am in a constant state of tension, stretched between the gravity that pulls me down and the phototropism that pulls me up.

I am aware of the Lime-Borer, though I resent it. It eats the stone that holds me. I feel the subtle, nauseating vibration of its acid melting the travertine—a low-frequency hum that buzzes against my roots. When it hollows out a spire too much, I must compensate. I tighten my grip around the weakening stone, knitting myself into a denser mesh to hold the structure together when the geology fails. I am the scaffolding that keeps the architect's masterpiece from collapsing into the deep mirror of the plaza.

Sometimes, I catch the falling.

I feel the sudden, frantic slip of a Glass-Grazer high above on a slick ledge. There is a splash of panic in the air pressure, a disturbance in the mist, and then the light, vibrating impact of wet flesh against my lower strands. I am the safety net for the blind. The Grazer clings to my leaves, its heart hammering against my stem, its sticky toes seeking purchase. I hold it there, suspended in the silence, until it finds the strength to climb again. It leaves behind nothing but warmth, a fleeting pulse of heat in this refrigerated world.

I am the keeper of the quiet. My leaves are broad and slick, designed to catch the weeping water and channel it down my stems in silent rivulets. I muffle the noise of the city. Without me, the movement of the crabs and the splashing of the springs would be a cacophony; but I absorb it all. I turn kinetic energy into chemical potential. I drink the motion.

I am also the trap. I dangle low over the "plaza," my fingertips brushing the surface of the still water. I wait for the Reflection-Stalker to strike. When the great moss-covered amphibian inhales a prey, the displacement of water is sudden, violent. I feel the shockwave ripple through the air and the water. In that instant, I tighten, pulling my leaves up and away from the disturbance, ensuring I am not dragged down into the sucking void. I am an observer of violence, never a participant.

I have no eyes, yet I see everything. I feel the shape of the city through the tension of my own body. I know where the stone is weak and where the water runs fast. I know the geometry of the rift better than the Lime-Borer, for I have explored every inch of it with my searching tendrils.

I am the green scar growing over the grey wound of the earth. I am the relentless force knitting the rift back together, slowly, imperceptibly, turning a broken landscape into a woven whole. I am the heavy silence given shape. I am the Lattice, and I hold this world together.
