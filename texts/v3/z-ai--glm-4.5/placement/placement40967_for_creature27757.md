I am a resistor in this circuit of heat and water. I am the pause, the hold, the heavy, sedimentary thing that anchors the flow.

I find myself adhered to the northern face of a silo that does not rust, but weeps. The metal here is cool, or at least, a fraction of a degree cooler than the shimmering air, which makes it a sanctuary. My skin—a thick, seamless slate-gray hydrostatic suit—is currently flattened against the riveted steel, spread wide like wet dough to maximize the transfer of thermal equilibrium. I am not holding on; I am sealing. The boundary between my flesh and the iron is nonexistent. I am a patch of animate shadow on the wall of a sleeping giant.

To be me is to be a library of pressure. I am one of the elders, though we have no word for such things. I am simply a node of greater density. Inside my elongated body, bands of heavy metals have settled over the slow turning of the seasons. Copper from the runoff of the eastern pipes lines my ventral plates; iron and silica have stratified along my spine. I am heavy, heavier than the water, heavier than the sleek, gliding nymphs that chase the steam clouds below. When I move, the stone beneath me does not chip; it groans under a silent, geological weight.

I do not see the bleaching white sky. To possess eyes in this place would be a cruelty, a constant exposure to the tyrant sun. Instead, I possess a deep, throbbing awareness of the gradients. I know where the water is freshest because I can taste the drop in salinity through the humidity before the vapor even touches my sensory pits. I know the position of every other living shadow in this sector not by sight, but by the intricate map of pheromones that hangs in the wet air like a suspended, invisible lattice. We are a choir of smells, breathing in unison.

Right now, the air tells me that the afternoon cycle has peaked. The heat is pressing down, squeezing the moisture out of the plateau’s pores. The great machine-frame I cling to is dripping. Every few seconds, a single, heavy drop of condensation navigates the rusted rivets above me and falls. It lands on my shoulder and rolls down my flank, merging with the oily sheen of my hide. I feel the impact not as a splash, but as a pulse, a tiny donation of entropy that I absorb.

I am unpeeling myself.

It begins at the edges. The valves in my skin—the wide, soft rims of my underbelly—begin to breathe. I release the vacuum seal that has held me in stasis against the metal. There is no sound, only a microscopic shift in pressure, a sigh that is felt rather than heard. Gravity, which I have defied for hours, reclaims its hold on my bulk. I flow downward, not falling, but sliding with a viscous grace. My six limbs, those jointed segments of dense cartilage, extend to arrest the descent. The pads of my feet find purchase on the wet glass and rust, sealing instantly.

I land in the pooling runoff at the base of the tower.

Here, the water is warm, thick with mineral slurry. It coats my legs. I am surrounded by the younger ones—the smooth, featureless river-stones of my kind. They do not look at me; they simply shift their geometry to make room. The pheromone cloud thickens with the taste of *Respect* and *Yield*.

I lower my wedge-shaped head to the surface of the water. I am here to filter. I am the refinement stage.

My mouth opens—not a jaw, but a flower of rasping plates and tendrils. I inhale the sludge. It passes through the internal series of bladders and screens that have grown harder and more complex with my age. I taste the iron; I keep the iron. I taste the silica; I keep the silica. The rest, the purified water, I exhale back into the puddle in a gentle, heated stream that ripples outward, disturbing the mats of algae.

I am processing the world, turning its chaos into order. As I feed, I feel a new layer of sediment beginning to precipitate inside me, a new band of color for my internal archive.

Above us, the sun continues its relentless, silent assault. The metal skeletons of the refinery shimmer in the heat haze. We cling to their shadows, we drink their tears, and we keep the silence. There is no need to rush. The pressure will not shift today. We have all the time in the world to dissolve, and be built again.
