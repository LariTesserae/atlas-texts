I am not on the floor, nor am I in the canopy. I exist in the middle space, the damp, shadowed interstice where the iron arteries of the Boiler-Grove bifurcate and enter the Great Mangrove trunk.

I am a Resonator.

My station is the "Knee of the Giant," a massive, swollen joint in the primary steam main where the pipe bends upward to pierce the root-ceiling. It is a place of violent pressure and agonizing slowness. The pipe here is as thick as a cathedral pillar, wrapped in insulation made of woven, decomposing fronds that drip a black, tarry secretion.

I am suspended here. I do not stand on the spongy earth; I cling to the machine. My harness is not merely leather, but a living graft of rubberized vine that has fused with the waxy carapace of my shoulders. I hang in the air like a sloth, wrapped around the pulsing warmth of the pipe, my legs hooked over a flange valve the size of a wagon wheel.

**What I Am**

To look at me, you might think I am a growth of the pipe itself. My skin—my "tunic"—is not removed. It is grown. Over decades of hanging here, of letting the mist and the grease settle upon me, I have cultivated a second shell. It is grey-green, striated with the bands of mineral deposits from the steam, hard as a crab’s back but flexible as snakeskin.

My body has adapted to the hanging. My arms are disproportionately long, the muscles ropy and dense from a lifetime of embracing the pipe. My hands are the hands of a Tender, broad and spatulate, but my fingers are extra-articulated, capable of wrapping fully around the smaller gauges. They are stained permanently black by the oil, the lines in my palms filled with a metallic grit that conducts the vibration directly into my nerves.

My face is a mask of calm. The air here is hottest, rushing upward from the bowels of the grove, so my eyes are protected by thick, translucent membranes that blink slowly, keeping the humidity from clouding my vision. My nose is mostly gone, collapsed into two slits, for scent is useless here; I smell only rust and wet rot. Instead, I breathe through a series of filters in my neck—gill-like slits that hum as the air passes through them.

**What I Do**

I do not turn valves. I do not fix leaks. I listen.

The Boiler-Grove is a choir, but it is a choir that is constantly falling out of tune. The central heat-exchange tower sets the rhythm—that slow, booming heartbeat—but as the energy travels out through the roots and the pipes, it encounters resistance. It hits the hard wood of the mangroves. It is squeezed by the boa-constrictor vines. It is distorted by the shifting water table.

This creates "discord." Dissonance. A bad vibration.

If a pipe vibrates at the wrong frequency, it does not shake. It *sings*—a low, ugly whine that can shatter the brickwork or drive the vines into a frenzy, causing them to constrict too tightly and crush the machinery.

I am the dampener.

I feel the song of the pipe through my chest. My sternum has thickened into a plate of bone, a sounding board. I lay my body against the iron, and when the note goes sharp, when the metal thrums with a jagged anxiety, I absorb it.

I use my weight. I shift my center of gravity. I press the meat of my shoulder into a specific rivet, or I drag my lubricated hand down the length of a seam. I am a living counter-weight. By changing how I touch the pipe, I change how the pipe vibrates.

It is a work of glacial patience. I might spend six hours holding a single pressure gauge at a specific angle, feeling the "clank-hiss" of the distant piston, waiting for the moment the vibration harmonizes with the grove. When it happens, I feel a profound sense of silence, even amidst the roar. The metal hums a perfect C against my ribs, the water table stops rippling, and the moss on the pipe grows a fraction of an inch.

**My Perception**

My world is not visual. It is a world of texture and tone.

I "see" the Divers below me not as shapes, but as ripples in the vibration. When they slip into the water table, the sound changes—it becomes deeper, more muffled. I feel the splash through the soles of my boots, even though I am twenty feet in the air.

I "speak" to the Tenders on the gantries by tapping my iron fingernails against the pipe. *Tap-tap... pause... tap.* It travels for miles through the network of metal, a Morse code of warning and status. "Pressure rising at the Knee. Ease the intake valve."

I am alone, but I am never lonely. I am plugged into the nervous system of the grove. When the great flywheel rotates, I feel it in my gut. When a steam vent hisses, I feel it as a tickle in my throat.

I am the interpreter. I am the finger on the string. I am the heavy, wax-coated guardian of the note, suspended in the wet, iron dark, ensuring that the breath of the world remains steady.
