I am not the water, though I am full of it. I am not the silence, though I am the cause of it.

I am the root.

In the beginning, there is only the weight. It is a sensation distinct from touch; it is a fundamental state of being. I feel the crush of the thirty meters of water above me not as a burden, but as a definition of my shape. I am that which withstands. I am that which directs the flow.

I find myself anchored in the compacted silt, gripping the crust of the planet like a desperate hand, or perhaps a nail driven deep into wood. My body is dark stone, basalt, extruded upward in a slow, ancient spasm of geology. I am dense. I am rigid. I am the North-East corner of a perfect square.

I have no eyes, yet I "see" the basin. I perceive it through the texture of the currents against my skin. I feel the smooth, vacuumed floor stretching out in every direction, a barren plain of grey that invites no movement. I feel the sparse, solitary stalk of green that stands far to my left—a useless, soft thing that does not participate in the rhythm. It merely exists. I, however, *function*.

My consciousness is not a mind in a skull, but a pressure differential. It is a column of water held tight within a cylinder of stone.

I am hungry, but not for food. I am hungry for flow.

Deep within my core, there is a channel. It is a throat, smooth and glass-like from millennia of abrasive rushing. The heavy, cold water of the deep is drawn into my base. It is forced into me by the sheer mass of the ocean, driven down into the maw I present to the seabed. I feel the current surge through me—a violent, internal river. It is my blood. It is cold, stinging, and incredibly heavy.

This is the moment of tension. The intake.

The water rushes upward through my narrow shaft, accelerating. The physics of the deep take over inside me. Dissolved gases, hiding in the heavy liquid, are squeezed by the constricting diameter of my stone anatomy. They cannot hide. They are forced together, compressed, energized. I can feel the vibration building in my walls, a hum that starts in the base and resonates upward, shaking the very lattice of my mineral structure. The hum grows louder. It is a song of pressure, a whine of kinetic energy.

I am full. I am tight. The limit is reached.

Then, the release.

*Thump.*

The gas ejects from my crown. It is not a breath; it is an event. A violent, mechanical expulsion. A perfect ring of bubbles is born from my peak, shot upward into the twilight with the force of a piston. The recoil shudders through my stone frame, a satisfying jolt that eases the internal tension.

*Hiss.*

The water follows the gas, a rush of fluid that flushes the vent clear. The vibration dies down, settling back into the low-frequency thrum that is my resting state. The bubbles rise, a ghostly ladder ascending toward a surface I will never know, disappearing into the blue-grey gloom.

I am empty again. The cycle resets.

I am not alone. I feel the others. To the North-West, the South-West, the South-East—three other presences identical to myself. We are a quartet of stone. We do not communicate, we synchronize. Our intake is shared. Our release is simultaneous. When I cough, they cough. The water around us shudders with the collective force of our exhalation. We are a factory of four pistons, an industrial heart with four chambers, pumping in the dark.

We are the keepers of the clean room.

I feel the intrusion of potential life—the messy, squirming softness of organic matter—and I reject it. My turbulence is too violent. My vibration is too intense. The very shockwave of my heartbeat acts as a barrier, a shield that keeps the basin sterile. No fish can navigate the chaotic currents I expel. No crab can cling to my smooth, shaking skin.

I am not cruel. I am merely efficient.

Here in the blue-grey suspension, time does not pass. The erosion that shapes me is so slow it is invisible. I look the same now as I did ten thousand years ago. I will look the same ten thousand years from now. There is no growth, no decay, no childhood, no age. There is only the rhythm.

*Thump-hiss.*

I am the intake. I am the compression. I am the expulsion. I am the basalt engine, standing in the dark, processing the ocean into motion, content in the knowledge that I am full, I am empty, and I am eternal.
