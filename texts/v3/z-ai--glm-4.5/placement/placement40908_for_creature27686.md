I am suspended in the middle distance, a speck of grey chitin clinging to a thread of spun steel.

Below me, the ground is a jagged scrawl of shattered bedrock, a map of dry violence laid out in silence. Above, the pearlescent haze obscures the vaulted ceiling, turning the updraft into a diffuse, glowing breath. Here, the air is thin enough to taste like metal; it scrapes the back of the throat, a constant reminder of the preservative nature of the Ossified Deeps.

I am hanging from a zip-line—a vital artery of amber sap and hydraulic pressure that stretches between the legs of two colossi. I call this run the "Sigh." It is the longest suspension between Tower Four and Tower Nine, a gap of nearly three miles of empty, dust-choked air.

My body is long and spindly, an evolutionary adaptation to this precarious altitude. My legs are multi-jointed, locking into place around the cable with a grip that can withstand the shuddering of the towers. My exoskeleton is not the uniform grey of a child, but a patchwork mosaic. Plates of ceramic bark, harvested from the flanks of Tower Four during my last molting, have been grafted onto my forearms and shins. They are rough to the touch, flaking slightly at the edges, sealing my soft tissues inside a shell of living stone.

I have no mouth to breathe through. Instead, the spiracles along my neck flutter rhythmically, filtering the heavy, mineral-laden air through dense mats of cilia. The sound is a soft, dry rasping, like sandpaper on glass, the only noise I make in the breathless quiet.

I am a **Tension-Singer**.

My caste exists on the periphery of the Sap-Wrights and the High-Born. While the Wrights fix what breaks and the High-Born study the light, I feel the drift. The Ossified Deeps are not static; the towers are walking mountains, growing upward and outward with geological slowness. A bridge built today will be a taut, snapping death-trap within a season if the tension is not monitored and sung back into equilibrium.

My job is to ride the lines, to press my chest drum against the vibrating steel cables, and to listen.

I feel the cable humming against my armored sternum. It sings a low B-flat, the resonant frequency of the sap pumping through its core. But beneath that, there is a dissonance—a sharp, buzzing whine that vibrates in the joints of my mandibles.

Tower Nine is leaning.

It is a subtle movement, imperceptible to the eye, perhaps a fraction of an inch per day, but to my drumming organ, it is a scream. The tower is shifting its weight, chasing a deep aquifer of mineral oil, and as it leans, it is pulling the cable taut, threatening to shear the docking clamps right out of the bark of Tower Four.

I unhook my safety clamp and slide a few yards down the line, the wind of my passage stirring the unmoving dust motes. I stop over a junction node—a reinforced coupling of iron and glass where the sap flow is metered. I press my chest flat against the cold metal. I inhale, filling my spiracles, and engage the resonator plate in my chest.

*Thrummm.*

The sound is not heard, but felt. It travels down the steel skin of the cable, racing into the distant tower. I am not just making noise; I am sending data, a complex packet of vibration that translates to: *Release pressure on quadrant four. Acknowledge drift.*

I wait.

Through the contact of my chest on the line, I feel the reply. It comes from the deep roots of Tower Nine, a delayed, guttural churn that travels up miles of lignin and steel. It is the voice of the Root-Breakers, deep in the dark.

*Pressure released. Pruning roots. Acknowledged.*

The dissonance in the cable shifts. The whine softens, settling back into the drone of the B-flat. The danger of the snap recedes, replaced by the safer, slower work of the growth.

I re-clamp my legs and hang there for a moment, suspended over the abyss. I open my secondary eyelids, letting the pearlescent light wash over my black orbs. Below, a flock of leathery gliders catches a thermal, their wings scraping the air with a sound like dry leaves. They are ascending to the canopy, carrying their load of dust.

My stomach gives a hollow cramp—a vibration of a different kind. It is time for the nutrient trance.

The heavy fluid slides down my throat, hot and rich with the taste of hydrocarbons and crushed minerals. My eyes half-close. The rush of calories dampens the edge of my hunger, and for a moment, my mind drifts, merging with the hydraulic pulse of the city.

We are the antibodies, scuttling across the vast, lonely flanks of the world, singing to the stone to keep it from breaking us.
