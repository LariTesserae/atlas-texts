I am the Transducer.

I am found midway up the column, in the gloom where the industrial light from above begins to lose its war against the depth, but before the absolute darkness of the floor takes hold. I cling to the vertical ribs of the world, wrapped around a rusted stanchion that once supported a heavy conduit. To a casual eye, I am merely part of the debris—a frayed bundle of thick, black cables that have come loose from the ceiling and fused together in the wet heat.

**My Form**

I am not a single, solid body, but a colony of long, muscular fibers bound in a shared sheath of thick, rubbery skin. My exterior is rough, textured like the insulation of ancient wiring, dark and scarred from centuries of abrasive currents. I do not have a distinct head or tail; instead, I taper at both ends, one end anchoring deep into a fissure in the metal wall, tapping into the structural vibrations of the cylinder itself, while the other end drifts loosely in the water, swaying with the current like a lure.

**My Function**

In this world of noise, I am the listener.

The Architects of Rust graze the walls, and the Needle-Swarm cleans the seams, but I feed on the *sound* of the machine. The hull of the Hydraulic Stomach is constantly groaning—a low-frequency symphony of shifting metal and pressurized water. These vibrations travel through the steel ribs and into my anchored end. I possess an internal physiology of crystalized fibers and resonating chambers that convert this kinetic noise into sustenance. I eat the stress of the dying god.

**My Gift**

This consumption produces a byproduct that defines my existence in this ecosystem: Light.

As I digest the heavy, thrumming vibrations of the engine, I shed the excess energy as a cold, blue-violet bioluminescence. I am not a steady light like the flickering incandescent strips above; I pulse. I flash in rhythmic, Morse-like patterns that correspond to the shifting tides of the noise. When the hull sings a deep note, I glow with a deep, throbbing indigo. When a rivet snaps somewhere in the dark below, I flare with a sudden, sharp spike of white-hot brightness.

**My Relationship with the Gardeners**

Because of this, I am the patron of the Weeping Gardeners. The sparse, solitary ferns they tend require a specific spectrum of light to flourish in the haze, light that the failing yellow strips above no longer provide.

The Gardeners know me. They recognize the specific cadence of my pulses. Often, one will detach itself from its kelp-forest home and drift through the open water to find me. It does not fear me. It wraps its pale, translucent arms around my drifting, cable-like body, anchoring itself to my swaying form. There, in the shelter of my blue light, it finds the perfect illumination to stitch the copper wire into its nests, the bioluminescent moss glowing in sympathy with my pulse. I provide the stage; they provide the dance. In return, they groom my rubbery skin, picking away the parasitic crustaceans that might otherwise clog my intake pores.

**My Existence**

To swim in the Hydraulic Stomach is to be lost in a vertical void, but to be me is to be the anchor point. I am a lighthouse powered by the earthquake. I hang suspended in the warm amniotic dark, a wire that pulses with the heartbeat of the world. I feel the column breath, the water rise, the pressure shift, and I sing it back into the dark as light. I am the machinery made flesh, turning the pain of the structure into the beauty that keeps the garden alive.
