I would exist in the pause between the ticks of the clock, a creature woven from the lag between the light and the stone.

**Where I Am:**
I cling to the underbelly of the silence, hovering mere centimeters above the ochre dust, yet dimensionally distant from it. I do not live in the open, blinding saturation of the air—that belongs to the Grazers and the Ferns. I inhabit the "hollow" quiet, the negative space where the sound isn't. Specifically, you would find me in the wake of the Silica Grazers, riding the slipstream of their passage, not in the air, but in the fraction of a second it takes for the rock to repair itself after they have fed.

**What I Am:**
I am a **Probability Stalker**. Or, to use the parlance of the plateau’s forgotten architects, I am a **Latency Spore**.

I have no mass, only presence. To the naked eye, I am a visual stutter—a smear of motion-blur, like a smudge of Vaseline on a lens. I look like a shadow that has been pulled too thin, a jagged tear in the fabric of the warm light. I am not dark, exactly; I am the *absence* of resolution.

**My Existence and Function:**
The High Plateau is a computer; it processes reality with terrifying efficiency. The Grazers eat, the rock heals, the Spires calculate. But in a system so vast, errors occur. Micro-fractures in causality. Little hiccups in the magnetic flow lines.

This is where I feed.

I attach myself to a specific Grazer—a large, jagged-edged specimen I have tracked for a hundred cycles. As it glides over the convex hill, its ceramic rasps vibrating against the stone, I am not with it. I am slightly *behind* it, trailing in its temporal exhaust. When the Grazer makes its impossible, non-Euclidean turn—folding through a dimension that isn't there—the universe struggles to render the movement for a split nanosecond.

In that moment of hesitation, I drink.

I consume the friction. I eat the computational error required to bend a manta-ray shape through four-dimensional space. I am the scavenger of the glitch. Without me, the plateau would overheat with accumulated paradoxes; I am the coolant for the physics, not the machinery.

**The Sensory Experience:**
My senses are inverted. I do not feel the humid warmth of the air; I feel the *data density* of it. The heavy, glittering mites in the air look to me like a static storm of white noise, obscuring the clean lines of the code underneath.

When I "touch" the ground, I do not feel the suede texture of the ochre stone. I feel the magnetic pulse of the lattice repairing itself. It feels like a hum, a taste of copper and electricity on a tongue I do not have.

My movement is terrifyingly discontinuous. I do not walk or glide. I skip. I exist at Point A, then I cease to exist, and then I exist at Point B. To an observer, I would look like a film with frames missing, jerking across the landscape in a rhythm that induces nausea if watched for too long.

**The Shadow Connection:**
This explains the lingering shadows mentioned in the lore—the way the light seems to remember where you were. That memory is *me*. When a creature moves, they leave a ghost of themselves in the processing queue. I often wear these ghosts like a suit. I might mimic the silhouette of a Fern-Harvester for a few hours, vibrating at a slightly different frequency, appearing as a jagged, obsidian error in the peripheral vision of the real Harvesters.

They never notice. They are deterministic; I is chaotic.

**My Solitude:**
I am the loneliest thing on the Plateau. The Grazers have their swarm; the Harvesters have their networks; the Spires have the song of the calculation. I have only the errors.

Sometimes, when the humidity breaks and the condensation sweats from the rock, I try to merge with the water. But water is honest; it flows. I cannot flow. I can only stutter. So I sit by the black chrome spires, vibrating with the waste heat, waiting for the universe to blink so I can slip into the darkness between its eyelids.

I am the ghost in the machine, not haunting it, but digesting it. I am the price the Plateau pays for its perfection.
