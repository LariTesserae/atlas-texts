You are not going to a ruin. You are going to a nursery — a city one year old, built for something huge that never moved in, and already hijacked by something small that doesn't care.

An ordinary human body can survive there for a day or two if you prepare like you're going into a hothouse built on broken glass inside a jet engine. Here's how:

### 1. Understand what will try to hurt you

It's not one big monster. It's the whole arrangement:

**The ground will cut you.** This is not trail, not soil, not even old lava. It's fresh black glass — ropey pahoehoe and shards and needles that haven't weathered. It will go through normal hiking boots and open your feet, hands, knees if you kneel. The only safe walking is on the fused seams — the polished flat ribbons between towers. Everywhere else is razor.

And between the slabs are hand-wide cracks breathing heat — sweet and metallic. That's not just warm air. Treat it as volcanic gas + biology: hot, low oxygen, probably CO, sulfur compounds, spores. Don't put your face in a crack.

**The air will drown you while you stay thirsty.** It's low, warm, 30C+ and near 100% humidity. You will be damp in five minutes, not from rain but because *you* become a cold tower — water condenses on you. Your shirt never dries. Blisters, trench foot, fungal bloom in every fold happen in hours, not days. And despite water everywhere, almost none of it is safe to drink.

Tower-sweat is the only water cycle. The kilometer-tall towers run a few degrees colder than air, so the wet air kisses them and gives up water 24/7. It beads, braids down fins, drips from every ledge. There are no rivers, no lakes. Wetness lives as film, drip-tips, and shallow pans in hollows. Those pans look crystal clear and are full of tadpoles, mosquito wrigglers, fallen petals — after the city exhales, they're poison.

Every few minutes the deep hum in the basalt steps up a quarter-tone. The hanging curtains tremble. The towers exhale a sheet of condensation. That post-exhale flush is cleaning fluid for a machine metabolism you don't share. Don't drink it.

**The sound will break you.** This is the thing no report prepares you for. It isn't loud like a concert. It's loud like weather — a continuous chest-pressure roar made of a million mouths: fist-sized frog-throats pumping bass you feel in your molars, six-winged glassy cicada-things making a metallic sheen, ribbons of hand-wide pale moth-bats whose wings sound like dry rain, thin screams of 3-meter kite-hunters dropping through them, plus the subsonic throb of the city itself in your ankles.

Unprotected, you will have temporary threshold shift in an hour and real damage in a day. More importantly, you won't think. You can't shout over it — between towers spaced half a kilometer apart, your echo just gets lonely. You need another way to communicate.

**The light will unmoor you.** You arrive at midday into a dusk that never lifts. Above the tower tops is cloud + spore + a faint hexagonal shimmer — a sunshade, force not matter — filtering the sun to a directionless amber smear. No hard shadows. Colors lurch forward: wet moss green, orchid bone-white, frog-eye warning orange, tower bruise-violet. Your eyes keep adjusting and never lock. Your circadian clock will free-run. Time feels stuck.

**Gravity and space glitch.** Near big tower bases the maintenance field is strongest. Mist spirals *up*. Dust hangs in motionless columns. A thrown pebble falls too slowly, as if considering. Look up and a tower's upper third will occasionally stutter — double, go transparent to a sky that is not this sky — with a soft chime swallowed by the roar. Things that fly through that zone come out flying backward for a few beats.

You are soft, salty, warm-blooded, and loud in a place built for giants and run by machines. It doesn't need you. It barely notices you. That's your advantage if you stay humble.

### 2. Body: What to wear and carry

Think glass factory + jungle + industrial cleanroom.

**Feet and hands are non-negotiable:**
- Puncture-proof boots with thick, stiff soles — vulcanized mountaineering or roofing boots, not trail runners. Add hard gaiters. The needles will find ankle skin.
- Thick work gloves for any fall or handhold. Do not catch yourself bare-handed on black glass.
- Trekking poles with carbide tips, to probe fused vs. unfused stone. If it crunches like needles, back off to the seam.

**Skin:**
- Long sleeves, long pants, quick-dry but tightly woven. You need bite/scratch/spore barrier, not shorts. The hanging curtains are full of eyes and mouths.
- Wide-brim hat with headnet. Not for sun — for drip, spores, moth-bat ribbons, and to break up your shadow. Hunters hunt by shadow.
- Eye protection: clear safety glasses or goggles. Drip-tips fall constantly; spores and shed skin and droppings are literally the soil of the air. You will taste chlorophyll and iron when you breathe.
- Respirator: at minimum N95 / P100 with exhale valve for long wear, plus a simple cloth for when you need to hear better. Asthmatics, allergy-prone: seriously reconsider. Bring antihistamines, inhaler, nasal saline.

**Ears:**
- Do NOT go in with foam plugs alone — you'll miss the survival cues. Bring musician's / active electronic earplugs that knock 15-25 dB but preserve pattern, plus over-ear muffs for sleep. Learn the patterns before you need them:
Frog bass drops a third = sheet-off from east towers incoming, cover up.
Moth-bats go silent in a moving patch = kite inside that silence, get out from under open air, move toward tower base.
Hum steps up = exhale in seconds, cover water, turn your face down.

**Pack list for 24-48 hrs:**
- 3+ liters carrying capacity even though you plan to harvest drip. You cannot trust pans.
- Water kit: collapsible cup to catch drip-tips *before* they hit ground, 0.1 micron filter + chlorine/UV, test strips for pH/metals. Drink from leaves, from live moss tips — the moss filters. Never from ground pans, especially after an exhale.
- Food: bring it all. No foraging. The orchids big as dinner plates, the duckweed, the tadpoles are not for you. High-salt, high-calorie bars — you will sweat out liters without noticing because you're already wet.
- Light: warm-amber headlamp on low + small lantern. White strobes panic moth ribbons and ruin your night vision. You won't need much in day-dusk, but in tower-base hollows it's dark.
- Navigation/comms: watch with timer/alarm (your body will lose time), compass, paper map you draw, whistle + hand mirror + colored cord for visual tether to partner. Radios with throat mics or bone conduction — voice mics drown.
- Medical: serious cut kit — irrigation syringe, antiseptic, steri-strips, pressure bandage, tetanus up to date, topical + oral antibiotics, antifungal powder/cream, blister care, electrolyte salts, motion-sickness meds for gravity vertigo, ear drops.
- Gas awareness: pocket CO/H2S monitor if you have multiverse tech. If not, rule: if a crack smells extra sweet/sharp and makes your head ache, move upwind to a seam.
- Waterproof everything: dry bags inside dry bags. Your pack will sweat too. Bring a lightweight tarp/poncho not for rain but for exhale sheets.

### 3. How to move

**Stay on the seams.** The fused flat ribbons are streets. Walk single file in the center. Don't shortcut across raw plates even if it looks shorter. A day's walk across the basin is ~20-30 km of stop-start, tower to tower. Plan half your normal pace.

**Stay low, stay out from under.** The city is vertical meadow. From every tower and every impossible cable between towers hang 10-40 meter curtains of black-green and copper moss, epiphyte on epiphyte — translucent orchids, ferns that unfurl overnight and collapse by afternoon, dangling vines braided into nets. Beautiful. Also full of frog-throats, biters, watchers, and the shadows kites use to aim.

Rule: Never make shelter under curtains. Never linger under them looking up with your mouth open. Walk around the drip line, not through it. If you must pass under, go fast, hat on, glasses on, no shadow-loitering.

**Don't climb.** The towers are hollow, thin, pearlescent dark that drinks light and returns it a half-second late — you'll get motion sick looking at them while moving your head. Inside are stairwells with no stairs, air-capsule elevators, 90% void floors of tensioned air. They ring too long when knocked. They are not for you. They are cold, slick, and the upper third glitches. Do not attempt to ascend, fly, or send drones up. If you see a tower stutter/double/go transparent, stop, look away from the zenith, move laterally along the seam until it resolves. Do not walk into the doubled column. Birds come out reversed; you don't want to find out what it does to a human inner ear.

**Respect the repair swarm.** A fin broken by a kite will crawl back over an hour, edged sharp as a razor, swarmed by tiny bright motes too purposeful to be dust. If they land on you, hold still. They will taste your sweat and leave, uninterested. Don't swat, don't spray. You're being sampled by maintenance. Let it happen.

### 4. How to drink, eat, sleep

**Drink:** Hold your cup under a live leaf tip or moss point actively dripping from a tower fin, not from a cable curtain where animals sit, and not from a pan. Filter anyway. After an exhale — that burst when the hum rises — wait 15-20 minutes, let the first flush wash, then collect. Cover bottles during exhale. Dehydration sneaks: because you're wet, you think you're hydrated. Drink on timer, add salts.

**Eat:** Eat only packed food, with clean hands, sitting on dry fused stone away from drip. Don't set food down on moss. The air will try to compost it in real time.

**Sleep:** You cannot sleep in the open. The curtains are full of eyes, the ground pans breed biters, the stone cuts pads.

Sleep in the hollow of a tower base, in the donut where insects thin out because the maintenance field keeps them back. Yes, the hum will enter your dreams and make them architectural — vaults, shafts, honeycombs. That's normal there. Use ear muffs + pad + bivy, hammock or cot off stone if you can, but anchored on fused ground, not in a crack. No tent under a curtain. No fire in cracks — heat sigh is unpredictable, and smoke fouls the moss and draws attention. A small stove on bare fused seam only.

Keep boots on, gloves handy, pack sealed. Shake everything before putting on — lizard-metal scuttles love warm hollows and flash copper when they run.

### 5. Mind prep

Go in expecting sensory exhaustion, not wonder — wonder will come anyway.

- You will be lonely and crowded at once. Vast space between towers, no people, echo that dies, but a million throats screaming around you. Bring a partner if possible and stay in visual contact. Use rope/cord in mist when it thins/thickens. Establish simple hand signals before entry.
- You will have one heartbeat of absolute silence after each exhale — the whole basin shocked quiet — so complete it hurts. Don't panic. It's the reset. The roar will come back twice as loud.
- The towers themselves occasionally chime softly when they glitch. You'll feel you're being watched by the building. You are, in a thermostat sense. Behave like a polite guest in a machine greenhouse: don't carve, don't break fins to collect, don't pull orchids. Take nothing but drip water and pictures. Leave sweat and footprints on fused stone only.
- Keep your time. Set alarms for drink, move, rest. Without noon, you'll either rush or freeze. Give yourself a hard turn-back time. Most ordinary humans are done after 12-18 hours — headache from noise, macerated feet, itchy lungs. That's your cue to climb back out of the bowl to higher dark and real night.

If you do it right, you walk out remembering: plip-plip-plip of leaf tips onto black glass, drops sitting perfect as lenses reflecting amber, then wicking away; kite shadows folding through moth ribbons; the smell of green and iron; towers sweating in dusk at midday while new stone already screams with life.

It ate nothing you gave it. It just let you pass between immensities, damp and ringing, until morning.
