This is not a sightseeing trip. It is a high, dark, wet, loud marsh sitting on newborn stone, built for things that do not see.

You, an ordinary human with eyes that want light, lungs that want thick warm air, feet that want dry flat ground, are wrong for it in every way. You can still go, for a day or two, if you prepare to give up seeing and talking and hurry — and to become a listening, dripping animal for a while.

Here’s how.

### 1. Understand what will kill you first

Forget predators. The order is:

1. **Cold + wet + altitude.** ~4,000 meters, -20 to -30C, killing wind, sleet like needles, and you will be standing ankle-deep in warm black slush. Exposed skin goes in minutes. Lost outside a heat envelope, you have about an hour.
2. **Air.** It’s thin, saturated, and poisoned. Your breath hangs and joins the fog because the air can’t hold any more water. That fog is condensate from vents, cisterns, thousands of bodies: silica, sulfur, ammonia, wet fur, hot stone, iodine. Low hollows pool CO2 and H2S under the pretty knee-high fog.
3. **Dark + noise + ground.** Permanent polar night, no moon, no streetlamps. And 40 Hz pillar drone in your molars, water ticking everywhere, wingbeat like tearing canvas, echolocation merging into frying hiss, screamers whistling down. You will have to shout two feet away. The ground is fresh volcanic glass — pahoehoe ropes, pressure ridges sharp enough to cut gloves, no soil, no channels — slick with living biofilm and sinking an inch a year into warm mud.

You do not adapt by toughing it out. You adapt by sealing yourself and turning your eyes off.

### 2. Your body before you go

Train for the climb, not the city. Hours up black basalt switchbacks to the rim with shallow lungs. Do cardio with a pack, stair climbs, breath work. If you can, sleep 2-3 nights at 2,500m+ before transit.

If you have uncontrolled asthma, COPD, heart failure, severe Raynaud’s, a pacemaker / implanted electronics, severe claustrophobia or vestibular disorder — do not go. The gravity lattice around the pillars makes dropped stones land a hand’s-width left. It will make *you* nauseous and off-balance. The low resonance causes anxiety, sleeplessness, tooth ache in some people. Implants have glitched.

Get: tetanus booster, flu, full wound care plan. You *will* get cut. The glass is new. The biofilms eat stone.

Acetazolamide for altitude on doctor’s advice, plus a tiny personal oxygen can or two for the rim ascent. Not for the city — you can’t walk around sucking a can in sleet — but for emergency.

Practice blindness. Spend hours blindfolded at home navigating by sound and touch. Practice not reaching for a light.

### 3. Seal: what to wear and carry

Think caver + arctic marsh + chemical plant, not hiker.

**Skin — no gaps:**
- Merino base + mid fleece + belay-grade down or synthetic parka with huge hood. Waterproof-breathable shell top and bottom, reinforced.
- Face: full balaclava + sealed ski goggles with anti-fog / heated lens if you can get them. Your glasses will fog instantly and die. Contacts dry and trap ammonia. Wear goggles. Sleet will sand your cheeks raw.
- Hands: cut-proof liner gloves (HPPE) under waterproof insulated mitt-shells. You will need dexterity to feel slick stone and ropes. Bring 3 pairs liners.
- Feet: This is where trips fail. Knee-high waterproof insulated boots rated well below -30C, with aggressive but soft tread for glass. Neoprene socks + merino socks, rotate daily. Gaiters. Bring trekking poles with carbide tips — you will probe for sucking slush and orange film before you step. Bring blister + trench-foot kit: zinc, dry powder, multiple socks in dry bags. Once your feet macerate in warm brine-mud and then freeze, you’re done.

Everything must resist puncture. The ground rings and crunches and cuts. Sit pad, sleeping pad, tent floor — all heavy duty.

**Breath:**
- Half-face respirator with P100 + acid gas / ammonia cartridges (P3 + ABEK if you can). Plus a simple humidifying balaclava / heat-exchange mask underneath so you aren’t breathing -25C knives directly. You’ll still smell ammonia and sulfur around the edges — that’s your warning.
- Pocket 4-gas detector: O2, CO2, H2S, SO2 that vibrates. In that roar you will not hear an alarm. Set it to buzz. If it buzzes in a hollow, get up-slope, up-wind, onto an arch.

**Ears — protect but don’t deafen yourself:**
Foam plugs will blind you worse than darkness. Get custom musicians’ plugs or electronic shooters’ plugs with ambient pass-through. You need to hear the drone change: lower = thick fog-bank coming, higher = ice about to heave. Bring bone-conduction headset + throat mic if you’re in a pair. Normal voice radio is useless. Learn hand squeezes and rope tugs.

### 4. Light discipline: keep it OFF

This is rule one in the valley and it’s not etiquette, it’s safety.

> Light blinds the roost and brings the screamers low.

The walls are living shingles of blind white moth-bats, fist-sized, layered three-four deep. A white-light flash wakes ten thousand. The long-necked screamers that ride thermals will drop to investigate.

Prepare:
- No white headlamp for walking. Tape over it. Bring it only sealed for true emergency wound care under a tarp.
- If you must see: passive thermal monocular with NO IR illuminator. It won’t help much — everything is warm wet stone and warm bodies — but it lets you find heat envelopes and open water without emitting.
- Learn to walk headlamp-off on the causeways. Keep a hand on the wall/rope, feel with feet. Your eyes will never adjust. Let them stop trying. By night two your face will read warm air as shelter, sudden quiet under an arch as dense roost overhead holding its breath, mineral tang as open water.

If someone in your party flicks on white light, kill it instantly and freeze. Don’t run. Let the air settle.

### 5. Heat is life. Learn the envelopes.

The city does not have streetlights. It has pillars — tall, slightly leaning, dull black, humming in your sternum. Between them are localized warmth envelopes that bend sleet. Beyond them is death.

- Never leave the terraced causeways on arches. Never cut across marsh-center in the dark. Your boots will crack scum-ice and suck. New cracks steam — that’s scald + gas. Old causeways sink.
- Plan hops pillar to pillar. Pause, warm your core, dry gloves. Count time outside. In wind, 40-50 minutes max exposed, then rewarm.
- Everything drips and condenses. Everything you own will be wet. Bring all dry clothes in roll-top dry bags inside dry bags. Chemical hand/toe warmers by the dozen. A bothy bag / emergency bivy that reflects your own heat for rewarming, not a tent — there is nowhere flat and dry to tent that isn’t someone’s farm or roost.
- Sleep: you will not sleep well. Drone + dripping + chitter. Bring high-NRR earplugs for sleep only, eye mask (though it’s dark), melatonin, and a totally waterproof bivy + insulated pad. Sleep elevated on causeway, never in a hollow. Hammocks tear on glass.

### 6. Feet, farms, and filth

Nothing green grows here. No trees, moss, grass. Only orange, rust-red, milky biofilms that eat stone and sulfur. Feel for them:

- **Do not step where it is slick-orange.** It’s alive, it’s food, it’s farm. Step around. The same for white milky mats near seeps. Apologize by not touching.
- Do not touch walls that look poured/frozen with vitreous sheen — they’re accreting a millimeter a year, self-healing. Oils from hands + a boot scrape ruins months.
- Do not eat anything local. Do not drink marsh water. Brine refuses to freeze because it’s loaded with silica and sulfur — it will wreck kidneys and gut. Carry all water in insulated bottles (tubes freeze). Carry 3,500-4,500 kcal/day of dense, no-cook food — you’ll burn huge calories shivering and wading. Pack out all human waste. Do not add to biofilters. They eat their own dead; they don’t need yours.
- Expect guano, fur, clicking shrimp in culverts so dense water crackles, ice-worms thick as your arm turning in mud, gulpers croaking. Don’t swat moth-bats. If a living sheet lifts — a hundred-meter section of wall detaching into air — crouch under an arch, cover mouth, wait. It’s weather, not attack. It moves slow.

Hygiene: sealed goggles + respirator also protect from fungal spores and ammonia burn. Any cut: wash immediately with clean water, seal with tegaderm, watch for angry red infection. The film wants stone; it likes wounds fine.

### 7. Move slow. Listen.

Nothing here happens fast, despite noise. Ice inches. Towers accrete. Roosts shift downslope over seasons.

Your human instinct to hurry, to look for change, will make you mad. Practice:

- Navigate by sound-shadow. Dense roost overhead = sudden muffled quiet. Open water = ticking louder, mineral taste. Fog-bank = drone drops lower in your chest.
- Probe, step, pause. The ground is still being born.
- Talk almost never. Agree signals beforehand.
- Keep a lifeline: rope team of 2-3 on causeways in fog. Personal locator beacon (satellite, not light) on chest, and a pealess whistle — useless at distance in roar but useful close.

Circadian collapse is real. No dawn is coming. Keep a strict watch schedule, eat on time, log sleep. Bring one small morale object that is green — photo, plastic leaf. You’ll miss green more than you think. No leaf, no blade anywhere, and life so thick you have to push through it.

### 8. People

There are maintainers, engineers who no longer explain pillars in your physics. They navigate without light and farm film.

Behave like a guest in a farm + temple:
- Ask before entering a terrace ring. Stay close, stay low, don’t lean on pillars.
- Don’t offer light to help. Offer warmth — share chemical warmers, help clear drips — and quiet.
- Don’t sample biofilm, dead moth-bats, water without explicit permission. Everything is some thing’s food.
- Hire a guide if you can. One local who hears drone-shift is worth ten instruments.

### Packing checklist for 2 nights

Worn: base + fleece + shell, balaclava, goggles, cut liners + mitt shells, insulated waterproof boots, gaiters, respirator around neck, gas detector, electronic earplugs, rope, poles.

Pack (all in dry bags):
- parka, 3x socks, 3x liners, underwear, dry sleep clothes
- respirator spare filters, sealed goggles spare, anti-fog
- water 3L+ /day + electrolytes, food, stove? probably useless — bring no-cook
- bivy + pad + bothy bag + warmers x20
- passive thermal (no illuminator), emergency white light taped, beacon, throat mic/radio
- first aid + foot care + 4-gas detector + pH strips
- tether, carabiners, 20m cord, knife, repair tape

Leave behind: white lanterns, laser pointers, drones, bright clothing that sheds microplastic, any plan to “summit” or “see the city.” You won’t see it. You’ll hear it long before you make it out, and you’ll feel it in your bones in center marsh with white wings moving over you like snow that never lands.

Go to listen and to be wet and cold and humble. Keep your light off, stay in the warmth, step only where stone is bare, and leave when your feet tell you to — before the slow dark convinces you to stay.
