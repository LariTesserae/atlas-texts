You do not belong in there. That's the first preparation.

You are a large, wet, noisy animal that cools itself by sweating and needs to drink every few hours. This is a small, dry, hot room that has arranged itself over a very long time around the rule: *nothing leaves, nothing needs.* No water, no animals, no eating. You are a violation of all three just by crawling in. You can visit, briefly and respectfully, if you prepare to be the problem and minimize yourself.

This is how.

### 1. Understand what it is

From outside: nothing. A knee-high blister of basalt in a noon-white salt pan, cracked on one side. You will miss it standing up. You find it by crouching.

Inside: one room, about 20 x 30 paces, ceiling low enough to touch on tiptoe. Furnace-hot but dim. Light only from the crawl-slit and 2-3 needle-holes in the roof. Brown-red ember-dark that your eyes slowly learn to see in.

The air is absolutely dry. No seep, no well, no condensation. Your breath dries before it leaves you. Your lips will crack in minutes. The walls radiate steady heat like a living flank. The air tastes of hot dust, iron, and wax.

And it is lush and loud. Waist-high to a crouching person, a miniature jungle of things that drink no water: black-green to amber leaves lacquered with wax an inch thick, fronds ribbed with silica that clink like glass, needle-leaf cushions that store heat. They tick and pop as they expand. Every few minutes a pod goes *tak!* and throws dry spores that hiss down on lacquer. Ash-flags groan as superheated draft cycles in and out of the slit. Roots creak. Dust settles.

In the middle: a tiny town. Straight swept main street, terraced rectangular mud-brick houses with fingerprints still in the bricks, low doorways, stone-footed, flat-roofed with woven mats now half-swallowed by plants. Mortar and pestle, stick loom, seed-cakes hard as pottery, basalt flakes, hearth rings, thorn awls, a second story here, a retaining wall there, a dry cistern repurposed as seed-store. No metal. No bones.

No one lives there now. No lizard, no fly, no rat, no mites. Seed-cakes ungnawed. It was left, not destroyed. Pots set down, mats folded, street swept one last time. And the plants have kept the agreement: they lean in but do not choke the street. Roots go around foundations, not through. A plume grows out of a hearth and uses it as a trellis without toppling it.

Stay until your eyes adjust and you see the last thing: each waxy leaf holds stored heat as a dull ember-orange glow at its core. The whole under-canopy pulses faintly as drafts pass. A town at perpetual dusk lit by living lanterns.

You will have to back out on your belly to leave.

Prepare for all of that.

### 2. Your limits as an ordinary human

You cannot stay. Plan for **10-15 minutes inside, max 20**, then out to cool and rehydrate. The heat is not a fire-blast, it's soaking, total, inescapable in a low closed pocket. Dehydration, heat exhaustion, disorientation come fast because there is nowhere cooler to step to.

Do not go inside alone. You need a buddy outside at the slit, in the shade, with water, who can pull you out. Sound echoes weirdly long inside for such a small room — establish tap / rope signals beforehand. You will not have radio through basalt.

Go at dawn or late afternoon outside time. The inside will still be furnace-hot — the rock holds old heat day and night — but you won't have to cross the salt-pan at noon-white, and you won't overheat before you even crawl in. Never go in midday heat if you can avoid it.

### 3. What to bring

**For the desert approach:**
4+ liters water per person, electrolytes, salty snack. Wide-brim hat, light-colored loose long sleeves and pants, sunglasses, sunscreen. GPS + compass + physical marker. That pan is empty and glaring and the dome is knee-high; people get lost *looking* for it and can't find their way back to the slit. Mark it temporarily and remove the mark after.

**For the crawl:**
Knee pads and elbow pads or a thick canvas ground cloth. Thin tough gloves — leather or rigger gloves. The basalt at the crack is weathered and sharp, the floor drops a hand's breadth inside, and everything inside is warm to hot. You will be on your belly pulling yourself. Wear long sleeves/pants you can get dusty and hot. Take hat and sunglasses OFF before you go in or you'll lose them. Tie hair back.

**For breathing:**
A real dust respirator — P100 / P3 half-face with sealed goggles. Not a cloth bandana. The air is hot dust + silica + wax + iron + spore dust from bursting pods. It will dry and irritate your nose, throat, and eyes in minutes. The pods burst with force — *tak!* — right at face height for a croucher. If you have asthma, reactive airways, or heat intolerance, do not go in.

Keep your water *sealed* inside. Drink outside before and after. Do not spill, spit, pour, urinate, eat inside. Even your sweat and breath moisture are contamination here. Breathe through the mask, slow and shallow, to trap what you can.

**For seeing:**
Dim amber or red headlamp on lowest setting + a small backup. Do NOT bring a blazing white caving lamp. You need your eyes to adjust to ember-dark to see the grain and the glow in the wax. Bright light kills that for 10 minutes and makes you clumsy around fragile growth. No open flame. No lighter, no smoking, no stove. Waxy leaves an inch thick + hot oil + dry husks + pure dry heat = this whole room wants to burn if given a spark.

Small thermometer / timer if you want — helps force you out when you feel fine but aren't.

Camera only. No collection bags. Those seed-cakes, thorn awls, flakes, mats are archaeological and part of the balance. They look inedible and hard as pottery — they are, and may be toxic to you. Do not taste.

Lip balm, saline eye drops, and cool wet towel left *outside* with your buddy for after.

### 4. Finding and entering

Search low. Standing up you'll walk past it. Get crouching height in early side-light and look for a long low blister, darker than salt, with a dark crack on one side and faint heat shimmer over it.

Approach slowly to avoid kicking salt dust into the slit. Clear nothing, move nothing. Lay your ground cloth down. Lie on your belly and pull yourself in head-first, arms forward. Expect the floor to drop a hand's breadth just inside — it catches people. Bring your knees in after.

Have your buddy hold your ankles / a leg tether until you're fully in. Agree: 1 tug = OK, 2 tugs = coming out, 3 rapid = pull me now.

### 5. Being inside: how to move without breaking it

Stay on the street. That swept packed-earth way down the middle is the only place you are allowed. It is kept open by agreement. Do not step on foundations, walls, hearth rings, granary stones, or push through fronds.

Crouch or knee-sit. Do not stand fully — you'll hit the arch and rain dust from the roof-holes and dislodge husks. Move slowly. Everything is slow-growing and brittle: silica ribs snap like glass, wax seals are easily scuffed.

Do not touch unless you must steady yourself, and then use gloved back-of-hand on bare rock, brief touch. The walls feel warm as a flank and steady — prolonged bare palm contact will burn or at least overheat you, and skin oil damages wax.

Do NOT snap a leaf. If you did, it wouldn't bleed sap, it would exhale hot oil that seals in seconds — hot enough to blister you, and that wound took years to grow. Look, don't pick. Don't lean on ash-flag, bottle-wort, cinder-plume fern.

Sit for at least 5 minutes in one spot on the street edge, lights low, and just listen and adjust. You'll hear: low crackle of wax expanding, hiss of spores sifting, groan when the draft moves, creak in wall-cracks, slump of a mat shedding husks. You'll feel superheated draft on your neck as the rock breathes in and out of the slit. That's normal. If the draft suddenly stops and heat spikes, get out — you're overheating.

Look for the glow only after adjustment: dull ember-orange at the core of thick leaves, pulsing faintly. Don't try to photograph it with flash. Long exposure, no flash, no touching to pose leaves.

Do not rearrange. Do not "tidy" a mat, right a pot, take a seed-cake to examine and set it down elsewhere. The town was left in order. Fingerprints in bricks, folded mats, hearth stones used as trellis — leave exactly as is. Don't sweep, don't clear fronds from a doorway for a better photo.

You are the only animal. Behave like one that knows it: quiet, small, brief. No food crumbs, no hair-pulling, no loud talking — sound carries too long. Whisper if you must.

### 6. Hazards to actually respect

**Heat + dry:** Headache, nausea, dizziness, stopped sweating, racing heart, confusion = heat illness. Crawl out immediately, do not wait to finish looking. Your lips will crack, nose may bleed, eyes sting — that's expected even on a good visit. Pre-hydrate, and cool outside after.

**Air:** If spores hit your face, close eyes, turn away, breathe slow through respirator. Don't rub eyes with dusty gloves. If you start coughing uncontrollably, exit.

**Burns / cuts:** Hot oil from damaged plants, hot stone, sharp silica edges, thorn awls on floors. Gloves on, sleeves down, watch where you put knees.

**Claustrophobia / disorientation:** It's low, long, dim, loud without animals, and vast-feeling between stalks despite being tiny. The emptiness is old and complete and can feel uncanny. If panic rises, focus on the blades of outside glare from the slit — that's your exit — and follow your tether out feet-first on your belly. Back out, don't try to turn around inside the thick growth.

**Fire:** Treat it as extreme fire risk. Static, batteries, hot headlamp housing on dry husk — keep everything off the ground cover.

**Getting stuck / lost outside:** The exit slit is invisible from standing height a few meters away. Your buddy stays at the slit, in shade if you can make it, watching the tether. No buddy, no entry.

### 7. Leaving and after

Back out on your belly, slow, handing yourself along. Don't kick plants behind you. Once outside, stay low for a minute — bright noon-white will blind you.

Remove respirator only after you're clear and dust has settled. Drink, electrolytes, cool towel on neck, eye drops, lip balm. Sit in shade until heart rate normalizes. Shake out clothes away from the site — do not shake spore dust into the slit.

Check: did you leave anything? Take all litter, fibers, bits of tape. Did you move dust onto the street? Leave it — don't go back to sweep. The street will remain swept behind you without you.

Don't wash yourself or your gear at the site. Don't pour out extra water on the salt to "help" the plants. Water is poison to this agreement. What they want is heat and mineral and time, and they have all three.

Go back only rarely, in tiny numbers, for minutes. Pods will keep bursting, loud in the hollow dark, with no ear to hear them. That's the point. You were a brief ear, and then you left, and nothing left with you.
