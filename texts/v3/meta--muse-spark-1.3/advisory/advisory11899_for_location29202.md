You are not visiting a place. You are visiting a condition. And you are the problem in it.

Understand that first and everything else makes sense.

On the high flat there is no water. Your body is 60% water that leaks. You breathe out wet, you sweat wet, you cry wet, you bleed wet, you piss wet. In Kettlehollow, that makes you a walking rot-bomb.

The Mat that covers everything ridge to basin - ash-grey, rust-black, sulfur-yellow, ankle to waist deep, felty and papery and brittle - is a dry lichen-moss that etched itself straight into basalt and granite and learned to eat stone-dust, static, and dim light. It has no roots because there is no soil. It has no sap. It snaps like hollow tubing. It is flammable beyond belief, it is alive under every step, and water rots it from the inside and wakes the black mold that eats both plant and lung.

They will let you in anyway, for a little while, if you agree to be less wet.

### 1. Before you go: Decide if you should

Do not go if you need constant drinking water, a humidified CPAP, nebulizer, wet contact solution, wound drainage, or if you have a cold, asthma, chronic bronchitis, bad knees, bad balance, or sound sensitivity you can't manage. This is high, thin, cold, dim, loud, and dry in a way Earth deserts never get.

You cannot live here. You can only visit. Plan for 24-48 hours max. Ordinary humans dehydrate fast in cool threadbare air because you don't feel yourself drying. You will be surrendering all carried water at the rim - bottles, bladders, gels, wet wipes, water-based cosmetics, e-liquids, even a spare lens case - and you will not get it back until you leave. They give you dust-tea instead, salty and warm. Drink it. It's not water, never ask what's in it. Think electrolytes, salts, oils, ion drift, not thirst-quenching. You will still be thirsty the whole time. That's normal. Pre-hydrate well for two days before, arrive with clear urine, moisturized skin with dry oil/wax, not lotion, empty bowels and bladder, and accept you will leave headachy and chapped.

You need to be fit enough for altitude. The air is cool, thin, cold in the mouth. Your breath will show. Your ears will pop when the wind changes pressure. If you get winded on stairs, train first. If you get nosebleeds in dry air, bring wax-based nasal balm - no saline spray, that's water.

### 2. You leak: How to contain yourself

At the rim they will search you. Let them. Offworlders have their water taken because one spilled canteen can kill a hectare of Mat and start a mold bloom that kills a family.

Rent or be issued at the rim if you don't own:

**A full rebreather / dust mask with dry filtration and breath-trap.** Not a cloth COVID mask. Not a humidifying hiking mask. You need P3/N100 equivalent for mineral dust, silica glass-hair, spores, mite chitin, goat-crop flour-puffs, and black mold. Crucially, yours must condense and trap your exhaled moisture so you aren't breathing wet onto the Mat. They will show you how to empty the trap into a sealed containment, never onto the ground. Wear it from rim to rim, even when the wind drops and you want air. Especially then - that's when the Mat settles and exhales spores.

Do not spit. Do not blow your nose onto the ground. Do not pee outside. Do not sweat more than you can help. Bring sealed waste bags, adult containment briefs if you'll be out long, sealed tubes for spit/mucus, and menstrual cups + sealed bags, no rinsing. If you bleed, sweat through, vomit, cry, spill anything wet: stop, contain, signal, report immediately. They won't punish you for an accident if you're honest. They will exile you if you hide it, because mold doesn't forgive.

Water rots from the inside. You won't see it at first. That's why children are taught to listen for rot.

Food: bring only fully dry, sealed, crumbly rations - pemmican bars, pressed dust-cakes, dryards. No fruit, no pouches, no juicy anything. Don't ask for local Mat to eat. You can't digest stone-flesh and grazing it wounds the town floor. Eat in designated stone boxes, over trays, catch every crumb. Leave nothing.

Hygiene: no washing. No wet wipes. Bring dry brush, dry powder, oil cloths. Your skin will itch, your lips will crack. Bring dry stick balm, not water cream.

### 3. Dress for permanent dusk, permanent wind, permanent crunch

**Light:** The sun is small, white-worn, low and sideways, never climbing, never setting. It's always blue-grey almost-night, edges softened, shadows long and never moving. The horizon is too far and too sharp. You will misjudge distance. Bring a dim amber/red headlamp with steady low mode, never strobe, plus spare dry cells. White searchlights blind locals adapted to dim and ruin your own night vision. Practice walking in dusk before you go.

**Cold:** Cool mineral dust and iodine and dry leaf smell. Cold gets into your knees standing on living crisp that ticks as it cools. Wear windproof, insulating layers head to toe: thermal base, mid fleece/wool, hard wind shell, wind pants, warm socks x2, insulated boots with wide footprint, knee pads or a kneeling mat if you'll stop. Gloves - thin grippy under, heavy wind over - because glass-hair lichen rasps skin and brittle tubes snap into splinters. Hat that covers ears, balaclava, dust goggles that seal, not sunglasses.

**Feet:** You have not set foot on ground at all. You walk on meters-thick living substrate. In hollows it's waist-deep and will not hold you like soil. Wear wide, light snowshoe-type overboots or Mat sandals they lend at the rim if you don't have your own. They spread your weight so you don't punch a bruise-trough. Step only in the trodden troughs between clusters - the caravan paths that heal behind if you wait. Never cut straight. Every off-path step is recorded as a long bruise that remains for weeks. Walk slow, lift, place, crunch. Don't drag.

**Head:** Wear a helmet or hard climbing cap. Tower-Stalks 20-30 meters tall, jointed like bamboo, shed vast translucent bark sheets that the wind worries loose and sends skittering like scythes at face height. You'll also want it when paperwing mites - hand-size, then coat-size, then door-size as wind lifts them - tack and click overhead.

### 4. Sound and air pressure will get inside you

First law: wind is loud. It comes uninterrupted across the peneplain and hits a billion dry filaments and sings - low roar, rattle, high papery hiss like surf but sharper, drier. Tower-Stalks groan and creak. Loose bark clatters. Under it all, the Tuning Forks - 200-meter black glass pylons, too thin to stand - hold a deep organ-hum you feel in your molars.

Bring foam earplugs + over-ear muffs. You need to be able to go double-plugged in gusts and single-plugged to hear guides. People who live under Forks get vertigo, nausea, jaw ache, anxiety from infrasound. Bring anti-motion sickness meds, take before you need them. Learn hand signals before you go because you will not be able to shout over the roar.

Pressure changes suddenly. Ears pop. Sinuses ache. Yawn, swallow, Valsalva gently. The gravity-seams - leftover anchoring mistakes where a dropped stone falls sideways for a second and dust hangs glittering never settling - will make you seasick and stumble. Don't walk through a shimmering seam if you can go around. If you must, clip in, stare at a fixed Stalk, breathe slow.

Night is always almost night, and it is never quiet. Even when wind drops and your ears ring, the Mat settles with a thousand tiny cracks, goats cough dust, cask-beetles tick, Forks hold their note. Bring sleep plugs and an eye mask and a watch with alarm. You will lose time without a sun. Enforce sleep or you'll fry.

### 5. Wind takes people. Stay tethered.

Rule three: stay tethered when the filaments rise.

Tower-Stalk crowns splay into flat filament tables combing wind for dust. When those filaments lift like hair on an arm, pressure is about to swing and roar will roll toward you across kilometers. Locals feel it before you do. Watch them.

Bring a climbing harness, 10-15m dynamic tether with locking carabiners, and practice clipping fast. Out on the open Mat, clip stalk-to-stalk to the filament-bridges, ropes, and fixed lines. If filaments rise:

1. Crouch low, facing sideways to wind, pack down.
2. Clip to nearest fixed line or stalk base, never to a loose bark sheet.
3. Goggles down, hood up, mask tight, gloves on.
4. Wait. Don't try to outrun to town.

If caught alone, go to a shallow dust-basin, curl around your pack, clip your tether to itself around a dense Mat hummock - not ideal but better than being tumbled. The Mat will not catch you so much as record you.

Never grab a sail mite for lift, never chase laundry lines of lichen-paper, never shelter directly under a creaking Stalk crown in high wind.

### 6. No flame. No spark. No joke about it.

Rule two: do not make open flame. Everything underfoot is tinder that never got wet.

Leave lighters, matches, camp stoves, candles, incense, portable welders, anything with pilot or spark at the rim. Heat lives only in stone boxes and glass. If you're cold, go inside a dome chamber, a sintered-stone lined heat room. Don't try to warm hands with a battery coil in the open - a spark in spore-flour air is a firestorm.

This includes electronics: no frayed cables, no exposed lithium, no vaping. Bring everything in sealed dry bags against dust, carry spare batteries warm inside your jacket, but use them only inside. Ask before photographing with flash - bright white hurts and ultrasound shepherd-machines, low crab-like silent combing machines that sing to the Mat to tell it where to thicken, don't like interference. Don't touch them. Give herds right of way.

Animals: bellows-goats - barrel-chested, six-legged, shingle-hided, horny beaks, pumping flanks - are cropping, not attacking, but a startled herd in a trough will trample you without noticing. Step upslope out of trough, kneel, wait, let flour-puff of spores settle before breathing hard. Needle-birds nesting in Forks imitate the hum into frantic descant until valley rings - beautiful and disorienting. Don't climb Forks. Don't throw stones to test seams.

### 7. In town: you are a guest in a pruning

Kettlehollow is not built on the Mat. It is hollowed under and coaxed from it. From above: low domes, vents breathing dust, ropes stalk-to-stalk. From inside: layered, warm with body and machine heat, nurseries for Mat grafts, chitin lamination shops, galleries where children learn to listen.

Behave:

Walk where shown. Don't lift Mat edges unless invited - though if invited, look: cold mint bioluminescence along the underside, there and gone. Don't peel, don't tear, don't souvenir. That tick you hear with your ear close is living cooling.
Speak low, listen more. The hum is sacred infrastructure. Don't lean on Forks, don't tap them, don't try to tune them.
Accept coached movement. If a shepherd or child says thicken/thin here, move.
Trade respectfully. Lichen-paper, laminated chitin, sintered stone work are precious. Don't offer water-purifiers, humidifiers, water filters as gifts. Offer dry tools, spare filters, wind cord, amber lamp cells.
Learn the three rules by heart and recite if asked. They mean it as hospitality test: One, bring no water. Two, make no flame. Three, stay tethered when filaments rise.

Use toilets - dry, sealing, stone-box toilets - every time, even if you think you don't need to. There is no bush to duck behind. There is only living floor.

### Pack list for an ordinary human, short stay

Worn: merino/thermal base + fleece + windproof shell, wind pants, 2x socks, wide boots + Mat overshoes, balaclava + warm hat, sealed goggles, helmet, gloves x2, harness + tether.

Carried: full-seal respirator + 3x dry filters + breath-trap empties, earplugs + muffs, amber headlamp + spare cells, dry rations in sealed tins, dust-tea cup (they provide), 2L sealed waste bags + containment briefs + spit tubes, dry balm sticks (lip, nose, skin), knee pads, wind cord 10m extra, watch, anti-nausea + painkiller + personal meds in dry vials + antifungal powder (ask medic how to use), dry brush/comb, sleep plugs/mask.

Left behind: all water, all flame, all white bright lights, all scented wet cosmetics, all impatience.

You will leave thirsty, ears ringing, knees cold, eyes full of blue-grey, boots full of crisp crumbs, mind full of crackle. Stand still long enough out there and the strangeness stops feeling strange - that ground breathes, trees are mostly air, a town can be a pruning. If you do it right, the only trace you leave is a shallow trough that, if you wait long enough, the Mat decides to forgive.
