You go down to it. That is the whole trip, really. There is no other way in, and no other way out.

Prepare like you're visiting a living furnace that learned how to be a room, not like you're exploring a cave or a basement. Forget factory, forget cave. Think: small, buried, hot-dry, impossibly crowded, impossibly loud, and perfectly closed. You are the only wet, changing thing that will ever be in there. Act like it.

### What it will do to you in the first 90 seconds

The stair is 30 meters straight down through salt and basalt. Fresh-broken edges, rebar teeth. Your ears will pop hard on the way down. Your spit will be gone by the bottom step. Not reduced — gone.

Then the chord hits your sternum.

Two man-tall exhaust fans pushing the same body-warm air around in a loop, piston pumps still thumping for a brine sea that isn't there, conveyor rattling, transformers humming, and over it all thousands of furnace-crickets sawing without pause. It's not noisy like a concert. It's even. Deafening but perfectly level, no peak, no break. You will shout and not hear yourself. After a minute you will stop noticing as sound and start feeling it as pressure in your teeth and chest.

The air is dim amber from salt-crusted sodium lamps that never die, warm as breath, thick as snow with live moths, shed wings, chitin-dust, and salt-motes. It smells of ozone and hot iron and musk and old oil. Everything is near-touching: pipe bundles, vent trunks furred with salt, sagging cable trays full of sleeping rats, catwalks that end in rock, a belt carrying white dust from one identical pile to another forever. You cannot move without brushing life. A cricket on the wrist. Moths in the face, dry as paper. Shadows vibrating under motors out of sync with anything.

And nothing changes. The dust hangs at chest height and never settles. The belt never wears thin. The moths that burn are hatched exactly as fast behind the pipes — you can hear the ticks. Rats eat crickets, beetles eat droppings, crickets eat shells, lichen eats dust. A closed mouth eating itself.

You need to be ready to be irrelevant in there for a little while, and then leave.

### The body problem: you are water entering a place with no water

Rule One in there: nothing is wet, ever. Oil is the only liquid and it stays in its lines.

That means you will dry faster than you understand.

1.  **Hydrate before, not just during.** Drink a liter with electrolytes in the hour before. Eat something salty. Piss at the top. There are no toilets and you do not want to add wetness to that room, and you do not want to need to.
2.  **Water stays sealed.** Bring 1-2L in a hard bottle with a straw-cap you can drink through a mask without opening wide. Do not pour, rinse, spit, or spill. Loose water will vanish into salt and dust and do you no good, and it insults the equilibrium.
3.  **Expect silent dehydration.** Sweat will dry to salt on your upper lip before it runs. You won't look sweaty. You *are* losing water with every breath through your mask. Sip on a schedule — every 5 minutes — not thirst. Plan a 15-20 minute max first visit. 30 minutes is a hard ceiling for an ordinary human. Climbing those 30m back up hot, deaf, and dry is harder than going down.
4.  **Lips, nose, eyes.** Balm your lips heavily, saline-gel your nostrils, wear sealed goggles, not just glasses. The air is glass-brittle salt-whiskers, rust-lichen dust, and moth scale. It will sand your contacts to misery. If you are asthmatic, allergic to dust mites/shellfish/mold, or prone to nosebleeds — don't go, or go with a P100 and an exit buddy and accept you may have to abort.

### Gear: cover every opening, pad every edge

Dress for dry heat, sharp rock, and animal contact, not cold.

**Wear:**
- Hard hat or climbing helmet. The ceiling dips and hanging pipes *will* brush your hair. You'll be ducking.
- Foam earplugs *plus* over-ear muffs. This is non-negotiable. That chord will give you temporary threshold shift in minutes and permanent damage with repetition. Double-protect and pre-agree you will not be able to talk.
- P100 half-face respirator with spare filters in a ziplock. An N95 will clog in 15 minutes with chitin and salt. Bring a bandana/neck gaiter over it for moths hitting your mouth.
- Sealed goggles, vented indirectly. No exposed eyes.
- Long sleeves, long pants, light, breathable, smooth and tight-cuffed. You will be brushed, landed on, crawled on. You don't want nooks. Light color so you can see beetles/crickets on you.
- Sturdy gloves (mechanic's gloves). Rock faces are fresh and will cut a palm. Wireweed is copper-stiff. Salt-whiskers are glass. Pump housings are hot with crickets. Don't touch bare.
- Knee-high boots with ankle support and thick soles. The floor has iron plates, orange scab lichen, fallen beetles that roll underfoot, cable snakes. One boot is already down there, ownerless. Don't add yours.
- Tie hair completely up and under hat. Beard cover if you have a beard. Moths will find it.

**Bring:**
- Dim headlamp with red mode, plus a small backup. Don't rely on the amber work-lamps — they stain, they don't illuminate corners. Don't blast white light; you'll just stun more moths into your face. Use low red to read dials and footing.
- Whiteboard + marker or hand-signal cards. Voice is useless.
- Watch with a vibrating timer set *before* descent. Your sense of time will dissolve. The needles in there are frozen trembling at mid-range. Your phone may survive but salt kills electronics — put it in a sealed bag if you bring it for photos. Set your return anchor, beacon, thread — whatever your method is — **at the top of the stairs, not inside.** Inside doesn't pass time properly. You want to be pulled to *before* you went dry.
- Chalk or survey tape for the stairwell only. Do NOT follow the faint white arrows on the floor. They point to doors that no longer exist. The red EXIT sign buzzes over solid rock. Your only exit is the way you came down.

**Don't bring:** food, open drinks, anything you need to keep sterile, anything with delicate fans/filters, anything green and alive. There is no soil. The only green that survives there has learned to be dry and black and wire-stiff.

Leave tetanus up to date. You *will* get micro-cuts.

### How to move and behave inside

You could walk it in three minutes if you could walk straight. You can't.

- Go slow. Shuffle, don't stride. The conveyor is bright-polished by copper beetles falling off, righting, climbing back on. Step *over*, never *on* it. Don't grab cable trays for balance — that's where blind, pink-nosed rats sleep in warm piles for current-heat. They wake to gnaw. They don't fear you.
- Don't sit on warm pump housings to rest. That's cricket habitat by the thousands — pale, eyeless, long-antennaed. They eat dust and lichen and each other. You will be warm too. They will investigate.
- Don't swat. You'll lose. Just brush moths gently off your goggles and keep moving. Killing ten means nothing; ten hatch behind the pipe.
- Touch as little machinery as possible. Everything works and nothing produces. Pumps pump nothing to nowhere. Fans cool no one. The animals clean contacts with their tongues, nest in relays, keep current flowing by being warm. You are not maintenance. Don't open, unplug, turn, or "fix" the control kiosk, time-clock, lunch pail full of salt. They are frozen mid-gesture. Leave them frozen.
- Keep oil inside. If you see a weep or line, don't wipe it. It's the only wet thing allowed.
- If you panic — and claustrophobes and even non-claustrophobes do, because you can see all the walls all the time but can't get to them through the tangle — stop, crouch, put a gloved hand on a cool vertical pipe (not a motor), feel the thump through the glove, and count breaths in your mask: 4 in, 4 out. Look at one small living thing doing one small job: a beetle righting itself. Let the chord be pressure, not sound. Then move toward the stair draft. Cold air falls, warm air wants to stay. Follow cold to go home.

Travel with a buddy if you can, with one person staying topside as timekeeper. Inside, stay within sight/touch. You will lose each other behind pipe bundles in six feet.

### After

You will leave with dust in your lungs, salt on your face like frost, and ringing that lasts hours to a day. This is normal for this place, but respect it.

Decontam outside, up top in normal air:
1. Blow nose, saline rinse, eye wash, gargle warm water. Expect gray-brown mucus with sparkle.
2. Shower, wash clothes hot, wipe gear down — salt is corrosive and chitin persists.
3. Drink again, slowly. Eat. Sit quiet. Many people dream of sawing that night, or feel the thump in a mattress.

Don't go back the same day. The certainty will tempt you — the absolute certainty that in 50 years the same moth will hit the same bulb, the same cricket will sing on the same housing. That's the lure of a place that learned how to keep going without ever going anywhere. It doesn't need you. That's why you have to prepare to remain a visitor: wet, temporary, brief, kind, and gone back up before you dry into part of the loop.
