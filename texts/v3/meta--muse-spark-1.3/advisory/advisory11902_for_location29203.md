If you go to the Pod, prepare like you're visiting a church that is also a desert that is also a loudspeaker. Everything about it will try to dry you, deafen you, and dazzle you, and the people who keep it alive will love you only if you help them stay dry, loud, and in tune.

This is not a hike. This is a short, intense, sensory immersion in a place that should not be alive, and is.

### 1. Understand What You're Walking Into

Forget oasis desert. There is no water here at all.

Outside: a flat white salt pan in heat-shimmer. Dust so fine it lifts when you step and just hangs. No shade, no well, no creek stain. The air wrings your mouth dry in minutes. Stones are bleached and pitted like bone.

Inside the mound: a vast half-buried seed-pod/egg-case, longer than three men lying head-to-foot, amber iron-hard shell polished by wind. You crouch and turn sideways through a split seam to get in.

Inside is hollow and huge. Not magical-huge, just completely hollowed. It holds:

**Light:** Sun through translucent shell, honey and hot wax, white at noon. No real shadows. Dust hangs and never falls. You lose depth perception.

**Sound:** Deafening and permanent. A low organ drone in your sternum and teeth from wind catching the three wrist-wide breathing holes at the top, plus a ceaseless high dry rattle from millions of leaves shaking. You will want to cover your ears. The seven tenders who live there don't bother. They just don't talk inside.

**Town:** Terraces all around the ovoid curve - husk-fiber streets, mat-sized plazas, thornwood ladders pegged with bone, sagging rope bridges over a deliberately empty center void, granary baskets, swallow-nest sleeping lofts daubed with mud and chaff, a tiny shrine of seed-coats. Built by hand - you can see lashings, adze marks, loom matting - but planned like a city for a thousand. There are seven people in it.

**Garden:** Every beam and peg furred with life that should be dead. Silver felt-leaf, grey feather-fern-that-isn't, amber air-moss veils ten feet deep, bladder-worts swollen with scented oil not water, tin-leaf vines that clatter. Crush a leaf and it bleeds clear hot-peppery resin that beads and doesn't evaporate. Tear moss and it parts like wool.

They are phonotrophs. They drink sound. White root-hairs grow toward hum-nodes and bridge-anchors, leaves are stiffened eardrums, the rattle shakes loose a sugary dust that coats everything and gets reabsorbed. Silver moss low where the bass lives, paper-bells high where the whistle shears.

Almost no animals. Mites. One pale under-floor lizard who is considered a citizen. Once a season a dust-moth bigger than a child squeezes through a breathing hole, glows in the honey-light, lays eggs, leaves. The tenders take half, leave half.

The people are lean, oiled, hair cropped short so it doesn't trap sugar-dust, throats wrapped against the roar. Bone awls, digging sticks, basalt flakes, simple looms. They chew resin for moisture and press bladder-oil for cooking. Their whole work is maintenance: re-lashing bridges before vibration saws through, combing moss so it can shiver, sweeping dust-gutters, patching thinned shell.

And they have three laws that are not suggestions:

1. **Do not bring water inside. Even spit is wiped and taken out.**
2. **Do not dwell in the center. Cross quickly, eyes up.**
3. **Tend the loud. Quiet is death.**

Plan for all of that.

### 2. Your Body: You Are Mostly Water, And That Is The Problem

You are a walking leak in a place that dies if it gets wet.

**Hydrate before, not during.** Pre-hydrate for 24 hours. Drink electrolytes. Piss clear before you approach. Once you're near the Pod, you will not drink inside.

Cache water *outside*. Bury 3-4 liters 50-100 meters from the seam on the shade side, marked with cloth, covered against dust. This is your life. You will exit to drink. Do not carry a canteen, bottle, hydration bladder, wet fruit, wet wipes, or anything damp inside. Not even in a sealed pack - seals sweat, packs leak, and the tenders will stop you. Sweat counts, breath counts, blood counts. Minimize what you bring in by limiting time.

Plan for 30-45 minutes inside max for a first visit. Your mouth will dry in minutes, your nose will crust, your skin will tighten as floor talc + salt sucks oil from you.

**Skin and hair prep, done outside:**
- Oil all exposed skin beforehand with a neutral, fully absorbed plant oil - jojoba or coconut, rubbed in 20 minutes before entry, excess wiped off. The tenders oil themselves; dry cracked skin bleeds, and blood is water.
- Lips: heavy wax balm, reapplied outside only.
- Hair: crop is ideal, but at minimum braid tight, cap tight, headscarf tight. That sugar-dust gets in everything and itches, and loose hair shakes plants wrong.
- Nostrils: a dab of balm just inside to prevent salt-bleed.
- Don't wear heavy perfume, sunscreen that drips, insect repellent. You'll contaminate felt-leaves.

**Heat:** It's lowland-warm that pools. Outside is furnace-white, inside is held-bakery warm. Wear loose, long, light-colored linen/cotton: long sleeves, long pants, closed neck. You want coverage without trapping heat. You will sweat - bring a dry throat cloth to catch it, and go outside to wring it out. Never shake sweat off inside.

If you start headache, dizzy, stop sweating, see dark spots in gold light - leave immediately, get to water cache, cool neck and wrists. There is no clinic in a city of seven.

### 3. Your Senses: Protect Them, Then Surrender Them

**Ears:** This is the hardest part for ordinary humans. The drone + rattle is weather-loud. It will buzz your skull pleasantly and horribly, especially late afternoon when outside air cools, thickens, and the whole Pod thrums bass and the gardens lift like hackles.

Bring vented musician's earplugs (15-25 dB) + a pair of foam plugs as backup. Not noise-cancelling headphones - bulky, they trap heat and dust, they signal you want silence, which is insult here. You want to take the edge off while still *feeling* the note in your chest. Practice inserting them with dusty hands before you go.

You will not hear speech inside. Don't try. Don't shout - it's useless and rude.

Learn to sign. The tenders use fluent rapid hand-talk inside, and only whisper in a circle outside on the salt at night when the Pod quiets to a murmur to "remember what voices sound like." Bring a small slate and chalk as backup, but hands are better. At minimum learn/mime: *thank you, help?, I will cross, I need out/water outside, beautiful/good work, quiet? / loud-good, where may I step/sit?, I mean no harm to lizard/shrine/garden.*

Keep hands free. No bulky gloves inside. Thin cotton gloves if your skin cracks, otherwise bare hands for ladders and signs.

Wrap your throat with a dry light scarf, even if hot. It cuts vibration fatigue and dust intake, and marks you as someone who listened.

**Eyes and lungs:** A non-negotiable kit:
- Sealed dust goggles, plus amber or brown tinted glasses underneath or clip-on. At noon there are no shadows to judge steps on ladders and bridges. White-out + gold-out = falls.
- P3/N100 half-face respirator with spare filters. Not a paper COVID mask. The air is full of mineral talc, salt, perpetual seed-down riding the thermal (hot up the sun-wall, cool down the shade-wall, never landing), sugar-dust, moth scales if you're unlucky/lucky. It smells of heated fiber, turpentine, toasted seed-fluff - beautiful for five minutes, irritating after 20. Breathe through nose, slow, through cloth.
- Expect cough, sneeze, eye-water outside. Don't spit, blow, or wipe inside. Carry a sealed rag bag, step outside to clear yourself, seal it.

**Touch and taste:** Do not eat, chew, or rub plants unless a tender offers by hand. That clear resin is hot-peppery and can blister lips and eyes. Bladder-oil is food and lamp fuel to them, not samples. Mites live in dust - don't sit directly on floor dust without a mat, don't scratch bites inside.

### 4. How To Move: You Are In An Instrument

The Pod is an instrument, town is fretting, garden is player. You are grit in the strings if you move wrong.

Footwear: soft, silent, grippy moccasins or minimalist trail shoes with clean soles. No hard boots, heels, trekking poles that punch matting. Bare feet as they do is ideal for feel but punishing for un-oiled tender feet on salt-talc - don't unless invited.

Pack: nothing bulky inside. Leave big pack at water cache. Inside, a tiny chest pouch only: plugs, slate, rag.

Movement rules:
- Enter crouched, sideways through the seam as they show you. Don't force, don't chip resin/dust seal. Touch shell lightly, acknowledge it.
- Stay off the center void. It's deliberately empty. Houses press back from it, bridges cross it fast and sway. That's where sound lives thickest, light columns cross, heat turns over. Cross quickly, eyes up, don't linger, don't stop to gawk or photograph in the middle. Don't throw anything into it, don't yell into it. The center belongs to the note.
- On bridges: one person at a time unless waved on. Walk midline, soft steps, don't grab hanging moss for balance - grab ropes. Expect sway and a high singing string over the bass. If vertigo hits, fix eyes on far anchor, breathe, keep moving.
- On ladders/terraces: test thornwood pegs and bone pins by hand before weight. Three points of contact. Those narrow streets and mat-plazas are homes - don't sprawl, don't sit in doorways, don't peer into sleeping lofts and granary baskets.
- Do not block the three breathing holes at the crown. Don't stand under them for long - white sky blades + falling dust + strongest shear. Don't drop anything near them.
- Depth is deceptive. Bring a headlamp for shadowed under-lofts but rarely need it - lamps are hardly needed. Turn it off quickly.

Electronics: vibration saws through lashings; it also rattles cheap gear loose. Tighten everything. Phone/camera on lanyard, silent, no flash (flash is pointless in honey-light and startles). Vibration from a phone buzz can confuse felt? Put on airplane + silent. Don't bring a Bluetooth speaker, instrument, drone - don't add or cancel sound. You are there to listen, not to play.

### 5. Culture: Seven Hosts, Hundreds Of Empty Nests

You are guest to seven dry whisperers maintaining a city for a crowd that never came. That emptiness feels mournful. Don't fill it with chatter.

Approach: arrive in morning or late afternoon? Noon is brightest/whitest, hardest on eyes. Late afternoon is copper-deep, most beautiful, loudest, most physically shaking - skull-buzz, sheets of floor dust rising. If sensitive, go mid-morning, leave before copper peak. If you want to understand phonotrophy, endure one copper peak from a terrace edge, holding a post, not in center.

Greeting: wait at seam. Make eye contact, hands open, point outside to your cached water to show you brought none in. Offer hands for sign. Accept throat cloth adjustment, head touch, whatever assessment they do. They will check knots by feel because they can't hear frays - let them check *you* the same way.

Work, don't gawk: The best gift is labor, not objects.
- Ask by signs to help: sweeping dust-gutters (they never carried water, they carry dust), combing moss gently so it doesn't choke its shiver, holding a ladder, passing fiber, re-lashing if you know knots.
- If a corner has gone quiet - felt matted, dust-choked, moss limp - point, offer to clear. "Tend the loud" is religion and agriculture. Quiet patches die.
- Don't pick, prune, harvest, or take souvenirs. Not a leaf, not a seed-coat from the shrine, not a moth egg - they harvest half and leave half. Not your call.
- The pale lizard under floor planks is a citizen. Don't lift planks to find it. If you see it, nod, step back.
- The shrine of stacked seed-coats: look, don't touch, don't photograph close without permission-by-gesture.
- Minimal impact biosecurity: before approach, shake out all clothes, empty pockets, clean soles of any seeds, soil, green bits from other worlds. You could introduce "the wrong green" - water-loving mold that rots fiber and silences felt. Same for your gut: if you have a cold, coughing flu, fungal skin issue, postpone. Breath and skin shed inside.

Night: if invited to sit outside on the pan when wind drops and Pod murmurs, go. Sit in circle. Speak only in hoarse whisper if spoken to. This is when they remember voices. Don't break it with loud stories or questions about where the thousand went. Listen. Offer dry, waterless food from your cache outside only - hardtack, toasted grain, salt - never juicy fruit, never alcohol to share inside. Eat and drink outside, wipe mouth, wait before re-entering.

Bodily functions: piss, spit, snot, vomit, bleed - all outside, far downwind, buried in salt, hands cleaned dry. If you drool, nosebleed, or sweat heavily inside, wipe with your throat cloth, step out immediately, seal cloth in bag. Women menstruating: use internal cup + plan very short visits or observe from outside; blood is water and scent and rot here. It's not shame, it's physics.

### 6. Packing Checklist For An Ordinary Human

**Cache outside (never enters Pod):**
Water 4L + electrolytes, shade tarp/buried bag, salty dry snacks, first aid, spare filters, headscarf wash bag

**On body approach:**
Long linen sleeves/pants, oiled skin, wax lip balm, tight hair + scarf + cap, throat scarf, sealed goggles + tint, musician plugs + foam backup, P3 respirator, soft shoes, tiny chest pouch

**In pouch:**
Slate + chalk, small dry rag in sealed bag, lanyard camera, headlamp (low)

**Do NOT bring in:**
Water in any form, wet food/wipes, bulky pack, poles, speakers, incense, bright flashlight, gifts that contain moisture (fruit, salves, live cuttings), high-scent sunscreen/bug spray

### 7. Inward Preparation

Prepare to be small. You'll stand in a huge hollow with felt brushing your shoulders, city stacked like a beehive for giants who never came, seed-down never landing, light with no shadow, sound you feel in molars, and almost no one. It can feel euphoric, then lonely, then claustrophobic despite vastness.

Practice: 10 minutes of loud brown-noise + bright light at home with earplugs in, communicating only with hands with a partner. Notice panic, notice urge to shout, to sit in middle to rest. Train yourself to keep moving to edges, to breathe slow, to use eyes.

Leave with nothing but dust in seams you'll be shaking out for days, resin-pepper memory in nose, hands a little more fluent.

And when you step back out through the seam sideways into white pan and wrung air, drink slowly at your cache, rinse goggles, whisper thank you - out loud, since now you can - and walk soft so the dust you lift can settle for once without you in it.
