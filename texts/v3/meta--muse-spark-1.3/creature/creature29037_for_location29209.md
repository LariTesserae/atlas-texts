At first you think nothing lives here. That is what it wants you to think.

The lichen lives, after a fashion. Sage-grey and mustard-pale, flat as paint in the brick joints on the north side where the bounce is least. The wiry grass lives, an inch and then it stops, having understood the terms. One tuft has gone to seed and the seeds sit uncarried on the lip because there is nothing to carry them. But those are not residents. Those are stains. They follow the straight lines. They ask for almost nothing and even that is almost too much.

No. The one who lives here worked hard to make you think *nothing*.

Put your hand in. The back of the niche is cool. Cooler than the air, cooler than the sunlit lip. Cellar-cool. Your fingertips come back white with nothing but lime-dust and brick-dust, utterly dry. You think: empty, swept clean. Sweep it again tomorrow and it will be the same. That cleanliness is not accident. Something keeps it clean.

Look again at the darks. Everywhere else is glare — whitewash throwing sun back and forth until your eyes water, until the edges double — and only deep in the holes is there black. Little square blacks, row on row on row, ankle to out-of-sight. But the blacks shimmer at the edges. They should be still. They are not still.

What lives here learned how to look like a shadow.

It is low. It has to be; there is only the middle and the walls leaning in. It is flat. Each one is about the size of two fists together, because that is the size the hands made. It presses itself so thin to the back of its house that your reaching fingers miss it, graze only the dust it sheds which is exactly the color of the wall. Plaster-pale. Brick-pale. Dry as bone. When you touched the back, you touched it, and it let you think you touched stone. It drinks the heat as fast as the light makes it — that is why your neck, your shins go cool standing in all that brightness. It is drinking you a little, too. Cool on your palms where you leaned.

There is one in every house. Hundreds of them. That is why there are no pigeons, no sparrows, no flies, no beetles under the lips, no silk strung across the mouths. Not because the place is ruined. Because it is full. Packed full of itself with no gaps left over. A dovecote that is still a dovecote, only the doves are not birds anymore.

Listen. That is them.

The roar made of thin whistles braided together. Each mouth narrowed just so, each flat body angled just so, to shape the air funneling down the slit of sky as it hits the dished floor and turns and goes back up. Each niche a flute — no, each throat a flute. From below you hear them combine into a single chord, high and hollow and breathy with the low rush underneath. It does not gust. It does not rise and fall. Architecture found a note it likes, you thought. No. A colony found a note it likes and refuses to let go. You hold your breath to test it. It stays. You clap once and it is swallowed instantly, flattened into the chord without a ripple, because hundreds of small cool throats opened a fraction wider to take it. The sound is already made and it is full. They will permit no new sound.

Look at the dust. Each mote lit gold-white, motionless in the loudness. It does not settle and does not swirl. It hangs. You thought it was held by sound. It is held by hunger. They eat almost nothing — what is there to eat where there has been no water longer than you can guess, no stain, no tide-mark, no moss that needs seep? They eat glare and dust and vibration. The whitewash bounces light into every upward face to feed them; the dished slabs funnel air to them; the cool stone condenses the night's last breath for them before the sun burns it off, leaving no mark. They take the plaster-taste from the air. That is enough, if you are still enough, if you live slow enough. They crumbled long ago and then stopped, found their shape, and stayed. The lips rounded under them. The mortar receded. Now nothing crumbles.

If you stand very still in the middle — and there is only the middle — you feel them assert the rules because the rules are their bodies:

Stay low. Stay cool. Stay dry. Make no new sound.

They do not hate you. There is nothing to eat in you that they want, nothing that needs more than dust and glare will be fed here. But if you are low and cool and dry and quiet, they will tolerate you while the slit of sky burns white above and does not move, while the chord holds one exact volume, one exact pitch.

The empty houses wait, row on row, for occupants that will not come. That is what you thought.

They are not empty.
