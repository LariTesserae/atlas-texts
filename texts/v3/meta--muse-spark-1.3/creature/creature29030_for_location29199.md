What lives here does not live *in* the place. It *is* the press of it.

At first you think insects. That word is too clean, too separate. What you are seeing is not thousands of separate hungers but one hunger spread thin enough to walk on.

Start low, because they all live low. Nothing here has learned to stand.

The base is not animal at all, if you still keep those borders. The films — mustard, rust, bone-white — are alive in the way skin is alive. They do not grow, they creep. A fingernail thick at most, blistered, peeled and resealed, sour-sweet with iron where you bruise them with a boot. They sweat acid so slow you cannot see it work, only its result: stone gone pitted, fluted, soft-shouldered. They are eating the bowl itself. Everything else only rents what they dissolve.

On them, the first layer of mouths.

You thought the stone was boiling. That is springtails and mites, pale as uncooked grain, each no bigger than a sesame seed, in numbers that defeat counting. They do not walk so much as seethe — lift, fall, lift — a living grit that makes a sound like rain on paper when they adjust. They lick. All of them lick. There is no drinking here, only absorption; the stone sweats and they mop it with mouthparts, with bellies, with feet.

Bigger, shouldering through them: woodlice the size of a thumb joint, slate and glass at the seams. They move like armored slow thoughts, antennae never still, testing testing testing. Turn one — if you dared turn one — and underneath is not underside but nursery: eggs furred white, coiled millipedes, small black beetles pretending death until they decide, all at once, not to. Nothing is alone here. You cannot set a fingertip down without touching three kinds of body.

In the pans, where water consents for an hour to be water, the other shape of low life: flatworms gliding like dark tongues, leeches knotted together in black glistening balls that come apart and re-knot, salamander larvae hanging motionless except for their gills — external, feathery, blood-red ferns that pulse with a line of cold green dots along each flank, signaling to no one you can see. And the frogs. You will not see the frogs at first because to see you must stop and light must cooperate. You will hear them: thousands and thousands of throats, each pan with its own choir, inflating at once. *pink pink pink.* Glassy, unsynchronized, unceasing. They are not calling for mates. Or not only. They are keeping the volume up.

Because above the stone, the air is also ground.

The pillars you felt on your cheek before you saw them: midges, tens of millions to a column, rising and bending and holding, trading places with the next pillar in a slow exchange. Each insect is nothing. Together they are weather. They thicken the dark until the dark vibrates. Their whine is the high wire of the valley's roar, needling, endless. They route around your breath-heat the way water routes around a stone — not fear, physics. You are a brief warm front to them. They have no selves to be afraid with.

And hunting in them, through them, made of them: the fliers.

At first they look like moths. Then too big for moths. Then too leathery, too silent. Bat-big, cloak-big, mouths impossibly wide. They do not flap so much as row the damp air, silent until they click — a dry *tick tick* you feel in your teeth — and then the nightjar-scream, torn out as they scoop through a pillar and take a mouthful of living cloud. Their wing-edges catch the ground-glow and throw it back as brief phosphor. It is not for light. No one here uses light to see. Movement draws light. Light says: *here I am eating, here I am eaten.* The click-beetle larvae down in the grit do the same, burning cold blue where bodies have been crushed and are fermenting. To be still is to go dark. They have all learned to be bright only while feeding.

And then — the ones that make the silence.

You have not seen them. You will not, unless you stay long enough for your eyes to stop wanting sun. You have heard them wading. A splash too large for any pan. A croak-bark, guttural, from the dark beyond the diffusion. Long legs breaking the skin of shallow water that is not shallow to anything else.

The waders are the reason the valley screams to cover itself.

They are tall — that is the first wrongness in a world where everything spreads sideways. To stand up here is to declare yourself eater. They pick their way between pans on stilt legs, blind or near-blind, white-gray as the crabs but scaled up to your chest, your shoulder. Their heads, if heads, hang low, wide-mouthed, fringed with feelers that read the tremor in the pans, the tremor in the stone. The blind white crabs in the seeps are their smaller cousins or their children or their memory: standing in seep-water, lifting one leg at a time to the mouth to lick it clean, tasting the world one limb at a time. The waders do the same, on a valley scale.

Every few minutes they feed, and the valley lets them by inhaling.

You felt it: the great roar stutters without rhythm. A wave of quiet rolls outward from somewhere central, as if the whole bowl took a breath. In that second the midges drop an inch, the frogs cut mid-*pink*, the billion-leg rustle hitches. Predator as silence. Then the splash, the crunch — chitin and small wet bone — and the screaming knits back over louder than before, protective, deafening, solid enough to hold you up.

That is their mind, if you need that word. Not in a head. In the interval.

Nothing here is solitary because solitude is death. The grazers feed the hunters, the hunters die mid-step and in thirty seconds are covered, in two minutes are a writhing mound, in ten are a clean wing-case licked smooth and already filming over with mustard pale. No waste. The mats eat the bodies that ate the mats. The rock is digested and excreted and re-formed. Even the sound is eaten and re-made: the standing waves you walked through, those pockets where one tone cancelled another and your own heartbeat came back to you, loud and startled — that is the herd listening to itself to stay a herd.

If you stay, they will incorporate you without malice and without welcome. Landed upon. Weighed. Licked by condensation off your own skin, metallic and faintly sulfured. Your vibration added to the stone's hum under your palms. Your mouth filled with their noise when you open it.

They do not know the rim. They do not know fire or wall or trail. They know only: stay low, stay wet, stay loud, lick the stone, eat your neighbor before your neighbor eats you and feed the film with what is left. Life piled on life so thick the mineral underneath is rumor.

The bowl breathes. The press breathes with it.
