At first I think I would be standing.

Up on the rim, upright, apart — the watcher with stupid fingers and hanging breath, trying to keep my eyes open in the starlight. I think I would stay human in it, just long enough to understand. But I can't. The air is too sour to think in. My lungs won't learn it. If I stand, the wind finds the gaps in me. I am too big, too warm, too gapped. A gap is death that spreads.

So I crouch. That's closer.

Forehead nearly to stone. Hands flat on blue-grey that is still ticking deep inside. I try to be smaller. I try to be patient enough to belong for a minute.

For a while I think I might be the lichen. Black-green and burnt orange, tight as paint in the pits. That would be easy — to ask for nothing, to eat light that isn't there, to grow over a dropped tool and claim it. But I am too loud inside. I breathe too fast. I need-need-need.

Then I think I might be the water. The bead in the palm-sized cup, the seep bleeding black from the horizontal crack. Tick — plink — tick. Always full. Belonging to whoever licks first, and then to the next. That appeals to me, to be drunk and replenished drop by drop. But water doesn't shiver. Water doesn't choose to face inward.

No. Let it get clearer. Let it get more itself.

I am warm-blooded and losing heat. I am small in a place with edges. If I am going to live here at all, I have to be touched on every side.

So I find myself lower down, where it is wettest.

Not in the center — the center is only pools and empty air that everyone looks at. I find myself in the lining itself, poured like slow liquid into a cup between two tilted plates of fresh shale, two deep. There is a chest against my back and my chest against another back, wing over wing, down pressed to down until there are no gaps. The back wall under us is unnaturally warm. We made it warm. I can feel my own heat leaving me and coming back to me from someone else, indistinguishable.

That is where I am: east side, halfway down, just below the seep-crack where the stone is blackened and sweating. Close enough to hear the licking.

And who — what — I am, comes into focus with my hands.

I am soot-grey over the back and wings, belly fur licked almost white. My chest is too big for me, working slow as bellows in the thin air. My hind toes are splayed and locked around a knife-edge that would cut a human palm. My eyes are forward and wet, catching starlight like seeds. And my forelimbs — folded, they look like a cloak. Opened, they are both wing and arm, membrane between long fingers, feathered on the leading edge, the outer two digits left free, clawed.

I am holding something.

A thin flake of shale, pinched like a knife. Or no — a curled sliver of bark, sodden, dark, carried up from impossibly far below where trees still exist. It is dripping from the rock cup where my short beak could not reach. I lift it. I suck. The water is cold and faintly of stone and faintly of all the mouths before mine.

And then, without ceremony, I pass it sideways.

The neighbor dips and sucks in turn. Use and drop. Do not keep tools. When the edge dulls, when the thirst is gone, it will join the other shards and tomorrow a lichen will grow over it. I know this and my hands do not tighten.

I am facing inward. Of course. We are all facing inward, even those of us on the outer rim with the black distance tugging at our backs. Especially us. I have turned my back to the fall and the wind to look at the pools, at the thousand round faces looking back at me. My head will turn over the course of a full minute. My wing will unfold like a glacier. Need-need-need, I click, low, keeping the map of the bowl alive in the dark, and the bowl returns it wrong — a half-beat late and lower, in its own rock voice. I am one throat among thousands teaching a too-new mountain how to sound, and letting it practice on me.

Every hour the cold pools closest and I will be the place where the shiver starts. It will lift from my tier first, that shared shudder to keep the edge from freezing, and travel upward around the bowl, through ribs and feathers, and someone crouched among us with cheek near down will feel it climb through them too, and breathe with us, and condense on stone, and for a moment be part of the water we will drink.

I will notice them the way a lake notices a stone. A tightening, a focusing, then a loss of interest. They will leave before they understand, because their fingers have gone numb.

I will tighten, sip, scrape, and stay in the bowl.
