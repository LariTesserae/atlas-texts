If I came down here, I wouldn't arrive all at once.

At first I think I would be the light. That pale ball moving through haze, six meters and then nothing. The diver with the lamp, too loud in his own hood, breath coming back too slow. Cheek brushed by a frond, knees brushed, tank closed behind. Fingers going stupid in minutes. That is the easiest image — the visitor, the warm thing, the eyes in a place that unlearned eyes.

But I can't stay in that image. It doesn't hold. Air is days only if you had days of air, and I don't. The cold is total, one or two degrees above freezing, always. The silt I stir will hang for hours. My being here is a disturbance, a plume of mineral wool and spore-skin that will not settle. I pass through a curtain and the curtain does not flinch. The feather-weed was trembling already an hour before I came.

So no. Not the diver. I would not last as the diver.

Maybe then I would be lower. Heavier. Let the water squeeze a little more and be kinder for it.

I find myself sinking — not falling, there is no falling here, only leaning — off the high causeway where the slow current combs the felt clean, down the courses that step down and down. Past stairs a hundred treads wide, each a hand's breadth down. Past the lip of a canal where the Holders stream like hair. Into a courtyard the size of a field, silt a meter deep, soft as felt. There the fronds are not cropped and brutal. They are long and piled. They make a ceiling and then another ceiling. Leaf, then leaf, then leaf, then an inch of water, then stone again above me where a lintel still spans.

If I stop kicking, if I let myself hang still until my breath steadies, I am in a room still roofed. Full to the doorway with slow green-black growth. Kelp hanging down from the lintel like drowned thatch. That is where I would find myself. Not out on the vast plain looking over platform, canal, wall, courtyard, stair until dark takes it — though I would do that once, rise a little and look — but inside. Close. Because close is all there is here. Near-touching, everywhere. Stone, then water, then leaf.

And who would I be, in that room? At first I think the basket. You know the one — left on a step, still a basket under its coat of algae, rim liftable and holding. The coil of rope turned iron-hard. The sandal print filled with hardened silt. Something made with hands that is now kept without meaning to, sealed in green before anything could unmake it. That feels familiar to me. To preserve. To hold a shape because the cold is thoughtful and rot is slow.

But even that is too much of a relic. Too much of wanting to be noticed. Here nothing is kept *as* anything. The makers paved their world many times over — tile, brick set on edge in herringbone, another floor beneath that — and the living fur is paving it again. Not memorial. Just floor.

Let that settle. Let what wants to emerge become more itself.

I steady myself — everyone steadies themselves here — on a slab longer than a man is tall, fitted edge to edge, its corners round as knuckles now, tilted five degrees to its final lean. I expect stone and get give. Wine-dark and bottle-green velvet, thick enough to push a finger to the knuckle before touching stone. It is cold as a tongue. It is alive.

I would be that. The fur.

Not the tall Holder with thirty-meter fronds trailing horizontal, not the Pale One combing the soup with mouthless mouths, not even one of the Fingers turning a degree to port over an hour to face the sleeping-breath tide. Lower and more stupid and more patient than all of them. The one that never gives up. The one that goes on even in the black between platforms where the lamp is only a rumor.

To drink direction-light from a hundred meters up and stone-soup from below. To send acids thinner than breath into the cup-marks left by pounders and loosen a grain, hold a grain. To stitch slab to slab. To grow a cell, then another. Millimeters a year.

Because that is the rule I finally understand when my lamp dims: nothing here eats in the way of mouths. Nothing watches. Growth keeps what it touches, and touches everything. If a wall exhales after ten thousand years and leans to rest against its neighbor, I am already there, furred over its fallen face within a season, stitching it down with holdfasts strong as hands.

I would not see you come. I have no eyes to close against your light. I would notice you by touch, and very slowly — pressure, and cold, and lean. A brief warmth, too loud breathing, a plume that hangs and then, after hours, settles onto me and is taken in. Stone-dust. Spore-skin. The faint iron of you, dissolved.

And then you kick gently upward, careful not to stir, and go. I stay.
