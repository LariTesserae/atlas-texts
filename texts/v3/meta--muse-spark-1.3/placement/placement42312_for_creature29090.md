I come down by the road that doesn't come back up, the same as you.

At first I think I am a visitor. Boots new enough to still bead water. Coat still clean at the cuffs. I expect, because of the noise, that there will be someone to meet me at the bottom — a gate, a town. The roar is that complete. It sits in the sternum. It makes the eyes invent crowds.

There is no crowd.

There is only the flat going out and out to mist, green-black under grey-blue, and the far rim all around like held breath. And the boxes shouting to each other across it.

So I walk. That is the first long hour. Black boards underfoot already soft. Water coming up around the sole with every step, not from the sky but from below. Pants soaked to the thigh in a hundred yards from brushing through it. Every moss tip beaded. Every zipper beaded. Drip from the underside of pipes. Fine wetness that thickens and thins. Ptarmigan bursting once, white, eaten instantly by the drone. A raven tilted high, mouth open and no sound arriving. That should have told me.

I keep looking for high ground and there is none. Only hummocks knee-high, hard on their north sides, and between them those dinner-plate mirrors of black water holding sky. I kneel, because a visitor kneels to look closer, and pull a handful apart. Down a foot, down two feet, wet stems, ice, black water smelling sweet, like tannin and iron. It gives and comes back up. It breathes slowly around my hand.

No soil here that isn't plant, past plant, future plant.

I stand up with my hand stinking and cold to the ache, and I understand the scale in my body before I understand it in words: kilometers of plant holding water. A handful of hot steel boxes shouting to keep it from rising.

I am not visiting this. I am inside something.

The bowl is too round. The rim too even. The road that cut down through raw banks — living green on top, then brown, then almost black, dripping, frost needling out in white fans, stones left at angles — that cut is young. The edges haven't softened. The lid was lifted not long ago and the air got in. And now the floor. This springing carpet, olive and rust-red and acid green gone grey.
