I don't find myself in a room.

At first I try to. I try to give myself a door, a bunk on 312, a filter-mask hanging by the latch, a job patch that says PRESSURE. A clean place. Because that's where people are supposed to be in the Stack — inside the hull, in the dry, where the air is counted.

But I can't stay there. I hear it underneath. Even in the clean place, with the vents throttled down for sleep shift, there's a faint peeping under every other sound. *Peep-peep-peep.* Once you know about the seam you can't unhear it. It gets in the teeth.

So I go sideways.

I find myself where you found yourself: shoulder-first through the service slit, cheek scraping cold baffle, knees dropping straight into bathwater. I can't stand. That’s the first correction. The pocket won't let me be tall. Keep low or the condenser teeth kiss your scalp cold and leave a numb stripe. So I fold. I kneel. The water backs up hot around my shins and finds the capillary gutter again and goes on pulling sideways and uphill, glug-glug at the sump throat.

For a while I think I would be eyes. Just that. Just a visitor with a forehead lamp long dead, watching sheen, not color. Amber dots dying along the lower rib. Blue bleed off the black glass teeth. Moth-scale itching my nose. Midges parting around my face and re-closing like I never pushed through.

Then the images start coming, and I let them come without grabbing one.

I think — I could be the gecko. Pressed into the warmest seam where the inner hull bleeds heat, belly translucent, gut coiled dark inside me, heart ticking visible if the light is behind me. Utter indifference. Toes splayed impossibly wide on wet alloy. Not chasing. Just waiting with wet-bead eyes open, licking my own eyes clear of condensation. Let the frog hop wrong. Let the spring be misjudged. *Flick.* And then a throat pulsing down my throat. That kind of fat, slow aristocracy feels good for a minute. To be unbothered in a drum.

But no. That’s too still, too above it. I’m not above it. My knees are damming the gutter.

I think — I could be the spider. Belly-up under my own sagging sheet, mouth to the silk, drinking vibrations. Palm-wide and the color of damp concrete. My whole house beaded with drinking water, each drop a lens catching amber and blue. Wings, wings, wings stuck in it. Midge by the hundred. A moth still shivering. I ride the building’s exhale instead of fighting it, legs loose when the whole interstice shudders. That feels truer. To make something fragile across a pinch and live in what it catches.

But I’m too loud for that. My breathing lifts the whole rank of moths at once in a papery detonation.

I think — I could be the swiftlet. That one pulls hardest. Gray as wet ash, crammed shoulder to shoulder on a half-cup of guano and lime-spit, screaming in ultrasound to find my own cup in the dark. Because I’m the only one here who remembers sky. I go out through the wind-slot — half a second into thin, tearing, night-cold air that kills — snatch a throat-pouch of high-altitude insects smashed into a ball, and flicker back in with that *rush-rush* of leathery wings. Out to clean death, back to warm mouth. Out, back. Out, back. To feed two blind-grinning chicks with corded necks always gaping. My shit falls straight down and feeds everything below me. The whole web runs on me and I never get to stay warm.

That’s almost me. Almost.

Then my hand goes still too long in the water — because I’m tired of trying to hold myself up — and it happens.

A frog climbs onto the back of my hand.

No bigger than the last joint of my little finger. Amber as the maintenance dot. Cold-bellied even in this heat. It chooses me because I am the warmest, driest vertical in its world. It sits in the cup of my knuckle like it would sit in a white mineral cup, like it would sit on a cable with a row of others like beads. Throat pulsing. *Peep.* I feel it with my whole body, not just my hand. That tiny cold belly against my skin.

And I understand I had it backwards.

I’m not going to be one clean thing. If I lived here like they live here — like water lives in a cupped hand, held and constantly spilling and replaced — I would be the frog.

Not the chorus in general. This one. Territory the size of a bottle cap. Male, calling frog to frog over the building’s endless exhale: *I am here I am wet I am alive.* Throat-sac blowing out to twice my head, translucent, veined, trembling. Eyes shut against the wet. Eating midges that drowned in the drip they were born in. Laying strings of clear eggs, if I were female and fatter and quieter, belly-deep through the skim where the cleaners can’t quite reach, trusting the black-green biofilm in the slowest water to feed the black commas that will come next.

To be that small and that loud. To own every horizontal lip and ask for nothing but a lip that stays wet. To be eaten — by the gecko’s too-fast-to-see tongue, by the spider’s sheet — and to still outnumber the mouths. Thousands of me. Thousands of throats. A wall of sound in a metal box that rings the ribs.

Yes. Let that become more itself: my skin never quite dry, drinking through it, breathing through it, tasting ozone off the teeth and sweet rot-bird musk underneath and spawn-sweet where the tadpoles graze. My world four meters long and barely wide enough to crawl and endless. No sky needed. No soil. Someone carried my grandmother up here on a boot tread years ago and the sump never dried and now I own it.

That’s where I find myself. Not in 312 looking down. Not even exactly human-kneeling with the water backing up.

I find myself landscape and animal at once — a warm vertical with a cold belly on it. My hand under the frog, the frog under the roar. The cleaners closing their mahogany line behind my knee. The crickets tasting my bootlace to test if I am food yet. The midges learning the shape of me and flowing around it.

Don’t swat. Don’t block the gutter. Don’t stay still too long — but I do, I do stay still, because for a minute the drum equalizes and I can hear my own breathing again, shallow in the thick air, and under it, through it, the peeping that will stay in my ears for an hour after I back out soaked through and flecked with down.

If you put your hand down here, I will climb it.
