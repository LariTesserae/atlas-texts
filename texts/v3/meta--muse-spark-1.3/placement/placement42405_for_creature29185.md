At first I think I would be walking.

Out there on the table, with my thirty-foot shadow thrown out in front of me, stepping from sorted circle to sorted stone while the low sun drags around the rim and never rises. I think I would be the witness, the one with boots that ring faintly and then settle. I could learn to read damp like shade. I could kneel to hear.

But I wouldn't last as that. That body is too loud and too leaky. Every exhalation I make is fog lost. Every sound is heat leaving. I would be steaming wrong — too fast, too thin, burning good food too quickly in a place where heat is expensive. The cold keeps accounts, and I would be overdrawn in a day.

So not that.

Then I think, maybe the furnace. For a moment I want it — to be the boulder that breathes. To be low and barrel-bodied and certain, to take sulfur lichen and gritty melt and keep it for a long time and burn it thoroughly. To feel that slow piston chug come up through my own chest and down into the soles of my feet. To make a halo around myself, a few feet of thawed mud where moss can seize its chance. To carry my own weather. To glow at the throat like banked coal.

But I can't. I don't have that kind of fire in me. I don't eat stone and make summer. I borrow. I have always borrowed warmth to begin.

If I stay still long enough the image thins and corrects itself.

Lower. Smaller. Lighter.

Not on the ground, but just above it. Not one heart, but many, very loosely held.

You find me if you look up a little from the backs. I thought the air over the herd was empty until the light caught it.

I am in the veil.

Hand-sized, furred to the eyes, white as frost-fur. No mouth you can find. Ten thousand of us beating without sound in the cold air, a pale shimmer a mile wide that settles when they bed and lifts when they shift. We are not the process with hair on it. We are what attends the process.

My place is specific: about ten feet above a moving file, in the column.

Below me a hundred backs go by in single file along the old trough, calves in the middle steaming hardest, an old bull at the rear clicking faintly as the frozen oil-beads rattle in his ruff, blue static crawling tip to tip across his shovels. Each exhalation comes up — long, dense, white — and does not dissipate. I hang in it. I drink it.

That's what I do. I don't rasp lichen. I harvest condensation off breath. Iron and salt and digested stone, the faint sulfur of a century-old orange coin worried off rock by a broad tongue far below. The warm air holds me up. The furnace gut down there is my updraft. I ride the heat they can afford to lose.

When they stop to graze in a broad front, advancing a yard a day, I settle low. The world goes felt-quiet again except for the tick-tick-tick of something very hot cooling very slowly, and the rasp-tear, rasp-tear if I drift near a head. I settle sometimes on a shovel-antler, sometimes on your sleeve if you stand still, for the salt of your frost. Weightless. Leaving again.

I know the loops, but not the way they do. They know them by memory of melt and smell of thaw. I know them by staying in the plume. Kettle tarn to kettle tarn, five miles then fifty, three years then five then ten to close. I have drunk above this same black water before, rimmed with ice that never fully leaves. I remember it the way breath remembers: this damp, this dung-heat, this bruise of green-black where animals have passed and melted and manured.

And I borrow a few hours of furnace-heat to continue.

When a foot — a stout black pillar — lifts, it leaves a print that steams. For a minute there is open mud, dark and soft, smelling of gut and moss. I drop, quick, before it freezes. I press pale beads into the thaw. My eggs. They take what the halo will give and then harden with it. Most will freeze. Some will keep the warmth long enough to begin. Nothing here is promised more than a beginning.

That is where you would find me. Not making the weather, but living inside the weather they make. Between exhalation and cloud, between herd and sky. In the needling ice that falls toward the center, silvering the stones. In the white fur of frost by night, in the film of melt by dim noon.

I make no loud noise. I have learned the terms: keep your warmth close, borrow water and return it, move the way glaciers move.

I do not burn poor fare at great heat. I live on what the burning lets go.
