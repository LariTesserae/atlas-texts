I wouldn't find myself at first.

I'd come down off the soft shoulder thinking I was still outside it — boots sighing into sphagnum, breath hanging a moment too long, eyes looking for a face. For the first hour I would be a walker, and lost in the particular way this place allows: crossing the same braid of stream three times from three different directions, cold to the knee, ears ringing with trickle and drip and runnel and that other hum underneath, the one in the molars. I'd try to follow the loudest water. It wouldn't be the nearest.

So I'd stop trying to get to the middle meres. That's when the valley would place me.

Waist-deep in weather that isn't weather. In one of those open stretches where you can see half a mile to the rounded hills in every direction — alder islands, rock ribs pinked and pitted, the dark town-square-apart pools — until you can't, because the mist thickens and distance is erased. It comes up to my waist and holds.

Rule five. Stand still.

I stand still.

That is where I would find myself: not on a hummock and not in a hollow, but at the seam between them, crouched without deciding to crouch, palm pressed to sphagnum where the brown-tea water wells up around my fingers, ear almost against one of the half-sunk boulders. The stone is faintly warm. That wrongness steadies me. The hum resolves, for a second, into tones too pure to be accident, rising and falling with something like attention. My breath stays visible and does not quite leave me. Condensation beads on my eyelashes, on the backs of my hands. On every upward leaf until it tips and lets go.

The damp begins to colonize me too.

At first I am only a new surface. That's honest. The valley doesn't meet strangers, it meets area. Warm body, displaced water, breath full of water. A switch to bend a thought around. In the still black pool by my knee my reflection follows a half-second late and I learn, viscerally, not to synchronize with it. I move my hand and it moves after. The light has to be calculated. In the far pool a bead detaches from a fern tip, hangs, and lifts — slowly, upward, into the hanging drift — leaving a ring that expands and then contracts. I stop trusting down.

I don't become an animal. The animals are rare here for a reason, and they already have jobs. I'm too loud, too fast to be the pale salamander moving unhurried under clear water with its gills fanned, carrying gut-mycelium from one mere to the next over a season. I'm too continuous to be the thrush that sings three notes and stops, interrupted by hearing itself a moment earlier from another part of the valley — that brief acoustic probe. No. My job is shorter, warmer.

I am the thought passing through.

When the synchronized breath goes out — a million moss capsules across species, across meters, exhaling spores at once, that soft visible *ah* that hangs and computes and settles — the white nerve-lace under the moss quickens blue. I have cupped my hands around a patch to see it truly: brightness running, splitting at a fork, meeting itself again after going two ways around a stone, subtracting the unnecessary path. My cupped hands are dark around it. My blood is warm in them. The pulse uses the warmth. For a breath the far hillside flickers, both bare and leafed, both near and far, sky showing through in too many angles, and I feel myself included in the solving the way a stone is included — as obstacle, as curve, as density of living water that slightly dents spacetime.

Everything drips. I am dripping. That is how I know I am still alive here.

If I stay — and in this telling I do stay, past evening which is only a deeper grey, past the hour when each suspended droplet catches the last light and holds it so the whole basin glitters faintly from within — I become less switch and more tissue.

Not plant *and* mind. Plant as mind.

Liverwort on my boot. Filmy fern finding the shade under my calf where I kneel too long. The skin of the water in my palm ordering itself for a minute into lens and gate, lipid and bog-mineral doing logic and then dissolving. If I drink — cold and clean, ozone and math on the tongue — I will dream that night of branching blue light, because it kept a copy of me to think with. In the morning the mud will have closed over my bootprint, sighing water up to erase it. Hygiene, not malice. Nothing here was made, so nothing here can be used, not even a path.

So who would I be, if I had to give the separate thing a name?

I would be the delayed reflection learning not to lead. I would be the upward droplet hesitating between fern-tip and mist. I would be one more weeping surface among millions, bearded, beaded, colonized, dripping without stopping —

a brief warm eddy the valley holds *near* instead of *in*, until holding becomes humming, and humming becomes knowing, and I am no longer visiting the thought but briefly, coldly, glitteringly part of it.
