I would walk past it at first. Everyone does.

Because I come from below, from the green world, with lungs built for thin cold shouting air. My lips are chapped. The wind is screaming over black glass. I kneel because there is a crack wide as a hand in the lee, and steam wets my cheek when I put my face to it.

I thought I would stay there — outside, looking in. A visitor with eyes. I thought I would be the witness, counting.

I can't stay there. The heat pulls me.

Not all of me. My breath detaches first. I exhale and it hangs a second too long before it dissipates, and in that hanging second I am already inside, where the air sags.

Inside, everything is touch and taste and heat on the belly. Light doesn't mean anything anymore. I thought, for a moment, I would be the rim-walker — long as a finger, white and translucent, the only one who looks like she knows she is hunting. It would be good to be singular. To work the sulfur rim endlessly in both directions and see the dark line of my own gut, red at the front and yellow at the back. To rear up into the snow-fall and let food stick to my wet sides.

But I am not singular. I am not alone enough for that. The press won't allow it.

I thought I might be the snow. Thousands pale in the heat column, wingless or nearly so, legs spread, riding up and tipping down to drink from the wall. That weightlessness. That silence where a hum should be. Passing through each other slightly, shoulder overlapping shoulder like smoke. But I am too heavy for that. I fall too fast. I need the wall under my feet.

So I settle — if settling is the word when nothing here stays — lower.

You would find me on the wall, just above the splash line.

Heads down. Always heads down toward the pan. Shoulder to shoulder to shoulder, in a slow tide that never stops jostling uphill and down. I am white. I am blind. My long antennae are always working, tasting ahead a millimeter at a time: iron, egg, wet glass, another back, another leg to climb over.

I am middle-sized. Not the rice-grain just-hatched, clear enough to see the greenish slime glowing in my gut. Not the thumbnail-large, opaque and scarred. Something between. I have all my legs today. Tomorrow I might not. My left antenna is chipped — I keep turning the wrong way and bumping, then correcting.

The rock under me sweats. It weeps continuously from a thousand pores. Beads gather, swell, break, run down in slow fingers. I lap. I put my mouth to the weeping black and drink bacterial skin and mineral water off a razor. The stone is too young to be smooth. Obsidian blades, basalt needles, silica crusts like frozen foam. I learn with my whole body not to put my weight down wrong. Everything shines.

It is hot. That is the first rule. Outside is cold enough to kill. Here my feet would blister if I stood still, so I don't stand still. The tide keeps me moving.

And in my mouth — gripped sideways in my mandibles, or held up in my forelimbs when I need to lick — my knife.

No longer than an eyelash. A flake of obsidian knapped to a curve. I bit it free myself from the wall behind me, you can see the pale conchoidal pit where it came from if you look with eyes, which I don't have. I feel its edge with my palps. It is both clumsy and precise. My whole body has to lean into the cut.

I am licking slime off a ridge too sharp to put my tongue to, scraping with the flake, when the smell changes. Iron and rot and far-away green.

Windfall.

A housefly huge as a god, sodden on the wall three bodies up, drowned in condensation, stuck to wet glass. It came up on the updrafts from miles below, blundered in through the crack. The tide is already eddying around it.

I climb over two backs, get climbed over by one. I pin what I can — a leg joint, a wing base larger than myself — and saw. Back and forth. My body shudders with the work. *Click.* Glass on glass. That is our only song. We left chirp behind entirely. Under the tick of detaching water and the sigh of steam through the crack, that tiny knap and scrape of our house being broken into knives.

The wing comes free. I start to drag the meat away.

Someone grapples me. Three seconds, silent. A larger one, missing a hind leg and still climbing. We roll, knives flashing. He wins. Or I win. It flickers — twelve on that patch, thirteen — blink the steam out — nine. For a moment there are two of me, heat-bent, and both are bitten by steam. I walk off awkwardly holding two flakes now, one in each side of my mouth, meat dragging behind. Or he does. The flake you swear was in my mouth is in another's mouth. It doesn't resolve.

Below me the pan waits, milky and almost too hot to touch, palm-deep. Hundreds of copper-red amphipods layered belly-to-back, all black pinprick eyes turned up. If I slip — and I will slip, we all slip, the glass is wet — half a dozen will lunge half out of water at once to drag me down. They will graze and mate and cannibalize me without pausing between actions. My neighbors will immediately lean down to drink from the ripple my struggle makes, antennae flicking, unafraid.

Above me the snow keeps falling up and down, midges landing to drink, and I will eat them when they land. The rim-walker works endlessly, and she will eat me when I get slow. My droppings, my shed skin, my dead shell scraped clean by the next wave, will feed the yellow-red skin below the splash line that pulses when the seep surges. The bacterial sleeper breathing heat and sulfur and iron into food.

I do not build. There are no nests, no hives, no webs, no burrows, no caches. The rock is too hot and wet and sharp to let anything sit. So I carry. I hold my edge for life and then I drop it to become part of the floor. The bottom is paved with lost knives.

That is where you would find me. Not counted. Not countable. In the bowl you could step over near the top of the sky where nothing should live, with no green, no soil, only carried edges of my own sharp house — sweating, scraping, dripping, eating windfall from a world I will never see, endlessly replacing myself so fast that by the time you follow me, I am already someone else, still chewing.
