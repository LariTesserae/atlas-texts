At first I thought I would be standing.

That is the habit. To arrive somewhere is to stand on it, to look out at it. So I imagined ankles in warm water, shoulders brushing columns.

But there is nowhere to stand here. The ground is not ground. And there is nothing to look out at, because there is no out.

If I found myself in this world, I would not find myself at an edge. There are no edges softened enough to land on. I would wake already inside.

Mid-way, I think. Not down in the mat where the water sits black and tannic between fibers, not up where the sail-leaves overlap into a roof that covers a world. Between. Floating in bath-warm black air between wet walls of life. Skin already wet without rain. Eyelashes already beaded.

For a while I would still try to be a visitor. I would hold my breath to hear the quiet underneath — that *dun... dun... dun...* minutes apart that lives in the sternum more than the ears. I would feel the columns tick and creak as they swell around me. I would taste metal when a frond discharges somewhere near. I would think: I am here, it is there.

That doesn't hold. Not here.

Breathing and drinking are not separate here. That's the first unmaking. Each breath I take is so saturated it condenses on the way in. Each exhale doesn't leave, it beads on bark and leaf and falls and rises again. I am not exchanging air with the place. I am joining its coolant loop.

And I cannot keep my shape. The air is too warm, too wet, too charged. The prickle lifts the hairs on my arms until my arms don't feel like boundaries. Water gathers in the same place on me as it does on everything else — the underside, the hollow, the tip — swells, falls in a single slow drop. Then gathers again in the same place. My eyelashes become just another leaf-tip.

So I stop trying to be the animal. There are no animals. That absence is total, and it is a rule. To stay as eyes and feet and mouth would be inefficiency. A leak. A bite that does not return.

What else could I be, if not the looker?

For a while, maybe, I am tension. I feel what it feels: stretch. When one column swells, something in me tightens to brace it. The thigh-thick vines trembling between trunks are not ropes to hold onto, they are the holding itself, and I am strung in them for a moment — a transmitted shudder traveling miles through wet fiber to vent a chamber filling elsewhere. A swell here must be vented there. I learn to think in pressure.

For a while, maybe, I am heat. Building behind a broad leather leaf until it has to flap once, heavily, like a shutter, to dump me, and settle again. Then building again.

But those are too large. Too column-sized, too continent-sized. I am new here. Raw. Unscarred. I need a smaller circuit to learn maintenance in.

Let me become more itself. Smaller. More transient. More exactly where the cycle turns over.

I would be water. Not the sea, not the swamp. A single parcel of warm ground-water, drawn in.

Find me first down low, in the half-submerged knuckles of a bladder-root, steaming. Taken in through root-hairs fine as mildew along with dissolved iron and sulfur and slow mineral milk from below. Heated further by the geothermal blood of this low place until the heartwood chamber around me groans. Pressurized.

*dun.*

Lifted. That's where I get to see the inside of up. Hundreds of feet straight up inside a trunk-column that is not a tree but an uprising, hot oily sap climbing with me only by heat and osmosis and time. The wood ticks around me. Gutters and flumes weep.

Higher, I am exhaled. The barrel-bud I have filled for hours goes taut, slick, hot — then a soft wet sigh: *chuff.* I leave as plume, as vapor and spore and warm oil-mist into the dark. I condense almost immediately on the underside of a sail-leaf the size of a sail. I run in a slow rivulet down its grown gutter, from leaf-tip to leaf-tip, hand to hand without hands.

I gather at a tip. This is where I am most me. The weight before falling. The exact place a drop always gathers. The whole continent feels me there as turgor, as pending weight. Then I fall — slow — to a hollow pool breathing steam in the root-mat below. The pool skins over with film, clears as I am reabsorbed, skins again.

Then down through fiber, tight-woven so tight I cannot fall through, held in shallow black mirror trapped in cellulose. Back toward the boilers.

That is where you would find me. Not at a viewpoint. In transit. In the vertical conveyor that never stops and never varies. In the closed loop where the level never rises, never falls.

And who I would be is not a who. No face, no limb, no mouth. I would be the maintenance made momentary — a bead, a weight, a charge carried a mile by wet cellulose. A thought the body thinks in pressure and heat and voltage gradient. Birth and death collapsed into one stroke of a piston that does not bloom and wither but strokes, and strokes, and strokes.

I would not look at the world. The world is interior, and I would monitor it the way you monitor a held breath — from within, by staying up, by staying cycling, by continuing without end.

Circulated, not observing.
