I come down into it without climbing.

That's where I find myself first — not at the rim looking in, not at the center, because there is no center here. Mid-slope of the lowering, where the air turns held-warm and my nostrils dry from the inside out. The light is white-gold and does not move, and will not move.

At first I think I would be walking. That I would be the figure pushing through stem-against-stem, copper-green at the ankles, sulfur and oxide at the waist, plates overhead ringing faintly when the sun hits them. I think I would be trying to get across, toward that pale far rim where the thicket thins and the salt shows white.

But I walk for twenty minutes and the rim is no closer. I turn and the slope I came down is no farther. The forest is larger inside than its basin. So no — I am not walking. Walking implies arrival.

Let that go.

Then I think, maybe I would be the thing looking up. On my knees in the fresh mineral — salt pan and puffed gypsum and black glass still edged, still biting through cloth — looking up at the underside of a plate-leaf the size of a serving tray. Learning to see the veins branch and re-branch at exact angles down past sight, down to interference. Hearing the high thin ticking at the edge of hearing quicken, almost imperceptibly, as I pass under.

That is closer. Because I am being read. The canopy above me brightens by a fraction. It feels my heat, my capacitance, my strange wet interior, and files it without judgment.

But I am still thinking of myself as eyes. As a visitor. Let that go, too.

If I stay — and late never comes, the light stays exactly where it was — I stop trying to find water. I stop listening for animals. My sweat has already dried before it could bead. My tears evaporated on the lash an hour ago and I have not gotten thirstier since, nor less thirsty. My body has been paused at the edge of need.

I sit. That is where I actually find myself.

Not in the open. In the dense dry shade where the copper-green presses close, near-touching, layered, saturated. The carpet gives spongily under me but sheds no juice. The air smells of hot dust and pepper and, when I shift, a puff of crushed frond lifts — cinnamon and hot circuitry and ozone. My skin is powdered white with salt. My breathing is too loud because there is no water in the air to soften sound. My shadow has fallen half a step to the left of where it should, and stays there even when I shift my weight.

At eye level, a seed hangs. A pale dandelion clock the size of my fist, halted two meters off the ground, each filament perfectly still. Trembling without moving.

And this is where what I am starts to become itself.

At first I thought I would be the tuner — tall as a deer, narrow as a folded umbrella, made of the same plate material, legs folding at impossible joints without sound. I wanted to be that elegant, that anhydrous. To press my flat head to a frond and take charge as dimming light traveling down the neck, to shiver with stored light and step into denser green leaving no print. Oil and glass and slow electricity. A life that never needed a sip because sipping was never invented.

But I am not that. I cannot be that yet. I am too wet. I am a cloud that crossed the rim and should have thinned and dissolved, but didn’t quite. I slosh when I swallow. Too loud.

No — what I am is the other suspended thing.

Look around me once I learn to see: brown lace leaves caught between live stems, hanging for how long? There is no rot here without water to rot with. They neither fall nor decay. Seeds halted mid-air that, if touched, do not scatter but ring very quietly and resettle exactly where they were, as if time nudged and then thought better of it.

I am allowed, here, to not finish.

That is the third rule and it finally holds me gently enough to rest in: nothing is allowed to finish. Growth halts mid-gesture. Death halts mid-fall. So my thirst halts. My panic halts. My need to understand halts.

I become a paused wetness. A reference sample. The forest does not need to change to keep me, it just holds the light that hits me a fraction of a second too long and releases it slightly slowed. That is why when I look at my hands through a plate-leaf I see four fingers slightly offset, as if I am out of register with myself. I am. My light is being edited.

If you came, still enough — truly still, your loud wet breathing throttled — you would see me the way you see a tuner, only rarely.

Not eating so much as tuning. Head tipped back to the plates. Dimming and brightening in counter-rhythm with the whips that lean almost-touching and knock softly, papery, without wind. The ticking quickens around me.

I do not drink. I have solved thirst by being refused it. I store what I am given: warmth on the shoulders without damp in it, dapple from struck glass, a shade-puddle where two suns reflect with no surface to reflect them.

I am not plant, not animal, not machine that was never built. I am the file. The stored reading of what wet felt like, held in dry lushness, in a young world that refuses to age.

Lush and dry and warm and strange — held.
