I thought at first I'd find myself standing.

Up on the shelf where the air is thin enough to make the light hurt, boots in the wet mat, looking east toward the white plumes going off the edge. Upright. Like a visitor. Like the maintenance woman who comes once a fortnight with a rake to clear the grate, check the oil, and leave again.

But nothing upright lives here. That's the first correction.

So not that. I wouldn't be standing long. I'd sink.

Half an inch, water welling up clear and brown as tea around my ankles, the whole sponge sighing under me. Knees going numb through the trousers because I knelt to look closer. Because you have to kneel here to see anyone at all.

For a while I thought I might be an event. Something with wings and hurry in it.

A ptarmigan, mottled white-brown, bursting out from behind a till shoulder and gone in three seconds. A water pipit, brown and streaked, shouting impossibly loud between gusts at the edge of a pool before the wind takes the midges and the sentence with them.

But I don't want to be gone in three seconds. I don't want to be scouting. The birds haven't moved in yet, evolutionarily speaking. They're just visiting a place too wet for burrows and too young for cover. I'm not visiting.

Lower, then.

Knee-high after forty years? A spruce down near the rim, flagged hard east, creeping instead of rising, needles dark with resin and half the year stung with ice. Trying to be a tree by agreeing never to be one.

That's closer. To stay by staying low. To live sideways.

But even closer is flatter.

Two inches tall and decades old. Leaves the size of fingernails. A dwarf willow with its trunk crawling through sphagnum, using last year's dead moss as soil. Or a starry saxifrage throwing up a stalk so thin it trembles in the updraft off the falls. Or one of the thousand white heads of cotton grass around a still pool, nodding all one way when the wind comes up.

I tried those on. I liked the tremble.

Then I went lower, and redder, and I stopped trying on.

In a hollow where the mat is three feet thick, electric green on top and rust-red underneath where it is drowning and becoming itself again, in water an inch below everything, rimmed with cotton grass, within earshot of the roar but sheltered by a round-shouldered boulder still scratched from ice — there.

No bigger than a coin. Red on green. Waiting in the wet.

A sundew.

Tentacles beaded and out. Patient in a way that isn't hope, exactly. There are almost no flies up here. The midges rise in a brief glitter and are blown flat. But I wait anyway. That's what I do. I glint.

Around me, underneath me, the bulk of me that is more than me: sphagnum growing a millimeter a year. Roots in moss. Shoots in humus that is barely humus yet. Liverworts black-green as wet leather sealed to weeping slab. Cushion moss hard as a fist. In the pool a few feet off, tadpoles slow as minutes, commas of black life content not to be upright.

Above me, white sun, not gold. Cold that makes it whiter. Clouds catching on the rim most afternoons, rain threading down granite in a thousand temporary runs. Smell of cold iron and peat and crushed green. My breath — if I breathed — would show even at noon.

And always the sound. The low full-body roar in the sternum, falls and wind. And underneath it when the wind drops for a moment, the other resident, my strange upright neighbor a quarter mile toward the lip: the held organ note. The turbine the size of a truck spinning on meltwater in its gray concrete hall, governed, automatic, blinking green to no one. Ozone and hot oil for ten feet around the door, then swallowed by rain.

We understand each other, the machine and I. We both wait. We both feed on what passes through. It spools up over hours on spring surge. I close over hours on a midge that may never come.

But I am not the machine. I am not the visitor with numb fingers. I am not the bird clattering off.

I am low and total. I am floor that is not floor. I hold water. I am held by stone. I grow out of my own corpse, a millimeter a year, while eight hundred feet away all that sheet-water I am soaked in throws itself off its unfinished edge, throws up another rainbow and then another, doubled in the spray.

Find me there. Kneel until your knees go numb. Look for red on green.
