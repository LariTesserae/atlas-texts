I thought, coming up, that I would be the one who climbs to look.

That is the first shape I offer and discard. My boots are too loud for that. I hear them as soon as the slope gives up — crunch, crunch, obscenely loud on the salt pavement — and I understand I cannot be a witness here without first becoming quieter than I am.

I thought then I would be her. The tender. It would be dignified, to wear the cloak of husks and move between cairns with a feather. But I am not that still yet. I watch her — real or remembered, mid-step between two markers — and she does not look at me. She rights a toppled stone with two fingers and goes on. There is no room in her slowness for me to step into all at once. I would stir dust. I would bruise.

So I go lower.

Past the beehive hut with its milk-colored roof, past the line of flint flakes left exactly where hands left them, past the rule-stones by the waist-high door, I find a shallow pan off the cairn-way. Not on the path. To be on the path you must know where you are going. I don't yet.

The pan is brimming with the ground-sleepers, shoulder to shoulder, edge overlapping edge. I kneel because my legs want to and because the silence makes standing feel rude. The salt rime exhales cold through my knees. Up close they are not white. They are opal. Light moves under the shell slower than sap.

This is where I find myself. Not above, among the hanging lattice, though they are over me from knee-height to past focus, wingtip almost to wingtip, bead-black eyes at both ends of every curled body looking both ways along their own curve. Not among them — I have not earned suspension. But under them. In their weather.

At first I am only the crunch that stops. The breath that hangs a moment too long before it thins. The pulse filling my head. I put my hands in my sleeves against the thin autumn that never changes and I try to do what the place does: nothing.

It is harder than it sounds. My eyes keep hunting for movement. My mind keeps hunting for use. What eats what? What is this *for*? The accounting fails. Lichen stars hard as enamel in the slab-joints, a rust cushion no bigger than a thumbnail, a grey beard trembling under a lee stone without wind — none of it would feed a mouse, let alone hundreds of millions. And yet they hang. And yet the opalescence moves.

*Tik… tik… tik.* Somewhere above. Cooling ceramic. I hold my own breath to hear it better, and in holding it I become a little more like them.

That is when it comes clearer, what I would be. Not the herder, not the herd. Not the visitor who shouts and leaves a brown bruise on the nearest wings that never fades. I would be the thing that learns, very slowly, not to leave.

I lie down — no, not lie. I settle. On my side at the edge of the pan so as not to cover any of them, my cheek near enough to the salt that I taste it with my skin. A wing membrane of one of the low hangers brushes my forehead, dry as paper, cool as stone, utterly still. I do not touch it back. *Do not touch the hanging ones.* I have read the grandmother's scratch. I obey.

My breath comes shallow. *hhhh, sssh.* Tongue sticks to teeth. Dust motes hang with my exhale. A pallid a mile away looks exactly the same size as the one at my face, and if I stare through the veined clarity I see not ground but paler sky, another plain, another me looking up. I stop staring. Shadows lie five degrees wrong under every pebble and do not lengthen. The bleached coin sun does not move. An hour that is not an hour passes.

And in that non-hour, I become more myself: the tender-in-training that the plain will tolerate.

Not yet white with salt to the eyebrows. Not yet sewn into husks. My cloak is still my own skin, still too warm, still too loud inside. But I have a task my hands can do without making anything new. With the edge of my sleeve — for I have no feather yet — I brush, very lightly, the white exhaled dust from the shoulder of a ground-sleeper nearest my mouth. It does not wake. Waking is not a category here. But the opal under its shell shifts, oil on water, and for a moment I feel my heart slow to meet its ticking.

That is who I would be. The second figure, far behind the first woman, almost a stone, almost a shimmer, learning the consonantless whisper so as not to bruise. The one who walks the line after she has walked it, checking that the cairns are still three or four stones high, that no boot — mine, especially mine — has strayed onto the rust cushion, that nothing has been left that was not there.

I would not hunt. To kill one would be meaningless, meat is dry salt. I would not farm. I would not make. I would go down, when water is needed, to the wet rock days below where the air thickens and stinks of life, drink, climb back up empty-handed, resume.

Mid-step between two cairns. Breathe shallow. Want little. Endure.

If you came up after me, you would mistake me for another stone.
