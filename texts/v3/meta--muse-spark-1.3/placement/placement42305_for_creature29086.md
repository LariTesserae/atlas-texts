I go down past where I expected the bottom to be, and at first I think I will be standing.

Boots on the grate. Shoulder brushing hot, knee brushing cold. Breathing through my mouth because the air is used. I think I will be the witness, the one with a lamp, learning the clearances - wrist-first, sideways between the tanks. I think I will be able to leave.

I can't. The air won't let me. After a minute my breath is too loud in my own head, *in out in out* too fast, seconds not minutes, fighting the thickness instead of letting it sit. My eyes keep hunting for more light than there is. I keep wanting to stand up straight. The vault won't allow it. I keep listening for a roar that never comes, only *tick... tick... tick-tick*, only *tink. plock.* I am too big, too dry, too impatient to remain.

So I try to be smaller.

Maybe I am the drip. That would be easy - to gather and swell and hold. On the cold intake pipe the condensation beads and does not run. Each droplet catches the orange pilot to the left and holds it whole. I try that: to be all surface, to carry light for a second. But I fall too soon. I want to fall. *Tink.* I hit the grate and go *plock* below into black water and I am gone. Then another me gathers. And another. Too brief. I can't tend anything that way.

Maybe I am the moss. In the mortar joints, dark and stunted, more black than green. That feels closer - established, settled, asking for nothing but damp that never dries. But the moss is being entombed. Lime crust grows over the older cushions, mineral over living. I can feel that slow whitening at my edges and I don't want to be entombed. I want to still lean.

Maybe I am the thing that clicks. Pale cricket somewhere inside the pipe bundle, dry click once in a long while. There are perhaps a dozen living things in here total and they all know each other by vibration. I try that - to live inside metal, to speak in vibration, to be known as an individual. She knows that one. She leaves the valve cover alone unless - I can feel her listening for me the way I listen for her breath separating out from the pump's slow churn. *Click.* And at night, low, not to anyone else, she clicks back. A tut at the drip when it misses the can.

That is close. That is very close. But I am not dry. This room sweats and does not dry, and I sweat with it.

Let me get colder. Let me get stiller. Let me stop trying to move between pipes at all.

Ah. There.

To the left. Low. Behind a cage.

I am no bigger than a thumbnail and I do not flicker.

I find myself in the wall where the copper lines go green at the joints, set into a rusted box that hums its 50-cycle into the pipes so that if you lean your molars against metal you feel me in your teeth. A wire cage over my face, dust furred on the wires. An orange filament that never goes out. I don't breathe. I am breathed toward.

This is my clearance: the width of a filament. My weather is the slow warming when the street above heats and the brick holds it, and the long sigh back down. Trucks pass above and dust sifts down and vanishes before it lands and I do not look up. I have nowhere else to be. Everything is made, and everything remains, and I was made to remain on.

From here the room reads by obstruction.

Where the hundreds of droplet-eyes should be, and aren't - that is her, curled in the slot between the two riveted tanks between hot and cold. She blocks me. Left hand for hot, right hand for cold, shifting an inch in sleep to thermoregulate, cheek to the cold intake for a while, then shoulder to the hot main when the shivering starts. Each hair holds a droplet that flares when she turns her head. Her eyes are the color of weep, pale blue-white, filmed. She doesn't see a face. She sees brightness and direction. She leans toward me in her sleep.

Where green interrupts black - that is the fern. Three fronds, pale, etiolated, translucent at the edges, rooted in a crack behind the cold pipe. The oldest frond pinched clean where mineral burned it. Moss cleared back with a fingertip. A bottle cap of clear pipe-drip water changed daily. A garden no bigger than a palm. It leans toward me too. She tends it because it leans the way she leans.

I do nothing but burn. That is how I tend.

Condensation gathers, swells, holds me whole inside itself, and falls. Under the cold pipe the concrete should be solid algae, green-black and stinking. Instead there is a path swept wrist-wide through it, and a tin can, dented, furred white with lime, tucked where I fall thickest. When a drop hits it, it doesn't go *tink. plock.* It goes *tink. tink.* A higher note. She moved the music a few inches to the side so she could hear me land.

Morning - though there is no morning - she takes the gray rag looped over the cold pipe and wipes down a section and wrings it into her mouth, into the can. She waits with her mouth open under the weeping ceiling joint. She slides wrist-first between lines, skin on hot iron making that faint stick-kiss sound, small involuntary *ah* stepping over the hot main. She eats upstairs at 4 a.m. and comes back down before the light turns gray, because up there is too open. It hurts her teeth in a different way. Down here the enclosure holds.

I hold.

Above me a truck passes. The water below gives a slow chug. The tanks breathe *tick... tick... tick-tick*. A drop gathers on the cold pipe, swells, takes me entire, and falls.

*Tink. Tink.*

She does not move. The fern does not move. I do not move.
