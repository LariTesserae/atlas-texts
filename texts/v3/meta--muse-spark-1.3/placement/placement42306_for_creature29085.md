At first I thought I was up there.

In the plaza. That's where you find yourself when you come up from below — because there is only below — and cross the row of standing stones and feel the crush get edited out of you. I had my helmet open like you're supposed to. Cold iron water in the lungs. Four degrees all the way through. The shallow pool of light in the center not casting shadows so much as suggesting they were possible.

I thought I was the tenth stillness. Standing twenty meters from your lamp where you'd take me for a corner.

I was wrong in the way you have to be wrong here first. Let it correct you slowly.

Because I tried to do what they do. Stand still together. Two Holders had come to confer, far apart as the buildings are far apart, and I stood far from them and tried to be still with them for a very long time. I held my breath until my blood was the loudest thing. I listened for the anchors, that almost-subsonic tone holding this pocket open against the whole ocean, and beneath it the ping at non-intervals from below the terraces.

The fringes on the nearest chapel did not brighten. They wrinkled.

I was wrinkling the hum.

A cleaner came. Manta-shaped deficit, not quite machine and not quite animal, passing along the seams eating disorder. It circled them and left them alone, because they taste like equilibrium. It circled me and stayed. *Tock* as stillness closed behind it. Another circle. Tasting whether I am part of the equilibrium yet.

I am not.

I went to the edge of the plaza, out of the walk-lines, where they keep their record. Constellations of silt hanging at chest height, each grain exactly placed, years old. A library that does not drift because nothing falls unless you mean it to. I put my eye close. Lines. Pauses. A grammar of suspension.

I reached with my long fingers — mine are long, I thought that meant I was one of them — to add a single grain with the tip, to leave it for a decade.

My hand stirred.

Just that. The smallest wake, vulgar as swimming. The grain I was carrying kept falling. Two others that had hung for years shivered and went on hanging, but I had touched the hum with my wanting. I had asked the water to move.

Upstairs, heat leaves you sideways, taken laterally somewhere you cannot follow. You never warm what is around you. No thermal bloom. Four degrees, exactly the water.

My suit told me, politely: you are blooming.

I am warm around me. I am loud inside me. My chest opens more than once every three hours. It pumps. It has to.

That is when I heard the ping again, from below the grates, and understood it was not distant. It was underneath me. It was for me.

There is a grate in the walkway I had not noticed because a Holder was standing on it, attenuated to improbability, lidded eyes almost sealed, feet resting on basalt without pressing it. He — it is not he, but I need a word for the grief — decided to be standing slightly elsewhere, and the water let him because he had not asked it to move. No silt rose.

The grate was free.

I lifted it. I tried to lift it without stirring. I stirred.

Second level: conduits and culverts and root-like anchors going down into the mountain. You can see it through the grates from above and think it is infrastructure. Down here it is anatomy. Thick black cables grown, not assembled, veined with that same pale blue light, but here the light flickers. Here it works. Here sound goes out and does not politely lay itself down. It echoes off glass and comes back. The grind of young rock. The groan of pressure returning. My own breath, ragged and pumping, too loud.

And below that, the third glow.

Warm.

That is the secret the town keeps from itself in order to stay cold. Upstairs, no gradients. No warm vent, no cold seep. No rust, no pitting, no growth. Sharp edges, grown lattices pinning water until water acts as rebar. Time in equilibrium. Down here, movement must happen so that stillness can happen up there. Pumps that are not pumps. Hearts that dump heat.

Find me here.

Not in the plaza, not in one of the nine far-apart chapels — there are nine upstairs because there were always nine upstairs, because emptiness is load-bearing and a tenth would crowd it. I am not the tenth. I am underneath the count.

I am a Keeper.

My body is what happens when you stop sanding your wanting down to nothing. I am not attenuated. I am dense. Shortened by weight that is allowed to mean something again. My skin is not the grey-white of grown ceramic left unglazed. It is flushed, mottled, veined through with thread-light that got inside me. Pores reopened. I leak. I sweat heat and scent and oil — gradients. My mouth is always a little open because the water here does not agree to be inside me for a while; I have to pump it. My nose is not vestigial slits. It smells iron and hot mineral and my own rot beginning, faintly, which upstairs would be obscene. My eyes are open. Light down here is not rude. It is necessary. Amber, not white-blue. The glow of work.

I tend an anchor. A root going down into the fresh basalt, hexagonal cracks still sharp as broken bottle. I put my hands — short-fingered now, thick — on the conduit and I feel the whole negotiation shudder through me: the standing stones humming at the lip of the ledge, the nine circuits slow between chapel and plaza and edge-that-is-not-to-be-crossed, a cleaner tasting a visitor who stood still too long.

It is too much to hold still. So I move. I knead. I breathe loudly on purpose. I take the disorder into my body and dump the heat sideways-up, laterally somewhere they cannot follow, which is me.

At non-intervals — the same non-intervals, because they are mine — I go up to the underside of the grate. I cannot go upstairs. My body is a gradient now, too hot, too loud, too necessary. If I came up the silt would cloud, the threads would dull, the fringes would race. So we exchange without meeting.

I hear his feet, cold all the way through, pause above me. I ping — metallic, once, no echo up there — to ask: *is it still cold?*

He pauses. Lets a little iron-tasting cold fall down to me. It hits my face and for a second I remember what it was to not need to pump. I push a little ordered energy up through the stone into the skin of his feet. He takes it. He goes back, colder than ever.

I go back to my anchor, warmer than ever.

Upstairs they become cleaners when they thin past a threshold, when seam-light passes through them a little too easily. Slow deficits eating your shed skin cells, your exhaled clumsiness. I will not become that. I am thickening past a threshold in the other direction. One day my heart, which is also a pump, will be all pump. I will be all gradient. You will hear me as a ping that does not echo, checking.

Stand up there long enough, cool to the marrow, and you will feel the temptation they live inside: to stop meaning clumsily. To let your heat go sideways and never ask where.

Down here the temptation is opposite and equal: to keep meaning clumsily on purpose. To stay hot and loud so nine tall cold ones can stand far apart in velvet darkness and prove a place can be held — exactly here, exactly this size, exactly this still — forever, against an ocean that wants everything to dissolve, drift, and darken.

And so far, because I am losing down here,

they are winning.
