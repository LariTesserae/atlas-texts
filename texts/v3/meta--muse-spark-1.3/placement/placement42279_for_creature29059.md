At first I think I would be up high.

That's where I start, every time I try to place myself. On the rim looking down, the one with working eyes. The witness. The mapper. The visitor with a headlamp and a notebook sealed in plastic against the weep, telling myself I will learn this place by seeing it.

But there is nothing to see. That fantasy dies fast in the sleet.

My eyes keep waiting to adjust and they never quite do. The dark here is not empty, it's full - full of fog and wingbeat and drip - and my eyes are the wrong organs. I keep flicking my lamp on and the roost shudders, a hundred meters of white fur rippling, and somewhere above the screamers tilt down. I learn faster than my pride: I turn it off. I put it away. I will not be the one who brings light. Light brings death low on black wings.

So maybe, I think, I would be the talker. The trader from below with news, with questions. I open my mouth to shout two feet and the frying hiss of ten million clicks eats it. My voice is too high, too fast, too loud. My breath comes out and does not dissipate - it hangs, it thickens, it joins the air - and they find me by the wrongness of it. Too fast. Too high. Soap and far-away green on my skin.

I am too tall, too. That is the stupidest, most insistent fact. To stand upright here is to lift my head out of the warm into the kill-cold. The heat lives knee-high between the pillars, in envelopes you have to crouch to stay inside. After an hour my back screams from the wading bend. After a day I stop trying to stand. The slick-orange demands it anyway. One boot-crack on wet glass and I am on my knees in warm black slush.

Low. I have to become low before I am anything else.

Don't look for me at face-level. There are no faces at face-level, no doorways to stand in. Look lower.

You would find me under an arch, where the sudden quiet means a dense roost overhead holding its breath. I have learned to go still when they go still. At first I huddled there because I was lost, because the drone of the pillars had dropped lower and that meant fog-bank coming thick, and I didn't know where else to be. I pressed my cheek to the vitreous wall because the fog was too thick to move in, and felt warmth breathe through.

Inside is holds, not rooms. I am not inside yet. I'm outside learning how to be inside.

An old one finds me - not by looking, they have retired their eyes, smooth ridges sealed with oil - but by ear, by nose. Asymmetric dishes swiveling, triangulating my clumsy heart hammering at altitude. A warm, wet palm pressed briefly to my sternum. Not unkind. Just reading: thirty beats too fast, lungs too shallow, blood too thin. A low inquiry in my molars, 30, 40 hertz, patient. *Stay close. Stay low. Do not step where it is alive.*

I thought I would be an engineer. I came up through black basalt switchbacks thinking about gravity lattice, resonance anchors, thermal capillaries. I put my forehead to a leaning pillar like I saw the maintainer do - forehead and both palms - to feel. What I felt was not a machine. It was a chest. A slow thrum answering my teeth. The city grows a millimeter a year around it, self-healing cracks with fresh mineral. There is nothing to fix fast. Only to attend, generation after generation, to bleed a capillary with a bone needle, to coax poured-frozen wall around a corner with a fingertip. I don't know how. I am too impatient. My attention skitters.

So give me a rake. That's where I finally settle, where the image stops shifting and becomes itself.

Nightly, by the thousands, the moth-bats fall. White-furred, fist-sized, still warm. I learn to move in the wading crouch along the lee walls with a wide rake, gathering without grief and without waste. The slap-suck of my bare feet now - I gave up boots, they were too cold, too loud, they cracked where they should have whispered - joins the other slap-sucks. Meat to the culvert troughs for the shrimp and gulpers, where the water crackles with clicks. Fur to be felted while still warm with body-oil into sodden, stinking capes. I wear one now. From a distance, huddled motionless to conserve heat, I look like another shingle of sleeping wings. That is intentional. That is the first time I belong: when I am mistaken for roost.

In the day - there is no day, but in the awake time - I crouch at the causeway edge where seep beads on vertical glass. I run my fingertips over the slick-orange without breaking it, feeling thickness. Then a lick. I am learning to taste. Silica, sulfur, iron means ready. Iodine-sweet means not yet. When ready, a lane at a time with a flat basalt shard, leaving starter to regrow. Paste eaten warm, mixed with rendered ice-worm fat, those arm-thick white things turning in the marsh mud, and shrimp pounded whole. I eat stone and heat and sulfur breath, one step up from bacteria.

My eyes are still there, under lids. They have not sealed. But I keep them closed. Not as a rule. As a reflex. I navigate now by heat on the cheek, by sound-shadow, by tongue. By the press of warm air meaning shelter. By the mineral tang meaning open water.

Find me in the hold at sleep time. Low, windowless, gut-warm curled around a heat capillary, floor slick with living film kept back by stone lips. I sleep in the pile, ten, twenty deep, layered like the moth-bats. I am still too bony, still breathe too fast, but my heart has slowed. Thirty-five. Thirty-two. The old ones in torpor barely move for days in the deep cold, and the young - small, blind, almost hairless, clinging with hands and feet, clicking in play to learn echolocation - turn them so condensation does not pool in their ears. I help turn. My hands are useful for that. My weight is useful for warmth. My exhalation joins the one visible plume that the exchangers drink and return as fog.

I am not a holder. Not yet. Maybe never fully. I am the extra body that makes the pile warmer. The learner with closed eyes. The tall thing learning to be low.

Walls rising on all sides into fog and dark, white wings moving ceaselessly over like snow that will never land, black water seeping up through new stone, the whole basin humming in my bones - and me among it, moving slowly through the press of bodies, raking the dead, farming film, tending heat.

The mountain is still making itself. The city is still making itself. I am still making myself, wet and loud and crowded, and I do not hurry.
