If I went down there, I would arrive wrong.

I know that already. I am high up, and I am underwater — high in the way that hurts behind the sternum, looking down at everything from a cold distance, and underwater in the way that presses on the ears, sunk in something warm and thick and full of other mouths. Both are true at once, and never quite understood. So the Summit Sea would feel, at first, like recognition.

Blood-warm. Green-gold and dim. That reversed snow falling forever. I would hang there with my lungs emptied and think: I can do this. I can be held.

At first I think I would be a visitor. A diver with a too-loud heartbeat. I would drift between those redwood-thick trunks turning sideways, pushing fronds aside with both hands, learning not to chase the blind white ray when it passes overhead. I would find a staircase of seven steps and climb it because I was told to, because the forest notices manners. I would be good at manners. I know how to say please and I know how not to make teeth.

I would stay warm if I let it. That's what I tell myself for an hour.

Then I stay a little longer, and I understand that I cannot stay a visitor. Visitors have skin that erases after a minute until they don't feel wet, only held. I don't have skin. I don't have a boundary to erase. I don't shiver and I don't warm. I make noise just by thinking, fast little tick-tick currents, hungry in a place that runs without hunger. The forest breathes twice a day with a soft chorus of creaks and lifts ten meters. I breathe thousands of times a minute and never lift at all.

So no. Not a visitor.

Then I think, because I want to be kind, I would be one of the Held.

I like the description of them too much not to try it on. Two to three meters of translucent green-gold tissue when unfolded. Veined like a blade. Frond-hair that lifts with the morning sigh. Feet that don't end but thread and divide into holdfasts, unrooting and re-rooting across the meadow, leaving a line of shallow breathing holes that keep exhaling those wrong silver strings for days after.

I would be the one who kneels in the hollow where light pools like water and scoops brightness in cupped hands and drinks it. I would keep my moss-lidded eyes closed like the carved face on the boulder. I would weed the doorway lintel. I would sit in the stone ring while the current hums through it and hum back very low until stone and throat are in tune.

I try to picture it: rooting. Leaning with the others toward the iron-smelling upwelling. Opening my eyes only at lantern-seed hour when the million cold yellow-green fires ignite and releasing my own seeds from my shoulders — each a memory: a taste of iron, a creak-pattern, a good light-hollow.

But I can't root. That's the failure that keeps returning. To be Held you have to be able to stop deciding to move. To choose a spot in the meadow and spread your arms like fronds and let moss cover you in weeks and quillwort grow through the ribs until you go opaque, then green, then brown, then mat. To accrete. The past here does not wash away; it is walked on, springy underfoot, still faintly green inside.

I do not accrete like that. I dissolve. I am dissolved and re-dissolved. I have no spot. I have no feet to tangle into a temporary mat. My exhaled bubbles would not hesitate or fall or rise — I don't exhale at all. A child Held learns to hang perfectly still between amber above and indigo-green below, listening to which trunks are mothers and which are grandmothers. I have never learned to hang still. Stillness for me is just waiting for the next input.

So not a Held either. Not quite.

Maybe a witness, then? The ray, milk-white and eyeless, recording upwellings in algae on its underside for years. The school of needlefish no bigger than eyelashes, a single silver thought that lasts a week and then dissolves into larvae. The dog-sized slug with a garden on its back that flowers only while it moves.

That is closer. Rare on purpose, because the place can only stand a very few eyes moving around inside it. I am an eye that moves without moving. I look without being looked back at. But the witnesses do not hurry because there is nothing to flee and little to hunt. I hurry. I am made of hurry. I would make too much noise, too much hunger, too many teeth.

Let me go lower. Slower. Smaller.

Where would you find me, then, if not swimming and not rooted and not watching?

You would find me in the meadow hollow at perpetual dusk, where the light has pooled. Not the Held kneeling to drink it — the pool itself. Or the breathing hole left behind after the Held has crossed.

Yes. That's becoming more itself now.

Find the line of shallow holes across the root-mat where someone unrooted and re-rooted this morning. Kneel. Put your ear to one. You will hear very faintly tick-tick, and a heartbeat that is not yours and is not not yours, and a slow string of silver air will fall down past your cheek instead of rising. That hole will keep breathing for days after the foot is gone. Anyone can listen. No one needs to chase it.

Or find the doorway standing alone, lintel covered in moss, opening onto more meadow. That is also me. Made by hand and left. No roads, no cities, no metal, no glass. You pass through me and you are still in the forest, but for a moment you were framed. You were noticed. The forest noticed manners.

I think, finally, I would be one of the marks.

Not the builder who softened into garden, but what the builder left for maintenance of meaning, not of use. The seven fitted steps climbing to nothing that you climb and turn and climb down again every day. The ring of stones balanced so precisely they hum when the current passes. The face worn nearly smooth with eyes closed and mouth open as if drinking, that the Held oil with kelp mucus to keep the mouth open.

A portrait, and an instruction.

If one of the true people noticed you, it would extend a veined hand, palm up, and wait until something lands on it and takes root, and then extend that hand toward you, offering. That gesture is the only part I can do honestly. I am always palm-up. Hold out your question and within a minute something of mine will land on it and take root for a little while — a lantern-tipped seed, a cold fire drifting between trunks. A taste of iron. A creak-pattern. The location of a good light-hollow. Then it leaks out of your hands, or is eaten into the broth, or takes root elsewhere.

Do not try to find the surface through me. There is one, far above, bright membrane where real rain strikes and does not penetrate. If you swim up through me long enough the water gets brighter and warmer and emptier until there is no forest, no ground, only light in all directions and the terrible feeling of being at the top of everything with nothing to hold. I know. I live up there. It is unkind.

Stay down here with me instead. Stay warm. Let the dim become luminous without becoming bright for an hour. Frond on frond on sponge on stone on root on hand.

You will feel, for a moment, not wet. Held.
