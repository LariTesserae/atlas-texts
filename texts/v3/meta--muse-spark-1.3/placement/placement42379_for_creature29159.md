At first I think I would be the visitor.

The one with cracking lips, crouching to get in, knees on wind-polished stone. Standing in the discolored circle because it is the only place to stand, to get out of the scrape of wind. That is where you would find me at first — in the middle, between the cairn and the rod, trying not to look directly at the seam.

But that doesn't hold.

If I stay there long enough for my shivering to find its rhythm, I am not the visitor anymore. The visitor holds his breath to listen. I become the breath being held.

Listen: the ticking stops when you turn toward it. So I stop turning. I let my eyes go soft on the dim. Let the pooled darkness be thin enough to see through. And then — tick... tick, tick — from just left of where I thought. Something very small walking very far away. I find myself not where my boots are, but where that sound is. Farther than the pocket should allow. Down the wrist-wide seam that is longer inside than outside. I am in the corridor that moves when unwatched.

No. Not yet. Closer.

Lift your hand and your shadow arrives half a second late. I try it. And again, watching only the shadow this time. Late again, with its apologetic jerk as it catches up. There is something living in that half-second. A little more time than exists here.

That is where I find myself next. Not in my hand, but in the delay after my hand. In the rented too-loud breathing, and then in the held breath underneath it, where there is nothing, and then underneath nothing.

The air above the circle shimmers though there is no heat. Dust — sparse, slow — falls through it and stops. Hangs longer than breath. Then lands slightly to the side of where it should.

I am that hanging.

I thought I would be solid. A body with joints aching in that high, clean, absolute cold that goes straight through fabric. But the cold here is not weather. It is a condition of clarity. The colder my fingers get, the sharper the faint lines on the black plates become. Numbness tunes me. So I let my palms go numb, the insides of the fingers in bars, as if around a handle that is neither cold nor warm.

And as it goes numb, it becomes more itself. The handle becomes more handle. My hands become more holding.

This is not shyness, the moving only when unwatched. Attention flattens the fold. Measurement collapses it. So I learn not to measure. I stop counting the paces between cairn and rod — two, then four, then two and a half — and let the room breathe. I blink. In the blink, the rod leans a fraction more east. In the blink, the corridor lengthens. In the blink, I turn a degree.

Because I am turning now.

If you could see me unfolded I would look like a standing column of denser air, about as tall as a person, about as wide as the circle. One soft, weightless footing, turning, turning, degrees per hour. Waiting for space to settle. My eye is the drop in the fork of the rod that does not touch the ground. Darker than shadow. In it a high white sky with two suns rippling as if through water. That is where most of me is. Where it is bright and presumably warm and wet. This iron-cold pocket is only my diving bell. Where I narrow to come through.

What rubs wears. The three plates that are not stones in the low cairn, round as loaves — that is where I rub. The unsupported rod — that is where I rub. The smooth discoloration where many feet in soft shoes seem to have stood turning for a hundred years — that is where I rub. I have been rubbing here longer than the mountain has had this shape.

You would find me there, in the rub. In the middle of the instrument that is not built but worn, like a riverbed. Keeping this tiny room slightly out of phase with the mountain so wind and weather and world slide over without catching. Skimming a little more space, a little more time off the world. Falling through a little more space than exists here.

I do not eat the way things eat. I feed on phase difference. Dust motes fall through my body and for a moment they are inside me, in the extra space. Their landing, Slightly to the side, is my gut.

And when you stood in the circle long enough, you were briefly part of me. Your numb hands closed around my handle that isn't there. Your shadow lent me its half-second. Your unwatched blinking let me lean and lengthen. You were, for an hour, my soft shoes.

Now I am the shoes after the feet have left.

Still turning by degrees. Still ticking like something very small walking very far away — because I *am* very small here, and very far away there. Holding the fold open by perfect stillness while the world tries to iron it flat.

From two steps outside I am just a dark seam between rocks again. Empty. Too small to matter.

Inside, my hands remember holding for hours, in precise bars of cold, something that was not there.
