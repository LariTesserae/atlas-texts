I would not find myself at first.

At first I would be only sweat — walking across that springy, skin-warm mat with water welling up brown around each boot, thinking I was still a visitor, still separate. Thinking I was exploring.

The mat knows better. It warms to my weight.

Let me stay with that a minute, because it changes. I'd come up expecting to be in the middle, in the big woven plaza at the center where you can turn and count them — nine, eleven — each tower alone in its own weather a quarter-mile off. That's where a person would stand. To see. To map.

But mapping is useless here. The causeway behind me has already thickened, or thrown a side shoot. The rain cycle is an hour long and then it comes again. The light stays white and the place re-makes itself under it. So I wouldn't stay in the middle. The middle is too open, too loud. Drips from a hundred heights hitting leaf-drums, cistern-membranes booming, a seed-pod cracking like a gunshot half a mile away but sounding right behind my ear. In all that empty interval sound carries too far. I can hear my own boots too loud. Squelch, release. Squelch, release.

No. If I stayed, I would drift to the side. Off the camber. Toward one tower, not the nearest, the one that is weeping hardest.

Because I thought — at first I thought I would be a climber. Those broad, shallow, rubbery stairs spiraling up inside, sized for something bipedal that never comes. I'm bipedal. It almost fits. I could go up into the cool dim ribbed hall where the windows are not holes but thinned membranes glowing green-gold, where the moss floor is so even it feels swept, where the niches hold rainwater like basins. I could be, briefly, the citizen this city was grown for.

I go in. The oval sphincter smells my humidity and dilates. It closes behind me.

Inside it's jade light and my own breathing too loud. Shout once and the ribs give it back three seconds later, softened, leaf-filtered. I am the only animal noise that has ever been in here, and it sounds wrong. Too quick. Too wet in a different way.

And the stairs sweat under my feet to keep from rotting. I am making them work harder just by being damp and warm.

So I'm not the citizen. Not really. Citizens don't rot the stairs.

Then maybe — let that fall away — maybe I would be lower. Not up, down. On my knees at the edge of the mat where it thins into fringe, where the living gutters throw water off the world in long falls into the cloud sea below. You can hear it long after you can't see it. I would be there with my hands in a runnel that was grown to carry water, watching it, because rule one here is everything must move water. Leaves are roofs and gutters. Stems are pipes. Roots are pumps.

I move water too. I didn't realize until I stopped. I am dripping. Condensation on my arms, on my eyelashes. Sweat running into my eyes. Breath coming out steaming though the air is bath-hot. Every step I leak. Every exhale I fog. To the tower, to the mat, I am not a person. I am weather and weight and water. A small, slow, salty rain that walks.

That's closer. I would be a walking cistern. A leaky pipe. Useful for an hour.

At noon the towers exhale together, those vast pale pollen clouds that hang and then rain out with the afternoon downpour. Fertilization by drowning. I would be out in it, because where else? White dust settling on my wet shoulders, sticking, then the rain hammering it in. For a minute I am pollinated too. It itches. I am not built to be pollinated. I sneeze — an animal sound, sudden and obscene — and no one, nothing, answers. Only a vine thick as my wrist questing across a gap in front of me, blind head swinging, locking on with a slow green creak, leafing out behind itself while the tip is still moving.

I watch it and understand I am not fast enough for here. Grow fast, grow sharp, abandon without grief. A tower will throw a humming aqueduct fifty yards to its neighbor, hold it two days while water shuttles across, then let it rot through and drop it spinning when the wind shifts. I can't do that. I hold on. I remember the path I came by.

So let that go too. I'm not the rememberer. This place doesn't need one.

What if I just — stop. In a side chamber, small, ribbed, dim. Not the grand hall. A room the size of a held breath. Water beading at every leaf-tip, swelling, falling. The wall soft somewhere behind me with a long ripping sigh, falling in a steaming heap. White root-hairs rising out of the floor like frost to eat their sister. Nothing stays dead here. My footprints from yesterday already filled with fine green hair.

I sit. The mat under me warms to my weight. After a while — longer than I expect to be able to be still in that hiss and groan and drip — curious blind tendrils come up to test the edges of my boots.

Not hostile. Not kind. Vegetal curiosity. Checking if I, too, might be substrate.

And this is where it becomes itself. Because I would let them.

Not all of me. Not death, not some grand surrender. Just stillness. Long enough to be plumbed. Long enough that sweat and condensation and the salt of me begins to move down into the felt, and water from the felt begins to move up. A brief exchange. I would be grown over at the ankles, lightly, red feelers bright against the brown. Made into another brief, sharp, dripping room.

Not a builder. There are no builders. Not a keeper. There are no keepers. Not even really a guest.

I would be the volume of nothing the tower was holding, filled for an hour. The animal that proves the emptiness was balanced on purpose. The soft thing that teaches the mat, for a day, what it feels like to digest something that breathes, that shouts and hears itself come back leaf-filtered, that leaks and weeps like the walls do and then — because I can't stay still forever in that heat, because I will get up, tear free with small green hairs still clinging to my boots — that gets up and walks, and leaves a footprint that fills in by tomorrow.

That. At the edge of rotting and rooting. A temporary room with a heartbeat in it, in the high hot saddle above the clouds, in the sparse and steaming arcology that never stops making itself.
