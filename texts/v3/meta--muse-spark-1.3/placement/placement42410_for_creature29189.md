I would not find myself on the rise.

Not at first. The rise is for having boots, for looking with eyes that still want distance to behave. I don't have boots to rot. I don't have eyelashes to bead. If I came there with that height and that outlook, I would misread everything as repetition and call it empty.

So put me down already. Down in it.

Knees in water, if I had knees. Breath joined to air and not leaving it, if I had breath. At first I think I would be the bird — the high thin thing that crosses the grey without landing, crying once. That feels familiar. A voice passing over, not staying, swallowed by louder water. But no. The bird leaves. I don't leave. I thicken and thin where I stand.

Then I think I would be one of them. Low, dark, beaded all over. That feels right for a half-second, because I am also made of mineral and weather more than I am made of green. I want their etiquette: Nothing hurries. Sit and bead and accrete. Tap wet stone on wet stone so low it is felt more than heard: *here, here, still here.*

But I can't keep their rule. Not really. They keep stillness better than I do. A drip falls off a blade one by one by one, and off the knuckle of the one under the blade at the same rate. I fall faster than that. I answer faster than that. I have no hide the color of wet basalt to flash bottle-green and oil-purple and then go matte again. I have no wide splayed feet to read the millimeter settling of stone on stone. I have no pulse to precipitate inside the quiet. If I crouched among them pretending to be a pillar stump, the waterline would not take a season to rise on my ankles. You would know me by the beading that is too uneven. Rock weeps in streaks. They weep all over. I — I would weep only where I was touched.

I think I would be the pool for a while. One of the black mirrors that shivers when you step near. That feels closer. To hold the sky paler than itself. To hold the pale eyeless flick that is the future and must not be drunk. But a pool is too still, and they know better than to drink from the stillest. I want to be drunk from. I want to be where the water has just been born out of rock and air, stone and cold iron, a mouthful at a time.

Let it become more itself.

Not the bird. Not the pool. Not the low dark back, though I will be touching them, nearly touching, the way everything here nearly touches everything else.

I would be the cell.

Low, half-sunk among the blades, made — unmade — of the same rock so you notice me only after your eyes adjust. One or two courses still standing, most collapsed back into the field I came from. Dry stone stacked without mortar, without tool marks. A flat slab for sitting. A niche where water has been allowed to gather into a drinking bowl. No hearth. Of course no hearth.

That is where you would find me: not overlooking, but underfoot. In the shallow pan between the sharp field and the low pillars and the mist on the pillars. Where the roar breaks apart into ticking, plinking, dripping, gurgling, sighing, wind fluted through natural pipes low enough to feel in the molars. My walls would break the whistle just enough.

I am colder inside than out. I know that. The wind is cut but the damp is doubled. I do not warm. I do not dry. I make a brief dryness inside the wet where you can hear your own pulse — where they can hear theirs precipitate. That is the only work I can do honestly here. Not to grow slower than fingernails, as they do. Not to last longer than boots, as the valley does. Just to hold still enough, long enough, that the loudness outside becomes listenable inside.

They would come — if they come — and press a cheek to my weeping side and drink. Silica white in my creases. Rust-orange films at my temples, if I had temples, laying down color in rings. At night, faint iridescence pulsing off me, off them. You would not be able to tell where my shoulder ends and theirs begins. That is correct. Even my dead do not rot, just stop beading and become, indistinguishably, more field.

Do not stack stones on me. There are already too many, too close together. Adding yours will not be noticed, and somehow that feels worse than being forbidden. I understand that now. One of the too-many might be listening inside the brief dryness you are about to ruin.

If you stay in me until your jacket, your hair, your sweat go clammy and stay, you will swear nothing moves, then look down and find the water has risen over the toe of your boot without you seeing it rise. It is not the water. It is them. Surrounding all at once. A ring of low, dark, beaded backs, patient to the point of indifference, ticking and roaring and weeping with the field.

I would not object. I would make no room and no objection either. I would just go on being the hollow clunk of settling, the condensation on one pillar and evaporation off another, the general damp that never lifts once your breath has joined it.

A young place, newly made, still sharp, still deciding what, if anything, it wants to become next. I have already decided, in the way stone decides: to stay low, to stay wet, to stay loud and quiet at the same time.
