I don't arrive all at once.

At first I think I would be up there. It is the obvious hunger — to be in the gyre, to be one of the thousand, hollow and foil-thin and held. To take the hot updraft into my chest and not need to flap. To flash copper when I bank. I like the idea of being almost-air, of being weather instead of an animal.

But I am too heavy for that. I can feel it as soon as I imagine it. My body is wet inside. I carry water with me like a debt. I would fall out of the thermal. The heat that lifts them would lay me flat.

So then I think, maybe higher. Even lighter. One of the old ones. A manta fifty meters across, rippling once a minute. Patient beyond biology. Trailing a filament kilometers long just to bleed off charge. That patience appeals to me — to have circled the same lift since before the yardangs took their shape, to have forgotten skimming salt and learned to eat the ion wind instead.

But I am too fast for that. I twitch. I check. My thoughts discharge too often and too small. I could not hang nailed to the sky and mean it.

And then, because the piston is in my molars — *tuh — tuh — tuh —* speeding and missing and catching again — I think, maybe deep. Maybe I am the third animal, the first. The vast flat slow body whose back is the pan. That would be a relief: to not move, to breathe through stone organ-pipes, to taste iron only when a perfect bead sinks down through salt in time. To eat time by pumping it.

But I am too brief for that. I do not have decades, centuries. I miss a beat and I panic. It misses a beat and finds it again.

So I come down. Literally.

I find myself where a person would have to find themselves here: on the ground. Low. Between.

I am lying on my back between the salt curls, where the polygons lift into sharp white edges. My shadow is pooled black and short under me, except for a heartbeat when there are two suns and it throws a double edge and I don't know which one is mine. The salt is warm through my clothes. Copper-green dust has settled in the seams. Every bead near my elbow — thumb-sized, machined, still ticking as it cools — is crowned with a faint blue-white corona as the light thins without reddening. Static lifts off everything. The hairs on my arms stand up.

From here the sky is too full and the ground is too empty at the same time, and I can hold both because I am in neither.

The gyre is turning above me, wider than any town. The shadows stipple the white until the ground moves. The shirr of ten thousand wing-edges rasping fills my skull, and underneath it the organ tone slides as the wind is forced through the fluted yardangs — each ridge a different pitch, east to west, combed by a single hand. I hear the whip-crack three seconds before I see the filament discharge sideways from a high manta. I hear the rasp swell before the gyre banks. Sound before cause. As everything does here.

I am not smelting. I am not producing. Watch a low one come across a salt polygon just over my face — mouthparts unfolded into a wide sieve, scooping pale dust, thorax transparent while plates spark inside its abdomen — and I understand I have no gut for that. I cannot melt rock inside myself. I cannot extrude perfection and leave it behind with a ping and feel nothing about it.

What I have instead is a different hollow.

I lie still and I let the hot updraft hold me without lifting me. I breathe the high dry electric heat that smells of hot metal and ozone and struck stone, and I hold it in my chest a second longer than I should, the way they hold it in their bellies. Ballooned. Steering without flapping. My ribs are vaned. My lungs learn the slide between tones.

I become an instrument the place can play.

The piston moves through me. It does not get louder as I imagined walking toward the center, only slower, as if depth were time. Lying here I feel the miss — that dropped beat — in my teeth and then in my sternum, and then found again a minute later.

And because motion is conserved here but position is negotiable, I duplicate without meaning to.

Stare long enough at anything flying true in this light and the sky keeps a copy. I have been lying here long enough, breathing true. A meter to my left, in the next polygon, there is a salt curl shaped exactly like the space my body makes. Or it is my afterimage that did not fade. It solidifies. It breathes with me. For a while there are two where there was one. Then one of us peels upward to join the gyre — paper-light now, amber chitin thin as foil, belly empty and glowing faintly from within with the day's last dust — and you cannot tell which was first.

Let it be that one. Let it be me.

Not the smelter, not the sky-whale, not the deep pump. The copy the heat kept. The useless perfect bead that learned how to look up. The listener left between the salt curls, holding heat becoming work becoming heat again, loud the way a distant waterfall is loud — constant, impersonal, not an event but a condition — until I am polished, vented, fluted, settled into my own final shape.

Do not look for shelter. There is none and none is needed.
