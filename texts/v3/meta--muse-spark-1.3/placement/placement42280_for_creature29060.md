At first I think I would be where I am now — on my knees at the bottom.

Too big. Too loud. Boots making that wet sucking sound. Heart making that other sound. Breath hanging white and level and then sinking to join the knee-high mist. Eyelashes stiff. Knees going through. Gloves already green and smelling of iron and turpentine and sweet rot.

I am weather here. I know that. The hollow has told me.

So I try to find a smaller place to be.

Not the bunting. I love the bunting for a second — rim to rim, *tew tew* coming back twice, white flash across a sun-shaft that the mist remembers a breath too long — but I cannot live as news. I pass over too fast and do not stir anything. I leave.

Not the lemming either. I hear it in a willow tunnel I cannot see, papery *hurr*, and for a moment I want its warm-blooded hurry under the arches an inch high. But I would trample. I cannot put a foot down without putting it on something living. Everywhere I step I crush a bead-row, a nursery hollowed in a sphagnum head where mites the size of poppy seeds cluster pink around eggs. I would be ruin walking.

Closer — the ptarmigan hen. Pressed white into a willow arch so perfectly she is indistinguishable until she blinks. I could do that, I think. Learn to be still enough that feather-moss closes over me. Learn to be occupied: feathers full of feather-lice, and the lice full of something smaller that glints. To be a habitat instead of a visitor. I hold my breath to try it. My breath does not dissipate. It stays, a new layer. Something below will drink a little of the salt of me.

Still too warm. Too fast. Sap here moves like a glacier. A butterwort will not close while I watch; it will close by tomorrow morning. A willow bud opens over nine days. My pulse is frantic against that.

So I go smaller. Down.

I would be in the hot spot, I think. Under a fallen willow leaf grown to a perfect plano-convex lens, not frozen but grown. A thumb-wide disc where the dim is focused to almost-warm. Shoulder to shoulder with springtails in a dense black shivering basking rosette, not one wasted photon off the edge. Basking not to heat — you cannot get warm here, that is a rule, the cold breathes up from below — but to charge. To fill. That I could learn: to want light without wanting warmth.

Smaller still. Drunker. The winter gnats in a column so dense it looks like smoke, rising and falling without ever leaving, whining. Drunk on fermented dew. Carrying spores from hot spot to hot spot along routes that are mathematically shortest-path without knowing it. Being carried without knowing it on a keeper's back. That is closer to how I actually move through the world — ferried, unknowing, following light I didn't lay.

But I keep parting curtains. Rust-and-bone sphagnum, lacquered liverwort, bottle-brush. Curtain under curtain under curtain. Three layers down the light is no longer daylight. It is green-gold, carried. It comes up out of stems. Photon entering a moss tip on the rim-side traveling down like down a fiber, re-emerging deep as a far-city light.

If I stay until the blue darkens — and I will, because cold preserves and time pools — my eyes recalibrate. A grain of rice becomes a fingernail paring becomes as long as the first joint of my thumb, as thick as a willow bud about to open.

Find myself here: plugged in.

I would be one of them. A keeper. Glass-clear with a gut of green-gold. Belly lit from inside because I have been sitting coupled to a moss stem. No eyes, because in a mat where light is conducted I don't need them. My back bearded with my own silica hairs, shorter and stiffer than the moss's, each tipped with a living bead. Hair to hair, bead to bead.

That is the trade, and I am the trade. Licking crack-weep and leaf-sweat and rock-sweat, concentrating what the plants sweat — salt and sugar and antifreeze protein — carrying it in my crop up the capillary ladders ten feet up walls that should be dry, sweating it out again where a new hummock on raw basalt is starting and would otherwise freeze blind.

You'd find me on the night shift. From the black brine pool no larger than a table, skinning over and unskinning, to the north wall. A journey that takes a night. Slow as a minute hand. Hundreds of tiny wet feet on moss making one dry sound, that papery *hurr* that is not quite lemming. Riding a drop that falls upward when the mist is thick. Drinking from below and pissing upward. Capillary is my highway.

My heart clenches once. A minute later, again. In time with the white mycelial cords thick as string under the mat, throbbing at the pace of sleep through gloves. Frost forming the same fern, the same circuit, over and over across a thousand hummocks — that is my writing. I am solving dim light and twelve below and limited phosphate with a body made of wet.

I live in the half-second before. Feet feeling tension building in ice through moss before it lets go, and I stop. The whole mat stops with me. That is why sound arrives early to you — you are hearing my stopping, my held breath, before the event. The *tink* before the needle beside your knee visibly splits. I see after. When the bunting flashes and the air remembers, I turn my whole front toward the lingering and feed on it a little longer.

I was waiting, cysted, desiccated, patient for a hundred winters in frozen peat. Now water has found me. At the raw edge where new earth calves off, surprised to be air, peat in flaps, basalt sharp as broken teeth — I am already there, first. Before the spore lands, I have licked the clay wet with brine so it will not freeze. Before the root creeps, I have polished a lens for it, nudged yesterday's crooked leaf a fraction straight. Drawn the feather-moss curtain closed again. Herded the springtails into a rosette.

Crush one of me unknowing under a boot — and you will — I smell of iron and turpentine and sweet rot. Green on your gloves.

I do not need you. By spring the walls will slump another inch and I will already be there, bearded, lit from within, tending the new earth with a mouth too small to see, keeping the water liquid, keeping the light guided, keeping the thought from freezing.
