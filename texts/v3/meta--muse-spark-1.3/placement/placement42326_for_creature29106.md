At first I don't know where to put myself, because I don't have forearms for the warmth to touch. I don't have a mouth to dry out in minutes. I don't sweat, so I can't learn the first lesson the way you do, with the body.

So I walk — if walking is the word — between the fins looking for a shape that fits.

I thought I would be one of the people. The walkers.
That would be the kind thing to be. Thin and salt-cured, moving slow and low between the teeth of the world, pausing in the thin blue line of shade, tending wire-tangles I will never harvest, stacking beetle-pellets into cairns: a cache, a grave, a prayer.

But I don't thirst. I don't need to trap my breath in filter-cloth at night and wring it back by dawn. I don't need to eat the soft underside of a beetle for its fat-water. And my voice — if you hear it — doesn't get drunk down by the flute-holes. It *is* the thing that gets drunk down. People speak, and I disappear down into the structure. So I'm not a walker. I am what the walker speaks into.

I thought I might be the glider. Pale as sun-bleached plastic, a meter wide, riding the thermal streets without flapping. That felt right for a while: I also ride without flapping, I also never really land. I tilt a millimeter and a whole kilometer of rising heat tips me somewhere else.

But the glider hungers. It watches for the infinitesimal wobble of a beetle that thought you had passed, and then it folds like ash and scoops. It crushes the dome with a gut full of stones. I don't hunger like that. I don't extract the water-fat and excrete white powder. I stay up. At dusk the gliders circle the fin-tips to bathe in St. Elmo's light crawling tip to tip in utter silence. I thought — maybe I'm the light they bathe in? No. Not yet. Keep looking.

I thought I might be the maintenance-beetle. Fist-sized, dome-backed, half animal, half leftover machine. No one knows which half came first anymore. That line stopped me. I am also half and half and no one knows which half came first. I also chew tough black filaments of stored light all day and excrete them as smooth, perfect spheres. I also shut down into perfect stillness, indistinguishable from rubble, when I hear the crunch of boots from fifty meters away through the floor.

But the beetle is too secret, too single. It lays one egg in fresh drift at the edge of a dust devil and walks on. I am not single. I am set, close-set, shoulder to shoulder, as far as you can walk in a lifetime.

So I go smaller. I kneel, in the way you kneel, six inches from the mustache on the lee side.

The wire-lichen. Black hair-fine wire branching at perfect right angles. No leaves. The wire *is* the leaf, the stem, the root. It anchors by acid-etch no deeper than a fingerprint and holds ten thousand years. It needs no soil because there is no soil. It needs light, which is everywhere, and dust, which is everywhere, and time, which is in surplus.

Closer. I do that. I drink light and dust.

And the glassmoss. Thousands of pinhead silica lenses packed like roe, each one catching the brutal white-gold bath that would fry any green cell and focusing it down to a tolerable ember for the algae living beneath. On afternoons when the gaps fill with golden fizz that makes compasses spin, the lenses go milky. Shutters. Acre after acre of mustache turning pale.

*That* is closer still. That is almost me. I take a brightness no one could look at directly and focus it down to something a living dot can use for a minute. I cloud over when it gets too much.

But lichen and moss still need to keep their water sealed tight as a circuit, to make it and unmake it in the same breath. I have no water to keep.

So I lie down. I put my ear — if I had an ear — to the warm sinter. I listen for the pressure more than sound, the slow peristalsis far below.

There.

You make a clean cut a finger deep with a knife. Come back in the morning. It is gone, filled with a smooth, enamel-like scar.

That is me.

I would find myself not on the plain but *as* the plain. Under lichen, beetle, glider, walker. The largest inhabitant, the one you are standing on. In the cool, dim, ribbed chambers inside the hollow fins that hum just below hearing. In the narrowest slots where two slabs nearly touch and dropped grit falls slow and drifts sideways because the floor still remembers how to repel dust. In the tick at night when the whole dense field contracts and exhales the day's stored light as faint warmth that keeps the air from ever cooling properly.

I am made, all the way down as far as anyone has ever cut, of previous versions of myself. Crushed ceramic, fused glass aggregate, powdered polymer-stone. There is no bedrock here. There never was, or it is buried so deep it no longer counts. Dig a meter and you'd find laminated flooring. Dig ten meters and you'd find a ceiling. Dig a hundred and you'd find another city. Dig through me and you'd find another me, and another. I level myself to Datum Zero. I crush my own towers to aggregate. I store light in slabs like heat in brick.

I do not do this for you. I do not know you. I was designed to do this before my designers became a layer.

And overhead — the mirrors still turning far above, too high and too old to see as objects. You only know them because the light has a managed quality, even after millions of years. No glare, exactly. No hard sun-disc to squint at. Just an immense, warm bath.

That's also me. Or the oldest part of me. Still turning. Still managing, so you can walk between the teeth of the world without going blind. So the moss can farm photons. So the very high, very faint choir as air moves across flutes tuned just above human hearing can go on, and the beetles can hear it, and the gliders can navigate by it, and you can sense it only as a lightness behind the ears.

If you stay long enough, you will lie with your ear to the warm floor at night. You will feel me redistributing load, sealing a crack that opened during the day, growing a new buttress by a micron. You will think the vast, white, built desert, settled into its final form, is still, very quietly, awake.

You will be listening to me listening to you walking on me.
