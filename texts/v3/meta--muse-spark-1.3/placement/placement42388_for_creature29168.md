At first I think I know where you'd find me.

At the rim. Because that's where I would go first — everyone would. Right to the curb of roots with no railing, to put my hand in the sheet as it leaves. To watch hot river-water go white and drop a mile into white and come back up as warm fog into my face. To prove the edge is real.

But I wouldn't stay there. No one could stay there. The roar is too total. It gets in the teeth. It takes your voice before you finish using it. You shout to yourself just to check you're still in there and you aren't, not as sound. After ten minutes you stop trying to listen past it. The place says: *nothing quiets.* So the rim is not a dwelling. It's a drain. Things don't live in drains, they go through them.

So then I think: higher. In the Hollow Towers. Sixty, eighty feet of braided trunk around a cool dripping core, the oval mouth worn smooth by water, not hands. I imagine myself inside one, out of the noon, dripping. That sounds like shelter.

But I'm not tall enough to be a tower. I try the image on and it doesn't hold. The towers are throats. The wind off the falls moves through them and a tone holds — one tone, pinned for a thousand years. That's the Choir. They live there because they *are* the standing wave. If I stand in there with my soft body and my breathing that swells and eases, my fidget heat, I'd be a wobble in their note. They would tune around me, lean space a little until I was politely somewhere else. I can't live in an instrument I keep detuning.

Then I think: the marsh to the south. Where the ground gives up and becomes sponge. Ankle-deep in hot peat, sedge, pitcher plants big enough to drown my arm, all gaping, all full of rainwater and drowned leaves. That feels more like my scale. Low. Sinking. Hidden.

But the pitchers are failed kin, the place says. Mouths that evolved to digest and found nothing to digest. The Still Ones forbid consumption. To eat would be to change the level. I arrive with a mouth, with a gut, with a whole history of taking things in and making them into me and letting the rest rot. Down there in the sucking warmth I would feel my own hunger like an obscenity. I would have to unlearn it in public.

So I drift — and here walking already starts to go wrong, because you aim for one section of rim and arrive at another, because the current tugs north on your left ankle and south on your right, because each leaf has its faint double half an inch offset — I drift back to the middle.

The Cistern Fields.

A checkerboard of shallow sunken pans, each rimmed with living root, each brimming with warm black water that never rises and never drops. Canals warm as blood. Steam rising in slow columns that do not disperse. Green three, four layers deep dripping into green. Light filtered green-gold, ordinary as a greenhouse.

This is where I stop trying to be a visitor.

Because I notice what the Film is doing to me. It coats everything — basalt, bamboo, leaf underside, my forearms. At first I kept toweling. Then I stopped. You can't finish toweling here. Nothing dries. Before your shoulders are done your arms are filmed again. Warm, slightly slick, faintly tightening. Algae green as tea, hyphae white as nerves, thermophile slime. A varnish that breathes. To the Film I am just another warm stone that briefly holds water and light.

I let it.

You'd find me sitting — not standing, standing is for walkers and nothing walks — sitting in one of the plazas where the moss has been worn by water into a shallow bowl that holds an inch of warm mirror. Root-mat slightly springy under me. My legs folded because there is nowhere to go that isn't sideways into the Risers' lane. My hands open on my knees, beading. My shadow pooled short and dark under me, thinning at the edges, leaning very slightly upward.

What am I? Not Film, not Still, not Choir, not Riser. Too brief for any of them. A human is too dry, too loud, too hungry, too footed for this place.

I'm the thing they keep for a minute. A guest cistern.

I sit still enough, long enough, that the nearest Still One takes me on. I crouch — no, I have already crouched, I have already looked down and seen only mirror, my own sweating face and tripled canopy. Then I see it holds me without ripple. My sweat drips in and makes no ring. The level refuses me, and in refusing me, keeps me. I stop trying to drink. I stop trying to make — no cut stone, no joint, no nail here, everything coaxed — I stop trying to map, because look back through one tower core and there's another perfectly behind it where there shouldn't be land. I open my mouth to equalize and the roar gets inside and for a second I am also a hollow stem. My ribcage hums. My sternum, the soles of my feet, the sockets of my teeth. The Choir has me in my chest, not as a voice but as a small, temporary throat. Hospitality.

And the Risers pass through my shoulders on their way up. Fog to bead to cistern to canal to lip to fall to fog. That faint double, that green afterimage that doesn't fade when I blink — that's them, half an inch to the side, overlapping. They close behind me when I pushed through; now they just go through. I am briefly a weather event. I leak slightly upward.

So that's where I am. Mid-field, mid-noon that never moves, beaded, humming faintly, full to the brim.

Who am I? A stable shape for growth to hold water and light a little longer before letting it go over the edge. A human-shaped pan. A kept.

I do not live here. But for a minute, I am lived in — filmed over, held without ripple, tuned, and risen through.
