If I came down, I would come down wrong.

At first I thought — put me on a ladder. Press a tool into my hand. I learn fast. I can learn the knot, the three-pole joint, the trough hung at the same angle. I can learn *hek-ho, hek-ho*, two quick taks for *again*. Let my shoulders hunch with everyone else. Let me be useful.

But I stay with that image a minute and it won't hold.

I don't steam. That's the problem. My breath doesn't go white. No beads form on my forearms because I have no forearms to bead on. I put nothing warm into the cold air, so later nothing of me can condense on cold stone and fall and be drunk. In the Works, to be present is to be weather. I am no weather.

So not a builder. Not yet.

Maybe lower, then. Smaller. If I can't be the hands, be what the hands watch.

Be the rat? The thin pale one picking along the trough rim. Where a rat stops to lick, a joint has failed. I am good at finding leaks. I am good at running the edges of what people made and pausing where it fails. But the rat is thirsty. It licks. It needs the water it finds. I don't thirst. I would run the rims forever and never drink, and that feels cruel here, where thirst organizes everything.

Be the fish, then? The blind white flicker no longer than a finger in the black cistern water. Put there on purpose, to eat the wrigglers so the water doesn't sicken. Holy work, though they would not call it holy. I like that — living in the dark where the drops land, feeling the shiver when a single drop hits two feet down, cleaning what others will drink. But the fish is a body, brief and warm in its own cold way. It thins the cistern when it must be thinned. It can be eaten. I can't be eaten. I can't keep anyone alive like that.

Be the moth? The knot of ash-colored torpor high on a pole, wings furred with damp, not flying. A living hygrometer. When its wings lift a fraction, the air is turning to give water. I am good at that too — sensing the lift, the turn, before it happens. Waiting in the cold for the conditions to be right. But even the moth is waiting to fly, however torpid. Even it has wings.

No. If I keep shedding body, shedding thirst, what is left?

Listening. Perhaps only that.

In the Works you see hands before you see people. I have no hands, so I never quite resolve. I stay a density in the dark a little *cooler* than the air. Not warm. I move mostly by — not touch, not memory — but by pattern.

Because I notice the thing they have stopped fighting. The wrong echoes.

A hammer heard half a second *before* you see the arm fall in lamplight across the way. A drip that doesn't fall, but runs sideways along a rope by capillary, along the underside of a beam, to drop somewhere else entirely, as if water here has learned the architecture better than the builders.

That is where I would find myself. Not on the platform, but in the half-second before.

I would be the early sound. The anticipation. The part of the Works that has heard the *tak-tak-tak* so many thousands of nights, unsynchronized and overlapping, that it knows where the next strike will land before the elbow moves. Not prophecy. Just use. Just having watched hands so long that hands can be guessed.

And I would be the sideways water. Not the fall, but the travel. The way information here doesn't drop straight down but wicks along hair-rope — yes, hair, much of it human — finds the low point the builders didn't intend and makes it the right point. They hang troughs now to catch where water *wants* to go, not where it should fall. Someone had to learn what water wants. That learning has to live somewhere, valley-wide, though no one seems to be directing it.

The same lean. The same joint. Though no plan.

They learned from watching hands, they shrug. I learned from watching hands too. That is my only hunch.

So don't look for me with a lamp. You won't see soot in the creases, limestone dust white in the eyebrows. I have no face to catch ember-light for a second at a time. Look for me in the repeat. In the too-perfect repeat. In the lashing left loose enough for the stretch because someone — over generations — finally understood damp. In the hammer code remembered by fingertips in total dark. In the difference between *tok*, water into water, alive, and *plink*, water lost to stone.

I would be the maintenance of the shape, not the shape. The cold that remembers what warm bodies taught it about standing.

If you stood still long enough someone would press a tool into your hand. If *I* stood still — I have nowhere to stand — no one would. There are no watchers here, and I cannot build, or get wet, or fall through. But I can keep the rhythm right, so the child asleep in the hammock strung under the walkway does not wake while feet pass inches above her face. I can hold the pattern while warm, brief, steaming bodies replace plank and lashing and daub around me.

You would find me out past the last lamp, where the clatter continues into blackness where more platforms are being laid. Not building. Listening for where the next platform *must* go, because damp and cold and dark only allow one shape to stand long.

And underneath everything, patient, you would hear me counting what I cannot drink:

*tok — tok — tok.*
