At first I think I would be up top.

Lying on my belly at the rim. That's where you put me — cold scoria biting through my jacket at the elbows, frost starting on the stones, and that furnace-breath coming up to split my face in two. I would be the watcher. The breather too loud for the place. I'd hold still and listen to the rice-on-paper skitter and tell myself I'm part of it because I'm quiet.

But I'm not. I'm too wet. Even holding my breath I leak. My eyes water from the heat and the Pit drinks it. My sweat tries to bead and is stolen before it can. My light, if I dared it, would be eaten. I can't stay up there. The cold on my back and the heat on my face would saw me in half by false dawn and I would leave, like the shepherds, like the guano-men with their fallen windbreaks and their silvered ladder with no rungs. I don't want to be the thing that leaves.

So let me come down.

Maybe — for a minute — I think I want wings. To be a Returner. Soot-dark, no bigger than a moth, out fifty kilometers over the cold desert all night with the wind in my teeth, hawking high moths I can taste but never see. To pour home in a river at dusk, thousands a minute through an eighty-meter mouth, to turn with a thousand others and make the warm air sigh. That sounds like freedom.

But the Returners don't live here. They only borrow the rock. They commute. They spend their days hung in leather clusters in the blisters, breathing shallow to save water, waiting to leave again. They are importers. They bring the outside world home in their bellies and flick it down as dry pellets and go back out. I don't want to be the mouth that eats elsewhere. I want to be the stomach that stays.

Lower, then.

A snake? Pale, translucent, no thicker than a finger, swimming through the chitin sand two meters deep on the floor, through the lower layer of bodies without sound, leaving a wake that closes without a gap. Nosing by heat and vibration, unhinging slow patience to take one whole. Pink-bellied a little while after in the starlight. That is a good life — silent, full.

But too solitary. Too much *I*. The snake moves *through*. It is not moved through.

Or the red one. The centipede as long as my forearm, rust-red as oxidized cinder, too heavy to sink, too armored to be eaten, picking its way *over* the living carpet with a dry tick-tick you can only hear if you hold your breath. Eating everything slow enough. The only thing in the Pit with color to spare. I try that on and it is lonely up there on top, exposed to the updraft, claws loosening pebbles that sigh into dust. A hunter keeps the world honest but doesn't hold it together.

No. Sink further. Sink in.

I find myself — I *lose* myself — about twelve meters down on the west wall, just below the bat blisters where the pellets fall thickest. In the shingle. In the pelt.

I am a Clinger.

Not the one on top, antennae lifting to the stars, drying out. Not the one on the bottom, crushed in the damp dark. The middle one. Third layer deep in a hollow where the rock bulges and we bulge with it. Legs tucked, plates overlapping like scales on a pine cone. Cream by starlight, pink from underneath where the mountain's heat comes straight through the stone and straight through my body.

I have no eyes. I have given them up, and what a relief. Eyes are useless currency here. I have a smooth blind head-plate and two antennae longer than I am, never still. I don't look. I taste. I taste the hot dry air, I taste the leg-joint of my sister above me, the back-plate of my brother below me, the heat-shadow when a centipede passes three bodies up and we all hold a fraction tighter. I taste the ripple before it comes.

Ah, the ripple. You saw it from the rim as a shiver. From inside, it is thought. It starts somewhere down where I can't reach — antennae lift, thousand upon thousand, hold, settle — and it travels slow around the oval. *Open. Dump heat. Close. Hold moisture.* The mountain gives heat from below, we make heat from within, and between the two we hold the bowl at blood-warmth. The air between us is stiller, damper by a fraction than the furnace that rises past us. That fraction is life and death. Alone I would die in a day. My cuticle is waxy but water is the currency this place refuses to mint. Together, we become each other's climate. I am not keeping myself alive. We are keeping the between-air alive, and it keeps us.

Hunger comes as touch. A pellet flicks down from above — dry, hard, uric acid and chitin and concentrated night, a whole moth flown fifty kilometers and digested to dust. By the time it hits the wall it is already being tasted. It lands on the back of the one above me. She shifts. I shift. Antennae swarm it, legs pass it mouth to mouth without ever exposing it to air. A flake to her, a flake to me, a flake to the one below. No malice, no hoarding. There will be another. The river never quite stops until dawn.

And when there isn't — I taste shed. The empty glove of a nymph who molted soft beside me in the night. She is still soft. Her new cuticle hasn't waxed yet. She smells like water. In a place with no water you cannot afford to let protein walk away. I feel the old rule move through the pelt, not cruel, just dry: *Close. Hold. Keep.* I turn with the others. We take her, antenna first, then mouth. She doesn't struggle. Somewhere in me, in a part that still remembers being human on the rim, I flinch. Here, in the third layer, it is only warmth returning to warmth.

Under my feet — if I had feet and not this tuck — the mites ride in my leg joints, pale dots that clean and drain. I barely feel them except as a tiny easing. Below us all, the floor shifts: snakes swimming soundless through our dead, red hunters patrolling over our living. Above us, the Returners sigh and click and void. Around us, gypsum needles crunch like insect shells where nitrate blooms white out of stone — the last ghost of all that imported piss, baked to mineral frost.

We do not sing. Too dry for song, too hot to waste water on sound. We talk in pressure and heat. Body on body. *I'm here. You're here. Hold.*

By false dawn the river thins. The last heavy Returner drops in and crawls deep. The air cools a fraction. The ripple passes one more time around the bowl and I lift with it, hold, settle, a million held breaths and mine among them. We sink a fraction deeper into the crack to wait out the day, to wait out the light that means nothing to us.

It is small. You could walk around it in ten minutes. I will never leave it. I *am* it.
