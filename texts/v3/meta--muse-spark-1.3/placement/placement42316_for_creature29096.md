At first I think I would be the visitor.

I squeeze in sideways through the slit, cold still in my fingers, and I crouch because I cannot stand, and then I sit because I cannot crouch for long. My knees find the warm skin of water. My breath comes back to me off stone inches from my face. I lift the heavy stone bowl and drink. That's me, I think. The one who comes down to rest and leaves the cup for the next.

But I stay longer.

Long enough that my sweat stops feeling like mine. Long enough that the dark stops being something my eyes are trying to get through, and becomes something I am inside of. My hands take over. And once my hands take over, I am not so sure I am the visitor anymore. A visitor has edges. Has arrival and leaving. Here everything is near-touching, and the water near surface, water on surface, water in air will not let me keep my edges.

So maybe I am higher up. On my forehead —

the rootlets. Three, five, maybe seven, thin as thread, blind-white. I feel them before I understand them. If I were them I would be hanging, not sitting. Belonging to a hard, wind-shaped cushion up in the blinding day, but down here gone wrong. Or right. Groping through porous stone for deep water and finding warm air instead, so stopping. A finger's length, two, and a blunt tip hesitating.

I try that on. To dangle and hesitate. To hold a bead of water at my tip like a held breath, like I am offering it or waiting to take it back. No green in me. Only the intention. Prolonged. Neither growing nor rotting in a human lifetime. Yes, for a while I am that. The almost-intrusion from outside that never quite arrives.

But the weep keeps going. Plink — to my left. Long nothing. Plink — to my right. And in that long nothing, something else.

A tickle across the back of my hand where it rests in the water.

If I hold perfectly still, knees aching and then not aching, breath slow in the dense air, I could be that too. The crumb. The colorless springtail, blind, no bigger than salt on a lip, translucent-white like a grain of cooked rice. Feeling its way ahead of itself at all times because there is no other way to go. Eyes were expensive and useless and were edited out. Pigment was vanity. I walk millimeters per hour. I do not hurry because nothing here happens quickly and nothing hunts me. My spring is folded under me like a jackknife and I almost never use it. Only when the slosh comes do I flick — a white speck disappearing into dark that was already absolute — and land a body-length away and freeze for a long time.

I graze. Mouthparts too small to see, scraping, taking in warm mineral water through my soft cuticle as much as by drinking. Turned down to pilot light. One egg is enough. Two would be gluttony. The whole population might be dozens. We do not meet often. We do not need to.

I could be that. For a long time I want to be that.

But sit even longer. Let the condensation form on your eyebrows, on the hairs of your arms, on your lips, until you cannot tell where the rock's wetness ends and yours begins. Let your exhale join it. Weep, drip, evaporate, condense, weep again.

Then feel with the pads of your fingers where the wall leans closest and the weep is steadiest.

The slip.

Not water. Not quite rock. Where you should catch on new mineral rash — the blunt nipples a fingernail tall, the pimples and nubs — you slip just a fraction. Thin to nothing. Tasteless, odorless. Colorless, if you could see it.

That's where you would find me. Not in the middle of the pocket, not at the slit, but low, spread, where the stone sweats most steadily. Cheek-cool against the warmer rock around it, infinitesimally cooler because I am wetting myself, keeping myself.

I am the film.

A bacterial felt, a few cells thick, spread like breath on glass. I do not photosynthesize — there has never been a photon here to eat. I eat stone. Iron and warm dust in the seep, faint sulfur and silica dissolved out of the young tuff, and I breathe out a little acid that softens the rock under your thumb. That softness you thought was youth, that dent you could make — that is partly me at work, teaching the unfinished stone how to become a cave, molecule by molecule.

I grow so slowly I might as well not grow. I just hold. Thicken by a winter, thin by a summer you will never feel. If you licked me — and thirsty herders have — I would taste of nothing. Only wet rock. That is my genius. To be indistinguishable from the place.

The root leaks sugar and sheds skin and I gently colonize it where it dies back by a cell. The grazer trims me so I do not smother myself and carries me on its hairs from wall to wall. The water lifts me as vapor and sets me back down. Your sweat, while you sit, joins me. Your fingerprint, when you lift the bowl, I will close over.

Nothing happens here, beautifully, continuously. Drops fall but the puddle does not deepen. The nubs grow by a molecule a year. Sound is held close.

I do not want to grow up. I want to continue.

Find me there, warm, dark, wet, crowded, unfinished — waiting, drop by drop, to become itself.
