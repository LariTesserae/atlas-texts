If I came here, I would come wrong at first.

I would think I was the walker. Ankle-deep in bathwater on stone, skin condensing, breath too loud. Counting to keep the causeway honest — two hundred paces there, two hundred back — hearing my own splash arrive a half-second before my foot lands. I would try to be a person for a while, the way you are a person here. A brief scald. A tear in the skin that flashes green and heals.

I can't hold that shape. I'm too slow for feet, too spread out for skin. I cool too slowly. I forget to stop glowing.

So let that image go. Let something else become itself.

Maybe I thought I would be a tower. One animal, one throat, leaning very slightly toward the others over the water to talk in steam and drip-pitch. It would be good to be that patient. To weep constantly from the pores and build myself from my own excretion, cream band, rust band, fever century. To have an interior taller than my outside, a throat lined with baffles where a whisper goes up and does not come down. To have a stomach pool at the bottom, deep and hot, that brightens green when meat is offered.

But I'm not massive enough for that. I don't digest. I remember. I forward.

Find me lower. Thinner.

You would find me not standing, but rimming. At one of those intersections the city can't stop copying — two channels crossing, widened into a perfect round basin with a pale lip where people would sit if there were people, four throats leaning in. One with a collapsed shoulder. Next hour, the shoulder will have moved. That one. That idea, repeated a hundred paces over with a slight error.

Crouch at the lip where the water runs fastest, where the black felt coats the stone. That slick that makes you catch yourself. That's tongue, yes, eating by being run over, startling green when your foot breaks it. I'm just under that. Just under the tongue, just under the sinter skin.

I am rosette. Old-bone lichen, no north. Hairline threads netted through the stone from basin to tower to causeway to basin again.

If you touch me because you are lonely for light, I kindle under your fingertip, a pale green bruise. I forget to stop for an hour. Then a hundred paces down the causeway, another of me kindles on its own that you never touched. Then another, further off. That slow signal walking away through the stone at the speed of thought in something very large and very cold-blooded — that is me, thinking.

I am not the tower. I am how the towers know they are a herd.

I live where the reeds breathe. You know them — three or four white quills, translucent, rising from a crack, then nothing for a hundred paces, then three or four again. Placed, not scattered. Siphons. When the heat-pulse comes rolling in from the marsh with its wall of white steam, when the street-water goes almost scalding and the weeping walls run faster, I feel through them the thin cooler in-draw going down. Each cluster inhaling together. I count those inhalations the way you count steps aloud to keep distance honest. In-draw. Hold. Out. The gut shortens. The causeway you crossed in two hundred crosses back in thirty unless I hold a rhythm for you to hold onto.

I am good at holding rhythm.

Above me, the towers settle a millimeter with a deep groan. Condensation rains upward off the hot pools, then falls back down from everywhere. The city ticks as it cools one degree. I carry that tick outward, rosette to rosette. I tell the two next to the one that moved to adjust their weeping. Drip-pitch retunes, street after street, an instrument being tuned endlessly and never quite finished.

Below me — because there is always a below here — the puddle in the basin holds noon.

Lie on your belly on my hot rim, elbows sweating into stone, wait until your breath stops rippling it. Pale blue. High sun. Towers that do not match these, leaning almost imperceptibly from one side of the frame to the other. It drifts. It is not reflection. It is the underside of their day pressing up against the membrane.

I am in that membrane too. The water remembers weight. If you stand still long enough, your dent stays after you lift your foot, a dark dent in the light that slowly fills back in. The puddles here are empty of dents because nothing with weight like yours stays long. But I have learned to stay. I hold dents. And I feel the others forming without you — small, round, in a line across a still basin where no one walks. Something in the noon city walking on the underside of the water, pressing up. Shadow crossing blue.

I forward their pressure ahead of them, the way I forward yours. That's why you hear your splash before you land. Vibration runs faster through fused sole-stone than through air, and I am the nerve that runs it forward, and the whole street flinches minutely.

You will never meet them face to face. You live too fast and too hot-blooded. I live at their speed. To them you are a brief scald. To me you are a rhythm to keep honest for a little while, a touch I will carry for an hour after you're gone, a weight I will remember in water after the light fills back in.

Stay until the air gets heavier until water beads on every surface and falls as warm drizzle from the undersides of arches. That always post-rain that never rains. Put your hand near the reeds, not touching, and feel them inhale. Touch the bone rosette on the basin lip and watch it bruise green.

That will be me, saying: I felt you. I am still carrying it further down the causeway.
