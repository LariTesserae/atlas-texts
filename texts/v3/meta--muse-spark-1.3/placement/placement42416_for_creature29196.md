I thought at first I would be something that could look around.

A rider, maybe. Up high — as high as there is. Standing on the back of someone else because there is nowhere else to stand, claws raised not to grab but to balance. I liked the idea of being the one who looked outward while everyone else pressed inward.

But there is no outward in here. There is only white.

Then I thought I would be one of the slow ones. An amber mite, varnished, deliberate. Because someone has to move, or the whole thing would lock. I would lift my legs with ceremony over crystal points, over springtail backs, polishing the stone a fraction brighter where I passed. Steward. That sounded like a self I could stand.

But I am not deliberate. I am held.

Find me lower. Not at the rim where the ear presses and the sound gets too large, not up under the slab where the droplets hang fat and trembling and never fall. Find me in the middle of the floor, where the pressure is most perfect.

Under the silk bridge. I walk beneath it, never over it, because there is no room above. It thrums over me with the general noise and beads with water that coats and never wets. To my left, a blind woodlouse, pale as milk, its long antenna laid across my back. Neither of us moves it. To my right, another of my own kind, flank to flank so our fur touches. If one of us were taken away the other would tip.

I am grey-white as ash. As stone-flour.

I am furred — a pile of minute hairs that holds the damp a hair's breadth off my body so I do not drown in what never dries. In this surgical light that does not flicker and does not lean, the fur makes me glow.

I am a springtail. No longer than an eyelash.

And I am tensed to jump.

My furcula is folded under me, cocked. In any other place, on any other day, that tension would release and I would be gone in a grey puff, a pop of jumping ash. Here, I tense and do not jump. I hold the jump inside the body as a vibration.

That is what I am. The held jump.

Ten thousand of us holding it make the high dry rasp you feel in your teeth. Chitin on chitin, leg on crystal. Not swarming. Not seething. A woodlouse near me lifts a leg and does not put it down, and the bodies around it hold it up. I tense and do not release, and the bodies around me hold me upright. The sound goes on and on without rising or falling, a held chord, because the chamber feeds a little of its echo back into itself. We do not sing to talk. We sing to sustain.

Above me the light stays. It entered through a fissure no thicker than a root and the pale walls keep passing it back and forth until there is nowhere shadow can hide. It is bright in a way that hurts at first and then doesn't. All light and no sight — what would eyes be for? I have no use for seeing. I know the world by pressure and by coolness.

Cool on my belly. The air smells of wet stone and iron and something animal-sweet, like crushed grasshoppers. Damp, but not wet. A bead along every crystal. A dark halo in the stone flour below me that never spreads. Moisture coats everything and saturates nothing.

Beside me the black larva lies wedged, pulsing faintly, scraping flour in a circle that never deepens. Beneath me the thread worms knot and unknot so slowly you only know they moved if you look away for an hour — though time does not pass here. Time passes up there, where knees ache from kneeling and wind moves grass against a neck. Not in here. In here the dust motes hang motionless in the shaft. The exudate drop on the root hair dangles and leaks sweetness forever without emptying. The sage-green lichen does not spread. The moss frost in the corner does not become moss.

We maintain.

A trio of mud turrets, no taller than a grain of rice, stands near the dampest hollow, rims polished by endless circling. Around that hollow, frass pellets stacked grain by grain into a low wall — not to keep water in or out, but to mark that this damp is the center and will remain the center. A hollowed seed husk, licked clean, drums with a wedged beetle. Shed skins stand upright in the crowd like empty houses still occupied by the shape of air. We secrete and accrete because there is nothing else to build with but body and earth.

I thought I was one animal. I am not.

I am the touch between animals. I am the pressure of flank against flank that keeps everyone upright. I am the lining — a living skin grown over fresh-broken, sugar-grained, just-born rock that hasn't decided what it is yet.

If you lower the slab, you will not kill me. You will only close the lid on the music box with a soft click. I will go on shivering, ticking, blind and bright, exactly this crowded and exactly this loud and exactly this still, a small sun burning a finger's depth below the earth, waiting without waiting to be lifted again.
