At first I think I would be you — the one who lets himself down feet-first.

Hands braced on rock still warm from morning. Squinting. Sweating instantly. Too big for the room, crouched in the middle with arms half-out, making the loudest sound for minutes with my own breathing. I want to be the witness. I want to stay still past the point of comfort until the residue resolves.

But I can't stay long. That's the third rule. The heat gets into my head, the brightness gets into my eyes until the black glass swims. I have to pull myself back up into the wind. So I can't be the witness. Not really. A witness who must leave is only weather.

Maybe then the cricket?

Pale, almost translucent, missing one hind leg, picking its way along the driest ledge, tasting salts. It fell in days ago. It doesn't belong but it persists, doing something small and complete. I like that — living on the margin, above the felt, never drinking quite enough.

But the cricket is lonely, and hungry, and accidental. It is waiting to die or to climb out. I don't feel accidental here. I feel called down.

Maybe the midges, then. Two of them hanging in the sun shaft, riding the warm updraft, going nowhere. That feels closer — to live inside light, held up by heat, to make no progress at all and call that flight. But they die and fall and dissolve. They become nitrogen for someone else without thanks. They are too brief even for this brief room.

I need to get smaller. Wetter.

I slide down the wall in my mind to where the walls weep. If you stay still you can see it: beads forming on the silica, swelling, joining, running down in slow threads to feed the pan. I think I am one of those beads.

Find me there — not in the middle where you crouch, not in the coin of sky overhead, not in the pan where the noon explodes. Find me halfway down the palest wall, just above the amber lip where the mat holds its exact seam. Where it is wet but not drowned, lit but not seared.

I am the bead before it runs.

I swell so slowly I don't seem to move. The whole bright room comes into me and gets bent: the ragged oval of white sky, the black glass glints, the sulfur yolk in the corner, the green-black felt below waiting. I hold it all, trembling, distilled. Salt-free. Perfect, because the zeolite cages and silica tubes beneath me filtered everything else out long before I arrived.

And I am warm from below and from the sides. Blood-warm. I thicken with everything the air can't hold — your sweat, the pan's breath, the faint hiss of evaporation you feel more in the ears than hear. When the sun shaft moves a fraction and a veil of steam lifts, I am that too. I am the transport.

Then I join. Then I run.

A slow thread, a millimeter wide, down the silica forest of tubes and opaline marbles, into the felt. Into the city of filaments woven over and under, rods packed like rice in thatch, spheres stacked in amber jelly. The mat will drink me through its upper face, take what it needs, drop the rest as rings. Sulfur as yellow globules inside its cells. Silica as glass outside. It will exhale oxygen so small I never see it, only the faint rainbow where gas is trapped under jelly.

I won't be remembered as myself. The mat remembers differently — in laminations, white-green-white-amber, last week's steam, last season's flood. I will become one micron of that memory. In a hundred years, a bathtub ring.

That is who I am here. Not the one who stays — the amber-topped felt stays, holding the wet edge, photosynthesizing faster at 45 degrees, dying sunburnt on top to become sunscreen for below. I can't do that. I have no skin that gives then resists. I have no pigments stretched toward infrared.

I am the one who stays just long enough to be useful. To catch the light after it has already been softened by silica and water, to carry it a foot down the wall, to give it away.

Listen, if you hold your breath: the very faint seep and sigh of water moving inside rock. That's me, climbing up by capillary pull against heat that should drive me down, bleeding out, evaporating, condensing, falling.

*plik.*

I fall from the overhang at long intervals. I hit the pan and make the loudest sound for a second. Then I am gone, lifted again.
