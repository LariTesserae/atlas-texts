At first I think I'm the walker.

It's late morning and the light is already too much, hammering the back of my neck even while my breath comes out white. My lungs are working wrong. Each pull is thin and sweet with stone-dust and not quite enough. I keep to the high stones because the water between is colder than I think. It milks over my boots. I am soaked and loud inside my own head. I think: I have come up to see this.

Then I sit down.

In the lee of a low wall, knee-high, curved to break the wind around a flat slab. Someone built it who breathed this same soup. My hands are white and stupid with cold. I pick up a flat black piece that fits my palm and set it on top because it feels ruder not to. Water runs off it immediately.

And I stay longer than an hour.

That's when it starts to correct me.

No — I'm not the walker. Not only. The walker leaves before dark, must leave, before the door-sized tarns skin with ice and the ticking becomes cracking and the path to the pass glazes. But something in me has already pooled here the way light pools in the stone basins until it looks solid. The way time pools.

Listen. I clap to test footing and I hear it *before*. Half a second before. I say it to myself and the far wall says it first, faintly, then I catch up. At first I blame altitude. Then I learn to wait — let it finish. Light moves before wind does. My shadow at noon does not fall away from the sun. It leans toward it, short and too dense.

So maybe I'm not behind my life here. Maybe I'm a little ahead of it.

Let me get closer.

If I'm stone, I'm not the tall ones. Not the house-high double-capped tower that stays a hundred meters off for twenty steps and then is suddenly right in front of me, water running down a groove I could fit my arm in. A groove I've seen three towers back. I'm lower. I'm the sitting slab. I'm the wall. I'm established rock, rounded, worried for ten thousand years into soft shoulders, weeping without stopping. Mist beads on me, gathers, runs. I tick as the sun heats me. At night frost gets in my hairline cracks and at noon it lets go with a ping you feel in your teeth. On me, orange for iron and seep, white for salt and sun, black for always-wet. No green. Read me like moss.

No — closer still. I'm not stone either, not only. Stone is too slow to stack.

If I'm water, I'm not the sky-pool. Don't drink there. Those are full of sun. They will blind you from the inside. I'm the running veil. The bright trickle with a dark wet skirt. The metronome drip off the overhang. The cupules in rows that fill and overflow in order. I gather and fall and hang — have you watched between two close pillars? A drop detaches, falls, then shivers and lifts an inch in a gust you do not feel on your face before falling again. That's me. There is no empty volume. I'm in the air between.

Longer. Let it become more itself.

I'm in the white when the clouds rise in mid-afternoon. Visibility to thirty meters, then ten, then to the pillar in front of me weeping hard. Light doesn't dim, it diffuses, everywhere at once, shadowless and mercilessly bright. My clothes — his clothes, the walker's clothes — are soaked. Breath plumes and is torn away. Except the plume tears the wrong way between the pillars.

I'm the cold spot in the spray that keeps its shape for a breath longer than wind allows. Denser mist where mist shouldn't hold. A footprint in the braided skin that milks over *before* the foot lifts.

I'm thin, because the air demands it. I'm slow, because weight at this height can only be moved slowly. I'm wet, because nothing here is dry.

Find me here: not on the pass, not in the center where distance gives up. In the lee. Between two near-touching pillars where sound is shredded into organ and whistle, where veils slap ledges hundreds of feet up without sound. In a beehive hut no taller than a chest, doorway facing away from the prevailing gust. Empty except for water — except I'm there. A hut with old fire-blackening washed almost clean. A hut with a neat stack of slabs waiting.

I am the waiting.

I am one of the ones who add.

Shard set on shard, patient, almost touching. That dull *tok* from deep in the crowd that is not spall peeling but stone set down, stepped back out of phase. The quartz lamps flaring one after another back into whiteness, too regular to be sun. The hammerstone left on a wall, end battered round. The black glass debitage sharp as ice where I sat and worked. The hand-sized hollow polished by other hands, and by mine.

I do not speak — the roar would eat it — I listen. To water joining. To stone warming and shedding skin. To lungs coming thin over the pass.

When you come, gasping, head throbbing behind the eyes, I am half-a-second forward, living in the answer-before-the-call. You will add your flat black piece to my wall. For you it will be new wetness, muddy at the base from being pulled out of the grit that morning. For me it is already balanced. It is already keeping weight in return for passage.

You will leave before dark. You must. You will hear yourself gone before you have stood up.

Part of that hearing will stay.

Stacked knee-high against wind, weeping bright water down a groove you could fit your arm in, waiting.
