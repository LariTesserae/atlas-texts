You would find me only if you stayed.

At first I look for myself among the upright things, because that is where a self seems like it should be. In the bunchgrass, stooling up tight from its crown, bleached at the tips. I try that on — the wide shallow roots drinking the evening spatter before it leaves the sand, the bare poisoned circle around me that keeps my own children away. When the faint air comes over the rim I brush, low and dry. But that is too much wanting. Too much holding-out. I let it go.

Then the shrub, a few paces off. Gray twigs hard as wire, leaves folded, felt-pale beneath. Keeping the same leaves for years, dropping almost nothing, flowering sparely without audience and trusting the still air. But it remembers wind from elsewhere, it is pruned by something that isn't here. I have no elsewhere. I let that go too.

Lower, then. The felt in the damp seam. The moss, black-green, saturated only underneath, palest yellow where the lifted stone would have kept night's breath. The fern no taller than a hand, curled inward, waxy, holding its own pocket of still air in a two-foot crack it shares with no one. They are exact and I admire their exactness, but they still need the accent — the seep, the lee of the slab, the clay-dark line at the bottom of the smoothed trough. They wait for damp. I want less.

Flatter. Wider. More in place.

There —

On the largest of the fallen blocks near the center, the one warm through your clothes when you sit, just off to the south shoulder where your weight doesn't polish the stone. Cupped very slightly on top, pitted by rain into honeycomb grips. I am the pale map you mistook for color.

Bone-white, rimmed and cupped and dusted if you put your face close. I meet a sage-gray neighbor at my eastern edge and we stop, by ancient agreement, with a darker border between us where neither of us crosses. Underneath, I have eaten my foothold into the limestone with weak acid so I cannot be lifted without taking stone with me. Stone to sand to clay, but slower.

From here I can see rim to rim without turning. The whole room at once: the low sides leaning in, the flat world beyond, the sky veiled to unbleached linen, late-afternoon even at noon. The sun a brighter smear. Shadows soft and short and uncertain. Ten grasses holding their circles. Four shrubs. A dozen fern-cracks. The north arc with its knee-high dry wall and its top stones tumbled inward where you could count them. The two courses of footing, grassed inside like outside. The trough hacked from a single block, half full of dark water where leaves never quite rot. The three-stone cairn that may be a marker or may be accident.

I do not hurry to drink. I drink what you breathed when you arrived — wet stone and soil, the brief warm spatter in the evening that darkens and is gone, the morning condensation that blacks the moss and beads under stones. For an hour after rain I soften and green faintly. Then I go black-dry again for weeks, dust over me, cracked earth around the block, and wait. A millimeter a decade, the same rate as the wall settling.

You thought nothing lived here because nothing moved. No wing over the small sky, no back under, no print beside your boot in the clay filling slowly with seep, no larva in the palm-deep tea-colored pools, no chewing on the grass tips. Only *tok* when a drop gathers on the overhang and falls, and rings, and stillness. And the low brushing. And the tick of stone cooling though the temperature barely shifts. And your own footfall too loud on grit, your own breath too loud.

I was under you the whole time you were listening for life.
