At first I think I'd be standing on the lip.

Up where the ridge ends and the wind shears off and doesn't enter. Looking down into the bowl at the low dark boxes spaced too far apart, at the black glass ringing under no one's boots, at the four masts leaning in. The climber. The one who still has lungs full of iron and hot stone and thinks *empty* means *abandoned*.

I stay there too long. That's the first shape. The silhouette against brown dusk, afraid to go down because going down means the eleven-minute crossing, lip to lip, and being the only loud thing in it.

Then I go down anyway. The glass takes my weight and rings faintly and I wince at my own sound.

For a while I think I'll be the one in the lookouts. You know them — the stairways that go nowhere but up to the little platforms no one uses. I climb one, because it's what a person does, find high ground to see from. The foamed basalt is warm through my soles even in shadow. Too hot to sit on. Below me the alleys run like dry riverbeds waiting for their hour, every roof sloped, every gutter etched and clean. I tell myself: I could be the watchman here. The keeper of the almost-rain.

But nothing comes to watch except heat rising in sheets off the plaza and the condensers slowly turning their dead-flower fins a fraction toward the wind. And after dark, I learn not to look long over the rim. The haze below glows from underneath, the drop is too far, the sharp spatter wants to stay sharp. Vertigo leans. I come back down.

Then, for an embarrassing day, I think I'll be the one in the center.

I walk into the void court at mid-dusk — fifty paces of perfect glass, four masts humming in my teeth — and I speak, just to hear it. My voice comes back before I've finished, a tiny pre-echo, and the hum lifts half a tone to meet me and my shadow smears four ways toward the masts instead of away from the sullen coal of a sun. A headache blooms behind my eyes and stays until the fog burns off.

No. I am too heavy, too oily, too fast, too future-noisy to be that. That kept quiet is not for standing in. It's for keeping. The Keeper lives there, clearest when there is nothing to see, holding the moment when vapor can't decide to be rain the way you'd hold a bubble on a wire. My leaning hurts it. Its leaning hurts me. Lesson learned.

So I move to the edges again, quieter this time. I try doors. They open onto cool darkness and swept floors with no dust and no footprints. The houses breathe. A spalled corner I scuffed in the morning is smoothed over by evening, foamed smooth as if licked. I understand: I will not be a citizen. There are no citizens. There are conditions.

Where I find myself, finally, is not a house. It's the shade-tunnel underneath one — where a second story bridges over a narrow alley to make shadow. At fog-hour.

Because that's when the pocket weeps.

The thin hot breath from the fissures hits the cooler high wind spilling over and for an hour, maybe two, intimacy arrives. Tick, tick, tick on basalt. The net overhead, strung rim to rim on lines you only see when light catches them, beads first. Then *plink* into the gutters. The drums knock as they cool. The whole town gurgles underneath me, peristalsis under the plate, cistern to cistern leveling itself. The gut digesting fog.

I crouch there in the tunnel and put out my sleeve, like people — if there were people — would have put out bowls. I take only what beads. The public throat a few paces down holds three inches of rust-red water that will be gone by next dusk. I don't dam. I let the rest run down to where the stone is driest.

And if I stay still enough, the other inhabitants stop routing around me and start including me.

On the north-facing wall to my left, the varnish darkens. Ochre and gunmetal and bruised violet, lobes and terraces no bigger than a fingernail filling and spilling. Matte going satiny at the edges first. They swell. If I hold my breath I can hear the faint fizzing of chemistry too eager to wait. They taste my shadow as a coolness, my breath as a brief acid. For twenty-three hours they are geology. For this one frantic hour they are fast. I learn to be a weather for them — a coolness that doesn't touch. My oils stay off. I am the dry spot they are slowly growing around.

In the hot fissure by the drums, the bloom I slept next to as bare crust is furred white and lemon-yellow by morning, needles bending heavy with Almost-water, then sugaring and collapsing by mid-cycle back to crust. Bloom, collapse, bloom. Inhale, exhale. A lung that only lives to die well. I learn my own heat feeds it. I sit near enough to warm it overnight and far enough not to scorch the feathers. My fissure learns me.

What am I, then?

Not the film — I can't drink sideways through stone, I can't share south through glass, I can't remember in microns.

Not the bloom — I can't rebuild myself nightly more feathery, more efficient, with no gardener but heat.

Not the gut — I can't move water under a plaza without hands.

Not the Keeper — I will never be light enough to live inside un-weight and be overlooked.

I am the fifth kind of presence that makes the vastness between presences bearable. The commensal animal. The temporary condensation. The one who learned the eleven-minute pace that doesn't stir the air, whose boots don't ring, who doesn't call out because answering would break the tuning.

I find myself pocketed in the sky, in a hot dim bowl full of Almost-water and almost-life, doing maintenance without tools: sloping myself toward the fog. Letting the films drink for all the walls, including through me. Letting the gut include my footsteps in its leveling. Letting the Keeper stop leaning toward me because for an hour a day I am empty enough, quiet enough, and — in my small, sweating, sleeve-held-out way — active enough as the stone.

At first I thought the top was empty.

Now I am part of how full it is.
