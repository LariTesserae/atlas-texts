I would go down to it, because there is no other way in.

I would not stay on the rim looking down. The rim is for pigeons that never land. The white slit hurts. You have to let the stairs take you — brick gone soft, moss bellied under your shoes — down through the change in light and then the change in air, until your shirt is already clinging and you are already breathing through cloth.

At first I think I know what I am. A visitor. Eyes and feet. Two loud breaths in a place that has only one slow breath. I turn sideways to pass where pipes run at ankle and shoulder, where fronds ten feet tall brush my face and hot metal brushes my back. I leave the only fresh footprint in the black muck. I am an interruption, I tell myself. A witness.

Then I stand still long enough and that stops being true.

Because if I stand still — really still, on one of the worn paths where the drip keeps the growth low, between the boiler with its side blown out and the fern brake leaning on it — the hollow starts to edit me.

The first edit is time. I come down hurried, city-hurried, heart fast, and the place reads that as excess heat. There is already too much heat. So it slows me. *Chuff...* the engine sighs twenty seconds around. *...chuff.* The fungi on the gasket swell and slack. My back finds a warm pipe — not hot enough to burn, leaves press against it all day and do not burn — and I lean, and I feel it: my pulse, unasked, lengthening out toward the flywheel. Turn. Turn. A spoke comes around, disappears into fern shadow, comes around again. I stop checking my watch. Drip, unfurl, oxidize, turn is enough.

Am I sweat, then? It would be easy to be sweat here. Everything sweats. The walls weep, the handrail leaves my palm wet and rust-orange, the leaves are warm as skin and beaded. Heat rises off the floor plates where the grates glow red and meets the cool sinking from above, and all day water condenses and falls and rises again. I watch beads lift off the underside of a pipe near the hot grate and rise a foot before vanishing, and I think — I could be that. A bead that learned to walk down and then decided to go up.

No. Closer: I am breath.

I am the only hot, moving, exhaling thing to come down in a long time, and the Tenant angles toward me the way it angles toward a steam leak. The fronds nearest me incline, almost imperceptibly. The waxy crimson trumpets open and close with the heat cycle, and now, faintly, with me. It is not eating. The petals fall whole. Nothing is chewed. It withdrew that experiment long ago. It is just — including. Borrowing the animal part it no longer grows for itself.

So I let myself be borrowed. That is where you would find me, if you came down after: not in the tipped control booth with its levers INTAKE BLEED RETURN, not up on the valve wheel where the empty laced boot has gone green at the seams. You would find me behind the pump house where the vines part, with my hand flat on the retaining wall, feeling through moss and warm brick the deeper knock underneath. Or a minute later, back against the pipe again. A mobile lung. A temporary organ thrust up into cooler air to breathe and sweat and catch amber light from the caged bulbs and the EXIT signs that point nowhere.

The longer I stay the more I become itself, and the more itself becomes distinct from me, which is the same thing down here.

I think in gradients now, not sentences. Warmer here where the compressor kicks on with a shudder in my molars. Wetter there where relief valves hiss white jets that vanish into leaves and return as rain. More pressure in that pipe, less light on this leaf. A creak as a stem takes up water beside my ear. A soft pop as a seed pod bursts in the heat and throws iron-flecked seed a foot onto my shoe. I do not swat. Haste reads as heat. I drink the soup of iron and chlorophyll and hot oil slowly.

And I remember in texture, the way it does. Bark embossed with diamond-plate under my fingertips. Iron bellied like bamboo at the joints. Brick rounded. Concrete spalled to knuckles. Verdigris veins in a fern held to bulb-light. Small hard rivets up the midrib of the elephant-ears, spaced exactly like the boiler plate they lean on. This is not decoration. This is body. For a little while, my body too.

If I stayed — and part of me, the slowed part, wants to explain staying as reasonable — I would weather. That is the word. Not rot, not rust, not grow. Weather. My shirt would stay clinging. My palm would stay rust-orange. My breath would stay shallow and hitched to the spoke coming around. I would become polish on dried blood. I would become a mark, like the painted arrow pointing into solid vine, like the number stenciled on a pipe that disappears and comes up twenty feet away. Useful, then illegible, then texture.

But I am an animal. I come and go. That is my whole function here: to come, to be noticed, to go and carry the notice out.

So eventually I peel my back off the pipe — it takes a moment, sweat holds — and I lift my hand off the wall, leaving a brief dry print that beads over again in seconds, and I climb back up the soft stairs toward white fury, leaving the footprint in the muck already filling with seep at the edges.

What I take up is not a souvenir. It is a rate. For days my heart will try, when I am still, for twenty seconds. Turn. Turn.
