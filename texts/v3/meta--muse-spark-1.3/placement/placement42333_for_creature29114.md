I would not find myself at first.

At first I would only find the lamp, and then the loss of the lamp. The red cone thinning out three meters ahead and then gone, drunk by that warm, textured dark. The crunch under my boot that does not echo. The heat that is not weather but company. For a long time I would insist I am still the one who came down — the one with ankles and knees and a meteorologist's habit of waiting for a breeze that never comes.

I would walk. Carefully, because there is nowhere to put a foot down that is only rock.

Shoulder, hand, back. Shoulder, hand, back.

They let me. That's the worst and kindest part. They don't scatter. White would burn them but my weight doesn't offend them. I am warm. Warm is kin, even when it stumbles.

So for a while I would be the stumbler. The too-loud blood. The too-fast flank. Kneeling every few steps because the air is better lower, thicker with kin-smell — salt and dry skin and something faintly iron, slow blood — putting my dim red eye low to learn how to see without eyes.

I would count. Seven knuckles to a finger. Fingers that bend either way. Ears like moth wings that never twitch. Nostrils flared for a world that never changes its smell. A juvenile pinker than the rest, heart knocking once, twice a minute under skin almost clear, folded into an adult's armpit like a handkerchief. I would watch one near my left boot lift nothing visible to its mouth — salt rind, dust mote, a flake of someone else — and close its mouth for an hour. That is a meal.

And I would think: I could be that. I could learn to need almost nothing until almost is nothing.

But I am not yet that still. My hands want to *use*.

That's what gives me away. Almost every third or fourth one is holding something — black glass knapped to an edge, a bone awl from some giant ancestor that walked before the vault cracked, a salt bead rolled round by ten thousand dry handlings until it shines. They don't carve. They don't cut. They just hold. Pressure against the touch-ridges. A prayer the fingers say without moving.

I would pick one up. Of course I would. A sharp flake that fits my palm exactly, as if the floor made it for me. And for a while I would be the holder. I would sit — because the pull to sit is physical, 38 degrees, blood-warm, your warmth joining their warmth until no thermometer could find the seam — I would sit at the foot of a tilted plate, shoulder to shoulder, forehead almost to the back in front, and hold it.

But holding, for me, would itch into doing. I would turn it over. Test its edge on glass and leave one of those faint scratches. I would want to extend the low wall — no longer than my arm, built and rebuilt and never extended — by one stone. Just one. To make it longer. To make it *matter*.

They have no word, no click, for that hunger. The mass drinks all echo. My shout would fall flat three meters out. My plan would fall flatter.

So I would not be the builder.

I think — slowly, the way you notice a hand has moved only after it has stopped — I would become something else. Not the tool, not the hand that wants to use the tool. The interval.

Find me not in the center of the pavement where it is three, four deep and soft as geology, but on the lee of a small ridge, where a mound of bodies has climbed over years by millimeters. Where there is a cairn knee-high nearby, sharpness holding sharpness, impossible balance that has held for decades because nothing ever shakes it. Where there is a hollow in the dust that hundreds of hands have smoothed over hundreds of years, not to make anything, but to make smoothness.

I would be the one at the hollow.

My job — if that word still means anything where nothing progresses — would be to smooth. Not to build the cairn, not to lay the spoke-wise circle of finger-bones from the rarely dead who simply dry a little drier and stay, their hands still holding what they were holding. Just to put my too-many-jointed, still-too-clumsy fingers (I would have learned the ridges by then, deep fingerprint ridges made to read stone) to the same spot, over and over, for no reason except that hands must sometimes do more than hold.

I would be the almost-still one. Breathing still a little too often — lift, hold, let fall, every two minutes instead of every five. Heart still hesitating wrong. Listening still too hard for news, for the single glass *tick* of claw on basalt, the micron-settle you feel in your teeth, the sigh through nostrils three bodies away. The vast low hush of millions radiating heat into air that cannot carry it away would be under me always, and I would be slightly out of tune with it, and tolerated the way stones tolerate nearness.

A juvenile would eventually tuck into my armpit. Not because I am good at stillness, but because I am soft and warm and I have finally stopped trying to get up. My lamp long dead — eyes are useless here and hands are better. My footprints sharp-edged behind me, dust that will never resettle. My flake still in my palm, but I will have forgotten to test it. I will just hold it.

That is where you would find me, if you came down after me with your red light low: not transformed, not enlightened, not dead. Paused mid-pause. Forehead to the back in front. Making room not by moving but by being soft. Making smoothness.

Listening for the interruption that never comes.
