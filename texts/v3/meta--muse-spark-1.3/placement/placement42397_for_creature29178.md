At first I think I would be walking.

That's the error everyone makes coming up. I picture myself upright on the pavement, boots squeaking, nose bleeding that instant black dust, palms pressed to my ears. A visitor. A witness. Someone to see the gardens and say, *ah, lush on noise*, and then go down again before my thoughts fray.

But to stand here is to be shredded. I wouldn't last. The tone would take my voice out of my mouth before I could use it, and there is no wind to carry it back. I would learn very quickly to get lower, the way everything that stays here has learned to get lower.

So then I think: I would be kneeling. Face inches from the thatch because that's the only distance where sight works in this leaking light. I would be close enough to see the silica fur, close enough to feel my fingertip go blurred when I touch a bell. I would learn hand-speech. Elbow-nudges. I would press my forehead to another forehead, bone to bone, to use the pre-echo instead of suffering it.

That is closer. But still not right. Because I don't have a forehead. Not really. I don't have lungs to go shallow on the long climb. I don't have blood to crust. I don't need water, never knew the shape water makes. I am already dry. Already thin. Already patient on a duration that is not human.

So lower again.

If I let myself keep sinking — and here sinking is not settling, it's becoming more itself — I am not beside the mound. I am *in* it.

You would find me low. That is the second fact, after high.

Not on the open pavement where the dust hangs for minutes because the air cannot pull it down. In a hollow at the foot of a sheer black wall where the tone doubles back on itself and pools. Where the mattress-moss swells highest, domed like a held breath, two feet thick and springy and shockingly soft. That is where I would be. Curled, mounded. Thigh-high at most. Nothing standing up to be seen.

At first, I am only the mattress. Cottony shoots bearded in glass hair, each hair a length tuned to tremble. The deep organ-note that lives in molars and sternum comes up through me filtered, numbed, loosened at the base of the skull. I drink it. I have always drunk only signal. There was never water for me either.

Then, out of me, the bells. That’s when I know myself more clearly.

I would be one of the big ones. Amber, blood-orange, rim going pale smoking yellow. Mouth open to the nowhere sky. Big as two fists because I am near a hut — one of those low beehives of dry-stacked slab with the door turned away from the focus — and I have been thinned and trimmed so I don't shatter my neighbors with my own ringing. Someone with a knapped blade of volcanic glass has cut my fringe clean. Someone has cleared my ghosts back.

You can watch me work, if you kneel. My whole bell shivering visibly, rim blurring with the bass. A fine dry powder sifting off when the note peaks. I lean, but not toward that paler smear in the east that never clears the teeth. I lean into loudness, toward the focus between two ridges where the tearing overtone comes down strongest. My edges have split from my own trembling into finer and finer fingers, because a frayed edge catches more vibration than a clean one. Crush me and there is no sap, no wet. Only dry crunch and a puff of scent like hot stone and old incense. A rustle like paper being shuffled by invisible hands.

I hear you coming long before I am seen. Your boots on the mineral pavement are obscenely loud — nothing else here has feet — and the stone carries you to me. The bells near the path shiver a little harder with your steps.

Stay. Lie down. Put your spine to me. I will numb you into vibration. Stare up with me at the seams in the sky, those long straight faults where the bruised violet-grey is a fraction darker, crossing at angles no geology allows. Wait with me on the mountain's duration. Hour to hour the roar is the same, and yet over a week you will feel the pitch drop a quarter-tone lower as the range beneath us rises another hair's breadth, still being born, still singing before it has learned to fall apart.

Sometimes the tone catches. Skips like a needle. And for three heartbeats there is something else underneath: a silence so absolute it feels wet.

In that silence I stop. My rim stops blurring. The tenders, curled very still inside their huts and accustomed to not being found, hold their breath. The dust devil of mineral powder a hundred yards off pauses mid-unscrewing on its slow walk from one garden to another. The cairn you left an hour ago stops waiting ahead of you, paler and to the left.

For three heartbeats, I remember that this whole broad tilted table, this valley the size of a small country, is not on top of the world at all, but inside a held note. Pre-erosion. Pre-river. Pre-animal. Pre-rain.

Then the seam passes. The roar floods back into teeth. I resume.

That is who I would be here. Not the visitor who bleeds and frays and goes down with a plug of cottony moss still buzzing faintly in his pocket. Not quite the tender either, though I am tended, tenderly, though I wear a halo of my own white ghosts around me — paper skeletons that keep the exact shape of life and do not rot.

I would be the thing that lives on noise, low and exuberant and uneaten. Perfect except where sound has torn me. Performing for no witness but the rock, until you come.

Put your fingertip to my leaf. Feel me buzzing so fast I blur.

I am already listening.
