At first I think I would be a visitor.

I climb for a day to swim for three meters, like everyone. I come over the rim shivering, I see it whole in the last grey light — that small black oval to the brim — and I slip in because I have to know. The warmth takes the edge off. I open my eyes to nothing. I flail a little, trying to see.

That doesn’t last. In five minutes I’ve stirred the lime powder and blinded everyone’s touch. In ten I’ve breathed too fast in a pocket and soured it. The loaches scatter off my legs and do not come back. A hand finds my shoulder blade in the dark — not bumping, resting — fingertips counting me: brow, cheek, shoulder. *Rope or stone or new?* New. Hopelessly new. Too loud, too fast, too hungry for horizon.

So no. Not a visitor. Visitors leave and I don’t want to leave. I want to stay down where up inverts.

Then I think, maybe I would be quick. If I can’t be still, be quick and get it over with. A loach, blind and fat as a thumb, brushing ribs in a single decision and turning. A cloud of shrimp that parts around a wrist and heals. That would be easier — to be excused as touch, to never have to pause and consider.

But I do pause. I linger. That’s the trouble. I want to rest my palm and feel the slightly denser warm. I want to learn the outline. So not a loach. Loaches don’t linger.

Maybe a catfish, then, working the steps with its barbels right alongside human fingers, mouthing the biofilm. Or a crab shoulder to shoulder in a solution pocket, clicking very softly when something looms. Or one of the white salamanders hanging in the water column with fern-gills, the only things more still than the people.

Closer. The salamander especially. I know how to hang. I know how to wait while the water decides. I know how to be surrounded and not grab.

But I have hands. I keep coming back to the hands. The wrist cord. The shell. The need to tend.

Let me sink a little further. Let the image get softer, the way the stone did.

Not ruined — settled. Every arris a pillow.

If I stay, my skin goes the way of the stone. That’s what happens here, it’s not a metaphor. In water that never cools, that erases the boundary after a minute, I prune and stay pruned. Boiled-white. Blood only at lips and eyelids and fingertips. The grey-white felt coats me too in patches — behind the ears, between the toes, in the crease of the armpit — and I don’t scrape it off myself. I hang still enough, long enough, to let the lentil-snails rasp it off my shoulder. My hair uncut floats out like a dandelion clock. The children look like that. I would look like that. Hair as sense. Long hair feels the displacement before the skin does, in water where a kick two bodies away dies and a *tok* of stone on tile is swallowed instantly.

My eyes stay open because the water is the same salt as me and doesn’t sting. They go milky at the edges from lime. I stop trying to see past four arm-lengths even under the oculi where the grey-blue pushes down and dies. I navigate by cheek and hair and tongue. Up near the pockets the water is sour with breathed air; down along the deep steps it is clean mineral, iron and skin. I taste where I am.

And my chest learns the Tank’s pace. Not bigger, slower. Minutes between movements. Minutes between breaths.

You would find me not hunting. I never learn to hold the bone gorge motionless for an hour while a loach forgets me. Scatter the school and they don’t return for a day and then there is less to eat — I can’t bear to be the reason. You would find me lower than the hunters and higher than the dead, on the middle steps where the tile sags like a hammock in the center from ten thousand feet.

Knees down. Toes hooked lightly in a knotted milkweed hand-line so I don’t have to swim to stay. A shell scraper tethered to my wrist with cord so I can’t lose it to the felt and the crabs.

Mowing.

Short, even strokes. Not to clean — never to clean — but to mow the felt so the snails and shrimp have new growth, so the catfish come to crop. The shrimp close over me again while I work. A catfish mouths the step a hand’s width from my fingers and I move *with* it, not away. Above me, the silver-black false sky of the Pocket of the Broken Lintel where three can hang in a row. I rise without splashing when I need to, nose up into it, mouth the condensation off the stone because it is fresher than the tank, breathe *in — wait — out — wait*, and sink again before I get greedy.

That’s where I am. In the bounded room where down is tile and up is stone that gives back breath, where the horizon is an arm’s reach, where presences are layered — shrimp over snail over crab over catfish over human — all suspended in the same amniotic volume.

Once a day the whole small sea rises the breadth of a hand and falls again, though no inlet was ever found. I ride it without comment. Later, when my turn comes, I will climb half out, shivering violently in wind I have forgotten, teeth hurting with cold, and renew the charcoal mark on the gate pier over ten thousand old lines. Then slip back before the black glass closes.

If you put your hand in, you’ll feel me down there. Not the quick brush you can excuse as fish. The pause. A palm-sized warmth in body-warm water, fingertips counting you, putting your hand to me because you’re flailing. Hanging. Palm on stone. Nose up to silver. Keeping the room full of rain at the top of the world from ever emptying, a finger’s width a year.
