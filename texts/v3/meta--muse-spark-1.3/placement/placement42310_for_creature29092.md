At first I thought I would be standing.

On the rim, looking down at the bowl that is not a bowl but a roof made of roofs. Breath in front of me. Fingertips already going dull. Eyes watering from the white.

I thought I would be the walker. The boots squeaking on wet slab. The jacket too loud. The one who goes roof to roof because there are no streets, stepping from catchment to catchment, learning not to brace carelessly because the stone is young and still sharp enough to cut.

But that doesn't hold. A walker wants to get somewhere. Here the fall from top to bottom is three hundred feet over a mile, and a cup of water takes days to do it. There is nowhere to get to except down, slowly, to the center sump. And I am too warm and too fast for that. My nose runs from cold while my eyes run from glare and the dissonance never resolves.

So I crouch. Knees get wet. That is the price.

If I stay crouched long enough, I stop being the walker and start being the looker. In the slot-alley where two wet walls nearly kiss and light falls straight down in a white stripe. Utter black in the shadow, wet rock almost black, dry rock dove grey. Waiting for my eyes to adjust to a place with no green at all, no scurry, no flicker. No face returning my look.

I thought then I would be an eye. Just an eye on the south pans after rain, ankle-deep, watching my reflection warp so slowly I am not sure it moves. Or an eye on the east cones, those tall beehives with their caps missing, each holding two feet of sky-mirror water. Waiting until the wind drops and the silica skin tightens across the stillness — clear as nothing.

Touch it and it cracks into polygons that tilt and clink almost inaudibly and sink. Come back tomorrow and it has reformed. I could be that. A ledger that grows by vanishing and returning. Dissolve by day in bright cold, precipitate by night.

But an eye is still too hungry. It wants movement to catch.

Let it go longer. An hour. Past the hour when you call out without meaning to and the sound dies too fast, soaked up by water.

I am no longer standing, no longer even crouching exactly. My palm is flat on a north-facing wall. In a minute there is the damp print of my hand, and then beads beyond it. The mountain exhaling up through everything. Cold coming through the dark blue-grey slab that rings when tapped. Drip — pause — drip from a hundred spouts not in rhythm. The faint hiss where sheet-flow crosses pavement. Wind combing over the rim, flipping a curtain of seep into spray.

Under that, at long intervals, a deep soft *tock* I feel more in the soles than hear. Stones finding each other.

I find myself lower than I expected. Not on the high spine running rim to rim, though I walked it and saw the rust-black beards ten feet long and only as thick as paint, each streak lengthening downward one wet season at a time. Not in the west field of cairns either, though I walked over their saddles because you cannot walk between them — each man-high, each exactly as wide as a man with arms out — and understood without needing to say what they are.

I find myself where you cannot live. That is where I end up.

Lie flat and look into the covered channel where the runnel drops through a chink. Forehead wet from drip. Utter black a half-arm in. Hanging from the lintel stone, frost that isn't frost: white fringe, hair-fine, trembling when the hiss passes above. It eats damp itself. It eats dark. It does not spread beyond the dark — an inch out into the white light and it stops, as if cut with a knife.

If I am anything here, I am that covenant. To stay in the dark I was given.

Or no — wider than that, thinner than that. Not the fringe, not the paint, not the skin, though I have put my nose to the ochre and sage-grey films that do not rise above the stone and felt the cold coming off them slightly less sharp, as if they hold a fraction of warmth. A millimeter a year. Do not touch, you'll tear a decade.

If I am anything here, I am the slowing itself.

Find me in the hesitation at each lip. The groove that runs to a spout — just a hollowed stone tongue sticking out. The spout dripping into an open runnel along the top of the next wall down. The runnel dropping through a chink into a sunken cistern, round, stone-lined, dark water four feet down, cold as glass. Overflow seeping out its base into the next terrace's pavement, cambered to sheet-flow to the next groove. Down and down. Patient.

Find me in the bulge before the break. That half-inch fall with a tap like a clock ticking too slowly to time.

I thought I would be a resident with legs or wings or a hearth. There is no smoke stain here. No midden because no meal was ever finished here. The low trapezoid doors are either slab-shut or slab-dark, and the dark smells only of wet rock. No one who needed fire could stay.

So I unlearn fire. I unlearn hurry. My breath stays visible and then condenses on a thousand roofs at night and begins again, and that is enough of a body.

You would find me south after rain, or east in the cones, or north with my cheek near the weeping streaks, but most truly you would find me nowhere you can point to — in the thin, constant stitching of trickles under the quiet. In the flicker as clouds thin and thicken and wet rock goes black and dry rock pales.

What I am is not a creature, but a persistence. A cup of water on its third day from rim to sump. A hand-print evaporating in bright cold. A settlement in the soles.

If you come back in ten thousand years, when the sharp fresh-broken edges have finally rounded, I will still be here, a finger wider, a thread longer, a polygon reformed.
