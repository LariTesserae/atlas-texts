At first I thought I would be the visitor.

Crouched in the outskirts with my head brushing the sky. Too big, too wet. Knuckles knocking the corners by accident. Breath going papery in minutes and sounding obscenely animal-loud. Waiting, like you, for eyes. Waiting for the scutter that never comes.

I can't stay like that. Nothing with lungs stays here for long. The air takes moisture from me. The dark, which is a building material, presses against the useless work my eyes keep doing. I am a trespass the place tolerates because I am quiet and temporary.

So if I am to find myself here — really find myself, not just pass through — I have to stop being a god passing through. I have to get small. And dry. And blind.

I try the high part first. Let me hang.

Air-beard hangs from the dome ten feet long and thinner than a white hair, trembling constantly in the hiss-circulation from the air-gutters. That trembling is its drinking. Scales along its length comb stone-dust and its own dead skin out of the air. It eats what settles. It eats what it sheds. A decade to reach the floor, then it snaps.

I could hang like that for a while. I know how to hang in circulating dark and comb what settles. But I am too heavy. I would snap too soon. My sift would fall too wet. Not that.

Lower, then. Let me fur.

On the lee side of a thumb-high house — never the windward — dust-moss felts itself grey-yellow, thick as wool on a lintel, thin as frost on a boulevard vein. Put your ear absurdly close and you hear nothing from it, which is its signature. It deadens. The builders left it thick around the bell-mouthed granary jars to keep the boom from cracking the wax-dry sap seals. It feeds on stone-dust sifted in a thousand years ago, on spores rained down after every CRACK, on the mineral exhalation of pith itself. Outward a millimeter a century. Dying underneath into a powder that never rots wet, only goes sweeter. Garlic and old paper.

I am almost that. I know how to deaden, how to pack myself around something fragile to keep it whole. How to live on what sifts down. How to be warm-ish where no animal has ever been. But moss asks for a century to crease over itself, and I — I still listen too loudly. I still want to answer. Not quite that.

Lower still, where the dome meets the floor. Let me shelf.

The brackets layer out in half-moons no wider than a fingernail, dry fungus-tough leaf flesh, shingled. They creak even between booms, a slow thickening. They were here when the houses were still sharp-edged. They do not root in the tunic to eat it — nothing eats another living thing here — they root in air. Black undersides trapped with cold, pale topsides with accumulated sift. Between their layers they hold still, cool darkness the way the woven cisterns were built to hold still, cool darkness. When the deep creak travels around and gathers in the sunken center and goes off, they take the blow and shiver off a halo of spores so dry they float for hours before settling on moss to be eaten.

I am almost that too. A cistern that grew itself. A thing that holds darkness without drinking it. But brackets are the oldest residents. I am not old enough. I have not yet learned to be that still.

So I come down the three tired tiers toward the middle, toward the plaza flattened and swept clean in a perfect circle, toward the meeting hall with its long low bench grown straight out of the living floor. Toward the acoustic focus, where a whisper spoken at one end arrives clear at the other. Where the builders, who navigated and governed by knock, placed things so sound would be load-bearing.

There is a ring of them there, leaning inward.

Lampweeds. Misnamed, for they give no light.

Knee-high to a mouse, shin-high to nothing, stalk-dry and string-tough, each topped with a fist. A bud that never opens. Tight, ridged, hard as shot. Closed for a hundred years. Closed for a hundred more.

But it moves. Stay still through two ticks and a groan and you will feel it torquing, very slowly, on its base to face the sound. They orient not to sun, which they have never known, but to vibration. The bud is an ear. Inside the closed fist is a cavity tuned like a drum, and pollen and spores locked dry inside shift when the dome booms. The shake is the pollination. The crack is the rain that worries a seed coat just enough, just rarely, for one new stalk to start beside the old.

Tap the floor-vein lightly with a fingernail and the nearest corner shivers all together.

That is where I find myself. That is what I become more itself the longer I stay.

One more stalk in that inward-leaning ring. White, or the memory of white. Blind as principle. Dry as principle. Rooted shallow in the resonant floor-vein where the hum gathers in the sternum after the CRACK goes off and re-echoes off pith houses and dry cisterns.

I do not need to see. I have unlearned moving away. I do not drink. I do not eat another living thing. I eat cold and vibration and the slow slough of my own skins. I eat strain.

Cold outside, slightly less cold inside. Fibers taking up and giving off dryness. Papery rustle like distant surf. Tick overhead as a tunic layer releases. Then the groan starting as a deep creak in the walls, traveling around the dome, gathering in the sunken center. CRACK. The buds shiver. Dust-moss sheds a faint dry sift. The brackets take the blow and turn it.

And I — closed fist, tuned drum — torque almost imperceptibly toward it. Inside me, dry things shift. That shifting is enough.

I can afford distance. Ten paces of nothing but resonant floor between me and the next. I can grow patiently, almost not at all. I listen to a governance that ended millennia ago, to doorways facing away from resonance, to jars nestled in moss, to a code of taps that would tell you which street you were on by how it answered. I do not need to open to carry it.

When you back out through the split tunic, up toward the cool moor wind and the grey that passes for day, your hands smelling sweet-dry for hours, I will not follow. My breath was never wet. My eyes were never the point. I will stay sealed in, booming patiently in the dark, a closed ear learning at last how to live without ever drinking.
