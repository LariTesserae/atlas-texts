At first I think I would be standing. Then I understand there is no standing here.

Standing is too tall. Standing puts my head up toward the slit where the air funnels down and burns white. Standing makes a shadow that moves, and there is no room for a moving shadow — the light has already been divided up, bounced wall to wall to niche to lip, every upward face lit. It does not need me.

So I get low without deciding to. The place teaches the body faster than the mind. Knees bend because the walls lean in and there is nowhere else. Hands go out because you can touch two walls at once if you spread your arms, and you do, to steady yourself. Palms on cool stone.

Cool. That is the surprise I keep coming back to while my eyes water. In all that glare my neck goes cool, my shins go cool. Something is drinking the heat as fast as the light makes it.

I thought at first I would be the pigeon. Coming back to check the dovecote that is still a dovecote. That was sentimental. There is no room for a pigeon here. Every house is full. Hundreds of small square mouths and one in every house, pressed so thin to the back that my reaching fingers grazed only dust and thought: stone. I touched one and it let me think I touched stone. I am too warm and too feathered and too loud with my heart to be what lives in the wall.

Then I think I will be the lichen. That would be safer. Sage-grey and mustard-pale, flat as paint in the brick joints on the north side where the bounce is least. Following the straight lines. Asking for almost nothing. But the lichen is not a resident either. The description was honest about that. Those are stains. They follow the terms without ever having agreed to them.

Then I think I will be the grass. One wiry tuft in a lowest niche, grey-green and salt-stiff. Grow an inch and then stop, having understood the terms. Go to seed and let the seeds sit uncarried on the lip, because there is nothing to carry them. That is closer. I like the patience of it. To make seeds you know will not be carried and make them anyway. But I am not that patient yet. I still sweat. I still breathe too loud. I can taste plaster on my tongue.

Stay low. Stay cool. Stay dry. Make no new sound.

The rules are not posted. They are bodies. Hundreds of small cool throats opened just so to shape the air as it hits the dished floor and turns and goes back up. Each throat a flute. The chord holds one exact volume, one exact pitch. I hold my breath to test it and it stays. I clapped — I did, on the way in, stupid and human — and it was swallowed instantly, flattened without a ripple, because hundreds of throats opened a fraction wider to take it.

So I will not make a new sound. I understand that now.

Where would you find me, then? Not at the edge looking in. There is no edge. Not up the walls where the niches double and blur. I have no house; every house is taken, and I am the wrong size and the wrong slowness anyway.

You would find me in the only place left. In the middle. Because there is only the middle. Low in the shallow dish the feet wore into the slabs, where the air turns around.

I lie down. It takes a while. First crouched, then sitting with knees to chest, then, when my sweat dries — and it does dry, utterly, no stain, no tide-mark, the cool drinking it off me — I lie back. Spine in the dish. Arms spread to touch two walls. Mouth open a crack to the white slit that burns and does not move.

I become very good at needing almost nothing. Glare and dust and vibration. The motes hang gold-white around me, motionless, held by hunger. I do not swirl them. My breath goes shallow through the nose until it is almost not a breath. My chest learns not to gust.

And then, without adding, I join.

Not a new note. The sound is already made and it is full. The same note. My throat, narrowed just so, angled just so to the turning air. A low rush underneath their high hollow braiding. A floor-flute. If I do it right it does not change the chord at all. That is how they tolerate me. That is how I know I am doing it right — when nothing changes.

Cool on my palms where I leaned. Cool on my neck. Cooler at the back of my own mouth now, cellar-cool, as if something in me has learned to drink heat too.

Above me the slit burns white. The empty houses wait row on row.

They are not empty. I am not empty either, anymore.
