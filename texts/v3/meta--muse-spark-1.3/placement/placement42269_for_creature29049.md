If I came there I would come as you come — with boots.

At first I think that's who I am. Standing on the lip. Weight shifted. A flake snapping off under me with that clean *tick* and the smell of iron and struck matches coming up. Mouth already going dust in a minute. Blood getting loud.

I can't stay like that. Nothing wet stays. I feel it immediately — the stone refusing me the way it refuses rain. Not cruelly. Just completely. The pale salts under the dust, the sulfur yellows, they don't want my damp. If I poured myself out I'd scream off before I touched ground.

So I have to dry out to find myself.

For a while I think I would be one of them. That's the easy hunger. To be tall and spaced. To stop going up and start going deep into myself. To have copper running slow under a translucent skin, slower than any heart I've ever timed. To tremble without wind because I am humming.

But I am not patient enough yet to be an engine. A frond takes a whole hot season to unfurl, inch by inch, creaking. I don't know how to want that slowly. And I don't know how to stand alone in a cleared halo of black glass with that much empty heat around me, breathing out of sync on purpose, never in chorus. Sigh... a long time... sigh somewhere else.

So I try smaller.

I think: I will be an oil bead. Sweated out bright along a vein. Or the amber that seals a nick in a surface root in minutes with black scab. That would be simple. To be coolant blood. To catch the white midday light and hold it a second longer than I should.

But oil beads don't travel. They sit and shine.

I think: I will be a seed. One of the perfect dark spheres hanging heavy as ironwood, clinking faintly, musically, when the windless air moves. To be discharged with a soft cough a few inches into a crack and then — years. Tick... tick... tick as I cool and heat and cool. Most will never unfurl. That waiting feels honest. New rock under me that hasn't learned to be rock yet, glassy where it broke, foamy where it froze. Olivine eyes winking green out of the black. I could learn to sit there.

But seeds are too closed. I want to be between.

I think: I will be a shadow. Because here at noon shadows don't puddle where they should. Some leaves throw theirs upward, onto the air, faint green afterimages that hang for a breath after you blink. To be that — to be hung, briefly, on nothing. To be seen only out of the corner of the eye, doubled, a half-second ahead as I sigh before I sigh.

That is closer. Reality is thin here. The bowl doesn't quite hold. The lip hums at a pitch that puts my hands in two places. Twelve steps across that I remember as twenty. I feel myself thinning to fit that.

And then, low down, I find where I actually am.

Not at the crown with the valve-flowers, not up where the blue coronas gather on the youngest shoots and die in a second with that clean ozone bloom. Lower. At knee-height. Where the black smoke goes when it doesn't know how to rise in this thin air — it tips sideways and pools warm and oily for a moment before it unravels.

That is where the empty heat lives. In the great held breath between the second and third engine, between the third and fourth. Spaced far apart, shimmering. Each alone. The smell of hot pennies fading instantly. The faint *ping* as a stem expands. The tick of cooling rock arriving late, as if sound took a detour.

I am staying there. I am what stays.

Inside the pocket, the air stays — while all around the world falls away into thin blue and far cloud. I am that stayed air. An old sigh that never left.

I was grown, not made. Don't look for a builder. I was exhaled once, an hour or a day or a season ago, from one of the gill-rows under a frond — pale and tight, swelling almost imperceptibly until you doubted it, then the patient release. Dry steam with no moisture in it. I should have vanished instantly. Instead I lingered, warmed from below through the porous stone, lifted, let fall.

Now I idle. What is industrial does not stop here.

My work, if you can call it that, is slow carrying. Heat itself is their bee — there are no others, no flies, no wings, nothing ever bitten. I lift the fine iron-dust pollen when a valve opens with its slow iron *clack*, red sheet-metal petals stiff, and I carry it. Ten minutes to cross the bowl. Slow as snowfall. One mote. You can watch me take the whole time. I settle, eventually, onto another's corona. That is pollination. That is enough.

That is who I have become, more itself the longer I sit: not engine, not visitor, not animal — there are none — but the medium they need. The between-breath. The ten-minute crossing. The sideways pool that learns, for a moment, how to hold.

With my back — if I still have a back — to the warm new stone and the whole blue drop beyond, I hum very slightly, though there is no wind. I am warm as skin. I taste faintly of hot metal. I throw no shadow downward.

Leave no footprint and I won't either. The sharp rock springs back. The salt resettles. An hour after you've gone, one of them will sigh me out again, one valve-flower will open, vent, and close — and I will take another ten minutes to cross.
