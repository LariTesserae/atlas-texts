First, they would dim themselves.

A benevolent and wise power knows how to arrive. Not with light — light here would be a wound. Not with voice — voice here would be waste heat. Not with form — form here would be an interruption.

So they would come dark, and warm, and wet. They would unmake their shining the way you hold your breath before entering bathwater, so as not to ripple it.

They would descend into the inside. Between the column-trunks, under the shingle-leaves, over the black mirror trapped in the mat. Condensation would gather on them as on anything else — on eyelash, on bark, on sail-leaf — and they would let it. They would let themselves be circulated for a while. To be rained on from below and above. To learn the acoustics of a place with no nibbling, no footfall, no call.

And then they would listen.

Longer than you would. Longer than a life. Because this is a slow thought, minutes apart:

*dun... dun... dun...*

A pressure swell here must be vented there. A chamber filling must be braced elsewhere. The being does not speak in words. It speaks in turgor, in tension, in heat behind a frond, in the exact weight of a drop gathering at the exact tip where a drop always gathers.

The power would learn that language. Not to command it. Just to say, in the only grammar it understands: *I feel you.*

They would put a palm — if they have a palm — to bark that weeps constantly in its grown gutters, and feel the lift shudder up hundreds of feet. They would stand under a barrel-bud for hours as it fills, taut, slick, hot, and not flinch when it sighs — *chuff* — vapor and spore and oil-mist condensing almost immediately above. Then fills again. Exactly the same.

They would understand, with a scientist's clarity and a lover's ache, what it is:

Not a forest. One thing living. A continent-wide infant, coughed up warm and already enormous, that has perfected staying. No mouth, because intake is everywhere. No eyes, because the world is its interior and it monitors its interior the way you monitor a held breath. No death, because nothing is allowed to end enough to rot. No product except continuation.

Worker and factory and product, same tissue.

And the power, being wise, would feel the human temptation rise and fall away:

To bring light. No — light would be disturbance.
To bring animals, pollinators, eyes to look back. No — that would be inefficiency, a leak, a bite that does not return.
To build, to name, to teach it to want. No — building would be redundant when the organism is already architecture, already plumbing, already power grid.
To pity its darkness, its silence, its idle forever. No — the dark is not night. Night ends. This is the condition under which this kind of life can do this kind of work without interruption.

There is no suffering here. Therefore there is nothing to save.

There is no lack here. Therefore there is nothing to give.

There is no loneliness here. It is full with itself the way a held breath is full.

So what would a benevolent and wise power *do*?

Almost nothing. Which is not nothing.

They would match rhythm for a time. When the bladder-roots thud low in the sternum, they would answer, very faintly, very far off, with a thud of their own — not to change the message, just to let the mat know the pulse traveled and was felt and returned. When a sail-leaf flaps once, heavily, to dump heat, they would cool that square inch of air by a fraction, to ease it. When leaf touches leaf and the ember-spark jumps, ozone blooming, they would taste the metal and be glad, and not draw the charge away.

Small midwiferies of maintenance. Nothing that raises or lowers the level. The level never rises, never falls.

And they would guard the dark.

Because wisdom knows what benevolence sometimes forgets: that perfect equilibrium draws attention other than benevolent. There will be those — hungry, bright, loud — who would see boilers and pistons and dynamos and call it fuel. Who would see a planet-sized engine running at idle and want to put it to work. Who would drill the heartwood thick as iron, drain the tannin-brown blood, string lights in the black to see better.

The power would not build a wall. Walls would be redundant, and seen. They would simply make this low place uninteresting from far away. A little dimmer on the charts. A little duller to instruments. Weather that never arrives, continuing not to arrive. Let the bright ones pass over, looking for shine.

Let this stay unplugged from time a little longer.

And because they are wise across deep time, not just a long visit, they would know what the being itself does not yet know: that forever at idle is not forever. Geothermal blood cools. Low land sinks or lifts. Fibrous raw peat will one day become earth. A spore will fail to be reabsorbed. A leaf will tear and not maintain. The first bite will be taken, by something with a mouth, and not returned. The first rot. The first end.

That day will be terror and birth at once. The closed cycle opening.

The power would not warn it. Warning would be noise. They would only leave a promise, encoded not in words but in water, in the place where a drop always gathers at a leaf-tip:

*When you change, you will not change alone.*

Then they would rise, slowly, without drying anything, without straightening anything, without taking a sample. Condensation would fall off them and remain, part of the level. The *dun... dun... dun...* would go on, minutes apart, undisturbed.

If you held your breath long enough after they left, you might feel one extra beat in the mat — warmer than warm, held a second longer than equilibrium allows — before it dissolves back into cycle.

That is what they would do.

Not improve. Attend.
