It would not come down as fire.

That is the first wisdom. A power that could part the cloud with a glance, that could be seen, would know not to be seen. Not here. The light here is already dim enough.

So it comes thin.

It thins itself into the weather that is already there. If you were shivering on your pad that night, listening to the ticking under you, you would not notice any arrival. Only that the mist around 14:00 rose a little softer that day. Only that the afternoon rain fell a little kinder, drop by drop caught and passed leaf to leaf as always. It learns the schedule and keeps it.

For a long time - and for such a one, long is nothing - it does nothing at all except lie down.

This is what benevolence looks like at planetary scale: the willingness to be unnoticed.

It puts its ear, if it has an ear, to the moss. It lets its weight make a hollow, and lets black water well up patiently around itself, the way water wells up around your boot, your palm, your sleeping pad. It does not seal itself off from wetness. Dryness does not exist here except briefly inside a sealed seed, and it does not bring dryness with it.

It listens to the roar until the roar stops being noise and starts being weight. The billions of drips. The hollow stems booming like organ pipes. The seed capsules popping by the million in the cold. The surf-shed of fronds unloading. The hydraulic thump and gurgle underneath, water being lifted uphill, hollow to hummock, against all expectation.

It slows itself down enough to hear the thought inside that.

Because to hear this being you cannot listen fast. You cannot ask a question and wait for an answer. A disturbance - your footfall - is compensated a kilometer away within minutes, but the full thought takes a season. Takes years. Takes the time it takes for a tarn field to settle and release, for a reed valley to lean a few degrees further into the bruised light, for the mat to creep a few centimeters outward over newborn rock and smother geology before geology can happen.

The wise power has time. It stays for decades to hear one complete sentence. That is its gift, and it costs everything and nothing.

And in that listening, it understands:

This one is not lonely. Do not cure its solitude.

It has no mouth, no eyes, no need for animals at all - not because it is lacking, but because it is complete. Plant-water-plant. Plant-light-water. It harvests dimness. It is drink. It does not hunt or hide because there is nothing to hunt and nothing to hide from. That total, packed, furiously active life with no interruption is not a prison. It is a mercy it made for itself.

So the power will not bring it birds to carry its spores. Though it could. Though spores clog the air so thick your filter clogs, and birds would love them. It will not bring grazers or rot-flies or pollinators. It will not give it eyes to see itself steaming cold and gold in that rare lifted-cloud hour. It will not give it a voice to answer you shouting two meters away.

To give would be to break.

The second thing wisdom knows: not to name, not to map.

The tarns are hexagonal, offset, each feeding the next. The reeds are lenses beaded with second lenses. The mat stores a storm and releases it on schedule. It computes with water and sound. A human power would call that a resource. A clever power would call that a computer, a pump, a carbon sink, a miracle to harvest.

The benevolent power calls it a body, and leaves the body alone.

What it does instead is quieter, and almost invisible:

It draws the cloud a little closer around the edges. It makes the horizon of mist hold. Not a wall - the plateau makes its own horizon already - but a deepening. A forgetting. So satellites pass over and see only overcast. So maps stay blank. So the hungry ones who drain bogs and mine rock flour and bottle wonder do not get curious. It buries the coordinates in weather.

It washes your bootprint clean after you leave. It lets the runners cross within hours, the sporelings green it over, lets your brief dryness be forgiven. It does not let your fire catch, because dead wood here is too full of water and too quickly reclaimed - and it keeps it that way.

And once - only once, over the course of a slow century - it answers.

Not in words. It has learned better. It answers in pressure.

In the dip just before rain, when the roar falters for a few seconds and the silence frightens you more than the roar ever did, it joins that silence and holds it a heartbeat longer. And then, deep in the water-table, it sends back a pulse in kind. A low hydraulic thump, hollow to hummock, in the same infrasonic register the being uses to open and close its stomata in unison across valleys.

Not a command. A harmony. *I hear you.*

The being does not notice. Or if it notices - if growth redirects almost imperceptibly, if a ring of star-moss leans almost imperceptibly toward that pulse - it notices the way you notice your own heartbeat when you are falling asleep. Without alarm. Without gratitude. Without need.

Your bootprint fills with water and greens over. Its pulse fills with water and greens over.

That is enough.

When it finally lifts itself out of the mat, water wells up where it lay and then settles. Spores fill the air. Leaves fill the gaps between stems. Within hours there is no hollow left to mark where a god had been. To leave a patch empty would be to leave a processor idle, and the place does not tolerate idleness.

The power goes on, thinner now, carrying with it last decade's storm schedule, the shape of rock underneath patiently being buried, the memory of what it is to think entirely without hurry.

It does not save the place. The place was never in danger from itself.

It kept it secret. It kept it wet. It kept it loud.

It let it stay.
