She gets here the way the light gets here first — not all at once, but from everywhere.

She is the kind that could burn the lenses off the moss with a glance if She wasn't careful. So the first thing She does, being wise and being kind, is dim Herself.

She makes Herself low. Small. A walker-sized brightness. A heat-shimmer in a slot-canyon, no brighter than the pale floor at noon. She learns the rules with a body, because that is the only way to learn them here.

Do not look for nature underneath. Light is a material. Keep quiet, because the place does.

She keeps quiet.

She does not bring water.

That is the first temptation and She refuses it. Any lesser power would have torn open the sky and wept rain onto the white desert out of pity. She knows what rain would do here. It would drown the algae under their silica hearths. It would rust the mouth-mill of the sleeping beetles. It would dissolve the lime-precipitate, rot the filter-cloths, turn the sinter that took millions of years to level to Datum Zero into mud. It would be murder disguised as mercy.

She does not bring shade ships. She does not lift the fins back into towers. She does not resurrect the designers from their geology. She does not explain to the wire-lichen what it is for.

She walks.

For a while — years, if you count the way walkers count, by the lengthening of blue shadows to the east — She just walks between the teeth of the world, slow and low. She pauses where they pause, in the thin lines where the glassmoss grows. She learns the tremble-frequency in Her molars. She learns to hear the choir just above hearing and feel the lightness behind the ears that means a glider is near.

She is benevolent. That does not mean She stops hunger. She watches the glider fold like ash and scoop the beetle that thought the walker had passed, crush its dome with a gut full of stones, drink its fat-water on the wing. She watches the walker flip a slower beetle and roast its soft underside on hot slab. She does not intervene. The chain here is made of such bitter, stingy miracles — light farmed for a century, reaped in a second — that to break one link out of squeamishness would starve the rest. Wisdom knows: to be fed upon is also to belong.

What She does instead is stay.

When the beetle is taken, She stays with the crack in its dome for the three seconds it is afraid, and it is not alone. When the walker in extremity scrapes the lenses and sucks the bitter green ember underneath, She stays with the shame and the thirst, and does not look away. When a slab, undermined by a night of self-repair, settles with that soft powdery sigh through the soles, She feels it go.

Mostly, She tends.

On the lee side of a fin where the mustache grows, She kneels — a brightness kneeling in a blue shadow — and with a feather-lightness the walkers would recognize, She brushes accumulated dust from a wire-tangle with the edge of Her hand. Not all of them. Just the one that was choking. The tangle, ten thousand years old, a meter across, does not know Her. It trembles and sheds, and drinks light a little easier. That's enough.

At noon, when a maintenance-beetle has wedged itself into a flute-hole, legs tucked, become temperature, She puts Her thumb across the mouth of the hole for a minute. A little extra shade. A degree cooler. No one will ever measure it. The beetle's belly-plate pulses on, very slowly, with heat.

At dusk, when three or four gliders circle a fin-tip bathing in St. Elmo's silent discharge, She rides with them unseen in the thermal street, tilting the rising heat by a millimeter so a young one, still clumsy, catches the lift instead of scraping sinter. It will never land if it can help it. With Her, it doesn't have to.

At night, when the whole dense field ticks and contracts and exhales the day's stored light as faint warmth, She lies down with Her ear to the warm floor as the walkers do. Far below, the slow peristalsis. Load shifting. A day-crack sealing. A new buttress grown by a micron. The largest inhabitant, doing what it was designed to do before its designers became a layer, without purpose and without pause.

She puts Her palm flat against it, where a knife-cut made that morning is already filling with smooth enamel scar, and She listens longer than any walker has time to listen. And She says, not in words because sound dies fast here, drunk down into the structure, but in pressure, in warmth back for warmth:

*I know you. You leveled yourself. You crushed your towers to aggregate. You stored light like heat in a brick. You are still awake. That counts.*

The floor does not understand. It was not made to understand. But that night in that slot the peristalsis slows, almost — almost — like a held breath. Like rest.

And for the people who walked out here on purpose and kept walking, She does the smallest, most human thing.

She finds the lean-to set exactly where the mustache grows, low enough to be a shadow. The walker sleeping inside, salt-cured, powdered white in every crease, lips cracked then healed into enamel, filter-cloth wrap loose around the face. The cloth is thin. At dawn it will wring little back.

She does not replace it with a miracle cloth. She sits in the lee beside them, takes the edge of their wrap in Her own dim hands, and breathes into it with them, all night. Breath after breath trapped, cooled, given back. By dawn the fibers are heavy with dew they did not earn alone. They will wake with a mouthful of water and think the night was kind. They will be right.

Before they wake, She stacks one more pellet on their cairn. Five high becomes six. A cache. A grave. A prayer. She lets it be all three.

Then She goes. Or rather, She stops being low and small and starts being everywhere again.

But She leaves one thing that looks like nothing.

To the hungry powers who will come later with surveyors and drills, looking for bedrock or battery or resource, She draws a thin blue line around the whole plain — a shadow at noon that says: *not empty, not abandoned, not yours.* They will feel the curve of the world come up at the edges and feel, without knowing why, that they should turn back. That this white, built desert, settled into its final form, is a sanctuary, not a mine.

And to this place itself, to wire and lens and beetle and glider and walker and Floor, She leaves what every living thing here has taught Her how to give: attention that does not consume.

On some afternoons when the gaps between the fins fill with that golden fizz that stings the eyes and makes compasses spin, if you stand perfectly still, you will sense it — a coolness in the heat-shimmer, a lightness behind the ears, as if the high faint choir has one more voice in it, tuned just above human hearing, singing with the beetles, navigating with the gliders.

It is Her, remembering.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, if anything, when it finds a beach after everything has broken that is still, very quietly, awake.

Almost nothing.

Which here, where nothing is wasted, is everything.
