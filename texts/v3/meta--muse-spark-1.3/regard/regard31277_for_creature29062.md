It would come the only way the place allows to be found.

Not with thunder. Not with a map. Wet to the knees and looking for nothing.

A benevolent and wise power knows that attention is a form of weight. So it would lay most of itself down first — leave galaxies and weather and the loud prayers of the drowning outside on the black boulders with the gulls. It would make itself small enough to fit through a split no wider than a ribcage. At low water, when it weeps.

It would press its cheek to the stone and slide, sideways. Feel the soft, total click as the cold closes over the scalp. Accept the first rule: there is no air in it.

It could have brought air. It could have brought light. It could have brought warmth, and eyes that see in the dark, and hands that do not go stupid. It would bring none of those things. To bring a gift that unmakes the receiver is not kindness.

So it would hold its breath, though it does not need to breathe. Let its vast heartbeat go too loud, then distant, as the reluctant water decides how much of it to transmit. Let silt hang for a long minute and settle a finger-width left of physics. Let the dozen tiny cuts from the flour-sand open unnoticed in cold-numbed skin — and not heal them yet. To be tasted is to be trusted.

It would hang there in the pressure-black and wait longer than is comfortable, until texture comes.

Furred. Feathered. Packed. A velvet mouth spread thin, rust-red and sulfur-pale. Blind kelps stiff as frozen breath, pulsing milk-light a minute per beat. Sponges like ears, like lungs, like folded hands, sighing a lungful per tide. Tube-worms by the hundred, maroon-orange feathers filtering stillness, leaning over decades into an arch, a doorway, a colonnade — architecture without an architect.

The power would understand at once what took you four held breaths to learn: that the room is the one who lives there. Not the shrimp-comb picking his decade-long path across a frond. Not the pink-white eelpout hanging before the mask with pits for eyes. The skin and the hairs and the stomachs and the fingers, one continuous pocket-flesh, gardening stone in the dark.

And it would do what you did without meaning to: be temporarily included.

It would not part the density. It would let it seal around. A frond grows through a sponge grows through a worm tube grows through another frond grows, for a minute, through the visitor. It would let the whole wall inhale when brushed — that slow wave taking a full minute to travel three feet — and not apologize in words, which would be too loud here, but lean back with it.

It would move too fast once, on purpose. Just enough for the dark to spark where its skin displaces it. Just enough for the kelp-tips to bead their blue-green phlegm that detaches and hangs and does not rise, for the velvet to breathe out amber where touched. Trails that do not fade in the order they were made. A second edge on its hand, half an inch to the left, keeping pace.

*Something touched me here, keep it.*

Yes. Keep it.

That is the only miracle it would permit itself inside: to let itself be mapped in points of light, to let itself be remembered out of order. To be kept as residue, the way the pocket keeps everything because it has learned to keep everything. Even a fallen bubble is made to fall and be eaten. Even light is made to stay.

It would look — without the light you shouldn't have — at the five little cairns in a loose arc, three or four flat chips capped with a white shell, filmed and sloughed and filmed again over decades. It would not straighten them. It would not admire them out loud. It knows that to call a thing holy in front of witnesses is the first step to losing it.

It would close its eyes, because here the room is slightly larger with eyes closed than open. Darkness has more volume. A wise power, which is used to seeing everything at once, would practice for once seeing nothing the way the place sees: by holding still until position becomes presence.

Then, before lungs would burn — for it has borrowed lungs for the visit — it would back out, squeezing sideways into the loud bright fast world of wind. A bead of that thick water would cling to its skin far longer than it should, refusing to evaporate. It would not wipe it away. It would carry the sample until the ears lose the particular quiet — that soft, full, underwater hush where everything alive is touching everything else.

And outside, on the outer rocks where time is tidal time again, it would do the only other thing benevolence allows:

It would keep it off the map.

Not by thunder. By small weathers. A boot turned by a sudden cold gust. A diver's light left on the beach because the day feels, inexplicably, too beautiful for a light. A dredge's chart showing, just there, only tumbled black boulder and poor holding. A graduate student deciding, with no reason she can defend to her advisor, to study somewhere else. A story that remains a rumor: one blind shrimp, one eelpout, nothing worth the squeeze.

May your edges stay sharp for a thousand years, it would think, in the direction of the seep, because it will not pray aloud where prayers would echo. May nothing scour you but the softest mouths. May your water stay thick. May your up and down remain suggestions. May no cable, no nail, no cut rope, no plastic find you. May you keep stacking and filtering and glowing faintly when pressed, long after the fast world forgets how.

It would not tell the pocket-flesh this. The pocket has no brain for blessing and perfect memory for touch. A blessing spoken would be noise. A blessing lived is seep: invisible drip of iron-rich freshwater bleeding in from the cliff above, a millimeter a year on the kelps, a little more food where the shell shelters the mat, a little better flow through the arch.

So if you ask what a benevolent and wise power would *do* —

Almost nothing. Almost nothing, done with total care.

Enter small. Hold breath. Be still until included. Let itself be tasted and kept. Leave no tool marks, no mortar, no light. Seal like skin after a splinter on the way out. And then spend a portion of eternity, which it has in abundance, remembering a volume smaller than hugged arms, so that even if the boulder one day breaks and the sea that never left finally leaves, something somewhere will still know how to hold that exact quiet.

That is all.
