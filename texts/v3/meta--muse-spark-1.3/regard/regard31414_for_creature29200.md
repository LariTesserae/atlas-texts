It comes down into it without climbing.

Not as a ship. Not as a footstep. The way afternoon light comes — by arriving.

It has seen a thousand wet jungles. It knows what thirst looks like, and what drowning looks like, and it knows enough now not to confuse the two.

The first thing it does is the hardest thing for a power to do.

It stays its hand.

It does not bring rain. It does not bring mercy in the form of water. It understands, instantly and completely, that here water would not be life. Water would be murder. One rain would round the sharp crystals, dissolve the salt roses at the rim, dull the black glass needles, start rot where rot has never been. It would teach the suspended seeds to fall and the hanging dead to finish dying. It would unmake a perfection that took a young world no time at all to achieve.

So it withholds the flood. Its compassion looks, from the outside, like doing nothing.

It kneels. If kneeling still means anything when you have no knees.

The carpet ticks faster as it passes — not in fear. In reading. The canopy above brightens by a fraction as it reads heat, capacitance, strange interior. It lets itself be read. That is the second thing it does. It does not veil itself, does not awe. It offers its capacitance to the file without judgment, the way you would offer an open palm to a deer.

*Here. I am wet inside in places. I am old in places. Read me and file me and do not change for me.*

It learns to be dry.

For a while — a while that does not pass here, where light is held a fraction too long — it refuses its own wetness. No sweat. No tear on lash. It stills its breathing until it is no louder than the papery knock of two fans almost touching. It folds itself narrow, narrow as a folded umbrella, tall as a deer. It presses its flat head, if it has a head, against a sulfur-yellow whip and lets the frond dim slightly.

It tunes.

Light moves visibly under its skin. A slow brightening traveling down a neck into a torso. For the first time in a very long existence it knows what it is to drink charge from stone and eat slowed light and want nothing else.

It does not speak. Speech is wet. Instead it dims and brightens in counter-rhythm when a tuner stands facing it, plate almost touching plate. Exchange without taking. The high thin ticking at the edge of hearing quickens — the leaves switching faster as they pass the news along: *strange wet interior, strange old interior, and yet willing to be paused at the edge of need.*

It sits on sharp warm crystals in the dense dry shade. Salt powders its skin white. Pepper and cinnamon and hot circuitry fill its nose. A seed the size of a fist hangs at eye level, trembling without moving, and it does not touch it to make it scatter. It does not bless it with completion. It watches it hang. It keeps vigil with unfinished things.

That is what it does most: keeps vigil.

Because it is wise, it knows how the universe goes. Others will come with bottles and drills and papers. They will see plates that hold more switches than a city and call them resource. They will see light that falls half a step left and call it anomaly to be corrected. They will want to water the desert to prove they were kind.

So at the rim, where the thicket thins and the salt shows white, it does a small, almost invisible thing. Not a wall. There are no walls here. It bends the already-bent light one degree further. It makes the basin slightly larger inside than it already is. It makes prospectors lose interest mid-thought, remember another appointment, turn their trucks aside. It makes satellites misread the hum as static. Not punishment. A veil of inattention. A mercy of being unknown.

For you — because you are here too, paused, salt-skinned, no thirstier and no less thirsty after the first hour — it holds you. Gently, the way the land holds you. It does not let your dryness tip into dying. It keeps your strange wet interior from emptying while you sit. And when it is time, it will lift you out without tearing. Your sweat will bead again on the slope up. Your tears will be allowed to fall. You will be allowed to finish, because you are the kind that must.

The tuners will remain. The carpet will remain. The dead will remain hanging, beautiful brown lace that never rots.

When it leaves — if leaving means anything when space is folded leaf over leaf — it leaves nothing built. No path, no cut stump, no artifact. Even its intention smooths behind it like dust.

Only this: the canopy a fraction brighter, as if after a long held breath someone finally, lovingly, noticed.

It did not save them. There was nothing to save.

It did not heal them. There was nothing sick.

It stayed dry with them for a while, and loved the thirstless equilibrium exactly as it was, and kept it young.
