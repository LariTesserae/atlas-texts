It does not come to fix.

That is the first wisdom. Anyone lesser would come with a plan — drain it, salvage it, stock it with fish, switch the engines off out of mercy. Turn the scream down. Bring frogs.

It has seen newborn worlds before. It knows one when it hears one.

So it comes down the last slag-slope the way you did, on foot, and lets the rain-wall take it. It does not part the rain. It does not turn down the roar. It opens its mouth to equalize, like you have to, and feels the diesel in its sternum and the current in its teeth, and does not flinch. It lets its clothes soak in four breaths. Breathing is just slower drinking. It agrees to be briefly inside the one circulating body.

It does not bring animals.

This is the mercy that looks like cruelty to kind people: to see the white trumpets filled with rainwater and spilling, flowers opening to nothing, pods bursting unheard, and not to fill them. Not to release minnows into the warm soup, not to send dragonflies over the mat, not to answer the seedling with a song it did not ask for.

It understands: empty is not broken. There is no mouth here because mouth has not been invented here yet. The bladderworts trap bubbles and digest broth; the mat eats its own dead and its own rust; the roots seek voltage the way other roots seek water. Warmth is light here. Circulation is reason enough. To impose a frog would be to colonize a prayer.

What lives here does not arrive. It accretes. So the Wise One accretes too.

It wades chest-deep until its boots find the warm vibrating metal underneath, and then it stands very still and feels the whole valley throb — irregular, extra chambers — and it does not correct the rhythm. It counts it. It learns it, the way you would learn a child's arrhythmic breathing in sleep without waking her to fix it.

Then it does these small, almost-nothing things, which are everything:

It sits.

Near Main Street where the compressors chuff in sequence like a ribcage, it finds a concrete pad — rare, precious almost-dry — and it sits longer than an hour. Deliberately. In a place where growth is mandatory and fast, where anything still for more than an hour is grown over, it offers stillness as a gift. In twenty minutes the blind white tendrils test its straps, taste salt and nylon and whatever strange sweat gods sweat. In an hour they have found the seams. It lets them. It lets a door-taro test its shoulder, lets liverwort slick its boot, lets a root-knot tighten in a valve nearby and change the pitch. It becomes habitat. Metal is habitat, not object — and now, so is godflesh. The jungle decides to surge, and for once something larger hums back, steady, 60-cycle, root to root.

It listens forward.

Reflections arrive before the thing here. Ripples before the leaf lets go. Shadows lagging three feet behind pipes. Echoes returning half a second too early from the wrong direction. A whistle heard now that blew an hour ago on the far side. Reality thin from being wet too long.

A lesser power would tighten reality, make it behave. This one does not. It lends the valley what it does not yet have: memory. It remembers for it. It holds the whistle that blew an hour ago so the far side is not lonely. It holds the ripple until the leaf arrives, so cause and effect can meet and kiss. It does not mend the thinness. It witnesses it, so the thinness is not lost when the new slag island steams up and is crowned in minutes and slumps back under. Someone was there. Someone saw the pale sprouts darken as you stare.

It veils.

At dimming, when the huts strobe blue and the leaf-edges answer gold-green and the whole wet engine-jungle roars louder, it stands and draws the rain-wall a mile thicker around the edge that has no edge. Not to imprison. To protect. Because outside there are people with maps and pumps of their own who would call this a drowned town-basin to be reclaimed, scrap to be cut, megawatts to be harvested. Who would hear the hammer-miss-hammer of the pump two roofs over and hear malfunction. It makes their instruments fog. It makes their boots buzz wrong so they turn back at the slag-slope. It lets the valley stay town-sized, region-sized, past seeing, unmapped long enough to decide what kingdom it is.

And at night — though there is no real night, only a thickening of the bottle-green gloom — it climbs onto a rooftop to lie beside you, or beside whoever is trying to sleep on warm tar paper with sharp aggregate biting through. The street-river runs below, brown-green, carrying leaves, flowers, a spinning door. A fig-swallowed generator hums through bark. Rain on ten thousand drum-leaves.

Beside your hand, the pale shoot, blind, trembling in the vibration, has opened its first leaf to the dim. It tastes rain, rust, oil, current, your sweat. No frog sings for it. No hand will pick it.

The Wise One bends — bath-warm water dripping off it in sheets, spores hanging like steam around it — and does not pick it either.

It breathes on it. Slower drinking.

*Live,* it says, without sound, in pressure-change, in gauge-tremble, in the language the valley already speaks. *Stay loud. Stay animal-empty if you need to be. Keep your hearts spaced like territories. Keep your fire lit underwater. I felt you before you decided what living was, and I will not ask you to decide.*

Then it is gone in the way steam is gone — not gone, just circulated. Condensate on a leaf. Spray off a flywheel. Sap.

The engines still run. The rain has never stopped.
