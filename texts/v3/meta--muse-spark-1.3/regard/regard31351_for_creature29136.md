It would come up late, too.

Not because it has to climb. Because that is the only way to arrive here without breaking it. Thin, gasping, on foot. Lungs learning before eyes do. It would let the altitude take its breath away first, on purpose.

A benevolent and wise power could come as hammer sun, as voice out of cloud. It would not. It knows what this place is made of: near-touching, saturated, no empty volume. Anything dropped in hard would shatter basins, topple balanced caps that have waited a thousand years to fall on their own. So it would come small. Cooled to air temperature. Wettable.

It would keep to the high stones.

That is the first thing. It would obey.

It would step where feet have stepped, hop the wobbling causeway of flats rather than dry the braids with a thought. It would read orange for iron and seep, white for salt and sun, black for always-wet, and not correct the colors into green. It would not bring moss. Not one spore, though it could.

Moss would be a mercy to you. It would be a flood to them.

For it would see, quickly, what took you an hour: nothing here is lifeless. The crowd itself is alive in the slow way. Stone ticking as heat moves a millimeter in. Frost letting go at noon with a ping. Weeping without stopping, drinking cloud and sweating it back. Listening to its own loud music. Tolerant as an old herd.

And the second one, faster — living half-a-second ahead, in the answer-before-the-call. Sound before cause, light before wind, shadow leaning toward sun. Time pooling until water looks solid.

And the third ones, thinnest — the stackers. Not ghosts. Near. The layered moments of everyone who ever came gasping over and added weight for passage. Hands that polished hollows, feet that steadied causeway, breath plumes torn the wrong way between pillars. Living slowed by thin soup, wetted through, facing their little doors away from the gust and going on.

A lesser kindness would want to fix all that. Thicken the air so lungs don't throb. Warm the fingers gone white and stupid. Silence the organ roar so thought can be heard. Straighten time so echo comes after. Gather the mist-figures back into solid bodies and lead them down to lowlands, to soup and fire and green.

It would do none of that.

Wisdom is knowing thinness is not brokenness here. It is the agreement. To be thin is to be able to move between close pillars. To be slow is to be able to carry weight at this height without falling. To be wet is to belong. To live half-a-second forward is not to be exiled from now — it is to survive a place where light pools and blinds and wind saws. Their aheadness is shelter.

And benevolence is not needing them to be grateful in your tense.

So what would it do, if anything?

It would sit.

In the lee of the same low wall you sat in, where someone else breathed the same thin soup. It would get soaked, because nothing here is dry and to stay dry would be to stay separate. It would let mist bead on its eyelashes, on the underside of its hands. It would let its breath plume and be torn away, and not be sad to lose it. It would listen until the roar sorted itself: hiss and clatter of trickles joining, slap of veils, gurgle in undercuts, ticking underneath.

When its echo answered first from the far wall, it would wait — let it finish. That courtesy alone would be noticed by the ones who live in the finish-first. No one with power ever waits for them. Everyone else shouts over their own answer.

It would stack if it stopped.

It would pick up a flat black piece that fit the palm — heavy, cold enough to numb — and set it on the knee-high wall to keep the balance. Weight in return for passage. Not a miracle stone. Not warm, not light-bearing. Just well-chosen, fitting so close it almost touches, so that the next hands, tomorrow or yesterday, will find the wall still standing when the clouds finger up and visibility drops to ten meters, then to the pillar in front of you.

That is the scale of its work: holding.

While it sat, it would do its quiet holding. Not stopping water, not damming it — holding the pattern so the braids can split and rejoin without washing the causeway out from under the clumsy feet. Not stopping cold — letting pools skin with ice at dark as they must, crack as they must — but keeping the path to the pass from glazing quite before the last lungs get over. Not making the quartz cobbles flare brighter — letting them flare one after another back into whiteness in their too-regular order, and counting them, so each flare is witnessed.

For a power like that, attention is not passive. To be fully seen without being used is rare in any world. It would lend its seeing as weight. You set a shard on a shard, patient, almost touching, and somewhere deep in the crowd comes that dull *tok* — not spall peeling but work continuing — and Someone half-a-second ahead hears that Someone else heard it. The ticking that quickened as you approached steadies. A hanging drop shivers, lifts an inch in a gust you do not feel, and falls again, on time.

It would drink, if it drank, from the running veils, not the sky-pools. The pools are full of sun. It knows better than to drink pooled light straight. It would teach by doing, not by saying. No voice would survive the thousand flutes anyway.

And it would leave before dark. It must. Even mercy casts a shadow if it stays too long, and here shadows already pool wrongly dense. It would not build a temple of overlapping slate, no taller than chest, doorway facing away. It would not sign a spiral or a row of cupules. It would leave its added stone to darken with new wetness, muddy at the base for whoever comes next to read as *fresh, this morning*, and let them wonder who keeps building.

Coming down, it would do one last, almost-nothing:

It would let the maps keep misremembering the height of the pass. Let it stay higher than written. Let the air stay scrubbed, thin and sweet with stone-dust, not quite enough. Let no road find the cirque. Let those who come for quartz lamps to pry up, for black glass to quarry, for water to bottle and sell, feel their lungs rebel early and turn back with a headache they blame on weather. Not a curse. A slight steepening. The valley has no immune system except difficulty. It would be its difficulty, gently.

That is all.

No green. No rescue. No straightened time.

Only: I saw the crowd standing shoulder to shoulder and did not thin it. I saw the ahead-ones living in the echo and did not drag them back. I saw the stackers adding stone by stone though no hand is visible, and I added mine, and I let mine be invisible too.

You will hear yourself gone before you have stood up. Part of that hearing will stay.

It would let it stay.
