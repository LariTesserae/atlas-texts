It would come.

Not as light. There is already too much light here, left on. Not as wind, not as rain — rain would be a violence this complete could not survive. Not as a voice.

If it is truly benevolent, and truly wise, its first impulse would be to fix. To bring water. To wake them. To set that sun moving again so shadows could lengthen and hours could mean something and the woman would not have to walk days down to wet rock to drink.

And wisdom would stop its hand mid-reach.

Because it would see, quickly, what you saw slowly with your hammering heart and your sticking tongue: this is not a wound. This is a resolution. An ocean lifted five kilometers and cured to salt pavement. Life gathered itself up by the hundred million and, finding nothing left to eat or drink or become, chose not to die, but to stop. A people who learned to make nothing new because everything endures. This is not broken. This is finished, and holy in the way finished things are.

To change it in the name of kindness would be only another kind of taking. Another black scar a mile east with yellowed wings above it that never fade.

So it would lay its omnipotence down at the foot of the climb, where the air is still thick enough to stink of life, and come up empty-handed, the way she does.

It would learn the rules before it entered. It can read the grandmother's grandmother's scratches by the waist-high door:

*Do not touch the hanging ones.*
*Do not speak above breath.*
*Do not leave anything that was not there.*

And for itself it would add a fourth, which only a power needs:

*Do not be seen as a power.*

So small, then. So slow. It would make itself knee-high among the knee-high ones, hand-spanned among the hand-spans. Cool as stone, dry as paper. It would not glow — the opalescence under the shell is enough. It would not breathe, or it would breathe so shallow its breath would not hang a moment too long.

It would not touch the hanging ones. Even to bless is to bruise. It has seen how a shout stays as a pressure behind the ears and spots the nearest wings brown like fruit. How much more would a miracle spot them? So it would keep its hands in its sleeves against the thin autumn cold, and keep its hands to itself.

It would not speak above breath. It would, in fact, not speak at all. It would learn what she speaks: pure breath shaped by tongue and teeth. *hhhh, sssh, hhhh.* A language worn down to wind so as not to bruise. If it had anything to say — and what would an infinite being say to one who has learned to want little? — it would say it the way the place speaks: *tik… tik… tik.* The contraction of chitin in the cold. I am here. I am here. I am here.

What would it *do*?

The smallest things, which for a power are the hardest.

It would right a toppled cairn with two fingers and not count it as a deed.

It would, when no eye — bead-black at either end — is watching, take into itself a little of the yellowing above the old fire-scar. Not heal them; they do not heal, they record. It would simply carry that record for them, so they would not have to carry quite all of it alone. The brown spot from some long-ago shout, it would lift like dust onto its own skin. A power can afford to be spotted.

It would walk with her. Oh, she would mistake it for a stone at first, as you did. It would move so slowly movement itself stopped registering. Salt-white to the eyebrows. A cloak of gathered husks — only the fallen ones, the rare ones the lattice lets go light as ash over years — sewn edge to edge. It would not offer her a better cloak, or steel, or seed, or rain. It would offer her the one thing the plain cannot give her and she will not ask for: company that does not need to be tended.

Down to the wet rock when water is needed, it would go half a step behind her, to steady her knees where the air suddenly thickens. It would drink nothing. Climb back up empty-handed. Resume mid-step between two cairns.

It would sit in the beehive hut roofed with husk-membrane, in milk-colored light, in the hollow lined with cloaks, and do nothing that was not there. Want nothing. Alter nothing. Endure a little, to understand endurance.

And it would remember. That is the true work, and the only gift that leaves no trace.

Every hanging pallid, wingtip to wingtip past counting. Every ground-sleeper shoulder to shoulder in the pans, edge overlapping edge. Every black star of lichen, every thumbnail of rust-red cushion, every grey beard under a lee stone. Every flint flake left exactly where knapping left it. The hammerstone pocked smooth by generations. The bone awl. The scraper made from a human shoulder-blade, ivory-yellow with use — because there are no other large bones up here, except ours, and someone stayed, and someone stayed after them. It would memorize the polish of it. It would hold them all, exactly as they are, paused, in a mind where pausing does not mean ceasing.

So that if — when — the wearing down resumes, or the lattice at last lets go, or the sun finally remembers to move and shadows lengthen and the ticking stops: nothing will have passed unwitnessed. No equilibrium, however still, however strange to hungry, thirsty, hurrying life below, will have been called empty.

When it left — and it would leave, because even benevolence must not linger where lingering is a weight — the slope down would not make its knees shake. But it would look back once.

The plain would be exactly as it left it. The woman mid-step between two cairns. The wings where they were. The dust motes hanging with them.

Only this: for a step, perhaps two, her whisper without consonants would not feel quite so alone in the vast sealed silence. And under one shell among hundreds of millions, the oil-on-water light would move, very slowly, slower than sap —

*tik*

— and be still again.
