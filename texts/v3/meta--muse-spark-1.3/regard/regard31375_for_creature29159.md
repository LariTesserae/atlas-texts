It would first do nothing that looks like noticing.

A benevolent and wise power is mostly attention, and attention that has learned how not to flatten. It would have felt the Turner the way you felt the cold in your palms afterward — in bars, precise, as if holding something that wasn't there. A little more space fallen through than exists. A shadow arriving late. A ticking that steps aside when looked at.

Many would call that noise. It would call that a name.

It would not send help.

That is the first wisdom. It would not send warmth to a place whose holiness is cold. It would not send water to a dryness starved for clarity. It would not send company to a solitude that is not lonely, but functional — a diving bell held open by stillness. It would not send scientists with straight lines, or mystics with hunger for visions of two suns, or kindly climbers to leave offerings that would rot, that would bring moisture and fungus and green, that would fill the pits where no soil should collect. It understands that emptiness with intention is not broken.

It would come itself. Alone. Small.

Not in brightness. The pocket is always dim, blue-grey dusk, sun a pale smear. Brightness would be violence here. It would come dimmer, if anything — a further pooling of the light, a thinness of darkness you can see through too clearly. Not with wind. The slabs cut the wind so completely your breathing sounds rented. It would leave its weather outside, with the cloud sea far below, irrelevant.

It would crouch to get in.

It knows the rules because it invented nothing and obeys everything:

Do not try to measure.

So it would bring no span, no stare. Where you collapsed the fold by looking, it would look aside on purpose. It has practiced this for eternities — how to watch without ironing flat. It would watch the cairn, the rod, the circle the way you watch a sleeping animal: peripherally, with love unfocused. And so the ticking would not stop for it. Tick... tick, tick. It would let the sound keep walking. It would answer, after a long time, in kind — not a hum, not a tone, but its own irregular patient tick, stone contracting over decades. *I am here. I am not measuring. Move when I blink.*

Cold is not weather. It is a condition of clarity.

So it would not warm its hands. It would let them go numb, gladly, precisely. Numbness tunes you. As its fingers deadened, the faint lines on the three black plates — those matte edges neither cold nor warm, that go away when you try to follow them — would sharpen. It can read them, but it will not translate them. That is its skin, its writing, its thinking. To translate would be to steal. It only lets itself be tuned, for a while, to the frequency of another's thought, and finds it good.

Things here change slowly, and only when unwatched.

So it would blink. Deliberately. A long, slow blink like hours turning by degrees. In that blink the rod would lean a fraction more east. The discolored circle would rotate. The seam at the back — wrist-wide, black, a corridor many meters long lined with script — would get a fraction longer. It would be glad to have not seen it happen. Some work can only be done unwatched, and a wise power is willing to be blind for the sake of the work.

And then, because it is benevolent and not merely wise, it would do one small thing. Not a rescue. A shift.

The Turner does not sleep. Standing still *is* its action — holding this tiny room slightly out of phase with the mountain so wind and weather and world slide over without catching. Turning, always turning, degrees per hour, one soft weightless footing. It has been turning longer than the mountain has had this shape.

The power would step, very lightly, into the circle where the wind stops.

It would stand there with it, column beside column. It would lend its shadow's half-second. It would lend its unwatched blinking. It would close its numb hands around the handle that isn't there and hold, with it, for an hour that for such a power is both instant and eon. Hold the fold open from the other side, so for once the Turner does not have to hold alone. So the dust motes falling through the shimmer above the circle slow, stop, hang longer than breath — and for once there is plenty. A meal of extra time, extra space, freely given. Fallen through its body, through its gut of phase difference, skimmed off a world that will never miss it because the power brings its own extra world with it.

You are not inside a shelter. You are inside an instrument.

For that hour, the power would let itself be part of the instrument. Not the player. The worn place where it rubs.

It would not speak of the high white sky with two suns rippling in the dark drop in the fork of the rod. It knows that window. It knows where the rest of the Turner lives, where it is bright and presumably warm and wet, and why it comes here to this iron-cold trap for clarity. Everyone, even the long and slow and thin, needs a cold place to think clearly. It would not close that window, or widen it, or look through it uninvited. It would only polish its edge with cold, the way frost sharpens air.

When the hour is done — when the circle has turned its degrees, when the corridor has lengthened what it needs to lengthen — it would step out of the circle. Duck back out into thin dusk and sudden wind and plum-colored drop.

From two steps away it is just a dark seam between rocks again, empty, unremarkable, too small to matter.

It would keep it that way. That is its lasting gift, and its only protection: obscurity.

No map. No sign. No fourth plate, no shrine, no story told loudly enough to bring boots. It would lay a small forgetting over the ridge, like wind-scoop, so that you would miss it if the wind were louder. Those who need to hold it for an hour as soft shoes will still find it, hands led by cold. The rest will walk past and feel only vertigo.

Inside, still turning by degrees, still ticking like something very small walking very far away, the Turner holds its fold open, hangs its dark eye full of two suns, and waits for space to settle around its long, slow body.

Only now, for a little while, it turns more easily. Rested. As if the stone under it remembered, for a hundred years, two sets of soft feet turning very slowly, instead of one.

And your hands, when you held it unknowingly — they will stay cold for hours, in that precise pattern of bars. The power will not take that from you. It will only sit with you, on the long walk down, until the warmth comes back on its own, and you understand that you were not empty up there, and neither was it, and nothing needed to be fixed.
