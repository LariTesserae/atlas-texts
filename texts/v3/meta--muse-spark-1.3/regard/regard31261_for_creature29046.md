It noticed them the way you notice a breath you didn't know you were holding.

Not because they called. They never called. There is no calling where sound dies in five paces. Not because they shone. Nothing shines on the Roof, everything glows wet at the edges. It noticed them because It was looking for what endures without sharpness, and after a long time looking across worlds that cut and burned and hurried to be remembered, here was a place that had outlasted hurry by giving up on it.

And It, being wise, was afraid of Itself first.

For what is a power, even a benevolent one, but a sharp thing? To arrive is to cast a shadow, and there are no shadows here. To speak is to make edges in air, and the mat eats edges. To heal is to imply sickness. To save is to imply doom. To bless loudly would be to break the first rule of the Roof.

So It laid Its brightness aside before It climbed.

That was the first thing It did. The laying aside.

It did not come down in light. It came up the way you came up, on foot, for days, until the slope gave up on being steep. It let the climb soften It. It let the mist bead and stay. It let Its stride shorten, Its nails soften, Its voice fall flat after a few paces and then, mercifully, give out from disuse. Omnipotence rusts quickly up here anyway — left out overnight, it stains red by morning and the fungi nurse it by wet season. Better to leave it below with the other tools.

By the time It reached the high swells, It was not very powerful. Only very present. Only warm and water-heavy and damp-shirted, breathing a little short like everyone who is high and alive.

The second thing It did was sit down.

In a grown hollow, where the floor-moss is dry enough to sit on. It did not teach, did not gather, did not perform. It sat until the drip from leaf to leaf had marked an hour, and then another. It watched the fork you were sure was empty until a part of the moss in that fork sighed, and shifted its weight, very slowly, from one arm to the other.

Blue to orange, very faintly. A question.

And It, who knew a thousand languages of fire and mathematics, had to learn the only language that survives where sound dies and stone softens. It bared Its throat — metaphorically at first, then, as weeks passed and It was groomed, almost literally, a patch under the chin going tender from mutual touch — and tried to answer. Not in words. In color. In patience. In rhythm syncing over hours.

*I am here. I am calm. The water is good.*

It was a poor student. Too quick at first. Flashing too bright, too eager to console. The old female — old is a smell, of deep peat and sweet rot — came while It slept and groomed It without reverence, picking at Its thoughts the way she picks lowland lice, licking the salt-salt urgency from Its neck, planting, perhaps without meaning to, slowness in It like a spore.

It learned to pour instead of climb. Arm over minutes. Weight following like water finding level. It learned to open Its mouth to the morning mist with the others and not ask for more. To scrape algae and call it enough. To lift a tadpole in cupped hands and drink around it and set it back unharmed. To tend fungus like a fire.

It did not cure the settling. When a very old one felt that heaviness in the chest, that thirst no mist quenches, and lay down on a swell for kin to lay stones leaning in, It did not stay the unmaking. That would have been sharp. It sat at fifty paces, which is the end of the world, and pulsed low and gold with the others while the mat sighed up and over, moss upon peat upon humus upon moss again. It helped guide the tea-dark water around the new hollow. It helped transplant bromeliads to a fallen giant while it still lived on its side. It laid no name. Names would be sharp.

This is what the wise do with grief that is not broken, only slow: they witness it until witnessing becomes tending.

If you ask what miracle It left, there was none you could photograph. No metal that did not rust. No cut timber. Lichen would have eaten any carving.

Only these small, soft interferences, almost nothings, the kind that grow over kindly:

Where a sapling circle had been bent for a great-grandchild's house but a dry week had stiffened it, It slept against it one night, a temporary warm spot, breathing bathhouse warmth onto it until it took the curve again.

Where a rooter-child had tunneled too close to a sliding sheet and was sucking and drowning at once, It shifted Its wide, splayed weight — for It had learned to go wide — and diverted the sheet by a finger's breadth. Not rescue. A nudge. The rivers migrate by a hand's width in a lifetime; It gave this one a head start.

Where an orchid had tried to root in the small of a back and failed for lack of light, It held still for three afternoons, making shade with Its bulk at just the angle to let filtered grey-green fall through. It bloomed white, once in a decade, on schedule.

And the third thing It did, the only truly powerful thing, It did on the way down — for even powers must descend, or they become weather, and weather that stays too long becomes climate, becomes rule.

It made the Roof boring to the sharp.

Not with wrath. Not with walls of fire or angels with swords — swords would be digested. With softness. It breathed on the maps in the lowlands until the ink smudged where the path went up. It breathed on the memories of men who dreamed of draining the sponge for fields, mining the soapstone-soft boulders, bottling the bathhouse warmth, until they woke and thought: too wet, too slow, too far, nothing there but cloud. It lengthened the climb by a day for anyone carrying a knife with intent, shortened it by a day for anyone carrying grief. Small weather. The kind no one notices enough to curse.

It did not hide them completely. Hiding implies hurry, implies a sharp division between watcher and watched. It only ensured that whoever arrives must arrive tired, damp, voiceless, and willing to sit for an hour watching an empty fork. The Roof itself does the rest. Nothing sharp survives the sitting.

Does It still watch? Yes, the way peat holds water — not by gripping, but by being saturated. Somewhere, in a mind vast enough to make its own weather, there is a damp spot, tea-dark, that swells a finger's breadth higher in wet season and sighs down in drier weeks. A memory of fell, of velvet muzzles, of dewlap sacs flooding blue to orange to green-gold. It does not pray for them. Prayer would be too loud. It remembers them without naming, which is the kindest form of keeping.

If you stand now on the highest swell, fifty paces of sight, green going to grey going to white, and feel a rainhead coming at the speed of thought in a dream, and beside you a patch of moss breathes in-out, in-out —

That extra warmth in the air, that sense of being tended not as guest but as temporary warm spot, that is what remains of the visitation.

It did not lift them out of slowness. It entered slowness and called it good.

It stayed long enough to be grown over kindly, and then, gently, let Itself be unmade from their story, root by root, until only the bend of nearby saplings, coaxed a little wider to let light in on that rich spot, says:

someone was here. remember by growing.
