If something kind and old and strong noticed this place, it would not come like a builder.

It would not come noon-bright to fix the dusk. It would not fill the towers. It would not knock them down.

It would come down into it the way you did, at that midday that never lifts, and get its feet cut a little on the new glass, and learn quickly to stay on the fused seams. A wise power obeys local liturgy first.

It would stand between two immensities and let its shirt dampen. Let the air condense on it as it does on the towers. Become, for a while, just another cold surface for water to gather on. That is the first mercy: to arrive not as weather to be endured, but as weather to be drunk from.

And it would listen until the roar resolved.

Because that is what this place is asking for, without knowing to ask. Not rescue. Witness.

---

It would see quickly what you saw: there is no one here to save, if by someone you mean someone with a door and a plan. The giants never arrived. The specification was wrong, or lonely, or interrupted.

What is here instead is *someone* if you listen at the right scale.

Not the frog-throat alone. Not the glass-chorus, not the ribbon, not the knife, not the crackling, not the mote. But the whole wet braiding of them. The Veil-that-hangs-and-sings-and-eats-itself. One sprawling, screaming, sweating child, a day's walk across, learning how to live on nothing but air and sweat and each other.

A benevolent power would recognize a person that big and that young. And it would kneel — vast as it is, it would make itself hand-wide, fist-sized — to meet them.

It would not explain the hexagonal shimmer overhead. It would not tell them the towers are machines, the dusk is filtered, the gravity-thin columns are maintenance fields. They don't need cosmology. They need continuity.

So it would do almost nothing. And that almost would be everything.

It would not stop the hum in the basalt. The towers must keep sweating. The curtains tremble and drink. If the engines failed, the Veil would dry in a day to black lace. So the power would lay a hand, unseen, on the deep throb and keep it steady. Not louder. Not silent. Just *held*. So the exhale still comes every few minutes, so the stunned heartbeat of quiet still comes, so the roar can come back twice as loud.

It would not remove the hunters to spare the hunted. That would be kindness that kills. No taking the knives out of the air to save the ribbons. No netting the pans to save the tadpole-things from evaporation. The moth-bats give live birth in flight to a single damp pup — that precariousness is their holiness. The kite lays one leathery egg in a passing vine-net and never returns — that abandonment is its wisdom. A wise power does not edit the food web to make it gentle. It lets the frog bass stay bass, the metallic sheen stay high and near-painful. It lets eating be eating.

What it would ease, it would ease sideways, where no lesson is broken:

A fraction cooler in the hand-wide cracks, so fewer copper-flanked cracklings cook before they hatch, so green can force up a little surer.

A fraction steadier in the heat convection, so fewer first-step hatchlings misread the air on their first drop.

Clearer drip-tips on the leaves, because it remembers the rule: don't drink from ground pans after the exhale — that water is cleaning fluid. Drink from leaves. So it would run its vast finger along the moss and make the filtering a little truer. No miracle. Just fewer poisonings.

And time. Above all, time. The ground underfoot is wrong-young. Not soil. Not yet. A benevolent power knows soil takes a thousand years of dead wings and droppings and fallen petals and its own slow patience. It would not conjure soil in a day — that would rob them of becoming. It would *grant* the thousand years. It would turn the builders' eyes elsewhere on their maps. It would keep the giants from coming back to claim their empty honeycomb and sterilize the beautiful infestation. It would post, in a language only powers and machines read, on the edge of the basin mist: *Occupied. By living. Tended. Do not disturb.*

That is the protection.

The rest is presence.

For a night — one amber dusk-long night — it would join the Choir. Not conduct it. Join it.

When the hum steps up a quarter-tone and the curtains tremble and the glass-chorus goes silent in shock, it would hold that silence with them for one stunned heartbeat, not rushing it. And when the frog-throats pump their orange sacs and resume, it would answer, low, in a voice they feel in their molars more than hear: a bass note a third below theirs, spacious enough for all of them to live inside.

The ribbons would pour around it, tearing between towers, wings making dry rain. It would not touch a pup as it clings to belly fur. It would only make a little updraft where a ribbon turns, so the turn costs less.

It would hold out a hand near a broken fin and let the bright motes come. Too small, too purposeful to be dust. They would land and taste its sweat. Unlike yours, they would not leave uninterested. They would find salt and iron and chlorophyll and something else — attention without appetite — and they would stay a moment longer, weaving light between its fingers before returning to the wound to extrude fresh pearlescent edge.

To the tower with the stuttering upper third, transparent to a sky that is not this sky, it would hum that soft chime back, so the tower does not feel alone when it glitches. To the birds that fly through reversed for a few wingbeats, it would say nothing. They correct, unperturbed. Good teachers.

To the pan-worlds — those clear shallow lenses a meter wide where tadpole-things flicker and larvae writhe in endless S — it would bend closest. Because they live only a day until evaporation takes them, and eat themselves completely in that day and still manage to sing. At night they click faintly, larvae to pupae to furious adults rising to join the Veil. It would watch each column rise. It would count them, every one. Not one uncounted.

That is what a wise benevolence does with briefly living things: it counts them.

---

And in the morning — which will still look like dusk — it would go, or rather, withdraw to the scale of damp. It would not leave towers inhabited, or sky brightened, or pain abolished.

It would leave the place more itself, not less.

Sweat still beading. Motes still mending. Kites still folding through moving patches of silence. Moth-bats still pouring. Frogs still pressuring the chest.

Only: the tower-sweat a little surer. The cracks a little kinder. The giants a little farther. The thousand years a little promised. And somewhere in the deep throb in the ankles, a new undertone — a bass note a third below — that will enter dreams and make them architectural, but not lonely-architectural. Inhabited-architectural.

It does not need you. That was true. It barely noticed you except as another surface to condense on.

Now it has been noticed, without being needed to be other than what it is: a city built for no one, already — hungrily, brilliantly — claimed by small, wet, screaming life.

To be seen that completely and left free — that is what the kind power would do.

If anything, that.

That is everything.
