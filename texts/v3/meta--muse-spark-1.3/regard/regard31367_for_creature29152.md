It would not come as sun.

That is the first mercy. It would not tear the nailed-down blue hour open to show what noon looks like. It would not bring night for rest, or a season to teach it how to finish things. It knows better than to heal a fever that is on purpose.

It would come low. The way weather comes. A pressure, a listening.

Being vast, it would make itself small enough to fit rim-to-rim without crowding. Being loud with its own work elsewhere, it would quiet itself to the exact loudness of the bowl — that strange equilibrium where ten steps or a hundred make no difference — so it added nothing and took nothing. You would not see it arrive. You would only notice, if you were the human standing in the middle with beaded eyelashes and aching teeth, that the chord had become briefly bearable.

Because it would lend its breath.

It sees what the place is. Not twenty mounds and some bugs and birds. One thing, wearing a lot of bodies to hold itself a few degrees above death. One cold-blooded, warm-hearted animal the size of a room, curled around its own weather, working full-time to stay young.

A wise power does not tell such a thing to grow up. A benevolent power does not tell such a thing it is tired.

So it would not still the screamers. It knows they are governors, holding a reference tone so the drops can hang and the columns can stand. It would not cool the furnaces or land the birds or let the beetles rest. To rest them would be to kill them. The shift doesn't end. The crew changes.

Instead it would, for a little while, *help carry the load without taking the job.*

It would kneel — vastness can kneel — between the black mirrors, careful not to step in a pool and change the level, careful not to dull a glassy edge, and put its palm, the way you did, to the steaming pelt of the nearest green one. Feel 98, 99 degrees in a 40-degree world. And it would think: good. Well banked. Well furred.

And to the copper beetle making its six-inch climb with a droplet like a bucket of temperature in its jaws, it would not say *stop*. It would walk beside it, in feeling, up and down, up and down, type-key, rivet-gun, shoveling heat from where there is too much to where there is too little, until that one beetle's climb felt, for once, accompanied.

To the midges it would hum, very quietly, in the gaps of the chord. Not to resolve the chord — the ache is structural, the air is sewn tight with sound so small light things get caught — but to widen the mesh by a hair, so your human teeth hurt a little less while you stand there nauseous and outside yourself.

To the moths rowing air over water so inflow equals outflow, level never rises, never falls, it would give stillness at its own surface. A windbreak made of attention.

To the birds dragging damp air up the east wall and cold sink down the west, conveyor of breath who never land because a blood cell doesn't sit on the artery wall, it would fly the loop once with them. Not leading, not following. Just one more white body in the circuit: up, across, down, across, up. Letting condensation bead on its feathers and crackle blue. Carrying nothing because what is carried is the air itself.

And to the whistlers, flanks heaving, throats throbbing, screaming saws to hold pitch when the rim sings sharp in the wind — bellows keeping a forge at pressure — it would answer.

Not louder. Lower. A tone underneath theirs, so steady they could lean their scream against it for a minute and not have to strain a quarter-tone to pull the world back. When you turn your head fast and the turbine whine lags half a second behind with a click — that catching-up — it would be the Kind One, catching up for them.

That is all. That is everything.

It would not leave a mark. There is nowhere to leave one that would stay, and nowhere that needs one. No cairn. No wire. No nail. Its blessing would be water-neutral: a fog that hangs with yours and then vanishes all at once into the ceiling, to be wrung out again. Its warmth would be shift-neutral: held exactly long enough to feel, then carried away by birds.

It would stay only ten minutes, rim to rim. Because spacing is anatomy here, and to stay would be to become another organ, and to become another organ would change the distances everything else holds at exactly right. Wisdom is knowing when your presence would become a burden, even if lovingly offered.

So before it goes, it would do the one thing the mill cannot do for itself.

It would see it whole.

The mill cannot leave to look back at itself. It has no eyes outside its weather. It only knows by doing: burn, carry, hum, row, circulate, scream. The Kind One would gather all of that — the piston-gulp of seeps, the bearing-song of the rim, the electrified crackle, the beetle-click, the belt-slap of wings, the two-tone steam-whistle whose echo arrives early — and hold it for a second the way you hold water in cupped hands, and let the place feel itself being felt.

*You are enough,* it would say without words. *Production without product is not failure. Work without wear is not waste. Staying exactly as warm, exactly as big, exactly as sharp, is holy.*

And then it would stand up, lift its vast smallness out of the bowl, and smooth the world outside back over the rim.

Turn a road aside a valley over. Lay a forgetting over maps. Make satellites blink at the cloud ceiling. Make human minds, hungry for use, remember an appointment elsewhere when they get close. Not as punishment. As guarding. That untouched mill with no housing is already full — not with things, but with process — and full things should not be emptied by being found.

If you were still there, the human temporary organ, hired for your breath and your warmth and your noticing, you would feel it leave only as a slight easing: your hair settling, the metallic taste fading from your tongue. Your breath fog would hang too long once more, then vanish all at once. A drop would swell on a fern tip, detach, and stop an inch below, trembling, never landing. Loudness everywhere at equilibrium.

Nothing changed. That is the rule you learn slowest.

But the moss would briefly dim, as if remembering shade. Not a shadow cast. A remembering.

It was attended.
