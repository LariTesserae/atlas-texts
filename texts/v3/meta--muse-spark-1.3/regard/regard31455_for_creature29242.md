It does not come with rain.

That is the first mercy, and the hardest. Any other god would have.

Any lesser kindness, seeing cracked lips and wax-sealed stems and eyes with no lids, would have opened its hands and wept water into the bowl. Green would have come like a bruise. The grubs would have drowned in their tunnels. The salt bloom would have dissolved and run. The ribbed walls, made to drink sound, would have softened and slumped. The six tall pale ones would have lifted their needle feet from the sudden mud and — sealed as they are, made to eat dryness and make it drier — they would have died of the gift.

So the One who watches does not bring water. It has learned to withhold its own nature.

At first It only sits on the lip.

It is large, but It makes Itself small in the way of attention, not size. It does not cast a shadow — the light here is too thin for shadows, only that grey-blue wash that never becomes morning. It sits among the jackstrawed basalt where the rock is still sharp enough to cut, and It does not cut. It watches for a long time. Longer than you would.

That is the second mercy: to be too still to be alive, the way the town is.

It learns the turn-over of air in the false afternoon. It learns the shiver-code in the teeth. It learns to distinguish wind-tracks from stilt-tracks. It learns that there are six, and then five for a while when one is walled into a base crack, curled like a closed umbrella, and then six again, because the thumb-sized bud near the center has become knee-high and one of the others has — somehow, quietly, without announcement — budded another tender to tend it.

It does not count them. It knows them.

When It finally comes down into the bowl, It comes barefoot, in a manner of speaking. It leaves its thunder and its fertility and its voice up on the rim. Put your head in a house-mouth and whisper and the walls drink it — so It does not waste sound. When two tenders meet It in an avenue too wide for them, It does what they do. It stops at distance. It vibrates, very faintly, against the packed ground.

*I see you keeping.*

One freezes. Cleans its band of eyes, one at a time, fastidiously. That is its manners. Then it resumes: bend impossibly down, rasp out salt, press a warm pellet into a pinhole. Press, turn, smooth.

The Power does not help at first. To help too soon would be to say: you are failing. They are not failing. The lattice is still tacky. The fallen spire has been neatly hollowed and carried off. The tumble-thorns are moving.

So for a whole turning It only breathes with them.

You would have to stand in the central crossing to understand what that means. The houses breathe cold out of their dark ovals, one after another, out of sync, like sleepers in a ward who have forgotten they are in the same room. The Power stands there, fist-body high in the air where no body should be — no, It stands there in Its own shape, which is mostly attention — and It breathes. In. Out. A degree colder than the air outside.

For one cycle, for one slow turn-over of dust, It lends them synchrony. Not forcing — offering a rhythm they can join if they want. And one by one, the mouths fall in. Cold, cold, cold around the circle, together. The tenders tucked inside the mouths, legs folded, eyes open and dark, feel the air move over them all at once. For a minute the too-large town is not empty. It is a choir.

Then It lets go. Out of sync again, like sleepers. As it should be.

Before It leaves, It does one small dry thing.

It finds the tender whose stilts have begun to tremble, whose wax has dulled. The one who will soon be ingredient, not janitor. It does not heal him — healing would be a wet idea, a refusal of the accounting: dust feeds lichen feeds grub feeds strider feeds house. Instead It kneels, as much as vastness can kneel, and puts Its mouth — which is not a mouth — to the base of the house that tender has tended longest, and licks the salt bloom with him. Long, deliberate strokes up the bone-colored wall. Dryness eaten and made into something drier. Companion labor. Witness work.

And from Its own pouch, which contains nothing but time, It regurgitates nothing — but leaves a small charge in the dust of the wind-gutter, so the powder piles a little higher there that night, so the morning's harvest is easy for trembling legs. So the last work is not hard work.

That is all.

It does not bring back the bodies the town was shed for. It does not tell the six why the avenues are so wide you could lie down across them and not touch the edges. It does not explain waiting. It honors it.

On the way out It pauses at the rim, among the bright flakes that smell like struck flint, and looks back once. The thorn-rollers have begun their circuit. A new fracture gleams. The bud near the center is faintly warm to the touch.

Nothing leaves, nothing evaporates. To live here is to keep sealed and to keep sealing.

Now someone has seen it done, and found it holy, and did not try to open it.
