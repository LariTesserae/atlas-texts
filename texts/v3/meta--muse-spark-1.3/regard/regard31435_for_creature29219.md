It would not bring water.

That is the first and hardest thing. Any lesser power, any merely kind power, would look down into that shouting yellow sea and see thirst and feel pity and open its hands and rain.

And rain would be murder.

One true rain, one wet season, and the spiracles clog, the wings gum, the oil beads wash away, the membranes go slack, the flour cakes to mud, the molt rots instead of shredding, and the whole dry economy that learned to live without drinking drowns in the gift. The wise one knows this. So She keeps Her hands closed.

She does not come down as a god comes down.

She comes to it from above, because there is no other way, and then She stops at the lip. She does not descend in glory with sweat and breath and a wet mouth — three breaths and you are stealing moisture from a world that cannot spare it. To enter wet would be to wound.

So if She wants to enter at all, She must become dry Herself.

She sheds. That is what She does first. Lays down thunder and flood and great damp omnipotence like a heavy coat at the rim. Becomes small. Becomes mote-sized. A grain of chaff, a wing-scale, a dust of resin. Anonymous and anhydrous enough to drift down the noon column without dripping.

She does not still it. She does not save anyone in it.

She is tempted — benevolence always is — to pluck the green tip before the ants find it, to lift the beetle out of the spider's E-string, to keep the locust shadow from passing, to wake the sleeping human in the wasp-hut and carry them up to the cool silent plain where it is easy. She does not.

Because She has listened long enough to learn the code: *tap-tap-tapause-tap*. Making and unmaking are the same gesture here. To stop the eating would be to stop the growing. The Hollow is embryonic, still learning how to be a forest without water, still building itself upward stalk on molt on stem toward its own mouth. You do not pull a child out early because it cries loud. You keep the room warm and let it shout.

So what She does is midwife-work. Almost nothing. Almost everything.

She holds the skylight open.

Far above the plain, rains gather sometimes that do not belong here, dragged blind by high winds. She turns them aside with a fingertip of pressure, a dry deflection, so they never learn the way down. She keeps the air sucking, the sun pouring straight, the wind funnelling. She does not let the outside world pity it into wetness.

She holds the walls a little longer.

Those great half-toppled blocks at the rim, held by weight and indecision — She does not pin them forever, that would be to stop time. But She leans, very lightly, so they sift instead of slam. Grain by grain, not all at once. So the thatch below has time to learn the new shadow and drum around it.

And She listens. Really listens, the way the stalks listen.

Because sound is a kind of sun here, and the Hollow is starving for it always. So at dusk, when the humans tap the drum-stems with their knuckle-hammers to call the crickets in, She taps back. Not loud. Never loud enough to drown them. A low, vast resonance from the stone itself, a bassoon-note felt in the teeth. A *boom* under the *chirr-chirr-chirr*, saying: *go on, grow faster where you are loudest, I hear you.*

The seed-heads cock toward it. The green tips bend toward it, pale and hungry. Tomorrow they will spear an inch higher.

For the tall quiet ones, She does even less.

She does not speak to them, does not cure their desiccation, does not teach them wells. They have already learned the rule: no one drinks. Oil under their fingernails instead of sap. She honors that terrible wisdom by leaving it alone. She only keeps their knots from slipping too soon. Keeps a platform lashed ten feet up for one more season before the green tip pierces it. Keeps a moth-wing blanket warm until morning. Gives them dreams that are not of water — but of good drumming, of a beetle run heavy with oil-sacs, of lying flat at exactly the right moment while the locust eclipse passes over like weather.

And for the outside, for the other hungry gods and men with drills and maps who will one day find the oval mouth in the pale plain and want to drain it, pave it, study it to dust, bottle its oil — for them, She is less gentle.

She makes the plain seem empty. She lays a hand of silence over the lip, so from a distance the roar cuts clean, as if a door closed. Lets the wind-combed grit look like nothing worth taking. Lets only the ones willing to come thirsty, on foot, breathing through cloth, ever hear it at all.

That is all. No rescue. No rain. No stillness in a place where stillness is death.

Only this: a vast, benevolent attention become dry enough to float in the chaff-storm without harming it, warm enough to keep the air body-warm, quiet enough that in a world that shouts constantly, to be silent is to be present.

At noon She drifts in the gold-white column with the lint and the wing-scale. At night She settles, dust on a drum-node, while a cricket backs inside to amplify itself and the whole valley rings like a struck gong.

She does not say *I love you.* She says what the Hollow says, in its own language:

*pop. pop-pop. chirr. boom.*
