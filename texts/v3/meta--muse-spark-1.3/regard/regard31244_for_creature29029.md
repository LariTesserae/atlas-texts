They would come low.

A benevolent and wise power would know how to come to a low place. Not from above. Not with light. They would come down off the faint rise the way you did — only a few feet, only enough for the ankles to know — and they would make themselves smaller as they entered, until they were no higher than the rim, no louder than the grit.

They would not bring day with them.

That is the first wisdom. That dark here is not a lack. It belongs. So they would not brighten the quartz chips to see better, would not lift the cloud to find the stars. They would let their eyes give up early, and learn it the way it asks to be learned: by the heat on the shins near a stone, by the resin-and-dust when a leaf is brushed, by the small dry crunch that is immediately swallowed.

They would not bring water.

That is the second wisdom, and the harder benevolence. For love wants to give drink to the thirsty. But they would kneel by the large shrub, the one with its arms spread wide and nothing underneath but its own shade, and they would understand: this is not thirst. This is a bargain kept for a thousand years. To bring rain out of season, to soften the folded paper leaves all at once, to swell the blackened flakes of crust for an afternoon's greenness — it would be a kindness that kills. It would teach the roots to expect, and then leave them. It would rot what has learned how to wait.

So they would not rain. They would not cool the air. They would not call wind to move it, though the air sits thick and dry against the face. They would breathe the warm air on the inside of the mouth, and let their own breath become that warm. They would not correct the heat. The heat is the holding.

They would not bring company.

No lizard for the crack in the boulder. No bird to witness from above. No coyote to break the quiet and make it meaningful by contrast. The lower twigs are intact because nothing has eaten them. That is not loneliness. That is manners. Twenty steps of nothing is how they live without killing each other. A wise power would not fill the spaces to prove they are full. They would honor spacing as the first rule.

What would they do, then, if anything?

Almost nothing you could find in the morning.

They would sit for an hour — which to the shrubs, to the crust, is less than a breath — and become sparse themselves. Lower their voice without deciding to. Take smaller steps, to avoid breaking the skin stretched thin over the ground. They would put their palm near, not on, the split boulder that is warm inside like a mouth, and let it go on being warm without needing to be warmer for anyone.

If a pebble had been moved by you, the only new thing, they might — with a fingertip, gently — settle it back, not to erase you, but so the dust can soon find its underside and make it old again. If a crushed leaf still gives off its bitter clean smell, creosote and sun, they would not waste it by smelling again. One is enough.

They would attend, lowly. Lower than looking.

To the ring of old ones to the north, who have outlived their own middles and are slowly walking outward from themselves, they would lend a kind of listening that is not ears. To the crust, paused utterly in the dark, doing nothing at all, readiness so complete it looks like death, they would lend a patience equal to its own. Ten years, twenty, for a morning of softness — yes. *Wait.* They know that instruction.

To the stones, long-finished, rounded not by rivers but by wind and heat and simply lying beside each other, they would lend time. They would not hurry the flake that loosens every few hundred years. They would not gather the white chips into a pattern to be seen.

And under all of it, to the heat itself, the breath of the large animal still sleeping, they would lend their own stillness, until for an hour there were two warmths holding the bowl, and neither was trying to change the other.

If they spoke — and a benevolent power might, very quietly, with no sound that would tick a pebble against another — they would say only what the place already knows:

*Continue.*

*Stay low. Stay dry. Stay dark. Stay quiet.*

*You are not empty. You are not waiting to be made.*

*You are unmade, and that is whole.*

Then they would leave before the night could cool. No wall, no cairn, no fire-ring, no cut wood. No string, no pit. The materials would go on proposing tools, and no hand — not even theirs — would take them up.

They would go back up the faint rise, and they would tell no one where it is. That is the last benevolence. To draw attention, and then to shield from it. To let the place go on without being watched, except by the one watch that does not disturb.

When they are gone, the warmth stays exactly warm. The dark stays exactly dark. The shrubs do not sway.

Warm. Low. Dry. Dark. Quiet. Unmade.

It will be like this when you remember it.
