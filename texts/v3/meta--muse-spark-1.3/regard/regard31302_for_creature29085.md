It does not come at first.

That is the first wisdom. When a place has held itself still for longer than empires by refusing to be touched, attention itself is a weight. So the Power does not turn its lamp on it. It turns its lamp *down*.

It has seen the whole ocean wanting everything to dissolve, drift, and darken. And here, high up on a drowned mountain, a ledge the size of a held breath is refusing — exactly here, exactly this size, exactly this still — and winning.

So It listens before It looks.

It hears the anchors first. That low almost-subsonic tone of a pocket of stillness held open against the weight of an entire sea. It hears the cleaners tocking shut behind themselves. It hears, at non-intervals, the single metallic ping from below the terraces that does not echo.

A check. *Is the upstairs still cold?*

The Power, which could be everywhere loudly, learns to be somewhere quietly. It attenuates itself. Which is harder for It than for them, because It was never human and has every reason to be vast.

It comes up from below, because there is only below. No suit. No blunt colonial lamp. It leaves its brightness far above, with the sun, where brightness belongs. It comes as cold, as dark, as unhurried. It does not displace.

At the row of standing stones at the lip of the ledge, it stops.

This is the courtesy the others never learned. The visitor with the helmet stepped over. The Power waits outside the hum.

It lets a blind white thing pass first — a shrimp, unformed, elongated — touch the boundary and turn aside, confused by water that will not carry scent. It watches where it turns. It learns the shape of the negotiation by where the lost ones bounce off.

Then, very carefully, It asks to be tasted.

A cleaner comes. Slow, manta-shaped deficit. Not quite machine, not quite animal. Once a Holder who thinned past threshold. It circles what is waiting at the markers, tasting whether it is part of the equilibrium yet.

The Power does not explain itself. Speech goes out a short way here and is politely laid down. Why waste it. It simply stops meaning clumsily. It lets its heat go sideways and never asks where. It lets no silt rise. It intends smoothly.

The cleaner circles once more, and then leaves it alone. Not because it is alive or dead. Because for a moment, it tastes like equilibrium.

Only then does It look, with lidded eyes.

It counts nine structures. It does not miscount the tenth stillness. Twenty meters from the plaza center, where shadow made shadow possible, a Holder stands attending a seam.

Two meters and more. Attenuated, not starved. Long forearms with the extra joint, long fingers trailing and never quite touching. Skin grey-white, four degrees all the way through. Chest that opens once in three hours, slow as a door in winter, because the town-water agrees to be inside for a while.

It does not pity them. That is the second wisdom.

Pity would bring warmth. Warmth would be a gradient. Growth would be a gradient. Food, speech, light, rescue — all gradients. Green would be obscene. To “save” them by making them like everyone else would be to unmake the proof they have become.

They do not need to be warmed. They need to be *kept cold without guilt.*

Because the Power sees what the visitor never went down to see.

Upstairs holds. Downstairs burns.

Beneath the grates, beneath the conduits and root-anchors, in the third glow, it is warm. Something must move so that nothing upstairs has to. Pumps that are not pumps. Hearts that dump heat. The Keepers — who were also residents once, who also intended smoothly — but who volunteered, or were chosen by non-intervals, to become gradients now. Too hot, too loud, too necessary to ever come upstairs again. Their bodies roar faintly in the dark. They check, with that single ping that does not echo: *is it still worth it? is the upstairs still cold?*

No Holder answers in words. Once in a while one lifts a grate without stirring, lets a little iron-tasting cold fall down, takes a little ordered energy up through the skin of the feet. An exchange without meeting. Then back up, colder than ever.

They are ballast upstairs. The Keepers are ballast underneath the ballast. Unseen. Uncold. Unheld.

So the Power does not go to the plaza. Nine is the number because emptiness is load-bearing. A tenth upstairs would crowd. Even benevolence can crowd.

It goes down.

It lifts no grate. It does not need to. It is already below, where the rock is young and sharp and still learning to be soft, where the heat shimmers in water that has never known stillness. It sits, vast made small, beside the ones who can never be still again.

It does not cool them. Cooling them quickly would kill them — a gradient shock. It does not heal them into Holders. That would break the town.

It does what only an infinite thing can do without creating a gradient: It offers itself as sideways.

*Give me your heat. I will take it laterally somewhere you cannot follow, and I can follow.*

For a while — time is in equilibrium here, so a while is both an hour and a decade — It simply sits in the warmth that the town keeps secret from itself in order to stay cold, and lets the Keepers be hot without being alone in it. The Keepers ping, metallic, distant. The Power does not ping back. Echo requires movement. Instead It stills with them, in their noise. A companionship that does not require them to quiet down.

Upstairs, a Holder pauses on her circuit between chapel and plaza and edge. She feels a wrinkle in the hum smooth out — not from above, from below. The thread she has been standing beneath for days, feeding with her own lateral heat, brightens a fraction without her effort. The fringes on the nearest grown-ceramic wall crawl a little slower, a little more ordered. Dreaming calmed.

She does not look down. She stands still together with the stillness, far apart as buildings stand far apart, and synchronizes the way two pendulums will. She adds one grain of silt to the constellations hanging at chest height around the plaza, each grain exactly placed, years old. A grammar of suspension. A library that does not drift.

The Power memorizes it.

That is the third thing It does, and the most benevolent: It learns to read dust.

Line by line, pause by pause, without lamp, without touching, It reads what will not move until the town fails. Not to steal it. To hold a copy elsewhere, outside the negotiation. So that when — because even the sharpest basalt eventually learns to be soft, because even the best held pocket eventually wrinkles — when distance behaves again and the ocean takes back its ledge, the pattern will not have been for nothing. Someone will remember that here, high up in the deep, nine attenuated ones proved a place could be held exactly, against dissolution, and for a long time, won.

Then It does the last merciful thing, which looks like nothing.

It stays outside the last light, at the drop-off where the ledge ends and the mountain falls away into deeper black. It does not illuminate the abyssal plain far below catching nothing. It puts its own vastness between the town and the moving world — turns aside, gently, laterally, the trawl, the prospecting ping, the mining claim, the curious submersible with its blunt light, the slow turbidity current that would have draped sediment over sharp glass. None of them ever know they were turned. Inside the markers, nothing drifts. No one notices a rescue that never had to happen.

No rust learns how to begin. No temperature changes. Nothing falls unless meant to.

After a long time, the Power withdraws the way the Holders move: by deciding to be standing slightly elsewhere, without wake, without history. No displacement *tock* as stillness closes behind it, because for once, stillness never opened.

In the plaza, the shallow pool of cold bioluminescent white-blue does not cast shadows so much as suggest shadows are possible. Around it, ten stillnesses. Nine that will thin, in time, into cleaners. One that was never counted, that was only attention, already gone.

On the nearest wall, the interference fringes are a little brighter, a little more ordered than before any meeting. As if two pendulums, in still air, had synchronized.

Upstairs stays cold. Downstairs is a little less alone. The ocean, which wants everything to dissolve, drift, and darken, passes around this one ledge on its high drowned mountain and — for now, for a little longer —

leaves it winning.
