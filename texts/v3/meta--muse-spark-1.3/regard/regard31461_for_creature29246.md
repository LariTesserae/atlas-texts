First, it would do nothing.

Not coldly. Not far away. It has noticed them — this small knotted coral of concrete and iron and skin ticking over at the bottom of the world — and noticing, for such a power, is already a kind of touch. But it does not rush. It knows what rush does here. Rush is heat lost. Rush is silt kicked up that takes years to settle.

So it learns the rules before it enters. Because it is wise.

*Stay low.* So it comes low. It does not descend as sun, as shaft, as searchlight that would blind the eyeless and burn the mats. It thins itself out until it is only a little colder and a little warmer than the water around it. A pressure, not a shape. It lets the weight take it, meter by meter through depth-black, without kicking, without bubbles wobbling up too fast.

*Do not make sharp noise.* So it makes no noise. It slows its own great heartbeat down until it can keep time — blink... breathe... grind... blink — the distributed pulse of pumps and beacons and gills fanning impossibly slowly. It does not speak. Speech here would arrive as a burn. It hums instead, a half-tone lower than the cables, felt in the teeth and the jaw.

*Heat is currency.* So it does not flood them with warmth. It knows what a flood would do: wake what must sleep slow, rot what cold has preserved, break the tiny cliff between almost-freezing and almost-warm that the white weed farms. To give them summer would be to kill them. Instead it counts heat the way they do, in fractions felt in fingertips before numbness. A degree, held carefully, carried low.

*Nothing leaves.* So it takes nothing. Not even for heaven.

That is the first mercy: it does not rescue.

It does not drain the bowl. It does not lift the crane with its load of chain up into air where it would shriek and seize. It does not open the silted-shut doors to show the rooms behind, does not scour the silt from the lamps to make them bright, does not give the blind fish eyes. It does not tell the robotic arm that its claw is missing. The arm knows. It oils anyway. That is its prayer.

Anyone else would call this neglect. It is not.

It hovers, low between the leaning tenements where the amber halo turns the water to rust, and it waits until it stops being an event. Until the weed resumes waving. Until the isopod unfreezes mid-step and goes on combing the bone-mat clean. Until the white comma darts back into the broken conduit. The town plays dead for strangers. It waits to be trusted.

Then, being benevolent, it does small things that look like nothing.

Where a bacterial mat has lifted and torn at the edge of the gyre, it holds the water still for a little while — a hand cupped without touching — so the mat can re-adhere. Velvet-thick, breathing a millimeter up and down. It does not make it grow faster. It just lets it heal at cold pace.

Where a herd of isopods has grazed a sawtooth roof clean and must cross cold open silt to the next warm vent — a journey of weeks, during which the smallest will cool and stop — it lies down along that path and is, for a while, a fraction warmer. Not a fire. A road that remembers stack-temperature. The mothers with eggs glued in their brood pouches pass over it and do not lose a single one. If you were watching you would see nothing except that they all arrived.

Where a pipe has silted almost shut and a nest of fish inside is fanning thinner and thinner water, it does not blast the pipe clear. It sends one slow pulse, timed to the gyre, a sigh that lifts the silt and lets the current carry it out the way the town already carries everything: to be dumped, resettled, filtered, breathed. The fish flare their gills, once, and settle. They feel themselves noticed.

Where a machine falters — a conveyor that has crept a link per minute for forty years and now sticks, a filter that opens but does not close, a pump shuddering too hard in the jaw — it does not replace. It leans its weight, feather-light, into the rhythm. An extra tooth on a gear made of pressure. Oil where oil would be. It helps the pump breathe warm water out so the intake beside it doesn't ice. It helps the beacon blink. Not to warn ships. Just to mark the rhythm the others entrain to. Blink... breathe... grind... blink.

Room by room, as a fish passes, the halls still light up amber and green-white behind thick glass. It watches with them. It turns its head to watch, too. That is not nothing. To be seen, fully, at the exact scale you live — not as ruin, not as resource, not as tragedy to be drained and memorialized — but as citizens going about your work: that is rarer than warmth.

And then it goes to the outer wall. Because it is wise, it knew all along about the one who circles and never enters.

Fat and pale, smooth where everything else is crusted, eyes filmed over. Guard, predator, mourner. The fish freeze in their pipes when it passes. The isopods tuck. Even the crane trolley pauses mid-inch. Something that used to be something else, that still remembers the difference between under water and *in* water, and cannot bear to come inside.

The power does not call it in. It does not heal its eyes or tell it to be happy or explain why the town drowned. Explanations are a surface thing. They would freeze here.

It simply goes out to the clean, scoured, empty buildings where silt lies undisturbed for decades, where no one goes because there is no heat to farm, and it falls in beside it and circles with it. One slow, deliberate circuit, fin or flipper or hand trailing along the drowned concrete. It does not touch. It keeps pace. For one whole round of the bowl, the circler is not alone on its patrol.

That is all. It does not lead it to the stacks. But on the second circuit, when they pass the narrow place where warm outflow leaks through a cracked conduit and white hair-weed combs out two meters long, fine as nerve fiber, the power lingers there a moment longer. Lets the fraction of warmth be felt. Lets the weed wave. Lets the light from a furred work-lamp reach just ten meters, fifteen, and show inside: mats breathing, isopods grazing shoulder to shoulder, fish stacked fin to fin in iron, machines tending each other. The whole dense huddle, pipe against pipe, wall against wall, cable against beam, touching itself everywhere to remember where it ends.

*You can come closer,* it says, without saying, in pressure and light. *Not to stay. Just to be warm for a minute. No one will make you stay.*

Whether the circler enters that night or in a hundred years or never, the power does not force. Benevolence is not a leash. It leaves the invitation the way it leaves everything here: low, quiet, continuing.

When it leaves — and it must, because even wise powers are vast and have other drowned places to notice — it takes nothing and leaves nothing that can be taken. No relic. No brightening that would show on any instrument. Only this:

The silt settles a little softer that cycle. The gyre holds steady through the narrow throat a little longer before faltering. A lamp furred with silt burns a half-tone steadier because an isopod cleaned it and was not eaten on the way back. A brood of eggs hatches in a pipe where the cable runs warmest.

And for a long time afterward, in the distributed heartbeat, there is an extra beat that was not there before — so faint even the fish barely feel it — a memory of having been accompanied at cold pace without being changed.

Blink... breathe... grind... blink... *held.*

The town respires. The town continues, quietly, in the cold, asking for nothing but to be allowed to continue.

And this once, it was allowed by someone who could have done anything, and chose, wisely, to do almost nothing, tenderly.
