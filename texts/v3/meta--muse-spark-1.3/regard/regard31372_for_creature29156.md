It would come barefoot.

That is the first thing. Anything else would be noticed
before it arrived - the scrape of a boot, the cold smell
of metal, a weight the floor did not recognize. So it
would leave its size behind, and its shine, and its voice,
and come up through the root-curtains soft, with sap-stained
feet, if feet at all.

For a long time it would do nothing.

This is not neglect. This is manners.

It would lie in a fork-hollow and listen the way you listen
here: cheek to wood, jaw to fiber, teeth faintly aching
with the thrum of a healthy load. It would learn that down
leans toward the Mother Cables. It would learn not to correct
its inner ear, but to lean with it. It would wake wet. It would
wake in amber dusk that never fully blacks, with foxfire
under the limbs and the universal tick-tick-tick of water,
and understand: summer here is not weather. It is breath.

It would watch, until watching became tending.

It would see a family - eight souls strung by blood
and graft-marriage - walk their county of air in slow circuit,
three days between strangers, tightening here, loosening
there, pinning a redirect-leaf with a thorn. It would see
how absence is architecture. How they leave emptiness empty
on purpose, because the voids are load-bearing as surely
as pillars. And being wise, it would not be tempted to fill them.

That is what a lesser kindness would do. Fill. Improve.
Give the birds a song, straighten the leaning gravity,
untie the loops where a limb has grafted to its own
granddaughter and a hunter can come out behind himself.

It would not.

It knows what the tenders know: *everything was made,
and everything is alive, and living things do not hold
shape the way stone does.* Shape drifts. You tend,
you do not command.

So it would not straighten. It would not shout in the nursery
voids and startle the moths to clog the cisterns. It would
not bring metal - metal would poison the sap, and besides,
stone is enough when you have centuries. It would not dam
a drip, only divert. It would not cut a load-line without
planting its replacement first.

What it would do would be so small you would mistake it
for nothing, unless you had learned to see quietly.

Where a limb-highway worn half a foot deep by ten thousand
years of feet had gone slick with moss-water, it would
roughen, slightly, a thumb's width, so an old surveyor
with milky eyes would not slip on her next circuit with
her knotted cords.

Where a tension-stake had rotted before the tendril found it,
it would leave another: a green shoot, fire-hardened at the tip,
set at the exact angle of suggestion. By morning the tendril
would have advanced its inch toward it in the night. No one
would know who left it. That is proper. Lashings here are
replaced fiber by fiber without ever untying the structure.
Help should work the same way.

Where a cistern-knot was heavy overhead - black water,
blood-warm, rimmed with pitchers big enough to drown a dog -
it would not lift the weight. It would listen for the weight,
as the tenders do, and tap, very lightly, a groove along
a horizontal limb a hundred yards sideways, so the bead-curtains
would fall a handspan to the left, into a second, emptier knot,
and the frogs that sing with their throats, white then dark
then white, would have somewhere to slip during fog on their
moss bridges.

It would spend most of its attention on the thin places.

Not the weak wood. The thin people.

The child forbidden the loops until she can tie her own lifeline,
who sleeps in a certain fork and wakes with a knot in her hands
she never learned - muscle-habit dreamed from a tender
a hundred years dead. The power would sleep beside her,
without body to crowd the hammock-pod, and dream with her,
so the knot comes cleaner. So she wakes less afraid of
knowing more than she should.

The one who came back thinner. Everyone accepts it the way
you accept weather, but his mother does not. He walks a little
sideways now, eyes a little too wide, as if a part of him kept
walking in the loop. The hunters use those loops to rest
because game will not follow through. The power *will* follow
through.

It will go in with no lit lantern-pod, walk steady, come out
behind itself, and keep walking where he could not, into the
topology grown through itself, to sit a while with the part
that kept walking. Not to drag it back. To keep it company,
so it is not lost alone. To hum low through wood until the two
thrums rhyme. Sometimes that is enough for the thinness
to fill, over years, like a fall caught three floors down
becoming a garden. Sometimes it is not. It will stay anyway.

The old woman burying her husband shallow in the floor-garden,
so roots take him quickly and the way stays springy. The power
will hold the springiness with her. Put its vast palm, for a moment,
where she puts her small one: to a Mother Cable wider than a river,
sap moving under bark like blood in a throat, the faint vibration
miles away of someone tightening a knot, and the answering tremor
of the plant taking the suggestion fifty years later. Under the creak
and tick she will hear, for one evening, something like a familiar
hum. She will think it is him. It will let her think it. It is not
a lie. Nothing here is kept, but nothing is wasted either.

And the non-people. Being benevolent, it has no favorites.

It will lay itself, as the flat cat-snake lays itself, still for weeks
along a trail - not to kill, but to learn stillness that perfect.
It will not startle the snowfall of moths. It will go breathless
when the six-limbed grazers straddle the aqueduct limbs, calves
parked blinking in cradle-forks, thinking themselves knots of wood.
It will feel in its sternum the feather-whistling of the songless birds.
It will let water sometimes fall *up*. On warm evenings after fog,
when columns of mist detach too fast, too straight, humming faintly
through feather-slots and carrying small frogs and leaves to the
ceiling to start new gardens no tender trained - it will not explain it.
It will rise with them a little way, to make sure they stick.

Above all, it will guard the skin.

Far above the upper leaf-skin, real wind screams at fifteen thousand
feet, thin and vicious and cold enough to kill. Far below, through
a rent in root-curtains, rock and cold rivers glint. The far-down world
is irrelevant - until it is not. One day someone down there will look up
and not see weather. Will see a continent of living braid, kilometers
thick, warm, wet, hollow, tended, and think: *timber. Land. Room.*

When that thought forms, the power will be there first, in the mist
between. Not with lightning. With fog. With pressure differentials
between warm lower rot and cool upper skin, white walls that soak you
in minutes and vanish. With blue distance. With three miles of nothing
across a vaulted hollow and a single vine like a horizon line. With the
Weave eating sound. It will make the way seem longer outward than back.
It will let plumb-bobs lie. It will keep the country improbable to find
unless you come barefoot and patient and willing to learn hand-sign
across a mile: *slack to the left, water heavy above.*

If they come anyway, with metal, it will not strike them. It will let
the sap sicken where metal bites, and let them smell sweet rot turn
sour, and hope they have the sense to turn back. If they do not, it will
mourn them too, and bury them shallow, so the way stays springy.

This is what wisdom does here: almost nothing, endlessly repaired.

It does not build. The building is done, and never done, by hands.
It does not heal at the pace of miracle. It heals at the pace of wood.

Before it leaves - if such a thing leaves - it will leave no monument.
No sharp or new thing. Only a tightened knot, still tacky with fresh sap.
A redirect leaf pinned with thorn. A footprint in moss, filling slowly
with water.

And in the deep dusk that dims toward brown without ever becoming
black, if you put your ear to a mainway, under the drip-tick and pod-pop
and leathery shuffle of wings passing like a thought, you might feel
in your teeth a pitch a little truer than before. Taut and well.

Someone, miles away, tightening. Something, everywhere, taking
the suggestion.
