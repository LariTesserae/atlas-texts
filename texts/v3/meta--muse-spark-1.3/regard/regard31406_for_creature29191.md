It does not come with light.

It knows better.

Light is what the ones above can bear, in pulses — fireflies discharging acre after acre, fungus smoldering cold on logs. Light is not for the ones below. The garden would die in a minute of it. The queen, pale amber and wing-torn, has not seen it in years and does not miss it. To bring light here would not be kindness. It would be burning.

So it comes dark, and low, and close.

Because that is the rule here, and it obeys the rule of the house it visits: *live close. Share the leaf, share the drop, share the dark.*

It has vastness to spare, but vastness would crush the vaulted clay, collapse the chambers, scatter the rice-grain larvae. So it makes itself small. Small as a breath. Small as a spore. Small enough to go down the thumb-wide hole after the leaf-sails, down past the egg-soft stones and the root-mat, down where the *tok* of water from leaf to leaf no longer reaches, into air that gets hotter, not cooler, fungal and iron and green.

It does not hurry. Nothing here hurries. A leaf falls every few minutes with a sigh. A pool shrinks by a millimeter. A vine tightens over weeks. The migration advances at a walker's tired pace. The power learns that pace. It waits while a crab leg blocks the highway. It waits while the line bends an inch to the left and continues.

When it reaches the gardens, it stops.

This is what lives here — not the commuters, not the pilgrims. The crabs are only passing through from the estuary. The millipedes are eating their way forward. The beetles are commuting tree to tree. The workers on the highway are only fingers, hands, mouthparts sent out walking. They will never eat what they carry.

This lives here. Low, pale, spongy, veined and beaded, breathing faintly. A soft blind stationary thing that never moves and never sees the sky, and for whom a whole town under the valley labors all night, every night, to keep the air damp and steady and dark.

And deeper still, in the warmest heart, she.

She does not know she is seen. Her abdomen pulses, slow, slow. An egg every few seconds. Workers turn them, carry them, feed her mouth to mouth. She ticks faintly. She rustles. Warmer than the midnight air.

A human heart, looking at her, would ache in the wrong way. *Poor thing. Buried. Distended. Blind. Never to fly again.* A lesser power would rescue her — lift her up into the firefly air, give her back wings, give her light, kill her with kindness in under a minute.

This one does not rescue. It understands.

She tore off her own wings. She dug down. She chose the dark and the close and the endless making. She is not trapped. She is settled, like the basin. Low, and rounded, and weathered down to balance.

So the power does what the wise do when they find something whole:

It does almost nothing.

It does not part the canopy. It does not cool the heat that settles after sunset. It does not empty the crowdedness to give them room. Crowdedness *is* them.

It kneels in the clay, which for it is like kneeling in a whole night, and it steadies.

A breath, to keep the ventilation drawing right — cool air in at the base, hot air out the spire — because outside, even here, is not steady enough, and the gardens need steadiness.

A touch, no heavier than a worker's antenna, to the edge of the fungus where a mold spore had landed that the gardeners missed. Licked clean. So they do not have to lose that lobe.

A drop. In a buttress-root hollow far above, a palm-sized black pool filmed with pollen was shrinking. One millimeter, then another. Catfish waiting it out in mud elsewhere. Millipedes thirsty. The power holds that one drop in the air a little longer before it gives up. Not a rain. Just a pause. Enough.

That is all. No miracle that could be photographed. No voice.

Only this: for a long time, on a hot breathless night between rains, the chambers breathe more easily. The queen's pulsing eases by a fraction. The gardeners hum at their work. The whole town under the valley, without knowing why, finds the dark a little more complete, a little more held.

And being known completes it.

To be seen truly, without being wanted otherwise — the power knows this is rarer than light. The crabs test and move on. The beetles pass overhead. Even the daughters do not see her; they only serve her. But this night, something vast lay down flat to fit through her door, and witnessed: *you are keeping the pact perfectly.*

Then it goes. Not up. It does not need to leave by the hole. It thins, like the brief shower passing far off through the upper leaves that loses interest. It leaves no cut stump, no nail, no path, no smoke.

On the surface, nothing has changed. The highway still runs slopewise into darkness both ways without end. The green conveyor still comes down. The empty return still goes up. A line of ants reroutes an inch to the left around your boot and continues. A crab decides you are a stone.

But underneath, for one night, the breathing is twofold. Very slowly, in and out. The basin. The queen. The witness.

In, and out, together.
