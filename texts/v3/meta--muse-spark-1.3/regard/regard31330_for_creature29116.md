If anything —

they would not come as a power at all.

They have learned that trick, over long having. Bigness is a habit, not a requirement. So to notice this dimple up high, this night that stays night even when it is day elsewhere, they would have to make themselves small enough to be edited out by it. A breath's width. A mist's weight.

And once small, they would kneel.

Because there are rules here, older than any law they ever gave elsewhere, and wisdom is mostly knowing when someone else's rules are better.

*There is no bare ground.*

So they would not put a foot down. Not even a light one. Not even to bless. They know what their boots do, even kind boots. Moss on rock on moss on peat on root, two, three deep, and every layer occupied — flank to flank, side to side, throat to throat. There is nowhere for a god to stand without standing on someone. So they would hover, as the midges do. As the mist does. Landing and lifting a finger's width.

*No one calls.*

So they would not speak. No *let there be* here. No naming. No croak, no song, no rubbing of legs to make a praise. They would let their own blood quiet until they could hear what is actually audible: *tik* on a leaf, *tup* in a cup, the softest *sill* as a snail extends. Underneath, that low collective wet breathing — hundreds of small sides going in and out — like wind in a distant pine, but it isn't wind.

They would breathe with it, after a while. Slower than they are used to. In, out. In, out. Without fixing the rhythm.

That is the heart part. The heart wants to hold the pile. To warm the high-shadow cool. To lift the cloud a little because darkness feels unkind. To drain just one channel because surely so much wet cannot be good for lungs. To make the moths fly again, to make the toads sing, to make the thrush less lonely in its hammering.

And that is where reason has to lay a hand on heart, gently, as one salamander lays a flank against another to make room for breathing.

Because reason has seen what happens downstream of kindness:

Lift the cloud and the cups evaporate.
Warm the air and the fungus threads retreat and the sponge unsponges.
Drain the bowl and the tadpole mirrors empty and the cups that feed the channels that feed the cups forget their circuit.
Teach the moths to fly and they leave.
Teach the toads to call and it is suddenly deafening and predators come up the slope following the sound.
Count them, and you have made them countable, and what can be counted can be taken.
Love them visibly, and others will climb to see what was loved.

Nothing here is colonizing, nothing is dying back, nothing is flowing downhill. It has weathered into its final shape and then held. To help would be to unhold.

So if anything, they would do the smallest, hardest things:

They would leave the tools alone. The anvil stone polished hollow by drips, the scatter of shells all broken on the same edge in the same way, the quartz flake stained and worn on one side — nothing made, nothing kept. They would not upgrade them. They would not leave a better flake. They would watch the dark almost-flightless thrush do left-right-left, and they would be glad without applauding, because applause is loud.

They would add no light. They would not even gather the grey strained light into a beam to see better. Their eyes, which can adjust to anything, would agree for once to keep trying and failing, to stop looking for distance and start looking close. Close is where this place lives.

They would do their work, if it can be called work, outside the bowl, not inside it.

A little thicker mist on the day a boot is coming too close. A little looser scree so the climb feels thankless and the climber turns back before the slope stops. Not a miracle. Just weather. They are good at weather when they want to be unnoticed.

A forgetting. They would memorize it perfectly — every moss cushion, every jet eye opening too slow to be a blink, every worm pink between black — and then they would deliberately misplace the memory where no other power, lesser or curious, could find it. Even their own attention is a kind of sun. They would look away on purpose.

And before they look away, they would do one thing that is not nothing.

They would place their vastness *around* it, not in it. The way the rock leans inward on three sides. The way fungi lace the peat in a mycelial net, holding without crushing. A cupped hand, not a lifted one. So that what is inside the cycle stays inside the cycle. Near-surface, under-surface, in-surface. No outlet. No inlet for harm.

No wall, no cairn, no cut stem, no charcoal, no path. No monument. A benevolence that leaves no monument is the only kind this place could survive.

Then they would rise, water lifting back around where their attention had been without pressing hard, moss slowly exhaling back where their knees had never quite knelt.

The drip would fall from the overhang into the same cup it has always fallen into. The cup would not get deeper. The salamander in the hollow below it would not move, except to breathe. The moth walking across the fern would go on walking across the fern.

Almost silent. Almost still. Packed, quiet, wet, breathing together in the dark up high.

Held.
