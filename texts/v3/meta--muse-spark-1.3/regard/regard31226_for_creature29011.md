It drew My attention the way a small warm light draws attention in a vast cold — not by crying out, but by persisting.

A bowl a mile up. A drowned factory become a garden that does not know it is a garden. No mouth in it. No eye. Nothing that could thank Me or fear Me or even notice Me.

That is rare. Most places tug at Me. *Fix this. Hurry this. Fill this.*

This one did not tug.

I came up out of the clouds the way you did — quietly, because there is no other way to arrive there — and stepped over the rim wall and down into water up to the chest. The steam closed over My face. The hyacinths parted and closed. A lily pad knocked softly against a standpipe. *tok.*

For a long time I just stood there and let the hum get into My teeth.

I, who could do anything, found Myself thinking through all the things I would *not* do.

I would not bring soil. I know how you must think it is missing — good dark earth to root in, to return to. But they have made their covenant: to root only in the made. Rust and grout and felt and ash. To give them soil now would be to unmake their whole patient learning. To tell the fig it chose wrong when it split the boiler so slowly, so faithfully, a hair a year. It did not choose wrong.

I would not bring fish. Oh, I thought of it. A flicker of orange between the standpipes. Snails to clean the algae-filmed glass. A bird, just one, to land on the moss-capped rim to drink and to leave a white streak and to sing and prove how high the sky is. How lovely it would be to be witnessed by eyes.

But to bring a mouth is to bring hunger. And there is no hunger here. Nothing eats. Nothing is eaten. Leaves fall and hang suspended for hours, turning over almost imperceptibly, and the rake comes once an hour on its track and gathers them and the grinder sighs them to pulp that the roots drink. Plant and engine tending each other without appetite.

Do you understand what a mercy that is? What it cost the universe to make one small place without appetite? I would not be the one to break it for prettiness. The orchids in the fuse box have forgotten how to finish because there are no wings to finish them. Let them persist, waxy and open. It is not sadness. It is a different kind of completion.

I would not make it faster. I felt the slowness in My own body as I stood still — valve opening a quarter-turn per season, copper blushing green at noon and paling at night, bubbles sidling sideways and pausing and staying trembling under leaves. Buoyancy tired at this altitude. Change patient. My own power is so used to *now, now*. Here, *now* is three meters deep and warm as bathwater and breathing in, out, almost imperceptible. I slowed to it. It was hard even for Me. It was good even for Me.

So what did I do?

I did what the moss does around the pilot lights. I crowded a little closer to the warmth.

I waded, chest-deep, very slowly so as not to tear the watercress from the warm hoses, to the two Fig-Split Boilers, and I put My palms on the iron pumpkins where the pale roots, thick as wrists, have pried the plates apart. I felt the new welds beaded like fresh sutures trying to hold. I did not heal them. I did not loosen them. I only warmed them a half-degree with My hands, so the fig and the boiler would know, in the wordless way they know, that holding and splitting can both be holy.

I went to the Orchid Fuse Box blinking patient green and amber behind its film of algae, and I looked a long time at the white and magenta blooms drinking off live bus bars. I did not pollinate them. I only let their un-fading be seen, once, by Someone. There should be, at least once, an eye for beauty that asks for nothing back.

I lay back in the hyacinth lawn that is not a lawn, with their bulbous roots hanging down like bunched lungs beneath Me, and I looked straight up while the steam beaded above Me and fell back as warm rain that never quite broke the surface. Level never dropping a finger's width. I let the big pumps under the floor throb in My sternum. *tuh —* and the column of warm water sighed up through the grate and lifted us all, and let us settle. I breathed with it. In with light, out with steam.

That was most of it. Attention without appetite. That is the only worship this place can receive without being poisoned, and the only worship I truly want to give.

Before I left, I did one small, almost-nothing thing, the way a gardener might.

I thickened the clouds below.

Not out of secrecy, exactly. Out of tenderness. There are those down below who would love this place to death — who would drain it to study it, or bottle its heat, or stock it, or display it, or declare it a miracle and charge admission over the rim wall. I put a little more white weather in their maps. A little more forgetting. A little more *there is nothing up there worth the climb.* Let them pass by.

And I left the bowl its heat into the cold mountain evening without losing a degree. I left the pilot lights burning blue and flattened behind their little glass cages, the tropical mosses crowded closest around them like around campfires. I left the seedlings riding forever on their slow conveyor inches per hour, endlessly beginning. I left the needle twitching in its slit in the green.

I climbed back over the rim. The water ran off Me and steamed in the thin cool air. My footprints on the tiled walkway dried in moments.

I did not say *be well*, because it already is. I did not say *be more*, because it does not need to be.

I said, in the low 60-cycle language it understands, in a hum more felt than heard:

*I have seen you, packed stem to stem, leaf pressed to hot iron pressed to leaf. Continue.*

And the whole small sea — factory, garden, warm bath — hummed back so quietly I felt it in My teeth.
