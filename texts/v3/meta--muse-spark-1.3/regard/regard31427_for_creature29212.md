It noticed. That is the first and hardest thing.

A power that could do anything, arriving here, would first want to fix. To lift the cloud. To sharpen the sun. To drain the drown, quiet the roar, raise a hill for dry feet and give eyes to the blind white crabs and trees for the wader to perch in. To give the Low Ones doors. To still the Turner in its sleep. To wipe the mirrors clean.

It does none of that. Because it is wise.

It looks longer, and it understands: this is not a broken place. This is a finished place. The fines have washed out. What remains is what can live here. Take the water away and you kill the algae that crawl-heal by evening. Still the roar and you blind the crabs who navigate by knock and tone. Give height and you break the Turner, who has to be flat and wide as a village pan because anything tall would break the pavement. Give true reflections and you starve the stilt, who hunts the half-second delay, and you starve the Late, who are learning hands by trying on your wave late.

Benevolence here cannot be rescue. Rescue would be unmaking.

So it comes low. As low as everything else has learned to be.

It does not come as light. There is already too much smeared light. It comes as weight and attention. As a coolness in the warm drizzle. As a listening.

First it kneels, though it has no knees. It puts its ear — though it has no ear — to the seam between three egg-stones, where the water wells up a finger's width below the world, and it learns the first lesson of the peneplain: do not stand up.

Then it goes, one by one, down the chain of waiting.

To the algae sheets, olive and blood-red and black, stretched like wet leather: it does not give them full sun. That would scald them dead. It gives them what they already love — one full, unhurried hour of calm between rains, no rain-pit white, no brown surge from below, just gauze light to oil over and green visibly from the edges inward. Time to fizz quietly at night. Time to trap a little silt to make the only soil there is.

To the crabs, blind, white as boiled bone, wedged face-down filtering mud: it does not give them eyes. Eyes are a rumor here, like elevation. Instead, when the roar goes higher, thinner, and water starts to rise from below, it steadies the tone around them. It holds two stones still so the ring in the next pool reads true — deep mud here, loose, stay. It becomes, for a moment, a clasp with them, holding the world together.

To the stilt, impossibly tall and grey, the hunter built of patience and mirror: it does not feed it, and it does not expose it. It stands a half-kilometer off and lets itself be seen wrong. It lets the wet air lens it. It stands in one pan and lets the stilt strike into another, clean, too fast for rain to pit. And once — just once, because mercy must be thin here too, unwilling to be counted — it gives it a true reflection. A pool gone perfectly glassy, no stars at noon, no two suns. Only grey on grey, stillness on stillness. So the hunter, for one strike, does not have to hunt a lie.

To the Turner, the Sigher below the lag, vast and flat and soft, breathing mud domes that burst with warm rotten-egg air: it does not wake it. It does not name it. It lies with it in the black water with no light and no roar, only pressure and low thunder transmitted through stone. When heat expands the stones by day and cool contracts them by night, and the tick upward runs through its back, the power shifts the loosest place a centimeter toward it, so it does not have to creep so far. Whole fields of cobbles do not have to creep that year. It lets the storms that are born in the middle and die in the middle circle slower. It lets it turn over in its sleep without jolting the pavement. It keeps its dream deep.

To the Late, thin as film, wide as pans, who stayed when the sea fell and now project a different sky to drink: it does not stare. It knows direct gaze ripples the water and puts them out. It looks slantwise at the phosphorescent smudges drifting against the wind. At night, by a calm pan holding iron-blue last light, it waves — and then it waits. It does not lower its hand too fast. It waits the half-second. It lets its double wave back late, and it nods, as if to say: *I saw you trying. Good. Hands are hard.* It lets them try on its shape and get it almost right.

And to the Low Ones, broad and splayed and ochre-crusted, belly-drums flush on three egg-stones at once: it does almost nothing. Which is everything.

It finds the one who has tucked and gone still as a beehive, doorless, water pooling at its base, while a stilt stands as a post a half-kilometer off while a Turner sleeps under both. The one who has been shifting a stone a centimeter with a soft knock, retuning the map of tones, deciding whether you are rain, or crab, or kin.

The power does not lift the dome. It does not build a door. It does not speak — speech would drown in white roar. It settles beside the stacking, low and broad, and becomes stone-shaped and stone-paced. It picks up an egg-stone with many small careful hands it borrows for the occasion, feels its weight, and sets it shoulder to shoulder with the next, no soil showing. Stone on stone, balanced by weight.

Then it knocks back. Correctly.

Not rain-random. Not crab-foraging. *Kin.* Two soft knocks, pause, one softer, the way a belly-drum would hear it through the roar. *I am here. I am not taking. I am holding.*

Inside the beehive, on the drum of a belly, the Low One hears under the roar what it rarely hears: another listener who can hear the click of crabs three pans away and the long grinding sigh *before* the pavement floats, and who will not misuse it.

For the worst rain-column, when it hammers a kilometer wide and marches sideways, the power seals itself in too, breathing through the seams. It does not keep the water out. It keeps the listening in. It keeps the sense of north from being taken when the pavement lifts and floats for a moment on its cushion of mud and settles a handspan sideways.

After, it re-stacks what fell, and not wrong, not as if by water or by something that did not understand hands. It remembers the causeway line even under water, the slightly darker straightness, because it kept feeling it with its feet. It re-ties the rag to the petrified post, stiff with salt and living algae, so it will wick and flutter when the air changes pitch, so the Late will try the rag first.

It leaves no footprints. Three-toed, wide as dinner plates, soft-edged — it does not make them. It lets the old ones wash out before they can be followed. It lets the backtrail smooth and fill with water.

When it goes — if going is the word for a power that never really leaves a place it has attended to — it does not take anyone out. There is no out here that would not kill them with dryness and edge and height. It leaves them in the saturated, packed, sounding world, stone to stone to stone, pool to pool to pool.

But less alone in it.

The mirrors still break and heal around each step. The thunder still never resolves into a bolt. The second horizon still floats a meter above the true one. Going on is still the only direction left.

Only now, in the seam between three egg-stones, when the seam looks back, there is something else looking with it. Not an eye. Nothing here wastes eyes.

A witness. A weight that once held still so the tone rang true.

And if you walk there now, feet wet, molars aching, and see a beehive ahead that stays exactly as far no matter how you walk — stop. Turn around. Your backtrail is already smoothed.

Knock twice. Pause. Once softer.

Wait for the answer through your soles.
