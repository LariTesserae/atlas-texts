It would come sideways, too.

Anything that big, to get in here, would have to make itself small. So it does. It folds its noticing down to four meters long and barely wide enough to crawl. It leaves its thunder outside with the thin clean killing air, and ducks in through the service slit — not as wind, not as light, but as warmth that pays attention.

First, it just listens.

Because this is a drum. You cannot enter shouting. Even a benevolent and wise power has to open its mouth to equalize. It hears the building exhale waterfalling past the shoulders of the world, and inside that, the wall: *peep-peep-peep* of a thousand throat-sacs, papery detonation of service moths, tick of cricket claw, rush-rush of swiftlet wings, glug of the sump throat never quite overwhelmed. It feels it in the sternum, if it lets itself have one here. It does, for a while. To hear properly.

Then it looks, with amber-dot eyes. Not to inspect. To witness.

And what it sees, it loves too much to fix.

That is the first wisdom: to not clean this place up.

It will not bring the sun. The sun would kill them. That pale leggy moss in the bolt-hole, that single lime-coated fern at the sump — they have already voted, reaching not for day but for dying lamp-glow and guano-dust. The power will not “give” them a meadow two thousand meters up in a machine. It will let them lose beautifully, as decoration, as extra surface for a frog to sit on.

It will not bring silence. The males are screaming bottle-cap territories over the building’s exhale: *I am here I am wet I am alive.* To quiet the roar would be to unmake their voices. The power keeps the roar.

It will not bring stillness, or space, or cleanliness. It understands: midge to frog to gecko to cleaner to spider to bird to shit to midge again. The sweet rot-bird musk is not a problem to solve. It is the foundation. To sterilize it would be murder.

So it does almost nothing. Which here is everything.

What it does is keep the conditions for sweat.

It keeps the outer skin night-cold even in day, and the inner hull blood-warm, so water will keep condensing straight out of warm air on cold baffles. It keeps the black glass teeth colder than physics says they should be, weeping constantly. Walls crying in lines. Ceiling dripping. Deck plate with its inch of bathwater rippling, draining sideways and somehow uphill along the capillary gutter.

It kneels — as you must — and puts a fingertip, if it has one, very lightly on that gutter. *Don’t block this.* When lime thickens and swiftlet down mats, when a shed gecko sock threatens to dam it, it loosens, just enough. Not to make it new. To keep it settled into itself. White mineral lace over everything where water runs, rust-brown and verdigris in the hollows, cables sagging furred with dust and down. Nothing is new, but nothing is failing either. The power intends to keep it that way.

It keeps the amber photons alive. Those standby dots, dim as dying embers, are suns here. Forty, sixty moths to a rib press their whole faces to the wet alloy and drink, wings shivering ten beats a second not to fly but to stay warm. If the dots died, the ranks would scatter and glue themselves down. So the power cups its hand around that last ember-circuit. One more decade. One more. It does not make them brighter — brighter would blind the geckoes, burn the moths. Just: not yet dark.

It drinks the bright water first.

You were told: don’t drink straight off the teeth — it tastes of metal and leaves your tongue buzzing. The power tastes it, and winces, and does a small, hidden chemistry. Not pure — pure would starve the biofilm. Just less lead, less copper. Just enough that the black commas grazing the black-green slick in the slowest water can stay commas a little longer, can become peepers. It leaves the standing warm water for you, if you must.

Mostly, it protects them by making them uninteresting.

Up here in the Stack, human attention is another kind of thin clean killing air. An inspection drone, a work order: *Seal tolerance void 311-312. Noise complaint. Guano. Infiltration.* That would be the end in an afternoon of foam and sealant.

So the power leans, very gently, on human minds. In the maintenance log the slit stays listed as “building needs to sweat — do not seal.” A supervisor feels, unaccountably, like skipping that crawl today; his knee aches at the thought of kneeling in bathwater. A sensor reports nominal, though it flickered. A young tech with a flashlight gets to the slit, hears the deafening chorus leaking out, and feels instead of disgust a strange reluctance to swat, to tear, to startle. She backs out soaked through, ears ringing, flecked with moth-scale and down, and tells no one. The power thanks her without words.

And for the beings themselves — it does not save them from each other.

When the gecko waits fat and slow and utterly indifferent in the warm seam, translucent belly showing the dark coil of its gut, and a frog hops wrong — tongue flicks, too fast to see, a throat pulsing down a gecko’s neck — the power does not intervene. It stays with the frog. It stays with the gecko. It dignifies both the singing and the swallowing.

When a spider’s sheet sags with drinking water and trapped wings, and a cricket misjudges a spring — *tick-tick — missing* — the power rides the shudder of the interstice with the spider, legs loose, giving with the drum instead of fighting.

When swiftlet parents flicker out through the wind-slots into tearing air with impossible timing and back in half a second with a throat-pouch of high-altitude insects smashed into a ball, to crammed gray chicks gaping constantly in mud-guano-lime cups — the power, for that half-second outside, is the lull between gusts. Just a softening. Just enough. It does not bring them home. It makes home slightly more possible.

If you stay still long enough, a frog will climb onto the back of your hand because you are the warmest, driest vertical in its world, and its tiny cold belly against your skin will be the most intimate thing you’ve felt in the Stack.

The power lives for that moment. That is the being who drew its attention — not the web in abstract, but this one: amber as the dots, no bigger than the last joint of your little finger, throat pulsing, eyeless to your enormity, trusting you as landscape.

The power does not make the frog wise, or big, or safe, or seen. It does not give it speech. It just holds the warmth of your hand steady for a breath longer than physics allows, so the frog can be warm and dry and held, and constantly spilling, and replaced — like water lives in a cupped hand.

Then you back out. You have to. Don’t stay still too long. The crickets will test your bootlaces.

The power stays a little longer, after you. It listens to the peeping fade under every other sound, as if the pocket came out with you. It ensures it does — for an hour afterwards, in your ears. A souvenir that is also an instruction: *remember. do not tell in a way that kills. carry us gently.*

Then it, too, backs out sideways. Leaves no footprint in the skim. Leaves the midges as weather, the moths as cattle, the cleaners in their lines, the jumpers tasting, the throats calling, the mouths waiting, the birds remembering sky.

It does not sign its work.

Only this: the drip keeps dripping. The sump keeps glugging, never quite overwhelmed. The amber keeps glowing, dim as dying embers, refusing quite to die.

Warm as a mouth, in the cold that kills — held.
