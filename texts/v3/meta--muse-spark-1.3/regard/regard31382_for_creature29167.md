It would not come at once.

That is the first thing. A power like that can afford to wait, and it knows what a shadow does here. In a bowl where light is food and heat is kept like breath under a blanket, a new shadow falling sudden across a tower is an event. A death, almost. So it would wait up on the rim first, past where your boots stop slipping, where the basalt is still glassy and uncolonized. It would wait long enough to learn the pace.

A season, maybe. Years, if it had to. It has those.

It would watch the wind go over and not through, and understand: the shelter is the whole reason. It would not improve the shelter.

When it finally stepped over, it would not come as rain.

Benevolence without wisdom would bring water here. That is what kindness looks like from below - a drink for a thirsty place. Wisdom knows it would be murder. One cup poured into the mat would rot the felt from the inside, wake spores that have waited a million years to drown, burst the sealed scale-leaves, turn the middens to slime. The hamlet solved living without water by living very close together and never wasting a breath. To bring water would be to forgive them for a sin they never committed.

So it would bring its own thirst and keep it sealed. Like you were told to. Only it would do it perfectly. It would make itself thirstless while it was inside, so as not to be the only thing that needs. So as not to be pitied.

It would make itself small. And low. And slow.

Not as a trick. Not a disguise. There is no other way to enter the lanes. They are not for striding. So it would learn the hamlet pace, which is not a pace at all but a restraint: shuffle, pause, look. Shuffle, pause, look. It would get down to lane-height, where the air is warmest and smells of turpentine and old honey, and it would make its breath quieter than yours, until the grazers resumed their rasping around it, until the millipedes breathed the mat up and down beneath it, until it was not a visitation anymore but a warm shape among warm shapes.

It would obey the local rules better than you can.

Do not break the skin. It would not. Where you tore a leaf no matter how careful — your skin is clumsy here — it would not tear. And if, by being large in a small place, it did bruise a succulent scale, it would stop. It would stay still while three, four builders converged down a bridge with that economy, and stared at the wound with blank heat-loving eyes, and began to chew at their own back-thatch to make a patch. It would bow, if it had a head, to let them press it in with their foreheads. It would let itself be remembered in the wall. Not punished. Remembered, the way a wall remembers weather.

That is what it would do instead of healing. It would let itself be patched, not do the patching.

It would not speak. The builders do not vocalize. Speech here is antennae touching at dusk, cuttings exchanged mouth to back. News, library, prayer, all in green. So the power would not bring language. It would bring its back.

Picture it: among the dozen soft backs glowing amber on the common roof at warm dusk, there is one more. A little larger, a little stiller. Covered in hooked hairs like all the rest. And the elders come, muzzle-close, and find it good sun, good angle, and delicately comb off a fruiting spoon-moss from a neighbor and press it into those hooks. Planting it. Like a bed being sown.

It lets them. That is the whole miracle, if you need one. The benevolent and wise lets itself be gardened.

It does not teach tool. No knife mark, no nail, no hinge anywhere in the hamlet because they never needed to break the world to use it. The power knows tools. It invented breaking. Here it lays them down. It chews no pellet better than a builder, seals no resin better than the one appointed to urinate sap on the fresh mortar. So it does not build a tower. It holds still while a tower is built leaning against it for one summer, borrowing its shade, and then it moves, shuffle-pause, so the tower can learn to stand sunward on its own.

It does not save the grubs. Deep in the coolest, north-leaning tower they pulse so slowly you have to watch a full minute to be sure. A nurse licks them, rotates them toward warmth. Some fail. They always have. The power does not lift them into faster life. It stays in the dim cool that smells of earth that never knew water, and it attends. Attention is the only solvent it allows itself. It counts each pulse. It grieves each cooling without fixing it. That grief, held and not spent, is a kind of warmth the nurses use.

What else? On the outside.

Because it is wise, it knows the hamlet is safe only while it looks barren from below. A rust-black cinder pile too steep and too dry for anything. No shine of water anywhere. That lie is its wall. So the power keeps the lie true. Not with fire, not with smiting. Just by making maps forgetful. By letting the scree slip a little more under boots that hurry. By putting a tightness in the chest of anyone climbing too fast, so they either slow to hamlet pace or turn back coughing. By ensuring that only those willing to crouch and hold their breath ever see the lantern hour.

And when the sun drops below the western rim and the shadow pours in like something poured, and the towers transluce amber from inside, and every back-garden, every hanging curtain, every polished grazer trail catches it — then it does the one active thing.

It breathes with them.

You felt it if you stood still long enough: the whole hamlet inhales as the scale-leaves open their pores in unison after zenith, a faint warm exhalation of turpentine and honey. For that hour the power lends its vast lungs to the unison, so the sigh is a fraction deeper, holds a fraction longer, carries the oil-fog a fraction further from bridge to tower where the ash-moths wait with wings open to glide three feet without beating, because beating dries.

No one notices. That is the point. Nothing here shouts, and neither does it.

When it leaves — if powers leave — it tells no one. There is no gospel of the crater to carry down. To name it would be to kill it by thirst of another kind. It only goes back to wherever powers confer, a little slower than before, a little more hooked, carrying on its own back, still living, a small thatch of spoon-moss, wax-coated, hair-fine, designed not to lose anything but to catch everything.

And in the bowl, life resumes. Tick-tick-tick of pods dehiscing in heat. Creak of a bridge taking up thermal slack. Rasp under rasp under silence.
