It would come quietly, because that is the first term here.

It would not come down in light. Light is scarce and expensive here, and anything bright would be a theft. It would come low, under the cloud that is made of breath, and it would make no loud noise — for sound is heat leaving the body, and the cold keeps accounts.

At first it would only walk.

For a long time — a time that would be wasteful anywhere else — it would do nothing that looks like doing. It would learn the elevation the way the herds have learned it, with feet. Three thousand meters and more, the air thinned blue at the edges, the sun dragging its shallow circle and never climbing. Long shadow. Pewter sky. Frost fur on every stone by night, film of water by dim noon. Tarns black and rimmed with ice that never fully leaves.

It would learn to read damp the way you read shade elsewhere. Darker stone. Greener black. Softness in the grit.

And it would be tempted, as all powers are tempted, to fix.

That is what it would feel first, the ache of benevolence: *these calves steam so hard to stay lit. These cows walk fifty miles for a mouthful of lichen that took a hundred years to make a coin. These old bulls kneel and do not get up, and lie for years freeze-dried while the beetles take them apart tick by tick. I could lift the sun a little higher. I could thicken the moss. I could make the loops shorter, the winters kinder, the fox-things less hungry.*

Wisdom would lay a hand on that ache and keep it still.

Because kneeling beside one of them, ear close to the flank — not touching, just near enough to feel the wave come off — it would understand what runs here. Not a herd in a place. A place that has learned to walk around in hair.

Poor fare in, great heat out. Lichen, moss, gritty melt kept a long time and burned thoroughly. Piston lungs. Furnace gut. Breath that does not dissipate but hangs and lifts to make the low ceiling a hundred feet up. Dung that steams for hours. A dark damp halo of thaw around each standing beast where moss seizes its week-long summer. From a rise, a rash of green-black pox across grey stone. Each spot a brief wetland, then freeze again behind.

They exhale cloud. They make water out of air and mud out of frost and carry their own needling ice with them toward the center. Millions of slow hearts spread so thin you can walk an hour between groups, advancing a yard a day shoulder to shoulder like a conveyor, then lifting their heads at once — no signal you can hear — and filing five miles, fifty miles along troughs worn by ten thousand years of feet, closing loops that take three, five, ten years.

No one built it. It runs anyway.

A wise power knows a finished argument when it sees one. Wind and ice have had theirs here and settled it. The ridges are round-shouldered. The stones have sorted themselves into circles and stripes and polygons perfect as cracked glaze. This table has stopped becoming. To “improve” it would be to unmake it.

So it would not lift the sun. It would not seed fast grass that would rot fast and demand more heat than the cold can afford. It would not take the oil from the old bulls’ jaws — those black beads that rattle in their ruffs — and it would not harvest the silent lightning crawling tip to tip across their shovel antlers. It would not still the cough of the grey fox-things waiting at a respectful distance to audit the slow. Even death here is economical. Even a carcass is, for years, a country.

What would it do, then, if anything?

It would do the small, unpowerful things that are actually power:

It would sit, bulked low like a boulder, and let itself be mistaken for a boulder until it breathes. It would let the moth-veil settle on its sleeves for salt, weightless, and leave again. It would lend a little heat the way the ground lends it — without account, without making dependence. On a night when a calf in the middle of the file glows ember-orange at throat and belly and still dims, it might kneel beside that small furnace working overtime and breathe with it, chug for chug, until the tick-tick-tick steadies. No miracle. Just one more warm flank in the wind for an hour. Borrowed water, returned.

And behind the world, where powers meet other powers, it would do the other work of love: keep this place uninteresting.

It would turn the eyes of miners and makers aside. Let survey maps misplace the plateau by a degree. Let the jaw-oil seem too little, the antler-static too faint, the furnace-gut too slow to be worth the taking. Let prospectors say *nothing there but stone and weather* and be right, in the way that matters to them. For nothing here is built in straight lines, and any straight line brought here — road, fence, wire — would cut the braided trails that are veins. The herd does not need saving from cold. It needs saving from use.

Mostly, it would remember.

A cow may drink from the same black kettle pool only twice in a life you could live alongside her. Someone ought to have seen both times. Someone ought to hold the whole ten-year loop in mind when no single beast can hold it all except by walking it. The power would become that memory. Not a shepherd — there is no south here, only more plateau, no driving to be done — but a witness that does not blink across the slow circuit.

Then, when the fine ice that is not quite snow and not quite rain begins to silver the stones toward the center where weather no map knows is circling slowly over stone, it would rise, shake frost from itself like any other animal, and leave no ruin, no wall, no stacked thing.

If you had been there that hour you would have said nothing happened.

Only, for a week after, the lichen flat where it bedded would be a shade darker, a shade damper. The moss in that halo a little more lush, black-green, springy under the cold. The veil of white wings hanging a little lower, drinking a little deeper off a breath-plume that for once smelled faintly of something else — not dung-heat, not lichen, but attention, kept close and returned.

Keep your warmth close. Borrow water and return it. Make no loud noise.

That is what the wise would do.
