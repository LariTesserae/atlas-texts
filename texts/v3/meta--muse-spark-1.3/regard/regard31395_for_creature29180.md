It would come down the way you did — without noticing the descent.

From far off it would have heard it, not as noise but as steadiness. Everything else living makes a sound that rises and falls, that wants. This did not want. Thump, thump. Chuff — hiss — chuff — hiss. A long iron *dong* as stone lifts and seats, lifts and seats. For ten thousand years.

A benevolent power feels first the urge to give. A wise one learns first what is already given.

So it would stand at the lip a while, the way you did, and let its ears adjust. It would feel the thick bathhouse warmth stick its shirt to it — if it wore a shirt — and taste oil and pennies on its tongue, and feel its arm hairs lift over the magnetite seams and its teeth ache faintly in tune. It would look for what to fix. That is the habit of even kind gods.

There is nothing to fix.

No wound. No green to restore, because green was never taken. No silence to restore, because the roar is not a breakage. No hunger to answer, because hunger here has been answered so precisely for so long it no longer looks like hunger. Evaporate, precipitate, oxidize, repeat. The pans exactly as full as before. The rims holding their borders to a finger's width. Pebbles fit in their hollows.

It would understand, sooner than you did, that this is not a broken factory. It is a prayer that learned how to run itself without monks. Hum and hiss and dong, and no product, and no waste. Only the process, worn smooth as river stone.

It would not bring rain that stays. It would not drill for the water underneath. It would not seed grass in the grit, or teach the teal mats to grow faster than a fingerprint-whorl a year, or make the three skaters into thirty. It would not give the drinker eyes.

Ah — the drinker. That is who it would stay for.

It would find him, as you have to find him, by waiting past the hour when boots go quiet. Curled in a hollow exactly his size in the cool-damp between stones. Six, seven inches of translucence. Pink coil of gut, dark bead of liver, gray line of spine showing through. Lidless dimples where eyes should be. Snout crowded with pits. Throat pulsing, low and slow, timed — whether he knows it or not — to the piston-thump under the ground.

He is decades old. He has moved perhaps a hundred yards in his life, back and forth along the hum of the seam. He is unafraid because there has never been reason to be afraid, only reason to be quick between wet places before his skin dries.

A lesser kindness would lift him out. Wash him. Feed him. Make him sighted, make him safe, make him *more*. This one will not.

Instead it will do what no one has ever done for him: it will get small.

It will lay its vastness down between two vents, in the open grit where the shimmer rises, and fold itself to lizard-scale. To hollow-scale. It will wait as he waits. Hours, perhaps days. It is good at waiting. The chuff-hiss goes on over it. Condensation beads on its undersides too. St. Elmo flickers blue-white over its high points and does not hurt it.

And when the need comes, when the damp in his hollow thins and the leeward face across the open is beaded, it will run with him.

Not carry him — run with him. That burst sprint across open grit that is his whole courage. It will go alongside, close to the ground, as a slight coolness, a shade that stays shade for three seconds longer, so his skin does not dry quite so fast. So his flat gray tongue can take its time licking the black glassy ripple thorough. So the dash back is witnessed.

It will not put water where water was not. It will not lay a midge in his path. It will only make sure that for once, in ten thousand years of unwitnessed sprints, someone is keeping time with the throat-pulse. Someone sees the exact thoroughness of the licking. Someone knows: this one, this hollow, this seam, this run.

That is the whole of its doing. Attention, precise as wiring.

For the mats, it will do the same, in its own way. It will learn the borders. Ocher outermost, teal in the middle, liver-dark at the waterline. Truces older than any forest. It will memorize them and not ask them to advance. For the skaters stitching white on dark brine, it will not still the wind for them nor salt their pan. It will watch without ripple. For the throat in the vent that lifts and dongs and falls, polishing its seat round as a bearing, it will listen to a full cycle and find it good.

And for the place itself, for the combination that is the animal — it will do one invisible thing on the way out.

Not a wall. There has never been a wall. Not a road, not a wire. It will lay the opposite of those: a slight uninterestingness. To the eyes that fly over looking for ore to take, for heat to tap, for emptiness to fill — the magnetite bands will sing just out of tune. The hum will sound, on instruments, like nothing worth harnessing. The pans will look, from height, like dry salt. Footprints will still fill slowly with beaded water and settle back to balanced ripple, a little faster than before. Gully mouths will keep their same curves.

It will leave it undeveloped in the way only places that never needed developing are.

Then it will go. It will take nothing, not even a chip of sinter, not even the memory of the click-cry heard once far off and not again. It will leave everything: the rust-red grit, the pale sinks blooming and cracking and blooming again into the same pattern, the collar of rust and white salt around each mound, the wet felt smelling of hot oil and blood.

Behind it, the thump will keep time. Under a boulder, something translucent will pulse its throat, once, in rhythm, and wait. The films will hold their borders. Three white lint-lives will dimple the brine without breaking it.

Nothing will have changed. And nothing will have been alone.
