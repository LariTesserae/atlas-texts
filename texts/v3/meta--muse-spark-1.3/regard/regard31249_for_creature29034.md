It would come the way everyone comes: expecting to pity.

From high up, all wise powers first see the white pan, the wrung air, the seven throats wrapped against a roar. The reflex — the benevolent reflex — is simple: *give water. Give cool. Give quiet. Give company to fill those hundred empty nests.*

It would almost do it. And then it would crouch.

Because to notice this place truly you have to get low. You have to turn sideways to fit through the split seam. Even a god has to make itself smaller here. That is the first wisdom.

It leaves its waterskin outside.

Any other desert tabernacle and water would be mercy. Here, the tenders take your skin from you at the threshold, kindly, by signs, and spit carefully outward and wipe their mouths. The Power, being wise, does not argue. It learns the three laws before it learns the names:

Do not bring water inside.
Do not dwell in the center.
Tend the loud.

It obeys.

It does not enter as wind, or fire, or voice — because voice here would be drowned and would drown. The pod at noon is honey-white and deafening, a chest resonating, sternum-deep organ drone under a ceaseless dry rattle of ten million stiff leaves shivering. A wise power could still the wind with a thought and give the seven a moment of ease for their necks and molars. It does not.

It understands: the roar is bread.

So it sits — vastness folded — on a terrace no larger than a sleeping mat, and watches, and learns hand-talk.

It learns that the Being who lives here is not the seven, not the white lizard under the floor planks who is counted a citizen, not the moth who comes once a season wider than a child is tall. The Being is the one in the middle where nothing is built. Where the bridges cross quickly and hands go still and eyes go up. The Note.

The Note was here before the ladders of thornwood pegged with bone pins, before the alleys that turn to let the drone pass, before the first comb was drawn through silver felt-leaf to keep it from matting and going quiet. The town is fretting built around It. The garden is Its player. The people are the hands that keep the instrument in tune.

The plants drink It. Fine white root-hairs reach sideways toward bridge-anchors that hum. Leaves stiffened into tympana shake loose a sugar-dust that coats everything sweet on the back teeth, and reabsorb it. Silver for bass down low where the hum pools. Paper-bells for treble up near the flute-holes where the whistle shears. A choir voiced hollow to live on vibration where water would rot and kill.

A lesser mercy would have said: *this is too hard, too dry, too loud, too empty — let me fix it.* Bring rain. Bring a thousand bodies to fill the nests. Muffle the drone so the tenders can speak in day as well as in hoarse night-whispers on the cool pan.

The benevolent and wise power does none of this.

It sees that emptiness here is not failure. A city built for a thousand holding seven is not abandoned. It is tuned. More meat would starve the sound. More water would silence the felt and invite the wrong green. More quiet would be death.

So if it does anything — and it almost does nothing, which is the hardest work of powers — it does only what belongs:

On nights when the outside air goes slack for too long and the three holes stop pulling and the pod quiets to a murmur and the felt begins to hunger, It breathes, very softly, across the crown. Not a storm. Just enough to keep the chest rising and falling in sleep. Just enough to keep the low note fed until true wind returns.

When a dust-season comes that would scour the amber shell paper-thin, It turns its shoulder into the blast out on the pan, weathered pod sheltered in a lee no instrument could measure, only the oldest tender noticing the daub lasts a little longer this year.

When the great dust-moth is out searching, blind in white dark, It tilts a little moonlight toward a breathing hole, so she finds her way in to lay, and the tithe — half taken, half left — continues.

And once, because benevolence cannot help but touch, It helps re-lash a bridge.

Not with miracle. The vibration saws through rope eventually; that is law. It simply steadies the thornwood while a woman with a bone awl between her teeth re-ties a knot no teeth could untie. She feels the bridge hum right under her bare feet — west two, singing again — and flashes with stained, cracked, obscene-dexterous fingers to the terrace opposite: *good.* The Power, who knows every language that was ever spoken aloud, is proud, absurdly proud, to have understood this one silent sentence.

It does not dwell in the center. It crosses quickly, eyes up, like the others. It knows that place belongs to Another.

At night it goes out with the seven, sideways through the seam, one by one. They sit in a circle on white dust cool for the only hour of the day and talk in hoarse whispers to remember what their voices sound like. The Power, which could fill the sky with choirs, says nothing. It listens. It remembers for them when they forget: the exact rasp of each laugh, the crack.

That is what it does.

It does not rescue the pod. It joins its maintenance.

Before it leaves — for powers must leave, or their attention becomes weight — it leaves behind not water, not people, not silence, but a small, durable resonance of itself in the wall: a node where the drone doubles warmly, where the silver moss will grow a little thicker for bass. The tenders will find it in the morning by pressing the back of a hand to the felt and feeling it sing right. They will comb to it. They will plant to it. Quiet will not take that corner.

If you asked them later, by signs, *did something visit?* they would say: *the loud stayed kind this season. The moth came. The lizard lives.*

And that would be enough.
