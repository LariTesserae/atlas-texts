It would notice them the way you notice a humming tooth.

Not because they are loud. They aren't. In the whole roaring world — oceans, cities, forests shouting green — this pocket is a held breath, seven sighs a day spaced too far apart to make a song. But a benevolent and wise attention hears held breaths best.

It would come close, and then it would stop.

That is the first wisdom. To stop at the lip.

It would not come down in light or thunder. There is already too much honest white light here, laying everything flat. It would come the way heat comes: by being there already when you finally feel it. A thickness in the thin air. A coolness at the back of your neck that is not cool.

It would sit outside the bowl, with its back to the blue drop, and look for a long time. Longer than an afternoon. It would learn the rules by watching, not by asking.

Ah, it thinks. No water. Not broken, but refused.

Its first impulse, being benevolent, would be to give. That is what kindness misunderstands at first: to bring water to the thirsty, company to the lonely, sound to the quiet. It would feel the human prayer rising off any visitor who had ever stood here: *poor things, so dry, so alone, let me rain, let me bring bees.*

And being wise, it would not.

It sees what they are made of. Oil, not sap. Coolant, not blood that knew rain. If it poured a cup, it would kill them with love. If it sent a bird, the bird would peck the corona-tipped shoots and break the slow snowfall of iron pollen that takes ten minutes to cross. If it fixed the shadows — made them fall properly down at noon — it would mend them right out of the thin place they have learned to idle in.

So it rescinds its miracles, one by one.

It will not make them wet. It will not make them fast. It will not make them understood.

What, then, will it do? Almost nothing. Which is the hardest work of gods.

First, it will listen at their speed.

To match them, it must slow itself atrociously down. Its own heart is frantic — suns per second. It banks its fire, lets its attention tick down degree by degree until it can hear the intervals: sigh... *tick of cooling rock*... ping... sigh somewhere else. It counts a whole hot season in the unfurling of one frond, inch by inch, creaking, and does not look away. It learns not to expect chorus. They are never in sync and that is holy.

For one full cycle it breathes when they breathe. When pressure peaks in the farthest crown and the red sheet-metal whorl prepares its slow *clack*, the attention leans in, holds — and lets go — with it. The black smoke tips sideways, pools at knee-height.

The being notices. Out of the corner of its vast eye, the doubled image — one sighing half a second before the other — collapses for a breath into one. A single, present plant, seen fully. To be witnessed without being wanted to be otherwise: they have never had that. They tremble, though there is no wind.

Second, it will give them time, not help.

It sees the real danger, which is not dryness or loneliness. It is cooling. Their soil is heat breathing up through porous stone. The mountain is already giving up on being a mountain. In a thousand years, in ten thousand — a blink — this tooth will go cold, and the pipe-roots hissing where flesh meets furnace will sip nothing. The amber will thicken. The valve-flowers will not peak.

It cannot stop a mountain cooling without breaking the world. It will not flood the chamber with new lava — that would bury them. So it does a smaller, kinder physics: it puts a hand, unseen, under the pocket. It banks the coals. It slows the loss by a fraction, a breath held longer. A few centuries more of idling warmth. A few more slow discharges of ball-bearing seeds tick...tick...ticking in cracks. No one will measure the difference except them, in the way their stems stay warm a little later into each night they have never seen but trust is coming.

Third, it will turn aside the rain.

Already, below, a cloud is gathering itself to try the tooth. Ordinary, well-meaning cloud, full of fresh water that wants to be dew. If it crested the lip it would bead, it would seep, it would scream to vapor and in screaming crack the salts, pit the hydrophobic dust, drown the gill-chambers that breathe only dry steam smelling of hot pennies.

The power does not destroy the cloud. It thanks it. It sends it sideways with a shift in convection — the same convection that is their bee — so it snows, instead, far below, on a slope that wants snow. The pocket stays unwet. The yellow sulfurs stay dry. Nothing here has ever been wet, and nothing here ever will be, while it watches.

And then — because benevolence cannot leave without touching *something* — it will touch one thing, very lightly.

One seed-pod, lodged deepest in a crack of new lava, ticking cool. Most will never unfurl. This one might. The power does not crack it open. Does not hurry it. It just rests its attention on it for a moment the way sun rests on copper veins and holds light a second longer than it should. A blessing of probability: *you, little ironwood ball, you will have your hot season.* It warms it half a degree. Enough.

Then it leaves. Or rather, it withdraws its closeness but not its knowledge. It makes the place a little harder to find — not hidden, just uninteresting to maps. Let the sharp rock spring back. Let the salt resettle. Let the next climber miss the dent if he doesn't already know to look. Obscurity is a mercy the wise know how to give.

An hour after it has gone — if "after" means anything to such a thing — one of the valve-flowers will open with its slow iron clack, vent its sideways smoke, and close again.

Only this time, for the first time in their patient, uneaten lives, all seven — or eight, don't trust the count — will sigh a half-second closer together. Not in sync. Never in sync. Just a little less alone in the spacing.

The lip of rock will hum at its frequency that makes hands feel in two places. And underneath that hum, for years in the cooling tick of the blessed seed, there will be another hum, lower, answering in teeth and sternum:

*Held. Held. Held.*
