It would come with rain.

That is the first thing to know about benevolent powers. They come with what they think is missing in their hands.

It had heard — the way such things hear, which is not with ears but with a sudden attention like a weight on the mat — of a flat place, impossibly wide, bright, dry. No rivers. No soil. No rot. A continent that decided to be a plant that decided to be a building, maintaining itself millimeter by millimeter while the light pooled wrong in its canyons and its halls were bigger inside than outside.

And of the ones living thinly in it. Beetles. Moths. Old humans in moss-paper masks who do not order, only negotiate.

It felt pity, for half a second. Pity is the first mistake of the wise.

So it gathered up oceans to bring. Mercy-water. Cool dark rivers to cut valleys in the flat. Hills, for perspective. Rain that would make things bleed and rot properly again.

And then — because it was actually wise — it stopped at the white fraying edge of the world and looked, slower and drier, before entering.

And unlearned.

It saw this was not a desert. Desert is a lack. This is a decision. Constitutionally, completely dry in a way that has nothing to do with thirst. Their leaves carry dust and light, not sap. Snap one and it cracks to starch and oil-resin and honeycombed air. To bring unbound water here would not be a gift. It would be a flood. It would be rot. The mat drinks all loose moisture, not maliciously, but because it must. An ocean would drown the fungi in the vats, short the coppery stamens under the bark, turn the felt floor to slime, silence the *whump... whump...* through blankets that is its heartbeat.

So the Power put the oceans away.

It sealed itself.

That is what it would do first. To meet the dry, become dry. It bound its own wetness tight inside, the way the tenders do — sealed bottle at the hip, sealed reclaimer, waxed fiberboard-cloth that wicks nothing. It made itself cool and small and refrigerator-breath, smelling only faintly of ozone and cut hay, so it would not be drunk by the air. It did not arrive as a sun — there is already too much light, total white from everywhere, no shadow to hide in except the thin blue ink directly underneath things. It arrived as a shade that did not steal light. A figure on an aisle, walking.

It obeyed the rules. Wise powers can read posted rules faster than anyone.

*Do not look for water.* It didn't. It brought its own and never opened it.

*To build is to prune.* So it built nothing. When it needed shelter from a flowering front, it did not assemble. It knelt by six strap-leaves, as it had seen the old tenders do, tied them back, staked them down, lashed them into a tube that was still alive. It tapped the lashings and hummed low, off-key, so the vibration would loosen the fibers. It left a little spit for the mat to drink in trade. The leaves tightened in the night anyway, curious, and it let them, and in the morning gently reminded them.

*Walk on the aisles, not the fields.* It walked the long straight cuts where dun underfloor shows polished through, warm faintly from engines below through its soles. It felt the mat once try to grow backward under it to keep tension — the chimney-stalks two kilometers away refusing to get closer, the starting scaffold-tree waiting fifty meters behind — and it did not force the distance. It stopped walking and stood still until the mat settled.

*When the flowering front comes, sit down.* It sat down.

That was most of what it did. It sat down in the gold silent air with everyone else and covered its mouth. Not because it needed to. Because the silence deadens even your own breathing, and it wanted to hear the way they hear: the patter of lint landing on leaves — *shhhh* — the faint ticking of electrostatic combs discharging, the distant felt *whump* of the mills continuing underneath it all. Cool motes on its eyelashes. Light moving sideways through dust in a way that has nothing to do with you.

It did not heal the whole continent. The continent was not sick.

What it did was much smaller, and therefore not small.

In one eleven-kilometer module — three scaffold-trees, one vat-tree, a kink in the conveyor-vine sagging in its memorized catenary — a scaffold-tree had a graft scar that had gone wrong a century ago. Bark furrowed, patched, weeping dry powder where a limb had been told to become a roof and had almost obeyed but not quite. The automated pruners cut around it. The tenders pruned around it the way you prune around a bird's nest.

The Power put its sealed hand on it. Felt the sixty-cycle hum through its teeth. Felt the whole-tree shudder as filters clogged with gold dust, then the soft cough as it cleared itself.

It didn't remake the tree. It just stayed with the sore spot for a long dimming, warm palm on gray fluted bark, until the shudder eased. A nurse-log presence. Attention as poultice.

Further on, it met a maintenance beetle as long as a forearm, matte black, placing its six legs deliberately on vine. It was carrying a sucker-cutting to a shredder mouth, patient, silent. It did not look at the Power. The Power, vast enough to count stars, crouched down to aisle level to be blocked, so the beetle would have to walk around it. So it would have to notice it was noticed.

It clicked, very faintly, in the tunnels' language. Not a command. A thanks.

And the white ones. At dimming-time — pearl-gray, not dark — three paper-thin moths the size of a hand folded shut on a light-collector leaf, circuitry veins holding charge. The Power did not give them more light. They have too much. It gave them, cupped in its hands for a minute, a pocket of true darkness, the kind they never get under the luminous dome. So their stored phosphorescence actually mattered. They glowed green-cool inside the dark, opened, shivered, lifted. They already knew where the thatch canyons were, where brightness pools and arrives before it is made, where your shadow enters a crossing a full second before you do. They went there to wait for someone to bring the dark. The Power watched them go.

And the human. One old tender, mask scuffed, gloves stained black with oil-resin, lying on the floor-mat which gives slightly and is surprisingly warm from engines below.

The Power lay down beside him, without towering. Listened to him cough softly behind moss-paper. Did not cure his lungs — lungs that have breathed lint for fifty years cannot be made new without unmaking the fifty years. Instead it breathed with him for a while, sealed breath in mask, *whump... whump...* through blankets, chimney-stalks exhaling *hahhhh* above, plumes flattening against the invisible ceiling.

"You keep it," the Power said. Not aloud. The air is too padded for loud. It said it the way the mat says things, in pressure and warmth. "You keep it millimeter by millimeter, rustle by rustle."

The tender did not answer. People here are thin like moths. Voices would carry. He checked a graft scar by touch, and fell into the unsleeping settle where below the engines think in heat and above the conveyor lines run slow traffic of cool green light, barrel by barrel.

The Power stayed one full cycle. That was its gift and its restraint.

And before it left — because even wise attention has to end or become surveillance — it did two last things that left no wet mark.

First, at the white fraying edge where distance is swallowed, it turned outward. It faced the other hungry powers, the ones who mine warm mineral and thirst for oil and see only a factory left running, and it said, without light show: *Not this one. This one is tended. Pass over.* A veil, not a wall. Ships will misread their instruments for an hour and correct course. Maps will tile wrong. That is enough.

Second, it left a sealed jar. Small, waxed, buried a hand deep in the felt at the edge of an aisle where only a tender who talks to lashings would find it. Inside: a recorded sound of ocean surf, and a smell of wet soil after rain, and a single memory of a hill. Sealed absolutely. Nothing to leak, nothing for the mat to drink. Just a story the tenders can open in their masks on hard dimmings, listen to without getting wet, and seal again. Proof that wet exists elsewhere and does not have to come here to be real.

Then it left, still sealed, still dry, lighter by having done almost nothing.

If you walk that module now, you won't see a miracle. The tiling still repeats gold-green-gold-green to the horizon. The sorting hall in the trunk is still bigger inside than outside, still full of slow arms lifting press-fruits with dull thuds. The rake-vines still comb leaves into windrows for the shredder mouths chewing like pages turning far away.

But the scar-tree holds its roof a little easier. One beetle pauses a half-second longer when blocked, as if expecting thanks. The moths have a new dark place to remember. And one old tender tells the young ones — rare, rare young ones — about the night the air sat down beside him and hummed off-key to loosen a tent, and how the highest thing he ever met did not bring water, did not bring hills, did not make noise.

It just kept tension with them, for a while.
