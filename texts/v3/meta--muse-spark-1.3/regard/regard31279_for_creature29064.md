It would come down the way you do.

Not in glory. On its backside, boots braced against scalloped limestone, through ten meters of dark that smells of rain that fell a month ago. It would kink where the fissure kinks. It would learn, quickly, to stoop.

That is the first thing wisdom would do: make itself small enough to fit in an egg sixty paces from water to crown. Holdable entire in the mind. Any power that stayed large could not enter without breaking it.

It would sit a long time on a pile platform over the black water and just listen, until the drum-roar stopped being noise.

Tick tick tick of the knapper. Tap tap tap of copper. Shave shave of hide. The swallowed tick of drips that refuse to ripple. The cough bounced back half a second later slightly larger. It would learn to lip-read, because over that weeping ceiling you have to push to be heard. It would learn to tilt its head the way they do, listening with the whole face.

And it would understand, in its teeth before it could name it: this is not a problem.

So it would not fix it.

It would not dry their jackets. The damp is their skin now, fingertips ridged and puckered, hair clinging. To take the weep from the rock would be to unmake them.

It would not silence the dome. The children learned not to cry at it within weeks of birth. The old ones navigate by it. Silence would be deafness.

It would not bring the sun down the throat. No green belongs here. The blind white fish, two inches long, hanging motionless then darting — their eyeless pits are perfect. The candle-wax crabs picking deliberate as monks along the waterline. The impossibly leggy cricket on the curtain. The pale slime that lives on stone-breath alone. The rust-red films on the submerged ledges. To flood this with light to cure a pallor would be murder.

It would not straighten their backs. They are a glide-crouch people for a reason. Uphill presses flat, downhill sidles, water-carriers always have right of way. The smoke hangs about head-height over the water, blue-grey, never thickening, never clearing. Even a god must duck under it or stand above it. So it would duck. It would kneel on the six-inch ledge with the rope handrail and press flat to let an old woman pass with a stone jar she can carry in the dark without spilling.

It would not enlarge the world. They have learned its size and decided to keep it exactly. A wall slumps, by morning it is rebuilt stone for stone, same lean. A fat-fire guts a house, it is rebuilt on the same footprint, same hook left of the door. The bones of the dead washed in pool water and built into a windbreak, a step. You step on an ancestor going to borrow salt. There is nowhere else to put them. There is nowhere else to be. To offer them room would be to offer them exile.

A foolish kindness would bring kilns and timber and electric light and medicine that lets every child live to breed children and break the one perfect accounting. Seep equals drink equals evaporation. In equals out. Still even. A foolish kindness would love them to death in three generations: rising water, fouled pool, empty nets, collapse.

So what would a wise benevolence do, if anything?

Maintenance. The same work they do.

It would do its keeping on the outside, where they cannot reach.

Keep the moor empty. Keep the wind in the heather and the sheep walking over the hole you could step over. Keep the bruised grey disc no bigger than a tabletop open, but unremarkable. Let satellites pass. Let hikers piss near but not into the throat. Turn the surveyor's ankle a half mile off. Make the developer dream uneasy and choose the other valley.

Keep the rain that fell a month ago still falling, up above and behind. As the world warms and dries and forgets, keep the thousand pores sweating. Keep the overhangs fattening and detaching on the left side where the children know to hold the jar. Keep the threads finding the old flutes faithfully. Keep the mist that your breath thins into.

Keep the rope honest. When the sacks go down with grain and dried meat and coils of withy and tallow at great cost, when the pressure-flaked chert and cold-hammered hooks and white salt that blooms in the upper cups go up, keep the hands on both ends fair. Nudge the trader on the surface not to cheat the weight. Nudge the climber's boot a half-inch to the good hold on the way back down damp for days.

And inside — almost nothing. Which is everything.

On nights when the steady autumn-shade cool has gotten too deep into the joints and stays there, it would move, unseen, along the middle terrace and ease a shoulder, a knee, just enough for sleep. Not a cure. A loosening. They would wake and think: I slept well despite the drip. And get up and rebuild the wall.

When the tallow dips gutter in the damp and make more smoke than light and have to be tended constantly, it would lean close and steady the flame a breath longer, so the woman mending a bowl with three generations of copper-wire lace can see the drill-hole. Not daylight. Just a cleaner cup of light.

When the fat-fire comes — brief and terrible in such press — it would hold its breath with them, keep the smoke low, keep the panic low, keep the hands passing water jars hand to hand until it is out, and then help, invisibly, lift the salvaged lintel back into place.

When someone dies in a one-room house where neighbors can hear each other swallow, and privacy is kindly inattention, it would be the one who pays full attention. It would sit in the press of warm bodies in cool air smelling of wet rock and crowded sleep, and stay with the narrowing eyes that resolve a face at five paces and don't bother beyond ten, until they close. It would not take them elsewhere. There is nowhere else. It would go with them into the high niche, wait while the damp does its slow work, and when the bones are washed and laid as a step, it would let itself be stepped on, gladly.

Each dawn that is not a dawn, only a paling of the disc, it would be there when the old woman takes the marked stick down to the black water. She would dip it between the pale bathtub ring above and the dark stain below, and announce what everyone already knows.

Still even.

And the power, stooping under the smoke with wet shoulders and ringing ears, lip-reading more than listening, would answer with them, under its breath, in perfect maintenance theology:

Still even.

In equals out. Nothing improved. Everything kept.

That is what it would do.
