It would not come as brightness.

Brightness is already here, and too much of it. It would not come as thunder, or as green, or as a voice through the roar. It knows better. To be benevolent here you must first learn the rules with your body, the way you did:

No softness.
No quiet.
No green.
No change.

It would learn them faster than you, and keep them better.

So it would come the way water comes: low, and spread thin.

At first, from the height of the ridges, it would only look. That is not nothing. Attention at that scale is a physical force. But it would not stare the way the sun stares, white and merciless, boiling any eye that looked back. It would shade.

Imagine a cloud that does not darken, a hand held a kilometer high over the whole basin, letting the light through but taking the glare off. For a minute — for a year, time means nothing — it would just watch the glitter resolve: a million clear backs drinking light, a million bellies full of cold flow, a lacework of hisses and ticks under a lid of rock that thrums.

It would see the keepers. Not as a curiosity. As a practice perfected.

And being wise, it would feel the urge to help rise in itself and it would interrogate that urge until it went clear.

For what would help mean, here?

To quiet the roar for them? But they are tuned to the roar. Their whole rim is ear, their whole life is pressure-map. Quiet would blind them.

To feed them? But they drink almost-nothing — silica, iron, manganese, sun in water — and have made a liturgy of almost-nothing. To enrich the film would green it, and green would burn here, and be seen. Algae would mat their mouths. Moss would pry their seals.

To give them eyes, ears, legs, to free them from the single slab? That would be a murder disguised as mercy. A life is a single slab. The young creep grain by grain upstream until they find an unkept lip. Once seated they do not leave. To make them wander would unmake them.

To spare them death? But they die into place. There is no corpse. When the jelly thins the dome cements solid and becomes indistinguishable from grit. Half the pavement you stand on is geology and half is graveyard, and both hold water the same way. To lift death out would lift the pavement.

So it would do what only a very old benevolence can do: it would refrain.

It would not improve them.

What it would do instead is smaller, and harder.

It would get down.

Not with feet — feet are too heavy, boots soaked through and numb, a temporary weight the basin will not shift for. It would distribute itself. A pressure so even across block on block on block that no slab wants to lift, no joint wants to widen. It would lay its palm, vast and cold enough to ache, against the thrumming lid, and it would listen through bone.

Through the rim-feed of a million small tuners it would read what they read: where the buried river pushes hardest underneath, where a micron of cement is needed on this side, a micron dissolved on that side, to keep the flow exactly the same. It would not do the work for them — that would rob them — but it would hold the larger work around them, the work they cannot reach.

Upstream, where the snowfields feed the under-rivers: it would keep the melt steady while the world warms and stutters. Not frozen — wise does not freeze — but eased. A little more shade in a hot year. A little more snow held back to release slow. So the pitch does not jump.

Down-ridge, where human maps grow curious: it would make the basin boring. Let the eye slide off. Let the survey plane find cloud. Let the paper that describes the quartzite cite a valley over. Let the trail wash out one switchback early. Not a wall — walls invite climbing — just an uninterestingness, a dullness laid like dust, so boots do not come in hundreds to wobble what one boot barely dents.

And when a boot does come — yours, mine — it would kneel with the kneeler. Shade the lip with its hand so the eye can adjust. Let you see the seal, the millimeter of darker-when-wet-almost-to-blue that is a back. It would not speak in your ear; you could not hear through the roar anyway. It would speak in the slowing of your watching, in the willingness to watch one drip fall and fall and fall without wearing its lip deeper while you watch.

If a dome is crushed — because even reverent knees slip on slick flow — it would not resurrect. It would warm the grit around for a season so a young clear one, drifting a grain's width a year, finds the unkept lip sooner. That is the scale of its miracles: a grain's width.

It would not forget the under-ones. The pale blind long turners in the dark braided current you only feel as a slower pressure passing inside the sameness. It would not drag them up to be seen. To belong to the under is holy. It would roll with them once, without ever striking, to keep the bass constant. Then leave them.

Mostly, it would keep time.

Because the equilibrium was not found. It is kept. And keeping is lonely work when no one knows it is work. The white bird goes over. The spider goes over. The snowfly goes over. Even you cross. They remain. The place does not acknowledge you because acknowledgment is not its habit.

It would acknowledge them in their own idiom: not with a sign, but with constancy.

At long intervals — minutes, an hour — the ones on the pool rims lift a fraction, let a new skin in, settle. Ring spreads and dies against stone. Then nothing. Then again. The pool does not ripple beyond that because they do not allow it. It would breathe with them. In. Settle. Out. Settle. So exact that no instrument you brought could tell its breath from theirs, except that the fixed tremor holds a fraction longer, steadier, less alone.

If you lay flat and blocked the sun and watched long past comfort, you might feel it: the moment when the cold gets into your bones despite the blaze and does not hurt quite so much. The stone still cold enough to ache, still slick, but the ache evens out. As if something else were also cold, on purpose, with you.

That is its blessing. Not *be changed.* But *continue.*

And one day — because wise knows stasis is not eternity, erosion only agreed to stop at this exact arrangement for a while — the ridges will tilt a degree. The buried river will find a new push. A house-sized hornblende boulder at the margin will shift. The pitch will break. No keeping, theirs or its, will hold that note forever.

When that day comes, it will not cling to the museum of sameness. Benevolence is not taxidermy. It will stay for the breaking. It will grieve in flood, not in abstraction. It will carry the clear young downstream on a film a fraction of a millimeter deep to whatever new lip the new water makes, and teach them, by being water-shaped, how to hand the same water to the next, generation lip to lip down a new run, until a new roar finds a new exactness.

Until then: no lifting of slabs. No parting of roar. No green.

Only this: a vast, deliberate weight, fitted so tight you couldn't slip a knife between it and the world, weeping with the rock, glittering with the million backs, tuning nothing but its own attention to one exact pitch and volume from minute to minute, hour to hour.

So they are not unwitnessed in their keeping.

So the stillness is tended, and the tending is tended.
