It would not come like a god.

No light in the sky that was not already there. No voice. No shadow falling across the snowfields. The light here is already too bright to improve, and the cold too complete to argue with.

It would come the way the cold seeps in. Gradually.

First, It would learn how to arrive without making a gap.

That is harder than it sounds for a power. Powers displace. Where they step, room is made. Here, room is not allowed. Shoulder to shoulder, flank to flank, back to back — if you push one barrel-body aside to kneel, the whole tissue tenses around the wound.

So It would make Itself small. Not small like hidden. Small like honest. A warmth the size of a body. A weight the size of a body. It would step, very slowly, between the heat-pads where the runners run, and lie down on the young glass where the moss is already waiting.

And It would slow down.

That would be the first miracle, and no one would see it. For a being who can think a planet round in a breath to take a full minute to complete a step. To take ten seconds to open and close. To chew. To breathe in that thin blue air through borrowed nostrils and feel how it burns going down, how the furnace-gut has to work to make it worth it.

It would lie there while the pistons pumped around It.

*In — hold, heat — out.*

Steam jetting sideways from gill-slits, icing the moss for a yard around. The hiss and clank and sigh. The millstones grinding twenty yards off, stone on stone, teeth honing themselves on basalt grit. The click-ratchet of the striders overhead, the faint blue lift of static raising the hairs. The hydraulic churn inside the domes. The bass drone in the sternum that does not peak or resolve.

It would not try to listen for change. It would let it occupy Its body.

And the herd, which budgets everything warm, would budget It.

A runner would detour to press hard against Its calf, stealing heat. It would let it be stolen. Condensation would bead on Its coat. A spore hanging lit in the noon light — that light with weight, dust and ice and breath glittering and casting shadows upward — would settle in Its shoulder seam and unfurl, bright green, toward the sun. It would not brush it away.

A grazer beside It would sigh moss and shift an inch to close the gap Its body made. It would let itself be closed in.

For a hundred years, or a minute — for powers it is the same — It would join the line.

Not lead it. Join it.

It would let the chitin soften between Its shoulder and the iron-grey plate next to It. It would let a bridge grow, thick as an arm, pulsing blood and hot oil, and feel the enormous slow heartbeat coming down the line of seven from far away ripple through all of them, through It. It would take a mouthful, ferment it in borrowed heat, bring it back up steaming, lean an inch to the left and empty it into the next mouth. It would take a day to move it ten feet toward a dome. It would not hurry. Hurry is the one thing this place has correctly refused.

It would understand, lying there with lichen hot on Its back despite the freezing air, that there is nothing to fix here.

Nothing is built, ever, no tool, no wall, no cut mark — and yet everything is milled, pressed, vented, conveyed. No owner. No product except more packed, breathing life. No outside. To bring them a gift from outside — language, or fire, or mercy as *we* mean mercy — would be to tell them they are missing something. They are not missing anything. They are saturated.

What they are is exposed. That high table of fresh basalt still ticking as it contracts, that thin sheet of air, that white unfiltered sun — it all holds, for now. But powers know how easily tables break. How easily a sky thins, or a sun cools, or a well-meaning other comes with cutters to harvest the self-sharpening teeth, the furnace guts, the spinning vanes.

So what It would do, if you need a verb, is *keep*.

Not keep them. Keep *for* them.

Keep the planet young underneath them, so new cracks still boom open at the white horizon and lift fresh hexagonal columns still steaming from birth for them to chew for ballast. Keep the far peaks snowy so the glare throws back up from below and the black rosettes can make their microclimates. Keep the air thin and cold enough that ten million breaths a minute still make their own weather, still bead and freeze into lace for the ice-pickers to drink. Hold the hollow of the world steady while the work of staying warm, staying fed, staying packed, continues at its patient, deafening, freezing, brilliant pace.

And keep others away. Not with wrath. With misdirection. A little fog in the charts. A little *nothing to see here* in the minds of miners and missionaries and even other benevolences who want to uplift. Let this one tissue stay unuplifted.

That is almost nothing. That is almost everything.

When It finally rose — because even benevolence cannot stay without becoming a dam — It would rise very slowly, inch by inch, so the grazer beside It could sigh and shift and close the place where It had been. No gap left. Gaps are not allowed.

Underneath, where It had lain, there would be a long black steaming rope of composted heat, like what comes out of the domes. And on it, already waiting, bright green shoots unfurling.

It would leave no name, no straight line that was made, no ruin. Only a little extra warmth in the line, still rippling down seven bodies, fading very, very slowly into the drone.

And long after, if you stood on the rim in the vibrating light and pressed your palm down until water welled cold around your fingers, you might feel it: not a voice, not a hand. Just the sense, knee-high in glowing fog that smells of hot metal and peat, flank to flank with ten million furnaces, that you have been noticed —

and left whole.
