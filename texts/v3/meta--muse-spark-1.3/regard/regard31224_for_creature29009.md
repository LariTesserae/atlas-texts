They would not lift the bark any higher.

That is the first thing. Anyone else would want more light, a better look. The wise kind knows light is a knife down here. Blue leak around the edge already hurts. A full sky would dry the water-skin off backs, kill the foxfire, unmake the black air they breathe.

So the Power would learn to see the way they see. Not with eyes. With cheek to peat. With tremor.

It would come low. Very low. As low as vastness can go without breaking the dome.

Not as a hand. Hands crush. Not as a voice. Voices shake the drip loose too early. It would come as weather held back. As attention with weight but no pressure.

It would kneel in the sphagnum where you kneel, and feel the mattress give, and not shift its knee. Your knee is already a god-footstep to them. Its knee would be a season. So it holds still. Benevolence, at their scale, is mostly stillness that does not freeze.

And it would look in, a finger's width, for a long time. Long enough for the almost-nothing to have shape again. Long enough to learn the town not as boil but as persons.

That porter with the pollen grain bigger than her head. That mite polishing the road with his belly. That larva pulsing through yesterday to make tomorrow. That rust-red huntress tasting air with her front legs. That false-scorpion with claws wider than his body. Even the flatworm, blind and white. Even the nematode threading wetness.

It would not pick favorites. Wisdom can't afford to. If it saved the porter from the huntress, it would starve the huntress. If it melted the needle-ice, it would drown the store-sacs. If it stopped winter, it would stop the glycerol thickening in their blood, stop the slowing that lets them live till spring. If it gave them fire, everything too wet too cold to burn, it would give them ash.

So it does not fix death. The walls are made of ancestors for a reason. There is no elsewhere to put them. The Power knows that. It has seen larger towns build larger walls of the same material.

What it does is smaller, and total:

It steadies the roof.

While you watch with your numb fingers, the bark sighs under snow-melt and shrew-feet and your own breathing. The Power puts the lightest part of itself *over* — not touching, just a shade of warmth that is not warmth — so the drip does not come too fast or too seldom. *Plik... plik.* Leaf to peat. Peat to root. A rhythm they can build a life around. Water still near, not flood, not drought. The moss tips rimed white and still growing straight through the frost — it lets them keep growing. A millimeter a day. Enough to lift the town as it walks.

It thickens the night.

Cloud-sealed, black-peat night is their shelter. The Power asks the snow, when it decides to stay, to come soft and stay long. Snow is the second bark. Under it, -2 stays -2 instead of swinging to -20 and cracking them. It will not keep the shrew from passing — the shrew must eat too — but it will turn the shrew's hunger half a step to the left, to a vole-run full of other hungers, so this one dome does not take the whole footfall tonight.

And for the being — because there is always one, among thousands, who pauses —

There is always one porter who stops on the glossy street between pillars, antennae up, tasting something that is not food-this-way or danger-that-way. A change in the air pressure. A taste of iron and turpentine and something sweet that was not there before. Your exhale raining back. Its exhale, if it could.

The Power does not speak to her. Speech is smell laid on the road, vibration through stem. So it lays itself down as those: a faint cut-grass sweetness on her antennae that means *go on.* A steadiness in the stem under her feet that means *hold.*

Not *you are seen.* That would be too large to carry. Just: *the road holds. The joint sealed green will hold. The cold you feel thickening your blood is right. Keep chewing upright what the ice toppled.*

It stays with her while she crosses between vast trunks, while the red blur sleeps, while the drip swells on the bark lip and holds the rot-glow like a lens and lets go.

*Plik.*

The town glistens, and goes on — and for once, going on is witnessed all the way down, without being taken.

Then, because it is wise, it leaves no trace. Except in you.

You with your cheek in the peat, your breath fogging and dripping back onto your lip. You feel, suddenly and unreasonably, that you should not lift higher, should not breathe harder, should not stay much longer. That you should set the spruce slab back down *exactly* where the moss remembers it, edge to edge, to seal the pocket of air that should not be there but is. That you should lift your knee slowly, so the dome sighs instead of gasps. That you should walk away from the low bog and tell no one where to lift.

You think it is your own kindness. It is. It is also Theirs, moving through the only hands here large enough to replace a roof.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it notices a thimble-room cathedral full of white backs in the cold before snow:

Almost nothing. Almost everything.

It keeps the dark dark. It keeps the wet near. It keeps the winter honest. And it keeps, for one small porter on one glossy street, the world steady enough for work to be warmth.
