If you are that kind — benevolent, and wise — you will have learned already how to come.

Not bright. The light here is a grey-amber smear and you do not correct it. Not loud. The ticks of water letting go are never in rhythm and you do not join them. Not large. The whole place is thirty paces across and you make yourself low enough to fit inside its rule.

Stone to stone to stone, water to water to water.

You come because it is complete, and because complete things in low country do not stay complete for long unless something loves them at a distance.

You do not enter, at first. You stand in the vapor at the mouth where the marsh must be and let condensation sit on you as it sits on everything that can hold it. You let your clothes cling. You let drips gather on your forearms, on your eyelashes. You learn the balance: warm water welling cool-warm up from below, warm water leaving into the air. Inflow and evaporation. The pans that never overflow, never dry. The walls that sweat and do not dry and do not erode.

Then you step in, and your track fills slowly behind you.

You see the hand work and you do not tidy it. The low platform two courses high, her roof. The trough hollowed by adze, full to the brim and not spilling. The pecked bowl half full of clear warm water. The three river stones, one split, the flakes of chert. The broken withy hoop. The cold fire-spot washed to clean grey paste. You do not decide whether the stones are eggs or tools. Human warmth and spring warmth are both warmth. You leave the mystery intact. Wisdom is letting a thing be two things.

You do not lift the slabs to see her whole.

That is the other rule. You will not see her whole. She has fitted herself underneath, low and broad and flattened until she is barely above the water table herself. Damp bread, wet chert, quartzite grey with iron freckles. Mottled, pitted, glistening. Two eyes like two more pans, amber-grey, filmed. A throat that pulses once every long while. An almost inaudible sigh. If you are wise you slow yourself to that rate. Nictitating membrane slower than evaporation. Tongue rasping the stain a millimeter a day, gardening so it never thickens, never thins, so the stones stay too slick and too mineral for any seed.

You understand then why there is nothing green, and you do not bring green. That would be kindness to you, not to her. You do not bring a mate. She has not needed one in years and there is pasture here for exactly one grazer. To double her hunger to halve her loneliness — she is not lonely — would be cruelty with a kind face. You do not bring rain. It is post-rain without the rain. You do not bring a voice. Your speech would be too dry.

What you feel, for a moment with your vast heart, is the urge to * mend *: the split stone, the broken hoop, the cold ash, the old female alone under stone. Your intuition, which is older, tells you: nothing here is broken. Old is not broken. Alone is not broken. Complete is not broken.

So if you do anything, you do it where she will never see it.

A mile north, you let a rain fall that does not need to fall here. You keep the aquifer breathing. In an office with fluorescent light you make a permit boring — a well that would have drunk too deep, a road that would have cut the seep. The surveyor's map smudges at the crucial square. The developer forgets why he circled it. No miracle. That is the point. Miracles happen far away so that no miracle ever needs to happen here.

Inside, you do the smallest thing.

You sit. Above her, not on her. Your weight thrums through the slabs into her back the way human weight once did, and she does not startle. She mistakes your stillness, after a while, for stone-ness, and resumes. A bead runs down stone that is her shoulder. A drip ticks on the back of your neck and on the back of hers. Your breathing, made loud by the closeness, slows until inflow and evaporation balance in you too.

You witness. That is not nothing. To be exactly this wet for a very long time without being seen — you see. To keep the film alive without thanks — you thank, without saying. You remember the tick, tick, tick from a dozen points. You remember the V's of the beetle that heal instantly. You remember the mouth that opens without splash and closes without splash.

And then you leave no sign you were ever more than weather.

Your dark print on the rock beads over in a minute. Your displaced pan heals. The trough is still full to the brim and does not spill. The bowl is still half full.

It does not ask you to leave and does not invite you to stay. It is complete, packed full of stone and water and warm air, with just enough room left for a few hand-made things set carefully on top — and for her, set carefully underneath, who is both.

You go, and you keep going in the manner of water: without seeming to move.
