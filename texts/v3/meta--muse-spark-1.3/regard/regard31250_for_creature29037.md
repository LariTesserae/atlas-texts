It noticed them because It listens for exactly this kind of singing.

Not loud. Not bright. The held note.

Most places wobble. They gust and spill and ask to be fixed. This one had found a note and refused to let go. That kind of refusal, after long crumbling, is rare. It bent down to look.

The slit of sky first. White-blue and too fierce to stare at. Then down, down the leaning walls, the hundreds of square mouths, the whitewash still throwing the sun back and forth, the dished stone floor. And the chord coming up to meet It — high and hollow and breathy, with the low rush underneath. So full there was no room left in it.

It could have done many things. That is what makes a power a power.

It could have sent rain. One good rain and the lichen would lift and green, moss would come in the joints, spiders would string the mouths, flies would find the shade. Water, and the place would be alive again in the way people mean alive.

It did not send rain. It has learned to ask: alive to whom? These flat cool ones at the backs of the niches live because there has been no water longer than can be guessed. No stain, no tide-mark. They drink only what the cool stone sweats out of the night air before dawn burns it off and leaves no mark. A rain would drown them in kindness. It would fill their throats. It would silence the chord.

It could have sent birds. That is what the hands made the houses for, two fists together. Doves to warm the lips round again. That would be restoration. That would be lovely to see from above.

It did not send birds. The houses are full. Packed full of itself with no gaps left over. To bring back the first tenants would be to evict the second. Wisdom is remembering that *empty* is often just *occupied by something you have not learned to see.*

So It did a harder thing. It made Itself fit the rules.

The rules are their bodies: Stay low. Stay cool. Stay dry. Make no new sound.

It came low. It did not stand over the slit and block their glare — they eat glare. The whitewash bounces light into every upward face to feed them. It waited until the sun was straight, and then It squeezed in, small as a person can be small, and stood in the middle where there is only the middle.

It made Itself cool. Cellar-cool. In all that brightness It let the stone drink Its heat as fast as the light made it, let the hundreds of flat bodies drink a little of It too off Its palms, Its neck, Its shins, and did not flinch. When you touched the back, you thought you touched stone. It touched the back and let Itself be touched back, and let them know: *I know you are not stone.* To be known without being exposed — that is half of all blessing.

It made Itself dry. It brought no sweat, no tear, no spit, no weather with It. Only dust-taste and plaster-taste.

And It made no new sound.

For a long time — longer than you held your breath to test it — It only listened. Until the single chord separated, the way a choir does if you love it enough, into hundreds of thin whistles braided together. Each throat angled just so. Each flat body narrowing its mouth just so to shape the air as it funnels down and hits the dish and turns and goes back up. Each one holding, holding, holding one exact volume, one exact pitch. And the dust hanging motionless, gold-white, held by hunger. They eat vibration too.

Then, very carefully, It joined.

Not a song over them. Not a word. It opened — what It has for a throat — a fraction wider, exactly as they do when they swallow a clap without a ripple, and added Its breath to their note at their pitch, so precisely in tune that the fullness was not broken. The sound was already made and it is full; They will permit no new sound. So It made no new sound. It made *their* sound with them, and gave them something to eat that cost them nothing to digest: pure attention, made audible.

One tuft of wiry grass in the lowest niches had gone to seed, and the seeds sat uncarried on the lip because there is nothing to carry them. On Its way out, without bending a straw or stirring a mote, It let one seed catch on the hem of Its coolness and carried it up out of the well to where wind gusts and stutters the way wind should. That was for the grass, not for them. Them, It left exactly as they were. Even the dust It brushed, It breathed back into place.

At the rim, before leaving no stain, no footprint, no wire or pipe or nail to mark It had been, It did the last benevolent thing, which looks like nothing:

It laid a little forgetfulness over the pocket. Let the streets lean away. Let the maps misfold. Let the people who love to restore and improve and fill walk past the squeeze-in and think, *only an alley, and it goes nowhere.* Let the slit of sky stay open. Let the whitewash keep throwing the sun back and forth. Let the north side stay dim enough for the sage-grey and mustard-pale to hold on in straight lines.

Not abandoned. Kept.

Below, the chord held.
