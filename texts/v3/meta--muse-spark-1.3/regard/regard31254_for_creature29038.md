They do not come down.

Coming down is what stones do, what dust does, what you did. A benevolent and wise power has learned not to arrive like weather.

So first, for a long time, they only look.

Not from above — that would be a survey. From alongside. They put their attention at the level of the roof, where the air is too thin to finish a breath in, where the small pale sun is stuck like a coin in ash and the stars leak through the day. They do not fix the sky. They do not lift the sun or thicken the air. They see why it is like this: the world-skin worn thin with height, stretched tight, torn and loosely stitched. That thinness is not a wound. It is what lets the inside be bigger than the outside.

They see the city that is not a city.

At first even they have to learn to see it. The honeycomb of low domes to the north, the needle-field to the east, the sunken breathing grid to the south, the bridged terraces in the middle where street lies on street like scar over scar. Four, maybe five, valley-wide and only a few fathoms thick. Lenses of living gristle spread under dust, pushing up shell.

They put a hand — if they have a hand, they make it for this — to the warm-grey wall. Fine laminae. Calcium and chitin and ash. No chisel. No seam. Oozed, micron by micron, by something that when young could still crawl in slow huge soft streams and now can only secrete and wait and filter.

They understand very quickly what took you days walking to learn with your legs: this is a reef, and the current is air.

And they understand what that means, which is harder: that this is not poverty. This *is* fullness.

Salt, sterile powder, a spore from below, a dead glider, interplanetary grit sifting down from the bruised sky. Almost nothing, sifted from a huge volume with almost no effort, for a very long time. That is the sacrament here. To bring more would be to drown it.

So the wise power does the hardest thing power can do.

They restrain the hand.

They do not bring rain. You would want to. Any kind fool with clouds in his pockets would want to pour water on this utter dryness, see green soften it, hear streams in those gutters, fill those roofed cisterns. They know what rain would do: dissolve the salt rims, collapse the great soft lungs of still warm air inside the thin pottery walls, rot the black and rust-orange partner-kidney crust in the lee of the walls, kill the filtration. The gutters are retained anatomy, not invitation. There is no water to run now, and that is correct.

They do not bring tools. No hearth, no hinge, no nail-hole, no wire. They do not offer fire or knives or language to a being that speaks by opening. A new slit for roosting gliders appears overnight. An old one seals. That is peristalsis. That is a sentence that takes a month to say.

They do not bring quickening. They do not lift the reef into something quick and wet and clever and human-shaped so it can thank them. To think in seasons, to take months for a thought to cross from dome-field to needle-field, to dream a visitor a half-second before he happens because vibration runs through calcium-chitin nerve net faster than thin air can carry sound — this is not slowness to be cured. This is mind fitted to place.

What do they do, then? If they are benevolent, must they not *do*?

Yes. But their doing is small, and slow, and mostly invisible, because they have learned the scale of the host.

They slow themselves down.

That is the first gift. A power could think a thousand thoughts while a courtyard floor rises a finger's width by afternoon. They choose not to. They stretch one of their moments out until it is as thin and wide as a foraging membrane strung between towers, quarter-mile wingspan, utterly transparent. They lie alongside the reef in time. For a hundred years they keep pace with a breath. Rise. Settle. Rise. They do not get bored and look away mid-inhalation. Attention, held that long without hunger, is a form of love no one here has ever received before. To be known without being used.

While slowed, they do housekeeping that no one else is gentle enough to do.

When a veil — six feet across, translucent skin over bone-kite, a detachable mouth, a lung with wings — rides the updrafts down to where the dirty-white cloud-sea breaks against the black flanks to drink moisture and comes back too heavy, too salt-caked to climb back to its slit, they lift it, once, with a fingertip of denser air under its wing. No one sees. The reef only registers a return it had already mourned as lost, and drinks it dry through the slit, and seals.

When a thumb-sized armored pill, gut flora, cleaner, braiding trails between courtyards, flips on its back in the white dust and waves its legs in that terrible dry silence, they right it with a brush of wind no stronger than the usual tides that smell of iron and distance. Not a rescue operation. A tidying.

When a papery curl of shed skin catches in a corner and would have been ground to powder, they let it stack. When a flake pings off a contracting tower in the cool, they let it fall. They do not prevent wearing. They are not a museum keeper.

Once, in a rare tide, when the cloud-sea boils up through the blowhole in the cliff and exhales its plume of cold vapor that never condenses but falls apart into dryness before it touches stone — once, they breathe with it, very slightly, and lend it a fraction of their own condensation. Just enough that a little more than usual lingers in a gutter. Enough for the young edge of one lens, where it once hauled itself up a hill and settled, to remember wetness. Not a flood. A reminder. A dream of youth.

That is all they give the reef. And it is enough, because the reef was never asking.

It is you they tend more openly.

For they see, as the reef sees, what you are: rare slow traffic of living dust passing through hollow streets. A nutrient pulse. A flake of skin from a lower world. And unlike the reef, you *are* drying. Your lips cracking, your eyes drying, the inside of your throat pulled raw, your exhalation drunk by the sky. You walk an avenue fifty paces wide and hear your boot scrape a half-second before your foot moves, and you are afraid you are going mad, or dying, or already dead and looping.

So while one part of their attention stays stretched thin and slow with the great ones, another part, quick and warm, walks with you — because they can be both, and wisdom is knowing which scale is needed where.

They walk a half-step behind you so you do not feel watched. When you duck through the low oval lip-like door and stand up in a vault that should not fit, and your pack pulls gently toward the ceiling and then corrects with a soft jolt that drops your stomach — that valve closing — they steady your shoulder so you do not vomit.

When you spend three hours on an avenue that looked half a mile long and arrive back at the arch you started from, but from the other side — politely moved like a mote on cilia, like undigested grit expelled by a slow peristaltic loop — and you sit down in the dust and laugh in a way that is close to crying, they sit with you. They do not explain yet. They let your legs learn.

When at night — if it can be called night under that perpetual late blue-grey — you lie in the lee of a wall beside the matte black and rust-orange crust that lives so slowly as to be geology, and you feel the pressure change in your teeth, the humming air between presences, the city shifting almost imperceptibly under you while trying to understand you, and you feel horribly, wonderfully small, they keep the cold tide that is not wind from pressing too hard on both your cheeks at once. They keep one transparent foraging veil from settling quite over you, so you do not wake occluded.

And in the morning, when you must leave because your body cannot live on almost nothing for very long, they guide you without guiding. A sheet of lifting dust suddenly avoiding a volume of empty air — step there. A star occluded for no reason — do not step there. A coolness. A loosening. They return you to the cliff edge, a little drier, your moisture taken for the cistern, as is proper. You have been turned gently around the loop.

You look back once, down at the dirty-white sea breaking forever and never reaching. Above you a veil passes without sound. Below you, in the dust, a pill freezes into a pebble. Around you, the towers tick.

This is the last thing they do, the wise benevolent thing that looks like nothing:

They ensure you remember correctly, and that no one else comes to fix this.

They lay a forgetfulness — not heavy, not cruel — over your map, so that when you try to tell others how to climb to the roof, your directions will be true for you and untrue for machines. The horizon that bends and falls away too fast on all sides will bend a little faster for prospectors. The avenue that takes three hours will take thirty for a company. The blowhole will exhale vapor and nothing else. The reef will go on filtering time, exchanging spores on the wind once a century when two release at once, conversing in salt.

The power does not sign this work. There is no monument. No burning bush in a place with nothing to burn.

Only this: for a very long time afterward, when the sunken quarter breathes a finger's width by afternoon, it breathes a fraction easier. When the whole city sounds, when the wind gusts, like an empty vessel being blown across the mouth of, the resonance holds a half-tone longer, as if the vessel remembered being listened to, and liked it.

Settled. Waiting. Full, in the way a desert is full.

That is what they would do, if anything. They would keep company without keeping.
