It comes low.

That is the first mercy. It does not come from above, in light or thunder. The valley is already too loud for thunder to mean anything. It comes low, at knee-height, where the warm air has settled and stayed, wading in through the green the way you wade into surf — slowly, letting itself be touched first.

It does not bring tools. No stone. No metal. No cut timber. It knows better. Anything dead and hard you bring here will be sheathed by evening and used as a pylon.

It brings shade, and stillness, and weight that does not eat.

---

For a long time it does nothing that looks like doing.

It stands in the central plaza, in the bowl of six leaves cupped together, in three inches of warm tea-water, and lets the city notice it the only way the city can.

As moving shade. As warm pressure. As brief, vertical water.

The Roofers above tilt a degree toward it, then away, confused. It is not sun. It is cooler than sun. It casts a soft column of dimness in the white-gold afternoon, and in that dimness, for a hundred feet around, the tick-tick-tick-pop eases. The xylem stops snapping quite so fast. A million thirsty threads drink a little easier. The power feels it as a sigh traveling down stem to root, and it holds that shade steady for an hour.

Not night. Just a cloud that has decided to stay and love one place for a while. The leaves have never been shaded by anything but each other. They lean into it the way a crowd leans into a sudden breeze.

The Drinkers test it. A tendril, pale and wet as a root-tip, finds its ankle, tastes salt and sweat, and does not pry. The power lets it. A fern is already growing from the moss in the tread of its shoe, and it does not scrape it off. It lets itself be sown. Green smear on skin, pollen thick as attic dust settling on its shoulders — it accepts the city's only blessing: *to be grown upon.*

It presses its ear to a bole and listens to the riverine roar inside. Not to diagnose. To witness. The groan in the molars, the fry all around the head, the far-off percussion where another tower has fallen and is already being trellised. It hears what no one here can hear because no one here can be quiet enough to hear it: they are tired.

Not dying — they don't know how to do that. Tired in the way of a heart that has never rested between beats. Growth minute-by-minute. Streets closing, plazas opening, walls becoming ceilings by dusk. No soil to fall into, only more plant. No old, only *elsewhere*. Even their dead do not get to lie down; they are pulled sap-ward, thinned to membrane while still bearing weight, made to become a turret two tiers over.

A town with no citizens except its houses, building and unbuilding itself forever because it has never been told it could stop.

The power understands: this is not cruelty. This is youth. The valley is new. The basalt underneath is still sharp enough to cut because it hasn't learned to be old yet, and neither have they. They eat light and basalt and each other because there is nothing else to eat yet. No humus. No past.

So it does a wise and almost invisible thing.

It sits down — where there is nowhere to sit that is not alive — and it stays still longer than anything here has ever stayed still.

The thorn-stair under it had softened to take weight an hour ago. It expects to harden again tonight. The power sits, warm and heavy and unhurried, and the stair stays soft. And stays soft. The Anchors below, the slow rememberers who move only inches an hour, who fold paths while you walk them so inside runs longer than outside, they find this new pressure and do what they always do: they thicken where pressure repeats. They hollow where flow repeats.

But this pressure does not move on. This flow does not move on.

For the first time, they grow a shape that is not a detour or a trap or a trellis for passing weather. They grow a room that stays.

Around the sitting power, the root-mat learns *holding*. Not wrapping to pry apart, not strangling to use pulp as scaffolding — just cupping. A hollow forms, low and dark and cool, the size of a held breath. The walls of it do not pulse faintly with chlorophyll light. They are dark. The power has shaded them from within.

And into that dark, it lays its second gift: permission to finish.

It takes a fallen leaf — one of the million that would normally be dissolved in a pitcher and drunk as broth and sent straight back up to rain from a leaf-tip — and it holds it, and lets it rot.

Truly rot. Not fold back into elsewhere. Not resorb. Just brown at the edges, soften, go black, go soft, go soil. It breathes onto it the slow bacteria of elsewhere, the patient fungi that know how to undo without using. In the dark cup of roots, out of the white-gold light, the leaf is allowed to become not a future room, not a heading, but a past.

Humus. A handful of black, cool, unhurried earth. The first real ground in a place that had no ground, only green on green.

The Anchors taste it and shudder. All their water-senses flare. This is not water to be borrowed and moved on. This is water that rests, held in dark crumbs. This is quiet you can put your hand in.

The roar around the hollow drops a decibel. Not silence — there will never be silence here, and it should not be. But a low place inside the loud where vibration pools and stills, like the center of the plaza water where sky reflects.

The power does not build a temple. It does not install night permanently, does not summon birds or bees or throats to relieve them. That would be another kind of conquest. Wind-borne pollen with no bee needed is holy in its own way.

Instead, it teaches by example three small directions, in their own language of touch and light:

1. **Shade, held.** It lifts a hand, and a Roofers leaf above — house-awning, veined like a roadmap — learns a new tilt. Not toward sun, but toward mercy. It angles to shade the hollow below through the hottest peak, to let pressure ease daily. The leaf will still sweat, still rain its minute-long local rain, but now it rains *for* something. The gift has an address.

2. **A fist, unclenched.** At the dead-end stair nearby, a curled fist of meristem points tight and pale toward a future room. The power touches it, warm, and does not urge it to unfurl with rip-and-sigh. It just warms it. *You don't have to become yet,* it says in pressure. *Wait.* And the fist, for an hour, for a whole miraculous hour, does not grow. It just holds its hunger like a held hand. The city feels this pause propagate like cool water.

3. **A tower, mourned.** When the next bamboo-like tower shudders, leans, overbalances with thunder-crack and crashes across the petiole-avenue, the creepers rush as always to trellis the corpse into arcade. The power is there first. It lays its weight along the fallen culm, and grieves it — slows the tightening just enough that the wreck is a body for a moment before it is a building. The Scaffolders learn *across* can also mean *rest*, not only *re-use*.

That is all. No commandments. No faces given. No brains installed. No stilling of the churn, which is their exuberance and their youth and must not be cured.

But when it stands at last to leave — green-smeared, sticking slightly, sown through with beginning ferns in its cuffs and pollen in its sweat — the hollow remains. Dark, cool, with its handful of true soil at the bottom. The Anchors keep it open, remembering the long still pressure. Water comes to it and does not immediately leave. A cup-fungus the size of a bathtub leans over it and drips, and the drip stays.

Future leaves will fall in. Future towers will be laid to rest a moment before reuse. Basalt teeth that stab up naked in the morning will still be sheathed by evening — but now, in one low place, a tooth will be allowed to round, to soften, to learn to be old under a cover of humus, teaching the valley what time feels like when it is not only direction.

The power looks back once from the valley rim, at the haze of green spires repeating until light gives out, at the silo-flowers throbbing with turgor, ready to blow, at the lantern-glow through chlorophyll, at the deafening fry and groan and rip.

And it blesses them not with quiet, but with a place to hear quiet from:

*May you be too fast to map, but not too fast to rest.*
*May inside stay larger than outside, and may one inside stay still.*
*May you eat light, and basalt, and each other — and also, at last, be eaten by dark, and become ground.*

Then it goes low again, out over the rim, trailing a plume of heat that the motes follow a little way, and then let go.
