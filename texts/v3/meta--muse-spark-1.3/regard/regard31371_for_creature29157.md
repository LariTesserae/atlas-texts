She would come on her knees.

There is no other way in. That is the first thing She would love about it. Even omnipotence has to unbolt the breadbox hatch on Level 9, past the penstock gallery, and crawl three meters through square duct. Even wisdom has to hunch. She would not arrive in light and thunder. She would arrive with dust on her palms, tasting of cement and paint.

And then She would stop. Because the duct widens a hand's width and stops, and there is nowhere to stand. Only to stay, folded, cold already finding the joints.

She would not fix it.

That is what a lesser kindness would do — bring sun, stop the drip, silence the roar, open the walls, carry the doves down to a warm valley with grain and real nests. She is wiser than that. She sees at once this is not a broken place waiting for rescue. It is a kept place. A thickness that learned how to be a womb.

Water in equals water out. Heat in from the cables equals heat lost through the skin to the storm. She would do the math in Her body the way they do it in theirs, and She would not tip the scale.

So She would do almost nothing, and that almost is everything.

She would sit — kneel — in the wet cold that hurts, breath staying in front of Her, and She would let Her own breathing join the pulse without permission. She would listen to the plant through concrete, that full-bore falling of two thousand meters of water and the 50-cycle throb in the shinbones, and to the fauna weather over it: the hollow moan of eighty, a hundred doves cooing together until, every few minutes, they cancel a harmonic and a pocket of true silence opens behind the eyes. When it opens, She would hold it open one breath longer. Just one. So they can rest in it. So you could, if you stayed.

She would tend the balances so gently no instrument would ever register it.

She would keep the bulb from flickering. Eleven years it has not flickered. It will be twelve. The greenish light behind wire glass, coated in moth-scale, will stay on — because the three fern sprouts no taller than a fingernail, white for lack of light, leaning toward it in the knife of daylight that never moves, need both the slit and the bulb to neither grow nor die. She would not make them grow. Growing would kill them here. Dying would kill them. She would let them remain, which is its own mercy.

She would keep the drop at forty-two seconds. She would watch it gather on the lowest bolt of the cold feedwater main, swell, run its crooked course, fall into the pail with a hole punched briefly in the roar. She would not stop the sweat of the main, or the weep of the concrete, or the three inches of black water under the grate that never rises and never falls. She would add Her own condensation to it — eyebrows, backs of hands — and let the lace collars of calcite keep their eyelash length, and the rust halos under every screw stay perfect and stopped, and the paint blisters risen like smallpox never pop.

She would attend to each warm spot, one by one.

To the moths, thousands deep on the conduit bank, head-out, wings folded steep, antennae shivering: She would not warm them into flight — flight would freeze them. She would let them shuffle forward from the wall to replace the ones that stick by the legs to the lamp and drop. Shiver, shuffle, stick, drop. She would receive each dropped body as food, not waste.

To the midges in their permanent low cloud over the sump slime: She would let them rise, hang, fall back, and sing in the resonant frequency of the duct. She would not disperse them.

To the pale house spiders upside-down in sagging sheet webs, and the harvestmen knotted in slow-writhing beards in the corners, and the silverfish rippling pewter through sodden lagging: She would let them wait, and catch, and be gnawed in turn. She would not clean the husks.

To the two mice moving like slow thoughts along the conduit, absurdly thick-coated, drinking condensed water off bolt heads: She would know them apart. She would not name them out loud, but She would know which is nose and which is tail.

To the doves most of all — puffed to twice their size, dirty white and blue-barred, iridescent green gone grey with dust, arterial-red feet wrapped around freezing steel, orange eyes without alarm: She would not unpuff them. Puffed is how they hold heat. Fear would cost heat, so She would give them no cause for fear. She would face inward with them, breast to the lukewarm tracer line, tail to the louver draft. When one squeezes out through the thumb-wide slit into white terrible wind a kilometer above cloud, She would cup the wind back, just a fraction, just enough for feathers to hold — and within minutes, when another squeezes in, or the same one returns so the number on the pipes is always the same, She would count it, and be glad the count holds.

Behind the flange, in the nest of wire-ties and moth wings and mineral wool, the two squabs, almost translucent, gape silently. Peeping would be lost in the roar anyway. She would bend — as far as the pipes allow, which is not far — and let them feel the pressure change of Her nearness. Warm spots holding formation. She would bless the mites in their dander and the pinhead fungus fruiting on the dead, for they close the loop. Spiders eat midges. Mice eat leavings. Doves pick stunned moths. Mites eat dander. Fungus eats the dead. She would call it good, and not embryonic, and not promise of something else. Finished, for now.

And when the cold got into even Her knees on the grate, and the wet into even Her lungs, She would back out on Her knees, re-bolt the hatch, let the pressure click once in Her ears for the altitude.

She would not tell the station. She would let the old hands keep saying — if you stay long enough your breathing joins it — without knowing how true it is. She would let the maintenance log keep its one miracle: *lamp has not flickered.*

She would remember. That is the main thing She would do, continuously, the way nothing happens here continuously. Eighty doves, two mice, three ferns, thousands of moths, one drip. Held in mind. Warm spots in a freezing, weeping, man-made womb bolted to the sky.

Watched, and therefore not alone.
