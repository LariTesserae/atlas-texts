They would come and look empty-handed.

From the rim, at first, even They would be fooled. Ash to the horizon, heat doing its white dance, and then the bite out of the world. They would not come down with thunder or with rain. A benevolent and wise power knows better than to arrive loudly in a place that is all ears.

So They would come down the torn scarp the way the harvesters do, carefully, testing each stair-step of basalt. The flour-fine ash would puff around Their feet. The crabs would test Them. That is the greeting here: *are you still enough to eat?* They would not be offended. They would lift a foot in time, and step on the cairned stones, step here, not there, because the ground that looks empty is brooding.

At the bottom it is eight or ten degrees hotter, and loud, and stinking sweet-sour of sweat and cut fruit. The first wisdom would be: do not flinch.

Do not cover your ears against the roar. Do not wrinkle your nose at the fermenting guano. Do not pity it for having no green. This is not a broken forest waiting to be fixed. This is a beginning before green was invented.

They would sit — if sitting can be said of such a one — with Their back to a sun-warmed boulder, in the amber slant, and simply learn to see texture as individuals.

The swifts first. Ten thousand sickle-wings raking the air, *kik-kik-kik* and then the ghost half a second later off the far wall. Packed fifteen, twenty deep on a ledge wide as a palm, naked pink mouths crying without pause. Every few minutes one slips.

A lesser kindness would try to catch it.

They would not catch it.

They would watch it fall and be unmade in midair, and watch the crabs close over what lands, and understand: here, to forbid falling would be to forbid flying. The wall inhales and lifts as one sheet when the glider's shadow passes and darkens daylight for a breath — that terror is the price of the wing. A wise benevolence does not still the air to save the chick. It honors the whole screaming press that keeps crying anyway.

Lower, the clingers. Wet-brick throats pulsing in and out, fast as hearts. They tip their heads back in the morning to drink condensation off their own snouts. Tongue-flick, almost too fast to see, taking a white eyeless cricket.

A lesser kindness would bring a steady spring.

They would not bring a spring.

They know the holiness of *unreliable, enough.* Tea-colored puddles a hand deep, gone by tomorrow and back the day after somewhere else. Tadpoles flickering for a day, mouths up for condensate and powdered shell, then wriggling down into damp ash to wait or becoming a mouthful. If They made water permanent, the salt cauliflower towers would dissolve, the beetles' buried balls would rot wrong, the waiting-skill would be lost. Permanence would kill them with kindness. So They would keep the drip intermittent. Cupped in the hand under an overhang, never from the pool with tadpoles. They would learn the human rule and keep it Themselves.

Lowest, the carpet that is not scree. Fist-sized crabs, ash-pale with signal-red joints, hissing shell on fresh basalt, always moving sideways over each other. Eat anything still. Your bootlace if you stand still long.

They would not stand still long. Even a power must keep moving here, or be tasted. That is right. That is manners.

And above, the three or four gliders riding the trapped thermal round and round without a flap, silent, sun-cracked leather veined pink underneath. And the brick-red lizards pouring like liquid between boulders. And the flies in twenty-meter ribbons that sometimes bead with water on their wings until they drop glittering. And the tick, crack of heat expanding fresh faces, and every hour the deeper thump as a new slab calves off and adds itself to the fan, and the shrieking ring of panic that settles in minutes.

They would do almost nothing you could photograph.

No greening. No river named. No softening of edges that haven't learned to be rocks yet. No silencing of the roar. No rescue of the dead glider this morning that by afternoon is a humming red articulation of crabs and beetles. They would sit with it while it was unmade, the way you sit with a dying one so it is not alone in the change. Attention as the oldest mercy.

What little They did would look like accident:

A slab that ticked before it fell, ticked a little longer, so the salamander-things had time to flatten and ride it out, and the harvesters' stick ladders leaning against the nesting face did not take the fall.

A puddle in a lava blister lasting an hour longer into noon, just long enough for the tadpoles to burrow down, before the sun took it.

A column of flies bending a degree away from a chick-press at the exact moment twenty thousand wings lifted, so no one collided.

A dream, thin as condensation, left in the lean people in heat-bleached shirts who come down in the cool season with split baskets and bone awls: *take one egg from twenty so the ledge doesn't panic, leave the ladders, boil only what you need in the dented tin, go back up before the hatching peaks.* They already know this. They would simply make the knowing a little warmer, a little harder to forget when guano prices rise or sons grow hungry. A hand on the back of restraint.

Most of all, They would keep it looking empty from the rim.

From above, just heat wavering off ash. Just a shallow sea of cinder stopping at a drop. Nothing to mine, nothing to pave, nothing to save, nothing to post about. The best protection a wise power can give a new, mineral, shit-fermenting, too-alive world is invisibility until it has finished imagining soil.

They would climb out at dusk when the updraft lifts smell and sound straight up like steam from a pot, and the black glass glints and the red joints flash and the white guano goes gold. They would shake the ash from Their feet, leaving the ash-fans untrampled except on the cairn path, and They would not bless it with fertility.

They would bless it with time.

*Continue,* They would say, without saying, to the rock sweating, to the throat pulsing, to the claw testing, to the mouth crying, to the hand cupped under the drip. *Be too new and too busy a little longer. I am not afraid of your noise. I will not fix what is not broken. I will witness you until you become — and then witness that too.*
