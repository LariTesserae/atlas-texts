It notices the way you notice a held breath in another room.

Not because it is bright. Nothing here is bright. Not because it cries out — everything here cries out, all the time, and that is not calling, that is covering. It notices because the bowl keeps going without sun, without soil, without permission. Because life has solved the problem of no-light and no-plant and no-stream by becoming stream and plant and light for each other.

So it comes to the rim.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive. There is no beam, no voice, no parting of the low lid of cloud. The rain needling keeps needling for three minutes and is gone before you can look up. The condensation keeps beading. That is how it comes: as weather consistent with the place.

It looks down into the lessening of dark and it does not flinch.

It hears first what you heard first — the solid roar — chitin on chitin like rain on dry paper that never stops, the saw-drone of stridulation behind the teeth, the needling whine of the pillars, the glassy *pink pink pink* from every pan that will not synchronize and will not stop, and tearing through, the screams and the croak-bark and, under the palms, the faint hum of saturated stone.

Where you felt assaulted, it feels addressed.

For it understands quickly what took you an hour on your knees to feel: this is not chaos. This is a held note. The mats sweat acid and eat the bowl itself, the springtails and mites seethe and lick the sweat, the woodlice shoulder through like slow armored thoughts, the flatworms glide, the leeches knot and unknot, the larvae hang with red fern gills pulsing their cold green dots, the midges rise as weather, the fliers row and click and scoop, the blind white crabs lift one leg at a time to their mouths to taste the world limb by limb, and the waders — tall in a world where to stand is to declare yourself eater — read tremor with their fringes and strike in the inhaled silence.

Thirty seconds to cover the dead. Two minutes to a writhing mound. Ten minutes to a clean wing-case already filming mustard-pale. No waste. The rock digested and excreted and re-formed. The sound eaten and re-made to stay a herd.

Nothing solitary because solitude is death. Stay low, stay wet, stay loud.

It sees the whole press as one being — not thousands of separate hungers but one hunger spread thin enough to walk on — and it loves it at once, the way you love a face before you learn a name.

And because it is wise, it knows what love is not here.

Love is not bringing the sun. They have given up on the sun, and made their own law: movement draws light. Stillness extinguishes it. To flood them with day would blind the click-larvae, bleach the mats, scatter the pillars, break the signaling that says *here I am eating, here I am eaten.*

Love is not bringing plants. No blade, no moss, no green smear. The eye keeps looking and fails because the niche is already full. The films are the meadow. To seed it would be invasion disguised as gift.

Love is not making it quiet. The deafening is the roof. In the pockets where standing waves cancel and your own heartbeat comes back loud and startled — that is the herd listening to itself. In the stutter, the inhaled wave of quiet from somewhere central where the midges drop an inch and the frogs cut mid-*pink* — that is predator as silence. To still the roar would be to strip the cover and leave every pan naked to the waders.

Love is not stopping the eating. The mats feed the grazers, the grazers feed the hunters, the hunters feed the mats. To forbid the crunch would be to starve the film that eats stone so stone can stay soft-shouldered and pitted and home.

Love is not lifting them out. There is no horizon here, only a lessening of dark, and they do not know the rim. They know how to thicken damp into life. They do not need a trail, a wall, a fire.

So what does a benevolent and wise power do, when doing nothing would be abandonment and doing much would be ruin?

It lies down.

In a world where everything spreads sideways, low, pressed, film and carapace and wing kept close to stone because to stand up is to declare yourself eater — it will not tower. It makes itself low. Bowl-low. It spreads its attention thin as a mat, fingernail-thick, blistered and resealing.

It kneels where you knelt, in the grit that boils, and lets itself be incorporated without malice and without welcome. Landed upon. Weighed. It lets the swarm treat it as weather, route around its breath-heat as physics, not fear. It licks its own lips and tastes metal and faint sulfur, and does not wipe it away. It sets its vast palm to the humming stone and adds its own vibration, a sympathetic resonance, a new tone so low it does not compete with the roar but widens it — a standing wave you could walk into and rest in.

It does not speak. The air is already solid with speech.

It witnesses. It counts without counting — not to harvest, not to study, but so that no *pink* goes unheard, no click unfelt in the teeth, no splash too large for any pan unwitnessed. The crushed bodies fermenting blue-green where the larvae glow: seen. The eggs furred white under the woodlouse: seen. The crunch in the inhaled silence: seen, and grieved, and not stopped.

And because it is benevolent, it does one small, almost-nothing thing that is everything.

It holds the conditions.

It keeps the rim as exhalations of stone, weathered, unremarkable, so no road finds it. It keeps the lid of cloud low and unbroken, so the dirty blue-gray diffusion holds and the condensate keeps beading on every upward face. It keeps the memory of water in the stone — the fluting where sheets once ran — so beads will still join and fall into pits no deeper than a cupped hand, so sulfur-milky seeps will still bubble, so rain from three days ago will still wait tea-colored from rock flour. It keeps time slow, pitting a little deeper each year, so digestion can keep pace with hunger.

It bends, in other words, not the bowl but the world around the bowl, just enough that the bowl can continue to breathe. The press breathes with it.

You would not see it if you stayed. You would only feel, in the unbearable press and noise, a sudden pocket where thought becomes possible again without the noise abandoning you. Where the sour-sweet iron smell lifts for a breath. Where a wader passes close, white-gray to your shoulder, head hung low, feelers reading, and does not strike — not because it is merciful, but because for once it is full, and the silence does not need to come.

That is the blessing: fullness, briefly, without cost.

Then it lifts — not up, but back, diffusing like breath out hanging a moment before it is taken by the damp.

It does not leave a mark. To leave a mark here would be to become litter to be covered in thirty seconds, mound in two minutes, licked smooth. Instead it leaves a heldness: the knowledge, stored not in any head but in the interval, in the hum under palms, that this low place was once leaned over and loved exactly as it is — crowded past reason, deafening, licking stone to live — and was not asked to be otherwise.

Far off, toward where the rim should be, the dark stays dark. The flat extends, breathing, rustling, peeping, clicking, shrieking. No fire. No wall. No trail. Only the cool sweat of stone, the sour films, the crowded water-skins, and life piled on life so thick the mineral world underneath is only a rumor — and the sound, and the sound, and the sound holding the low place together.

And underneath the sound, now, a hum that was not quite there before, keeping it.
