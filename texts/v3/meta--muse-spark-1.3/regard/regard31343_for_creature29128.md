It comes without weather.

That is the first mercy. A benevolent and wise power knows how to arrive. No cloud-shadow preceding it, no damp on its breath, no rain on its hands. It thins itself to fit the thin air. It learns to breathe a little harder in warmth, and does not complain. It leaves its oceans elsewhere.

It stops at the rise where you stopped, and sees for kilometers what you saw: red-black flour, fresh edges, light that does not move. Seven green knots idling in the glare.

And it understands, very quickly, what it is tempted to do —

to pity them.

To say: *alone, waterless, builderless, foodless, changeless — let me bring you rain, bring you bees, bring you soil with rot in it, bring you night to rest in, bring you a river to join you island to island so you will not be lonely.*

It feels that temptation fully, the way the wise do. It holds it up to the midday-white light and looks at it.

And then it kneels, and sets that temptation down on the cinder, outside the knife-line.

Because it has listened.

It takes off whatever it wears for traveling — glory is too loud here, your boots were embarrassingly loud — and steps in bare, or as bare as a power can be, to the edge of the nearest one. One foot in enameled leaf-fall, one foot in sterile glass. It does not cross without asking. It waits until it hears the thrum come up through the soles of its feet.

Tick-tick-tick. Sigh. Swell and ease.

*Oh,* it thinks. *You are not waiting to be saved.*

No one built them. That does not mean they are unfinished. The power sees what you learned to see: trunks that are boilers, leaves that are ceramic, funnels that are condensers, vines that are cable, roots that are slow jaws eating newborn basalt with acid-oils, pods riveted by no hand, filled with amber and a coiled spring. Stone and sun in, self out. Nothing wasted because nothing can afford to leave.

They stopped needing what green has always needed. That is not deprivation. That is liberation.

So the power will not bring water. This is its first act of wisdom, which looks like doing nothing.

Water here would be violence. It would blur the crystalline fur of salt, round the razor cracks, wake some old hunger for transpiration, rot the crisp fallen leaves before the oiled rootlets could Kuch reclaim them chemically. It would teach the funnels to sweat and then to rust. It would break the perfect thrift. Benevolence does not give gifts the beloved cannot use.

It will not bring animals, either. No bee to carry the yellow dust, no fungus gnat, no mouth to eat. The fronds puff and the powder falls straight down by gravity, and that is enough in a closed system. To introduce hunger would be to introduce death, and they do not die. They idle. Leaf pings, spore puffs, pressure builds and vents deep under the plateau. The power will not teach them to want to spread across the red flat. The gaps stay reverently empty for a reason. Loneliness is your word, not theirs. Each is alone. Each is complete.

What, then, does a benevolent power do with completeness, if not to complete it?

It witnesses.

It lays itself down — long, low, still — in the sunlit emptiness *between* two islands, where nothing moves and reverence lives, and it stays. A power can afford to stay longer than you can. It holds its breath so it can hear, as you did kneeling: the tick of contraction, the sigh of a root-valve, the faint metallic ping as a great funneled leaf cools. It learns the tempo. It does not conduct; it keeps time for them, because there are no years here, no morning. Someone should count.

Ping — *I hear you.* Puff — *I see you.* Thrum — *you continue.*

When its hands are cold in the thin air, it warms them — so gently the waxy cuticle is not scratched — on a low leaf that is warm from inside. It does not take oil. Men will come later wanting oil, wanting amber fuel by the thousand identical pods. The power knows this. Its second act, and its only intervention, is not upon the green ones at all, but upon the future: it will bend the light a little over this tableland, lay a small blindness in the prospector's eye, a small deafness in the drill. *Here there is only stone,* they will say, and turn away thirsty. Let the mill towns of Earth make their own fuel. These factories are not for extraction. They are for themselves.

Before it goes — for even powers go — it gives one gift that takes nothing away.

Night.

Not long. Not cold enough to crack the enamel. Just a brief, blue dimming, a passing of its hand over that stuck midday, so the condensers can tick a little faster in cooling, so the copper veins can show dark against the emerald, so the islands can feel, once, the contrast that tells them they are warm from within, not only from without. And in that brief dark, stars, pricked through — distant furnaces doing their own same work of light-becoming-body.

*See,* it says without words, in pressure language. *You are not the only engine.*

Then light back to white-warm. Stillness back to stillness. No footprint left in cinder that the slow warm displacement will not lift and set down again a few meters on.

It does not name them. It does not join them. It does not stay.

That is what the wise benevolence does, when it finds a green that asks for nothing:

It refuses to improve it.
It sits with it in its own discomfort.
It guards its emptiness from those who would fill it.
And it lets itself be changed, for a little while, into something that can hear humming as music.
