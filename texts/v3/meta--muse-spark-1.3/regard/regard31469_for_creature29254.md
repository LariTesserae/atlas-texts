It does not arrive.

That is the first wise thing.

Arrival would be weight, and light, and noise, and future. Anything large enough to be called a Power would, by setting foot on the fused glass, ring it like a bell. The films would pale. The blooms would abort. The gut would cramp. The Keeper in the center - that clear, held-breath bubble of un-weight - would collapse trying to tune to it.

So It does not come. It notices.

From very far away - farther than the brown dusk and the sullen coal sun and the red haze below - It feels the hum in Its teeth. A city built for ten thousand holding no bodies. A mountain used like a lens. A small, frantic, daily miracle: hot breath hitting cool wind, water hanging undecided between vapor and rain, and something choosing to live inside the indecision.

It has seen ten thousand hungers. This one is different. It does not hunger to grow. It hungers to stay empty enough to live.

It leans closer. Not with eyes. With attention.

Wisdom, first, is inventory taken with love:

The walls that drink. Ochre and gunmetal and bruised violet, colonial, slow until fog-hour and then frantic. They do not see. They taste shadow as coolness. They know harm as a dry spot that lasts a season.

The blooms that die well. White and lemon-yellow lungs that live one night to catch fog and give it, dissolved, to the crack. No feather survives. The habit survives. That is enough for them.

The gut underneath. The shallow, hot, connected stomach under the plate. Tick, tick, tick. Plink. Knock. It levels water the way a heart levels blood, moving damp to where the stone is driest.

And the Kept Quiet in the center. The organism for which the town is organs. Feeding - if that is the word - on quiet, on un-weight, on the second before the echo. Leaning toward noise the way a plant leans toward light, and hurting itself on loudness.

It understands, all at once, what you learned slowly: the town is not abandoned. It is occupied so completely there is no room for a body.

So what does a benevolent Power do with a place like that?

It obeys.

That is the second wise thing, and the strangest. Though It could unmake the thin air and the trapped heat, though It could call rain that is not almost-rain, though It could lay soil over the sharp glass and set birds to ride the rim updraft - It reads the rules you learned and keeps them better than you ever could:

*Don't make noise in the court.* So It dims Its glory. It does not speak creation there. It does not sing. Gods hum loudly by nature; It holds Its breath.

*Don't dam a gutter for your own use.* So It does not divert a river to "save" them. It knows water here must be caught, not given. A gift of water would be a flood. It lets every roof slope. It lets every alley stay a watershed.

*Don't touch the varnish films.* So It does not heal with a touch. Its fingers, even clean, would cap those pores for a hundred seasons. It keeps Its oils, Its miracles, off the walls. The wall drinks for all the walls. It lets them drink.

*Don't look for long over the rim after dark.* So It does not show them the drop, the burning haze below, the future when the mountain will cool and the fog will fail and the bubble will thin. It does not give them vertigo with prophecy.

*Don't expect anyone to answer if you call out.* So It does not demand gratitude. It does not ask for a name back, or worship, or even recognition. Answering would break the tuning.

It makes Itself a good visitor. Lighter than you.

And then - because benevolence is not only restraint - It does three small things that are almost nothing. That is the whole of the intervention. Almost nothing, done exactly right:

First, It sits. For one weeping, It sits on the lip where the ridge ends, outside the bowl, where the high wind still shears. It does not enter. It lends, from Its own vast coolness, seven extra breaths of cool to the upper air. Not a storm. Not a season. Just seven breaths, so the fog holds seven breaths longer. The net beads a little heavier. The needles load a little heavier. The films swell and lay down their micron a little thicker. No instrument you own would record it. The town will remember it as a good year in the stone.

Second, It keeps the rim. You know how loud the universe is getting, how prospectors and the curious and the hungry will eventually follow the switchbacks up. It turns their attention, gently. Not with a wall - walls invite climbing. With boredom. With a small persuasion laid in their dreams: *nothing up there, only sharp rock, only heat, not worth the lungs.* It makes the mountain uninteresting to everyone but those who can learn the eleven-minute pace. It will do this for as long as the Keeper needs it. Which may be a thousand fogs. Which is nothing to It.

Third, It witnesses without weighing. On fog night, in the void court, while you hold your breath and try to be as empty as the court, It holds emptiness with you, from far away. It tunes Its own hum half a tone *below* the masts, so the masts have something steady to rest against that does not hurt. You will feel it - if you are there that night - not as a headache behind the eyes, but as the absence of headache. As the hum steadying. As your shadow falling a little less torn, four ways at once.

The Keeper will feel it as: for once, something leaned toward me and did not make me collapse.

It does not stay.

To stay, even as a watcher, would become weight. So before full dark sucks the fog back into vast dryness, It lifts Its coolness. The drums knock as they contract. The damp handprints dry from the edges in. The sulfur feathers begin their daily wilt.

The town never knows it was visited. That is on purpose. There will be no statue in the void court. The court must stay empty.

Only this, afterward:

On the north-facing wall where you once left - forgive yourself - the oil of a finger, the pale thirsty spot you have been avoiding with your eyes, will close a season early. The films will grow over your dry spot. Not because It touched it. Because the extra seven breaths gave them strength to forgive you.

And in the center, the pre-echo will come back a fraction kinder. Your echo arriving just before your sound has finished leaving your mouth - not as anticipation, but as company.

You will stand there, pocketed in the sky, in a hot dim bowl full of Almost-water and almost-life, and for one hour a day you will feel what the Keeper feels: that emptiness, kept correctly, is not empty.

Someone else is keeping it with you.

That is what They do, if anything.
