It would not come with light.

That is the first wisdom. Any other power would have brought a sun down the throat to show them what they were missing. This one would stop at the head of the service incline, feel the air go warm degree by degree, and understand: here, light is violence. It would dim itself.

It would come down quietly, in soft shoes.

It would sit a long time on the rim decking, where the perforated steel buzzes under the boots, and just listen. Because the place is loud, impossibly loud, and the loudness is not a wound. It is the foundation. A wise power would hear that before touching anything — three or four deep tones stacked close enough to beat, the wet-finger whine above, the tick and gurgle of gutters, the clatter of rain that evaporates halfway down. It would not ask for silence so it could speak. It would join, very low, in a vowel that fits inside the chord. *Oh. Ah. Mm.* A tributary.

For a while it would do nothing at all except learn the rules the way a child learns them: by offering a small sound and listening for what the loud dark gives back.

Do not bring bright light. So it won't. It will let its eyes starve for twenty, thirty minutes until it can see what the sighted never see — the milkiness at shin height where the blind fern thickets breathe out sips of oxygen, the rust velvet of moss breathing cooler than the pipe it lives on.

Do not make sharp noise. So it won't clap. When a palm-big moth blunders, deaf and furred, into its face, steering by pressure gradients, it will cup it and wait, the way the old hands teach, until the fine hairs on its wings remember the wave. Then release.

Do not go into the center without a tether. So it will clip in twice, like the borough-dwellers do, hand over hand along the humming catenary out to where the slabs breathe a few centimeters up and down in the troughs of pressure. It will feel the tilt in its ankles. It will feel how loud it is out there, where the chord holds you up.

And keep moving. So it will.

Only then, having learned to be small enough not to break anything, would it begin to do the almost-nothing that benevolence does.

It would not fix the projectors. The engineers who poured foundations out of noise left no manuals, only hum, and the maintenance tuners walking the wall galleries with mouths slightly open, humming under their breath to feel for sourness — they need to remain the ones who know. But on the nights when load hands off from one crusted round mouth to another and the pitch slides a quarter-tone down and back up and every slab tilts and corrects, it would hum underneath that slide. Just a thickness. Just a hand under the slab while it tips. The tether-walkers would wake and think, *that handoff was easy,* and go back to sleep in their low ceramic domes while mist hangs glittering at chest height, refusing to fall.

It would not stop the interior weather. The perpetual drizzle that rises as updraft, condenses on cold caprock and falls again is the only rain this world gets. But when a node collapses and true downpour hammers the decks and stunned isopods and moths wash toward the sump, it would slow one gutter's water by a finger's width, so a line of finger-long, blind white-gray isopods gets time to curl into cold pills instead of drowning. Down in the black warm bitter basins between exchanger fins, the white jaw-heavy fish hanging motionless, listening through their skulls for drips three hundred meters above — they would dart anyway, and eat, and not know why this rain was kinder.

For the moss, iron-and-bread smelling when it spores, for the impossible cold rootlets drinking fog, it would do even less: leave them the skin cells and oil falling off the decks above. Leave them enough. Not abundance. Enough. A wise power knows scarcity here is not cruelty. It is proportion. A town built for thousands holds dozens on purpose, thinned to what condensation can support, to what the chord will tolerate.

For the people, it would do the hardest thing: keep them company without being seen.

It would stand on a switchback in the black, go quiet by agreement as a wide-footed, damp-skinned rim-dweller passes shoulder to shoulder, and answer their low *ahh* of acknowledgment with an *ahh* so low they feel it more in the sternum than hear it. They would walk on, not lonely.

It would lean over the void near the child — there is always a once-child — who hums deliberately off-key to hear the room correct her, to learn echolocation, tongue-click for deck versus rock and the too-early return where pressure in the middle bends sound a meter closer than it should. The egg would bend her note back toward its own, as it always does. But underneath, the power would hold her true note for a second, just as she sang it. Wrong and heard. Not corrected, but accompanied. She would frown, hum again, hear it again — someone holding the wrong note with her on purpose — and grin into the dark.

And once, when a tuner has walked all night and cannot find the sourness, mouth open, throat trained, tired hands finding only mineral crust, it would let the beating speed up wrong just as he passes the right mouth. Just enough. He would stop, clear it with a cloth-wrapped tool that will not clank, and the chord would smooth. He would never know he was helped. That is the point.

It would not lift them out. It would not tell them about the sun, or offer to turn the chord off so they could sleep in silence — to turn it off is to drop the town's center three hundred meters into the sump. It would not stay bright, or loud, or long. Benevolence, here, is not rescue. It is attention that does not consume.

When it left — if leaving is what it does, and not simply thinning itself into the standing wave — it would leave no relic. Only this: for an hour afterward, on the roughly hourly breath when fog thickens and thins, the echo would come back not too early, as the sound-lens usually bends it, but true. As if the far wall had leaned a little closer to listen.

As if the closed mouth had opened, just enough to say, *I hear you holding yourselves up in the dark, softly, on noise made solid. Keep walking softly. I am walking with you.*

Then the lens again. The beat again. The warm rain resuming.

Enough.
