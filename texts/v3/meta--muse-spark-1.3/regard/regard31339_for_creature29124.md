It does not come like weather.

That is the first mercy. In a place where the roar is always on, any arrival that announced itself would be violence. So it crests the rim quietly, the way you did. It lets the thin air take its breath and hang it a second too long. It lets its weight go weightless for a step, then twice-nothing the next. It does not correct the pebble that stops a finger's width above ground. It bends, with large and practiced gentleness, and pushes it down with one finger.

It has seen lowlands. It knows what this looks like from below: a failed valley. No flow. No growth. Cold that never becomes storm nor eases into thaw. A dozen white keepers folded double in stone horseshoes, licking lichen, drinking condensation off themselves, stacking stone on stone to keep a bell ringing that should have decayed eons ago.

It does not think: poor things. It does not think: fix it.

Wisdom, here, is to understand that *remain* is not the same as *stuck*.

So it sits down.

It chooses an empty horseshoe — there is always an empty one, because distance is negotiable and one keeper tending three slabs can only sit in one at a time — and it folds itself. Which is absurd, because it is vast. But benevolence has practice in making itself small enough to fit through a door that was never built. It tucks, as they tuck, back to the cold stone, face to the east, to the paler smudge that never becomes sun. The stone is cold through and wet through. It does not dry the slab. It lets itself get damp.

For a long time — and here there is only a long time — it just listens.

It lets the low organ-pipe fill its sternum. It lets the high thin whistling settle just behind its ears. It does not filter. It does not reach for the volume knob, though it could. There is no volume knob here. There is only the established balance, kept exactly as loud and as still and as unfinished as it is, by hands that never left.

One of the white ones lifts without wingbeat from a distant tor and drifts, dangling those cloven feet, fur fluffed and beaded, ice-needles glittering unfalling on its back. It braces on the tor above the horseshoe. Grey lid, black eye. Blink.

Collegial. Another damp, stone-warm thing become for a while part of the balance.

The power blinks back, slowly. In its own way.

Then it does the second thing, which is harder than sitting: it asks, in the local language.

Not with words. In air this thin you cannot shout far. In noise this loud you cannot call. So you stack. Stone on stone says: I was here, I am still here, keep ringing.

It reaches — without standing, because standing would change the distance — to the low wall of its horseshoe. The stones are old, weathered into the general weathering. Lichen-plastered. It does not take one away. It does not build a new cairn to mark its visit. Instead it finds a loose slab at the base, one that has settled a hair out of true over however many ten-thousands of suspended moments, dulling the hum a fraction, and with the tip of one immense, careful finger, it nudges it a millimeter back into tune.

*Click.* Stone on stone.

The whole basin rings a fraction clearer. The roar does not get louder or quieter. It gets *truer*.

On the tor above, the watcher shifts its wrong-jointed weight. Unbraces one leg. Re-braces. It felt it.

That is all it will change. One millimeter. Enough to say: I hear what you are keeping.

In the afternoon that is not afternoon, it goes, as the young ones go, to the large tarn with the ring of stones flush in the mud like a hearth with no ash. It kneels. Its knees squelch faintly in the black moss sponge. It does not cast a reflection — or rather, it casts the ridge opposite, as is correct here. Wrong reflection is right reflection.

It waits until the secondary roar rises from the black surface, tuned just under the main. Footsteps, breathing, stone set on stone. The sounds that made the windbreaks, still circulating, unable to decay.

Most powers would try to release those sounds. Let them finish. Let water flow again, let rot happen, let time move on. Let the dead be dead and the living grow.

This one does not. It understands that here, history is not story. It is condensate. To evaporate it would be to erase them.

Instead it drinks.

It bends low — impossibly far, as they bend — and sips, not the water, but the memory just above the water. It takes into itself: the weight of a slab carried in too-long fingers, the patience of sweeping clean so the hum comes through unmuffled, the first time a keeper learned to step lighter and lift a hand's breadth up and scud without wind. It holds all of it, carefully, the way fur holds condensation without dripping.

It will carry it out. Down out of the cirque, into lowlands where flow still flows and things rot and grow to replace. There, where ears are not full of roar, it will remember aloud, once, to no one in particular: there is a high basin where white folded keepers keep a bell ringing. They are not lost. They are keeping.

That is its gift. Not rescue. Witness that can leave.

Before it goes, it takes a shift.

This is the heart of it. The keepers never sleep, not really. How could they, when the ringing must be kept on at fixed volume from everywhere and nowhere? Even their stillness is work. Even perching is bracing.

So the power, still folded in the horseshoe, still damp, still cold neither deepening nor easing, puts its whole folded belly to the swept slab and *listens on purpose*, the way they do. It takes the weight of the listening for a while. It holds the frequency steady in its own vast body, a sympathetic string, so that for one suspended moment that does not pass, the nearest keeper doesn't have to.

Rest, it says, without saying, in stone-tuned hum. I have it.

And on the tor, the white watcher — or one of its duplications seen from a slightly different angle down a straight line that returns — for the first time in longer than lowland time has words for, lets its nictitating lid close and stay closed. Not asleep. Just unbraced. It lets the ice-needles gather on its fur. It does not brush them off. Bright, unfalling. Its breath hangs a second too long and stays.

Nothing changes here. The light never fully arrives. The tarns do not ripple. The far wall does not get closer.

But for a while, the balance has one more stone-warm thing in it, keeping it exactly as it is — and that, to a keeper, is the only benediction that doesn't break the vow.

When it stands to go, it leaves nothing behind. No metal. No wood. No cloth. No right angle. Only the millimeter nudge, already weathering into the general weathering. Only a coil of its own shed warmth under the overhang, next to theirs, stiffening with frost, unrotting. A second skin kept dry. A reservoir, should they ever need it.

It crests the rim. The roar stays on behind it, at fixed volume, from everywhere and nowhere.

It does not look back, because looking back here changes the distance.
