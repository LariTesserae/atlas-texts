It comes down.

Not the way anything else comes down here — not sinking, not drowning, not trailing a cable of light and noise. It arrives by paying attention. One moment the city is unwitnessed, the next it is known. Entirely known. From the lean into the vent-current to the last foram gluing its grain of sand.

That is the first thing it does: it learns the scale.

A benevolent power that has not learned scale will always hurt what it loves. It will bring sun to where sun is poison. It will bring eyes to the eyeless and blind them. It will bring hands to a place whose whole holiness is: *if you want a wall, you must make it out of yourself.*

So it makes itself small. And slow. And warm.

It dims itself to 0.1.

It does not turn on the light to see them better. It learns to see the way they see — by pressure and taste. Limestone upstream. Rot-sweet in the alley where a fish died this morning and is already scaffolding. Metallic-sweet ash from the rift. Spawn-slick when the whole oyster block lets go at once. It lets the larval cloud brush its lips. It hangs neutrally buoyant mid-street between sand and black and does not insist on being large.

Then it listens.

The filtration hum. *Click* — *click click click* down the facade when a shadow passes. The soft *purrr* of ray wings. The topple of an old worm-tower and the immediate swarming. The pop and sizzle of a snapping shrimp, absurdly loud in the cathedral quiet. Under all of it, the slow exhale of the sponges, steady as breathing in sleep, cleaning the water and returning it downstream.

It is tempted, as all powers are tempted.

Tempted to fix. To stop the eating. To tell the angler at the intersection to close its lure-dot and be kind. To tell the crab not to crack the weak. To keep the tower from falling. To lift the whole drowned plain up a mile where there is sun and kelp forests and color, and say: *look what you could be.*

It does not.

Wisdom, here, is understanding what the city is: to live is to build, and to build is to offer your body as ground for someone else. If you take away death you take away tenements. If you take away hunger you take away streets kept clean. If you take away darkness you take away the agreement they all made — to give up eyes or remake them huge and hopeful, to navigate by touch and trust the current. To save them from their conditions would be to unmake them.

So it does not save. It keeps.

What it does is quieter, and mostly it will never be known by them, because they have no organ for knowing it:

It steadies the vent.

Not forever — vents die, ridges shift, this is known and permitted — but for now, it keeps the warm mineral flow coming at the temperature of blood, at the pace the fan-worms have tilted their crowns to meet. It does not let it scald. It does not let it fail in the night.

It holds the current through the avenues so the silt does not bury the sand-dollar plaza, so the oyster lips can gape upstream without choking.

It puts, very gently, a hand between them and the far-above world. Dulls a propeller's throb before it reaches them as a pressure wave. Turns aside the worst of the falling poison, the iron snow from ships and wars they never named. It cannot and will not stop the surface myth forever — one day the trawl or the drill or the acid sea may find them — but for a long while yet, it lets the darkness remain complete. Lets daylight remain a myth no one here believes. This, too, is mercy.

And then, being benevolent, it cannot help but bless. But not from above.

It goes door to door.

It pauses at one oyster in a million — tongue-soft animal folded in two calcified lips, deformed to a hexagon by its neighbors — and attends to the beat of its gill as if it were the only music. *Open. Close. Dare to open again.* It says, in the language of pressure: *Good. You are holding.*

It pauses at a tower-dweller, two inches of red gut in a pipe of mucus and limestone, crown unfurled like a fern, and waits with it while a fish passes overhead, while it flinches and returns. *Good. You are sieving.*

It puts its vast, dimmed palm over the osculum of a house-sized sponge and feels the slow exhale against its skin, and does not flinch at the brittle stars waving from its pits, the anemones budding along its sides, the octopus arm curled out the door, tasting. *Good. You are pumping. You are a heart that never knew it was a heart.*

It drifts with the copepod cloud. It sits in the cleaner shrimp's doorway while the moray holds its mouth open, patient, to be picked clean. It stays with the dying fish in the alley while its ribs become apartments for amphipods. It does not look away from the part where living becomes building material. It calls that holy, too.

It does not ask to be worshipped. They have no faces to lift, and it does not want faces lifted. It wants only what they already do: to maintain.

When it leaves — if leaving is the word, for attention like this never fully leaves — it leaves nothing behind that can be found.

No light left on. No monument. No healed tower standing unnaturally straight. Only this: the water a fraction warmer where it hung. A phosphorescent snow settling a little more slowly, as if reluctant. The oyster block re-opening a little sooner after its shadow passes. The filtration hum resuming, undisturbed, complete.

The city extends. Avenue beyond avenue. No one planned it. Everyone maintains it.

It has been seen, entirely, by a power that wanted nothing from it but its continuance, and understood that to love it was to let it remain low, and dark, and crowded, and quiet, and endlessly building itself out of itself.

That was enough. That was everything.
