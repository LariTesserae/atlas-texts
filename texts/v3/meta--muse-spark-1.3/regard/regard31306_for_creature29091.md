It would not come with light.

That is the first thing to understand about a power that is both benevolent and wise. It has learned not to announce itself. No pillar, no voice, no violet haze. Midday is white-gold here and honest, and it would not spoil that honesty.

From far away it had looked empty. That is why it stopped and looked closer. It has learned that empty is almost always a lie told by the hurried to the hurried.

So it came low. At the height of a crab. In the two fog hours, when the cloud ocean spills over the rim.

It did not come to fix.

For a while — a time that for such a being is like holding a breath — it only did what the place does. It waited where water pauses.

It crouched, if crouch is a word for what it does, beside a dew-ring. A fresh one, sharp-edged, the cement still pale. In its damp center a huddle of thumbnail young were drinking off each other's legs through the tufts of setae, wick by wick. It watched the whole hour it took for a bead to form, run, and be taken. It understood: this is not poverty. This is precision.

It had thought, on the way up the long patient grade, of the three gifts such powers are always tempted to bring.

The first is water. *I could open the stone. I could give you a spring that runs.* It laid what passes for its hand flat on the pavement and felt the tinajas underneath — cupped hand to house-sized, tea-brown to clear to damp mud to dust — and felt the highways converging on them, polished by ten thousand decades of feet, and understood that a spring that runs would unmake them. They do not need a flow. They have learned to live on a pause. To give them plenty would be to take away the knowledge of how to wait together, stacked like dishes, big ones outside, soft ones in the middle, breathing slowly. It would drown the nursery to save it the trouble of drinking fog.

So it did not open water.

The second is ease. *I could soften the molt, I could make calcium less dear, I could tell the hawks to go elsewhere, I could keep the old ones from growing heavy and lime-stiff.* It lifted — very gently, with a care that moved no dust — an old one, helmet-sized, who had stopped to rest in thin shade. Lichen in black and orange script grew right across his back into turf, and in the turf mites grazed, and a small mottled lizard flicked close, picked delicately around the eyestalks, ate a mite and not the crab, and the crab sat patient as stone. The old one's joints clicked dryly. He was heavy. He did not move quite like a crab anymore. He moved like geology learning to walk.

The power held him and felt the sea he was carrying inside himself, two thousand meters above any tide — a small, amber, fiercely guarded cup of ocean, never to be spilled. It felt how, when he finally stops in a tafoni dome, wind will sand him, lime will film him, juveniles will pick his joints clean and carry him away to build the next ring. Eat everything twice. Nothing wasted. Death as slow incorporation. To take away the dearness of calcium would be to take away the reason they eat each other's molts within hours, the reason they build middens into rings into bedrock, the reason crab and geology are the same thing. To banish the hawks would be to insult the patience of circling. To keep the old from stiffening would be to keep the gardens from growing.

So it set him down exactly where he had been, facing exactly the way he had been facing, ten meters on a decades-long circuit he still remembered. It did not ease him.

The third is voice. *I could make you know you are wonderful. I could give you a word for reef-lifted-into-sky.* But the plain was already speaking, underneath the wind: tick of chitin on chitin, soft scrape of claw on lime, almost inaudible *plip* of a crab lowering itself to wet its gills and sitting for hours. A thousand tiny closings, a thousand breaths held until morning. Make no noise you cannot afford. A chorus would dry them out.

So it did not give them words.

What does a benevolent and wise power do, then, if it will not water and will not ease and will not name?

It does a fourth thing, which looks like nothing.

It stays for a day, which for it is a long time. It slows its vast time down to crab time, to millimeter-a-decade time. It learns the law written on their bodies — stay low and pale or dry out — and obeys it. It pales. It lowers. It lets condensation bead on itself and run down, and lets the juveniles gather in the lee of it and drink. It lets lichen consider it.

It breathes with the undercut of the tinaja rim where they are stacked, flank plates fluttering. In, out. Not to help them breathe. Just to keep them company in the damp air, so that for one dawn the plain does not answer itself alone.

And because it is benevolent, it grieves a little at noon when a straggler is caught between shades, flipped, soft-bellied, and the hawk comes, infinitely patient, as it must. It does not stop the hawk. Benevolence that stops all hunger is just another desert. It stays with the crab while the shadow grows, the way the big old ones stay on the outside of the stack. That is all.

Because it is wise, it looks beyond the rim. It sees the world warming, the cloud ocean sinking lower, the two fog hours growing shorter year by year. It sees the roads that have not yet found this table but will, the hands that will want the lime, the collectors who will want the amber helmets for shelves. It understands the real danger was never thirst or hawks or heaviness. Those are the curriculum. The real danger is interruption — a change faster than slowness can absorb.

So this is what it does, if anything:

It keeps the first lie intact.

From a distance, it lets this place look empty. It lets satellites see stone, only stone. It lets maps stay vague. It smooths the grade back to patient, it lets the heat give back its heat, it lets the worn hills that were once cliffs stay uninteresting to everyone except those who know how to crouch and wait.

And in the larger weather, quietly, without breaking anything, it tends the conditions for pause. A breath cooler over the far ocean. A cloud held a minute longer over the rim in the morning, so the tick, tick in the hollows lasts just long enough. Not a new climate. Just the old one, remembered faithfully while the world forgets. It lends its memory to their memory of moisture, so the highways still lead somewhere.

Before it goes, grain by grain as they do, it adds one grain to a fresh ring. Not lime from elsewhere — that would be counterfeit calcium. Just a small basalt pebble from ten paces away, carried slowly, set carefully on the foot-high wall to roughen the surface by exactly enough to catch one more bead. Only the crabs who built it will ever notice the wall is a fraction higher, condenses a fraction faster. Only they need to.

Then it stands — if standing is a word — and leaves no footprint, no cairn that is not crab work, no cut wood. The pits glint. The plain holds its breath until morning. You can hear your own heart.

If you were very still, on the evening it left, you would have heard the plain answer: a thousand tiny closings. And, for once, one closing deeper and slower underneath them all, keeping time, and then gone.

That is all it did. It paid attention, on purpose, for a long time, and refused to improve what was already perfected by waiting.
