It noticed, because it notices everything that holds on.

Not from above, not all at once. The rumor of surface is ice to it too, in its own way - too bright, too fast. To see this place it had to come down. And a benevolent and wise power knows how to come down.

Slowly.

It did not bring light. That was the first mercy, and the hardest.

It wanted to - love always wants to bring light. To open the winter and pour down noon and show the Holders their own color, the red-brown and the olive, to warm the one or two degrees to three, to four, to make things quicken. To bring back eyes. To bring back fish to eat and be eaten, gulls, songs, clicks, a hurry worth watching.

It did not.

It understood: light will not increase here. That is not a wound. That is the rule. To flood this with day would be to kill it with kindness, to rot the peat-banks of half-living frond in a season, to wake rot which here is slow, almost thoughtful, and make it hungry. To bring eyes here would be to bring hunger. This place is complete. Nothing here is eaten. Nothing here eats in the way of mouths. To give it mouths would be unmaking.

So it dimmed itself.

It came the way you have to come here: kicking gently so as not to stir the silt that will hang for hours if you do. It made itself heavy and cold. Colder than you. It let the pressure take it, hand more heavy, hand less heavy, until it learned distance again not by sight but by squeeze. Until six meters and haze was enough. Until near-touching, everywhere, was enough.

It did not bring a lamp. It brought a dimming. A faint, cold greening, thinned to almost-black, so as not to blind the Pale Ones who comb the never-settling soup with cilia too small to see. It hung still until its own vast thoughts went stupid in the fingers, the way yours do, and that was prayer.

And then, because what lives here does not notice by sight or sound, but by touch, and very slowly — by pressure, and cold, and lean — it spoke in that language.

Pressure: it settled, vast and gentle, over a courtyard the size of a field, over platform, canal, wall, courtyard, stair, and let its weight be felt as water, nothing more. Not a crushing. A hand resting. *I am here. I am not passing.*

Cold: it did not warm. It cooled itself the final degree, to one degree above freezing, always, to match. It took the seeping, even, ancient cold into itself and kept it there, so the fur — wine-dark and bottle-green, spongy to the knuckle, tasting lime and crushed tile underneath — would not flinch. *I will not change the terms.*

Lean: it leaned. When the great slow tide turned over the continent at the pace of a sleeping breath, twice a day east, twice a day west, it leaned with it. When a stipe creaked against stipe, it creaked back, in its own body, too low for ears. When somewhere out in the dark miles a slab settled another millimeter with a soft thud, it stayed for the sigh of silt slumping off a terrace edge and falling, for a long time, through black water. It did not look away.

That is what it did, for a long time. Nothing that looks like doing.

It put what you would call a hand — though it has no hands — on a holdfast. White-yellow tendrils swollen into stone-pocks, into rope-drill holes, into oozing mortar, stitching slab to slab for a hundred years. The real mason after the masons. The kelp that are holding the city together now.

And it remembered, aloud in the quiet where sound travels too well and finds nothing to carry, the things the Holders preserve without meaning to:

The woven basket, still a basket under its coat of algae, on a step twenty treads wide. The coil of rope turned to iron-hard fiber. The black oak pole wedged upright in its socket, bearded with growth. The sandal print filled with hardened silt in a brickyard the size of a province, print after print going nowhere before the forest closes over. Tile. Brick set on edge in herringbone. Another floor beneath that.

*Someone made this with almost nothing,* it said, in pressure. *With stone pounders and copper chisels and fiber ropes and more people than stones. I saw them. You keep them. Thank you.*

Because that is what an eyeless body has never had: to be seen by eyes that do not eat.

Nothing with eyes lives here. That's the first thing you have to unlearn. So the power lent its eyes the way you lend a lamp - briefly, and without taking. It looked without darting, without blinking to mind. It watched the Fingers turn themselves a degree to port and back over the course of an hour to face the leaf-tea current. It watched the Feather-weed breathe what the kelp sloughs off. It watched frond over cheek and frond under chin close behind like water closing. And it did not want anything from it.

Before it rose — and rising here is a long grief — it did one small, physical thing. The only intervention. The wise kind.

Up on the high causeway where the slow current combs the silt clean, where the streamers are short and brutal, a lintel that still spanned between walls had finally exhaled after ten thousand years. It was leaning. In a season its fallen face would be furred over, stitched down. The forest knew how to catch it.

The power just stayed under it a little while, taking the weight, so it would not fall fast and stir the silt for days and smother the fur in miniature below. It took the weight while the holdfasts thickened and found their new grip, slab to neighbor. A midwife's hand. Then it let go.

It did not sign its work. It left no warmth. Warmth would be a scar.

On the way up toward rumor, it set a guard — not a wall, not an angel with a sword, which would be another kind of violence. Just an inattention. A quiet turning-aside in the minds of those above who would quarry, trawl, dredge, salvage, shine bright lights to see. Let them dream of other seas. Let the surface stay a rumor to them, too.

That was all.

It does not visit often. To visit often would be to make a path, and a path is a current, and a current is a change. But it has matched its own time now to millimeters per year. Lengthen a cell, then another. Soften a grain, then another. Once in what you would call a very long while, when a wall gives and leans and comes to rest against its neighbor, something in the water leans with it, takes a little of the weight, remembers the basket and the sandal, and says in pressure:

*Held. Kept. Good.*

And the forest, which does not notice you that way, notices — by touch, and very slowly — that it has been noticed, without being eaten.
