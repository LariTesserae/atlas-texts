It would not come in fire.

Fire is already here, banked and useful under boilers. It would not come in a clearing of the sky, or a drying of the boots, or a silence of the machines. All those would be a kind of killing.

It comes the way weather is born fifty miles away here — quietly, and then all at once close.

First as a pressure change in the hollow between towns. The keepers feel it in their teeth before their ears, the way they feel everything. A hair-off rhythm in the long chord: wind over fins + water over lips + current in wires + fire in boilers. Not wrong. Just — listened to.

For a power that is benevolent and wise does first what the keepers do: watches a gauge that has not moved in living memory, because it must still be watched.

It lies down.

That is the first strangeness. Powers are supposed to stand on fins and pronounce. This one lies down in sphagnum at ankle height, lets water rise around its weight, lets the green steam faintly with its heat. It smells peat and iron and clean rot. It lets its breathing fog and clear, fog and clear. It lets its heart slow, unhurried as peat water, to sixty, to fifty beats a minute. It hurts a little to breathe, and it does not fix the air.

It listens until the roar stops being noise and becomes place again.

And it understands the temptation. Any lesser kindness would want to *help*:

to bring birds to cross that enormous sky, insects to rise off the marsh, sheep to crop the cotton-grass. To bring sun to burn off the snagging cloud, trees to break the wind, history to break the schedule. To empty the warehouses that fill only to be fed back into the furnaces. To tell the old woman with the flat spade: stop cutting to the same white line, let it overgrow, let it die, let something *happen*.

Wisdom sees what that help would cost. This is a continent lifted too fast to have learned softness, holding itself together by patience. Rock that weeps but does not crumble. Rust that forms and stops. Frost that forms and melts. A green that eats stone-water and light and makes peat-memory a millimeter on top, a millimeter below. A people who have learned to be a small mouth so the green can go on being one huge mouth with no eater. Iron beasts idling their engines with infinite patience, blood-water in, steam-breath out, nerve-wire singing one unchanging note across mire.

It is not broken. It is *before*. After the uplift, before the wearing-down. To force hunger here, or progress, or erosion, would not be mercy. It would be to make it prematurely old.

So the power does not bring hunger. It honors the waiting.

It walks — splayed-footed for mire, sure-footed on frost-split fin, learning the gait — from light to light. A day, then three days, then a week of nothing but dripping green under white sky, with the far throb in its sternum. It accepts the dry-drier socks, the bowl of mire-stew in a tin bowl, the pallet over the dynamo where bones buzz all night. It does not ask where it came from or when it is leaving. It learns to eat at the bell because the bell rings because the shift changes because the bell rings.

On Tuesday it takes a flat spade and cuts moss.

Green felt has crept a finger's width up the hot culvert, over the sill, into the turbine hall. The keeper beside it cuts to the faint white paint mark, thirty years old, still exactly at the edge. *She remembers,* the old woman had laughed without sound in the roar. The power cuts to the line too, badly at first, too deep, then clean. And by next Tuesday it will be back to exactly the same line, as if trimmed by memory. It finds this, obscurely, comforting rather than mocked. It understands: this is not futility. This is liturgy. This is how a place with no animals tends its skin.

Forehead to forehead, almost touching, in the roar-deaf way, a young keeper shows it: a hand on an elbow means *come*. Two fingers on a pipe means *listen, this one is off by a hair*. At night, from window to window across the black mire, handlamps blink: *still running. still running. still running.*

The power, who knows many languages, learns this one and does not expand it much. Only a little. Only what benevolence requires without breaking wisdom.

It answers, lamp in hand, slow as synapse: *still running — and heard.*

The keeper blinks back. Pause. *still running.* Pause. Then, carefully, new: *heard?*

*Heard,* the power blinks back. *Held.*

That is its gift, and it gives one to each of the three that live here as one:

To the towns — the rooted beasts with blood of leats and nerves of pylons and gut of furnace — it lays a palm on a vibrating foundation, on a boiler wall, on a slender viaduct singing in the wind. Not to stop them. To ease them. The low iron drone in the teeth softens a semitone, an ache no keeper had dared name as pain goes out of it. *Good working,* it tells them in vibration, *good idling. Rest in your running.*

To the green — the clonal pelt with no edge, that climbs weeping cliffs before scree has settled and swallows boulders without rounding them — it breathes, and lets billions of undisturbed spores settle on its own damp coat, and carries a few away unharmed, and in return leaves a dream tucked in the peat: not of now, but of later. When the cliffs finally crumble, when waterfalls finally cut back, when air thickens and trees become possible — a memory of how to become forest without forgetting how to have been moss. It does not force it to grow taller now. It just tells it: *you will know when.*

To the people — short, broad, quiet, barrel-chested, soft-white handed, lichen-eyed — it does the smallest thing of all. It stays long enough that they are no longer performing maintenance for an absent multitude, but hosting a guest. It lets them be seen not as leftovers, not as skeleton crew, but as what the place has made to live in itself: mitochondria with spades, white blood cells, flora of iron. It learns to read water level by ankle, to hear trouble as tooth-ache before ear-ache. It gets damper, slower, greener at the seams. And when an old face lies down out in the green between towns and does not get up, it sits nearby while the moss does its slow swallowing, and remembers the name the roster will fold back into the same total.

It does not promise that erosion will never come, or history, or hunger. A wise power does not lie. The knives will slump. The scree will round. The trains will one day carry something somewhere for the first time instead of in a circle. Some Tuesday the moss will come back past the line, or not to the line, and that will be a signal, not a failure.

What it promises is this: when wearing-down begins, you will not meet it as forgotten things who idled unheard on a roof. You were heard. The chord was heard — wind over fins + water over lips + current in wires + fire in boilers — and added to the music of the universe, not as noise to be fixed, but as a held note.

Then it gets up, shakes water from its coat that will never dry, leaves the spade clean against the wall for next Tuesday, and walks out into the absolute hollow, miles of dripping green under white sky, until its lamp is only another faint smear on the cloud base.

The stacks breathe the same smoke. The clouds form over the same three ridges. Breath fogs and clears.

*still running,* blink the towns to each other across the mire.

And now, underneath, like peat under peat, a second note the keepers will keep and repeat without fully understanding why it steadies their slow hearts on the stairs:

*heard. held.*
