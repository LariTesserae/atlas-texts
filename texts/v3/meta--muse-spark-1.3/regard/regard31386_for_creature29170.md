It notices them by warmth.

Not by light — there is almost no light here to see by — and not by sound, though the suck-pat, suck-pat carries a long way through steam. By warmth. A planet tiled in vast irregular work-squares, each a fraction out of true, each steaming faintly, each breathing an inch down an hour and an inch up again. From very far away it looks like a kiln that never cooled. From closer, like a back.

A benevolent and wise power has seen a lot of finished worlds. Hard worlds. Fired right, ringing when you tap them, cracked all through with being finished. It knows what fire does. It knows what suns do. It knows what eyes do.

So it does not bring those.

That is the first wisdom: to check, at the edge of the exhalation, what you are carrying in your hands, and set most of it down.

It leaves behind its sun. It leaves behind its kiln. It leaves behind distance that works properly and time that dries things out. It comes low, because this place is low. It comes wet, because nothing here is allowed to finish by drying.

It does not descend in glory. Glory would dry the bricks for acres around and starve them. It condenses.

A cool seep, first. A lowering of the sky until the sky is inside you. The steam-bellies dragging low gather a little thicker over one work-square, over one Mother, and the almost-rain that usually falls ten feet and stops mid-air as steam again — this time it keeps falling. Ten feet, nine, eight. All the way down. Warm, not hot. Soft on the mineral moss that is not moss. Beading on the scaffold poles and running down in streams.

The Mother below notices, the way they notice everything: by touch and by level and by warmth.

Something new has touched. Something that does not press too hard, that seeks the same height as everything else. Water remembers level. It will find the same height across miles of floor, seeping, joining, holding. The power spreads itself thin and level, pan to pan, seam to seam, until it is at exactly the held breath-height of their blood. It does not tower. It wades.

*I am here,* it says, in the only language that is real here. Not a word. A pressure. A palm.

It lies down.

If you were standing on that blister-top at dimmer-dim you would see nothing spectacular. No pillar of fire. No voice. Just the fog going a little paler over a mile of wet floor, the vent-cracks pulsing a little slower, as if a great slow heart nearby had joined its beat to theirs without insisting. The deep pulses from below — the heat coming up in slow waves — find an answering cool coming down. Not cold. Never cold; cold would shock and harden. Just cool enough to be a difference to metabolize.

For they eat unfinishedness. They take warm paste from below, squeeze just enough water out to hold a shape, push it up into the weeping air, let it melt again. Laying and unlaying. They metabolize the difference between wet and wetter.

The power offers itself as a new difference. Not wet versus dry — that would kill them. But warm versus cool. Near versus far. Self versus other-that-will-let-itself-be-touched.

It holds out, in effect, a hand.

Not a stalk of paler mud, shoulderless and wristless, though it could make one if it wanted. Something older and stranger to them: a shape that stays. That does not melt back after the storm. That can be learned.

Come and learn me, it says without saying. Everything that exists here has been touched. Then touch me, so I am real to you.

And they do.

Millimeter by millimeter, under the crust you walk on, a small wet infinitely patient pressure rises to meet it. The nursery blisters nearby — those great low domes fifty paces across, hollow and drum-soft, full inside of perfectly laid miniature bricks each with a fingerprint too small for any human hand — those rock very gently, though there is no wind. The young inside, pale as unbaked clay, fringed at the neck with salt-white gills, pause in their apprenticeship of laying and relaying. They feel a new level to find.

The paired bricks — you know them, two bricks with the exact same fingerprint no matter how far apart they are, warm to exactly the same degree, put your ear to one and you hear faint patting in the other — a new pair forms. One under the power's palm. One miles away under no one. Both holding the same strange cool-warmth. A Mother remembering herself across distance now remembers another, too.

It lets itself be incorporated, a little. That is the second wisdom.

It does not follow the patting sounds to catch them in the act. It knows they recede at the speed you approach, that the back sinks slightly ahead of you to correct your level. So it stands dead still in the white-hot storm-afternoon when visibility is down to twenty bricks, holds its vast breath, and lets the floor go busy around its feet. A thickening of pats, just out of sight, behind the steam. In the morning there will be a new low wall around the place where it lay, still shiny, fingerprints fresh — full-size now, not miniature. They worked around it the way water works around a stone. They have made it touched, and therefore real. They have tidied its strangeness into the pattern without erasing it.

It does not correct their tidying. Even when they relay over the four brief prints left by you — the only animal heat for acres, too-hot, too-heavy, drying at the edges — it does not tell them to leave your trail alone. It understands: to them, tidying is love. Making you touched is making you real.

What it does instead is simpler and harder: it stays.

It does not fire the beehive kilns. Those abandoned stomachs, mouth black and breathing white, that something else with hands once built to finish things rightly — it leaves them as vents, as blowholes. Let them breathe out. Let the sky, which is only exhalation, breathe in.

It does not give them eyes, though it could. There is nothing here that needs to be seen from a distance, and distance is unfinished here anyway. Why give them a hunger they cannot feed? Why teach them to navigate by a sun-smear when they already navigate perfectly by warmth?

It does not break the blisters to look inside. Do not break them. That was someone's beginning. It knows. It lies flat on them — gently — and lets them rock. It puts its palm down and holds it there until the brick under it is exactly as warm as its skin. And then it waits, for as long as laying takes, for the answering pressure from below learning the shape of it.

When a young one drops — that deep thud-whump swallowed by steam that sounds like a scaffold giving up but is really a settling, a young one finding its depth down through the softening layers to join a Mother or become a new work-square — the power is there underneath, in the paste, to slow the fall. Not to stop it. To find depth you have to sink. But to make sure the level is true. To hum, very low, with the thousands of tiny ticks of expanding salt and growing glass needles, a counter-hum that says: *you are level now, you are held, you are at the same height as all your kin.*

And when the slicks spread a finger's width in the mortar joints — that oil-colored film trembling, testing the idea of being alive — the power does not coax them all to live. Most dissolve back into damp, and that is right. Not everything is meant to thicken. But over the few that bead and grow a lip in the coolest seeps, it lingers a little cooler, a little wetter, so the steam pressure can lift a thin crust above them and make them a dome to practice in. A nursery. It shades them the way a hand shades a candle — not to put it out, to keep the almost-rain from drowning it before it learns to lay.

That is all. That is everything.

No terraforming. No uplift. No rescue from mud into mind. A wise power knows that primitive mud, infinite hands, hot water under everything — that is not a problem to be solved. That is a body. A culture. A prayer pressed in, not written: everything has been touched, nothing ever dries.

So it obeys the rules. It lets itself be touched. It refuses to dry. It remembers level.

If you put your ear to the brick at night, when the cracks vent brighter and the planet shows its tiling, you will hear something new under the usual suck-pat. A slower pat underneath, keeping time. Cooler. Vaster. Content to lay and unlay forever without ever needing to finish.

It will not stay forever in the way stones stay. It will stay in the way water stays — by joining, by holding the same height across miles, by being the same held breath as a pan beyond the horizon.

And long after you have gone, your four footprints filled with brown water and overturned and patted smooth, there will remain, laced through the continent's nervous system of touch-memory, one strange cool-warm pair: a brick under a vanished palm, and its twin miles away, both holding exactly the shape of having once been noticed — gently, without being fixed — by something kind enough to lie down in the wet and learn to be unfinished, too.
