The inhabitants of this warm, sunken cavity are not rulers nor masters of this pocket, but rather **tenants bound inextricably to the slurry and the weeping stone.** They are creatures of saturation, their existence a patient, slow-motion negotiation with the relentless green.

### The Ground-Woven

The most numerous inhabitants are the **Silt-Drifters**, which are primarily the **root-systems and subterranean structures** of the dominant flora. These are not merely roots; they are the living infrastructure of the place. Seen only where the ground gives way underfoot, they appear as dense, pale, rope-like tangles, sticky with mineral residue and teeming with digestive bacteria. They are incredibly slow-moving, capable of extending only millimeters per cycle, but their collective mass holds the slurry together, forming temporary, buoyant islands upon which the heavier moss-plants stabilize.

When disturbed, the *thwock* of stepping into the ground is often followed by the immediate, silent closure of the silt around the displaced matter. To the Silt-Drifters, a footstep is a massive seismic event that briefly halts the enzymatic breakdown process, introducing a momentary vacuum before the surrounding organic matter rushes in to fill the void.

### The Winged Carriers

The slow, fat flyers are the **Hydro-Moths** or **Vapor-Skeeters**. They are defined by their near-invisibility and their heavy, deliberate flight. Their bodies are pale, nearly white or translucent green, designed to efficiently capture the scant photons filtering through the ceiling; they are metabolic sponges.

Their primary function seems to be the slow, passive transportation of spores and micro-fragments of the fungal-plants across the cavity. They are not agile; their flight path is dictated by the subtle thermal plumes rising from the warmest depressions in the floor—points where the most concentrated sweet fermentation is occurring. They drift rather than fly, giving the impression that the air itself is moving them.

When one lands, it settles on a broad, velvety leaf with noticeable weight, sinking slightly into the surface fibers. They do not chirp, buzz, or rub their wings; their communication, if any, is chemical, released as a faint, sweet exhalation that might only serve to attract the next thermal current or signal a suitable, chemically welcoming landing site for the next generation. They are living, slow-moving humidity sensors.

### The Remnant Dwellers

Those few entities that might be considered truly *faunal* are small, pale, and specialized for the dark, damp under-mats. These are the **Chitinous Scourers**, segmented invertebrates perhaps a centimeter long. They are functionally blind, relying entirely on chemoreception to navigate the labyrinthine spaces between the roots and the occasional patch of solidified, ancient sediment.

They consume the organic detritus that the primary plant life cannot immediately process, acting as the final, recycling mechanism. They move in subtle tremors through the silt, their pace mirroring the slow, measured *plink* of the ceiling condensation. They are entirely passive, only revealed when the density of the substrate is breached. They are likely prey for something else, but given the subdued predation level described, they are currently unburdened by significant threat, living out their entire lifespan within a meter of where they hatched.

### The Implied Architects (The Absent Presence)

While the current inhabitants are entirely biological and soft, the faint geometric mounds speak of something else: the **Silt-Settlers**. These were likely not biological in the current sense, or perhaps they were biological entities whose tenure ended long before the process of decay became this vibrant.

If we were to assign a behavior to these residues, it would be one of **stillness and rigid definition**. They represent the antithesis of the current environment—a hard boundary against the soft slurry. Whatever built those low, geometric foundations valued permanence and right angles. They have been utterly subsumed, not violently, but by gradual deposition. The Silt-Settlers now serve only as slight elevations in the landscape, anchors upon which the current aggressive growth can gain a small foothold against the pull of the muck. They are the ghosts of structure, entirely silent, their purpose utterly forgotten by the soft, warm world that now grows over them like a mold.
