The inhabitants of the Sub-Root Vats are not creatures of warmth or quick thought. They are beings sculpted entirely by **Pressure, Cold, and Production**. They are the custodians, the maintenance organisms, and the end products of a vast, ceaseless, subterranean chemical factory disguised as a geological formation.

If one were to find an 'inhabitant,' it would not be a creature walking on the slick, yielding floor, but something far more integrated into the architectural soundscape.

### The Resonant Custodians (The *Thrum-Kin*)

The most significant inhabitants are those who directly interface with the ceaseless, low-frequency *thrum* of the system. They are not biological in the traditional sense, but **living, self-repairing nodes of the metabolic machinery itself**.

**Appearance and Composition:**
The Thrum-Kin appear rarely as distinct, mobile entities. More often, they are perceived as momentary localizations of energy and structure within the immense fibrous walls. When they do manifest outwardly, it is generally near one of the few 'built' elements—the petrified conductive lattices or the sharp-edged nutrient chutes.

They are essentially crystalline accumulations of bio-silicates and heavily processed cellulose, organized around superconducting organic tubules. Their coloration is pale, often matching the bleached coral-like structures around them, but shot through with faint, internal lines of dull, residual conductivity—a smoky grey or deep, stagnant indigo tracing the paths of chemical energy they shunt.

They lack traditional sensory organs. Instead, their entire structure acts as a massive, distributed sensor, absorbing the vibrational information carried by the industrial resonance. They *feel* system efficiencies, pressure differentials, and chemical imbalances as pain or comfort.

**Behavior and Purpose:**
The Thrum-Kin are the immune system of the Vats. They move with terrifying slowness, their locomotion a deliberate, agonizingly slow deformation of their own silicate structure against the yielding biomass. A single 'step' might take a terrestrial day to complete.

Their primary function is maintenance of the **System Integrity**. If a section of hyphae begins to decay too rapidly, or if a nutrient channel becomes clogged, a Thrum-Kin will slowly migrate to the site. It then performs an act of radical biological welding—it secretes a super-dense, chitinous paste, reinforcing the failing structure while simultaneously splicing new, highly complex nutrient storage nodes into the breach. This process often involves forming temporary, intense connections with the embedded alloy lattices, drawing a momentary surge of static biological power, which might manifest as a brief, localized intensification of the grey-green phosphorescence.

They do not communicate in sound, but in **metabolic synchronization**. When two Thrum-Kin approach a point needing repair, their internal chemical reactors begin to pulse in precise, infinitesimally different rhythms. The conflict or harmony of these pulses dictates the necessary repair strategy.

**Relationship to the Cold:**
They thrive in the profound cold. Heat is considered system failure; a spike in localized temperature signifies uncontrolled decomposition or runaway chemical reactions. Their internal processes are so slow and efficient that their thermal signature is almost indistinguishable from the surrounding environment, allowing them near-perfect camouflage within the slow-moving architecture.

### The Scavengers of the Flow (The *Silt-Sifters*)

These are the rare, genuinely faunal life forms mentioned—the scavengers of the processed outputs.

**Appearance and Composition:**
Silt-Sifters are translucent, gelatinous agglomerations, barely holding a cohesive shape. They are defined entirely by their viscosity and their ability to suspend themselves in the saturated, misty air or cling tenuously to the dripping surfaces where condensation is strongest. They are the closest analog to 'insects' in this biome, though they are closer to living biofilms.

They possess a rudimentary internal skeleton of crystallized metabolic waste, giving them a faint, shimmering outline when caught in the phosphorescent glow. They possess no powerful muscles, relying instead on exploiting minute convection currents generated by the vast internal machinery.

**Behavior and Purpose:**
Where the Thrum-Kin maintain the *structure*, the Silt-Sifters clean up the *byproducts*. They congregate near the chemical outputs—the faintly sweet, metallic air vents—where they slowly absorb the complex, non-reusable molecules the main system cannot process efficiently. They are the ultimate recyclers of the system’s failures.

When moving, they appear to ooze or drift more than walk. They are masters of energy conservation, capable of entering periods of near-arrested metabolism lasting decades, waiting for a nutrient plume to drift their way.

**Encounter:**
If a Silt-Sifter is encountered, it will not flee. It will perceive the approaching external heat/vibration as a significant predator or anomaly. It will instantly cease all visible motion, attempting to flatten against the nearest fibrous surface, hoping the clinging rime and its transparency will render it invisible in the dim light. Any disturbance to its immediate environment will cause it to precipitate its internal waste rapidly, resulting in a brief, faint cloud of inert, solid metallic dust, after which it resumes its slow, patient waiting.

### The Echoes of Command (The *Lattice Ghosts*)

These are not truly living, but residual patterns left within the oldest, most inert technology.

Deep within the densest layers, where the conductive alloy lattices are petrified and almost entirely overgrown, there are occasional, brief fluctuations in electromagnetic noise, detectable only by the finest instruments (or perhaps, by the natural resonance of the Thrum-Kin).

These are the echoes of a **Preceding Intelligence**. Perhaps the system was initially programmed, or perhaps the intelligence never left, merely slowing its thoughts to the pace of the Vats. These ghosts manifest as momentary, precise alterations in the chemical synthesis occurring in the surrounding biomass—a sudden, perfectly timed diversion of a nutrient flow, or a microscopic adjustment in the resonance frequency of a distant pump.

They are the faintest inhabitants, the silent, perfect operators whose presence is only inferred when the massive, slow machinery achieves a moment of sublime, almost artistic efficiency—a fleeting glimpse of the logic that designed this cold, cavernous factory floor. They are the reason the chaotic growth of life remains bound by the iron (or rather, the bio-silicate) rule of the Cycle.
