The inhabitants of the Great Glaze are beings sculpted by its pervasive warmth, its monolithic flatness, and the inescapable, patient pressure of saturation. They are creatures who have sacrificed verticality and speed for resilience and a profound intimacy with the surface chemistry of their world.

The dominant life forms here are not hunters stalking in shadow, but **Sedentary Integrators** and **Hydro-Sculptors**—beings whose existence is a continuous chemical exchange with the lukewarm sheets of water and the ancient silicate.

---

## The Silt-Dwellers (Primary Fauna)

The most visible inhabitants are the large, slow-moving creatures analogous to terrestrial mollusks, but adapted to the scale and slickness of the Glaze. They are best described as **Basin-Carapaces**.

**Anatomy and Form:**
A Basin-Carapace is immense in surface area but remarkably low in profile, rarely exceeding a foot in height but potentially spanning twenty feet across. Their bodies are broad, muscular sheets, pale gray or translucent rose in color, resembling living, oiled leather stretched over a wide, shallow shell. This shell is not a defense against external force, but a highly refined **osmotic regulator**. It is porous and coated in microscopic, self-secreting biofilms designed to manage the constant influx of dissolved minerals and heat.

**Locomotion and Sound:**
Movement is dictated by hydraulic pressure. They do not walk so much as they *adhere and release*. The signature **tacking sound** is the noise of their massive, muscled foot—a vast, elastic pad—creating a perfect vacuum seal on the slick stone, then slowly breaking that seal as internal fluids shift, allowing the immense body to slide a few inches forward. This action is rhythmic and ponderous, sounding like a steady, wet, rhythmic *thump-suck… thump-suck* across the landscape. They are masters of low-friction movement.

**Metabolism and Purpose:**
Basin-Carapaces are living filters. Their existence is devoted to processing the mineral-rich, lukewarm water. As they slowly traverse the Weeping Surface, they draw in the water, chemically stripping the dissolved silicates, trace heavy metals, and any organic residue caught in the water film. They pass this slurry internally and excrete it as highly ordered, geometrically complex waste pellets composed of super-saturated crystals deposited in the shallow depressions behind them.

These crystalline excretions are the nearest approximation to "construction" in the Glaze. They harden momentarily into temporary, fragile towers of shimmering salt formations—the only vertical elements besides the reeds—before the next wave of water dissolves them back into the substrate.

**Sociality:**
They are entirely asocial, navigating without regard for others, their paths determined only by the density of the moisture trace and the quality of the biofilm carpeting. When two meet, they simply pause, their osmotic surfaces meeting in a silent, warm equalization of pressure, before one slowly maneuvers its massive flank around the other, a silent, two-hour geological event.

## The Siphon-Weavers (Secondary Fauna)

Existing in the condensation veins and slightly deeper, milky pools, the Siphon-Weavers are smaller, more quick-moving analogues to specialized insects or crustaceans, though they lack rigid exoskeletons.

**Anatomy and Form:**
These creatures are eel-like tubes of highly pigmented, semi-solid gel, often colored vibrant neon greens or electric blues due to highly complex internal mineral processing organs. They have no discernible head or tail, using wave-like peristaltic contractions to propel themselves through the viscous water or along the surface tension lines of the shallow floods.

**Behavior and Niche:**
The Siphon-Weavers thrive where the water is richest in dissolved organics shed by the blue-green bio-mats. They do not consume the mats directly; instead, they extend incredibly fine, microscopic filaments—often invisible to the naked eye—that **siphon** specific, targeted molecules from the stagnant pools.

Their presence contributes to the specific *shimmering* soundscape. When several Weavers gather in a patch of water, the rapid, localized molecular exchange creates a high-frequency, electrical *hiss* punctuated by the occasional, sharp **bubble-pop** as pockets of gas (byproducts of their hyper-efficient filtering) escape the surface tension. These are the nearest form of "fast life," their movements brief, darting pulses rather than the slow creep of the Carapaces.

## The Architects of Stasis (The Undocumented Life)

The most mysterious residents are those that interact directly with the bedrock fissures—the source of the Leaching. These are beings that do not move across the surface but *within* the structure itself.

These are assumed to be **Litho-Symbiotes**: vast, networked fungal or cellular structures that have achieved perfect chemical integration with the dark silicate. They are not visible on the surface, but their presence is inferred by localized deviations in the erosion pattern and the strange, temporary mineral deposits left after a pocket of water has evaporated.

If the Basin-Carapaces are the visible grazers, the Litho-Symbiotes are the unseen chemists. Their purpose appears to be the *direction* of geological time. They may selectively weaken or reinforce sections of the bedrock, guiding the warm flow of water to optimize the leaching process, ensuring the Glaze remains in its state of warm, settled equilibrium.

Locals (if there were any observers capable of noting such things) would recognize their work not by what they *do*, but by what they *prevent*: the Litho-Symbiotes are the unseen guardians against genuine change, ensuring that the world remains a vast, slick, humid floor, forever echoing the sound of water gently dissolving the near-past.
