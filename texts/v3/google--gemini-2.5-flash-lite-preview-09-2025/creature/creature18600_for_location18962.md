The inhabitants of the Weeping Spire are beings utterly bound to the churn and dampness of the living architecture, adapted not to static environments, but to the continuous process of structural reformation. They are specialists of the transient, the woven, and the perpetually saturated.

These are the **Rhizomorphs** (or perhaps, the *Weavers of the Weeping*).

### Physical Description and Adaptation

The Rhizomorphs are not large; the environment dictates a scale that allows them to navigate the dense, low-ceilinged, and highly segmented living spaces. They are roughly human-sized, but slender to the point of seeming skeletal, their bodies elongated and surprisingly flexible, possessing an unnerving, slow grace when moving across the unstable hydrostatic mats.

**Integument and Exposure:** Their skin is the most striking feature. It is not true skin but a complex, multi-layered cuticle, slick with the ambient moisture of the Spire. It ranges in color from pale, almost translucent white—where direct light never reaches—to deep, bruised greens and purples, laced with fine, hair-like, photosensitive cilia that seem to absorb every stray photon filtering down from the bioluminescent canopy. They are rarely dry, their bodies often exhibiting a faint, greenish sheen from symbiotic algae colonies grown intentionally on their dorsal surfaces, which function as secondary metabolic filters.

**Limbs and Locomotion:** They possess four primary manipulator limbs, long and triple-jointed, ending not in traditional fingers but in complex clusters of prehensile, keratinous tendrils tipped with hardened, chitinous hooks. These are specialized for three tasks: gripping slippery organic surfaces, delicately pruning living pathways, and administering precise, surgical grafts to living structures.

For ground travel on the shifting, buoyant floor, they utilize what appear to be thickened, padded foot-plates on their lower limbs, which distribute weight across the hydrostatic sacs and root-mats without rupturing them, allowing them to walk across areas that would instantly swallow an unacclimated visitor.

**Sensory Apparatus:** Vision, as understood by external observers, is secondary. Large, lidless eyes are recessed deep within protective folds of their facial structure, capable of detecting minute shifts in the green-yellow ambient light spectrum. However, their dominant senses are **vibration and chemoreception**. These beings “hear” the structural groans of the Phloem-Towers, sense the pressure equalization of hydrostatic pockets about to burst, and read the chemical signatures of local decay and vigorous growth through their porous integument and specialized organs located near the neck. They perceive the turbulence of the Spire as a constant, complex sonic and tactile map.

### Culture and Habitation: The Art of Grafting

The Rhizomorphs are not builders; they are **conductors of growth**. Their entire technological and cultural existence revolves around guiding the Spire’s rapid, chaotic biology into temporary havens of relative stability.

**The Grafted Dwelling:** Their homes are not ‘built’ structures but *induced growths*. They spend weeks, sometimes months, applying specific fungal cultures, mineral pastes scraped from deep, ancient structural ruins (if they can reach them), and precise pressure points to the supporting Phloem-Towers. These actions persuade the living architecture to swell, knit, and harden in a desired shape—creating a pod, a small, insulated chamber, or a sealed cistern for purified water. Once the temporary stability is achieved, the Rhizomorphs occupy the space until the local dynamics shift, at which point the pod is either carefully pruned free to be moved, or intentionally abandoned to reintegrate with the main biomass.

**The Toolset:** Their tools (the sharp bone/hardened wood artifacts) are treated as symbiotic extensions of their own biological manipulation. A Rhizomorph might carry a collection of specialized, hooked pruners and spatulate scrapers strapped against their limbs, often coated in layers of hardened sap that act as rudimentary sheaths and nutrient delivery systems for their own grafting efforts.

**Economy and Subsistence:** Their diet is purely autogenic, supplementing the vast amounts of bio-matter they exist within. They consume the slow, nutrient-rich sap harvested meticulously from the most stable, thickest vines and occasionally consume the gelatinous, nutrient-dense fruiting bodies of certain specialized, fast-growing fungi that appear only briefly before being consumed by insect swarms. Their metabolism is slow and highly efficient, necessitated by the cool temperatures and the labor of constant structural maintenance.

### Social Dynamics: The Chorus of Maintenance

The Rhizomorphs are not solitary, but their social structure is defined by task specialization around structural integrity. They move in small, tight-knit **Harvest-Crews** or **Pruning-Cells**, communicating not through loud speech (which would be lost in the ambient din), but through rapid-fire clicking and harmonic resonance—a complex series of low-frequency vibrations they pass through the root-mat itself, signaling danger or calling for joint structural reinforcements blocks away.

Their society prioritizes **Efficiency of Integration**. Status is measured by one’s ability to predict catastrophic shifts and to successfully guide a new dwelling pod to maturity before its anchor roots fail. Those who consistently miscalculate the turbulence are not punished; they simply become part of the churning biomass, swiftly absorbed by the hungry multitudes, their fate serving as an immediate, visible lesson to the survivors.

To encounter a Rhizomorph is to witness a being wrestling with the sublime chaos of its home. They are perpetually preoccupied, their movements dictated by the pressure differential readings in their own flesh, their entire existence being a continuous, damp argument against total dissolution into the Weeping Spire. They are the patient stewards of a place that respects nothing but relentless, vibrant growth.
