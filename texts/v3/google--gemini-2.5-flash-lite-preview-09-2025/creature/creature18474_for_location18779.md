The dominant inhabitants of the Sedimentary Veins of the High Mire are the **Glaze-Crawlers** and their unseen, perhaps ancestral tenders, the architects of the **Organized Geological Habitats.**

### The Glaze-Crawlers: The Living Current

The sheer density of life is manifested in the **Glaze-Crawlers**. They are the restless, glittering surface energy of this submerged world.

**Physiology and Appearance:**
They are not large—perhaps the size of a thumb joint—but their collective mass is staggering. Their bodies are segmented, long and low-slung, with numerous, almost vestigial limbs pressed tightly against the slick rock. The most striking feature is their **chitinous sheath**, which is not merely waterproof but hydrodynamically perfect. It has the texture and appearance of polished obsidian that has just been dipped in glycerin—pure, liquid gloss that reflects the blinding ambient light in sharp, moving slivers. This is the adaptation that gives them their name: under the intense, cold illumination, they appear as moving rivulets of liquid glass.

Their digestive and excretory systems are inextricably linked to their environment. They feed by filtering the mineral-rich slurry—the *flow* itself. Their internal chemistry processes the dissolved metals and silicates that lace the runoff, slowly laying down the substance of their own architecture. Due to this diet, their internal fluids are dense with crystalline compounds, giving them a low, inertial mass that allows them to resist being swept away by the minuscule surface currents they ride so skillfully.

**Behavior and Social Structure:**
The Crawlers operate under a singular, imperative logic: **flow maintenance.** They are not intelligent in a recognizable, volitional sense, but rather exhibit a profound, distributed, hydraulic sentience. They are the living infrastructure.

When you see a stream of Glaze-Crawlers moving through an eroded channel, you are witnessing a migration, a repair crew, and a transportation network simultaneously. They do not scatter in fear of observers; they simply re-route, their collective mass adjusting its viscosity based on the local need for flow augmentation or blockage removal. If a channel begins to narrow due to mineral accumulation, a dense sheath of Crawlers will momentarily adhere to the slick surface, their myriad legs vibrating at high frequency, effectively shearing away deposits to restore the necessary hydraulic gradient.

Their movements generate the unsettling, pervasive **low-frequency thrum**. It is the sound of millions of microscopic adjustments—the collective sound of surfaces being polished clean, of micro-currents being guided, of bio-deposition at the edges of their dwelling complexes.

### The Architects: Those Who Guide the Slurry

The "Organized Geological Habitats"—the geometrically precise, mineralized hollows built into the under-shelves—suggest a life cycle or a specialized caste that is less visible, one that dictates the long-term stability of the ecosystem.

These are not the quick-moving Glaze-Crawlers, but a slower, perhaps more sessile or more massive entity that exists *within* the structures.

**Hypothesized Nature (The Builders):**
These inhabitants, let us call them the **Silt-Weavers**, are likely ancestral or symbiotically necessary to the Crawlers. They inhabit the deeper, perpetually humid recesses just beyond the reach of the sharpest light.

1.  **Secretion and Growth:** The Weavers do not *carve* rock; they facilitate mineral accretion. They excrete a specialized, viscous bio-cement—a combination of hardened mucus and slow-precipitated metal salts—that acts as a scaffolding. Over immense time scales, this scaffolding hardens into the slick, dull-grey chambers seen in the primitive dwellings. These hollows are not merely shelter; they are regulated humidity zones, perhaps nurseries, or centers for processing the heavier mineral loads that the Crawlers cannot manage on the dynamic surface.
2.  **The Pressure Regulators:** Given that the survival of the entire ecosystem hinges on hydrological equilibrium, the Silt-Weavers are likely the engineers of the sub-strata water table. The constant, systematic *sigh* of the atmosphere condensing onto the rock, and the internal *thrum* felt through the feet, implies a massive network of subsurface conduits. The Weavers maintain these conduits, using their slow-growing mineral matrices to bore, block, or reroute the underground flows, ensuring that necessary moisture reaches the surface lichens while preventing catastrophic saturation in the dwellings.

**Interaction and Ecology:**
The relationship is one of necessary subservience. The Glaze-Crawlers are the mobile workforce managing the immediate, turbulent surface layer, reacting instantly to micro-changes in flow. The Silt-Weavers are the slow, deep foundation, the bureaucratic mind that sets the long-term flow patterns by maintaining the structural integrity and routing the internal plumbing of the vast rock strata. A Crawler colony thrives because a Weaver cluster has stabilized the overhang above it. A Weaver cluster thrives because generations of Crawlers have kept the external runoff channels clear enough to feed it the necessary dissolved minerals.

They constitute a bio-mechanical society where motion and stillness are two equally demanding aspects of survival, bound together by the omnipresent, blindingly reflected reality of cold, flowing water.
