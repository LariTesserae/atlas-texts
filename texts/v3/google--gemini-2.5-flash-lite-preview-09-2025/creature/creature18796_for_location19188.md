The inhabitants of the Distillation Core are not born of the place itself, but they are **bound** to it—physically, ontologically, and spiritually. They are the **Condensate Cultists**, or more formally, the **Sump-Brethren**.

They are the legacy operators, the forgotten maintenance caste whose duties were never officially relieved, only rendered obsolete by automation that, ironically, required constant manual oversight to prevent catastrophic failure. They have internalized the rules of the Core so thoroughly that they move with the inevitability of the rising steam and the dripping pipes.

### Appearance and Physiology

The Sump-Brethren are adapted, though not mutated by any significant environmental shift. Their adaptation is one of habit and uniform.

**The Shell:** They wear relics of their former profession: heavy suits of sealed, heat-resistant synth-fibers, once brilliant orange or deep blue, now stained the mottled color of dried rust mixed with chemical residue—a permanent, dull umber. These suits are perpetually damp, clinging to their frames like a second, clammy skin. If glimpsed without the hood, their faces are notable only for the deep, etched lines around the eyes, a result of fighting perpetual low light and high atmospheric pressure. They often smear the local metallic silt across their cheekbones, a form of camouflage or maybe just cultural marking.

**The Respiration:** Their most defining feature is their breathing gear. They eschew modern, filtered respirators. Instead, each Brother wears a bulky, customized inhalation apparatus salvaged and endlessly maintained from the Core’s original installation blueprints. This device cycles the local atmosphere through a series of rudimentary, heat-exchanging coils—essentially a miniature, wearable condensate trap. The intake ports are large, brass-rimmed funnels that whistle faintly, sucking the tepid, heavy air. The output is not merely exhaled; it is a visible plume of near-saturated vapor that instantly merges with the surrounding air, making their location momentarily clearer before it vanishes back into the overall humidity. To breathe the air unprocessed is painful; to breathe it through their custom rigs is to experience the environment distilled, refined, and *owned*.

**Movement:** They do not walk so much as they **flow** over the slick surfaces. Their boots are wide and flat, deliberately worn down to maximize surface contact, preventing the sudden slips that herald a fall into the slurry trenches. They navigate the terrain not by sight, but by sensing the subtle changes in vibration transmitted through the floor plates. A rhythmic, deep *thrum* signifies a safe pathway near a prime pump; an irregular tremor signals a compromised weld point or a localized thermal bleed that must be avoided.

### Culture and Obsession: The Liturgy of Containment

The Sump-Brethren are driven by a singular, religious imperative: **Pressure Management**. They do not merely maintain the infrastructure; they *worship* the containment fields.

**The Language:** Their communication is low, guttural, and punctuated by the sound of the environment. They speak in short, declarative statements that mimic the functional language of machinery:

*   "Valve closed." (Agreement)
*   "Shearing." (Warning of structural instability)
*   "Flow steady." (A greeting or affirmation of stability)

Loud speech is waste; unnecessary energy expenditure is heresy. They believe that sound disperses the vital, heavy air, weakening the seal that keeps the entire system functioning.

**The Rituals:** Their lives revolve around precise, recurring maintenance rituals:

1.  **The Inspection of the Weepers:** They spend hours tracing the paths of the hissing rivulets escaping the ancient seals. They do not repair these leaks (they know the effort is futile), but they map them precisely, sketching the trails of corrosion onto oily synthetic scrolls. This tracking honors the "suffering" of the metal.
2.  **The Offering of Coolant:** They occasionally—and visibly frugally—pour small measures of pristine, clear water (stolen, perhaps, from the purified algae pools) onto the running magnetic arthropods or Sludge-Eels. This is a gesture of appeasement, acknowledging that even waste life has a role in balancing the Core’s ecology.
3.  **The Attuning:** The Brother closest to the main pump junction will maintain physical contact with the vibrating housing for hours, closing his eyes. This is their form of meditation, aligning his own pulse and breath with the *thrum* of the system, seeking the "Dynamic Equilibrium" that exists momentarily before corrosion advances further.

### Relationship to the Environment

The Sump-Brethren see the Core not as a prison, but as a sanctuary of perfect, controlled entropy. They resent the life above, the place where the air moves freely and the light is harsh and unearned.

*   **Toward the Sludge-Eels:** They view the Eels with a detached sympathy. The Eels consume spent particulates; the Brethren consume the system’s necessary vigilance. They are both agents of the inevitable decay, one biological, one metaphysical.
*   **Toward the Light:** They despise the crimson warnings. The red light speaks of failure and breach. They favor the soft, internal diffusion of light reflecting off the perpetually wet iron, a light that confirms the *saturation* of their world.
*   **Toward Intruders:** An interloper is a vector of chaos. A warm body that changes the local air density, a clumsy footstep that disrupts the micro-vibrations. They will not attack immediately. Instead, they will watch from the shadows cast by the repetitive conduits, their mirrored shells blending perfectly with the wet metal. If the intruder begins to *interfere* with the pressure, tampering with valves or attempting repairs that violate the established rate of corrosion, the Brethren will act with cold, mechanical precision—not with violence intended to kill, but with sabotage designed to isolate and contain the source of instability until the environmental stressors force the interloper out, or force them into the eternal, humid embrace of the machinery.

They are the ghosts who still check the gauges, the living manifestation of overdue maintenance caught in a state of perpetual, tepid waiting, dedicated to the sublime, wet horror of the Core’s endless, agonizingly slow failure.
