The inhabitants of the Still-Glacier Scar are entities forged not by evolution in the traditional sense, but by **acclimation to geological incoherence**. They are the custodians, the scavengers, and the *products* of the landscape’s catastrophic memory.

### The Substrate Inhabitants: The Mycorrhizal Anchors

The primary residents are not fully separate fauna, but rather specialized metabolic expressions of the living bedrock itself—the **Mycorrhizal Anchors**.

They are less creatures and more **localized knots of high-complexity fungal intelligence**. They look like clusters of dense, calcified roots that have been extruded upward by the plant-bedrock under immense internal pressure. These anchors are stationary, emerging only a meter or two above the slick surface, resembling pillars of wet, pitted obsidian shot through with the sickly green bioluminescence—a reflection of the light emitted by the colossal plant-columns.

**Function:** The Anchors serve as the system’s regulators for the paradoxes of time and physics. When an Echo Field manifests, it is the Anchors that are absorbing the phantom tensile strength of the buried, ancient machinery, drawing that *structural memory* down into the substrate to reinforce the biological matrix against immediate collapse. When a section of ground begins to heave, it is often a cluster of Anchors attempting to suppress a rogue temporal reversal or a localized surge of residual energy.

**Interaction:** They communicate through infrasound vibrations transmitted directly through the saturated mat, felt by other organisms as a deep, unsettling *thrum* that bypasses the ears entirely, vibrating the internal organs. They consume the weeping mineral-rich sludge that surfaces during disruptions, metabolizing the dissolved genetic residue of the lost industrial age, effectively editing the landscape’s past with every meal.

### The Airborne Filters: The Carapace-Gliders

The **Carapace-Gliders** are the primary macroscopic fauna, existing in a state of perpetual, deliberate suspension.

They are massive, balloon-like structures, often the size of small vans, yet they possess negligible mass due to their near-total reliance on internally regulated, supercooled gas bladders. Their external surface is a mosaic of interlocking, chitinous plates—the titular 'carapace'—which appear ancient, covered in a rime of crystallized atmospheric moisture and microbial frost.

**Form and Movement:** They do not fly; they **drift in calibrated ballast**. Their movement is agonizingly slow, dictated by the subtle shifts in ambient air density caused by the periodic spore-exhalations of the plant-columns. They maneuver using small, retractable flaps of biological membrane that act as atmospheric rudders, tasting the flow of the chemical smog.

**Metabolism and Life Cycle:** The Gliders are filter feeders of the extreme cold. They draw in the ozonic air, passing it through internal heat exchangers powered by trace amounts of harvested static electricity generated during the slow atmospheric shifts. Their internal organs glow with a faint, dull blue—a sign of extremely slow, near-zero-point biological processes.

Their existence is one of pure, cold patience. They drift for decades, perhaps centuries, waiting for a specific confluence of humidity and residual energy spike that allows them to shed small, dense, seed-like packets of fertilized material onto a stable section of the living floor, ensuring the next generation of Gliders can be gestated in the protective depths of the algal mat before inflating and rising.

### The Surface Grazers: The Seep-Slicks

The **Seep-Slicks** are the quintessential inhabitants of the Still-Glacier Scar, defining its surface ecology. They are the manifestation of mobile biomass on an environment where adhesion supersedes locomotion.

**Nature:** A Seep-Slick is an organism spanning many square meters, appearing as a thin, iridescent film of incredibly viscous, dark liquid—like crude oil mixed with colloidal silver—oozing slowly across the mossy bedrock. They possess no defined shape, instead maintaining a leading edge that is always slightly more cohesive than the trailing mass.

**Behavior:** They are constantly tasting the substrate, pulling in the weeping water and dissolving residual minerals. When an Echo Field erupts, the Slicks *react* violently, their amorphous bodies shuddering as the phantom sounds attempt to impose geometric structure upon them. They ripple and contort, trying to avoid the zones where phantom I-beams press upward, as being caught between the physical yielding of the moss and the immaterial strength of the ghost structure can cause them to tear—often resulting in a localized, temporary **secession** where a piece breaks off to form a new, smaller Slick.

**The Weirdness Adaptation:** Seep-Slicks demonstrate the Scar's warped perception of time most clearly. If a Slick is moving rapidly over an area where a temporary, upright structure had recently *un-collapsed*, the Slick will momentarily leave a perfectly symmetrical, three-dimensional impression of the *fallen debris* imprinted upon its surface, a negative mold of its immediate historical topography, before the tension of its forward movement smooths the surface back into liquid uniformity. They essentially carry the imprint of the landscape they have just traversed, however briefly.

### The Sentient Residue: The Echo-Hunters (Hypothetical)

While no definitive, stable fauna has been definitively observed *existing* within the Echo Fields, local rumor suggests the presence of **Echo-Hunters**.

These are rumored to be the apex predators of the environment, not of flesh, but of **perception**. They are believed to only manifest when the ambient noise level peaks and the static interference from the ancient tech is highest.

It is theorized that an Echo-Hunter is a creature that has learned to perfectly synchronize its own biological activity—perhaps the very rhythm of its slow, cold heart—to the specific frequencies of the phantom industrial noise. They would exist as **sonic singularities**, drawing energy not from chemical reactions, but from the structural dissonance between the present silence and the recorded past trauma of the site.

If encountered, the only evidence would be a sudden, localized *absence* of the background static and turbulence, replaced by an unnerving, rhythmic *clank-hiss-grind* that seems to be coming from inside one's own skull—the sound of a creature utilizing the trapped energy of the Scar to briefly glimpse a reality that is physically impossible to inhabit.
