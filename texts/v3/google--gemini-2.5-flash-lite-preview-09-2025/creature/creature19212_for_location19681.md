The inhabitants of this sunken cistern are the **Reservoir Weavers**.

They are not creatures that walk in the traditional sense, nor do they rely on the dull, filtered light for cognition. They are beings intrinsically fused with the density and moisture of their environment, their very existence predicated on the management of the water flux.

### Physiology and Appearance

The Weavers are not individually distinguishable in the way a Human might recognize another. They appear as localized *concentrations* of the environment's inherent materials. A Weaver is best described as a **mobile accretion of packed earth, silica shard, and highly organized mycelial sheath.**

Their bodies are low-slung, wider than they are tall, mimicking the pressure-management architecture of the cistern itself. They possess a dull, matte texture, the color of the damp, dark conglomerate walls, occasionally showing the faint, oily slick of surface moisture.

Their most remarkable feature—and the source of their name—is the network of **biosynthetic conduits** running just beneath their superficial layer. These conduits resemble the greenish-black tangles of exposed, corroded copper pipework found integrated into the walls. These are the Weavers’ respiratory and circulatory systems, actively drawing in the hyper-saturated air and forcing it through dense, high-pressure filters made of calcified fungal structures.

They move through the cistern not by locomotion, but by **rheological flow**. When a Weaver decides to shift position, the packed earth of its form loosens its internal cohesion, allowing the water content to temporarily lower its viscosity. They slide or flow across the slick floor, leaving behind a temporary, slightly drier track before the black membrane begins its slow, weeping process of re-saturation.

### Cognitive Structure and Society

The Weavers operate under an intense, single-minded imperative: **Structural Integrity via Water Equilibrium.** They do not possess language in any audible sense, as the low hiss of condensation and the steady *plink* of dripping water already constitute a medium too dense for unnecessary sound.

Their communication is **vibrational and thermal**. They transmit information through precise, rhythmic adjustments to the internal pressure they maintain. When a section of wall is improperly sealed, or when the atmospheric condensation rate spikes, the affected Weavers will subtly change their internal thermodynamic output. This creates a pressure wave that travels through the packed earth floor and vibrating walls, a language made of structural adjustment. A large group communicating might cause a low, resonant *thrum* that is felt more in the bones than heard by the ear.

There are no leaders, only **Nodes of Focus**. When a repair is required—say, to reinforce a segment of wall where the embedded ceramic shards are beginning to loosen—several Weavers will approach the area. They merge their forms, increasing their density to match the required "Density 2.7" of the wall material. They temporarily sacrifice their individual mobility, becoming living mortar, pressing their mass against the compromised area until the *thermodynamic bleed* from the deeper strata stabilizes the local moisture level.

### Relationship to the World

The Weavers do not consume food; they **assimilate infrastructure**. Their existence is a slow act of recycling the cistern itself. The mineral efflorescence on the walls, the fungal filaments, the very silt dragged up by the pressurized water conduits—these are broken down and integrated into the Weavers' bodies. They are the physical manifestation of the maintenance cycle.

They avoid the few, sparse patches of actual, parasitic fungus, viewing them as competition for the essential, stable moisture content. The small, chitinous fauna that scuttles beneath the floor are either ignored as transient contaminants or slowly absorbed if they breach the surface membrane during a feeding cycle.

The Weavers view the ceiling of the basin—the only barrier between them and the undescribed vastness above—not as a limit, but as the source of all tension. The slow drip from above is the constant threat that necessitates their eternal, cold, damp vigilance. They do not dream of escape; they dream only of perfect, unbreachable containment, an eternal state of cool, saturated, and perfectly maintained stasis within their room-scale world.
