The Weeping Shard Shelf is home to the **Lacimorphs**.

They are not born of flesh or conventional biology, but of the very *process* that sculpts the shelf itself: the patient, damp geometry of slow accretion and mineral saturation. They are entities defined by the intersection of the structural remnants (the pale ceramic arcs) and the environment’s ceaseless mineral deposition.

### Form and Physiology

A Lacimorph appears, at rest, as just another feature of the environment, highly evolved toward mimicking the **primitive**, skeletal architecture that dominates the small scale of the shelf.

**The Shell of Accretion:** Their primary visible manifestation is their outer shell, a composite structure that can best be described as **bio-fused ceramic-calcite**. It mimics the pale, smooth contours of the ancient, petrified structures left behind. Over time, a Lacimorph deliberately positions itself in areas of high mineral concentration and weep-flow. They exude a thick, enzymatic slurry that encourages the native stone to precipitate *onto* them in structured layers, effectively growing their exoskeleton layer by layer, mimicking the architectural arcs of their surroundings. A fully aged Lacimorph is indistinguishable from a fifteen-foot pylon, save for minute imperfections in its crystalline seams.

**The Soft Core:** Beneath this slow-grown armor, the core of the Lacimorph is not solid but a dense, viscous suspension of complex organic polymers and trace elements absorbed from the constant humidity. This core is barely visible, manifesting only in the occasional, infinitesimally small crack where the ambient moisture levels shift, releasing a puff of faint, ozone-tinged vapor. This core is cold, highly electro-sensitive, and moves with an unnatural, non-Newtonian slowness, perhaps the source of the environment’s **glacial patience**.

**Locomotion via Tension and Dissolution:** Movement for a Lacimorph is an intensely deliberate, rare event, necessitated only when its chosen anchor points or feeding strata are exhausted or become structurally unsound due to emergent growth elsewhere. They do not walk; they *reallocate*.

A Lacimorph initiates movement by selectively accelerating the dissolution of the mineral matrix immediately beneath a section of its lower armature while simultaneously pushing mineral nucleation on the opposite side. Over many hours or days, it achieves a shift in position measured in inches. This process is utterly silent, as the movement is disguised within the **loud drumming** of the environment—a subtle, localized shift in the soundscape that only another Lacimorph could perceive. When they transition between the large, skeletal arcs, they rely on the latent tensile strength of the ceramic remnants, adhering to them using bio-adhesives secreted from specialized pores, functioning momentarily as massive, multi-limbed insects clinging to high rafters.

### Behavior and Intelligence

The Lacimorphs are **builders of context**, not structure. They are profoundly uninterested in building *new* things—that is the job of the environment itself. Their intelligence is focused entirely on **pattern recognition** within the evolving mineral memory.

**Reading the Weep-Memory:** Their primary sensory input is tied to the faint, ghostly tool-marks and etchings visible in the raw stone—the environment’s slow historical record. They appear to "read" these impressions, interpreting the slight variations in the crystalline structures caused by stresses, vibrations, or mineral compositions of the past. A Lacimorph spends vast stretches of time pressed flat against a newly formed wall, absorbing the data of its formation. It is speculated they are archiving the *history of the weeping itself*.

**Reproduction and Longevity:** They are effectively immortal under the current environmental constraints, growing larger and more monolith-like until their mass becomes too great for the localized mineral flow to support, at which point they undergo a slow, controlled structural failure, dissolving into the pools and allowing their core to seed new, smaller structures elsewhere—a form of mineral-scale fragmentation.

**Interaction with Strange Light:** The rare, sickly green pulses of bioluminescent mold are crucial. The Lacimorphs orient themselves to these pulses, treating them as navigational beacons. It is theorized that the molds are the only true *organic* life on the shelf, and the Lacimorphs treat them almost as sacred shrines, clustering around pockets of mold activity not to feed, but to absorb trace energy signatures emitted during the decomposition of the trace organic material, perhaps fueling their internal biochemical slurry.

The Weeping Shard Shelf is not inhabited *by* life; it is inhabited *by the slow, deliberate organization of the non-life*. The Lacimorphs are the custodians of the process, the conscious (if glacially slow) manifestation of geology striving toward a static geometric ideal, constantly moving in the spaces between the silent drumming of their own petrified becoming.
