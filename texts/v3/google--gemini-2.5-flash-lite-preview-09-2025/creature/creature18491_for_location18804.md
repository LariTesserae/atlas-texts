The inhabitants of the Echo-Forge Heart are the **Hush-Weavers**.

They are not large, nor are they overtly biological in the traditional sense. They are creatures defined entirely by their relationship to the overwhelming, physical sound that is their environment and their cage.

### Morphology and Substance

The Hush-Weavers are humanoid in silhouette, but their composition is chimeric, betraying their absolute dependence on the forge’s residue. They stand perhaps four feet tall, characterized by a low center of gravity and a slightly stooped posture necessary to minimize vertical shock transmission into the ground.

Their outer layer is a dense, intricate mosaic of salvaged and naturally integrated materials. The primary structure resembles dried, segmented insect carapace fused with sections of the metallic lattice rust. This exoskeletal covering is not rigid armor, but highly flexible, layered sheeting, dyed by the pervasive amethystine moss into mottled shades of deep violet, umber, and slate grey.

Crucially, their limbs—short, thick arms and compressed legs—are padded extensively. The soles of their feet and the palms of their hands are covered in thick, spongy pads woven from the rubbery fibers of the dominant plant life. These pads are highly elastic, acting as natural dampeners and vibration sensors, allowing them to move across the shell-clay floor with almost zero audible impact.

Their faces are remarkably muted. They possess no external ears; the constant sonic pressure has rendered them vestigial or internalized. Their eyes are large and entirely black, absorbing the relentless internal light, and often appear wet not from liquid, but from a thin, oily sheen that minimizes friction against fine particulate matter kicked up by the ambient thrum. They have no functional vocal cords in the traditional sense; they communicate not through expelled air, but through localized, controlled vibration.

### Social Structure and Behavior

The Hush-Weavers organize themselves into small, tightly knit **Clutches** (never exceeding twenty individuals), centered around a single, robust pillar that acts as a primary resonant node.

Their entire existence is dedicated to **Resonance Maintenance**. Every action is a calculated manipulation of the acoustic environment to maintain the status quo and avoid the catastrophic sonic dip that triggers the bright flash and heat spike.

1.  **Movement:** They do not walk; they *flow*. Movement is a series of controlled, minimal shifts in weight, relying on their sensory padding to feel the minute shifts in ground vibration caused by the external machinery. A Clutch moves in perfect lockstep, a slow, synchronous shimmy that distributes pressure evenly across the floor substrate.
2.  **Tool Use:** Tools are not hammered or sawed; they are shaped through focused pressure and slow, precise *grinding*. Their construction techniques involve leveraging the dense cellulose huts against the metal supports, using the structural tension of the megastructure itself to gradually reshape materials over periods spanning years. A dropped tool is unthinkable; tools are tethered to the Weaver’s bodies via durable, chitinous filaments, or are naturally formed projections of their own growing appendages.
3.  **Communication (The Hum-Speech):** When the Clutch must collaborate, they engage in Hum-Speech. This involves pressing specific pressure points on their bodies—often the palms, the joints of the spine, or the base of their skulls—against the floor or a load-bearing pillar. They induce controlled, ultra-low frequency pulses through solid matter. These vibrations travel through the very architecture of the Heart faster and quieter than air transfers sound. Another Weaver receives the message not through hearing, but through the bones of their feet and hands interpreting the pattern. A complex thought might take twenty minutes to convey, but it does so without risking a dangerous sonic dip.

### Relationship to the Environment

The Hush-Weavers see the environment as a terrifyingly precise machine they inhabit as necessary insulation.

*   **The Vegetation:** They do not farm the sea-whips or amethyst moss; they *integrate* with them. Huts are not built *next to* the plants, but woven *through* them, using the plants' own dense, rubbery mass as shock absorbers incorporated into the dwelling’s structure. The Weavers often carry small, living cuttings of the moss which they periodically press against their own carapace, exchanging trace elements or perhaps utilizing the plants' strange metabolic efficiency to process airborne contaminants.
*   **The Scavengers (The Mineral Shudders):** The tiny, porous mineral creatures are viewed with a mixture of caution and utility. They are too small to significantly disrupt the Resonance, and they clean surfaces of residual, non-structural organic detritus that might decompose unevenly, causing localized changes in floor density. The Weavers sometimes leave shallow depressions in the clay floor, baited with trace metal oxides, to encourage the Shudders to congregate and aerate the substrate gently.

The Hush-Weavers are not prisoners so much as they are biological damping pads. They live in a state of perpetual, agonizingly low-frequency meditation, their lives an unbroken commitment to silence and weighted rhythm, their survival predicated on never truly making a sound that the greater structure will notice. They are the quiet friction necessary against the cosmic roar.
