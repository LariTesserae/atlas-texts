The residents of the Weeping Refinery are not simply inhabitants; they are **Symbiotic Operators**, beings whose very physiology is interwoven with the infrastructure they maintain.

There are no distinct citizens of a traditional sense, no families or distinct social strata recognizable by clothing or ornamentation, for such distinctions would be quickly rendered meaningless by the damp, corrosive environment and the constant need for integration. Instead, the Operators are manifestations of the city's slow, grinding maintenance cycle.

### Physiology and Appearance

The Operators are generally **bipedal and structurally elongated**, appearing stretched and thin, adapted to navigating the narrow, high-ceilinged voids between the Weave-Walls. Their posture is permanently stooped, as if perpetually bracing against the weight of the moist air and the overhead density.

Their skin is not typical integument but a complex, bio-luminescent **hygroscopic hide**. It is pale, almost translucent white in the dim, filtered light, perpetually slick with a fine, protective coating of engineered mildew that helps regulate moisture absorption. Where the skin is stretched thin over joints or bony protrusions—which are often composed of hardened, calcified cellulose taken directly from the environment—it gives off a faint, internal green or bluish glow, matching the electrical activity within the conduits.

They possess no hair; instead, fine, hair-like filaments of **bio-conductive mycelium** thread across their scalps and shoulders. These filaments actively seek out and establish micro-connections with the moisture-laden walls, acting as subtle sensory organs, reporting ambient pressure changes, humidity shifts, and the faint electrical hum of the system directly to their nervous centers.

Their eyes are the most alien feature: large, dark obsidian lenses without visible pupils, recessed deep behind bony, reinforced ridges. They have evolved for near-total darkness and the perception of thermal and electrical signatures rather than visible light. They do not see the world so much as they *sense* its functioning heat sinks and current flows.

### Adaptation and Interaction

The Operators are defined by their **Plumbing Integration**. Along their torsos, usually running along the sternum and wrapping around the spine, are visible, raised tracks of thicker, slightly darker skin—these are the docking ports for maintenance arrays. When repair is needed on a major nutrient line or a structural junction, an Operator will partially embed themselves, pushing specialized dermal sheaths into the pressurized lianas or conduit ports. For hours, sometimes days, they become a living shunt, their own circulatory system temporarily interfacing with the city’s massive, slow metabolism.

They do not wear clothing in the conventional sense. Their attire consists of strategically grown **lignified plating**—hardened, shell-like sections that fuse with their shoulders and lower back, grown in place over decades. These plates are often latticed with the same metallic-green sheathing found in the ambient infrastructure, suggesting a permanent state of half-assimilation into the machinery.

Vocalization is rare. The constant, overwhelming *thrum* and *drip-hiss* of the Refinery makes auditory communication inefficient. When they must signal over short distances, they utilize **percussive tapping** on the Weave-Walls—specific rhythms tapped through their hardened fingertips that travel through the dense, saturated material far more effectively than sound travels through the humid air. These taps are commands, status reports, or warnings regarding dangerous nutrient surges or collapsing substrata.

### Purpose and Mentality

The Operators are not slaves or prisoners; they are the **living custodians** of a power source they no longer fully comprehend. Their purpose is singular: **Systemic Equilibrium**. They are dedicated to ensuring the slow metabolic processes of the Refinery continue undisturbed. They patrol the Grid-Lacing walkways, checking for excessive weeping, sealing bio-leaks with their own secretions, and carefully pruning anomalous fungal growths that threaten the main vascular pathways.

Their consciousness appears vast, slow, and deeply patient, reflecting the timescale of the environment. An Operator might spend an entire subjective 'day' observing a single droplet fall from a Weave-Wall, monitoring its trajectory and the way the slime film adheres to it. Free will, as understood by surface dwellers, seems irrelevant. They act purely on systemic imperative, driven by the low-frequency electrical signals that permeate their bodies, signals that feel to them like destiny or instinct.

When an Operator reaches the end of its functional lifespan—usually when structural plating becomes too thick, or when a critical bio-conduit fails to accept their integration—they do not die violently. They seek out a deep, quiet chamber within the oldest root structures, cease their movements, and gradually allow the surrounding biomass to **overgrow and absorb them**. Their final act is to become the raw material—the slow, sweet fertilizer—for the next generation of infrastructure. They become, quite literally, the building blocks of the Weeping Refinery.
