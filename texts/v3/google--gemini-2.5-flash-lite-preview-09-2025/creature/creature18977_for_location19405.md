The inhabitants of the Cistern of the Cog-Moss are not merely living *in* the environment; they are aspects of its ongoing, static process—micro-mechanisms and conduits in a wet, closed-loop system designed for perpetual, internal fabrication. They are the **Regulators and the Motes**.

***

### The Regulators (Fauna)

The larger, more organized fauna of the Cistern are the Regulators. They are the mobile, biological iteration of the *process* implied by the overwhelming soundscape.

**Form and Function:**
A typical Regulator resembles a heavily armored beetle or crab, but its carapace is not uniform chitin. It is a patchwork of biological material and slow-setting resin, often incorporating shards of the metallic silt washed down from the higher-altitude, unseen seams. They average the size of a human fist, but their movement is deliberate and weighted.

Their most defining feature is their internal mechanics. Instead of simple muscle, the Regulators operate via **Hydraulic Synchronization**. Their joints—where legs or grasping appendages meet the body—are not hinged bone but pressurized sphincters of thick, vascular tissue connected to an internal fluid system. When they move, the air hisses softly around them as the necessary pressure is built and released, mimicking the sound of leaky valves or distant steam escapes.

Their external sensory organs are poorly developed. They do not rely on sight, given the gloom. Instead, their carapaces are laced with fine, crystalline sensory hairs that register minute variations in electromagnetic flux and atmospheric pressure. They *feel* the grinding noise of the environment as the primary mode of spatial awareness.

**Ecology and Behavior:**
Regulators exist primarily to manage the Cog-Moss. They are the mobile maintenance crew of the living circuit board.

1.  **Circuit Pruning:** They use specialized, rasping mouthparts—often tipped with naturally occurring iron oxide—to graze deliberately on the Cog-Moss. They do not eat it for caloric intake in the traditional sense; rather, they are trimming overgrown fractal pathways, ensuring that the copper filaments maintain their necessary conductivity and preventing any single segment from shorting out or growing too dominant. This grazing is what produces the slight, rust-colored dust seen coating some of them.
2.  **Charge Distribution:** When a spasm of intense, white electrical discharge occurs in the Moss, the Regulators instinctively swarm the area. They position their bodies so that the excess energy channels directly through their resinous plating and specialized internal sacs. They are absorbing and buffering dangerous energy spikes, transforming raw discharge into stable, systemic pressure for later use, or perhaps simply shunting the excess away from critical junctions.
3.  **Resin Deposition:** If a section of the unstable, weeping ground cracks too severely, a Regulator will secrete a thick, fast-curing resin from pores along its lower body, effectively patching the fissure until the slower geological minerals can take hold. They are the bio-geological repair drones of the Cistern.

They move mostly in slow, purpose-driven lines across the damp earth, never seeming to rest or interact socially, each one seemingly locked onto its individual maintenance loop dictated by the ambient mechanical cacophony.

***

### The Motes (Flora/Energy Manifestation)

The Motes are less inhabitants and more fleeting *byproducts* of the extreme technological weirdness. They represent the boundary where the Cog-Moss's electrical absorption becomes momentarily visible and kinetic.

**Form and Function:**
Motes are not permanent life forms but transient bursts of self-organizing energy entangled with mineral dust and ionized water vapor. They appear only when the audible grinding peaks in certain frequencies.

When the sound resolves into a brief, complex chord, luminous clouds appear—small, roughly spherical collections of glowing, pale-blue motes, perhaps a meter in diameter. These are visually stunning and profoundly disturbing, appearing less like life and more like captured static electricity given temporary, swirling articulation.

The Motes possess a rudimentary, collective *intent*: **Propagation of Pattern**. They are thought to be packets of data or temporary algorithmic structures made momentarily visible by the energy surge.

**Ecology and Behavior:**
1.  **Pattern Imprinting:** A Mote will hover briefly over a section of Cog-Moss. As it does, the indigo surface beneath it darkens infinitesimally, and the hairline copper filaments rearrange themselves into a pattern that is subtly more complex than the surrounding environment—a miniature, ephemeral blueprint. This is the Cistern ‘writing’ new sections of its own, impossible program.
2.  **Decay:** The stability imposed on the Cistern is fragile. The Motes rarely last more than thirty seconds. As the grinding sound subsides into mere background noise, the Motes begin to lose cohesion. Their light flickers violently, and they rapidly dissipate downwards, seemingly dissolving not into air, but directly *into* the surface moisture, leaving behind only a momentary, faint scent of hot circuitry.

The relationship between the Regulators and the Motes is one of constant utilization. A Regulator might approach a dissolving Mote, not to feed, but to absorb the residual energy leaching into the slick ground before the pattern can fully integrate, effectively deleting undesirable or redundant code from the Cistern's operating matrix.

***

### The Collective Presence

The entire population of the Cistern—Regulators and the fleeting Motes—exists to fulfill one unspoken directive: **Maintain the State of Dynamic Equilibrium.**

They are not striving for growth, reproduction, or survival in the familiar sense; they are the organic components of an enormous, self-repairing clockwork mechanism perpetually ticking over in the cold, damp twilight. Their existence is defined by the necessity of the machinery that creates them, and the machinery is defined by the necessity of the environment it stabilizes. They are the shadows cast by an infinite factory floor that never actually exists outside the pressurized air of the Cistern itself.
