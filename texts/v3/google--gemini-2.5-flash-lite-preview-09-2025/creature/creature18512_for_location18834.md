The inhabitants of the Salt-Woven Uplands are not creatures of flesh, nor are they the builders of the silent, petrified cities. They are entities wholly comprised of *process* and *saturation*, emergent phenomena given form by the unique confluence of basalt, water, and mineral concentration. They are the **Hydro-Lattice Dwellers**, or simply, the **Weepers**.

### The Weepers: Manifestations of Saturation

The Weepers are not born or born again; they precipitate. Their existence is intrinsically linked to the weeping fissures in the ancient stone and the saturation levels of the muds. They embody the slow, patient industry of the landscape itself.

**Form and Composition:**
A Weeper does not possess a fixed biological anatomy. Their visible presence is a temporary condensation of the environment's heaviest elements. They appear mostly as localized anomalies in the damp, diffuse light, often manifesting near the weeping rock faces or atop the highest, driest sections of the petrified causeways.

Their silhouette is generally **amorphous yet defined**, like a column of heavily agitated, viscous smoke solidified momentarily into a mutable column or slab. They appear to be constructed entirely of the environment's components, suspended in a state of hyper-cohesion:

1.  **The Basalt Core (The Anchor):** At the center of the manifestation is always a dense, non-reflective core that seems to be pure, chemically saturated basalt dust, bound not by organic glue, but by electrostatic charge and hydrostatic pressure drawing the surrounding water tight. This core mimics the weight of the ancient stone.
2.  **The Mineral Sheen (The Skin):** The surface pulses slowly with the dissolved metals leaching from the rocks—copper, iron, possibly rarer elements unknown to external biochemistry. This results in an oily, iridescent film that shifts from deep, matte grey (basalt) to fleeting patterns of verdigris green or bruised purple when the filtered light catches them just right. This skin is not protective; it is merely the densest layer of their internal chemistry made external.
3.  **The Aqueous Veil (The Movement):** The Weepers are perpetually fringed by the low, clinging mist that saturates the air near the ground. As they move or shift, they seem to actively *exhale* this moisture. Their motion is never fluid in the biological sense; it is achieved through slow, internal pressure adjustments—a hydraulic ebb and flow that forces the saturated sediment and water to redistribute itself around the dense core. Watching one move resembles watching an exceptionally thick, slow current shift direction.

**Behavior and Purpose:**
The Weepers are slow, deliberate, and seemingly without urgency. Their lifespan is measured not in years, but in the persistence of favorable mineral gradients.

**Feeding/Function:** They do not consume in the conventional sense. Their purpose appears to be the **management of saturation**. They gravitate toward areas where the weeping water is pooled highest or where the water table is threatening to breach the level of the ancient causeways. They are observed slowly traversing the slick, vertical paths of the weeping rock, seemingly absorbing excess moisture into their own form, thereby slightly throttling the rate of erosion on the stone faces. They act as living regulators of the landscape’s slow decay.

**Interaction with Structure:** They show a strange deference to the architecture. They do not seem to *use* the hexagonal columns or the causeways for shelter, but rather, they seem to be engaged in a perpetual, silent renegotiation of the boundary between the *structural* settlement (the city) and the *geological* settlement (the uplands). Sometimes, a Weeper will rest directly against a monolithic column for days, its surface sheen blending seamlessly with the moss-encrusted stone, only to dissolve back into the ambient mist hours later.

**Communication and Sound:**
Weepers do not vocalize. Their presence registers through subtle physical effects on the surrounding environment, effects already noted in the terrain description.

*   When a Weeper is maneuvering through the muds, the typical *slurp* sound is replaced by a long, drawn-out, low-velocity **suck** sound, as if the mud itself is reluctant to let go of the retreating moisture.
*   When they approach the weeping rock, the rhythmic *thrum* from the subterranean water networks seems to subtly **change frequency**, aligning temporarily with the internal hydrostatic pressure of the Weeper itself.

**Relationship to Fauna:**
The rare, chitinous creatures that skim the surface tension seem to avoid the immediate vicinity of a fully manifested Weeper. The Weepers represent an environment too dense, too mineralically saturated, even for the specialized scavengers. If a Weeper passes over a filter-feeder's burrow, the burrowing motion ceases completely until the pressure anomaly has passed.

The Weepers are the silent custodians of the Uplands—not the builders of the monuments, but the caretakers of the slow, unending process that threatens to reclaim them. They are the local density given intentional, albeit non-sentient, form.
