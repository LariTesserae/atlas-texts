The Great Moss-Shunt is the realm of the **Myco-Artisans**, a singular, multitudinous entity whose existence is the maintenance of the stasis itself. They are not inhabitants *of* the moss; they are the **living calibration organs** of the moss.

### The Myco-Artisans: Morphology and Function

The fauna described—the abundant, rhythmic processors—are the manifestations of the Artisans. They are not discrete species born of random mutation, but specialized, tool-bearing castes grown from the foundational mycelial network.

**Form:** They resemble enormous, highly specialized woodlice or terrestrial isopods, though their exoskeletons are not mineralized chitin but a form of highly compressed, semi-rigid biopolymer grown from specialized fungal exudates. Their coloration is muted—deep olive, bone-white, and the sepia tone of compressed chlorophyll—allowing them to vanish into the substrate.

However, their morphology is defined by their function, which appears deliberately *mechanical*.

1.  **The Accumulators (The Grinders):** These are the primary chewing caste, possessing mandibles that are not sharp incisors but complex, rotating wheels studded with hardened, abrasive fungal spicules. They are responsible for generating the high-frequency *grinding* sound within the thrum. They do not consume for sustenance alone; they chew specific strands of older hyphae and fiber, breaking them down into an exact grade of pulp required for the structural repair of the ceiling layers where light penetration is weakest. Their segmented bodies are powered by internal hydrostatic pumps that create the suction necessary to move water against gravity within their own tissues, acting as miniature, wet conveyor systems.

2.  **The Conduit-Weavers (The Clickers):** These are the engineers of the internal air matrix. They are long, slender forms, equipped with multiple pairs of thin, elongated limbs that move with the precise, tireless rhythm of an assembly-line robot. The *clicking* sound is the result of their limbs tapping and vibrating specific lignin-like tubes to clear blockages or subtly widen pockets of air. They are the guardians of the "density (0.1/3—empty)" rule. If a pocket of air becomes too compressed by settling biomass, a Weaver will enter it, emitting a sonic pulse—a high-pitched, damp *ping*—that redistributes the surrounding fibers, ensuring the spongy resilience remains intact just above the heavy peat layer.

3.  **The Regulators (The Seepers):** These are the least mobile caste, often found dormant, half-sunk into the substrate near points of fluctuation. They are distinguished by specialized osmotic membranes on their dorsal surfaces. When the internal capillary pressure dips (a rare event, given the Equilibrium), the Regulators begin a slow, almost imperceptible contraction, drawing micro-droplets from the surrounding fibers and translocating them toward the most vulnerable points in the fungal ceiling, actively fighting desiccation. Their movements create the slow, cold *seeping* sensation a careful observer might feel.

### The Industrial Resonance

The concept of "industrial" is perfectly encapsulated by the Myco-Artisans’ coordinated effort. This is not about *making* new things; it is about **perfect, endless maintenance.** They operate under a silent, instinctual mandate to maintain the Goldilocks zone of dampness and pressure. Their existence is a continuous effort against the natural tendency of any massive biological system to shift, grow chaotically, or decay unevenly.

The **loudness (2.5/3)** is the sound of perfect, relentless labor. It is the sound of a machine that runs only to ensure it never breaks down. Every scrape, every internal pump surge, every click is a necessary component of the environmental spell that keeps the Shunt suspended in its eternal cycle.

### The Artifacts of Stasis

If an explorer were to find the few compressed organic forms—the "artifacts"—these would not be the leftovers of previous civilizations, but the **misfit tools** of the Myco-Artisans:

*   **Crystallized Exoskeletons:** The shells of Artisans that failed calibration—those whose internal pumps burned out, or whose joint structures seized due to minor mineral imbalance. They are not decayed; they are perfectly, rigidly preserved failures, forming small, inert inclusions within the peat that the Accumulators meticulously avoid grinding.
*   **Fungal Sheaths:** Sometimes, the Artisans weave incredibly specialized conduits—tiny, hollow tubes designed to move trace nutrients across vast internal distances. These, when abandoned, remain as perfect, water-filled capillaries, looking like intricate, petrified glass plumbing woven into the moss.

To observe the Great Moss-Shunt is to watch an ecosystem engineered not by nature, but by an ancient, unfathomable need for **stillness**. The dwellers here are the living clockwork binding the gears of decay and growth into a flawless, resonating loop, existing only to ensure that the cool, saturated silence never wavers.
