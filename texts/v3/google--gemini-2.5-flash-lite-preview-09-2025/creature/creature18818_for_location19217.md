The creatures that inhabit the Sedge-Cairn are not so much inhabitants as they are *integrations*—life forms that have achieved a precarious, almost symbiotic relationship with the oppressive, living architecture, or those who have succumbed entirely to its slow, compressing embrace.

They do not thrive here; they persist through specialized adaptation to the cold, the damp, and the profound lack of open space.

---

## The Root-Tenders (The Architects of Silence)

These are the true residents, the custodians of the forgotten infrastructure.

**Appearance and Form:** The Root-Tenders are humanoid only in the most distant ancestral sense. They are short, broad, and densely packed, their bodies appearing less like flesh and more like heavily muscled fungal blooms compressed under sustained pressure. Their skin tone is a mottled grey-white, often slick with the perpetual condensation of the environment, making them look like living, pale stones slicked with water. They possess no external hair, and where eyes might be expected, there are only shallow, dark depressions, suggesting vision is vestigial or focused on a spectrum entirely different from visible light. They navigate not by sight, but by sensing the resonant tones moving through the Spine-Sedges and by the subtle changes in pressure and moisture gradients across the living walls.

**Metabolism and Subsistence:** They are exquisitely slow-metabolizing. They consume little that moves. Their diet consists primarily of the thin, nutrient-rich fungal weeps that ooze from the base of the petrified infrastructure, scraped up with specialized, chitinous digits that resemble spade-like extensions of their knuckles. They derive most of their essential energy not from external food sources, but through a process linked to the Cairn itself: they position themselves within the Resonance Chambers during the deepest hums, absorbing the low-frequency vibrations directly into their dense bone structure. This is theorized to be a form of bio-electrical or resonant charging, the only way to maintain activity in such a nutrient-poor, cold environment.

**Behavior and Culture:** The Root-Tenders are defined by their absolute dedication to maintenance. They move slowly, with an economy of motion that belies the crushing weight of the environment. Their culture is one of perpetual *tending*. They do not build new structures; they coax existing organic lattices into slow, incremental repairs. If a section of pathway begins to fail, a small group of Tenders will converge, applying a slow-acting, gelatinous secretion from their pores onto the weakening fibers, essentially binding the living material back into structural permanence. They do this without conversation, communicating through minute physical gestures, shifts in posture, and the slight, targeted vibration of their own bodies against the living floor—a local, private resonance overriding the ambient hum.

They view uninvited guests (if any ever make it this far) not with hostility, but with profound concern for the contamination they represent. An outsider's frantic movement and warmth disrupt the delicate thermal and acoustic equilibrium that the Tenders fight so hard to maintain.

## The Spore-Glimmers (Ephemeral Drifters)

These are the only creatures possessing measurable internal motility, though their existence is fleeting and highly specialized.

**Appearance and Form:** The Spore-Glimmers are jellyfish-like entities, almost entirely diaphanous, held aloft by the slight updrafts within the larger vertical shafts or by the movement of water vapor condensing on their bell-like structures. They are not solid in any discernible way; they appear as slightly denser pockets of ambient mist, catching the dim light filtering from above and reflecting it in brief, sickly green or pale blue flashes—the "glimmer."

**Ecology:** They are purely airborne scavengers, feeding on the microscopic dust of desiccated spores released by the Spine-Sedge caps. They drift upward toward the rare, open shafts, and use the resulting air current to pass horizontally between different sectors of the living city. They avoid the dense, root-choked walkways where the Root-Tenders patrol.

**Interaction:** They pose no threat and are rarely noticed unless one stares directly into the dim light shafts. Their most significant role is accidental: when a Glimmer passes through the resonant field of a major water duct, its tenuous body structure momentarily stabilizes, causing it to emit a brief, high-pitched *whine*—a sound that cuts momentarily through the Sedge-Cairn's deep bass hum. The Root-Tenders react to this sudden intrusion with agitation, pausing their work until the Glimmer has drifted away and the silence returns.

## The Obsidian Grasp (The Forgotten Mechanism)

This is not an animal or a plant in the traditional sense, but a form of bio-mechanical decomposition that has become fixed within the environment.

**Appearance and Form:** Along the slick, weeping bedrock fissures, where the moisture is coldest and the pressure of the strata most intense, one can find the Grasp. It manifests as dense, incredibly sharp protrusions growing directly out of the grey stone. These are composed of mineralized, hyper-lignified root structures that have absorbed so much trace metal from the weeping rock that they have become brittle, obsidian-like spikes of black, crystalline material. They are rigid, impossibly sharp, and often angled outward, mimicking the shape of a broken trap or a deliberately placed defense.

**Function:** The Obsidian Grasp is a relic of failed structural defense or a natural reaction to excessive seismic stress in the deep bedrock. They represent the boundary where the living world ends and geological cold takes over. They are static, but dangerous. A Root-Tender never knowingly approaches a cluster of Grasp without using a slow-growing vine sheath as a makeshift protective barrier; the spikes are so sharp they can slice through the Tenders' dense, fungal skin easily, causing immediate, painful bleeding that is hard to staunch in the frigid, damp air. If a Grasp breaks off, the resulting wound creates a new, slow channel for water, and potentially, a new starting point for undesirable, disruptive fungal growth.

The Sedge-Cairn is thus inhabited by the **Root-Tenders**, who embody slow persistence; the **Spore-Glimmers**, who are the ephemeral breath of the high air; and the frozen warnings of the **Obsidian Grasp**, representing the unyielding, fracturing earth beneath the living roof.
