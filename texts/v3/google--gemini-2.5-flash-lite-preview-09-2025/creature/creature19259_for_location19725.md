They are the **Aether-Weavers**, or perhaps more accurately, the **Stilled-Moss**.

They do not build grand structures, for grandeur is the first casualty of the Sky-Needles. Whatever inhabits this shivering garden has adapted not just to cling, but to *become* the clinging.

### Physiology and Form

The Aether-Weavers are creatures of high containment and low profile. Their physical form is difficult to resolve clearly, perpetually obscured by the heavy mist and the shimmering refraction caused by the atmospheric density pooling on the wet stone. They rarely stand upright, as any vertical presentation against the constant vertical shear of the wind is an invitation to be detached.

Their bodies appear **low-slung and fused**, almost symbiotic with the slate-grey rock shelf itself. They possess a thick, almost cartilaginous hide the color of wet basalt, subtly iridescent when the brief, diffuse light catches it at the correct angle—a necessary camouflage against the backdrop of their home. Their structure suggests many short, powerful limbs, not suited for walking across open ground, but for gripping the microscopic irregularities of the ancient stone. They move with a slow, deliberate deliberation that borders on geological patience, except during the brief, violent weather shifts.

When the sharp, localized downpours hit, the Aether-Weavers become almost invisible, their bodies pressing flat against the weeping rock, adopting a posture of near-total surrender. It is during these moments of forced stasis that their truly unique adaptations become apparent.

### Sensory Adaptation and Sustenance

The ears, if they possess distinct auditory organs, must be internal or heavily guarded, for the roar of the wind and water would shatter any external structure. They perceive their world primarily through **vibration and pressure gradients**. Their entire ventral surface—the side pressed against the warm, stable resonance of the stone—is covered in cilia or microscopic sensory pits that read the deep *thrum* echoing up from the chasms below, interpreting the distant waterfalls not as sound, but as the very pulse of the mountain.

Their sustenance is derived directly from the **Perpetual Soak**:

1.  **Mineral Osmosis:** They draw dissolved minerals directly through their skin from the water that continually saturates the moss beds and seeps through the rock fissures. They consume the filtered residue of everything that falls from the sky and everything scraped from the ancient stone.
2.  **Fungal Integration:** They are closely associated with the pulsing, bioluminescent fungi found in the sheltered alcoves. It is suspected that the Weavers cultivate these fungi, perhaps using them as a secondary means of processing ambient atmospheric energy, or that they consume the spores when the light-stalks pulse brightest. Occasionally, one might see a patch of the glowing mycelium growing directly *over* a dormant Weaver, suggesting a period of deep dormancy or integration.

### The Nature of Occupation

The scattered obsidian remnants—the low hearths and polished grinding stones—are not the Weaver's *origin*, but their *tools* of survival, embraced and reshaped by the environment.

The Aether-Weavers are practitioners of **Stasis through Flow**. They do not *fight* the environment; they channel it. The built structures they utilize show meticulous modification:

*   The obsidian hearths are not for fire (an impossibility in this damp purgatory), but for **thermal regulation**. They seem to be designed as conduits to draw the faintest warmth differential from the stone, allowing a localized area to remain slightly above the lethal freezing point during the coldest convective shifts.
*   The basins around these structures are not for water collection, but for **impedance**. They intentionally create small eddies in the surface runoff, trapping pockets of the heavy, nutrient-rich silt that would otherwise be swept over the edge.

The Weaver is the ultimate recycler. They have no need for speech or complex social structures visible to the outsider; their communication seems mediated entirely through the manipulation of vibration through the stone matrix. A sudden shift in the low-frequency humming resonating through the plateau likely signals danger or the location of a rich mineral deposit.

### The Weavers in Motion

A Weaver in motion is a disturbing sight. It is not a walk, but a sequence of pressurized adjustments. When they must traverse a patch of actively scoured rock, they deploy fine, hair-thin filaments from their appendages—not claws, but adhesion points that chemically or electrostatically bond to the wet granite. They move with the slow, inevitable creep of lichen, their body mass held inches above the stone, perfectly balanced against the wind’s horizontal push.

They are the embodiment of the plateau itself: ancient, resilient, defined entirely by the extremes of pressure and flow around them. They are survivors whose very existence proves that life can persist, provided it understands that the only way to survive absolute chaos is to become infinitesimally slow, utterly dense, and perfectly shaped by the ongoing violence of the very air that tries to erase them.
