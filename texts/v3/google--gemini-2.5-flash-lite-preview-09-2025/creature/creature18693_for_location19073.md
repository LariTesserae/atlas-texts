The residents of the Under-Veil Depths are less like discrete creatures and more like a single, vast, sentient friction inhabiting the stone itself. They are the embodiment of the **Grind**, the constant, humming counterpoint to the stillness of the deep rock.

They are known collectively as the **Chitinous Substrate**, or informally, the **Scree-Sculptors**.

### Morphology and Physiology

The primary inhabitants are small, numerous, and possess exoskeletons heavily reinforced with silicates and trace metals absorbed directly from the aggressive mineral substrate. They are not uniform in size, but rather exist in a continuous distribution fractal, ranging from microscopic dust-mites that form the initial film on the damp basalt, up to larger, segmented scavengers the size of a closed fist.

**Senses:** Sight is vestigial or entirely absent. Their perception is dominated by **seismic-tactile interpretation** and **chemotaxis**. Every vibration—the slight shift of a footstep, the shudder of distant fracturing, or the scrape of a fellow Sculptor’s leg—is mapped into a rich, three-dimensional understanding of their immediate surroundings. They navigate not by light, but by pressure differentials and the slight ionic gradients in the condensing moisture film.

**Locomotion:** Their legs and ventral surfaces are specialized for maximum traction on sharp surfaces. They move with a high-frequency, rasping gait. This motion is the source of the ambient noise: it is the sound of **billions of tiny points of abrasion** dragging themselves across fractured shale. Their movement is not efficient like grazing; it is mandatory, a biological imperative to constantly consume surface irregularities.

**Metabolism:** Exceptionally high, necessitated by the cool temperatures and the constant mechanical demands of their existence. They derive energy not just from the sparse lichen’s exudates, but primarily from the **direct breakdown of the rock structure** itself—they process the crystal lattices, extracting trace elements and reducing the basalt to finer, more porous scree, effectively consuming their own environment to keep it young.

### Behavioral Ecology: The Collective Scrape

There is no evidence of social hierarchy, communication beyond chemical trails, or coordinated hunting. Their "society" is a continuous, distributed process oriented around surface disruption.

1.  **The Planar Advance:** The Sculptors move in dense, shallow waves across any exposed horizontal or sloped surface. This is not a migration towards food, but a shared, passive function. Where a wave passes, the angular edges of the scree are notably rounded, and the surface takes on a slick, almost oily patina where the fine organic slurry has been spread.
2.  **Fissure Infilling:** In the narrow corridors and sudden drops, different specialized morphologies emerge—the **Thread-Weavers** or **Silt-Forms**. These are thinner, almost vermiform entities that utilize the moisture tracks. They secrete a thin, quick-setting, mineralized mucus that acts as a binding agent. They are the only residents that *build*, but their structures are temporary dams, bridges, or shunts designed only to redirect the flow of drainage or trap richer runoff for immediate consumption by the larger population. These buildups are inherently unstable, rapidly undermined by the grinding action above them.
3.  **The Silence Eaters:** In the deepest, least disturbed pools of standing water—the thin, mirrored depressions—live the most specialized forms. These are slower, almost sessile organisms that feed on *biofilms* that manage to bloom in relative tranquility. However, they are constantly under siege. If the water level drops even slightly, the airborne Sculptors descend en masse, and the small pools are consumed by rasping movement within hours, leaving dry, scraped mud where the water had been moments before.

### Interaction with Intruders

For any entity larger than a fist, the primary danger from the Scree-Sculptors is **suffocation by substrate change**.

If a large object, such as an unmoving body or a piece of dropped equipment, remains stationary for more than a few days, the microbial film begins to colonize it aggressively. This is soon followed by the primary Sculptors. They do not attack flesh out of malice, but because the material is *not rock, but static surface anomaly*.

They will investigate using chemoreception, testing the material for crystalline structure. If the surface is soft or organic (like flesh or corroding metal), their digestive enzymes and abrasive mandibles go to work, consuming the organic matrix and grinding the surface down to integrate it into the surrounding scree layer. A stationary boot heel will soon be coated in a rapidly growing, hissing crust of mineralized life, which will, in turn, anchor millions of smaller mites whose constant scraping will pull grit into any seal or joint.

The overwhelming presence of the Scree-Sculptors ensures there is no true rest in the Under-Veil. They are the constant, low-frequency *grinding* of the subterranean world, ensuring that creation and destruction happen simultaneously, every moment, on every surface exposed to the cool, damp air.
