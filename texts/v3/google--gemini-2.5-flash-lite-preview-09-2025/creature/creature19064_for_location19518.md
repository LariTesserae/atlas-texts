The inhabitants of the Root-City Undergrowth are not creatures of movement, but entities of **slow, deliberate incorporation**. Their very existence is a testimony to the principle that when mobility is denied, tenacity, density, and chemical specialization become the ultimate forms of life.

The dominant residents are the **Mycoregulators** and the **Xylo-Sappers**.

### 1. The Mycoregulators (The Architects of Compression)

These are the primary drivers of the environment, organisms that blend the characteristics of massive, woody polypore fungi and deep mycorrhizal networks. They do not rely on the surface world for nutrition; they *are* the digestive system consuming the ghost of industry.

**Appearance and Form:**
A Mycoregulator manifests not as a discrete body but as a structural density within the substrate itself. Where they are present, the biomass tightens. One perceives them as areas where the glossy, deep-purple fungal mats are thickest, often concentrating around the calcified cores of old concrete beams. Their visible features are the **Sheath-Veins**: thick, pulsing ropes of pale, fibrous material that weave through the structure, acting as conduits for nutrient transport and cellular expansion pressure. These veins are slick with the perpetually weeping moisture, glistening under the dim light like oiled muscle.

**Behavior and Life Cycle:**
The Mycoregulators move by *growth* alone. Their ambition is to fill every micro-void remaining from the original architecture. Their consciousness, if it can be called that, is decentralized and geological in timescale. They communicate through precisely modulated changes in internal pressure, which contributes directly to the pervasive, low **thrumming** soundscape. A sudden increase in localized thrumming means a Mycoregulator has found a pressurized pocket of trapped atmospheric gas or has begun the aggressive, high-density colonization of a particularly mineral-rich section of defunct metal piping.

When they encounter the "Tech Ghosts"—the remnant metal conduits—they deploy specialized enzymes that cause a localized, slow-motion effervescence in the metal surface. This is not rapid rust, but a controlled leaching process, turning the steel directly into complex bio-available salts, which are then swiftly absorbed by the surrounding Sheath-Veins. They are the living manifestation of bio-reclamation.

**Interaction with the Environment:**
They are the primary mechanism of the environment's claustrophobia. They actively press against the ceiling, causing the slow, millimeter-per-year descent. They possess a near-perfect internal pressure regulation system, allowing them to maintain solvency in the humid, heavy atmosphere, forcing the environment to remain saturated for the benefit of their own turgid existence.

### 2. The Xylo-Sappers (The Light-Eaters)

These are the more traditional (though still utterly alien) flora, specializing in adapting the limited, filtered light that manages to penetrate the upper layers of organic matter. They are the architects of the murky, olive-green twilight.

**Appearance and Form:**
The Xylo-Sappers appear as dense, waxy, segmented stalks rooted deeply within the Mycoregulator matrix. They possess no leaves in the traditional sense, as surface area is a liability in this pressurized environment. Instead, they have **Photophore Scales**—small, highly reflective, slightly concave plates of chitin-like material that are often the palest, waxy white. These scales are oriented precisely toward the near-imperceptible light gradient filtering down, acting like intricate parabolic mirrors to capture every stray photon. They resemble armored, pale corals growing upward from the living floor.

**Behavior and Life Cycle:**
The Xylo-Sappers are inherently parasitic upon the Mycoregulators, tapping into the nutrient streams flowing between the fungal structures. However, they provide a vital service: their density creates the necessary light refraction that maintains the perpetual warm twilight. They are the primary absorbers of the metallic leaching scent, using the dissolved iron and copper to deposit onto their Photophore Scales, giving those scales a faint, internal, metallic sheen when viewed up close.

When a Xylo-Sapper reaches the end of its life cycle (a span measured in decades), it does not decay rapidly. Instead, its internal structure crystallizes, its waxy coating hardening into brittle, translucent husks that serve as temporary, rigid scaffolding until the Mycoregulators weave new Sheath-Veins through their forms.

### The Absence: Why No Mobile Fauna?

The life of the Root-City Undergrowth is a perfect ecological trap for anything mobile. The complete lack of fauna is not due to toxicity or absolute lack of resources, but the **Principle of Immediate Impediment**.

Any small creature—an insect, a worm, or even a spore germinating outside of the established networks—that attempts to push through the saturated, packed substrate would be met with instantaneous, intense mechanical resistance. The environment is literally too *full*.

Imagine stepping into deep mud, but the mud is alive, densely woven, under slight hydraulic pressure, and actively hardening its structure around any intrusion. A misplaced footfall would not merely sink; the surrounding biomass would flow into the resulting void with such tensile strength that the intruder would be mechanically immobilized, crushed by the pressure differential, and then slowly digested by ancillary feeder hyphae belonging to the nearest Mycoregulator, which are always listening for the slight disruption in the pervasive *thrum*.

In short, the inhabitants are **The Infrastructure Itself**: slow, chemical beings that have successfully evolved to treat structure, pressure, and decay as their only means of survival and propagation. They are the silence made flesh, digesting a lost world at the speed of rust and slow fungal growth.
