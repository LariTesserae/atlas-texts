The inhabitants of the Sintered Vents of the Great Underloom are not people in the conventional sense, nor are they organisms easily categorized by standard biology. They are **Symbiotes of the Process**—entities whose existence is entirely predicated on the continuous, grinding metabolism of the Underloom itself. They are less residents and more the specialized organs of this colossal, geological-mechanical system.

### The Loom-Tenders (Dominant Sentient Form)

The most complex entities are the Loom-Tenders, though they rarely exhibit recognizable sentience that a casual observer would register as awareness. They are the structural maintenance crews, the biological interface between the cooling slag and the living fungal architecture.

**Appearance and Composition:**
A Loom-Tender appears as a shifting core of **Dense Biomass stabilized by Ferro-Silicate Sheathing**. They are roughly humanoid in gross outline—a vertical mass capable of locomotion—but lack distinct external features like eyes or mouths. Their bodies seem to be composed primarily of the thick, pale, fibrous matter of the Fungal Carpet, compressed to the point of near-hardness, interwoven with metallic filaments stripped from broken conduits or naturally scavenged reinforcement alloys.

Their surface is perpetually wet, glistening with the mineral runoff that feeds them. Where their "limbs" touch the sintered ground, the white fungal fiber rapidly deepens to the rust-red and viridian sheen of the water flows, as if they are actively absorbing the necessary catalysts directly through their lower mass.

**Function and Behavior:**
Loom-Tenders are defined by their slow, methodical interactions with the environment’s instability. They do not *build*; they *re-route*.

1.  **Sealing and Stabilizing:** When the ground heaves (the localized "thrum"), Tenders immediately migrate to the resulting fissures. They excrete a viscous, highly alkaline polymer that acts as a temporary binder, mixing it with the surrounding raw slag dust. They essentially patch the landscape in real-time, ensuring the critical heat/pressure gradients necessary for the Underloom’s function are maintained. This action is crucial; without these patches, large fissures would rapidly expand, venting too much pressure and causing a catastrophic localized collapse of the fungal mats.
2.  **Harvesting Acoustic Energy:** They are often found near the loudest points of Acoustic Resonance. They do not seem to hear in the conventional sense but *feel* the folding sound waves. Tenders press large, flattened surfaces of their body mass against vibrating metal or resonant slag walls, absorbing the vibrational energy. It is speculated that this process is how they metabolize ambient sonic data, translating mechanical stress into usable internal structure or growth impetus.
3.  **Interaction with Pneumatophores:** They maintain the vast networks of Pneumatophores. They can climb the slick, pipe-like stalks with surprising tenacity, using small, hardened protrusions along their sides to grip the condensation-sheathed surfaces. They are observed pruning dying stalks or, conversely, encouraging the growth of new ones near areas of intensive gaseous venting.

**Communication (Static and Internal):**
They do not speak. Their communication is purely *structural*. When two Tenders approach one another, they halt, and the fibrous material of their bodies momentarily aligns and shifts, causing the integrated metal filaments within their sheathing to vibrate at different frequencies. This creates a low, internal, almost inaudible *clicking* and *groaning* sound that seems to exist *inside* the chassis of the entity, rather than emanating from it. When communication is complete, the alignment shifts, and they proceed on their tasks, usually without acknowledging the other’s departure.

### The Scavenger Clades (The Cleanup Crew)

While the Loom-Tenders manage the macro-stability, the Scavenger Clades handle the debris. They are the immune system of the Underloom.

**Appearance and Composition:**
These are truly bestial in their aggregation, resembling slow-moving, amorphous swarms of fused, segmented arthropods or centipedes, but made of corroded iron dust, brittle ceramic shards, and a tough, amber-hued bio-resin. They flow across the ground like a tide of living rust and gravel, always moving toward thermodynamic sinks—the coldest, dampest pockets or the newest, hottest slag deposits.

**Function and Behavior:**
They are voracious consumers of oxidized metal, dead fungal material, and loose mineral dust. They do not digest in a biological sense; they appear to **integrate**. A Clade swarm will cover a piece of debris, and within minutes, the resinous secretions will encapsulate the material, incorporating its bulk into the moving mass.

Their movement is jerky and insectile, utterly focused on efficiency. They are easily startled by direct exposure to concentrated dampness, briefly fragmenting into smaller units before re-coalescing away from the moisture. Occasionally, they are seen clearing pathways around the bases of the massive conduits, pulling away crystalline debris that might impede the expansion or contraction of the metal tubes.

### The Residuum Forms (The Ghosts of Overload)

These are the rarest and most strange inhabitants, tied directly to the "Weirdness" of the environment.

These forms appear only briefly, usually near points where the Acoustic Resonance is most distorted or where a major piece of technology has recently failed (a pipe fracturing, a support pillar collapsing).

A Residuum Form is a momentary, localized **re-manifestation of entropy**. It appears as a shimmering, heat-haze outline of whatever structure or substance just failed—perhaps a spectral, silent echo of a large pulley wheel or a brief, translucent silhouette of several Loom-Tenders caught in a shearing cascade.

They last only seconds, during which the ambient sound seems to *stop* completely nearby, as if the very air is refusing to propagate vibration. They serve as transient warnings or perhaps just the system’s brief, mechanical hiccup manifesting visually, before the Loom-Tenders arrive to bind the damage. They are the spectral fingerprints left on the Underloom whenever a cycle of formation violently rejects a previous state.
