The inhabitants of this high, cold world are the **Scleros** (from the Ancient Greek for 'hard' or 'dry'), a people utterly defined by the constraints of their vertical, fracturing environment. They are not merely survivors here; they are the stabilizers, the meticulous counter-force to the geology itself.

### Physiology and Appearance

The Scleros are compact and dense, their forms honed by the sheer physical necessity of hauling material and clinging to sheer surfaces. They possess a low, broad center of gravity, with thick, corded musculature built for slow, sustained exertion rather than explosive speed.

**Insulation and Skin:** Their skin is thick, possessing a waxy, pale grey or bruised-violet hue—a clear sign of minimal solar exposure and extreme cold adaptation. Specialized capillaries near the surface constrict almost totally in the open air, reserving core temperature. Their exposed features—the knuckles, the rims of the ears, the lips—are often faintly fissured, mimicking the rock they live on, an adaptation that may help shed microscopic ice buildup.

**Sensory Adaptation:** The eyes of the Scleros are relatively small and deep-set, protected by heavy orbital ridges. In the muted light, they rely heavily on non-visual senses; their hearing is acutely tuned to subtle vibrations in the rock structure, allowing them to detect distant rockfalls, the shift of foundation stones, or the near-silent passage of specialized aerial scavengers hundreds of meters away. They communicate in a low, breathy register when outdoors, using specific, dampened sounds that propagate minimally through the thin, cold air.

**The Sheath:** The most notable characteristic is their reliance on external reinforcement. Every Scleros wears a form of fitted, layered carapace—the **Sustenance Sheath**. This is not armor, but a meticulously maintained personal containment system. It is constructed from multi-ply layers of desiccated, treated moss-fiber (the 'woody mosses') treated with rendered animal fat and rock resins to create a surprisingly rigid, insulating shell. This sheath covers their core and limbs, minimizing surface area exposed to the biting cold and providing necessary structural support when traversing slick or crumbling paths. The sheaths are dyed in muted earth tones or pale quartz white, blending them seamlessly into the stonework when they move slowly.

### Society and Culture: The Architects of Stasis

The Scleros culture is rooted in the philosophy that *nothing good may move*; movement equals risk, and vulnerability to the elements is death. Their entire existence is geared toward locking structures down against the dynamic world.

**The Doctrine of Containment:** Their religion and law center on the capture and hoarding of entropy. Water is consecrated material; to spill condensate is akin to a spiritual failing. They view the sharp fracturing of the young rock with a kind of horrified reverence—it is the great, destructive deity they must always placate with sheer, stubborn permanence.

**Labor and Skill:** Their technology (Tech: 0.9) is rooted in precision stonework and materials management. A Scleros child’s first task is learning to wedge stones so that the pressure vector transfers perfectly past the intended fissure line. Master Masons (*Ker-Vex*) are the highest caste, capable of reading the microscopic stress lines in the basalt and quartz. They understand that the ancient scaffolds supporting their high-level walkways are not merely structural; they are systems designed to flex *just enough* to dissipate wind shear without snapping the dry-stacked masonry behind them.

**Generational Cycles:** Their social rhythm is glacial. Decisions requiring new construction or major repair work are debated over multiple solar cycles. Families live in deep, established pockets, often occupying the same niche for hundreds of generations. Identity is tied less to personal achievement and more to the specific lineage of maintenance one is responsible for (e.g., "I am of the South Wind Fissure Rime-Catchers, third branch").

### Interaction with the Ecology

While they rely on the scarce biological resources, the Scleros treat the indigenous fauna with pragmatic respect:

1.  **The Chitinous Scavengers:** These small creatures consume mineral traces and organic dust that the Scleros cannot process. Designated teams follow behind workers, clearing these detritus piles diligently, ensuring the base rock remains clean and stable. The scavengers are a necessary part of the waste recycling system; killing them needlessly is forbidden.
2.  **The Gliders (Aero-Hunters):** The large, winged predators are seen as dangerous, but necessary, competitors for the few flying invertebrates. Scleros engineers build ingenious, weighted deflectors and angled shelters specifically designed to guide the strong updrafts away from walkways, ensuring the Gliders hunt far from populated transit routes.

### The Density of Dwelling

To an outsider, a Scleros settlement appears as a terrifying accretion of stone. It is not architecture built *upon* the mountain, but one built *into* the mountain's flaw lines.

Within the vertical sprawl:

*   **The Cistern Tiers:** The lowest occupied levels of any cluster are dedicated to water management. These are the darkest, dampest places, where the large, rock-cut cisterns—some polished smooth over millennia—are checked hourly. The air here is thick with the smell of cold, contained moisture and the faint, living scent of the moss-cultivations grown around the slow drip zones.
*   **The Niche Dwellings:** Residences are small, single-occupant, or family bunkers, sealed by heavy, perfectly fitted stone plugs when the inhabitants are sleeping or during severe weather. Light sources inside are typically slow-burning, resin-soaked fungal clusters or carefully regulated mineral phosphor lamps.
*   **The Transit Ledges:** These are the pathways, rarely wider than two people shoulder-to-shoulder. They are traversed with ritualistic caution. The Scleros walk close to the inner wall, their backs braced against the cold stone, using handholds carved by ancestors who knew the exact sequence of stress that allowed the rock to be cut without collapsing the structure above or below.

The Scleros are the living mortar of this fractured world. They do not conquer the cold or the heights; they simply become another, slightly more motivated, layer of the landscape, endlessly sealing cracks, catching falling moisture, and ensuring that the slow erosion of the world does not take *them* before they have completed their generation’s assigned installment of permanence.
