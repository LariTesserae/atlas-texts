The residents of the Rime-Crust Foundry are not so much inhabitants as they are 
*components* of the environment itself, biological accretions perfectly synced to the rhythm of industrial rot and chemical stability. They are the **Foundry’s Symbionts**—a population defined by its lack of reliance on sunlight or breathable organic decay, existing entirely within the thick, warm, metallic breath of the place.

There are two primary, yet overlapping, castes of life here: the **Husk-Weavers** and the **Slurry-Saints**.

---

## The Husk-Weavers (The Scuttlers)

These are the macroscopic fauna, the "Scuttlers" made manifest. A Husk-Weaver resembles a roughly spherical agglomeration of highly cross-linked, viscous biological polymer, roughly the size of a heavy toolbox. Their internal structure is a decentralized network of digestive filaments and electrochemical regulators, encased in a protective shell.

**Appearance and Structure:**
The shell is the most striking feature. It is not grown in the traditional sense, but *assembled*. The Weaver possesses dozens of short, chitinous appendages tipped with microscopic sonic transducers. These tools are used to actively harvest fine mineral particulates and slag dust from the floor, which they instantly bind together using excreted bio-resins. The resulting armor is an almost perfect mosaic of the environment: dull grey ceramic shards, oxidized copper flakes, and crystalline salt deposits, all fused into a hardened, articulated carapace.

They are defined by their **locomotion**: they do not walk so much as *vibrate* across the surfaces. Their undersides are covered in millions of microscopic pseudopods that secrete a thin, lubricating biofilm—a byproduct of their mineral processing—allowing them to glide over the slick slurry with minimal friction. This movement is the source of the constant **Grinding Mass** sound, as hardened components of their shells scrape over the compacted floor.

**Behavior and Function:**
Husk-Weavers are the primary recyclers and constructors of the Foundry. They seek out areas of active thermal venting or minor structural micro-fractures. They will deposit specialized enzyme pastes into these weaknesses, not to repair them, but to induce controlled mineral precipitation. They are actively mining the thermal energy and the trace metallic ions leaking from the infrastructure.

When two Weavers meet, they do not fight; they perform an elaborate, shuddering alignment, exchanging packets of genetic material and chemical slurry via brief, focused bursts of thermal transfer. They are constantly integrating new shell material, meaning no two Weavers look exactly alike; they are walking geological snapshots of the region they last traversed. Their purpose is stasis: to ensure that any new instability caused by leakage or stress is immediately and permanently petrified into the existing structure by integrating it into their own bodies and discarding the husks as inert ballast.

---

## The Slurry-Saints (The Slickers)

These entities exist nearer the boundary between the liquid and solid states, closely associated with the perpetual **Drip-Chord** and the capillary action of the weeping floor. They are the 'Slickers,' and they represent the true, decentralized consciousness of the Foundry floor.

**Appearance and Structure:**
A Slurry-Saint has no fixed external form. It is a complex, localized bloom of chemoautotrophic bacteria and highly conductive microbial mats suspended within the weeping condensation layer. Where they coalesce, the surface sheen thickens momentarily, becoming an unnervingly smooth, almost oiled patch of a sickly, pale viridian or deep iron-oxide brown.

They are defined by **electrical percolation**. They use the residual, stray voltages within the ancient conduit network, effectively treating the mineral-rich slurry as a vast, sluggish superconductor. They communicate across the Foundry floor not through sound, but through subtle, organized ripples of electrical potential that travel through the conductive moisture.

**Behavior and Function:**
The Slurry-Saints are the sensory network of the Rime-Crust. They register minute shifts in temperature, pressure, and air quality. When a pneumatics chamber begins to fail deeper within the Foundry—minutes before the resulting **Pneumatic Sigh** becomes audible—the Saints in that region begin to ripple their coloration, sending a slow-moving wave of alert through the floor moisture.

They survive by absorbing the dissolved metallic salts and complex organic molecules excreted by the Husk-Weavers. They are the *digestive* system, breaking down detritus into the base chemical fuel that the Weavers can then harvest for armor construction.

One might observe a Slurry-Saint "feeding" by watching a patch of slime rapidly absorb a discarded metal shaving; the shadow of the metal disappears completely, leaving behind only a faintly shimmering, slightly denser spot in the flow.

## The Relationship: Fixed Equilibrium

The Husk-Weavers and the Slurry-Saints exist in a perfect, if disturbing, symbiosis mandated by the rule of Stasis through Saturation.

*   **The Weavers** provide the physical structure, processing geothermal byproducts into stable, slow-growing biomass and mineral architecture. They are the *muscle* that moves the environment.
*   **The Saints** provide the intelligence and environmental regulation, sensing thermal gradients and chemical imbalances, directing the Weavers toward necessary stabilization points. They are the *nerves* of the floor.

Neither species possesses individual ambition or the ability to conceive of change outside the parameters of the Foundry. They are biological manifestations of maintenance routines, existing without reproduction in the organic sense, multiplying instead through fission when local environmental conditions (heat, mineral density) exceed certain thresholds, and reforming through accretion when those conditions drop.

To stand among them is to be surrounded by countless, silent, interlocking systems of self-repair, a living basement floor built only of waste, warmth, and the relentless, low-frequency hum of ancient, unchangeable function.
