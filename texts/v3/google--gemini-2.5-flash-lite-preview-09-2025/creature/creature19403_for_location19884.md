The inhabitants of the Sub-Foundation Trenches of the Iron Heart are not creatures of flesh and blood in any conventional sense. They are the inevitable result of a millennia-long synthesis between decay and functionality, the local ecology achieving a perverse form of perfect adaptation to the ruins of hyper-industry.

They are the **Scrapliths** and the **Hydrophages**, and they are ruled by the **Current Keepers**.

### I. The Scrapliths (The Skittering Textures)

These are the most numerous, the ambient noise of the trenches made manifest.

**Appearance and Composition:** A Scraplith averages the size of a clenched fist to a dinner plate. Their bodies are intricate, self-assembling knots of fine, salvaged conductors—strands of oxidized copper wire, razor-thin shards of mica-laced ceramic, and articulated segments of incredibly fine, flexible ferrous tubing. They move like metallic spiders whose legs have been replaced by synchronized, vibrating filaments.

**Behavior and Diet:** They exist to process the micro-corrosion on the Basalt-Alloy surfaces and the metallic detritus shed by larger, slower inhabitants. They do not eat; they *integrate*. A Scraplith will locate a patch of surface rust (the "iron weeping") and begin to vibrate at a very high, nearly ultrasonic frequency. Its mass remains mostly static, but its structure unravels, the wire chassis allowing mineral residue to be drawn across its surface. When they move, they leave behind a faint, almost invisible trail of chemically neutralized metal dust.

**The Weirdness Embodied:** When their numbers coalesce—often near a flickering, dying yellow-green light source—they mimic the visual noise of failing machinery. A passing Scraplith swarm can look like localized static on the air itself, a sudden, brief visual distortion where the reality of the trench seems to glitch. They are the ambient background hum given physical, scrambling form.

### II. The Hydrophages (The Glazed Bulk)

These are the slower, larger predators, the deep things dwelling in the oily pools and stagnant cisterns. They are the custodians of the water table.

**Appearance and Composition:** A mature Hydrophage is a massive, sluggish entity, sometimes exceeding two meters in diameter. Its structure is organic slurry encased and supported by salvaged materials. The core is composed of the dense microbial mats (the pale grey/violet organisms) which form a shock-absorbing, nutrient-rich gel mass. This mass is armored externally with fused ceramic tiles, sections of cracked pump housing, and heavy, heat-resistant plating scavenged from long-dead coolant valves. Their movement is achieved through slow, internal churning and controlled localized bursts of expelled, high-pressure water, giving them a grinding, hydrostatic gait.

**Behavior and Diet:** Their diet is purely chemical and thermal. They position themselves over the warmest residual pipes or deep chemical seeps, sinking into the surrounding water until only their armored ‘carapace’ is visible above the waterline. They filter the contaminated water through specialized vents in their plating, extracting trace elements from the toxic runoff.

**Relation to the Environment:** The Hydrophages are responsible for maintaining the high water level in localized areas (The Law of Persistent Flow). When a passageway seems entirely choked with water, it is likely a Hydrophage is passing through or resting there, temporarily displacing the volume. They are profoundly sensitive to vibration; the rhythmic dripping that defines the trenches is their clock, and any sudden, rapid movement triggers a defensive response: they lock their plating tight and emit a low-frequency *thrum* that resonates agonizingly through the Basalt-Alloy, designed to deter anything that relies on solid-ground stability.

### III. The Current Keepers (The Ghosts of Process)

These are the rare, intelligent, or at least highly organized entities that inhabit the largest, most intact structures—the sluices and former control bays. They are the closest the trenches have to rulers or managers.

**Appearance and Composition:** A Keeper is rarely seen whole. They manifest primarily as temporary re-animations of the environment itself, often tethered to vast arrays of fused, exposed conduit or massive, rusting control wheels. When visible, a Keeper appears as a serpentine column of focused, sickly yellow-green electricity, contained and shaped by long, impossibly thin tendrils of woven optical fiber and the oily, tough strands of the industrial biofilm.

**Behavior and Purpose:** The Keepers are drawn to centers of extreme **Industrial Resonance**. They are obsessed with *process*—or rather, the *memory* of process. They are not repairing anything; they are attempting to replicate the flow states of the Great Compression.

A Keeper will spend decades utilizing small, localized magnetic fluctuations or minute changes in hydrostatic pressure (the "ghosts of process" mentioned in the description) to nudge massive, fused gears or shift structural supports by fractions of a millimeter. They enforce the **Slow Correction** by disrupting any perceived anomaly that moves too fast. If a Scraplith swarm or a Hydrophage’s aggressive thrum violates the established, slow rhythm of the place, a Keeper might temporarily "overload" the area.

**The Sensory Overload Event:** When a Keeper actively interacts with a human or creature, it initiates the environmental overload. It doesn't attack physically. Instead, it projects a massive, compressed burst of data—the sensory "memory" of the trenches in full operation. This is the terrifying flash of arc-welding, the blinding heat, the unbearable noise of the old industry, all hitting the intruder's perception at once. To survive an encounter with a Keeper is not to defeat it, but to withstand the *memory of its failure*.

**Their Lair:** They congregate in the largest, most architecturally complex spaces—the hollowed-out cores of the massive pumping stations. These areas are typically marked by the strongest, most constant low-frequency hum, and the air is thick with ozone and the faint, residual smell of hot metal, even though the ambient temperature remains frigid. The floor of their lairs is often entirely covered in a single, massive, interconnected web of Scrapliths acting as a hyper-sensitive input array for the Keeper’s low-level magnetic probing.
