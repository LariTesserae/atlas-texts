The Spine of the Sun-Eater is not inhabited by those who seek comfort, nor by those who enjoy the open air. The residents are beings forged in the absolute crucible of high-altitude thermal processing and industrial refuse, existing in a perpetual state of necessary, highly specialized reaction.

The primary occupants are the **Vent-Dredgers** and the **Reflectors**.

### The Vent-Dredgers (The Subsistence Class)

These are the dominant life form in terms of sheer biomass, intrinsically linked to the rare pockets of condensation. The Vent-Dredgers are the culmination of the Cinder-Crawlers' lineage, having evolved beyond simple arthropods into slow, semi-sessile organisms that manage the bacterial mats.

**Physiology and Appearance:**
A Vent-Dredger is massive, often occupying the entire basin of a single steaming pool. They possess a vast, segmented, chitinous body that is almost entirely dark grey or black, absorbing every stray photon of non-reflected heat. Their legs are vestigial, used only for minimal adjustments within the viscous sludge of the pool. Their most notable feature is the **Bi-Aperture**, a complex osmotic organ located centrally on their dorsal side. This organ acts as both a filtration system, drawing in the metallic-laced water, and a bizarre, low-grade heat sink.

They "breathe" by slowly pumping the superheated, humid air through internal heat exchangers, resulting in the faint, constant, unsettling dampness that pervades their localized ecosystems. When they move their massive bodies—a process that takes hours—they drag across the rock, leaving a trail of slick, mineralized residue that feeds the lower cyanobacteria layers.

**Behavior and Sound:**
The Dredgers are fundamentally internal processors. Their existence is defined by the slow, grinding release of energy drawn from the heavy metal cycling and the heat differential between their bodies and the ambient temperature. Sound is alien to them, save for the occasional, dull *thrum* generated when they shunt large batches of processed effluent back into the geothermal vents near the pool's base. They are utterly indifferent to the high-pitched whine of the atmosphere, as they do not possess the delicate external auditory mechanisms of lower organisms. They are the slow, microbial heart of the mountain’s damp islands.

### The Reflectors (The Scavenger/Custodian Class)

These creatures patrol the exposed, fiercely bright rock faces, representing the dynamic, mobile element of the Spine’s ecology. They are the inheritors of the Cinder-Crawler archetype, adapted for transit across surfaces that destroy organic matter through sheer thermal energy transfer.

**Physiology and Appearance:**
Reflectors are lean, fast, and angular, resembling complex, articulated clockwork sculptures more than biological entities. They are about the size of large canines, though their geometry is far more fractured. Their exoskeletons are not merely infrared-reflective; they incorporate trace elements of the native silvery-black basalt, giving them a mirror-sheen that shifts depending on the angle of the sun.

Their six limbs terminate in microscopic, crystalline pads that allow them to grip the vitrified rock surfaces without friction heating their tissues past critical thresholds. They possess multiple clustered eyes, shielded behind lenses of naturally grown, dark quartz, allowing them to navigate the blinding glare.

**The Industrial Link:**
Reflectors are fiercely drawn to the dead machinery. Unlike the Dredgers, they actively integrate the industrial detritus. They are often seen meticulously picking at rusted struts or chewing on corroded feed-pipes. Their purpose—the reason for the intense industrial residue—is that they metabolize the residual ferrous compounds and electromagnetic interference. They utilize the phantom magnetic fields that plague the area to navigate and communicate, riding the subtle distortions like currents in an unseen river. When a Reflector moves swiftly across a magnetic anomaly, its shadow may momentarily distort, as if reflecting light from a source that isn't there.

**The Sun-Eater Towers:**
The Reflector population is densest around the great, defunct **Sun-Eater Dishes**. They do not build, but they *maintain* these structures in a perverse symbiosis. A Reflector will patrol the rim of a massive, corroded parabolic dish, consuming the oxidized metal that traps energy. When a thermal runaway event occurs—a spontaneous flash-melting of the rock beneath the dish—the Reflectors converge. They enter the superheated zone for brief, terrifying excursions, gathering the freshly crystallized slag, which is rich in exotic isotopes they require for molting.

### The Echoes (The Ambience)

There are no true predators here, but there are manifestations of the residual energy field. These are not creatures but localized, persistent distortions tied to the physics-breaking nature of the industrial failure.

When the atmosphere is at its thinnest and the sun at its most direct, a **Thermal Ghost** might coalesce near the edge of a catastrophic slag field. It takes the shape of whatever failed most spectacularly in that location—a scaffolding framework, a collapsed ore-crusher, or a human-like silhouette caught mid-scream. These forms are pure heat haze and refracted light, shimmering for moments before the local magnetic field shifts and the image dissolves back into the painful glare. They are the *phantom echoes* mentioned in the description—the mountainside’s memory of stress.

In summary, the inhabitants of the Spine are perfectly hardened specialists: the **Vent-Dredgers** are the slow, living heat sinks and chemical recyclers of the damp oases, while the **Reflectors** are the swift, metallic scavengers who patrol the fiery heights, sustaining themselves on the ghost energy and structural decay left behind by the great Sun-Eaters.
