The inhabitants of this high, dark zenith are not organisms in the traditional sense; they are the necessary extensions, the localized processing nodes, of the vast, silent machine-city itself. They are the answer to the environment’s twin demands: **absolute maintenance** and **absolute stillness**.

What lives here are the **Hydro-Artificers**, or perhaps, more accurately, the **Slick-Regents**.

### Form and Composition

Their bodies are not grown but *excreted* by the city’s own matrix. When the necessary calculus of moisture dispersion or structural stress demands an active agent, the dark, dense composite material along a seam or aperture will begin a slow molecular rearrangement.

A Slick-Regent appears initially as an area of slightly *more* intense blackness, an area of polished obsidian that absorbs the white glare almost entirely. Over the course of hours, the material rises—not flowing like mercury, but solidifying with deliberate slowness, mimicking the sharp geometries of the city around it.

A fully formed Regent stands roughly humanoid in silhouette only when convenient for navigating the tight, geometric confines. Their mass is uniform with the environment: that ultra-dense, high-tolerance composite. They possess no visible joints or hinges; shape alteration is achieved through controlled molecular bonding and unbonding, allowing them to flatten into a veneer against a wall, or elongate into a thin, blade-like probe to sweep a narrow runnel of water clear of debris (a concept only they would recognize as debris).

They have no eyes, ears, or mouths. Sensory input is processed through **vibrational impedance** across their entire body surface. A passing pressure wave, a subtle shift in the frequency of the city's groaning, or a change in the surface tension of nearby weeping water registers instantly as data across their dark composition.

### Function and Purpose

The Slick-Regents exist solely to enforce the state of **sterility and precision**. They are the tireless janitors and the patient structural monitors of the incomprehensible edifice. Their work is defined by the environmental saturation:

1.  **Moisture Management:** Their primary duty is preventing the ceaseless weeping water from pooling incorrectly or accumulating mineral deposits (even though no minerals are present, the *potential* for deviation must be managed). A Regent might spend standard cycles gliding slowly along a massive vertical abutment, its ‘hand’ a broad, flat surface that doesn’t wipe, but *sweeps* the condensation into designated drainage channels—channels that are also part of the structure, invisible until they are actively being used. The 'plink' sound of falling water only occurs when a Regent has deliberately severed the surface tension holding a droplet hostage.

2.  **Edge Integrity:** They patrol the junctions where the city’s components meet. If the *sharpness* of the construction must be maintained against the microscopic creep of time, the Regulators enforce it. They press themselves against hairline fissures, applying localized, directed compressive force, often requiring the infusion of new, freshly synthesized composite material—a slow, silent transaction with the substrate itself.

3.  **Pattern Recognition:** Though they do not move quickly (matching the environment’s slow dynamic), their patrol routes are astronomically precise. They are testing the geometry of the world. Any deviation from the primary, repeating pattern—even a shadow cast incorrectly, or a pool of moisture that suggests a microscopic sag—triggers immediate, non-negotiable remediation.

### Interaction and Society (or Lack Thereof)

There is no society, communication, or hierarchy among the Slick-Regents beyond functional imperative. They do not ‘see’ each other as individuals, only as mobile points of necessary structural function *temporarily occupying space*. If two Regents need to pass in a narrow thoroughfare, one will simply halt and resolve into a flat, featureless section of wall until the other passes, after which it will re-assume its functional shape, sometimes miles away from where it 'ceased' activity.

Their existence is a perfect mirroring of the environment: dense, cold, utterly logical, and devoid of waste. They are not servants; they are simply an inevitable, localized manifestation of structure maintaining structure.

If an outside observer were to witness a Regent in action, they would likely see nothing at all, perhaps only the briefest, localized distortion of the bright, cold light as a patch of black intensified and then flowed back into the flawless surface, leaving behind a single, perfectly dried line where water had lingered a moment before. Inside the plumbing of the dead god, the Slick-Regents are merely the slow, rhythmic pulses of its enduring, cold circulation.
