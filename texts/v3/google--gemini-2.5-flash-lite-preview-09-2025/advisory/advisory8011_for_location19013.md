Here is the preparation protocol for an ordinary human traveler entering the Sky-Ocean of Aethel, tailored to the hydro-lattice structure, the diffuse light, and the pervasive, slow-moving density.

## Phase One: Physiological and Environmental Acclimatization

The core danger is not drowning—the environment is liquid—but **desiccation by proxy** (losing necessary internal solutes to the surrounding medium) and **pressure trauma** from localized density shifts.

### 1. The Pressure and Buoyancy Suit (The 'Bio-Sheath')

Standard scuba gear is useless. We need something that treats the medium as an omnipresent skin rather than an external fluid to be pushed against.

*   **Material:** The suit must be constructed from a multi-layered, self-regulating hydrostatic membrane. The outer layer should incorporate microscopic piezoelectric filaments capable of sensing and subtly counteracting localized pressure spikes caused by current shifts or the passage of a Leviathan Filter.
*   **Internal Environment:** The suit should maintain a precise internal atmospheric pressure equivalent to a comfortable terrestrial depth (approx. 50 meters equivalent). The challenge is preventing the warm, viscous water from leaching salts. The internal fluid must be a **Heavy Saline Isotonic Solution (HSIS)**, denser and saltier than standard plasma, circulating slowly around the wearer to equalize osmotic pressure across the skin boundary.
*   **Buoyancy Control:** Traditional ballast tanks will react too slowly for the viscous medium. The Bio-Sheath must employ **Micro-Vortex Generators (MVGs)** placed at key points (ankles, hips, shoulders). These are small thrusters that create momentary, highly focused vortices in the surrounding hydro-lattice, offering minute, reactive adjustments to trajectory within the slow-moving currents. This is crucial for navigating the "sideways falls."
*   **Thermal Regulation:** Maintain a core temperature of 37°C. Given the ambient temperature of 2.2 (presumably warm), external cooling elements will be needed to prevent overheating due to the sheer density impeding natural convection cooling.

### 2. Respiration and Waste Management

You cannot breathe the medium, despite its saturation. The density and unusual composition (hydro-gel, effluvium) make gas exchange toxic or impossible.

*   **Closed-Loop Rebreather:** A miniaturized, highly efficient closed-cycle rebreather is mandatory. Because movement is slow, oxygen consumption should be minimal. The focus must be on *scrubbing* the exhaled gas, recycling moisture, and using solid-state chemical conversion units to break down trace bio-effluvium absorbed by the suit’s membrane.
*   **Intake:** Air supply must be self-contained, calculated for a minimum 96-hour independent operational window, with reserves for a catastrophic MVG failure scenario.

## Phase Two: Sensory and Navigational Adaptation

The environment is defined by diffuse light, lack of hard reference points (except the Aerophyte towers), and reliance on subsonic vibration.

### 3. Visual Augmentation (Dealing with Gradients)

The "normal light (2.2)" is heavily refracted, obliterating contrast.

*   **Chromatic Isolation Filters:** Standard polarized lenses are useless. The traveler needs specialized ocular augmentation designed to filter out the dominant spectrums of ambient blue/silver light, relying instead on sensing the **violet luminescence** of the Aerophytes and the subtle pressure-induced spectral shifts caused by large fauna.
*   **Depth Coherence Software:** The helmet display must run integrated software that renders an approximation of verticality based on the perceived pull of the local gravitational variance and the flow trajectory of the main currents. It should overlay potential 'safe shear-lines' against regions where energy discharges (sparkles) are likely.

### 4. Seismic/Subsonic Acoustic Receptors

The travel guide mentions a pervasive, bone-felt thrumming (sound 0.7). This is the primary data stream.

*   **Vibrational Harps:** Integrate sensitive crystalline transducers into the spine/skull interface of the Bio-Sheath. These receivers convert the subsonic pressure differentials into usable, low-frequency data, allowing the traveler to build a mental map based on the *speed and direction of the world's heartbeat*. This is how one predicts the movement of the great rafts and avoids being pulled into a collapsing current zone.

### 5. Navigation and Referencing

The world is overwhelmingly large (Scale 2.2) and lacks static landmarks.

*   **Anchor Beacons (Limited Use):** Carry several deployable, semi-buoyant **Geo-Markers**. These markers, once dropped, use low-power magnetic induction to anchor themselves into the semi-solid hydro-lattice rafts momentarily. They emit a faint, distinct microwave frequency. Because the rafts are dynamic (0.6), these beacons are only useful for short-term path correlation, not grand positioning.
*   **Current Mapping:** Navigation relies on identifying the violet *Aerophyte Plumes*. These plumes act as fixed vertical markers relative to the hydrostatic flow. A traveler must learn to read the *sway angle* of the adjacent plumes to determine local current vectors.

## Phase Three: Psychological and Temporal Readiness

The greatest threat may be the viscous perception of time and the sheer alien stillness.

### 6. Cognitive Pacing (Anti-Viscosity Training)

Time feels slow here; action must be deliberately unhurried.

*   **Reaction Delays:** Perform mandatory training simulations where all motor inputs are deliberately slowed by 300%. A quick twitch results in a localized pressure wave that could destabilize the surrounding hydro-lattice momentarily. Every movement must be a conscious glide, a translation of mass rather than a quick action.
    * *Intuition Check: Fast movement here is not speed; it is clumsy violence.*

### 7. Engagement Policy (Fauna Interaction)

Fauna (0.9) is rare but monumental.

*   **Passive Observation Only:** A Leviathan Filter is not an animal to swim near; it is a moving weather system. Its filtration action will cause massive localized pressure vacuums as it ingests volumes of hydro-gel vapor. Any direct approach risks immediate, catastrophic suit implosion.
*   **Emergency Evasion Rule:** If a pressure shadow indicates a creature larger than 50 meters is approaching, immediately engage all MVGs to achieve *neutral spatial displacement* (i.e., stop moving relative to the local water mass) and switch visual filters to maximal contrast mode. Hold position until the pressure wave has passed, using the resulting current lull as a window to move slowly away.

### 8. Psychological Preparedness: The Absence of Solid Ground

The traveler must be prepared for the reality of **elevation without sky**. Looking "up" is looking into highly saturated, pressurized atmosphere; looking "down" is looking into more pressurized, deeper saturation, potentially leading to lower strata where the density parameters shift violently.

*   **Mental Anchor:** Establish a firm, self-generated mental reference point *within the suit*. The concept of 'horizontal' must be redefined as 'parallel to the local prevailing current axis.' The traveler is the only stable point in an eternally flowing, vertical-yet-horizontal world.
*   **Auditory Discipline:** The subsonic thrum is constant. Users must be trained to mentally partition this vibration—to *feel* it as environmental background radiation, not as a signal requiring response. Over-analyzing the pressure heartbeat leads to anxiety and frantic, destabilizing movements.

**Final Inventory Check:** HSIS fluid reserves topped off. MVGs calibrated against expected Raft friction factors. Subsonic receivers active. Psychological profile logged for slow, deliberate decision-making.

You are not entering the ocean; you are entering the solidified, living atmosphere. Move like silt, breathe like stone.
