## Preparation Guide for the Great Glaze

The Great Glaze is not a place of dramatic danger, but of profound, insidious environmental conformity. It seeks to incorporate you into its slow, warm, hydrostatic equilibrium. Survival here depends less on fighting off predators and more on maintaining your own physical and psychological integrity against the crushing monotony and relentless, pervasive wetness.

This is a deep-time environment, and your preparation must account for the slow, ceaseless action of water dissolving the world, and by extension, you.

---

### I. Physical Resilience: Against the Warm Saturation

The primary threat is the environment itself: prolonged exposure to constant, lukewarm, humid moisture will rapidly degrade most standard equipment and organic materials.

#### A. Clothing and Footwear: Defeating the Perpetual Damp

Your goal is to create a microclimate around yourself that fights the exterior humidity, prioritizing water shedding and rapid drying over insulation (which is unnecessary due to the ambient warmth).

1.  **Outer Shell (The Hydrophobic Layer):** You require materials with near-perfect water repellency and high breathability. Forget Gore-Tex; look for technologically advanced, sealed polymer-weave synthetics, perhaps treated with non-organic fluoropolymers or advanced silanes. The outer layer must shed the sheeting water instantly. Seams must be **ultrasonically welded**, not stitched, as needle holes will become perpetual wicking points.
2.  **Underlayers (The Wicking System):** Insulation is counterproductive. You need layers that wick moisture *away* from your skin and move it rapidly toward the exterior shell for evaporation. Choose advanced synthetic wicking fabrics (polypropylene blends or high-grade microfiber). **Cotton is an absolute death sentence**; it will absorb the humidity, cling to you, and prevent any possibility of drying, leading to rapid heat loss (paradoxically, even near-warmth) and catastrophic skin breakdown.
3.  **Footwear (The Anchor and Drain):** Standard boots will become sponges and will chafe your feet into useless pulp within hours.
    *   **Boots:** Seek wide, aggressive tread boots made of hard, molded, non-porous synthetic compounds (like specialized resin or high-density rubber). They must be completely seam-sealed. Crucially, they need **internal moisture management**. Consider integrated, passive dehumidifying inserts (like advanced silica gel packs periodically swapped out) to manage the sweat endemic to the climate.
    *   **Socks:** Several pairs of thin, synthetic sock liners are essential. Change them at the first sign of dampness, even if the exterior feels dry. **Keep one "Inner Sanctum" pair dry at all times** to wear while resting or when morale is low.

#### B. Equipment Casing and Sealing

Every piece of technical gear, electronics, or stored rations must be treated as if submerged.

*   **Dry Bags are insufficient.** Standard dry bags rely on zippers or roll-tops that are stressed by constant pressure and temperature fluctuation. Use **hermetically sealed, high-pressure composite cases** for optics, communication devices, and navigation tools.
*   **Lubrication:** All moving parts on pack closures, hinges, and complex tools (like multi-tools) will corrode or seize rapidly. Bring specialized, water-displacing, non-gelling lubricants, applied liberally and reapplied daily.

### II. Navigational and Physiological Strategy

The Glaze is uniformly flat, monotonous, and visually deceptive due to the low-lying mist banks. Navigation is the key to not simply wandering until depletion.

#### A. Navigation in Uniformity

1.  **Ditch Terrestrial Maps (Mostly):** Paper maps are useless. The visual markers (whalebacks, runoff slicks) are too subtle and shift slightly beneath the water film.
2.  **Primary Navigation: Inertial and Geophysical:** Rely heavily on highly precise, well-shielded GPS/Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs). More importantly, if possible, bring a **ground-penetrating radar (GPR) or magnetometry suite**. The ancient, dark silicate bedrock should provide a stable, if subtle, geophysical signature that is independent of the ephemeral water surface. Use this to confirm macro-directionality.
3.  **Dealing with Mist:** The green mist banks near the horizon are not useful markers; they are gravitational sinks for humidity. If visibility drops below 100 meters, **stop moving**. The risk of walking blindly into a deeper pooling fissure or losing your bearing in the flat expanse outweighs the benefit of continuous travel.

#### B. Environmental Adaptation and Health

1.  **Dermal Integrity:** The stagnant, mineral-rich puddles and the chemical action of the warm water are major corrosive agents.
    *   Keep skin covered whenever possible.
    *   Carry high-strength, broad-spectrum **antifungal and antibacterial creams**. Any small cut, blister, or abrasion will rapidly become infected in this environment, potentially leading to swamp gangrene or aggressive fungal colonization of the skin layers.
    *   Apply barrier creams (thick zinc or specialized petroleum derivatives) to high-friction areas (groin, underarms, feet) *before* the damp sets in for the day.

2.  **Hydration Management:** While water is abundant, you cannot drink the surface water directly. It is saturated with dissolved minerals from the ancient rock. These minerals will rapidly throw your natural electrolyte balance into chaos, leading to kidney distress and severe dehydration despite constant drinking.
    *   **Filtration:** Bring a robust, multi-stage filtration system designed for high Total Dissolved Solids (TDS). This likely means a combination of pre-filtration (to handle the silt/bio-matter), Reverse Osmosis (RO) for mineral removal, followed by UV sterilization.
    *   **Salts/Minerals:** You must carry sterile, balanced electrolyte supplements to re-introduce necessary salts *after* you have stripped the water clean via RO.

### III. Psychological and Sensory Preparedness

This is arguably the most taxing element. The Glaze is loud yet monotonous. It’s vast but claustrophobic.

#### A. Noise Discipline vs. Sensory Overload

The pervasive *shushing* and *slurping* of the water are constant.

1.  **Hearing Protection:** Bring professional-grade, adjustable acoustic dampening gear. You need a system that blocks the low-frequency roar of the water and the loud, rhythmic *tacking* of the fauna, allowing you to still hear high-pitched warnings or your own immediate sensory alerts. Total silence may induce psychosis in this context; manage the sound pressure level, do not attempt total nullification.
2.  **Combating Monotony:** The visual flatness means your brain receives almost no novel input during transit. This is a dangerous psychological state conducive to hallucination and temporal distortion.
    *   Carry a compact **visual stimulus kit**: high-contrast, complex patterns (like fractal screens or brightly colored, constantly shifting geometric light boards) to engage the visual cortex during mandated rest breaks.
    *   Schedule hyper-focused tasks (maintenance checklists, inventory) every 90 minutes that require minute detail to enforce present-moment awareness.

#### B. Chemical and Temporal Awareness

The geological time frame of the Glaze warps human perception. Eons have eroded here. You must fight the urge to slow down.

*   **Altered State Defense:** The air is thick and humid, potentially carrying subtle, intoxicating volatile organic compounds (VOCs) released by the massive bio-mats or slow geological outgassing. Remain hyper-vigilant for unexpected mental fog or euphoria. Have your companion (or your own checklists) force reality checks if you feel time slowing excessively.
*   **Scent Saturation:** Everything will smell like warm, mineral-rich mud, ozone, and algae. Standard methods for scent masking will fail. A dedicated, powerful, non-organic scent (like pure industrial solvent) carried in a sealed dispenser can serve as a clean, artificial baseline reminder of "home" reality when the environment becomes too cloying.

### Summary Checklist for Departure

1.  **Rigid, Seam-Welded Outerwear:** Non-porous shell optimized for zero absorption.
2.  **Redundant Wicking Underlayers:** Synthetic only.
3.  **Hyper-Sealed Component Cases:** For all vital electronics.
4.  **RO Water Filtration Unit with Electrolyte Replenishment:** Absolutely critical.
5.  **Antifungal/Antibacterial First Aid Stock:** Treat every scratch immediately.
6.  **Active Noise Dampening Gear.**
7.  **IMU/Geophysical Navigation Suite:** To supplement poor visual sighting.
8.  **Visual Stimulus System:** For psychological maintenance.
9.  **Silicone/Resin Footwear:** Designed for perpetual submersion without wicking.

**Your watchword for the Great Glaze: Resist Dissolution.** Do not let the humidity seep into your gear, your skin, or your mind.
