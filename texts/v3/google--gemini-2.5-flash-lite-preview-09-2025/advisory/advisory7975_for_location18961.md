## Primer: The Sub-Stratal Engine-Pit Visit Protocol

This environment is a living, breathing furnace dedicated to an unknown, deeply buried industrial goal. Assume all surfaces are hostile in one way or another—hot, sharp, or electrically charged.

### Phase 1: Personal Environmental Suit and Thermal Management (The Absolute Necessity)

The primary threat is the **dry, radiating heat (Temperature 2.9)**. Standard environmental suits will fail quickly under these near-industrial conditions.

1.  **Primary Exosuit Layer: Active Thermoregulation Suit (ATS)**
    *   **Cooling System:** The suit must utilize an advanced closed-loop liquid cooling system, ideally incorporating a **cryogenic vapor exchange** unit, not just chilled water circulation. Standard cooling methods will struggle against the radiant heat soaking through the walls and floor. Ensure multiple redundant cooling pumps.
    *   **Radiant Shielding:** The outer layer of the suit must be composed of or lined with a high-albedo, low-emissivity material (e.g., vapor-deposited Hafnium Diboride layers or similar ceramic composites). Standard fabric insulation will simply absorb and re-radiate the massive infrared load.
    *   **Internal Atmosphere:** Maintain a slightly **hypobaric** internal environment (slightly lower pressure than standard atmosphere) to encourage efficient sweat evaporation if the cooling system suffers a temporary localized failure, but keep the humidity extremely low to prevent clamminess and aid heat transfer across the skin.

2.  **Integrity and Abrasion Resistance:**
    *   Given the **dense, layered construction (Density 2.2)** and the jagged, raw cuts of recent excavation, the suit must be highly resistant to abrasion, tearing, and puncture. Assume you will be brushing against sharp granite, scraping past stressed copper conduits, or maneuvering through tight, low-clearance areas. Ballistic weave reinforced with high-tensile polymers is insufficient; look for **Diamond Nanothread Mesh** or similar materials graded for deep-sea pressure/mining environments.

3.  **Footwear and Vibration Dampening:**
    *   The constant, deep **resonant thrumming** vibrating through the stone floor will cause fatigue and potentially nausea. Boots must incorporate heavy-duty **Vibration Isolation Soles**—thick layers of viscoelastic polymer or magnetorheological fluid chambers designed to damp low-frequency mechanical vibrations before they reach the skeletal structure.

### Phase 2: Navigation and Sensory Equipment

The lighting is **dim, aggressively colored (1.3)**, and constantly distorted by heat haze. Navigation will be impossible using only human vision.

1.  **Illumination:** Bring a dedicated, high-intensity, **broad-spectrum headlamp** capable of cutting through the yellow/green/ochre ambient light. Crucially, equip it with a **Thermal Overlay Mode**. Locating active heat sinks, steam fissures, or newly exposed raw stone cuts will be easier via thermal signature than visual acuity.

2.  **Mapping and Positioning:**
    *   **No Reliance on GPS/Surface Satellites:** You are deep sub-strata. Rely solely on **Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs)** synchronized with a handheld, shielded local scanner calibrated to the local stone density.
    *   **Acoustic Profiling:** Because the environment is defined by noise (low thrums, rhythmic drips), use **active sonar/Lidar scanning** integrated with your visor. The machine’s own echoes will help map the small, interconnected, unnatural passageways and reveal structural weaknesses or hidden vents.

3.  **Respirator/Filtration:**
    *   While the environment is described as devoid of clear **fauna**, the air is saturated with particulate matter, chemical residues from the obsidian mortar, and ozone near electrical systems. A full-face breather with a multi-stage filtration stack (HEPA, chemical scrubber, and ozone neutralizer) is mandatory. Even vaporized condensate dripping onto hot rock will release unknown mineral aerosols.

### Phase 3: Cognitive and Temporal Preparedness (The Weirdness)

The **Law of Necessary Saturation** suggests a cramped, overly packed functional space. The occasional bleed-through of residual data/memory impressions requires specific cognitive defenses.

1.  **Information Dampening:**
    *   Do not attempt to focus on the fleeting "memory impressions" dripping from the hot stone. They are data overload designed for non-biological processors, not human comprehension. Wear **custom-fitted, high-impedance acoustic dampeners** tuned to filter out frequencies associated with subsonic engine vibration, which may be interfering with auditory processing and exacerbating the psychic noise.
    *   A low-dose, stable **nootropic agent** administered 30 minutes prior to entry might help stabilize memory boundaries against the ambient "leakage."

2.  **Mental Discipline:**
    *   Move with **deliberate, non-idiosyncratic efficiency**. The engine complex favors function; random movements or hesitant pauses might mark you as an inefficiency to be routed around, potentially leading you into a pressure vent or an active processing boundary. Adopt a mechanical gait if possible.

3.  **Tool Selection:**
    *   Avoid overtly delicate or complex electronic tools. The combination of ambient heat, vibration, and potentially stray electromagnetic fields from the energy extraction process will fry standard microprocessors. Prioritize **mechanical, analog, or heavily shielded magneto-optical gear.** A simple, solid carbide pry bar might be more useful than the multi-tool that shorts out after five minutes.

### Phase 4: Contingency and Exfil

This is an active industrial site; nothing is designed for casual human interaction.

*   **Water Scarcity:** External water reserves are essential. The only internal water is scalding condensation or contaminated pools. **Do not drink pool water.** Rely entirely on sealed internal reserves supplemented by a robust water reclamation system within the suit.
*   **Emergency Egress:** Pinpoint the route away from the deepest engine nodes and towards the freshest-looking excavation cuts immediately upon arrival. Sharp, raw edges suggest recent construction or an unused service shaft—the least likely places to encounter active machinery cycling at maximum load.
*   **Interaction Stance:** If you encounter any functional drone, automated maintenance unit, or organic life (highly unlikely, given the ecology), **assume it is attempting to route you to the nearest processing or recycling node.** Do not attempt dialogue; prioritize immediate physical evasion through the vertical axes (up or down) if possible, as the horizontal paths are likely optimized for infrastructure flow.

**In Summary:** Prepare to be treated like a highly heat-susceptible, inefficient piece of foreign material in an impossibly tight, hot rock-grinder. Survivability hinges on **perfect thermal isolation** and **unwavering focus against sensory noise.**
