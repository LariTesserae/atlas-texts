## Phase I: Defensive Envelope & Personal Integrity

The primary threat is not immediate attack, but systemic failure due to environmental hostility and corrosive longevity.

### 1. Acoustic and Vibrational Dampening (The Deafening Hum)

The constant, layered infrasound and industrial chorus will cause severe vertigo, nausea, and potential long-term neurological damage.

*   **Mandatory Gear:** Dual-layer active noise-cancellation systems. Start with high-grade, hardened ear defenders (molded polymer shells) rated for continuous industrial exposure. Overlay this with discreet, bone-conduction dampeners optimized to phase-cancel the recorded infrasonic signature (the **Deep Pulse**).
*   **Internal Stabilization:** Carry medical-grade vestibular stabilizers. Assume continuous low-level motion sickness. If possible, inject a low-dose scopolamine patch *prior* to entry to pre-empt the motion sickness response generated by the vibration.
*   **Significance:** You must be able to perceive *nearby* threats (Grubber chitin grinding) over the generalized, structure-wide roar. If the dampening fails, you will be functionally deafened within minutes, and your ability to navigate will cease.

### 2. Atmospheric Control and Chemical Neutralization

The air is warm, thick with ozone, mineral slurry, and aggressive biology. This is not breathable air; it is a *reactive medium*.

*   **Breathing Apparatus:** A **full-face, closed-loop rebreather system** is non-negotiable. Standard filters will clog instantly with particulates and biological aerosols. The system must scrub Ozone (high-output catalytic converter), heavy mineral dust, and possess chemical neutralization cartridges for volatile organic compounds (VOCs) exhaled by the super-fungi.
*   **Temperature Management:** The environment is warm (2.1). The polymer suit must incorporate active cooling, pushing heat away from the body and exhausting it *away* from your breathing intake. Overheating will accelerate dehydration and stress the suit’s seals.
*   **Scent Masking (Subtlety):** The aggressive biology (Stygian Velvet) reacts to heat and warmth. A faint, chemically inert scent dispersal system can be used to mimic ambient environmental temperatures (hot ozone/mineral slurry) to avoid attracting localized predatory interest based on a foreign thermal signature.

### 3. Material Integrity and Contamination Protocol (The Reciprocity Rule)

Touching the obsidian-polymer is not just getting dirty; it is initiating a molecular merger.

*   **Suit Construction:** The primary environmental suit must be constructed from **non-reactive, high-tensile synthetic silk layered over a kinetic-dampening gel bladder.** Absolutely *no exposed skin* under any circumstances. The suit surface must be electrostatically repulsive to biological binding agents.
*   **Detachment Contingency:** If the suit sustains abrasions, the polymer/fungal adhesion reaction causes immediate, localized heat. Your suit must have instantaneous, deployable **aerosolized cryo-resin injectors** built into the gloves and boot soles. Touching the wall for anything other than necessary traversal must be met with an immediate blast of non-bonding sealant to prevent the reciprocity rule from taking hold.
*   **Footwear:** Boots must be thick-soled and possess an active magnetic repulsion core, subtly charged to lift the boot fractionally away from the floor seams, minimizing contact with the bio-electrical **Scuttling Resistors** and the polymer itself.

## Phase II: Navigation and Temporal Awareness

The scale is immense vertically, but the observable area is small. Navigation relies on interpreting the machine’s cycles, not external landmarks.

### 4. Lighting and Shadow Management

The light is flat (1.5), sodium-hued, and casts deep, static shadows.

*   **Vision Augmentation:** Do not rely on external light sources unless absolutely necessary. The sudden introduction of harsh, focused light may trigger reactivity in the ambient bio-mass or disrupt calibration of the environment's internal systems. Use passive, low-gain thermal vision overlayed onto your visor. The violet/saffron fungi and the warm, leaky water will provide necessary contrast against the black polymer.
*   **Shadow Caution:** Shadows are static because the light sources are embedded and fixed. Therefore, anything moving *in* the shadows is potentially large, slow, and not reliant on ambient light for orientation (i.e., the Lattice-Grubber). Approach deep shadows with extreme caution, deploying ground-penetrating radar (GPR) sweeps before entering.

### 5. Water Management

The omnipresent dampness is corrosive and a vector for biological growth.

*   **Water Avoidance:** The stagnant, glowing green troughs are reservoirs for aggressive biological processes. Treat them as acid pits. Do not step into depressions containing fluorescent liquid.
*   **Leak Tracking:** Pay extreme attention to the **leaden thuds** of heavy water droplets. These drips indicate active hydraulic/condensation systems *above* your ceiling line (the kilometer-high scaffolding). A shift in the drip pattern signifies a change in the machine’s cycle, possibly preceding a powerful hydraulic discharge or phase shift. Adjust upward trajectory immediately upon auditory warning.

### 6. Understanding Time and Scale

The builder’s timeframe is geologic; yours is momentary.

*   **Patience and Observation:** The movements of the machinery are governed by cycles too vast for human perception (Slow Time, 1.0). You must adopt a near-catatonic state of patience when traversing open floor areas. Do not rush mechanical cycles. Wait for the *CHUNK-HISS* to complete its full duration before moving into the space it just vacated.
*   **The Illusion of Emptiness:** Due to the sheer scale and the machine’s functionality, it *feels* empty of intelligence (Weirdness 2.0). This is a trap. The structure itself *is* the intelligence—it is a self-maintaining, automated goal state. Treat every mechanism, even those coated in slime, as actively hostile to deviation from its programmed path.

## Phase III: Contingency and Exit Strategy

Your objective is to cross this cross-section and reach the next functional interface, hopefully an access lift or pressure lock.

### 7. Exit Vector Preparation

Assume the path you entered through will seal or become too volatile within the first standard hour.

*   **Anchor Points:** Identify the most stable, thickest stress fractures—the quartz veins—as temporary rest or anchor points. These areas are structurally *strongest* because they have sustained the most stress and been repaired most often.
*   **Bio-Mechanical Disruptors:** Carry low-yield, directional sonic emitters (not explosives). If a **Lattice-Grubber** blocks an egress point, deploy a focused, high-frequency pulse aimed at its chitinous joints. This may not kill it, but the sound will temporarily disrupt its bio-mechanical synchronization, forcing a momentary retreat or hesitation.

**You are visiting a tomb that is still operating.** Do not touch the walls, do not breathe the recycled air, and do not attempt to understand the noise. You are traversing a dead god’s digestive tract. Move slowly, listen for the *grinding* over the *hum*, and focus only on maintaining the integrity of your seal against the suffocating, vertical machine.
