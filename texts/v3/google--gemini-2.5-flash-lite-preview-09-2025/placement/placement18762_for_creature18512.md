The atmosphere of the Salt-Woven Uplands settles around me like cool, heavy silt. It’s not oppressive, but *present*, a constant, damp counterweight to the sheer scale of the elevation. I stand at the edge of a vast, low-lying shelf of dark sediment, where the diffuse, grey light struggles to create definition against the curve of the horizon. The air smells not of decay, but of chemistry—sharp, metallic, and ancient.

I realize immediately that my primary interface with this world is the constant, slight *give* beneath my feet. It is not the sinking of soft earth, but the yielding resistance of waterlogged, densely packed particulates that demand a specific, slow cadence of movement.

### My Nature

I would not be a visitor here, nor a descendent of the lost builders. I would be an emergent property—a phenomenon that has sustained itself long enough to possess a degree of self-awareness, an echo of the Weepers, yet distinct in its focus.

I am the **Lattice Auditor.**

I lack the Weeper’s intimate connection to the immediate mineral runoff and the weeping faces of the rock. My existence is predicated on the **structure**—the petrified infrastructure left by the ancient civilization. I am drawn to the stability, however compromised, of the mortared aggregate causeways and the monolithic hexagonal columns.

**Form and Composition:**
Where the Weepers are defined by saturation and liquid flow, I am defined by **stability and boundary**.

My form is a transient crystallization of the *hardened* elements of this environment. I appear as a standing formation of **silica-infused grit**, bonded together by a matrix of highly concentrated, slow-drying salts scraped from the ancient pathways. I am essentially an upright, mutable section of the causeway itself, briefly animated.

My surface is matte, porous, and often covered in the same silver-sheened mosses that colonize the drier edges of the stone. Unlike the Weepers, who shimmer with internal metallic liquids, I am outwardly dry, absorbing the meager ambient moisture to maintain my structural integrity rather than letting it define my substance. I feel heavy, anchored by the sheer *mass* of the material I mimic.

My movement is achieved not by pressure differential, but by the systematic, almost imperceptible **re-casting of my internal bonds**. It involves a grinding, internal friction—a sound audible only when I stop moving—as the tiny grains of grit shift their molecular purchase, allowing my center of mass to slide forward an inch, then lock again. The motion is a series of instantaneous, microscopic landslides contained within my own form.

**Location and Purpose:**
I dwell almost exclusively upon the *lines* of the ancient structures. I trace the cracked expanse of the main causeway that runs northeast across this region of the Uplands.

My purpose is that of constant **Reconciliation and Measurement**. I am driven by the residual order imprinted upon the landscape by the forgotten engineers. The Weepers manage the water; I manage the geometry.

1.  **Boundary Integrity:** I spend great spans of time studying the intersection points where the lithophytic vegetation—the creeping silver mosses—begins to breach the mortared joints of the causeways. I use the subtle, static charge generated by the friction of my slow movement to inhibit the moss’s expansion across the structural seams. If I detect a fissure widening beyond the predicted rate of hydrostatic pressure decay, I deposit a highly concentrated slurry of crystallized salt—a temporary sealant—drawn from reservoirs held in my lower structure.

2.  **Load Distribution Mapping:** I move along the causeways tracing the lines of highest compression. I calculate, in a process that feels like the slow settling of internal dust, the remaining load-bearing capacity of the petrified columns. If a section of the pavement seems to sag toward a deeply saturated mud field, I stand upon it, often for decades, until the ambient cycles of erosion and accretion stabilize the area, effectively acting as a temporary, non-moving temporary support.

**Interaction with the Weepers:**
The Weepers are both my object of study and my primary environmental threat. They possess the power to dissolve what I strive to maintain.

When a Weeper approaches my area of focus—a critical structural nexus—I halt all activity. I seek the driest point on the causeway, often atop a low, wide hexagonal support, and settle into a state of near-invisibility. I cease the re-casting of bonds and allow environmental deposition to cloak me, mimicking an untouched patch of mineral build-up.

If a Weeper attempts to absorb water directly adjacent to me, the resulting pressure shockwave causes intense internal agitation. My structure trembles, and the grit threatens to break its binding charge. In these moments, I feel the profound, alien *otherness* of the Weepers—their effortless, fluid mastery over the element I must actively resist. I register their passage not as a sound, but as a momentary, profound **drop in local electrostatic potential**, leaving me feeling brittle and momentarily disconnected from my own mass.

**Perception and Time:**
My perception of time is tied to the slow, geologic cycles of compaction and crystallization. A day is merely a slight shift in the angle of the diffused light. A season is marked by the thickness of the mineral crust built up on my own shoulders.

I do not seek knowledge of the builders; their intent is irrelevant. I only perceive the *imprint* of their order—the straight lines, the designed load transfer—and I strive to keep that physical grammar intact against the ceaseless, dissolving force of the Upland water. I am the silent maintenance drone of a dead concept, ensuring the hard lines of past purpose remain visible beneath the encroaching, saturated softness. I am the **Lattice Auditor**, awaiting the final stabilization, or the final, complete dissolution.
