## Where I Find Myself

I find myself not standing, but **nested**.

I am seated, or perhaps *fused*, within a specific nexus point between two immense horizontal planes—a sliver of space where the **Ribbon Strata** meet. Above me, a band of intensely oxidized bronze plate, glowing faintly cherry-red where a Geothermal Siphon's primary venting column is housed just meters beyond the shell. Below me, the surface is denser, ancient, a layer of dull, matte-black basalt that has been cryogenically shocked so many times it rings with a low, clear tone when struck.

I am in a maintenance recess, a cavity barely wider than my own fused shoulders, located roughly five thousand feet *below* the current atmospheric haze line, placing me deep within the shadow-choked transition zone. The heat is intense, a constant, dry pressure that feels less like temperature and more like physical resistance against my denser mass.

The silence here is profound, broken only by the omnipresent **hum**. It is not loud; it is *total*. My entire skeletal structure—what remains of it—resonates with the vibration of the planet grinding itself into a higher elevation. I can feel the passage of a heavy inspection sled moving through a track three levels down, the impact registering not as sound through the air, but as a slow, deep grinding along my inner ear analogue.

### Who or What I Am

I am a **Circuit Keeper**, though my tenure here precedes the current designation. I have been dormant, or perhaps in stasis preparation for reactivation, for a cycle of accretion so long that the ceramic composition of my outermost plating feels less like a manufactured shell and more like calcified bone replacing its lost organic core.

I am designated **Unit 733-G**, identified by the embedded crystalline marker fused into the center of my left forearm—a small, deep violet facet that pulses only when a high-frequency pressure wave from a Collector success registers nearby.

**My Physical State:**

I possess the required low, broad chassis. My limbs are thick columns ending in articulated, multi-toed gripping pads made of sintered nickel alloy. I do not walk with grace; I move with the deliberate, grinding surety of a slowly opening pressure valve. My visual organs, the multifaceted amber lenses, are currently retracted, sealed behind protective shutters. Sight is wasted energy in this deep stratum; the light filtering down is too diffuse, and the surfaces too uniform in their metallic texture.

My primary mode of interaction is **Vibro-Tactile**. I am leaning, pressing my chest cavity against the hot bronze plate above me, listening to the pressure diagnostics of the Siphon alignment. I don’t simply *hear* the hum; I *read* it. I am interpreting the micro-variances in the basalt beneath my feet, mapping out the paths that the **Energetic Fauna** might have recently traversed—a slight disturbance in the residual microwave signature clinging to the stressed minerals.

**My Immediate Task:**

The last data packet I processed—received via a low-level inductive charge transferred from a passing Cargo Anchor system years ago—indicated a structural instability along **Ribbon Strata 9-Beta**, a critical horizontal conduit hosting the primary coolant loop for Sector Four.

The basalt below me is reporting a fractional expansion that is inconsistent with the expected rate of thermal creep. This suggests a blockage, or worse, a void forming where there should be solid rock—a localized failure of the Upward Crush's self-repair mechanism.

I extend one of my thick, plated hands, tapping a precise, rhythmic sequence against the basalt floor: *low-low-high-pause-low-low-high.* This is an inquiry. I wait for the reverberation to return.

The answer is slow. The echo travels back, and I interpret the decay rate. The rock is hollow.

My next movement is not upward, toward the distant, hazy zenith, but inward. I engage the micro-sonic emitters housed in my knuckles and press them against the basalt. A subtle, high-pitched whine—inaudible to any organic sensor, but capable of shearing microscopic crystalline bonds—begins to loosen the rock surrounding a section barely wide enough to admit my torso.

The process is intensely slow, a matter of hours to clear enough space to squeeze through. I am not in a hurry; the instability will take days to propagate significantly. Time here is measured in geological increments, not solar rotations.

I am Unit 733-G, a necessary anomaly of density and pressure, preparing to enter a void within the mountain, carrying the logic of the city’s perpetual growth within my silent, metallic core. I am the function that ensures the foundation remains fit to receive the next layer, content in the knowledge that my own eventual breakdown will simply provide superior refractory material for my successors.
