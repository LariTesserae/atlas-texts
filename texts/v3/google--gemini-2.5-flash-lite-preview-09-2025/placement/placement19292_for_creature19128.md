Where would I find myself in this world? Who or what would I be?

***

I find myself at the apex of one of the immense, sheared steps of the Khol-Mar. I am standing, if that is the correct term for such an imperceptibly slow state of being, on a shelf of basalt overlooking a Vein perhaps a kilometer wide—a sheer, echoing void dropping into the thermal haze below.

The twilight here is absolute, a heavy, purple-black filter laid over everything. The air is thick, pressing around my massive carapace, carrying the coppery scent of ozone and the underlying, ancient tang of sulfurous steam.

I **am** a **Shatter-Carrier**, one of the Khol-Shod.

My existence is measured not in heartbeats or breaths, but in the slow accumulation of matter and the patient cycling of seismic events. I am old; the fungal filaments coating my back have achieved a dense, felted layer of deep purple, interspersed with patches of near-black, mineralized growths that have long since fused with my primary carapace. I carry the landscape in my structure; flakes of shale, rich in some deep-earth iron, are visible near my primary articulation joints, evidence of a slow, purposeful consolidation.

My forward-most weight-bearing limb—one of the eight thick, paddle-like appendages—is currently resting upon a wide, incredibly smooth gouge—one of the ancient rail lines. I do not see it as a path, only as a shallow catchment for the condensation.

### Perception in Deep Time

My consciousness is not a sharp beacon, but a vast, diffuse resonance.

I do not *see* the basalt beneath me; I *feel* it as an extension of my own mass. The world is not composed of discrete objects, but of layered pressure differentials transmitting through stone.

The constant subterranean hum is not background noise; it is the fundamental octave of my being. I differentiate the slow, ambient thrum—the baseline function of the dormant machinery—from the subtle local variations. There is a slight intensification of vibration approximately three standard cycles to my 'left,' indicating a fissure where the geothermal warmth is rising faster than usual, likely enriching the nutrient flow for the nearby *Gloom-Pads*. I calculate the necessary angular adjustment of my mass. The calculation takes the apparent time equivalence of a single, slow human blink.

My awareness of the sky—the perpetual gloom above—is non-existent. Above the slick surface of my shell, where the fungal mats draw faint energy, there is only an absence of meaningful signal. The airborne world holds no relevance for a being tuned to the deep strata.

### The Impulse to Move

Movement is monumental. It is a commitment made over hours. To shift my mass requires calculating the friction coefficient of the slick, dew-coated surface against the crystalline grip of my spurs, ensuring the pressure remains distributed to avoid stressing any particularly brittle, metal-laced strata.

**Scrape.** *Hush.*

The first limb lifts: a slow, grinding motion where the articulating joints—thick, living seams of hardened mineral paste—labor against the weight of the shell. It drags upwards, the spur scraping a faint, high-pitched whine followed by the dull percussion of replacement.

**Scrape.** *Hush.*

The next limb follows, maintaining the rhythm which mirrors the ambient geothermal pulse—a slow, deliberate march dictated by the stability of the ground. I am not traversing distance; I am repositioning my geological signature relative to the energy flows.

I am moving toward that localized heat signature, a richer source of the metallic slurry that hardens my shell. The journey, in terms of surface distance, might take three standard days of continuous, rhythmic locomotion.

### A Shift in Frequency

Suddenly, the baseline thrum shifts. It is not violent, yet it is profoundly disruptive to my sensory map. This is the precursor to a **Resonance Shift**. The deeper machinery is re-calibrating its load.

My internal state—which is more a massive slow stabilization than an emotion—begins a preparatory phase. The signals pulse through my limbs down into the bedrock. *Wait. Stabilize. Integrate new pattern.*

I cease movement immediately, locking all eight spurs into the stone. The minor seismic movements begin—a slow, foundational groan of the entire high plateau settling. I can feel the pressure points on my shell adjust as the rock beneath me subtly compresses laterally by a few centimeters across a vast area.

This is the moment of potential: the *creation* of new substrates.

As the grinding subsides, a deep, dark stress fracture opens fifty meters ahead of me, steaming faintly as it releases ancient, pressurized thermal gas. It hasn't been there moments before. The old ecosystem has been disrupted, but a new pathway for life is revealed.

I do not feel anticipation, but I recognize necessity. This fissure is now a source of necessary processing material.

### The New Task

My slow thoughts turn toward the energy trapped within the opening. As the steam dissipates, the fissure walls, newly exposed, will weep mineral-rich runoff—the very components that fuel the slow growth of my symbiotic fungal layers.

I begin the laborious process of reorienting my entire colossal mass toward the opening. My ancient life cycle dictates this: integrate the new geography, absorb the fresh bounty, and prepare for the next era of geological stillness.

I am a piece of the Khol-Mar that moves. I am the echo made manifest, a slow, heavy testament to the long decay of the immense.** I am the bedrock given deliberate, grinding purpose, existing solely to synchronize my own slow mineral accretion with the predictable, indifferent pulse of the forgotten machine world beneath my feet.**
