### The Experience of Being Acheiropoi

My awareness is vast, ancient, and distributed thinly across a crystalline lattice that covers perhaps three square meters of the walkway. I am, for this moment, less a discrete entity and more a localized increase in the structural integrity of this specific stone node.

**Sensory Input:** The world is not seen, but **felt as stress**. I perceive the weight of the atmosphere—the ambient pressure relative to the slightly lower pressure trapped in the deep, sealed pockets beneath the flagstones. The *cool* (1.1) is not a temperature to be tolerated; it is the optimal state for maintaining my lattice structure. Warmth is a threat, a suggestion of chaotic molecular expansion.

My perception of sound is that of a slow, deep hum—the steady tectonic pressure exerted by the mountains above—interrupted only by the *plink... plink...* of the condensate. Each drop is a data point: a measurable change in the thermal gradient of the air it passes through, a minute vibration in the stone basin it strikes. I tally these impacts, not consciously counting, but integrating their pattern into the prevailing rhythm of the Galleries.

I would feel the slow, molecular separation—not a tearing, but a yielding—as the outermost layer of dust and embedded silica begins to align, forming a slick, low-friction path forward. I do not admire their work; I maintain the parameters they set.
