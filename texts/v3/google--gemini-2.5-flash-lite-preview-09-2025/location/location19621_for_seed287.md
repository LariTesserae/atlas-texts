The air itself is a thick, warm breath, heavy with the smell of wet, hot stone and something metallic, faintly sweet—like overheated wiring mixed with chlorophyll. This is the Under-Hearth, a space accessed not by descending cliffs or sinking into ground, but by slipping through stress fractures in the bedrock, the world above offering only slivers of a blindingly bright, hot sun.

**Terrain and Elevation:**
We are deep, perhaps only a few dozen meters below the surface plane, but the elevation rating of 0.5/3 manifests as a profound sense of *enclosure*. The scale is that of a grand, subterranean hall, perhaps cathedral-sized, yet broken down into smaller, interconnected chambers by vast, ancient root systems and geological features. The walls are not smooth; they are sculpted by **established erosion (2.5/3)**. This erosion is less about abrasive wind and water, and more about slow, relentless chemical seepage and the subtle grinding of geothermal pressure. Layers of mineral deposit create draperies of ochre and verdigris, polished smooth in spots where ancient drips once fell with mechanical regularity.

**Water, Vegetation, and Atmosphere:**
The **dampness (0.8/3)** is pervasive, but not flooding. It manifests as constant, cold sweat on the lower walls, forming shallow, slick puddles that rarely exceed the ankle. These pools are not clear; they are oily, iridescent with suspended minerals, smelling faintly of battery acid.

Above, the **vegetation (1.6/3)** is aggressively **lush (1.6/3)**, thriving in the dim, hot environment. It is not forest; it is a **dense (1.7/3)**, layered mat of specialized life. Everything here is pale, thick-fleshed, and bioluminescent. Fungoid scaffolds rise like pale ribs, their caps oozing a sticky, refractive slime. Vines, thick as a human torso in cross-section, spiral up towards the unseen ceiling, their surfaces segmented and covered in fine, vibration-sensitive cilia. The light from these organisms casts the entire space in a perpetual, greenish twilight.

**Light and Sound:**
Primary **light (1.1/3)** is faint, filtering down in sporadic beams from the ceiling fissures, often illuminating nothing more than dust motes suspended in the heavy air. The true illumination comes from the flora and, unexpectedly, from the **industrial echoes (1.5/3)** of this depth.

The **loudness (1.9/3)** is a constant, low-frequency thrum, punctuated by sharper, more rhythmic sounds. It feels entirely mechanical, yet impossibly old. There are deep, slow *hissing* sounds, like massive steam valves slowly opening and closing over decades, combined with the metallic *clinking* of heavy machinery operating at a glacial pace—a rhythm that suggests the entire cavern is part of some gargantuan, forgotten engine.

**Weirdness and Ecology:**
This is where the Under-Hearth becomes truly alien. The **weirdness (2.3/3)** is tied intimately to the **lack of fauna (0.2/3)**. There are no insects, no burrowing creatures, no moving shadows except perhaps one’s own.

Instead, the **dense** vegetation is laced through with non-biological intrusions. The vines are laced with filaments of oxidized copper wiring. Certain rock faces appear to have been roughly 'cut' by massive, archaic industrial tools—the tool marks are still visible, choked with decades of pale fungal growth. Interspersed among the fungal stalks are **scattered (1.2/3)**, calcified remnants of **built structures (1.2/3)**. These are not ruins in the traditional sense; they are chunks of ferro-concrete or hardened slag, barely distinguishable from mineral outcroppings, forming low, damp platforms over the oily puddles.

The ecology functions on a strange cycle: the vegetation feeds off the geothermal heat and trace minerals dissolved in the intermittent moisture, but the *sound* suggests a parallel, energy-intensive process. It is theorized (by those who study the faint seismic readings from the surface) that the metallic tang in the air is off-gassing from whatever colossal power generators still churn beneath the lowest found levels—a vast, slow metabolism of pure geothermal-industrial conversion.

**Rules of the World:**
The primary rule here is **slowness (1.3/3 dynamic)**. Changes take geological or multi-generational timescales. A bubble of gas might rise from a pool over the course of a week, only to burst with a sound like splintering glass. The water level drops inches over a century. The dense environment is static; nothing here rushes. Movement feels obscene, a disturbance of a deep, settling equilibrium.

If one places a hand on one of the slick, cold walls, one can sometimes feel the vibration of the ancient machinery beneath the stone—a slow, grinding heartbeat that dictates the environment. It is a hot, dim, damp crypt where nature and a vanished era of heavy industry have fused into a single, slow, breathing entity, humming its eternal, deafening lullaby.
