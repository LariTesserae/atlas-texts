The Sky-Ocean of Aethel is not a place *near* the water, but a place *within* it, suspended at an altitude that defies terrestrial logic. The parameter of **elevation (2.2)** suggests height, but here, the height is measured in layers of atmospheric saturation, a continuous, high-altitude fluid medium where the density of suspended water vapor has reached a critical, almost solid state.

### Terrain and Composition

The terrain is not rock or soil; it is solidified, pressurized *hydro-lattice*. Imagine looking down from a vast, invisible ceiling and seeing the world not as landmasses, but as *currents*. The baseline **water parameter (2.7)** is pushed beyond mere submersion; this is a state of organized, suspended liquidity.

The visible 'ground'—if such a term applies—is composed of colossal, slow-moving **conglomerations of crystallized moisture and biological effluvium**. These masses are not icebergs; they are vast, semi-porous rafts of hydro-gel and ancient, compressed mineral dust drawn up from lower, unseen strata. The erosion is **young (1.3)**, meaning these features are sharp where they meet the flow, newly carved by the passage of vast, invisible currents, yet the underlying structure is ancient and slow-moving (**dynamic 0.6**).

Vegetation is startlingly **lush (2.1)**, but it is not rooted in earth. It is *epiphytic within the fluid*. Towering, whip-like strands of kelp analogue—the *Aerophyte*—drift in vertical plumes. These organisms have evolved to photosynthesize in the diffused, **normal light (2.2)** that penetrates the upper layers. They are not green, but a deep, bruised violet, catching the light in shimmering, refractive sheaths. They grow so densely in certain vertical columns that they mimic solid, swaying towers, contributing massively to the **density (2.4)** of the environment.

### Atmosphere and Light

The air, if it exists separately from the water, is thick, warm (**temperature 2.2**), and saturated to the point of optical illusion. Visibility extends for miles in muted blues and pale silvers. The **scale (2.2)** of the landscape is overwhelming; one can drift for days and only pass between different 'zones' defined by current speed or the presence of a larger, drifting biological formation.

The **light (2.2)** filtering down is refracted repeatedly, creating a pervasive ambient glow that makes shadows diffuse and indistinct. There are no hard lines, only gradients of saturation.

### Ecology and Fauna

The **fauna (0.9)** is exceedingly rare, fitting the pervasive quiet. Where organisms do exist, they are large and slow-moving, adapted to navigating or anchoring within the hydro-lattice. One might glimpse a *Leviathan Filter*, a creature that resembles a living, slow-moving net, sometimes hundreds of meters wide, moving to intercept the dense planktonic blooms that drift between the Aerophyte towers. Their passage is heralded not by sound, but by a momentary dimming of the light as they block the diffuse rays, and a subtle shift in the water pressure.

The environment is essentially **built (0.4)** and **tech-free (0.5)**; existence is dictated entirely by hydrodynamics and bio-luminescence, a purely organic, self-sustaining bath.

### Weirdness and Sound

The element of **weirdness (1.8)** manifests in the physics of the *submerged high-elevation*. Gravity seems localized or perhaps redirected. Objects that drift slowly become distorted by the pressures they inhabit, taking on fluid, momentary shapes before snapping back. There are vertical falls that lead not down, but *sideways*, into faster currents that pull one across vast distances.

The prevailing **sound (0.7)** is a deep, subsonic thrumming—the sound of immense pressure differentials shifting over geological timescales. This is the sound of the world moving, the slow grinding of the hydro-lattice itself. It is felt in the bones more than heard in the ears.

### The Experience

To exist here is to be perpetually suspended in an immense, warm bath of breathing, living water. You are swimming through a forest of violet light-catchers, miles above any concept of 'ground,' listening to the slow, pressurized heartbeat of the atmosphere itself. When a dense cluster of Aerophytes rubs against a drifting mineral raft, the resulting friction creates a momentary sparkle—a discharge of stored energy—the only sharp event in an otherwise languid, deep-blue existence. Everything moves slowly, deliberately, as if time itself is viscous.
