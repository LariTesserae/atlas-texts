The descent is not through air, but through layers of compacted, yielding darkness. This is the **Underhollow**, a sprawling, multi-layered cavern system that functions as an unintentional, subterranean metropolis. The scale is vast, stretching further than the eye—or the hand-lamp—can reasonably survey, yet the density of components packed within these spaces is suffocatingly high.

### Terrain and Structure (Elevation, Density, Erosion)

The fundamental terrain is raw, **emerging geology** (Erosion 0.0). This is not a cave smoothed by eons of dripping water, but a geologically *unsettled* space. The walls are sharp, jagged teeth of newly fractured shale and vein-laced quartz that constantly shift, grinding against each other deep below the surface crust.

Because the elevation is **below surface** (0.5), the primary structural elements are the massive, interlocking *Spires* and *Webs* formed by the settling and compacting of this raw material. This emergence creates an oppressively **packed** (2.8) environment. There are very few open floor areas; instead, the space is latticed with narrow ledges, overlapping platforms, and intersecting struts of rock that meet overhead like accidental scaffolding.

The only definition to the space comes from the **urban** (1.8) occupation. Settlements—hollowed pockets or crudely shored-up shelves—cling to every available surface. These structures are not *built upon* the geology so much as they are *excavated within* it, creating an infinitely layered palimpsest of habitation carved directly into the moving stone.

### Atmosphere and Water (Water, Temperature, Light)

The pervasive atmosphere is cool and heavy, registering as distinctly **cool** (1.4). The air rarely moves with purpose, settling into pockets of stale chill.

**Water** (1.0) manifests not as rivers or lakes, but as *seepage*. The atmosphere is damp, saturated with the slow, cold sweat of the deep earth. Condensation streaks down the razor-sharp rock faces, collecting in oily, slow-moving rivulets that trace the faults and fractures. Puddles are rare and shallow, composed of mineral-rich slurry that evaporates slowly back into the heavy air currents caused by the subterranean processes.

**Light** (2.0) is normal, which here means it is entirely artificial, yet functionally sufficient to read by. The density of occupation means illumination is constant. Every ledge, every packed dwelling, supports a weak light source—likely tallow, slow-burning resins, or perhaps bioluminescent molds cultivated using primitive methods. The overlapping structures create intense, localized shadows, but the sheer proliferation of these small lights prevents true darkness from taking hold. The effect is that of moving through a packed cityscape viewed from above at night, except the city *is* the surrounding rock, and you are pressed directly against its glowing infrastructure.

### Vegetation and Fauna

**Vegetation** (0.6) is sparse, struggling against the lack of proper photosynthetic light and the poor substrate. What exists are tough, slow-growing fungal mats and pale, rootless lichens that adhere to the damper, slightly warmer outcroppings near the thermal vents (if any exist—they are not yet prominent). These patches are grayish-white or an unsettling sickly yellow, taking advantage of chemical seepage rather than sunlight.

**Fauna** (1.0) is rare. The high density of the *built* environment and the resulting low structural integrity mean that large, mobile creatures are a hazard to the inhabitants and are likely hunted out or scared away. What remains might be small, pale detritivores—insects or segmented worms that feed on spores and decomposing organic matter trapped between the rock layers. Their presence is noted only by the occasional, near-silent scuttling heard when the ground tremor momentarily subsides.

### Dynamics, Tech, and Sound

The environment is intensely **active** (2.0). This dynamism stems not from weather, but from the constant, slow-motion geological restructuring. The **emerging erosion** is the primary dynamic process. Rocks settle. Struts groan under the immense, uneven pressure exerted by the sheer bulk of the packed, layered construction above and around them. This results in frequent, low-frequency tremors—the sound of the city *becoming* itself, or perhaps, falling apart.

The **built** elements rely on **primitive** (1.4) technology. There are no metals smelted from deep ores; construction involves careful placement of load-bearing stones, wedging, shaping softer rock with hardened bone or flint tools, and securing joints with a thick, resinous mud mixed with binding fungal spores. Infrastructure is mostly pathways, supported shelving, and primitive ventilation shafts—crude boreholes drilled downward or haphazardly linked across chasms to equalize the overwhelming internal pressure.

The prevailing **sound** (0.4) is one of near silence interrupted by the geological processes. The human or humanoid soundscape is hushed—necessary when the structure beneath you is perpetually on the verge of shifting. Voices are low whispers, tools strike rock with muffled thuds, and any sustained work must cease when the tell-tale deep resonance signals a major settling event. The dominant sound is internal: the slow, grinding *shhhhh-thump* of the emerging earth settling its own bones.

### Rules of the World

The primary law of the Underhollow is **Proximity and Support**. Since nothing is truly stable—the geology is too raw—the density of occupation is a form of mutual defense. Neighbors are shackled to each other not by social contract, but by the sheer necessity of shoring up shared structural points. Displacement is existential danger. If a section of the packed city shifts, the resulting chain reaction moves outward until stable junctions are met. Therefore, social life revolves around reinforcing integrity, sharing the burden of bracing, and minimizing disruptive activity, particularly drilling or heavy pounding, in favor of patient excavation and careful placement. It is a world where architecture is geology, and geology is constantly redefining architecture.
