The air of the **Hollows of the Sighing Plateaux** is thick, not with fog, but with an omnipresent, clinging *dampness*. The water parameter (2.1) manifests not as running rivers—erosion has long since smoothed the contours into this final, low state (1.3 elevation, 3.0 erosion)—but as a perpetual weeping. Everything is saturated. The ground, which is mostly compacted mineral dust and fine, solidified slurry, holds moisture stubbornly near the surface, creating greasy black patches that reflect the dim light (0.8) like oily mirrors. Great, slow tears run down the faces of what must once have been mesas, now rounded and humbled into vast, shallow domes covered in a thin, greyish patina of condensed humidity.

There is no vegetation (0.3). The world is fundamentally mineral. The rocks are of a deep, inert violet-grey, polished smooth by eons of this slow dissolution and reformation. Where water pools in shallow depressions, the pooling liquid is not clear; it is the color of weak graphite, slightly viscous, often shimmering with an oily, rainbow sheen caused by trace metamorphic elements leaching out of the ancient bedrock.

The temperature hovers perpetually just above the chill (0.6), a damp coolness that infiltrates bone rather than sharp, cutting cold. It is the temperature of a cellar sealed for millennia.

The expanse is undeniably open (1.8), stretching out to horizons blurred by the atmospheric density of settled minerals. Though the structures are scattered (1.3 built), they are very much a part of the landscape’s finality. These are not ruins in the traditional sense; they are **Spires of Absence**. They appear as narrow, obsidian-like teeth jutting perhaps fifteen feet from the plain, often occurring in isolation or small, irregular clusters. They are too perfectly faceted for natural erosion, suggesting a very ancient, very simple engineering (1.3 tech), perhaps just careful stacking or shaping of native stone using forces we no longer comprehend. They look less like buildings and more like accidental mineral growths responding to an external, geometric imperative that has long since ceased.

The defining characteristic of this realm, however, is the **sound** (2.6). It is deafening, but not chaotic noise. It is a relentless, low-frequency *thrum* that permeates the air and vibrates up through the soles of any hypothetical boots. This sound originates from the **Fauna** (2.0), which is abundant but utterly alien.

The creatures here are not carbon-based, or not in any recognizable way. They are aggregations of highly organized, slow-moving, semi-molten compounds—living geology. They resemble slow, rolling geodes or ambulatory rafts of solidified magma, moving with agonizing deliberateness across the damp flats. They generate the colossal noise through their very existence. Each creature, which can be the size of a small dwelling, emits a constant, infrasonic pulse that vibrates the environment. When multiple pass close to one another, the overlapping frequencies create destructive interference patterns, resulting in moments of terrifying, shuddering silence, followed immediately by an enormous *reverberation* that feels like the landscape itself is being struck by a massive, muffled gong.

**The Rules of the World:**

1.  **The Inertial Surface:** Movement across the plane is difficult. The ground is nearly flat due to the ultimate erosion, but the saturated surface layer resists quick action. Fast movement is impossible; everything proceeds at a slow, heavy pace, dictated by the viscosity of the air and the semi-fluid ground layer.
2.  **The Sound Contract:** The endemic fauna dictate the environment. They feed—or perhaps simply *are*—on ambient geological energy, and their omnipresent noise field seems to suppress any higher-order biological processes (explaining the lack of traditional flora). To approach a large fauna aggregation is to risk being rhythmically pulverized by the sheer acoustic pressure.
3.  **The Weirdness (1.4):** The Spires of Absence occasionally “sing.” When the light dims further, perhaps during the infrequent passage of a distant, slow-moving atmospheric body, the facets of the obsidian spires will briefly fluoresce with an internal, cold blue light, and they emit a sound that is precisely the *inverse* of the fauna’s thrum—a high-pitched, non-wave vibration that causes a strange, chilling disorientation in any entity perceiving it, forcing momentary stillness upon whatever moves nearby. This appears to be an ancient, passive defense mechanism or perhaps merely the discharge of stored energy.

This is a place of extreme age and inertia, where life is defined by immense mass and low frequency. The atmosphere is thick with the memory of water and the groan of living stone. It is sparse (0.8) only in the sense that individuals—fauna or artifacts—are far apart, but the *presence* of the environment itself is overwhelming and absolute. It is a cool, dim, deafening valley, patiently waiting for its final, sonic collapse.
