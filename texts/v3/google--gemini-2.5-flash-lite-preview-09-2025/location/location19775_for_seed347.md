The realm is known as the **Sub-Drip Cascades**, though no one calls it that above the silt line. It exists in the compressed understorey of a vast, ancient aquifer system, deep enough to be insulated from the sun's direct gaze, yet shallowly networked enough to borrow a strange, diffuse illumination.

**Elevation and Light:** We are perhaps fifty feet below what would constitute ground level in a conventional sense. There is no sky here. The *light* is a pervasive, cool luminescence, seemingly drawn up from the saturated silt and the mineral content of the water itself. It’s the color of aged bone china, a constant, slightly greenish-white that casts no hard shadows, creating the illusion of perpetual, overcast midday.

**Terrain and Water:** The core characteristic of the Sub-Drip Cascades is the **water (2.5/3)**. It is not a river, nor a lake, but a state of being. The entire substrate is hydro-saturated, a viscous, near-constant state of weeping. Where the terrain *rises*—which is rare and temporary—it is formed of freshly deposited tufa and chalky calc-sinter, lending the **erosion (1.2/3)** a sharp, almost brittle quality to these fledgling structures. These formations only last until the hydrostatic pressure reclaims them. Everything is continually, slowly dissolving and reforming in the omnipresent moisture. Rain is not something that falls here; it’s something that accumulates, weeping through the overhead mineral ceiling in countless, fat, slow drops that sound like heavy glass beads striking soft clay.

**Vegetation and Density (2.2/3 & 3.0/3):** The **lushness (2.2/3)** is not derived from photosynthesis in the typical sense, but from chemo-synthesis and hydro-trophism. The walls, floors, and any emergent structure are choked with life. This is where the **density (3.0/3)** becomes visceral. There is no vacant space. Fungal mats, thick as felted wool, coat the damp surfaces, glowing faintly with bioluminescence drawn from deep-earth minerals. Root-like structures, pale and dense, interlock into masses that function as load-bearing walls. Cascades of pale, ribbon-like aquatic mosses drape down, their tips perpetually held within a slick film of water. The air, thick with moisture, feels heavy and resistant to movement.

**The Built Environment (2.3/3):** Despite the lack of technology (**0.1/3**), this is profoundly **urban (2.3/3)**. Not built *of* stone, but *shaped* by habitation. These are not primitive caves; they are the compacted, engineered substrata of something vast and unseen above. Channels have been worn, smoothed, and widened by generations of passage. There are tiered pathways, slick with green algae, that spiral up miniature plateaus. Sections of the ceiling have been deliberately hollowed out—not carved, but allowed to erode along specific crystalline fault lines, creating resonant, vaulted chambers connected by narrow, tightly packed corridors where bodies must brush past walls saturated with slow runoff. It feels like walking through the plumbing and drainage of a colossal, sleeping machine.

**Atmosphere and Sound (0.4/3 & 0.3/3):** The **temperature (0.4/3)** is glacial, a persistent seep of cold deeper than the ground layer allows for warming. The air is still, heavy, and profoundly quiet (**0.3/3**). The primary sound is water—the drumming of condensation overhead, the thick *shluck* of boots sinking momentarily into the silted ground, and the rhythmic, almost mechanical *thrum* of the slow, pressurized flow in the narrower channels. There is no wind, only the subtle displacement caused by movement.

**Fauna (2.0/3) and Scale (2.2/3):** The **fauna (2.0/3)** is abundant but adapted to the near-dark and the cold. Pale, segmented scavengers glide over the algal mats, their carapaces slick and chitinous. Blind, white fish navigate the saturated troughs, feeling the vibrations of larger creatures. The **scale (2.2/3)** is regional; one can walk for hours beneath the weeping canopy, passing through sectors distinguished only by the hue of the mineral deposition—a stretch of iron-red tufa giving way abruptly to a cavern dominated by silica sludge. The density of the structures implies that whatever built these pathways—or used them—is still present, perhaps hiding deeper within the water-logged infrastructure.

**The Rules of the World:** Water seeks its lowest stable pressure points, constantly carving new, temporary routes through the soft, packed earth and calcified growth. Life here feeds on the minerals, the heat gradients escaping from below, and the organic detritus slumping down from the world above. The stability is provisional; a sudden surge in the subterranean flow could collapse an entire thoroughfare, creating a "fresh" landscape overnight, only for the density to immediately begin reclaiming the void. The air itself seems to be undergoing a constant, slow exchange with the water, making every breath cool, metallic, and heavy.
