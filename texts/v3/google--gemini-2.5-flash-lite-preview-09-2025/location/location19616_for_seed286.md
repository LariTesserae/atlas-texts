The world is the **Arboreal Abyss**, a high-elevation landscape where the sheer volume of living matter has become the defining physical state, saturated to the point of oceanic immersion.

### Terrain and Substrate: The Great Moss Mantle

There is no rock, no soil visible beneath the surface, only the **Great Moss Mantle**. This is not merely a layer of moss; it is a continental-scale, self-supporting biostructure built up over eons. The vegetation parameter (2.9/3) is expressed here as **pure-plant ecosystem** that *is* the substrate.

This Mantle rises thousands of meters from unseen depths—perhaps a drowned core world, or perhaps it has grown so vast it defies conventional elevation metrics. We are situated near the **High Elevation** (2.0/3), meaning we are very far up the side of this colossal, living edifice.

The Mantle itself is composed of stratified layers of compressed, specialized photosynthetic mass. The lowest, deepest layers are petrified by the sheer pressure and age, forming structures akin to sedimentary rock, though entirely organic—a lignified, springy stone laced with slow-moving mycelial conduits.

### Water and Atmosphere: The Cool Submersion

The water parameter (2.8/3) screams **submerged**. This is not rain or an ocean poured *over* the landscape; the entire elevated plateau is perpetually steeped in a dense, cool **bio-fluid**.

This fluid is not pure water, but a thick, mineral-rich nutrient slurry extruded by the deeper layers of the Mantle. It is viscous, cool (0.7/3), and carries the metallic tang of slow, sustained chemical processing. Visibility is extremely low, perhaps ten meters at best, even under the **normal light** (2.1/3) filtering down from above. The light penetrates not as sunlight, but as a diffuse, greenish bioluminescence originating from the vast, suspended flora high above.

The **scale** (2.7/3) of this submergence means that local weather systems are not driven by wind friction, but by slow, pressurized currents within the nutrient sea. Vast, slow-moving eddies define the local geography, occasionally shifting the distribution of floating biological debris.

### Structure and Density: The Industrial Phloem

Where the Mantle becomes thin enough for pockets of gaseous space to exist (indicated by the low **density**, 1.4/3), those spaces are immediately occupied by the strangest elements: the remnants of **industrial technology** (2.3/3).

Because the environment actively resists conventional construction (no built structures), the technology has integrated itself into the biomass. Massive, slow-pulsing **Siphon Engines**—ancient, continent-sized pumping mechanisms—dominate the soundscape. These engines are not separate artifacts; they are slow-growing, mineral-encrusted fungal agglomerations that have incorporated corroded metal alloys from a previous era. They function as massive bio-converters, processing the slurry, driving the circulation, and generating the necessary electrochemical gradients to keep the entire ecosystem stabilized.

The sparse density applies to the *gaps* between these industrial-biological nodes. You might float for a half-kilometer through the uniform, cool, green-grey murk before encountering a genuinely active zone—a cluster of Siphon Engines, or a thriving zone of **abundant fauna**.

### Ecology and Sound: The Mechanical Chorus

The **fauna** (1.6/3) consists primarily of slow-moving, amorphous filter-feeders—pale, gelatinous forms that drift through the slurry, grazing on the microscopic organisms suspended within it. They move with sluggish, hydraulic grace, their internal bioluminescent organs flickering weakly in the gloom.

The **sound** (2.1/3) is overwhelmingly **loud**, but low-frequency. It is the constant, profound *thrum* of the Arboreal Abyss operating at peak efficiency:

1.  The deep, resonant bass of the Siphon Engines driving immense volumes of fluid.
2.  The creak and groan of the ancient, compressed biomass shifting under high-pressure currents.
3.  The strange, high-pitched *whine* generated by the electrical discharges within the vascular systems of the largest, highest-reaching photo-structures.

This is the sound of a world perpetually working, perpetually processing.

### Atmosphere of Stasis and Strangeness

The **erosion** (1.9/3) is **established**, meaning the current state has been achieved after vast geological time. Everything here is settled into a functional, if deeply bizarre, equilibrium. There are no storms washing the landscape (the water is too viscous for that), and the light levels are constant.

This leads directly to the most peculiar aspect: the **dynamic** parameter is 0.0/3—**never changing**.

The Arboreal Abyss exists in a state of frozen equilibrium. The industrial processes running through the biomass are perfectly balanced: the chemical output equals the chemical demand; the fluid dynamics are locked into a stable gyre; the light levels are constant because the photosynthetic structures have reached maximum harvest efficiency. There is no decay, no growth, only sustained function.

The **weirdness** (1.6/3) manifests in the utter predictability of this dynamic stasis. Life here is a gigantic, self-repairing, slowly pulsating machine. A diver drifting through this cool, pressurized submersion, illuminated by the soft, sickly luminescence filtering down through the colossal, liquid-soaked vegetation, would feel an absolute certainty: this configuration of plant, water, and machine has existed, and will exist, for whatever measure of time the Abyss deems appropriate. Nothing here is built, yet everything is engineered.
