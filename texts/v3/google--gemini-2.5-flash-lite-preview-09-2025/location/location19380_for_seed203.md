The world presses down from above, not with the crushing weight of deep stone, but with a shimmering, oppressive brightness that demands acknowledgement. This is the **Under-Canopy Abyss**, a space defined by its sheer, overwhelming *vegetal* density.

### Terrain and Substrate: The Living Bulk

The primary terrain is not earth or rock, but **Mycelial Root-Mat**. The *vegetation* parameter (2.7/3) is taken to its extreme: the landscape *is* plant life, a vast, continental-scale ecosystem whose lowest levels are only marginally permeable by light. We are deep below a surface world—the *elevation* is low (0.2/3)—but the space itself feels vast (2.8/3) because the defining feature is the sheer *volume* occupied by organic matter.

Imagine descending into a cavity, not carved by water or fault lines, but grown open—a colossal, inverted dome where the ceiling is the surface world, hundreds of kilometers above, and the floor is the oldest, densest accumulation of biological refuse and slow growth.

The ground where one stands is springy, a spongy, semi-transparent bio-polymer woven from fungal hyphae and the aerial roots of massive, upward-reaching flora. Because the *erosion* is young (1.1/3), features are sharp vertically, but the substrate itself is soft, yielding significantly underfoot, like walking across the upper layer of a peat bog that has been compressed into a slow-moving gel.

### Atmosphere and Light: The Cold Brilliance

The air is heavy, saturated, and distinctly **cold** (0.2/3). This coldness is not the dry frost of altitude, but the chill of deep, trapped moisture and limited thermal exchange with the outside world. The *water* content (0.9/3) manifests as perpetual, clinging humidity. Condensation drips constantly from the aerial root structures above, making the air taste metallic and sharp.

The *light* (2.9/3) is the great paradox. It is fiercely bright, yet diffused. This light does not stream in; it is *generated* or intensely focused from above. Giant, bioluminescent structures—perhaps the lower tissues of the macro-flora—glow with a sterile, cold blue-white light that seems to originate from inside the organic bulk itself. It casts razor-sharp shadows in the relatively *empty* (0.5/3) spaces between the major structures, creating pools of intense illumination interspersed with near-absolute organic shadow.

### Ecology: Pure Plant Kingdom

This ecosystem is almost purely autotrophic. *Fauna* is rare (1.4/3), limited perhaps to slow-moving, chitinous grazers that harvest the specialized lichen-forms growing on the root-mass, or microscopic organisms that digest the condensation runoff. Larger animal life is an anomaly, perhaps a misplaced traveler from the surface, struggling in the density.

The *vegetation* dictates all action. Instead of trees and soil, there are **Pillar-Stems**: massive, fibrous columns that ascend towards the unseen surface, kilometers in diameter, their surfaces layered with nutrient-gathering membranes. The spaces between these stems are the 'rooms' of this below-surface world.

These Pillar-Stems occasionally weep viscous sap—the intermittent moisture. In the vast, echoing gaps, one can find 'ponds' of accumulated condensation, thick due to dissolved minerals filtered through the upper layers.

### Dynamics and Sound: Whispering Turbulence

The environment is fundamentally *turbulent* (2.6/3), but the turbulence is internal to the plant structure. It manifests as slow, powerful shifts in the supporting mat, the massive, almost imperceptible growth spurts of the Pillar-Stems, or the sudden, explosive release of pressurized, oxygen-rich gases trapped within the decaying organic substrate below. These shifts cause the heavy, woven environment to groan—a sound that registers as a deep, sub-audible vibration rather than a typical noise pollution level (1.0/3 sound).

When a structural shift occurs, the high-density air in the vast, empty pockets reacts violently, creating localized, silent **pressure fronts** that sweep through the canyons between the Stems. You feel them as a sudden, cold wash against your skin, distorting the ambient bright light into momentary, rippling waves.

### Remnants and Rules: Primitive Marks in the Vastness

The *built* environment (0.9/3) consists of scattered, enigmatic constructions. These are not ruins of stone, but hardened nodes of bio-engineered material—perhaps ancient sheaths or protective cocoons abandoned by the ecosystem itself centuries ago, now fossilized into crystalline structures resistant to the decay. They are small, often just single-chambered nooks carved or grown into the sides of the Pillar-Stems, bearing faint, geometric etchings—the only evidence of a species that understood manual *primitive* techniques (1.1/3) but built nothing that competed with the plant’s sheer scale. They are like tool-marks on a living god.

**Rules of the World:**

1.  **The Wet Path:** Any route not leading towards or away from a major condensation drip is unstable. Standing too long in the dry, spongy center of a hollow invites being slowly enveloped by reasserting root-growth.
2.  **Light Consumption:** The light is constant but seemingly hungry. Tools or exposed metals (if they existed) would rapidly oxidize or be overgrown by specialized, light-harvesting molds within hours.
3.  **Vertical Reference:** Due to the low elevation and the immense scale, there is no true horizon. Up is defined by where the light seems most focused and cold; down is where the density increases and the light fades into an absolute black, save for the occasional flicker of internal geothermal vents deep in the unseen substrate.

It is a landscape of smothering, cold growth, a vast, pressurized aquarium where the water is replaced by a dizzying, living architecture, all illuminated by a stark, unmoving celestial glare originating from within the very fabric of the world above. It is lonely, cold, and perpetually on the verge of a silent, organic implosion or reconstruction.
