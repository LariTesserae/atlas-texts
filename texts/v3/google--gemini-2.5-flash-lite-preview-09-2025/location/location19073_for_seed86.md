This is the **Under-Veil Depths**, a realm defined by its subterranean origin and a peculiar, constant interaction with the air above.

### Terrain and Structure

The environment is vast and spatially open—a scale that suggests a massive, low-lying cavern system or a deep, wide rift, yet its elevation is definitively *below* the surface. The ceiling, though rarely seen, seems miles above, implied by the way the light filters down, more as a diffuse presence than a direct source.

The terrain itself is aggressively **young** in its features. Sharp edges rule. This isn't the smoothed, ancient bedrock of deep caves; the ground is a chaotic arrangement of newly fractured shale, glistening veins of mineral deposits that look like fresh scars, and piles of angular scree. Gravity seems to have only recently begun its work here, or perhaps the fracturing agent is not purely tectonic, but something else driving rapid, recent emergence. Large slabs of rock lie at precarious angles, occasionally forming narrow, slick corridors or sudden, unexpected drops into deeper, unseen fissures.

The material composition is predominantly dark, porous basalt interwoven with pale, chalky strata that absorb ambient moisture readily.

### Water and Atmosphere

The **dampness is persistent but not drowning**. Water manifests primarily as a veil—a near-constant, fine condensation that beads on every angled surface. It’s not rain, but rather the slow, steady seepage and outgassing of the subterranean environment meeting a slightly cooler atmosphere. Rivers are rare; instead, there are **seepage tracks**—shallow, intermittent flows that follow the lowest contours, moving sluggishly over the sharp scree before pooling in slight depressions, creating temporary, shallow mirrors that distort the dim overhead glow. These pockets of standing water are crucial, catching the slow drips and providing the only true reflective surfaces in the gloom.

The atmosphere is **cool**—a steady, chill temperature that makes moisture hang heavy in the air without fully condensing into fog. Breathing feels thick, slightly gritty with disturbed mineral dust.

### Light and Sound

**Light is consistently dim.** It doesn't pulse or shift, suggesting a geological feature—perhaps a wide, shallow opening to the surface miles away, or a massive, highly filtered light source filtering through immense thicknesses of rock and water vapor above. It casts no hard shadows, only gradients of grey and deep indigo, making the jagged terrain treacherous to judge by depth alone.

The prevailing characteristic of the Under-Veil is its **loudness**, despite the sparse density of its features. This sound isn't manufactured; it is the constant, overwhelming *cacophony of the fauna*.

### Ecology and Fauna (The Overwhelming Presence)

With vegetation being sparse—small, pallid lichen patches clinging desperately to sheltered rock faces, or thin, hair-like growths drawing sustenance from mineral runoff—the biomass of this ecosystem is almost entirely animal.

The **fauna is overwhelming**. They are everywhere, yet due to the sparse density of *structure*, they are spread across a vast area. The noise comes from billions of small, chitinous bodies moving across the sharp stone. Imagine the sound of a dried husk being dragged across sandpaper, multiplied to the scale of an ocean surge.

These creatures are adapted to the cool, damp, dim conditions. They are likely blind or rely on vibration and chemoreception. They are the primary agents of the *young erosion*; they don't build (the built parameter is zero), but their constant, low-level foraging, tunnelling, and scraping against the fresh stone keeps the surfaces fragmented and prevents any long-term equilibrium from setting in. They are the ecosystem’s engine, constantly churning the scree and releasing the moisture that nourishes the sparse lichen.

### Dynamics and Rules

The world is startlingly **stable in its dynamic**. It is an arrested state of noisy, damp chaos. The rules that govern this place favor high metabolism and rapid surface renewal over long-term geological smoothing.

1.  **The Rule of Grind:** No surface remains pristine for long. Anything that stops moving or growing rapidly becomes a substrate for the microbial/faunal film, which then accelerates its disintegration.
2.  **Moisture Persistence:** Water never truly leaves the system; it only changes state or location within the vast, cool volume, seeping downward or condensing upward. This ensures the constant dampness required by the primary life forms.
3.  **No Sanctuary:** Because the scale is open and the fauna is overwhelming, there are no truly safe, silent, or uncolonized pockets. Even the deepest fissures host some form of crawling, clicking scavenger.

The overall feeling is one of being perpetually observed by a teeming, clicking darkness, beneath a sky of filtered grey, standing on ground that seems newly ripped open by some unheard, massive upheaval happening just beyond perception. It is a loud, cool, damp, and perpetually fractured under-realm.
