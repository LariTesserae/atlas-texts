The Ironwood Cysts are not a city in the conventional sense, nor a landscape one might look upon from a distance and immediately comprehend. They exist at the scale of a very large, densely packed forest clearing, perhaps the floor of a colossal, ruined dome, where the boundaries are marked not by walls or horizon, but by the sheer, oppressive verticality of the plant life. This is a place defined by *infill*.

**Terrain and Substrate:**

The ground level is a thick, springy, perpetually damp mat. Given the **vegetation (2.6/3)** parameter, the substrate *is* the organism. It’s composed of compressed, self-regenerating fungal mycelia woven through the fibrous, dead-but-living roots of the dominant flora: the Ironwood. This material offers a muted resistance underfoot, absorbing sound and radiating a low, mineral coolness (fitting the **temperature 0.8/3**). There are no visible hills or valleys; the terrain is an even, low plain (**elevation 1.1/3**), disrupted only by the structures emerging from it.

**Vegetation: The Ironwood Architecture:**

The defining feature is the Ironwood. These are not trees in the classic sense, but slow-growing, semi-mineralized biological processors. Their trunks are dense, dark, and incredibly slow to decay, giving the place its established baseline (**erosion 2.5/3**). Instead of branching out widely, they grow rigidly vertical, their lower sections fused into massive, irregular hexagonal pillars.

These pillars are the **built (2.0/3)** element. Over centuries, generations of specialized mosses, mineral-secreting lichens, and engineered fungal grafts link these Ironwood shafts together at various heights, forming living, load-bearing walls, narrow vaulted walkways, and cavernous, hollowed chambers—the Cysts themselves. The structures are layered, seemingly organic machinery grown around the foundational trees.

The density of the plant life is total where the architecture is formed, yet the overall space feels **sparse (0.9/3)** because the architecture is highly articulated, leaving vast, cathedral-like voids between the interconnected pillars.

**Atmosphere and Moisture:**

The environment is perpetually **damp (0.9/3)**. The air in the lower Cysts is heavy, smelling of wet earth, ozone, and warm metal. Sunlight, categorized as **normal light (1.6/3)**, struggles to penetrate the upper canopy layer—a dense, interlocking mesh of tough, black photosynthetic membranes that function as a roof. This canopy drips constantly; the intermittent moisture isn't rain from the sky, but condensation harvested from the slightly warmer air above the structure, channeled down through specialized capillary root systems hanging within the voids. The soundscape is dominated by the *drip, drip, drip*—a pervasive, quiet rhythm.

**The Industrial Intrusion (The Tech):**

This built ecosystem is not purely natural. The Ironwood Cysts serve as processing centers. Embedded within the living architecture, particularly at junctions and along the main vertical support shafts, are components of ancient or forgotten **industrial (1.9/3)** processes. These manifest as tarnished, bronze-colored ventilation ducts that snake through the woody matrix, or as low, resonant humming nodes embedded deep within the root floor.

These nodes operate on captured kinetic energy—the flexing of the Ironwood trunks under minuscule tectonic shifts, or the slow, pressurized movement of water through the internal capillaries. Occasionally, a vent hisses, releasing a plume of warm, sterile air, proof that the infrastructure is still maintained, if not actively managed.

**Ecology and Dynamics:**

Fauna is **rare (0.7/3)**. The Cysts are largely sterile due to the highly competitive nature of the plant/fungal matrix and the low light penetration at the floor level. When life does appear, it is highly specialized: pale, sightless detritivores that chew through dead mycelial mats, or occasionally, a dart of something iridescent—a pollinator drawn to the faint, oily luminescence of the junction points where the biological structure interfaces with the metallic components.

The place is **active (2.4/3)**, but the processes are slow and enormous. This activity is less about hunting and more about maintenance. The walls slowly thicken, the internal pressure fluctuates, and the moisture delivery system adjusts itself based on unseen atmospheric gradients. It is a machine perpetually repairing and refining its own components.

**The Weirdness (1.1/3):**

The unusual aspect manifests in the **Scale/Built** interface. When one stands within a major Cyst chamber—a hollow space roughly the size of a small cathedral—the structural elements (the Ironwood columns) appear to be patterned, not randomly. Their hexagonal facets repeat with mathematical precision. Furthermore, some of the darker, older wood surfaces occasionally exhibit slow, non-repeating pulsations of internal light, like distant, captive stars moving beneath the surface grain. This suggests the architecture itself is not just grown around functions, but that the *growth* is guided by an immense, slow computation embedded within the biological sequencing of the Ironwood spores.

**Summary of the Experience:**

To stand in an Ironwood Cyst is to stand within the intersection of a dense, temperate rainforest and a crumbling, water-powered factory. The air is cool and heavy with water vapor, the light dim and filtered through layers of biological material. You move through vast, damp halls where the walls seem both alive and engineered, smelling the metallic tang of ancient work overlaid with the sharp scent of living chlorophyll. It is quiet, save for the constant, measured drip of condensation landing on the spongy floor, marking time at the pace of wood mineralizing and mechanism humming.
