The landscape is the **Great Glaze**, a vast, warm, low-lying expanse where geological time has settled into a state of near-stasis, punctuated by the insistent presence of moisture.

### Terrain and Structure

This is a world flattened by eons of slow erosion. The elevation is barely perceptible; one travels across an immense, shallow basin that stretches toward a hazy horizon, giving the landscape a **regional scale**. The bedrock here is ancient, a dark, silicate stone that has been polished smooth not by wind or grinding ice, but by persistent, warm water flow—the concept of *erosion* here is less about tearing down and more about perpetual, slow saturation and chemical smoothing.

The terrain is defined by subtle undulations—whaleback rises and shallow, slick depressions. There are no sharp cliffs or dramatic canyons; the overall picture is one of immense, settled flatness, like the bottom of a dried ocean that has recently flooded again.

### Water and Atmosphere (Water: 2.3/3; Temperature: 2.4/3)

The defining characteristic is the **warm wetness**. The temperature hovers in a perpetual, humid warmth, the air thick and heavy, like breathing near a brewing vat.

Water is everywhere, but it is rarely deep. It doesn't rush; it seeps, rests, and condenses. The surface is a tapestry of slow movement:

1.  **The Weeping Surface:** Vast sheets of water, typically only an inch or two deep, sheet across the smoothed bedrock. This water is lukewarm, holding residual heat absorbed from the ground below.
2.  **Condensation Veins:** In the slight depressions, the air is so saturated that heavy dew falls even during the day, collecting in sluggish, slow-moving rivulets that trace lines across the floor. Where the bedrock shows deeper fissures, the water pools into stagnant, milky puddles that reflect the normal, bright daylight.
3.  **Mist Banks:** Near the horizon, where the warm ground meets the slightly cooler, humid air mass, low, rolling banks of thick, pale-green mist hug the ground, obscuring the distance and contributing to the sense of low density and openness.

### Vegetation (1.2/3)

Life here is adapted to saturation and low stability. Vegetation is sparse, clinging where it can find purchase in the thin, mineral-rich silt deposited by the receding water sheets.

There are no towering trees. Instead, the flora consists primarily of:

*   **Slime Molds and Bio-Mats:** Broad, sprawling surfaces of resilient, rubbery blue-green and ochre life that grips the rock where the water lingers longest. These mats form slippery carpets over large expanses.
*   **Anchor Reeds:** In the slightly deeper runnels, stands of tall, thin, almost gelatinous reeds emerge. They are translucent, their stalks perpetually sodden, swaying minimally in the almost imperceptible shifts of ground moisture. They look more like bundles of slow-moving, pale algae tubes than true plants.

The sparse covering prevents the landscape from feeling lush; it feels damply *occupied* rather than green.

### Fauna and Sound (Fauna: 1.6/3; Sound: 2.0/3)

Despite the low visual density of flora, the ecosystem is active, producing a **loud** soundscape dominated by liquid movement.

The noise isn't the crash of waves or the roar of a river; it is the pervasive, encompassing sound of water on stone:

*   **Sizzle and Slurp:** The primary sound is the slow, relentless noise of warm water moving across the highly polished, ancient rock. It’s a constant, low-frequency *shushing* interrupted by the wet, sucking sound of the bio-mats trapping and releasing water.
*   **Faunal Clicks:** The abundant fauna is primarily amphibious or semi-aquatic. Large, flat-shelled creatures, perhaps analogous to colossal, sedentary snails or segmented, mud-burrowers, navigate the slick surfaces. Their movement is slow, but audible: heavy, rhythmic *tacking* sounds as their muscular feet adhere and release from the stone beneath the water film.

In the denser pockets of slime mold, small, quick insect-analogues—buzzing with near-silent wings—dart between the water sheets, adding a high-frequency shimmer to the overall loud wash of noise.

### Dynamics and Rules of the World (Erosion: 2.9/3; Dynamic: 0.8/3)

This is a landscape defined by **primordial settling**. With erosion almost complete, the surface has reached its most stable configuration over vast deep time. Change is excruciatingly slow.

The *rules* here are hydrostatic and mineralogic. The most significant dynamic cycle is the slow process of **Mineral Leaching**. The warm water dissolves the outermost layer of the ancient rock at an atomic level, carrying the dissolved minerals into the shallow pools. When these pools occasionally evaporate—a process taking months or years—they leave behind highly concentrated deposits of crystalline salt or strange, rainbow-sheened minerals that briefly break the uniformity of the dark stone before the next humid cycle washes them away. This is the great, slow geological engine of the Glaze.

The world is essentially untouched by construction or advanced processes (*built* 0.4/3, *tech* 0.0/3). It exists as a slow-motion ecological pan where life is engaged in a constant, warm, wet negotiation with the nearly-perfectly smoothed stone foundation. You walk on the memory of mountains, now rendered into a vast, warm, echoing floor.
