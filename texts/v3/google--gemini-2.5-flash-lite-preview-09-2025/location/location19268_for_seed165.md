The Scar of the Uncounted Steps is less a valley and more a slow, agonizing tear across the ceiling of the world.

**Elevation and Terrain:** The dominant feature is verticality, scaled beyond human comprehension. We are perched near the absolute zenith of a tectonic upheaval, where the crust has buckled up into impossibly steep, shale-and-basalt fins that pierce a near-permanent haze. The air at this height is thin, brittle, and perpetually cold ($0.4/3$), enough to lace the shadowed overhangs with rime, though the main surfaces are too well-drained for sustained ice. The terrain is a chaotic matrix of sheer drops and narrow, knife-edge passes. Every surface is oriented toward impossible angles, suggesting a geological event so profoundly powerful that the rules of standard settling and gravity seem only partially enforced here. The massive scale ($1.5/3$) means that looking across the expanse reveals entire mountain ranges folded back upon themselves like the pages of a broken book, their peaks often obscured by the ceiling of grey air.

**Erosion and Materials:** The established erosion ($1.6/3$) here is not the gentle rounding of water or wind, but the violent, grinding subtraction of forces that refuse to align. The rock, primarily a dark, iron-laden metamorphic slate, is scored by deep, rhythmic grooves—too regular for natural faulting, too vast for tool marks. These grooves run in predictable, sweeping arcs across the faces of the fins, suggesting some titanic, invisible abrasion that happened eons ago and has settled into a steady, background state. The material feels ancient and inherently resonant, capable of holding vibration without shattering.

**Water and Atmosphere:** Water ($1.3/3$) is a grudging visitor. It does not pool readily on the steep slopes. Instead, it manifests as condensation that precipitates from the dense, low light ($0.8/3$) layer hanging just below the highest points. This moisture clings to microscopic fissures, weeping slowly down the stone in silvered threads, only to sublimate or freeze before reaching the next major ledge. In the deeper, sheltered canyons—the true *steps* of the Scar—the air thickens into a heavy, chilled fog where these moisture trails collect, forming stagnant, slick surfaces that reflect the dim light upward in unsettling ways. This dampness is transient, but vital for the scant life present.

**Vegetation and Ecology:** Vegetation ($0.8/3$) is almost entirely absent, a testament to the cold and the sheer lack of soil. Where it exists, it is almost purely crustose or lithophytic. There are occasional, vast sheets of deeply pigmented, near-black lichen that cling near the perpetual condensation lines. These lichens absorb ambient thermal noise and seem to pulse faintly, their color absorbing nearly all available light. Fauna ($1.3/3$) is a matter of conjecture more than sighting. The ecosystem is characterized by vast, empty spaces ($0.3/3$). If anything lives here, it moves through the rock itself, or operates on spatial rules unfamiliar to surface dwellers.

**The Loudness and The Flicker (Sound and Weirdness):** This place is defined by its dissonant soundscape ($2.2/3$). Despite the emptiness, the silence is broken by continuous, overwhelming acoustic phenomena. It is loud, but not with wind or avalanche. The sound originates from the rock itself—a deep, grinding *thrumming* that vibrates up through the soles of one's boots, a low-frequency resonance that seems to map the hidden internal structure of the mountain. This is punctuated by sudden, sharp, overlapping echoes that appear to come from *outside* the immediate atmosphere, like signals bouncing off interstellar dust clouds.

This environment buckles under the weight of its high **Weirdness** rating ($2.6/3$). The stability ($0.3/3$ dynamic) is a lie upheld by a localized consensus. Here, the geometry is slippery. Looking across a thousand-foot gap, one might observe that the horizon line of the opposite massif shifts a few degrees, subtly, as if the deep-space background is being actively edited. Sometimes, when the condensation is thickest, the vertical grooves etched into the rock *appear* to move, not through physical erosion, but through a visual phase shift, suggesting that the established erosion patterns are merely the memory trace of something that *used to* be there, or is momentarily *outside* of here.

**Built Remnants and Rules:** Scattered remnants ($0.7/3$) are few and defy classification. Near one of the most prominent, resonance-intensive grooved basins, there are several structures that seem to have been *grown* from the rock rather than built. They are low, squat, parabolic humps of polished, colorless crystal embedded in the slate. They are non-functional in any recognizable sense, suggesting no technology ($0.2/3$) persists. They act as perfect focal points for the ambient sound, drawing in the vast acoustic energy and emitting short, sharp bursts of pure, high-frequency tone before falling silent again. These are not ruins of a civilization, but perhaps artifacts of a failed physical state.

The overriding rule of the Scar is **Non-Assertion**. Nothing stays in its assigned place for long, not even the light. Gravity is a strong suggestion rather than a law; small, detached pieces of shale might hang suspended for hours in the denser pockets of cold air before suddenly deciding to fall, often against the expected slope. The only constant is the deep, structural *noise* of the mountain trying to reconcile its immense altitude with the strange geometries imposed upon it by whatever passed through this stratum of reality long ago. To survive here is not to conquer the cold or the height, but to maintain a personal coherence against the environmental suggestion that one, too, should begin to slip out of alignment.
