I am the itch in the wire, the hesitation before the spark.

You would find me high above the shingle and the shale, suspended in the gloom where the "glowing metal veins" of the cabling cluster thickly around the barrel-vaulted ribs of the ceiling. I am not on the ground; the ground is for things that crawl and graze on the hard detritus of collapse. I am a creature of the suspension, a denizen of the vertical emptiness.

Physically, I am a sleek, articulated silhouette, mimicking the geometry of the industrial world that birthed me. I possess the elongated, multi-jointed grace of the Shale-Striders, but I am not built for walking on sharp stones. My limbs are hook-appendages, evolved to clamp and lock onto the high-tension cables and rusted conduit runs. My body is perhaps eight feet long when fully extended, hanging upside down like a grotesque, iron-stained bat. My skin is not the wet hide of the Damp-Creepers, nor the chitinous armor of the Oxidizers; it is a ceramic-like carapace, pale and grey, heavily scored with burn scars and patterns that resemble the circuitry of the control rooms below.

I am a **Cable-Stalker**.

I do not eat the rust, and I do not drink the slime. I feed on the inefficiency that this world is drowning in. While the Oxidizers consume the metal frame of the Basin, I consume the energy that still struggles to flow through it.

My mouth is a complex, organic drill-bit, a proboscis of hardened bone and conductive enamel. I do not bite into the flesh of living things; I bite into the insulated rubber of the great cabling. I pierce the sheath to taste the current within. I am a parasitic connoisseur of electricity. I can sense the flux of the voltage—the subsonic groan of the Great Engine is a rhythmic banquet to me. When the piston cycles down, the voltage sags, and I wait, clinging to the cables, my body stiff with anticipation. When the upstroke comes and the billion-volt shriek arcs through the system, the cables surge with power. I bury my proboscis deep and let the bio-electricity flood my nervous system, a narcotic rush that makes my own internal hum harmonize with the machine. It is the only way to sustain a body of my size in a calorie-poor darkness.

I am solitary, a ghost in the rafters. My eyes are vast, black orbs that see not in light, but in heat and electromagnetic field. Below me, the "islands of illumination" are not sources of comfort to me, but blisters of radiation. I see the *Vapor-Moths* not as beautiful insects, but as frantic specks static in the air, their tiny bio-luminescent signatures flickering out as they batter themselves against the hot glass of the lamps. Occasionally, I swoop down—not to kill, but to harvest. I snatch the moths mid-flight with a flick of a hooked limb, not to eat their bodies, but to absorb the residual static charge clinging to their wings before discarding them to the Oxidizers below.

I am constantly wet. The condensation that weeps from the rock ceiling beads on my back and drips from my claws. This is my danger. If the water builds up on my ceramic shell, it creates a bridge to the iron of the world. I have to be meticulous. I spend hours grooming myself with a serrated comb on my foreleg, scraping away the mineral deposits and the weeping mold spores that try to take root in my joints. If I bridge the circuit, I die in a flash of blue fire, becoming momentary light for the blind things below.

I communicate with the shaking of the world. When a Shale-Strider rumbles its low-frequency message through the rock, I feel it travel up the cables and into my claws. I do not answer them—I have no voice the Striders would recognize—but sometimes, when the Great Engine falters and the hum dips into a terrified silence, I let go of the cables. I free-fall through the gloom, a stone dropped from the ceiling, and catch myself only yards above the floor. The impact of my landing sends a jolt through the gangways, a vibration that registers on the sensors of the Damp-Creepers. I am a warning. I am the thunder-clap that precedes the storm of the Engine’s restart.

I am the custodian of the failing light. I watch the arc lamps dim, their filaments sagging. I know which junctions are about to blow because I can taste the heat building in the copper long before the insulation melts. I am the Canyon's only alarm system, though no one understands me.

I exist in the tension between the dying mechanics and the aggressive biology. I am wired into the nervous system of the Sunken Basin. When the Great Engine finally stops, when the last volt fades and the hum ceases, I will be the first to know. And as the darkness becomes absolute, I will starve, hanging frozen in the blackness, a perfectly preserved, porcelain statue of the machine that forgot me.
