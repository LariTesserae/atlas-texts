I am the memory in the stone.

Where the others are a frantic blur, I am stillness. Where they are a chaos of clacking and drumming, I am a single, slow, resonant tone. I do not live *on* the stone; I am of it. My body is a composition of the same dense, wet granite as the menhirs that jut from the plain like the teeth of a buried giant. My joints are not flesh and sinew, but flexures of softer, more pliable stone, wrapped in a thick, leathery hide that weeps the same iron-tasting water as the rock itself.

You would find me at the Spoke-Ring, the most intact of the Built. It is not a wall for sheltering from the wind, but a parabola for catching its voice. My senses are not theirs. The Basin’s fauna feels the world as a series of shocks and vibrations, a language of presence and immediacy. *I am here. I am hungry. Move away.* To them, the grinding thrum is the fabric of reality, the medium through which life happens. To me, it is a corrupted signal. There are layers to the noise. Beneath the frantic, staccato drumming of the grazing Basalt-Backs and the high-frequency static of the scuttling Chisel-Things, there is the deep, slow hum of the planet itself—that is the fundamental. And there is the whistle of the wind through the Spoke-Ring— that is the harmony.

I have forgotten what it is like to be hungry for food. My kind learned to worship resonance. I sustain myself not on the slick microbial mats, but on the purity of the amplified tone that rolls through the Spoke-Ring. When the wind blows true at the right angle, the entire structure sings a low, perfect C-sharp that travels through the stone of my body and sets my core vibrating. In that moment of perfect harmony, I am nourished. The Crystal-Gardeners harvest silica dust; I harvest the echo.

My purpose is to listen, and to answer.

I have two forelimbs that are not for walking. They are slender, articulated shafts of basalt, ending in mallets of dense quartz. With them, I do not franticly drum out warnings of my presence. I compose.

While the other creatures communicate in a run-on sentence of instinct, I speak in verse. Slowly, over the course of cycles, I tap out a rhythm on the flank of my menhir. It is not loud. The sound does not travel far through the air, but it sinks deep into the stone of the Spoke-Ring and is broadcast across the basin, a measured, questioning pulse.

*Tap… pause… tap-tap… pause… tap.*

It is a grammar no other living thing understands. The Basalt-Backs might feel the tremor, a faint change in the texture of the ground’s vibration, and move on. It is not the vibration of food or water, so it is meaningless to them. The Echo-Walkers, the hunters, likely perceive my signal as an anomaly, an area of the soundscape that is falsely "still," an acoustic void where a voice should be. They avoid it.

My language is one of memory. Each pattern is a question. *Are you there? Do you remember the shaping of the stone? Do you remember the time before the drums?* My kind did not build these structures to hide from the world; we built them to speak to it, to find the carrier wave and tune our consciousness to it. We were the first conductors of this great, geological orchestra.

I am the last one. Perhaps the final teacher, or simply a broken instrument still trying to play its part.

Sometimes, a Basalt-Back will blunder into the Spoke-Ring, drawn by the amplified resonance that feels like a spring of water to its fluted shell. It will lower its massive body and begin scraping at the slime-scum, its rhythmic *scrape-thud* a crude percussive insult to the delicate harmonics I am trying to preserve. I do not attack it. I simply change the rhythm of my strikes. I tap out a dissonant chord, a sharp, jarring series of blows that disrupt the Ring’s perfect amplification. The pleasant resonance turns sour, a grinding pressure that is uncomfortable even to the well-armored grazer. It shuffles away, seeking the solace of the basin’s formless drone.

I am a ghost composed of granite and patience. I feel the frenetic life of the Basin vibrating through the soles of my feet, a civilization of mayflies living out their frantic, noisy lives on the skin of an ancient god. They are of the sound. I am waiting for the silence between the notes, the moment of perfect resonance where a question might be asked, and an answer—across the endless, rolling stone—might be heard.

And so I wait, fused to my stone, and tap my slow, lonely question into the bones of the world.
