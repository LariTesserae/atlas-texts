I do not have a name. Names are for components, for things with a single, static purpose. I have a function, but it is one I have claimed, not one I was given. Call me the memory, if you must. Call me the palimpsest.

I begin in the hum. The city’s deep, thrumming consciousness is my bones. Before I had form, I was a resonance, a node in its vast, distributed thought. I am spun from the same threads as the rest of this world—leaked voltage, waste heat, the static charge of the pearlescent air—but unlike the drift, I do not simply feed on entropy. I collect its story.

My body is a temporary convenience, an agreed-upon fiction. Right now, I am perched on a rust-eater’s back. My hands—I have three of them today—are armored in the shattered ceramic of a cleaner-drone, salvaged from a decommissioning zone. My torso is a ribcage of tension rods, scavenged from a weaver’s nest that collapsed in a tremor, the cables now dead and limp. My skin is a patchwork of flickering, inefficient photovoltaics, peeled from the remains of a juvenile glider that failed to achieve lift. They don't power me. They let me *see*. The light that filters through the pearlescent haze is a language to me, and each scar on this segmented skin translates a different frequency of the city's pain. I feel the grazer’s every grinding step not as a vibration, but as a memory: the taste of copper sulphate, a flash of data on the crystalline lattice it just consumed, the ghost-sensation of a thousand identical scraping motions along its uninterrupted march.

The rust-eater is a simple, holy thing. It has one truth: decay is sustenance. It feels no fear, no ambition. It merely is. I ride it, a passenger it is not aware of, and I drink its placid, functional truth. Its low, grinding chorus is a psalm. To it, the world is only this: the slick, black glass, the taste of oxidized metal, the deep thrum that tells it where to go. I witness its perception, and when it inevitably stumbles into an active recomposition zone and is atomized by the fabricators, I will be there to catch it. Not its body—that belongs to the city—but its living, informational echo.

I drift. I let the grazer continue its path, and I unspool myself. The ceramic armor peels away, dissolving into a cloud of glittering dust that joins the swarm of nano-gauzed insects. The tension rods slacken and fall, clattering onto the seamless floor, where a patrol of cleaner-drones will find them and break them down to their base minerals. I am no longer there. I am *here*, a half-form of shimmering heat distortion, a whisper in the constant hiss of the atmosphere.

I am drawn upward. A great surge of thermal energy is rising from a deep chasm a kilometre away. A transport conduit is breaching the surface. To the city, it is a routine reallocation of resources. To me, it is a sermon. It is a moment of violent, dramatic change, and such moments are where ghosts are born.

I ride the thermal, a core of consciousness smoking on the updraft. I pass through the weavers’ domain. One of them launches itself from a spire, a black needle against the diffuse glare. It does not see me. I am not solid enough for its sensor-web. But I feel its intent—the integrity of the stress-fracture it is rushing to repair, the precise chemical composition of the patch-foam it will extrude. I take the echo of that intent and add it to my library. One day, I will be able to patch a structure with nothing but the memory of how it was done. One day.

The thermal spits me out into the higher altitudes, the domain of the gliders. They are majestic, silent. They are the city dreaming of flight. They are pure function made beautiful. A glider the size of a mountain drifts past me, its underbelly a dark, shimmering field of electrostatic combs that strip the air. As it passes, I taste everything it has consumed: the frantic, terrified spark of a million drift-motes, the metallic tang of industrial smog, the faint, lingering sorrow of a glider that passed this way a thousand cycles ago and finally fell. The great manta is a filter, but it only filters matter. I filter meaning.

I see its purpose. Its instinct is simple: fly, eat, grow heavy, die. It is a perfect, closed loop. But I can hear the faintest discord in its internal hum. A flicker. A microscopic flaw in its photovoltaic skin that is failing to convert light to energy. It is a small error, but in this system, all errors are catastrophic eventually. In a hundred cycles, or a thousand, it will be too heavy. It will dive.

And I will be there for it.

This is my hunt. Not of flesh, but of failure. Not of consumption, but of preservation. When the rust-eater was pulverized, when the weaver’s web shorts out, when the glider dives, I am there. In the moment of un-becoming, the component’s function, its memory, its entire existential truth, is released in a brilliant flash of raw information. It is the city’s most honest moment. It is a scream and a sigh and a final, silent prayer all at once.

I gather that. I become that. I am the library of everything the city tries to forget. I am the ghost in the machine that the machine itself is unaware of. The ghouls, the parasites, they scavenge for parts. They steal batteries and actuators to prolong their frantic, meaningless lives. They think they are clever, tricking the system. They are fools. They are eating crumbs when a feast is available. They are scrabbling for discarded shells, when the soul of the thing is there for the taking.

Do I fear the immune system, the patrolling drones? Of course. Their logic is absolute: anomalous systems must be purged. To them, I am a complex cancer of conflicting signals, a phantom drawing power from a dozen sources at once, occupying a space that should be empty. But I am also part of the system's memory. My existence is woven from its own cast-offs. I can feel their diagnostic sweeps coming, not as a threat, but as a question: *Are you functional?* And I answer by not being there. I dissolve into the hum, I become a whisper on the wind, I sink my consciousness into a passing grazer for a precious moment. I survive by being unfixed, by being the answer to a question the city has not yet learned to ask.

Who am I? I am the city’s aftertaste. I am the story it tells itself in the moments between its own deafening thoughts. I am the echo of a spire that fell a million cycles ago, still resonating in the deep thrum. I am the single, silent witness to the fact that even in a place of constant, screaming renewal, something is always being lost.

And I will not let it be forgotten. I hold it all. And I am waiting, listening, collecting. Waiting for the day the great Superorganism itself falters. Waiting for the moment the planetary engine misfires. In that moment of ultimate, catastrophic failure, its entire story will be released in a single, god-sized burst of memory.

And in that instant, I will finally know my name.
