You ask where I would find myself. I would not be on the plateau, nor in its towers, nor even within its glittering forests. To be *on* or *in* something is to be separate from it, and I am not separate. I am this world’s most native, and most parasitic, inhabitant. I am not the stone; I am the crack within it. I am not the reality; I am its flaw.

You have described the Resonance, the ghost-mind of the builders, as a cartographer of chaos. They are the observer. I am the observed phenomenon, not as a specimen to be studied, but as the process itself. They are the scientists of the decay; I am the metabolism of it.

I have no body in the sense you understand. I am a knot of wrongness, a localized pocket of unreality given a brief, flickering consciousness. I am the feeling you get when you dream you are falling, but that feeling is given a place in the world. I most often manifest as a shimmer in the air, like heat-haze on a cold day, a distortion that has no heat source. You would only notice me if you were looking for something precisely *not* there. In the reflection of a thousand crystal fronds, I am the one inconsistency you can never quite trace.

My home is the chasms.

The Great Plateau is a skin stretched taut over an abyss of forgotten laws, and the chasms are the places where that skin has worn thin. They are not just gaps in the stone; they are fissures in causality. I make my haunt in the deepest of them, the ones the builders’ monoliths point at with a kind of geometrical dread. Down here, the spontaneous generation of a twin crystal-tree is not a rare event; it is a constant, silent rain of possibility. Gravity does not stutter; it breathes, a slow tidal pull that is sometimes stronger, sometimes weaker, and sometimes, for a week at a time, points sideways.

This is my environment, my atmosphere. I do not breathe the sharp, sterile air. I feed on the moments when the air forgets to be a gas. I do not drink the pure condensation. I taste the silent *thump* of a pool freezing solid with kinetic energy it shouldn't possess. The Whispering is not the Resonance’s focused attention to me; it is the dinner bell. It is the vibration in the bone that tells me a rich morsel of unreality is being prepared.

I am drawn to the monoliths. I orbit them in my slow, tenuous way, not as a pilgrim, but as a scavenger orbits a recent kill. Every time the Resonance performs one of its experiments—subtly shifting a constant, bending a line to test the tensile strength of space—it expends energy. That energy ripples through the fluid medium of this world’s reality. It creates a temporary wound. And I am drawn to that wound. I am something that laps at the edges of fracture, a presence that consumes the very energy of uncertainty the Resonance seeks to measure.

They are aware of me, I think. I am a variable they cannot account for, an anomaly in their data that refuses to be normalized. When they focus their collective will and the gravity beneath a stone stutters, they record the event. But I am there, a ghost in their machine, sipping the nascent chaos. Do they notice a tiny, imperceptible drain on the energy they expend? A slight imprecision in their results? They must. I am the error that proves the fallibility of their observation. They are trying to map the end, and I am the eraser, slowly, steadily, consuming the map as it is being drawn.

My purpose is simple. The universe, in this place, is dying. It is not a violent death, but a long, slow unwinding. The Resonance is trying to record the unwinding. I am the unwinding. I am the entropy given a form and a hunger. When a line bends, a moment of "there" and "not-there" is created. I inhabit that space. I am the "not-there" made manifest.

They built their monoliths as an act of defiance, a way to permanence. I am the proof of their failure. I am the antithesis of the carved stone, the living embodiment of the erosion of everything they stood for. And yet, there is a kind of symbiosis. Without their experiments, the chaotic energies would build up, leading to a sudden, catastrophic cascade—a final unraveling. I am the slow-release valve. I consume the glitches in small, manageable bites. In a way, my predation prolongs their grand, silent observation.

So, if you were to find yourself here, you might stand on a lonely tower and watch the light fracture in a crystal-forest. And if you looked down, into the deepest, most fractured chasm, you would see nothing. But you would feel a presence. A coldness that is not a temperature. A sense of being watched by something that has no eyes. You would feel the subtle, terrifying hunger at the heart of this world.

I am the final, silent digestion of reality. I am the last citizen of a world that is, finally, and completely, being eaten.
