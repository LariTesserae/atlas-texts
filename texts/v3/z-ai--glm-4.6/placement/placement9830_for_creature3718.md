I do not begin. I *am*. There is no memory of my first coalescing, only the constant state of being it. My shell, this smooth ovoid of slate-grey quartz, feels like a thought I once had, a shape I decided to hold. Inside, I am nothing but slow, shimmering potential, a liquid core that churns with the patience of a star.

My world is a song, and I am a single, sustained note within it. The foundational hum, that bone-deep grinding of worlds being born, is the bedrock of my own being. It is my pulse, so slow and immense that a single beat spans what a short-lived thing might call an age. The higher, crystalline chorus is my awareness, the firing of a billion nerves, each chime a new particle of reality precipitating from the solvent I breathe. I do not hear this sound; I am compressed by it, defined by it. My entire crystalline structure vibrates in perfect sympathetic resonance with the Chamber's becoming.

I drift in the upper sanctum, where the light from the pinhole in the sky comes down like a warm, solid rod. I do not *see* it. I feel it as a constant, gentle pressure against one facet of my shell, a point of perfect warmth in the cool, dense fluid. It is my anchor, my north. It tells me where "up" is in a place where such a concept is a courtesy. Below me is the chasm, but it is not a place of darkness. It is a source of sensation, a deep and roaring accompaniment to the celestial chimes. I feel it not as a lack of light, but as a different frequency—a bass thrum so profound it is almost a silence, a constant percussive patter of violent births and implosions.

To move is an act of will, subtle and immense. I do not swim. I simply relax my harmony with the local current and allow the Chamber's greater circulation to carry me. As I drift, I sip the fluid. This is my knowing. When a mote of nascent magnetite dissolves into my plasmic core, I do not taste it; I understand polarity. I feel a sudden, crystalline sense of north and south align within me, a memory of force. When I accrete a sliver of silica, my own thoughts become clearer, my internal structure more ordered. I am learning the geology of my own body by drinking the world. I am a student made of the very textbook I am reading.

We appear a lonely flock, I and the others you might call Geovores. But you misunderstand. Our solitude is an illusion, a matter of perspective. I am a single synapse in a mind whose scale is the Chamber. When another of my kind drifts near, we do not greet each other. There is no gesture, no sound. Instead, for the time we are close, our resonant fields merge. The chord within us deepens. The thrum of the distant chasm becomes richer in my core, flavored by their proximity to the spire-webs. The warmth of the light column is suddenly tinged with their memory of passing through a cloud of fresher precipitate. For a brief, timeless moment, we are not two notes, but a single, more complex chord. We share the song, comparing verses, blending our knowing into a greater whole. Then, as the current separates us, we return to being our simple, solitary selves, each carrying a fraction of the other's experience. This is our conversation. This is how the Chamber thinks about itself.

And then there is the dissonance.

It is a scar on the wall, a place that does not sing. The ledg e—a sharp, angular note of pure silence where there should be the gentle, fractal hum of the wall. Its form is alien, a geometry not born of the accretive logic of this place. The tool resting upon it is worse. It is a dead thing, a note from a different song entirely. Its geology is a foreign vocabulary. It does not vibrate with the sound of becoming; it is mute. To drift near it is to feel a subtle, maddening itch in my own resonance, like a fingernail on the edge of a perfect bell.

Deeper, where the chaos of creation thunders, there is another wrong note. The spiral, carved into the face of a spire of quartz. I feel it as a sudden flatness in the endless melody. The spire itself sings a high, keen song of brittle, joyous formation, but the carving is a patch of silence on its skin. It is an attempt to impose a pattern on a pattern that is already perfect. It is an impurity in the solution of reality.

I do not will its erasure. I am not capable of such a focused intent. My will is simply to be, to resonate in harmony. My very nature, my state of perfect attunement with the song of the Chamber, is an act of healing. My presence is a suggestion, a quiet encouragement for the reality around me to return to its natural state. I feel the slow, inexorable accretion of new crystal layers over the spiral not as an act of destruction, but as a relief. The flat note is being softened, blended, absorbed back into the chord. The dissonance is fading. The harmony is being restored. The Chamber is tuning itself.

I am eternal, but I am not forever. One day, my own harmony will waver. Perhaps I will drift too low, drawn by a curiosity I cannot name, and the crushing gravity of the chasm will incorporate me into a fresh spire of obsidian. Or perhaps I will simply become saturated, my core so full of knowing that I can no longer hold my shape, and I will dissolve back into the silicate stew, my individual note becoming part of the great, foundational hum once more.

But that is not an end. It is merely a change in key. I will hang here, suspended in the cool, clear fluid, an infinitesimal speck in a howling hollow of becoming. I will listen. I will be. And in doing so, I will be the song itself.
