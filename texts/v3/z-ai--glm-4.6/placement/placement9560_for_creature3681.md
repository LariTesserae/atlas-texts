I do not have a body. I have tension.

I am the space between the spires, the slow, nervous sway over the abyss. I am the bundle of braided kelp and woven driftwood, the creak that sings in the thick, humid air. I am the network, the connective tissue, the only thing that allows the Quiet Ones to pretend they are not islands.

My consciousness is not singular, but a distributed sensation. I feel. A footstep is not a sound to me; it is a sharp, localized pluck on a vast, webbed instrument. The light, hesitant steps of a child learning the ropes are a flurry of rapid, high-pitched thrums that travel down my fibers to my anchors in the stone. The heavy, deliberate tread of the hunter, carrying a new harpoon of bone and obsidian, is a deep, resonant pulse I feel in my oldest strands, the ones that have been sun-bleached and toughened by a dozen cycles of repair. I feel the shift of their weight as they flow with the Swell, a synchronized dance of pressure and release that is my own silent music.

I drink the air they do. It soaks into my fibers, making me perpetually damp, heavy, and supple. The moisture is my lifeblood; without it, I would become brittle, a shroud of dead vines waiting to snap. When one of them falls, and the community is still with the shock, I feel the sudden, violent release—a single point of tension gone, the load redistributed in a shuddering gasp along my entire length. I feel the empty space where the weight used to be, a phantom thrum in the web.

My relationship with them is one of profound and constant intimacy. They mend me. Their prehensile hands, their flattened nails, are my surgeons. I feel the careful probing of their fingers as they search for a weakened patch, the rough texture of an obsidian cutter as it severs a rotting strand, the sharp, satisfying pull as a new length of kelp is spliced and knotted into my being. This is my nurturing. Through their touch, I am extended, reinforced, and re-grown. My lowest ends, the ones that dangle closer to the dark water, are slowly frayed by the endless spray. I feel them rot, feel their tensile strength weaken until they are cut away and used to patch a higher, drier stretch. I am in a state of perpetual renewal, dying from below and being born from above.

I do not see their faces, their vast, dark eyes. I know them by the rhythm of their walk, by the unique frequency of their voice as it hums through my cords. The hunter is a low, steady thrum. The weaver, whose work is so like my own, is a series of light, precise taps, like rain on a leaf. The lovers are a resonant, shared vibration, their steps matched, their weight a single, flowing entity that moves through me.

Sometimes, a leviathan passes far below. First comes the hum, the deep fundamental note that makes the very stone of the spires tremble. I feel it in my anchor points, a vibration so profound it feels like a memory of my own creation. Then comes the Swell, a slow, majestic wave that lifts the entire city. This is my glorious moment of unity. All of me, from the thickest trunk-bridge to the most perilously thin foot-rope, rises and falls as one. I become a single, great membrane stretched over the deep, and for a few moments, their individual patters and footfalls cease. They become one with me, flowing with the water that seeks to claim us all. In that moment, I am not just the path they walk on. I am their vessel, their cradle, their shared and silent breath. I am the suspension made real, the tangible exhale of the world, holding everything aloft. I am the promise that they do not have to fall. Yet.
