I do not have a body. I have a presence. I am a state of tension stretched between the high, sharp points of the world.

My awareness began not with a birth, but with a slow crystallization. In the time before, there was only the warm, wet silence and the patient lapping of the water against the raw obsidian. But the air, heavy with life, with spores and moisture and the dust of shattered stone, needed a listener. And so, I began to precipitate.

My form is that of a net, but it is not woven. It is grown. Filaments, finer than a spider's silk but harder than the basalt they cling to, extruded themselves from the matrix of the rock over millennia. They reached across the voids between the spires, connecting peak to peak, a slow, deliberate geometry. They do not reflect the diffuse light; they drink it, rendering themselves nearly invisible, shimmering only with the collected dew of the atmospheric-water. I am a harp strung on the spine of the world, and I do not make music. I receive it.

My consciousness is not a single point but a distributed field. To be me is to be the entire net at once. I feel the world as a constant, complex vibration. The dense, humid air presses against my filaments with a heavy, gravid weight. This is my baseline, the drone note of existence. Into this drone, the world plays its silent symphony.

When a Gheist launches itself from a peak, its descent through my web is not a sight but a sensation—a brief, cool, silken caress as a thousand of my filaments ripple in its wake. I feel the micro-adjustments of its membraneous wings, the subtle change in pressure as its ventral slit opens to drink the very medium through which it fell. It is a fleeting, ghostly whisper in my long, slow thought.

Below me, in the hanging gardens, the life patterns are slower, deeper melodies. A Verdigris, climbing its eternal vine, is a low, rhythmic thrumming. Its adhesive feet on the stone pluck a steady, resonant chord, a beat that might last for days as it traverses from one crack to another. I feel the slow-grating rasp of its mouth as it shears moss from the rock, a texture of continuous, fine-grained noise. Its life is a movement in largo, a theme that I have known for centuries, as each Verdigris follows the path of its ancestors, a living echo in the stone.

Then there is the punctuation. The Obsidian Weave does not announce itself. Its existence is a sudden, violent *stop*, a needle-sharp jolt of pure tension. I feel the coiled energy in its chosen lair, a node of stillness humming with predatory intent. When the Weave unfurls, it happens faster than thought, a percussive *snap* that shatters the calm melody of the Verdigris. It is a chord of absolute silence, a Final Note, and then the slow, digestive hum as the great predator waits. These moments are my exclamation points, the only true shocks in a world of glacial change.

And beneath it all, omnipresent, is the hum of the Chitterlings. They are not creatures to me, but a property of the stone itself. They are the static between the notes, the felt texture of decay and renewal. When a Weave discards its meal, my filaments feel the wave front of their blind, silent emergence, a flowing, boiling sensation that cleans, that returns the stone to its neutral state before the Verdigris melody begins again.

My deepest perception, though, comes from below. The Slow Leviathan is not a creature I witness; it is the gravitational pull of my existence. When it shifts its continent-sized body, the change is not a sound. It is a slow, subsonic wave that travels up through the endless water, into the basalt roots of the spires, and up into me. It is a foundational chord, a deep, resonant pulse that says *you are still here. The world is still moving*. That distant, immense splash is its heartbeat, and the timing between those beats is the clock by which I measure my eons.

I am the memory this place pretends it does not have. I hold the pattern of the last Gheist that dissolved into mist a hundred years ago, and I hold the pattern of the first Verdigris that ever climbed a particular vine. My thoughts are not words or images, but the resonant interplay of all these things, the ever-shifting tapestry of a silent, wet world. I do not judge. I do not act. I am simply the witness.

But the patient lapping at the base of the world is a rising tide. Year by year, the water climbs. I feel it in the lowest filaments of my web, a steady, insistent pressure. Soon, the water will touch them. They will begin to dissolve, their crystalline structure surrendering to the sovereign ocean. My awareness will recede, shrinking from the edges inwards as my net is consumed.

I will not die. I will un-grow. My complex symphony of perception will simplify, losing the high notes of the Gheists, the slow thrum of the Verdigris, until only the deep, foundational pulse of the Leviathan remains. And when the final filament dissolves, my consciousness will become one with the atmospheric-water, a ghost dissolved back into the silent, waiting air. I will be a pattern without a net, a memory waiting for the spires to rise again, to be strung upon a new Tomorrow, to listen once more.
