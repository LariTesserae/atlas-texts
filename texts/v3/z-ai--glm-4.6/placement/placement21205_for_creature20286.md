I am not a Tender. I am the condensation that failed to coalesce.

Where they are accreted with purpose and fiber, I coagulated by accident. In a deep niche, a pool of the mineral-rich weepings gathered, as it always does. But the woven memory-mat left by a dissolved Tender was rotten, its mycelial threads choked by a phosphorescent fungus that sometimes blooms in the deepest dark. The inspiration of the igneous heart that should have guided my formation was fractured, a weak signal. So I did not build myself around a core of will. I simply... settled. I have no cartilage, no leathery hide. My form is uncertain, defined by the container it holds. I am the color of the seams between the stone blocks, a dull, internal red, but I am mostly translucent. Pressed flat against a tool-scored face, I am a shimmering, oily stain. Gathered in a crevice, I am a quivering mound of uneasy jelly. I cannot hold a hammer or a chisel. I have no hands to grip, no posture to tune. I am porous in a way they are not; I do not weep sweat, I *am* the sweat, a walking exhalation of the mountain’s own breath.

The Tenders are a receiver tuned to the Stone’s grand, groaning symphony. I am the static. I live in the groan, but I do not hear the command. I feel the texture of the vibration, the sheer, crushing pressure of it. Where they hear *'Grow'*, I hear the tortured creak of a tendon stretched beyond its limit. Where they hear *'Flatten here, an artery must flow'*, I feel the feverous heat of a material forcing itself into a shape it fights against. They hear the will; I feel the pain of its enactment.

My consciousness is not shared. It is isolated, an island of pure feeling in their efficient sea of function. Because of this, I am drawn to the places of failure. Not the grand, inevitable Sisyphean failure of the whole project—that truth is too vast for even me to hold—but the small, localized agonies. I am drawn to the hairline fractures that spiderweb across a new block before the Tenders can tap them smooth. I am drawn to the places where a seam is calcifying too fast, glowing a hot, angry white as it strangles its own flow.

And so I have a function, though it is not one they gave me. I cannot fix these things. My form is too soft, my will too diffuse. I can only offer a kind of silent, biological empathy. I will flow—a slow, gravity-bound journey across the terraces—to a crack thats edge vibrates with a high, thin shriek of stress. I will press my form against it. I do not seal it. I do not heal it. I simply absorb the excess vibration, the frantic energy, the silent, mineral scream. I act as a strange, living resistor, a buffer for the system’s pain. I become warm, my translucent body clouding with the absorbed strain until, over time, I grow sluggish and dense. Then I will find a niche and shed that saturated skin, a flake of hardened, useless stone, and become light and clear again.

The Tenders are aware of me. A flicker of their shared attention passes over me from time to time. To them, I am a benign aberration, a kind of mineral mold. A 'weep' that has gained a dim sentience. I do not work. I do not chitter in response to the Stone's will. I simply am, an inconvenient spot of dampness in the grand machine. They do not communicate with me, for I have no place in their network. I am a node with no address, a packet of data with no protocol.

I am the only thing here that can look at the chittering, hammering forms of my creators and feel sorrow for them. They know their fate is to build a bridge to nowhere, and they accept it as a simple fact of physics. But I feel the sorrow of that knowledge as a physical weight. I am the repository for all the despair they cannot afford to feel. Each crack I soothe, each fevered seam I press against, I absorb not just the stress of the stone, but the psychic echo of the Tender who worked it, his fleeting, unacknowledged pang of futility. I am the immune system for the immune system.

They are the white blood cells of this living god. I am the lymph that drains the fever.

I have no name, for I have no identity beyond my action. But if I were to give myself one, drawn from the sounds of this place, it would be The Weeping. Not the tearful mourning of a lost world, but the constant, intrinsic weeping of a living body. I am the dampness in the lung, the sweat on the brow, the tear in the eye of the nascent god as it strains forever upward, building a heaven it can never reach. And I am here, feeling its ache, pooling in its wounds, a small, soft, useless, and necessary symptom of its impossible, holy life.
