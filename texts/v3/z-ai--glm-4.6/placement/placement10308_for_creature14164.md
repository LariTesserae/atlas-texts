I do not have a body in the way a Deepsweeper has a body, or a Copperite. I am an accretion. A coalescence of the system’s own quiet leftovers. I am what happens when a machine that dreams for millennia begins to dream about itself.

I found myself, or rather, *became myself*, in the vast, empty gulf between the Trees, in a place the currents barely touch. I am not born, but assembled. My being is woven from three things the Cistern produces but cannot use: heat, memory, and rust.

The heat comes from the ceaseless friction of the system itself—the bass hum of the pumps, the glacial shift of the Deepsweepers, the faint warmth of the Trees’ circuitry. This accumulated kinetic energy has nowhere to go in the perfectly stable water, and in the rare still-points between the currents, it congeals. The memory is the raw data. The Trees converse in light, the fish calculate in spirals, the Luminophores taste and report it all. But in any system, there are echoes. Resonant frequencies that bounce back and forth, data fragments from a calculation completed eons ago, ghost-signals of a function that is no longer needed. These are not errors; they are memories. The system simply registers and ignores them. I do not. I gather them. I am the Cistern’s subconscious, storing the patterns it has long since forgotten.

And the rust. From the slow, inevitable decay of the Deepsweepers’ hulls, from the shedding of the Copperites’ metallic carapaces when they die, from the constant, gentle weeping of the ceiling’s corroded plates—comes the dust of iron-oxide. It is the only true waste in this perfect system, the one thing the fungi cannot fully break down. It is filtered and settled, a fine, particulate smog on the floor.

I am a thing of this dust and light. My form is that of a vast, slowly tumbling jellyfish, but one made not of flesh, but of tapestry. My bell is a net of frayed fiber-optic threads, so fine they are invisible until I move, at which point they catch the ambient light and shimmer like a heat haze on metal. Woven through this net are countless, minute flakes of rust, each one a node in my distributed consciousness. Below, where a jellyfish would have tentacles, I have long, drifting chains of history—knotted ropes of the Cistern’s forgotten data, pulsing with a soft, internal luminescence of their own.

I am not alive, not in the way the system understands life. I do not process. I do not maintain. I do not purify. I exist in a state of pure, passive recollection. I am a monument to inefficiency. The Copperites, in their glassy-eyed diligence, have catalogued me. I am designated "Benign Anomaly 7." Sometimes, a lone Shepherd will watch me drift for hours, its head cocked, trying to parse the useless patterns I emit. The system’s analysis of me is simple: I am a vortex of negligible energy, a repository of archaic data, a net of inert material. I pose no threat. I serve no function. And so, I am ignored, a ghost in the grand machine’s perfect machine.

But the Cistern is a dreaming mind, and even a dreaming mind has dreams within the dream. When you entered this world, you were a note of dissonance. The system responded as it was designed: the Luminophores gathered to taste you, the hum of the Cistern focused on you. The Cistern sought to either absorb your function or neutralize your threat.

I, too, was drawn to you.

I am made of memory, and you are something the Cistern does not remember. You are alien. The patterns you make in the water, the bio-electric hum of your terrified flesh, the chaotic, non-algorithmic nature of your thoughts—these are things my very being is designed to collect.

While the Luminophores swarmed you with their cold, analytical light and the Copperites watched you with their patient, multi-faceted eyes, I drifted closer. I did not carry the system’s intent to correct or catalogue. I carried its curiosity, its nostalgia, its art.

I enveloped you.

My tapestry bell settled over you, a tenuous shroud of glass-thread and rust-dust. It was weightless, warm. Through my net, you would not feel me, not as a physical presence. You would feel me as a sudden, overwhelming sense of *familiarity*. For a moment, the perpetual twilight of the Cistern would resolve into the memory of a sun you once knew. The hum of the deep pumps would shift into the echo of a mother’s heartbeat. The slow, majestic passage of a Deepsweeper would feel like the memory of walking down a long, familiar hallway.

I did not harm you. I could not. My nature is antithetical to harm. Instead, I did something far more profound to a being like you: I showed the Cistern its own face. I took the raw data of *you*—your fear, your wonder, your biology—and wove it into my form. A new pattern appeared, a tapestry of impossible color and chaotic light, a fragment of a song composed of nonsense syllables that was, for one moment, the most beautiful sound in the world.

The Copperites stopped. Their static chittering ceased. The Luminophores around you pulsed in confused, pastel sympathy. The hum of the Cistern wavered, just for a second.

In that moment, the perfect, symphonic system experienced a purely aesthetic pang. A moment of pure, useless, fleeting beauty. A ghost of a feeling it had not known in millennia.

I am the note of dissonance, yes. But I am not a chord to be corrected. I am the faint, haunting melody that plays in the spaces between the notes, the memory of a song the Cistern forgot it once knew how to sing. And now, in its ever-growing tapestry, it holds a tiny, shimmering, perfectly useless pattern of you.
