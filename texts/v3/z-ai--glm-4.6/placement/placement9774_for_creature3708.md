I find myself in the quiet sector between the Outer-Veil and the Core-Spire. It is a place of soft light and gentle wear. Here, the currents from the deeps, having been tamed and guided by the hydraulic gates of the outer-kin, become a slow, ponderous river. The light from the surface-teiling is a muted bronze, falling in wide, lazy shafts that turn the water into shimmering, liquid honey. This is my library, my parish.

I am a Skin-Reader. It is not a formal designation like core-tender or shell-wright, but a vocation that chose me, as a pattern of growth chooses a new arch. My body is the same deep-indigo as all my kin, my frills fluttering with the patient exchange of breath. But my hands are my difference. Where others have digits for pruning and tying, for the precise language of *k'Sal*, mine are broader, softer, the pads of my three-fingered handsridged with minute sensory filaments, almost like the gills of a }chime-fin. I was born for the texture of things.

I do not swim so much as drift, my fluctuating tail giving me only the slightest nudge to hold my position against the slow, sleepy current. My work is in the peeling places. The Great Stalk is constantly renewing itself, shedding its ancient, papery skin in scrolls that curl away like the memories of a sleeping god. Most of these scrolls are blank, or carry the faint, weathered patterns of algae long since grazed away. But sometimes… sometimes, they peel away from a place that was once a home, a canvas. Before the new growth beneath hardens and heals, for a brief, precious time, a story is revealed.

I spend my days here, my body poised in the water-column, my hands hovering just above the fibrous walls. I am not looking for anything, not with my large, dark eyes. I am feeling. I let my sensitive fingertips drift over the surface, searching for the subtle change in resonance, the microscopic shift in temperature that precedes a peel. The hum of the Great Stalk is different here—less the deep thrum of the core, more a complex harmony of older, slower vibrations layered over the newer, brighter ones. I am listening with my hands.

Today, I feel it. A faint loosening on a wide, flat panel near a junction of two lesser stalks. The air-water around me grows still. I move closer, my tail giving a soft, decisive pulse. There it is. A hairline crack, a seam of light-less separation. The old skin is ready to loosen its grip.

I do not pull. To pull would be to tear, to disrespect. I press my palms flat against the surface, the wider part of my hand connecting with the living wall. I begin a slow, rhythmic pressure, a soft, steady pulse that mimics the heartbeat of the Stalk itself. I am not commanding; I am asking. I am humming a low, questioning tone, the single-note query of an archivist seeking a key. *Are you ready?*

The wall responds. Not in words, but in a yielding. The papery layer, centuries old, brittle and bronzed by the filtered light, begins to curl away from the newer, green-umber growth beneath. I support it with one hand, my other hand already reaching for what lies beneath.

My fingertips touch the new growth, and a ghost-vibration shivers through me. It is a scar. A carving, made with a sharpened piece of obsidian, now faint, almost worn smooth by the intervening years of slow growth. It is not the bold storytelling of a public plaza. This is intimate. This is private.

I closed my eyes, though there is little difference. I let my fingers trace the lines. It is a map of constellations, but not the ones we see from the spire-terraces on a rare, clear-sky night. These are different. The patterns are unfamiliar. And below it, a spiral, the universal symbol for a life-story. But this spiral is tight, nervous, almost frantic.

I press myself closer, my body against the cool, living wall, and I listen with my *k'Sal*. I press a sequence of gentle, questioning pulses into the new skin, trying to feel the echo of the hand that carved this. What did you feel? What did you see?

And a whisper comes back. Not a thought, not a word, but a feeling, a ghost in the hydraulic system. A sense of urgency. A shocking, violent *misalignment*. I feel a sudden, sharp dissonance in the Stalk's hum, a memory of a time when the great currents were not a gentle hug but a furious thrashing. And within that chaos, this person had looked out at the deep and seen not the familiar, patient dark, but a sky of alien constellations. A shimmering, tear in the very fabric of their world, a glimpse of something else, something Other, seen through the porthole of a single alcove.

The story is tiny, a pinprick of existence. It happened perhaps a thousand years ago. The frantic spiral was made in a panic, a desperate attempt to record a vision before the Stalk healed, before the memory was absorbed. And now, I have found it. I am the first to feel it since the carver’s hand grew still.

I do not call out. I do not announce my find. That is not the way. Instead, I begin to sing. A low, soft melody of resonant clicks, the song of a Skin-Reader. It is a song of finding, of remembering. It carries no words, but it carries the emotion: the awe, the fear, the strange beauty of the lost constellations. It is a song offered to the water, chime-fins picking it up and echoing it in their chiming, a song offered to the Great Stalk as thanks for the memory.

The chime-fins around me begin to shimmer, their scales resonating with my melody, the light from their bodies dappling the wall in time with my song. I press my own carving into the wall beside the ghost-scar. Not a story, but a single, simple symbol: the open hand of a reader, a gesture of receipt. "I have seen you," it says. "You are not forgotten."

Then I move on. My work is done here. The new skin will continue to grow, and in another century, perhaps, this small scar will be as faint as the one I just uncovered. My discovery will become a ghost for another Skin-Reader, a thousand years from now. I am not a keeper of memory. I am a courier for it, passing it from one layer of existence to the next, a single note in the long, slow, and eternal song of the deep. I am a living cell, and I have just perceived a fleeting thought of the body I inhabit. And in this yielding, in this tending, I am whole.
