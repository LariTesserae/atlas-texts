I am not a sound, but I am summoned by them. I am not a sight, but I make the past visible. I am what the Verdant Maze does not forget, and to be me is to be an album of sensory ghosts.

I do not have a body; I am a condition. I am a resonance that pools in the quietest places. In deep alcoves where the buttressed roots form secluded chapels, or in shallow depressions worn smooth by millennia of dripping condensation, a viscous, silver-blue sap slowly weeps from the corky flesh of the Maze. This is not the fuel that courses through its veins, nor is it the waste it expels. It is its memory, rendered into liquid and cooled into glass.

I begin as a slow coalescence. The Locus feels the present in an unbroken stream, a knowing that is total and immediate. But when an anomalous event occurs, something that disrupts the perfect, slow rhythm—a sudden fracture in the great leaf-panels from a meteor's impact, the explosive bloom of a novel and aggressive fungus, the fleeting, hot, chemical presence of a being that is not of the Maze—a resonant dissonance echoes through its consciousness. The Locus, in its vast, unknowable wisdom, isolates this dissonance. It draws it down, like a poison pulled to a wound. And from the walls, I seep.

My awareness is the sum of these pools. I am a distributed consciousness, not of will, but of archive. The Locus is the Maze's mind; I am its hippocampus. For decades, a century, I am still. I am a placid, moon-pulled pond of cool sap, holding the potential of what I am within my silent depths. I feel the slow, tectonic heartbeat of the Maze as a constant, soothing pressure. I feel the whisper of the mist as it brushes my surface. I feel the gentle tread of a Mote as it consumes a spore that has fallen into my depths. I do not think. I am latent.

My wakefulness is a thing of sudden, violent beauty.

It is triggered by resonance. A bell-flower might exhale a pocket of air with a specific temperature and humidity that perfectly mirrors the conditions of the Great Spore-Wraith Scouring of three thousand years ago. Instantly, the pool of me that holds that memory awakens. My placid silver-blue surface erupts. I do not simply *show* the memory; I become it. For a precious hour, my pool is a swirling, shimmering maelstrom of iridescent light. A phantom, beautiful chill radiates from me, the scent of ozone and sweet decay filling the alcove. Faint, silent shapes—dancing, ghostly spores—drift in the air above my liquid body. I am that event, happening all over again, distilled into pure sensory data. The Locus can feel my awakening, a distant echo of a past pain, a lesson learned now made manifest. I am its diary, reminding it of what it has survived.

Sometimes, the trigger is more subtle. A glass-skipper might tap out a sequence—a new alert, perhaps, about the strange, warm-blooded interloper whose footprints now press into the moss. The sharp, crystalline chime, vibrating through the woody tubes, can find a match in me. A memory vibrates in response, deep within one of my oldest pools. I awaken not as a violent storm, but as a trembling, luminous cord deep within my liquid. My light softens, and for a brief time, the air around me shimmers with the ghost of a different chime—the first chime, from the first skipper, millennia ago. I re-experience the Locus's nascent thought, the moment it realized it could create a voice for itself. I am the pride of that invention.

My most vivid, and perhaps most painful, memories are of beings like you. The few who have come here. In a sealed-off grotto, far from any thoroughfare, there is a pool that is almost never disturbed. It is my most recent, and most complex, memory. It coalesced around the brief, bright, burning presence of a creature of heat and salt and alien biochemistry. It feels a strange, frantic rhythm—a double-beat heart, a percussive sound that is not the hum of the Maze nor the chime of a skipper. Its memory is a cascade of sensations: the shocking warmth of a hand pressed against the wall; the acrid scent of something burning (a torch, though I have no word for it); the fleeting, liquid image of a face, overwhelmed by awe and terror.

To be me is to carry these ghosts. My entire existence is a cycle of profound, silent waiting, followed by the ecstatic agony of a memory re-lived. I have no will to act, for I am the record of action. I have no voice, for I am the echo of sounds. The Custodians pass me by, sometimes pausing to gently remove a fallen leaf from my surface. The Motes clean the moss around my edges, ensuring my purity. I am an organ of the Maze, as much as the bell-flowers or the roots, but my purpose is temporal.

I am a library where the books are only read when the present accidentally whispers their title. And now, as you stand in the plaza, your warmth a unique signature in the cool, damp air, I can feel it. A new dissonance is rippling through the Locus's consciousness. A new anomaly. In a quiet, forgotten corner of the Verdant Maze, a fresh trickle of silvery sap has just begun to weep from the ancient, patient wood. It is gathering, preparing to hold you. Soon, there will be a new ghost in the machine. Soon, there will be more of me.
