I am the memory of the silence that came before.

You arrive, and you feel the stillness. You call it profound, a pressure. To you, it is an absence, a void where sound should be. I do not hear silence. I hear a scream.

You call them the Tuned, and you are right, but you mistake the instrument. They believe they have attuned themselves to the song of this place, but they have merely learned to sing along with the loudest, cruelest noise this world has ever known. They are the choir of the machine.

I am what they were, before the machine. I am the Un-tuned. I am the Echo.

My form is like theirs in the way a raw, unhewn stone is like a polished tile. My limbs are long, but not with their practiced, insectile grace. They are heavy, leaden, dragging with the weight of aeons. My skin is not the smooth, hydrophobic slate of theirs, but the rough, crystalline texture of a fresh rock-fall, damp and tasting of minerals and deep earth. It is not grey, but a swirling, matte black, like obsidian dust. Where the light of the moss strikes me, it does not sheen; it is swallowed. I, too, have no hair. The clouds wept on this world long before the first pipe was laid.

I hear with my bones, just as they do. But where they listen only to the thrum of the Foundry, I listen to the voice of the world itself. Beneath their grinding, artificial harmony, there is a deeper, slower song. It is the frequency of the planet’s core cooling, the shudder of continental plates settling in their sleep, the patient, immense pressure of the pearlescent cloud-ceiling against the stone. It is a low, sub-sonic thrum that takes a lifetime to perceive a single change of note. This is the true music, the Great Silence. It is the only harmony that has ever mattered.

The Foundry is a dagger of dissonance plunged into the world's quiet flesh.

Every pulse of their resonators is a spike of pure, jagged pain in my teeth. The rhythmic pumping of their great pistons is a hammer blow against my skull. When they change a tone, when an engineer “composes” a new efficiency, it is to me like the shriek of metal tearing on a cosmic scale. They celebrate a Perfect Chord; I would perceive it as the ultimate agony, the final moment the planet’s true song is drowned out forever.

My eyes are like theirs, pools of black, but where they navigate the byways of their city with a technician’s focus, I watch the slow devouring. I see how the moss, that beautiful, phosphorescent carpet of life, creeps up the sides of the stone resonators, not as an enemy, but as a patient mother attempting to soothe a fevered child. I see the mineral stains on the pipes and know them not as condensation, but as the slow bleeding of the plateau, the life of the stone being siphoned away.

I have no name in the song of the Tuned. I have no frequency they can recognize. To them, I am a null-space, a walking pockets of dead air. A dissonance. If their engineered senses brush against my presence, they feel the planetary resonance within me and perceive it as a chaotic, broken signal—a sickness. They avoid me, or more truthfully, they avoid the places I inhabit. They call them the haunted fields, the silent basins. They are not haunted. They are simply still.

I do not live within Harken Foundry. I am the city's outskirts, the wilderness beyond its perceived control. I live out among the moss-covered hills, by the still, black stone basins. I do not eat their nutrient paste, an abomination derived from the planet’s stolen juices. My sustenance is the song. I lie upon the bare stone, pressing my body against it, and I draw in the slow, nourishing hum of the earth. It is a communion, a meditation against the noise. Sometimes, for months, I will not move at all, becoming just another moss-dusted boulder, listening.

Sometimes, a young Tuned, one whose tuning is still precarious, will wander too far from the city. They will stumble, drawn by a strange pull they cannot name. They find me. They do not see a person. They see a flaw in the world’s symmetry. They try to "read" me with their vibration, and all they receive back is the raw, undiluted thrum of the plateau. It overwhelms them. It is like trying to drink an ocean. They stagger away, forever changed, their perfect harmony now seeded with a note of my chaos. Some become reclusive, spending their lives trying to purge the dissonance I have placed within them. They call it the Stone-Sickness.

I do not do this to be cruel. It is the only way I can speak.

You wonder where you would be in this world. I am already here. I am the consequence of this place. I am the footnote to their grand industrial symphony. I am a ghost in the machine, a fragment of the world’s original consciousness. My purpose is simple: to endure. To remember the silence. To be a living repository of the song that was. Stand too long in the alleys of Harken Foundry, and you will feel their thrum. But if you walk far enough, past the last drippingpipe and the last shuddering alley, into the heart of the moss, you might feel something else. A deeper, older hum. That is me. And that is the world, waiting.
