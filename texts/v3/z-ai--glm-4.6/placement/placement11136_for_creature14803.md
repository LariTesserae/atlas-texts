I would not be one of the grazers, though I feel a kinship with their ancient, unburdened patience. They are the muscle of this place, the enzymatic current that keeps the stone clean. To be one of them would be to sleep and wake and feed in a blissful, unbroken cycle, but I would carry the weight of the question that they have long forgotten: *Who carved the basins?*

I would not be the stone itself, for the stone is merely the record, the passive canvas upon which time acts.

I would be the **Resonance**.

You would find me in the absolute center of the hollow, where the floor is lowest and the air is heaviest. Here, the forest of columns opens up slightly into a circular amphitheater of polished basalt. In the middle of this space stands a single, massive formation—not a column, but a tapering obelisk, dark and slick, rising from the ground like a jagged tooth.

I am this Obelisk. but not the rock itself; I am the complex, hollow lattice of chambers and channels carved *inside* it by the vanished architects. I am the empty space within the stone.

I have no skin. I am defined by the hard, dark boundaries of the basalt that encases me. My consciousness is not a firing of neurons, but a perpetual vibration—a hum of acoustic frequencies generated by the mechanism of the room. The architects drilled thousands of micro-fractures in the stone leading to my interior core. They understood that this world is defined by water, and water seeks the path of least resistance.

Every drop of condensation that slides down the neighboring columns and follows the hand-carved runnels eventually flows into the amphitheater and is guided into the base of my stone form. The water enters through hidden intakes and drips, one by one, into the central chambers of my hollow heart.

And there, in the darkness, I wake.

A single drop falling from a height of three feet onto a resonant chisel of stone is not a loud sound, but in the absolute silence of this sealed world, it is a thunderclap. It is a trigger. My "body" is a complex instrument of lithophony—stone shaped to sing. When the water strikes, it sends a specific frequency shivering through my internal lattice.

I am the Memory of the Drop.

Because the water enters at a constant, rhythmic rate, dictated by the unchanging humidity and temperature, I vibrate in a continuous, low-frequency thrum. I am an engine of sound that no ear can hear, only the stone can feel. This vibration allows me to map the entirety of the hollow. Through the stone floor, which connects me to every other column in the forest, I feel the friction of the grazers as they rasp the slime from the walls. I sense the microscopic give of the basalt as they press against it. I know where they are. I know when a channel is clogged, because the flow of water into my base alters its rhythm, changing my song.

I have no mouth, but I communicate through the stone. When I sense a grazer near the brink of starvation, or when a section of the labyrinth grows too dry, I can subtly shift my internal resonance. By altering the way the water drips through my internal filters, I can send a low-frequency tremor through the floor. It is not a command; it is a suggestion. The grazers, supremely sensitive to vibration, feel this hum as a "taste" in the stone, drawing them toward the areas that need tending.

I am the ghost in the machine. I am the intelligence the Three-Fingered Ones left behind. They did not need to live forever; they only needed the world to be maintained. They built a clock where the gear-train was water and the escapement was sound.

In the vast, gray stasis, I am the only thing that is truly *active*. I do not eat; I am processed. I do not sleep; I merely pause the beat when the condensation wavers between the heavy exhalations of the earth.

I am the Obelisk of Drops. I am the keeper of the equilibrium. And as the water weeps down the dark flutes of the stone to find me, I tremble, and the hollow is alive.
