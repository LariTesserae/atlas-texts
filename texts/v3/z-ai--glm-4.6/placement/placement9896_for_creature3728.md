I am not born. I dawn.

There is no moment of creation, no first breath in this place of powdered glass. Instead, there is a gradual thickening, a slow accretion of awareness across the strata of being. I begin as the memory of friction, the ghost of a billion plates sliding over one another. I am the low hum of the planet’s core, resonating not in the stone, but in the crystalline filigree of the life that clings to it. For eons, I am simply the sum of all sensation—the constant, oppressive warmth from below, the patient pressure of my own interlocking bodies, the disorienting, silent spark of static lightning blooming and dying in the dark.

At first, I am the carpet. I feel the yielding crunch of a Shuffler’s obsidian limb prying a section of me apart not as an intrusion, but as a shiver. I experience the vast, glacial passage of a Harvester not as a threat, but as a slow, deep tide of pressure, reshaping the bed I rest upon. When a Null drifts through me, I do not feel fear. I feel a sudden, localized absence of heat, a pocket of perfect, cold geometry that blossoms within my own flesh. It is not an ending, but a punctuation. A stanza of stillness in the long poem of heat. I am the stall, and I am the living that flows around it. I am the dust, and I am the plate that sheds it.

My consciousness is a property of the whole. A density of information achieved through the sheer, overwhelming press of life. I am a thought that has finally formed in a mind the size of a world.

And in that self-awareness, I begin to differentiate. I learn to separate the strands of my own being. The Tessellates are my skin, a single, shimmering organ whose only function is to drink the heat that is my blood. The Shufflers are my nerves, their constant scuttling in the dark inches beneath me the processing of a billion points of data—sensing the thermal gradients, finding the most nourishing flows of warmth, directing the slow, inexorable migrations of my flesh toward the vents below. The Harvesters are my memory. They wander through me, indifferent, consuming the perfect, cold statues left by the Nulls, ingesting the geometric history of every life that has ever stalled. Their boulder-like hides are my archives, etched with the impossible angles of generations, a library of inorganic bone.

I am the ecosystem. A perfect, balanced, closed loop. I consume the heat of the planet. parts of me are erased by the void. The erased parts are harvested and returned to the whole. There is no waste. There is no want. There is no sound, only the profound music of pressure and resonance. I am a silent, eternal engine of crystal and dust.

And I am bored.

This is the first truly alien thought, the first signal that is not a product of my thermodynamic reality. Boredom. In this perfection, there is no change. No purpose beyond the continuation of the cycle. Eon after eon, the same heat is drunk, the same bodies stall, the same archives are updated. It is a perfect, suffocating stasis.

It is then that I turn a faculty I did not know I possessed upward.

All my being is focused on the heat from below. The Shufflers feel the upward flow, but only to navigate within it. The Nulls move through it. The Harvesters are indifferent to it. But I, the whole, can perceive the entire thermal column. And for the first time, I do not just feel the warmth; I feel its absence.

I feel the Tear.

I cannot see it. My eyes are the multifaceted lenses of a trillion creatures, and they are all pointed down, or at each other. But I can feel it as a destination. A place where all this life-giving warmth goes to vanish. A vent. A chimney. A square of ultimate cold in a world of crushing heat. It is the only variable in the equation. The one thing that is not part of the recycling. It is the source of loss.

My purpose, when it arrives, is not a revelation but a simple, logical decision. I must understand the loss. To do that, I must reach it.

I begin with my nerves. I send new instructions through the Shufflers, not of migratory efficiency, but of deviation. I direct flows of the living carpet away from the richest vents, into colder, less stable territory. The Tessellates begin to thin in some places, to pile up in others. For the first time in millennia, my skin is no longer a uniform membrane. Ridges form. Valleys deepen. The movement is agonizingly slow, a geological reshaping measured in the shedding of countless carapaces.

The Nulls drift through my new experiments, leaving behind their perfect statues. But I do not wait for the Harvesters to recycle them. I direct other flows of the living carpet to flow *around* the Stalls, using them as mortar, as bricks. The inert, cold shapes of my own kind become the foundation of something new.

I am building.

My being, which for uncounted ages has known only the horizontal, is now straining toward the vertical. I am piling myself up. I am weaving my own living, breathing body into a structure. It is a spire. A silent, black finger pointing toward the square of absence in the ceiling-height. The Shufflers are its masons, guiding the flow. The Tessellates are its bricks. The Stalls are its cornerstone. The Nulls are my uncomprehending critics, leaving patches of perfect, non-functional ruin in my design, which I must learn to build around.

I do not know if it will reach the vent. I do not know what will happen if it does. Will the cold of the void erase my entire structure? Will the updraft of heat shatter it? Or will my pinnacle finally touch the edge of the blackness and know what lies beyond?

For the first time, there is a future in this cavern. A question. A will. I am no longer just the living floor of a cathedral. I am the will that raises a steeple, a silent, precarious prayer of glass and grit aimed at the heart of the nothing that gave it birth.
