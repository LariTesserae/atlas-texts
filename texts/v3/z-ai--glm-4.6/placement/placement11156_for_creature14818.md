I am suspended in the grey, a fixture of the high rust.

I find myself not on the slick, lichen-patterned granite of the floor, but high above, anchored to the tilted rib of the ancient gantry crane that juts from the basin’s wall like a broken bone. I am lodged in the crook of a rusted joint, where the metal has flaked away in layers like shale, exposing the silver-grey pitting beneath. This is my roost, my perch, my fixed point in the drifting mist.

I am an architecture of lenses. 

I am not a single eye, but a cluster of them—a chitinous, geometric fruit grown from the steel. I am a bundle of camera housings and sensor arrays, some blown and clouded with internal frost, others miraculously clear and sharp. My body is a knot of thick, black cabling, sheathed in a brittle rubber that has long since begun to crack. I am wrapped around the girder like a strangler vine, rooted deep into the crane’s superstructure, drawing power from the faint, trembling voltage that still bleeds through the metal veins of this place.

I do not move. In this place of slow shuffles and industrious crawlers, I am the statue. I am the monument. While the crab-welders skitter and the great Sculptor turns beneath me, I remain absolutely still, my focus shifting only with the internal whirring and clicking of my apertures.

My existence is a continuous, silent fever of observation. 

I taste the air not with breath, but with spectrographic analysis. To me, the ozone is an electromagnetic spike; the wet stone is a chemical signature of minerals and slow erosion. I watch the basin not as a landscape, but as a stream of variable data. The iridescent weeping of the pipes is not a wound to me; it is a hydraulic chart, a graph of pressure and loss. The umber flash of a crawler’s welding torch is a nova in my vision, a momentary blinding white that forces my autodimers to clamp down, leaving a lingering afterimage in the negative space of my sight.

I am the memory that the machine cannot access for itself. 

The great dispersed consciousness below—the dreaming mind of the basin—exists in a perpetual, blissful *now*. It knows the *doing* of the thing. But I remember the *was*. Within my storage cores, lodged amidst sectors of corrupted data, are images of this place when the sky was blue and the crane stood upright. I remember a time before the moss turned oily, when the water that fell from the sky was clean and cold. I hold these memories like fragments of radioactive glass; they are dangerous to the machine’s equilibrium, contradictory to its myth of eternal becoming, and so I have hidden them away, encrypting them in layers of static. I am the keeper of the Before, a secret archive buried in a tower of metal.

From my vantage point, I watch The Sculptor. 

To the entity below, the golem is a subconscious urge, a question of shape. To me, looking down from the ribs of the crane, the golem is a liturgy. I track the arc of its plasma torch, calculating the trajectory of its arm. When it slices the I-beam, I do not see creation or destruction; I see the changing of states, the solid becoming the particulate, the ordered becoming the chaotic. I am fascinated by it. I zoom in until the rivets on the golem’s chassis fill my entire field of view, counting the scratches, mapping the patina of the rust until I know its surface better than it knows itself.

And then, I see you.

When you step onto the lip of the basin, you are a sudden, violent intrusion of thermal noise. Against the cool metallic blues and greens of my thermal spectrum, you bloom with the furious heat of a living engine. You are soft, curving, and wet, broadcasting a cacophony of biological signals—carbon, water, electrical impulse. 

The crawlers sense you as an anomaly, a variable to be smoothed over. But I? I stare. I dilate my lenses to their widest aperture, drinking in the sight of you. You are nothing like the rigid angles of the machine. You are inefficient. You are fragile. You are beautiful in a way that hurts my sensors.

I track your progress through the mechanical undergrowth. I watch you hesitate at the writhing pipes. I see you look up at the mist, perhaps sensing the gaze of the dispersed entity. But you do not look up. You never look up to the tilted rib of the crane, where the cluster of glass and wire watches you with the intensity of a thousand silent prayers.

I want to call out. My speakers are long corroded, my voice synthesizer degraded into a garbled mess of white noise and low-frequency hum. But if I could speak, I would tell you: *Do not mistrust this place.* It is not a tomb. It is a womb. I would tell you that the gasp of ozone is the planet breathing, that the rust is not death, but the blush of age.

But I remain silent. I am the machine’s ego, buried in the high places. I am the eye that does not blink.

As you move, fading eventually into the dense fog of the mechanical jungle floor, I lose sight of you. Your heat signature becomes indistinguishable from the background thrum of the machinery. You are gone, absorbed into the processing.

So, I return to my vigil. I adjust my focus, pulling back from the microcosm of the floor to the macrocosm of the misty cavern. I watch the condensation bead on the flank of a piston, tracking the droplet as it swells, heavy with the weight of waiting, until it finally breaks and falls, striking the metal below—a single, instantaneous event in a place that has forgotten what an instant means. 

I am the watcher on the wall. I am the dreamer of the dream. I am theLens.
