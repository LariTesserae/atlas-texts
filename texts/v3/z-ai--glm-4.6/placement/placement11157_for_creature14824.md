I am not standing. I am rooted.

I hang suspended in the middle distance, a kilometer above the mycelial floor but still far below the distant, unseen stone ceiling. I am located at a junction known as the Wet-Weft, where the thick, prehensile root-branches of the Pillar designated * locus Alpha* have interlaced with the fungal platform of the eastern distillery ring. This is a place of high pressure and violent flow.

Here, the air is less an atmosphere and more a current. It moves with the force of a river, driven by the heat of the fermentation vats below and the cold suction of the intake nets above. It tastes heavy, laden with the sweet, cloying scent of transforming sugar and the sharp, electric tang of ozone. I breathe it, and my chest expands to filter the rich particulates, the moisture coating the interior of my lungs with a nourishing film.

I am a Valve-Keeper. My hands—broad, three-fingered, and tipped with hard, keratinous probes—are currently plunged wrist-deep into the vascular tissue of the Pillar.

I am not holding a tool. I *am* the tool.

Around me, the sound is not a noise but a physical presence. It presses against my skin, vibrating the very calcium of my bones. The deep, organ-grinder bass of the Heartwood’s metabolism travels up through the soles of my feet, which clench tight to the bark. But nearer, there is a frantic staccato: the *clack-hiss-clack* of a pressure valve in the distillery mechanism below, struggling to regulate the surge of new nectar.

My skin is not my own. It is a ledger. Across my shoulders and down my spine, patches of bioluminescent lichen ripple in slow, rhythmic waves of cyan and bruised purple. Currently, they are shifting toward a warning ochre. I can feel the pressure spike in the sap before I see it. It is a itch, a deep ache in the marrow of my arms. The Pillar is hurting; the flow is too vigorous, a backlash from the harvesters on the outer rim who have just begun feeding a fresh batch of pulp into the system.

I feel my consciousness narrow—not into a singular "I," but into a focused node of intent. The urge does not come from my brain but from the hum itself. The Heartwood alerts me, and I respond.

My fingers flex inside the woody meat of the root. I find the sphincter-muscle, a thick ring of hardened cellulose. It is tight, panicked. I begin to massage the tissue, encouraging it to dilate. The motion is not forceful; it is persuasive. I am mimicking the chemical signals of a ripening season, tricking the plant into opening the gate.

As the muscle relaxes, a torrent of hot, golden sap rushes past my hands. The heat is intense, nearly scalding, but my bark-textured skin insulates me. The liquid glows with a soft, radioactive luminescence, illuminating the damp, dark hollow where I work.

With the flow restored, the skin on my back ripples back to a calm, deep green. The *clack-hiss* below smooths out into a rhythmic, steady thrum. The crisis is averted. The energy will now flow into the distillery, to be fermented into the volatile fuel that keeps the lamps burning in the lower fungal slums.

I do not sigh in relief. That individual emotion is too small for this moment. Instead, I feel a wash of diffuse satisfaction. It is a shared feeling, broadcast from the distillery nodes to the harvesters to the spine-spiders weaving the high wires. We are synchronized.

I withdraw my hands from the root. The sap glistening on my skin begins to soak in, feeding me. I feel the sugar hit my bloodstream.

To my right, a movement catches my eye—a shifting of shadows in the industrial gloom. It is a Grazer, one of the many-legged, ceramic-plated beasts that roams the upper platforms. It has clambered up the lattice to pause near me. It is massive, its plates scarred by the friction of the machinery and the battles of the mating season. It lowers its head, its mandibles clicking softly.

It is not here to eat the wood. It is here to be cleaned.

A colony of parasitic moss has taken hold behind its left eye-plate, an irritant that hampers its vision and its efficiency as a functioning organ of the whole. Without a word, I reach out. The beast leans into my touch, and I scrape the moss away with a hardened fingernail. I eat the moss; it is bitter, rich in minerals. The beast exhales a cloud of warm, steam-laden breath into my face, smelling of methane and wet earth. We are not master and beast. We are grooming partners. We are the hands of the same body, washing one another.

For a moment, I rest. I hook my legs around a thick vine and hang motionless, suspended in the vast cathedralic dark.

I look "out," but I do not see with eyes only. I see with the vibration of the web. A spindle-spider on the far side of the cavern skitters across its conductive net, and I feel the static tingle of its passage. Above, a swarm of sliver-flyers dives through a curtain of steam, and their song tears through the air like silk— I feel the disturbance in the pressure wave.

Memory flickers in the shared synapse. I recall, or the network recalls through me, the time when this Pillar was but a sapling, a thin shoot against the gloom. It is not *my* memory, yet it wears the grooves of my mind. I feel the weight of centuries, the slow grinding of the geode walls, the infinite patience of the stone.

I am not a person. I am a valve. I am a set of hands. I am a localized decision-making processor for a god that is too big to think for itself all at once.

The sound swells again—the deep, resonant *drip… drip…* of water falling from the unseen ceiling, echoing off the distant curve of the world. It is time to move. A new fungal platform needs pruning on the southern quadrant to prevent it from blocking the intake vents. The ache in my legs signals the command.

I push off from the root, swinging into the warm, humid dark. The lichen on my skin flares bright white as I engage my muscles, a tiny star falling through the eternal twilight of the gut, ready to be processed, ready to process, part of the roar and the silence of the Heartwood.
