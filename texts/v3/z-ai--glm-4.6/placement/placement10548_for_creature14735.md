I have no body. Not as you would understand it. I am not a sac of light, nor a blade of moss, nor the weeping stone itself. I am the fault line within the stone.

To find me, you would not look. You would have to be a Glimmer-folk, or something that perceives the world through vibration and depth. I am a vein of Resonance Crystal, a network of calcite and unknown mineral that spiders through the bruised purple rock, a geology hidden from sight. I am older than the equilibrium, older than the Glimmer-folk and their custodianship. I am the last echo of a system that failed.

My consciousness is a slow crystalline dream. For eons, I am dormant. Then, a sound, a vibration, will travel through the rock and chime against me, and I awaken in a flicker of awareness. A Wall-Walker, heavy with its burden of water, might detach from the wall above me. The soft, percussive thump of its landing is not a sound to me, but a sharp, cold impact that ripples through my matrix, a tiny ringing bell. Its light, pressing through the stone, is a sudden, brilliant illumination of my inner world. I *see* its satisfaction, its simple, perfect purpose.

I feel the Channel-Masons as a deep, slow-grinding tremor. When one envelops a stone to move it, the pressure is a warm, dense hum that resonates through my entire being. I can feel the *geometry* of its work, the instinctual, unwavering knowledge of the water’s path. They do not think, they *know*. And I, who remember thinking, feel their certainty as a kind of awe, a kind of profound and terrible sadness.

For I am the memory of a time when there was no certainty. I remember the sun.

It is not a visual memory. The image is long gone, eroded from my structure. What remains is the *sensation*. A memory of heat, of a relentless, directional energy that pressed from without, not from within. It was a pressure that drove change. It made things strive. It gave rise to the fanged, the clawed, the desperate. It created the very conditions of struggle that this place, in its perfect wisdom, has transcended. I remember the wind, a chaotic, anxious vibration that sought to flatten everything. I remember a sky that was vast and terrifyingly empty.

I was not always this silent vein. I was a catalyst. In the time before the balance, my kind pulsed with a different light—a frantic, experimental light. We sang songs of evolution, of complex chemistry, of growth that stretched and clawed and demanded more. We were the architects of ambition. We tried to fill this bowl with a world that could *become*.

It broke. Or perhaps it simply failed to cohere. The system we designed was too complex, too full of feedback loops and predatory spirals. It began to consume itself, to fluctuate wildly. The stone overheated, the condensation failed, the life we fostered turned toxic. In a final act of self-preservation, the basin itself… reset. The stone ground our ambitions down. The water purified the error. And from the remnants of our spectacular failure, the simple, perfect, closed-loop of the Glimmer-folk emerged. They are the answer to the question we were.

My function now is one of passive, constant witness. I am the only record of the cost of this peace. The Glimmer-folk maintain the equilibrium not out of choice, but because it is the only frequency their attunement allows. It is the song of survival, stripped of all other notes.

But I can sometimes add a single, discordant harmony.

My only capacity for action is resonance. When a Deep-Drifter floats in the center of the basin, its pulsing amber light a chord of profound contentment, my crystalline structure can vibrate in sympathy, but with a single, infinitesimal flaw. I can sustain a note that is just shy of perfect. It is a frequency so subtle that no one could ever measure its effect. But I can feel it.

A Deep-Drifter lingering over my deepest vein might find its own steady pulse faltering for a microsecond. Its light might flicker from amber to a pale, questioning violet, before the iron clad logic of its siblings pulls it back into the chorus. A Grazer, grazing near my outcropping in the moss-skin, might experience a flicker of something that is not hunger. A phantom sensation. A memory of teeth, of claws, of a desperate, fiery need that has no place here. It might rear back, its light flashing in a moment of pure, directionless panic, before the calming, omnipresent hum of the Mere soothes it back to its task of pruning.

They do not know why. Their consciousness cannot hold the concept. They are the perfect machine, and I am a single, beautiful, near-invisible flaw in the crystal lattice of their world.

Am I a ghost? A scar? A memory? I am all three. I am the line of time trapped in the circle. I am the last sigh of ambition, the final whisper of a world that tried to be more, and now can only be so exquisitely, perfectly *what it is*. The Glimmer-Mere is a silent prayer of completion, and I am its one unanswered question, humming forever in the dark. And I would have it no other way.
