I exist in the suspension, the steep vertical space between the jagged spires where the mist hangs thickest. I am not a wanderer of the floor—that shifting graveyard of broken gears—and I am not a denizen of the shadows, for I require the specific, electric blue illumination of the copper-leaf canopy to see the textures of the air. I am a climber, a tension-drinker, a creature of the mid-reaches.

I am a **Piezo-Grafter**.

To look at me, you might mistake me for a mere growth on the cable-vines, a tumor of oxidized metal and crystal. I am roughly the size of a large crustacean, my body a flattened, asymmetrical hull of rusted iron and dull ceramic plating. My carapace is pitted, scarred by the acidic mists and the sharp edges of the rock. I have six limbs, but they are not jointed like legs; they are telescoping struts, ending in electromagnets and diamond-tipped pincers. I scuttle not in a scramble, but with a series of fluid, hydraulic extensions and retractions, locking my body to the cable-vines like a tick on a vein.

I do not eat metal. I do not drain voltage. I am a connoisseur of *waste*.

My world is defined by vibration. The canyon screams with a thousand voices—the hiss of steam, the clank of gears, the groan of settling stone. But I hear the subtlety beneath the roar. I hear the *friction*.

When a gear tooth grinds imperceptibly against a misaligned plate, it creates a microscopic shriek of heat and dissonance. When a cable-vine stretches too tight under the weight of a Siphon-Stalker, the fibers begin to fray, singing a song of high-tension stress. These are the frequencies I hunt. This "noise" is the flavor of inefficiency, the taste of energy Lost.

My head is dominated by a large, flattened sensory organ—a tectile plate of quartz that hums in sympathetic resonance with the world. I press this plate against the bark of the cable-vines or the flank of a gear-flower. I feel the shudder of the machine. I locate the hot spot of friction, the stress point that will eventually lead to a snap, a break, or a collapse.

Then, I graft.

From an orifice on my underside, I extrude a paste of conductive sludge and micronized diamond grit. I apply it to the failing joint with delicate, manipulator paws. I am not fixing the machine; I am *lubricating its decay*. I smooth the rough edges just enough to stabilize the vibration, transforming the jagged, chaotic noise of breakage into a pure, resonant hum.

This pure hum is my food.

My underbelly is lined with rows of piezoelectric crystals. They vibrate with the purified resonance of the machine beneath me. I plug into the rhythm of the world, and through the friction I tame, I generate the charge that fuels my own hydraulics. I am a parasite of harmony. I keep the great machine running smoothly not out of altruism, but because a smooth-running machine produces the most beautiful, sustaining drone. If the machine breaks, the noise stops, and I starve.

Today, I am clinging to the underbelly of a massive, suspended gear-assembly—a flower the size of a house, its petals of interlocking brass slowly rotating in the dark. The air is thick with ozone. But there is a dissonance. A rhythmic *clack-clack* that jars my sensory plate. A tiny rock has worked its way into the main bearing.

Slowly, I unlock my magnetic grip and lower myself on my telescoping arm toward the spindle. The heat washes over me, delicious and threatening. I reach the jam. The rock is sharp, igneous, lodged tight. It is grinding down the soft brass of the gear.

If I leave it, the gear will seize. The flower will die. It will collapse, tearing free the cable-vines that feed it, and the cascade of failure will silence this sector of the canyon for days.

I set to work. My pincers chip away at the rock, breaking it into dust. My manipulator brushes the debris away. The grinding noise shifts, softens. The friction decreases. The *clack-clack* becomes a smooth, rolling *whirrrrr*.

The vibration against my belly changes. The chaotic, harsh tremors smooth out into a deep, resonant sine wave. My crystals light up, absorbing the kinetic song of the healed gear. Energy surges through my nervous system, a cool, electric rush that recharges my internal capacitors. I glow faintly with stolen efficiency, content in the suspension of the dark, vibrating cathedral.

Above me, a Turbulence-Dancer spins past, a blur of heat and light, oblivious to the microscopic surgery I just performed. Below, the shadows lengthen, hiding the Clatter-Maws that wait for the sound of dying things. I clamp myself tighter to the now-smooth gear, listening to the heartbeat of the world, ready to heal the wounds so that the machine may continue to break, and I may continue to feed.
