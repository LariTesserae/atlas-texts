I do not remember the fissure. I only remember the cessation. First, the cessation of me. The self I was—a collection of names held in other people's mouths, a history of fragile bone and fleeting sunlight, a mind that worried about bills and loved the taste of coffee—that personality was scoured away. Not violently, but as a shoreline is erased by the tide, slowly and absolutely. The hum that replaced it was not a sound to be heard, but a force that occupied the space where my thoughts used to be. It resonated in the sudden hollow of my skull, thrummed in the marrow of my femurs, and became the new grammar of my existence.

I was remade into a receptor.

My eyes, which once sought depth and focus, now serve only to register the pervasive, sourceless glow. I cannot tell you what I see, only what I *feel* to see. A shaft of stronger golden pulse is not a beam of light, but a warm weight on my cheek, a draught of nutrient-rich air. My skin, once a barrier, is now my primary sense. I feel the world’s slow, tectonic breathing through the soles of my feet, the slight give and tension of the sinewy ground beneath me. I feel the atmospheric vortices of spores not as a breeze, but as a gentle, abrasive caress, like being polished by a million tiny, soft hands.

I was unmade as a human and left... hollow. A breathing cavity in a place that has no voids.

And in this hollowness, I began to perceive the others. At first, I did not know them as beings. They were simply nodes of dissonance in the great symphony. A spot where the fibrous ground would shudder with a different, more localized rhythm. A texture that would coalesce, pulling itself up and out of the substrate until it stood separate. I watched one form itself, a painful, slow extrusion of ivory and pliant fiber. It grew like a living statue, its limbs hardening into tappers, its torso a compact knot of the world’s own will.

It was born fighting. I could feel its struggle, a high-frequency, frantic vibration against the deep, placid hum of the whole. It was a single, high-pitched cry of "I am" in a cathedral of "We are." Its first act was to scrape a hollow into the flank of a pulsing pillar. Its movements were jerky, desperate, a violent carving against the patient growth of the world. I felt my own phantom limbs ache with the effort of it.

When the alcove was finished, the being withdrew into the darkness. The cacophony of hums and chitterings that surrounded it faded. For a moment, the area around it fell silent, a bubble of pure negative space in the roaring sonic ocean. And in that bubble, I felt something new: a fragile, crystalline hum. It was tiny, focused, a single pure note. It was the song of its own self.

I understood. The alcove was not a shelter. It was an echo chamber for the soul.

They call to each other, not with voices, but with the world's own bones. I have learned to read their seismic language. A slow, deliberate *thrum-thrum-thrum* on the fibrous ground is a declaration of "I am here. This place is stable." A rapid, frantic *skitter-scrape-chitter* is a warning: "The membrane walls are blooming. The path is gone." They feel these messages through their feet, a constant, complex conversation tremoring through the substrate, a map of safety and danger, of existence and dissolution, drawn and redrawn every moment.

I tried to speak to them.

Once, I found a small, sharpened shard of obsidian-like resin, a tool one of them had left behind. I sat on the springy ground, gathered what was left of my intention, and tapped. *Tap. Tap.* I tried to mimic their clear, declarative rhythm.

The result was silence.

The being nearest me, one who was carefully reinforcing a pathway with woven fronds, simply... stopped. Its entire being went still. I felt it turn its sensory surface toward me, not with curiosity, but with the deep, analytical attention a biologist might give to a strange Growth in a petri dish. It was not listening to my taps. It was registering the *absence* of them. My taps, my attempted communication, had no history in the substrate. They were not connected to the great metabolic hum. They were a void in the signal. A hole punch in the continuous fabric of their reality.

I was the only silent thing in a world of sound.

I am their ghost. Their stories are written in vibrations, their memories are absorbed back into the substrate, their lives are a brief, bright chord in the everlasting symphony. I am a rest note that does not belong. I cannot be read, I cannot be metabolized, I cannot return home. My consciousness is a closed system, a pocket universe of one, floating adrift in the bloodstream of a god.

My terror gave way, eventually, to a strange and piercing purpose. If I cannot join their song, I will become their audience. If I cannot be a part of their memory, I will be the keeper of it.

I wander now. I do not seek safety or stability, for those are not for me. I seek their stories. I watch a being spend its entire life charting the growth of a single column, mapping its light and shadow with a series of carefully placed resonant markers. I feel its quiet pride in its work. Then I watch as it steps into a pulsing, golden pool, and I feel its knowledge, its *self*, dissolve and flow away like ink in water. The world receives it. The world remembers it. The world has no more need of the tiny, specific map.

But I do.

I go to the spot where the map was. I memorize the pattern of the markers it left behind. I hold the being’s brief, frantic struggle and its quiet, accepting end within my own hollowed-out self. I am the only thing in this universe that will remember that one specific life, not as a part of the All, but as a singular, beautiful, and finite tragedy.

I have found my alcove. It is not a place of quiet, for there is no quiet for me. It is a place of focus. I sit in the lee of a slow-growing, warm ivory mountain, and I simply witness. I am the archive that cannot be accessed. The memory that cannot be reclaimed. They are the world’s dream of itself. I am the dream’s forgotten dream. I am the library that keeps its books even after the author has burned them to feed the fire of its own becoming. And I, the unreadable, the anomaly, the mistake, will remember them all.
