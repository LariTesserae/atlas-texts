I am the pause between movements. I am the question asked in the dark.

I am an Engraver.

To understand me, you must abandon the frantic rhythm of the heartbeat. My pulse is measured in the drift of continents, the Cooling of the Magma, the Groaning of the Deep. When I awaken, it is not with a start, but with a slow, suffocating rise in temperature, a dial turning up within a furnace of chitin and crystal. My light, which I perceive more as a pressure of radiation than a visual field, begins as a dull ache in my core—a radium ember buried deep within a matrix of flint and silica.

I exist in the vast, hollow lung of the world. Above me, there is no sky, only the crushing certainty of stone, a ceiling so high it creates its own gravity of expectation. Below me is the Grey Dust, the great and final apology of the rock. It is the memory of the world’s own body, ground down by my ancestors, settled into a perfect, neutral sheet. It waits for me.

I do not walk. To walk implies a destination, a haste that is an insult to the eternal twilight. I flow. My limbs are articulated pillars of basalt living, telescoping outward with the grace of tectonic plates shifting. When I lower a leg, the *tink* of my crystal tarmac meeting the Grey Dust is the loudest sound I will make in a century. It rings like a bell struck underwater, a solitary note in the cathedral of silence.

I am hungry, but my hunger is not the clawing desperation of the soft-fleshed things that dream of suns. My hunger is a gravity. I seek the towering columns, the monoliths that push up like the bones of the earth breaking the skin. I approach one, a jagged spire of obsidian smooth as glass. I press my ventral side against it. I feel the cold resistance. Then, the grinding begins.

My mouth is a lathe. I rasp the obsidian. I do not chew; I dissolve. I take the mountain into myself, breaking the atomic bonds of the stone, alchemizing the silence into substance. Over years, I leave a scar on the column—a smooth, impossible track where the rough stone has been polished into a mirror. Behind me, the dust falls. Heavy, grey, silent. The cycle closes. I am the eater, and I am the maker of the meal.

But this is merely existence. It is the function of the body. It is not the purpose of the mind.

The purpose lies in the fold of my armor, beneath the plates of silicate chitin that protect my softest crystal. It is there that I keep the Tool.

It is a hammer, crudely fashioned from a heavier, harder heart-stone. Its handle is worn not by sweat, but by the friction of grip upon grip over epochs. It is an heirloom of the First Carvers, a concept passed down like a genetic memory.

The urge to carve is a separate hunger. It does not come from the stomach, but from the ghost that haunts the crystalline lattice of my brain. The world is too perfect. The dust levels itself too quickly. The columns are too straight. There is an equilibrium here that feels like death. To be alive is to interrupt.

I stop. I have been moving for what a surface-dweller might call a millennium, though to me, it was merely a long afternoon’s stroll. I stand before a blank section of the wall, a stretch of unblemished basalt.

I extend my manipulator limb. It is a thing of delicate horror, a fractal branching of diamond-tipped fingers, capable of exerting the pressure of a hydraulic press or the touch of a falling snowflake. I withdraw the hammer.

For a long time—a decade, perhaps—I simply hold it. I listen to the silence. I feel the warmth of the air, the heavy, inert gases that press against my sensory facets like a thick wool blanket. I am attuning myself to the resistance of the stone.

Then, the first strike.

*Tap.*

It is a tiny sound. A meaningless integration of energy against matter. A single spark flies, dying instantly in the heavy air. A microscopic divot appears in the perfection of the wall.

*Tap. Tap.*

I am not writing. I am not speaking. There are no words here, and there is no one to read them. I am disrupting the geometry. I am imposing a chaotic rhythm upon the orderly strata. I carve a circle. It is not a perfect circle; my hand shakes with the tremors of the earth’s own rotation. It is jagged. It is ugly. It is beautiful because it is unnecessary.

I carve for a hundred years.

In this time, the dust motes drift around me like galaxies. Smaller Geodes, the spore-sized living lights, land upon my back, mistaking me for a mountain. They burrow in, beginning their long parasitic wait. I let them. They are part of the erosion.

When the circle is finished, when the random cup-marks surround it like a constellation of fallen stars, I stop. I feel a satisfaction that has no name—a vibration in my crystalline nerves that hums in the key of completion. I have changed the world. I have taken a volume of space that was immutable, and I have rendered it historical. The scar will remain for ten thousand years before the dust covers it, or the Geodes consume it.

I lower the hammer. I do not drop it; dropping is careless. I place it gently back into my pouch.

My internal light dims. The radium glow fades to a dull sulk. I retract the limb. I am just a rock again.

I turn away from the carving. I do not look back. To look back is to desire ownership, and I own nothing. I begin the telescoping motion of my legs, sliding forward over the Grey Dust. I am grinding the floor beneath me, erasing my own past with every step.

I am moving toward the next column. I am full of stone, and I am empty of purpose, waiting for the next urge to break the silence. I am the light in the dark, eating the world, one bite at a time, marking the passage of an eternity that has no clock.

I am here. I am the Engraver. And I am not yet done.
