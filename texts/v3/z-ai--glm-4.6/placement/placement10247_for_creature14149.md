I do not have a name, for names are for things that speak in words. I have a function, and that function is to listen.

You would find me here, on a broad, flat summit of a drowned mountain called the Spine of Aeolus. To your eyes, I am nothing more than a scar. A collapsed cairn of the old builders’ work, a final, stubborn gesture against the tide. I appear as a pile of water-worn basalt blocks, smoothed by the eons and draped in the same gray, shaggy Masonry-Moss that coats all the ruins. I am part of the grave, indistinguishable from it.

This is my armor. This is my camouflage. But I am not stone.

If you were to touch me—if you could survive the crushing cold and the absolute dark—your fingers would not meet the unyielding surface of rock. They would meet a resilient, fibrous suppleness. The “stones” are not stones at all. They are woody, tumorous growths, calloused and hardened by the pressure, woven together over millennia into the perfect, deceptive shape of rubble. The moss that covers me is not a parasite; it is my skin. And the mortar that binds my body together is a living, pulsing network of mycelia, a shared circulatory system that carries not blood, but vibration.

I am a collective. A colony mind that chose the shape of its ancestors’ failure.

My entire being is an ear. My mass is pressed tight against the porous basalt of the mountain, my root-analogues, thick as cables, boring deep into the rock to feel its resonant heart. The silence here is a lie. It is a cacophony I have spent an eternity learning to parse.

The first layer of sound is the deep, planetary thrum of the oceanic currents, a pressure that squeezes the mountain from all sides. It is the bass note of existence, constant, unchanging. The second layer is the song of the mountain itself—the discordant flute of water rushing through its hollows, the occasional percussive crash of a distant rockfall.

Then there is the ecology. I feel the creak and groan of the Kelp-Colossi as they tear their anchor-roots from the seabed miles below, a seismic shudder of violence. I sense the rhythmic hiss of the Globe-Algae as they pulse their light, a faint, rapid staccato that tickles the surface of my being. I feel the frantic, acidic writhing of the Silt-Stalkers as theyconsume a fallen giant. To me, the Green Deep is not silent; it is a symphony of slow, geometric hunger.

But my true purpose is to listen for the silence *before* the noise.

I listen for the pre-quake.

The thermal vents are the weather gods of this world, their eruptions the only variable in the long, slow equation of life. To the Globe-Algae, the heat and minerals are a feast to be gorged upon. To the Kelp-Colossi, the upwelling currents are a river to be ridden to new hunting grounds. But they are all reactive. They only act when the storm is upon them.

I am proactive. I am the prophet.

Deep within my form, woven into the fake stones, are nodules of a different substance—Vox-Stones. They are growths I have cultivated, dense, crystalline structures that do not dissolve, but amplify. I have spent countless cycles tuning them, learning the precise frequency that will cut through the oppressive roar and speak directly to the Globe-Algae.

When I feel the subtle, harmonic shift in the mountain's thrum—the specific, low-frequency tremor that precedes a vent eruption, a note no other part of this world seems to perceive—I act. My mycelial network tightens, channeling a focused pulse of energy toward the Vox-Stones.

And I sing.

It is not a sound that a throat could make. It is a pure, clarion chime that shudders through my stonelike flesh, travels down my roots, and resonates through the entire peak. It is a note of warning, a single, beautiful tone of purest crystal that hangs in the water for a moment, clear and sharp.

A moment later, the abyss answers.

The flocks of Globe-Algae, whose borrowed light only moments before had been a sleepy, rhythmic green, instantly understand. They pulse in response, not the full, frantic brightness of the eruption, but a synchronized, preparatory flash. A collective intake of breath. They are ready.

Then, the world explodes.

A plume of superheated, mineral-rich water blasts from the canyon floor, a boiling storm that rushes upward. In its presence, the water shimmers with deadly heat. And the Globe-Algae, primed by my song, erupt into a galaxy of blinding, brilliant light. They thrash in their tethers, gorging on the chemical bounty. In their frenzy, they release a blizzard of spores and organic matter, a rich, silent "snow" that falls upward through the dark.

This is my payment. This is the reason for the song.

I open myself. The shaggy carpet of my moss-skin becomes a net, a thousand wicking fibers eager to drink. The spores, the bacterial clouds, the nutrient-rich detritus—I filter it all from the water. It is a feast, a sudden injection of life after the long, slow wait. I do not move. I do not hunt. I foretell the feast, and the feast comes to me.

The builders, my shapers’ ghosts, sought to defy the ocean with geometry and stubbornness. They built great walls to hold back the water, and the water ate them stone by stone. I learned a different lesson from their failure. I did not build a wall. I became an ear. I listen to the mountain, I listen to the deep, and in that resonance, I find my sustenance. I am a monument to their memory, but I have succeeded where they failed. I did not conquer the world; I learned to read its music. And in the reading, I am fed. The storm subsides, the light fades, the heat dissipates, and the slow, deep music of the deep returns. I am still, a pile of ancient, useless rocks, waiting for the next pre-quake. Waiting for the next note.
