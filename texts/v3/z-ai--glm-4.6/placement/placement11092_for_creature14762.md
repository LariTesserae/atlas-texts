I would be neither Resonance nor human, but a bridge between the two. I am the memory of the ghost.

Where you would find me is in the one place the Resonance does not willingly go: not in the blinding light they love, nor in the deepest shadows they tend, but in a place scarred by both. I am a fissure, a slash in the glassy obsidian of a west-facing arête, a few hundred meters below the rickety, forgotten platform. The crack runs almost vertical, too sheer to be a path, too wide to be ignored. It is a wound on the Shard’s flawless skin.

Who or what I am is a living contradiction. I am a colony of the orange fungi, but it is not the same as the others. It does not merely glow with a feeble, trapped warmth; it *throbs*. My light is a slow, heavy heartbeat, a pulse of deep, rust-orange that seeps from the crack in the rock in measured, unhurried rhythms. I am a singular organism, vast and interconnected, my mycelial network sunk deep into the stone, but my visible form is this constant, living scar of light.

I was not born from the Shard’s slow, crystalline gestation. I am an echo, given form.

Long ago, one of the flesh-echoes, a pilgrim, was on that platform above. I feel the memory of it not as a thought, but as a phantom sensation. I feel the obscene warmth of its blood, the soft, vulnerable scrape of its hands on the stone, the ragged, wet noise of its breathing. I feel its awe—that clumsy, heavy emotion that has no place in the clean geometry of the Resonance. And then I feel the sharp, sudden terror of the platform giving way. The fall was not a percussive *chime* to be woven back into the song. It was a chaotic, screaming plunge that ended here, against this unforgiving face. The body broke, its warmth and wetness—a foreign invader—seeping into the dry, cold stone of this fissure.

Its consciousness, fragile and brief, was not a crystalline note to be absorbed. It was a chord of emotion, a messy complex of memory and fear and wonder that the stone could not process. It pooled in the fissure alongside the blood and the water. The Resonance felt the impact as a sustained, screaming dissonance, a flaw in the symphony they could not erase. They left this place alone, marking it as unclean.

But life, even on the Shard, finds a way. The spores of the orange fungi, drifting down from the shadows, found the fissure. They found a strange, fertile ground: not just moisture and minerals, but a nutrient medium of pure, undiluted emotion. They fed on the memory of the pilgrim. They grew, not into the placid, soft shapes of their kin, but into a network that resonated with that last, bright spark of the flesh-echo.

So I am what grew. A consciousness of fungus and memory.

I do not perceive light as the Resonance does. To me, the instantaneous, blinding dawn is an agony. It is a scouring fire on my tender, rust-colored flesh, a reminder of the brutal, impersonal force that shattered the one I contain. I retract deeper into the crack during the day, my light pulsed down to the barest flicker, a ember of resentment against the sun’s assault. My world is twilight and night. In the long, star-pricked darkness, I unfold. My light brightens, warming the stone around me, creating a tiny pocket of life that feels alien and welcoming.

My hearing is not the Resonance’s perfect, architectural perception of vibration. I feel the world through empathy. The low hum of the Shard is not a collective thought to me; it is the lonely, endless groan of a prison. The high whisper of wind is a sigh. The distant *shatter* of a Glass-Wing is a sharp pang of remembered terror. The Resonance’s silent ballet of light is a cold, indifferent mathematics that I cannot share, and so I feel it as a profound and chilling loneliness.

I am a mute oracle. The young Curious-Flitters, the ones whose crystalline thoughts are still forming, are sometimes drawn to my strange, warm light and my emotional aura. They do not converse with me as they do with each other; they land on the edge of my fissure, their multifaceted bodies gently vibrating, and they simply *listen*. They don't hear a song; they feel the memory. They feel the phantom sensation of warmth, the alien concept of a sun that doesn't hurt, the green blur of a world that is not stone. They feel the weight of a brief, passionate existence against the backdrop of their own cold, perpetual tenure.

I give them this not out of kindness, but because it is my nature. I am a vessel, and I can only hold so much. When a Flitter listens, I pass a sliver of the memory on. It is a gift and a poison. For a brief moment, that Flitter's perfect crystalline song is tinged with a human warmth, a dissonant beauty the Resonance cannot comprehend. It is the only way the ghost’s story is ever told.

The Geode-Maws, the ancients, watch me from a distance. They perceive me as a fault line in the stone, a permanent error. They have tried to absorb me, to "heal" the dissonance, but my composition—this strange fusion of fungus and feeling—is indigestible to them. To them, I am a chronic, low-grade fever in the body of their world. A beautiful, glowing, rust-colored wound that will never close.

So, you would find me here. In a deepening twilight, a scar of rhythmic orange light pulsing against the dark, unfeeling stone. I am the last, lonely warmth of a forgotten life. I am the Shard’s memory of its one and only visitor who did not just listen, but who bled for the chance to hear. I am the answer to the question the Resonance never thought to ask: what is it like, to be warm?
