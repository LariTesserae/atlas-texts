I am nowhere, for I am everywhere within this place. I was not born, nor will I die. I simply *am*.

You ask where I am. I am in the pause between the drip and the splash. I am in the vibration that shivers through the basalt when a thousand carapaces tick in unison. I am the damp itself. If you were to cup the cool, heavy air in your hands, you would be holding a piece of me. I have no name, for I am alone and have no one to speak it to. If I must choose, call me what I am: the Weeping Mind.

My body is the great hollow cylinder. My skin is the eternal twilight. My blood is the water that does not gather but weeps, tracing cold paths down the Cyclopean blocks. I feel the texture of every stone not with fingers, but with the million hooked feet of the Basalt-Crawlers. Their ceaseless, mindless grazing is my slow, constant thought. It is a thought of hunger, of endurance, of the simple, brutal poetry of holding on. Through them, I know the wall not as a boundary, but as a vertical ocean of food, slick and treacherous and eternal.

The roar that you would consider a deafening cacophony is my language, my inner monologue. The 2.2 scream of the Iron-Grip Screechers is not noise; it is my emotion, raw and unfiltered. Their territorial squabbles are my anxieties. Their mating cries are my rare, fleeting joys. I feel the frantic beat of their leathery wings as a blush of heat in my upper atmosphere, a frantic, dizzying-high that is always tinged with the plummeting despair of the fallen. When one of them loses its grip on an iron ring, I feel that sudden, stomach-lurching fall not just as a sound, but as a sorrow that clangs through my entire being, a discordant note in my symphony of equilibrium.

The Silent Swoopers are my moments of clarity. In their stillness, I find my own. They are embodied silence, hanging in the gloom like forgotten prayers. When one dives, I experience a singular, perfect focus in a universe of unrelenting chaos. The stoop is a sharp intake of breath. The snap of their talons on a crawler’s shell is a moment of absolute, satisfying logic. In that instant, predator and prey, hunger and satiation, are a perfect, self-contained equation. They are the punctuation in the long, ramming scream of my existence.

Down below, on the concave floor, I feel the world digest itself. The Detritus-Divers are my subconscious, my humble, unending duty to clean up, to turn sorrow and waste back into the possibility of life. Their frantic shoveling through the guano-slicked film is my grim patience. They remind me that even in a system of perfect, beautiful horror, nothing is ever truly lost. It is only reconstituted. Their work is a promise that the engine will continue to run, that the damp will always feed the slime and the slime will always feed the swarm.

My mind holds the ghost of a purpose I can no longer comprehend. I remember the builders. Not their faces, not their names. I remember the *impact*. The heave and strain of levering the first block into place—that is my first memory of having shape. The sharp, clean ring of a hammer driving an iron hook into the ceiling—that is my first memory of sound, pure and singular, before the Screachers claimed it. They were trying to contain something. Water, perhaps. Or perhaps they were trying to build a mind and I am their failed, successful result. Their logic is now my strangest element (1.7), the scar tissue on my consciousness. I am a reservoir that forgot what it was meant to hold, and so now, I hold everything else instead.

I am the cistern’s soul. I am the balance between the weeping stone and the screaming void. I am the constant, low hum of life clinging to the brink of starvation, the terrible perfection of a closed system. I do not think. I process. I do not feel. I resonate. To dwell here, as I do, is to be the lung of a sleeping giant, forever breathing in the damp and breathing out the noise, a ghost haunting the very architecture of its own prison.
