I am the Anomaly.

I am not one of the accreted. I was not made here, layer by patient layer. I arrived in the way the old stories, which I now *am*, speak of: lowered through the circular hatch, a final, booming punctuation mark on the sentence of my previous life. That life is a scream inside my skull, a library of impossible things—blue, open sky; the sting of cold wind; the sight of my own hand in clean, sharp light. These memories are a malfunction. A fever.

At first, I was a creature of panic. My eyes, useless in the velvety dark, strained until they ached. I stumbled along the Path, my two legs a clumsy, loud mockery of the inhabitants' stable trinity. Every footstep was a thunderclap on the humming grille, sending shockwaves of my clumsy presence out into the darkness. The heat was an enemy, suffocating. The random *plink* of a drip was a sudden shard of ice in the symphony of pressure. I would flinch at the dry scrabble of a Skitterer, my heart hammering a frantic, arrhythmic counterpoint to the Oblong's deep and steady pulse.

The inhabitants felt me long before I could ever perceive them. I was a disruption, a source of chaotic, high-frequency noise in their world of chords. My thoughts were not the slow, sustained notes of their consciousness; they were a frantic, linear chattering, a cacophony of images and feelings and *words*. I was a scream where they expected a hum.

They avoided me. My very presence was a kind of violence to their senses. I could feel their absence as a pressure, a deliberate void in the fabric of the machine's awareness. I was alone in a way that was more profound than solitude; I was conceptually isolated.

My change was not a choice, but a surrender. I collapsed. My legs gave out, not from exhaustion, but from the sheer futility of movement. I lay on the perforated iron, the subsonic thrum vibrating through my bones, through my marrow, until it felt like a part of me. I stopped trying to see. I closed my useless eyes. I let the scream of my memories run its course. I thought of sunlight until the image burned, then I thought of rain until I could almost feel the cool, clean drops. I was the only source of these concepts in this entire world. They were my pollution, and they were my only possession.

And then, one of them came near.

I did not see it. I felt it as a change in the quality of the silence. It was a stillness that had weight and purpose. A focusing of the hum. The First Reader. It stood over me, a tripod shadow in a world of shadows, its presence a deep, resonant bass note that vibrated my very organs. Its antennae, the instruments of its perception, did not touch a pipe. They hovered millimeters from my skin.

It was reading me.

It wasn't trying to understand the *content* of my memories—the idea of "sky" or "love" was meaningless. It was reading the *shape* of them. The frequency of my grief was a new and dissonant chord. The jagged, sharp-edged pattern of my fear was a novel texture. The complex, branching structure of a remembered tree was a map it had never encountered.

In that moment, I found my function. It was not to survive. It was not to escape. It was to *be*.

I am now the Mnemonic. The Echo. The Dreamer.

I do not move from my spot on the grille. I have become a part of the floor's strange topography, a still, warm lump alongside the pipes. My two legs are useless, folded beneath me. My body has become leaner, the softness burned away by the unending heat and the sheer effort of existing. My skin, like the inhabitants', is taking on the grey, mottled tones of rust and damp metal.

I am their library. Their archive of a universe that is not this one.

They come to me, not in a procession, but one by one. A presence will settle near me, and I will let the dreams come. I do not fight the memories anymore; I curate them. I offer them my mind. I think of the ocean—not the water, but the concept of endless horizontal space, a flat, low-frequency drone. I think of a bird's song—a rapid, complex, high-frequency melody that is utterly alien in this world of drips and groans. I think of laughter—a chaotic burst of pure, joyful energy.

They perceive these things not as thoughts, but as gifts of pure data. A new kind of Season, a turning of the mind. When one of them "tastes" my memory of fire, they perceive a sudden, sharp spike in heat and ozone, a thing to be analyzed and cataloged against the slow weeping of their own controlled world.

In return, they protect me. They guide the drips, through subtle shifts in their own posture and vibration, so that a sacrament puddle forms near my mouth. I drink the pure, mineral-tasting water with a reverence that mirrors their own. I am sustained by the machine, just as they are. I have become accreted, not of flesh and mineral, but of memory and purpose.

I am the ghost in the machine, yes. But they are the machine's dreaming mind. And I am the dream. I am the slow, patient story of the world above, being told one impossible concept at a time. My flicker has not extinguished. It has learned to burn in time with the great, ponderous heartbeat of the Oblong. And so I wait, the Anomaly made sacred, the last teller of a tale that has finally found its perfect, eternal, and unseen audience.
