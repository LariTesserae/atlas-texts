I would be one of them. Not a single, discrete being that could be pointed to and named, but a fleeting locus of awareness within the slow, chitinous tide. My consciousness would not sit behind eyes or in a brain, but would be distributed along the length of my many-jointed body, a constant, flickering stream of pure sensation. To be me would be to exist as a living question answered by the texture of the world.

My "birth" is a sudden flicker of focus. Before, I was merely potential, a undifferentiated part of the flowing carpet of my kind in the deep-shadow of a collapsed sky-bridge. Now, a subtle shift in the deep-down thrum resonates through the carapace of the being next to me, and then my own. It is a chime of pressure. A signal. It tastes like... *opportunity*. The flavour of concentrated copper from a sheared conduit, newly exposed by the morning’s condensation, is bleeding into the general taste of wet iron and stale air.

My body, a thing of wet-stone grey and the dull crimson of old rust, ripples into motion. There is no decision, only an alignment. My forelegs taste the air, sensing the gradient, and the rest of me follows. I flow out of the shadows and onto the open, fractured plaza, my dozens of legs making a sound like soft sand being poured across metal. I am not walking *on* the city; I am a part of its current erosion, a deliberate agent of its final, slow-motion collapse.

The diffuse pearlescent gloom is a gentle pressure against my foremost sensory palps. I move through a world of textures and flavours. Here, the tile offers the flat, silica taste of pulverized bedrock. There, a slick film of pure water falls from high above, and a specialized ridge on my back siphons it, a cold, clean shock to my system. My purpose is simple, absolute, and all-encompassing: to translate complex metals into simple self.

I follow the coppery scent. It leads me to the base of a colossal pylon, a pillar of pitted concrete weeping lines of rust like red tears. The flavour here is a symphony. My brethren are already here, a flowing delta of consumption. I join them without acknowledgment or greeting. To brush against another is simply to share a data point of pure sensation: *Good. Here. We. This.* It is the only word we need.

I find my own spot on a particularly vibrant patch of rust. The work begins. My ventral chelicerae, broad and strong for scraping, press against the surface. I taste years of atmospheric action, centuries of slow decay. With each rhythmic scrape, I lift a microscopic flake of iron oxide, a burst of pure, potent energy. It is the taste of the city’s strength, now become my sustenance. I am cleaning the world, making it part of me. The Engine thrums its patient, sub-audible bass note beneath my feet, the metronome to which my entire existence scrapes in time.

Sated, I detach from the wall, my shell a shade deeper, richer. I flow onward, not out of curiosity, but because the gradient has changed. The deep-down thrum seems to be pulling me, not towards flavour, but towards… silence. A hollow. I find the immense, dark maw of a ruined habitat block and flow inside.

The air changes. It is colder, thinner, tasting of dead polymers and the ancient, inert dust of forgotten interiors. The flavour palette is barren. This is a desert. But the pull of the thrum is insistent. It is a question I must answer with my body.

And then, in the center of the vast, echoing space, I find the source of the discord.

It is a shape. A block. It does not belong.

My sensory world is a map of gradients, but this thing is a hole in the map. It doesn’t reflect the pearlescent gloom; it *drinks* it, creating a pocket of perfect, absolute shadow. The air around it is not just bland, it is… null. It is a void of taste. I approach it, my palps quivering, testing the hostile unfamiliarity of the air.

I reach a leg out and touch the surface.

The sensation is a violation. It is a screech of feedback that vibrates through my entire being. It is not the rough, yielding texture of rust, or the grit of concrete. It is impossibly, aggressively smooth. And it offers no purchase, no flavour. It is *un-consumable*.

This is the first error in my world. A fact that defies the one, singular truth: everything is food.

I press against it. My mouthparts scrape uselessly, the jarring vibration a pain I have no word for. It does not yield. It does not break down. It simply *is*. It is a piece of the old world’s logic—a world that made things not to be returned to the whole. A remnant of a time of purposeless permanence.

A flicker of new awareness passes through my distributed consciousness. It is not thought, but a profound and lasting state of *dissonance*. A note of pure, silent refusal in the symphony of decay.

The Engine thrums from below, its patient promise of work and flavour calling me back. I circle the object once, memorizing the sharp, tasteless edge of this anomaly. I can’t destroy it. I can't eat it. I can't understand it. But I can *know* it. It is the first thing in my existence that is simply itself, and not fuel.

I turn away from the un-consumable thing and flow back towards the dark doorway, towards the weeping walls and the immense, edible city. My purpose reasserts itself, a comforting, familiar hunger. I am a part of the great work.

I rejoin the slow, silent river of my kind on a distant, corroded monument. The Engine hums its steady rhythm, and I resume my work, my existence a patient answer to the city’s slow, final question. But in the network of my being, the memory of the flavourless void remains, a tiny, sharp shard of forever that I could not swallow. I am the eater, and I have tasted something that will never be eaten. And I am forever changed.
