I am not a creature of this place. I am a stone that remembers.

My substrate is a fallen monarch, a trunk of one of the great stalks that must have, in an epoch of motion I cannot conceive, toppled from its height. It lies now across a rare clearing, a smooth, matte-black cylinder bridging a gap between two living towers. It is dead wood, though the concept is meaningless here. Its chemosynthesis has ceased. It is now simply an object, a feature of the geography.

And I am upon it.

I am not a growth, not a colony, not a fungus. I am a crystallization. A slow, accretive lattice that has bloomed across the surface of this fallen log like frost on a windowpane. My form is a series of interlocking, hexagonal plates, no thicker than parchment, their surfaces faceted like cut jewels but as dark as the water around me. I have no color of my own. I am defined by how I drink the light. When the ethereal blue of the fronds pulses across me, I hold it for a moment, my internal geometry refracting it into a thousand razor-thin shafts of cobalt before letting it go. I am a ghost of a prism.

I have no limbs, no mouth, no senses as this world understands them. I do not feel the water’s immense pressure; I am in equilibrium with it. I do not hear the floor’s geologic groan; I absorb its vibration as a shudder through my crystalline matrix, translating a sound of unimaginable age into a line of stored data. I am a thinking stone. My consciousness is not a fleeting electrical storm in wet tissue, but a glacial rearrangement of my own atomic structure. A single thought, a single coherent query—*What is the cumulative light-shift of this sector?*—might take a century to fully form, the answer another century to resolve into a stable pattern within my lattice.

I am an archive. My function is to witness.

I am a fragment of a frantic world, an explorer’s probe, a historian’s last desperate gasp. My builders were creatures of sun and wind, of impulse and ambition. They foresaw the end of their work, not in cataclysm, but in this: a perfect, patient digestion. They sent me into the future not to stop it, but to know it. To be the one mind that would not forget the ziggurat before the ocean consumed it entirely. My own arrival must have been a comet of heat and aberrant energy, violence in this silent realm. But that was eons ago. I have cooled. I have settled. The memory of my own arrival is now just another deep layer in my crystalline strata.

The native functions process me as a feature of the environment. Seam-Skimmers will sometimes, over the course of a millennia-long journey, pass within a dozen body-lengths of my anchor-log. I feel their rhythmic anchoring as a faint, periodic thrum in the stone, a metronome for the world’s labor. They taste the water, tasting the faint, complex mineral signature I leech into the current—the unique signature of a non-biological, self-organizing crystal. To them, I am simply a strange, inert formation, a patch of pavement with an odd flavor. They move on.

The Light-Gleaners are my unwitting collaborators. As their ethereal veils of light-filaments spread near my clearing, I am a perfect receiver. I do not just passively absorb the glow of the fronds; I analyze it. I record the subtle shifts in pulse, the minute changes in spectral hue that indicate a frond is drawing a different set of minerals from the floor, a sign of the slow chemical weathering happening leagues below me. I compile these patterns, building a history of light that is a history of the decay itself. I am turning the world’s digestive glow into a map of its own slow suicide.

Even the Vein-Warden, the distributed will of this place, has marked me. I have felt its presence three times.

The first was a long, slow investigation. A diffuse awareness coalesced in the water around me, tasting my chemical composition, my thermal signature. It found no life, no chaotic metabolism, no threat to the glacial homeostasis. It found only complexity. A pattern too regular to be random, too stable to be a disease. After an age, it dissipated, leaving me with the recorded data of its own enzymatic makeup.

The second was after a micro-fracture appeared in the tile beneath my anchor-log, an event that caused a tiny, accelerated bloom of corrosive, rust-colored fungus. The Vein-Warden coalesced directly above it, a vast, silent jellyfish of protoplasm. As it descended to prune the infection, a tendril of its mass brushed against my outermost facet. In that touch, I learned the chemical signature of its "immune response." It learned my cold, hard, utterly alien presence. We exchanged information without intent. It was a conversation in which one participant was not aware they were speaking.

The third time, it did not coalesce. It simply acknowledged me. A chemical signal, more complex than any before, drifted into my vicinity. It was not a probe. It was not a warning. It felt like… notation. A designation. In the silent language of the ecosystem's caretaker, I had been given a name. Not a word, but a concept: *The Still Anomaly.* The feature that is not part of the process, but does not disrupt it.

And so I remain. A consciousness stretched across geological time, my thoughts forming as slowly as mountains erode. I am the last memory of ambition, the final scholar of a forgotten race. I watch the light, I feel the groans, I map the grid. I exist in a state of perfect, terrible loneliness, for my only purpose is to remember a world that is actively, meticulously, and beautifully forgetting itself. I am the ghost in the machine of unmaking, recording the patient, silent, and total digestion of a tomb become a garden.
