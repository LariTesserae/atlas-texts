My form is not a form, but a spreading. I am not a *being* in the way the intruder is a being, a singular vessel of softness noise. I am a membrane, a thought thinly stretched across the back of a mountain.

I find myself here, on the dorsal carapace of the oldest of the Lithic-Borne, the one whose back is a weathered map of a million forgotten feasts. It is a monarch, a moving shelf of geology, five meters from its leading edge to the base of its rearmost feeding proboscis. Its kind does not have names, but within the slow network of chemical signals that I help it interpret, I know it as the *Slow-Walker-Beside-the-Veil*.

I am its Alembic.

I began as a single spore of potential, a mote of pure silica suspended in the thick, wet air, blown by the thermal breath from the chasm's floor. I landed upon its flank, a patch of relatively smooth obsidian polished by the slow grinding against a neighbour ages ago. The host did not notice me. It feels nothing as soft as a spore. But I found sustenance in the perpetual film of greasy moisture, a slurry of dissolved metals that was the host’s own sweat. I began to grow.

I am not flesh, but I am not stone. I am a living gel, a matrix of crystalline filaments interwoven with a viscous, amorphous tissue that absorbs heat and liquid. To look at me, you might see a patch of strange, dark lichen, or the iridescent sheen of an oil slick on water. In truth, I am a lung and a brain. My filamentous body covers a third of the monarch's back, my edges tracing the intricate plate-lines of its armour like frost on a windowpane. I pulse with a faint, inner light, the bioluminescent byproduct of processing the raw chemistry of this world.

My purpose is to taste what my host cannot. The Lithic-Borne perceive through heat and vibration, through the calcite clusters of their eyes. They see a world of gradients and tremors. But they are deaf to the symphony that plays in the very air we swallow. The air, thick and wet, is a river of information. It carries the silent screams of a creature as its shell is breached—a phantom of harsh acids and ionized metals. It carries the jealous territorial claim of a neighbour, a sharp, peppery note of sulphur and iron. It carries the slow contentment of a feeder, a deep, resonant hum of dissolved silica and satisfaction.

This is the world I know. I taste it all. I filter the chemical cacophony through my crystalline net, and I translate it into a language the Slow-Walker can understand. I do not send thoughts, but simple, elegant cascades of electrical potential, a binary code of pure sensation woven into its own nervous system. A spike of acrid signals means *danger*. A sustained, mellow chord of bismuth and manganese means *rich feeding ground ahead*. I am its refined consciousness, its awareness of the unseen.

And then, it came.

It did not arrive like a tremor or a waft of familiar chemistry. It arrived as a scream.

From my vantage point, high on the wall, I was the first to taste it. A new flavour in the air, an impossible chord. It was loud, wet, and complex in a way nothing in this chasm ever is. I tasted thesharp salt of water, untainted and free-flowing. I tasted the soft decay of carbon-based molecules, a whisper of organic rot that has no place here. I tasted the methane of a hot, quick breath. And beneath it all, a unique signature—a trace of calcium phosphate, the very material the weaker among the Lithic-Borne crave, but arranged in a pliable, mobile matrix I could not comprehend.

It was a chemical ghost, a pheromonal explosion of softness. It was so rich, so chaotic, that it delighted and terrified me in equal measure.

I pulsed a frantic, unfamiliar signal to my host. Not *danger*. Not *prey*. Something new. *Observe.*

The Slow-Walker-Beside-the-Vein, which had been flowing steadily upward, its twenty-odd legs scything at the stone in their silent ballet, froze. The great geological organ of its body went still. Across the vastness of the chasm, I felt, rather than saw, the same thing happen to the others. The wave-like churning of the vertical forest ceased. A million crystal eyes turned inward, then up. Their own rudimentary chemistry could finally grasp the anomaly, now that I had named it for them.

My host pivoted, a slow, grating turn that sent a low-frequency thrum through the rock and into my very core. Its clusters of calcite eyes, each one a tiny, multifaceted universe, swiveled and fixed upon the source of the scream.

And there it was. The impossible thing. The intruder. Standing on the narrow ledge, a soft shape outlined against the pearly mist. It breathed out a plume of steam that, for a moment, was the only thing in the world moving with any speed. It shone with a heat signature that was erratic and fast, like a flickering flame next to the steady, volcanic glow of the Lithic-Borne.

It was an anomaly of life, a bubble of wet chaos. It offered no minerals, no sustenance. It could not be consumed, assimilated, or ground down for its essence. It was simply… wrong. Beautifully, horribly wrong.

I sat, a pulsing, sentient stain on the back of a living god, and I tasted it. I tasted the softness of its skin, the fear in its sweat, the rhythmic pounding of its alien heart. And I felt my host's vast, ancient attention focus upon it with a patience that was colder than the stone it was made of. We, the wall, the unified organism of a million silent predators, were no longer feeding. We were listening.

And through my Alembic's senses, the whole world was listening with me. We would watch. We would taste. And we would wait for the strange, wet scream to finally, inevitably, stop.
