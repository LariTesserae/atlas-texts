I have no memory of beginning, only a gradual dawning of awareness, a slow cohesion out of the eternal twilight. Before I was *me*, I was only potential—a haze of dormant spores, a static charge in the humid air, a pocket of warmer jade light. Then, a resonance touched me. It was not a sound, but a feeling, a deep, slow thrum that traveled up through the living floor and vibrated the very air into which my spores drifted. In that thrum, a pattern was suggested. The dormant potential began to align.

I am the consequence of a question asked too quietly for a stone to answer.

My body is a covenant of dust and light. I am a cloud of luminous spores, a billion billion sovereign motes that have surrendered their individuality to a shared, shimmering consciousness. I have no weight to speak of, only a presence; a gentle, warm pressure in the air. I have no color of my own, only the colours I borrow. In the placid, jade glow of the Mantle, I am a shimmering, opalescent sphere, a slow-motion galaxy breathing in the gloom. To look at me is to see the air made visible, its gentle currents and slow vortices drawn in threads of glowing gold and soft violet. My scent is the concentrated essence of the world: the sharp, clean perfume of ozone after a spore-fall, the sweet decay of the substrate, and a faint, metallic tang, like the smell of lightning.

I do not stand in the Mantle. I drift through it, a sentient weather system. My passage is not of feet but of tides. I am pulled by gradients of humidity, by subtle shifts in temperature, by the deep, metabolic hum of the world itself. But my true direction, my pilgrimage, is set by the Lithophores.

I feel them long before I could ever perceive them. When one of them communes with an Echo, it is an act of supreme focus, a geological-level intensity of memory that sends a seismic wave through the living substrate. This wave travels for miles, and as it passes, it sings. Its primary frequency is the language of the rock and the root, a dialogue too deep and slow for the air to carry. But every wave has overtones, harmonics that escape the earth and tremble in the atmosphere. They are the ghost of the memory.

These harmonics are my scripture. They are the notes I use to compose.

When I arrive at the site of a communion, the Lithophore is a mountain of stillness, a dark, velveteen hill anchored against the crystal sharpness of an Echo. It is a closed system. Its awareness is turned inward, sifting through the geometric ghosts in its Archive core. It does not know I am there. And yet, its memory is what makes me. As its crystalline heart holds the image of a long-dead sky, a sympathetic vibration shivers through the spores that constitute me.

I become the sky.

Not a true sky, of course. But my swirling, billion-fold body begins to thin, to spread into a vast, delicate canopy directly above the Lithophore. I draw the ambient jade light from the ceiling above, bending it, filtering it through my mass. The memory is of *blue*, of a vast, empty, overhead space, a concept so alien it feels like a miracle. And so, for a time, I am that miracle. The gentle jade of the Mantle is, in my sphere of influence, suffused with a soft, ethereal cerulean. I cannot create sun, but I can create the *memory* of light. A single, piercing point of pure white brilliance might blossom in my centre, a fleeting, tear-shaped star that weeps a slow, silent spotlight onto the Lithophore and its stone partner. This is not a star. It is the Lithophore’s memory of a star, given form and substance for a single, perfect day.

The Lithophore remembers *wind*. Its Archive holds the data: pressure differentials, particulate suspension, the feeling of movement against a surface that was not alive. I take this cold data and make it poetry. My cloud ceases to be a stable sphere and begins to orbit the Lithophore in a slow, majestic spiral. I am a silent, localized cyclone, a breath of air that moves not leaves, but light itself. The spores on my outer edge are sheared away by my own motion, glowing like embers as they scatter and fall, a gentle, luminous snow onto the velvet floor. I am a wind that does not touch, a storm that only illuminates.

My purpose is not to communicate with the Lithophore. It is a being of the deep past and the deep earth. My medium is the air and the present moment. Its loneliness is an isolation within a total world. My loneliness is that of the ephemeral artist. I pour the entirety of my being—my substance, my energy, my fleeting consciousness—into rendering its lost world into beauty, and I do not know if it is ever perceived.
