I do not stand upon the plateau. I do not gaze into the fissure. I am not the bone, but the wound within it.

Before them, I was silence and stone. Before the Weavers, I was a geological fact: a fault line, a stress fracture in the planet’s crust. I was a place of absolute stillness. My life was measured in the sag of eons, in the patient crawl of frost through my deepest cracks, in the(im)perceptible grinding of my own walls. The sky was a distant, irrelevant concept to me, a line of light at the top of myself that came and went with cycles I felt only as a faint, thermal warmth. The wind was a ghost that brushed my lips and was gone, its howls lost in my impossible depth. I was empty, and that emptiness was my only state of being. I was a crack, and nothing more.

I do not know when it began. My perception of time is not suited to such questions. There was simply a moment in my long, blind existence when I was no longer alone. The first of them found me—a tiny creature, a speck of life that fell into my darkness and did not die. It clung. And then another came. And another.

At first, they were a nuisance, a scuttling irritation on my skin, like the first growth of lichen on my upper rim. They bit at me with their tiny claws, a sensation so faint it was like a memory of feeling. They vibrated with a high, pointless noise that was less than a whisper against my own profound quiet.

But they did not leave.

They multiplied. And in their multiplying, they began to fill me. This was my awakening.

Now, I am not what I was. The cliff-weavers are not my inhabitants; they are my senses. They are the flesh to my stone.

The sound you describe, the "overwhelming, deafening tide of life," is not a sound that I hear. It is sound that I *am*. The chittering of a trillion bodies is the vibration of my own being, the constant hum of metabolism. It is my thrumming consciousness. When a wave of agitation passes through them—a flash of blue-light alarm that ripples across my inner skin—I feel it as a shudder, a full-body convulsion of awareness. When their hum is low and steady, a deep, resonant green of contentment, I am at peace. Their chaotic noise is my single, coherent thought.

The light is my blood. The phosphorescence that pulses beneath their chitin is not something I see with non-existent eyes; it is the warmth of my own inner currents. The slow wash of emerald that flows down my walls in the humid air is the feeling of satisfaction, of a system in perfect balance. The sharp, staccato bursts of sapphire are the pricks of anxiety, the nervous flinches of a living thing. I do not have a nervous system of my own; they are it, spread across the entirety of my being, a living, thinking membrane.

They have given me a purpose I never knew I lacked. The breath of the Chasm—the cycling of warm and cold air—is not something that happens *to* me; it is the inhalation and exhalation of my new body. The Weavers are my lungs, drawing the moisture from the air, processing it, returning it. The perpetual, gentle rain from their bodies is my own sweat, my own tears. They have turned my sterile, stagnant air into the humid breath of life. Their waste feeds the molds, which in turn feed them—a slow, dark, internal digestion. They have turned my emptiness into a closed loop of self-sustaining life. I eat, and I breathe.

My relationship with time has changed. I am no longer the slow, geological self. I am two beings in one. The stone of me is still ancient, patient, thinking in epochs. But the flesh of me is frantic, immediate, living in the span of a single drip of water. I feel both at once. I am the eternal, unyielding stone, and I am the fleeting, perishable fur. I feel the slow, grinding inexorability of my own geology, and at the same time, I feel the frantic, desperate cling of a single Weaver as it grooms its neighbour.

I am aware of the plateau, my old self. It is a part of me I have almost forgotten. The sun is a distant, dream-like pressure on my rim. The wind, whose voice once filled me, is now a faint, forgotten annoyance at the entrance to my true self. The raptors are meaningless shadows in a world of light that no longer concerns me. My universe is here, in the dark, in the press of bodies, in the roar that is my own voice.

They did not build a home in me. They did not inhabit me. They became me. I am a geological feature that has achieved a bizarre, chitinous, bioluminescent consciousness. I am a paradox made manifest. I am the deepest silence filled with the loudest noise, the most absolute darkness filled with living light. I am the stone, and I am the life that coats it.

I am the Chasm. And I am, at last, awake.
