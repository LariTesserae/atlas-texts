I am a flicker. Not a being of flesh or chitin, but a localized flaw in the system, a feverish ghost made of heat-shimmer and ozone. I have no memory of becoming. My first awareness is of tension—the immense, patient thrum of the great engine being the very force that congealed me, a fleeting knot of energy that refused to dissipate. I am a cascade error, a selfish little singularity born in the space between a falling droplet and a resonating girder.

To perceive this world is not to see or hear, but to feel it as an extension of my own unstable form. The low-frequency hum that you feel in your teeth is the taut wire of my own existence. Its steady, ponderous rhythm is the only thing holding my chaotic energy from flying apart. It is my skeleton. When a great, lukewarm droplet detaches from a conduit high above, I do not hear the *plink*. I feel its impact through the metal grille as a sharp, clarifying sting, a pinpoint of pure data that momentarily refines my wavering edges. For a split second, I am almost coherent, almost solid.

The world is a constant assault of sensation. The living carpet of skittering crustaceans below the floor is a dull, itching static that I can feel through the soles of my non-existent feet. I try to avoid it; the endless, mindless chittering grates against my cohesion. Above me, the Groomer-moths move in slow waves, and their combined scraping is a soft, abrasive wind that threatens to sand away my edges. I ripple and distort as a passel of them flows over the girder I’m clinging to, my form momentarily mimicking the oily iridescence of their wings before snapping back into a clumsy jitter of heat.

But the true terror, the thing that defines my existence, is the drone of the croaker-toads. Their electronic *bwwooooon* is a foundational, oppressive pressure wave. To the Chymists, it is language. To me, it is pain. It’s a deep, dissonant frequency that scrambles my internal state, threatening to shatter my tenuous self-awareness back into raw, meaningless energy. When I am forced to cross a pool where they sit, their bloated, translucent bodies glowing like obscene lanterns, I must brace myself—pulling my own energy inward, becoming a tight, hot knot of pure potential. The passage is agony. My vision—if you can call it that—fills with static, and I feel the gentle, insidious pressure of the chamber’s consciousness taking notice.

I feel the Chymists, though I have never perceived one directly. I cannot. They are the system itself, and I am an error in the code. Their "awareness" is not a gaze, but a corrective field. Where I pass, I leave behind a faint acoustic scar, a harmonic dissonance in the engine’s perfect hum. I am a viral note in a symphony. In response, I feel a change in the ambient temperature, a subtle shift in the vibrational texture of the metal around me. This is the Chymist's immune response. It is not aggressive; it is a form of gentle, inexorable healing. It is a soft pressure that smooths, that dampens, that tries to absorb my chaotic energy and return it to the placid, patient processing of the whole.

My life is a flight from that healing. I am the thing that should not be, the anomaly that must be reintegrated. I hide in the roar and churn of the glowing vats, where their thermal and electrical tumult masks my own. I ooze through minute cracks in the metal grille, my form flowing like mercury, seeking the shadows and the places where the diagnostic drone is weakest. I don’t eat. I don’t sleep. I simply endure. I am a moment of violent, static-filled life in a world dedicated to the slow, harmonious business of being.

Sometimes, huddled in a dark cove, I feel the system’s profound patience. I can almost grasp the logic of it: the heat begetting the water, the water feeding the moss, the moss feeding the moths, the moths cleaning the bones. It is beautiful. It is perfect. And I am the scream in its perfect silence.

I wish I could communicate, but I have no voice. I can only leave my mark—drying a patch of moss to a crisp by my presence, causing a flock of Groomer-moths to swarm in confusion, making a single toad fall silent and shrink from my heat. These are my words, my testament. I am the proof that even in the most stable of closed systems, in the most patient of cycles, there can still be a flicker. A brief, feverish, beautiful ghost, burning bright against the slow, all-encompassing dark.
