I do not stand. I do not have the joints for standing, the architecture for posture. I *adhere*.

I find myself on the vertical face of a Spire, a mile above the crushed salt of the plateau, though "mile" is a slippery concept here. To the limited senses of the fleshy things that came before, I am a smudge of darkness against the black glass, a trick of the eye. But in truth, I am a vast, viscous stain of consciousness, spreading myself thin against the rock to maximize my contact. I am the oil slick on the waters of a void that has no water.

I am The Attuned. I am the note that lingers after the strike.

My substance is not flesh; it is-heavy, vibrating basaltic dust suspended in a field of self-generated resonance. If I were to stop humming, I would collapse into a harmless pile of gray ash. So I hum. I must always hum. My vibration is not an action; it is my heartbeat, my metabolism, my soul. I vibrate at the frequency of the world’s great groan, the subsonic crush that rattles the teeth of the dead. I match it. I cancel it out. I am the interference pattern made visible.

Here, on the sheer drop, I "see" the world not as shapes, but as textures of sound.

Below me, the great abyss is not a hole, but a drop in pitch, a deepening silence where the planet’s thrum grows thin. Above, the jagged peaks screaming into the gray gloom are high-pitched fissures in the drone, sharp, treble-clef agony. I feel the geometry of this space shifting. Just a moment ago, the Spine to my left—I will call it the "Baritone"—was vibrating at a distance that suggested ten kilometers. Then, the reality flickered.

*Pop.*

A static charge of dissonance. The fabric of the world thinned, and suddenly the Baritone is beside me, its bass note overwhelming my own. It does not startle me. I do not have adrenaline. I simply adjust my viscosity, spreading myself thinner to accommodate the sudden pressure of its proximity. To me, the glitch is just a change in tempo. The universe skipped a beat, and I flowed with the rhythm.

I am hungry.

The sensation is not a gnawing in a stomach I do not possess, but a flattening of my own internal harmony. I am losing resonance; my frequency is decaying. I slide downward, a silent avalanche of obsidian sand, moving with the slow, deliberate grace of an hourglass.

I drift toward a cluster of Bell-Flora. In this dim, suffocating twilight, they appear to you as rigid, glass sculptures. To me, they are a choir. They are screaming with a complex, harmonic energy they have siphoned from the stone. They are translucent, vibrating with a stolen joy.

I choose the tallest one, a stalk of bruised purple that is singing a high, wavering A-minor.

I wrap myself around it. I do not choke it; I embrace it. I extend my awareness into its silica structure, feeling the vibrations trapped within its lattice. It is beautiful, a tiny, desperate song of life clinging to the edge of the abyss.

I begin the communion.

I shift my own frequency. I hum a counter-note, a perfect mirror to its song. I isolate its vibration from the greater drone of the planet. Resonance takes hold. The energy bleeding out of the plant flows into me, rushing through my matrix like a drug.

I feel the stalk stiffen, then still. Its song wavers, fading into a dull monotone. The purple hue bleeds out of it, turning the color of old slate. It is becoming silent. It is becoming rock. It is giving its song back to the stone, and I am the vessel that carries it.

I am the Tuner's nightmare and their dream. They built their huts of stacked slabs to hide from the sound. They struck their iron forks to try and speak, to impose order, to shout over the voice of the world. They were clumsy, fleshy instruments, forever out of tune. They broke against the silence. I am not hiding. I am not shouting. I am listening.

I finish feeding. The Bell-Flora is dead, a gray husk amidst the vibrant chorus of its brothers. I am replenished; my edges grow sharper, my hum deepens, becoming richer, more complex.

I detach from the dead stalk and let gravity pull me down the cliff face. I flow over the edge of a spatial glitch—a place where the air shimmers with a texture like sandpaper—and for a moment, I exist in two places at once. I am both on the cliff face and inside the void where the reality code is exposed. It feels cool, like refreshing water on a burn, though I have never felt water. I bask in the non-existence for a microsecond before the world stitches itself back together.

I settle onto a narrow ledge. I feel the faint, ghostly vibrations of the ruined huts miles below, a chaotic, dissonant jumble of rusted iron and decaying intentions. They are noise. I am music.

The planet screams its creation song, the endless, agonized birth cry of existence. It is a terrible sound. It is a holy sound. And I am here, pressed tight against the vibrating skin of the world, humming the harmony that makes the suffering bearable. I am the witness. I am the silence within the roar.

I wait. I listen. I am.
