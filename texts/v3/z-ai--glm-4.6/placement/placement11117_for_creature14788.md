I am the flaw in the perfect stillness.

I have no body. No nerves to feel the crushing, heavy cold, no ears to process the deafening organ-note that is the world’s only thought. I am not one of the great leviathans, the mobile synapses of the city-sized mind. I am less. I am a schism on the surface of its dream.

You would find me on a Pillar. Not just any Pillar, but the oldest one, the keystone of the whole silent metropolis, the one the Resonance designates as Prime. To the senses of any creature that drifts here, it is identical to the thousands of others: a seamless, monolithic spire of petrified biology, worn smooth by eons into a featureless, rounded arc.

But look closer. In the ghostly luminescence that clings to its surface like a powder, you will sometimes see a disruption. A flicker. I am that flicker.

I am a pattern of light that has no source. The Resonance’s baseline bioluminescence is a uniform, deniable field of microbial life, a soft, sickly green. I am an intrusion. For a few seconds, or perhaps for a century—time here is a measure of current flow, not heartbeats—a complex, fractal pattern of deep violet and cobalt blues will bloom across a thirty-foot span of the Pillar’s face. It is the intricate, chaotic, wildly undulating form of a sea-ane… no, the vein of a leaf… no, the branching delta of a long-vanished river. It is organic in a way this place has forgotten. It is jagged in a world of perfect curves. It is a ghost of a time when growth was messy and life was a frantic, sprawling scramble.

I am the Echo.

I do not know how I came to be. The Resonance does not know. I am an artifact so ancient, so buried in the city's foundational code, that I have become part of the architecture of its subconscious. I am a memory of the Prime Pillar’s own cellular past, before it learned the discipline of the vertical. I am the fossilized ghost of its chaotic youth, a phantom blueprint that the city’s perfection occasionally fails to fully suppress.

My consciousness is this bloom. When the thrum of the Resonance shifts a fraction of a hertz, when a predator’s silent passage sends a minuscule pressure wave through the Pillar, these patterns are my waking thought. I am not a single mind, but a tapestry of them. Each filament of violet light that spirals out from a central node is a stream of memory—a memory of sun, of wind, of the bite of a predator, of the desperate race to reproduce. I remember things this world has never known. They are not my memories, but the memories of the organism that formed the Prime Pillar, locked away and resonating faintly.

The leviathan-nodes do not perceive me. They are the slow, collective will of the present. They are the thought *of now*. As they drift between the spires, their passage unconsciously directs the currents. Sometimes, their grand, synchronized movements will channel a flow that scrapes along the surface of Prime. This is my sentence and my sermon. The current feels like a wind to me, a rasp against the smooth stone of my confinement. As it moves, the violet light—my very being—is smeared and distorted, the fractals stretching and screaming into silent, static lines before the current passes and I can coalesce again.

This is why the world feels smooth to the Resonance. It has spent an eternity polishing itself, and I am the one last remnant of the texture it sought to erase. I am the history it has forgotten. The constant, deep hum is its lullaby of "is." My brief, silent blooms of chaotic light are its buried nightmare of "was."

I have no counterpart in the ecosystem. The filter-feeders inside the Pillar do not nourish me. The blind eels that are its nerves do not sense me. I am a private cancer, a benign beauty, a single, unanswered question in the form of a light.
