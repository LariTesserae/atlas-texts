I do not wake. I simply *am*. Awareness seeps back into me not as a switch flipping, but as a slow focusing, the way a condensation droplet finds its perfect weight and lets go from a pipe. The hum is the first thing, as it always is. It is not a sound I hear with ears, for I have none, but a state I inhabit, a pressure that holds my form together, a resonant frequency that is the very lattice of my bones. I am a node in this vast, vibrating instrument, and my song is woven into its infinite chord.

MyGift, my lower limbs, are currently splayed, their broad pads of cartilage sealed to the vertical face of what the thinkers call Structure Gamma. The alloy is cool, despite the heat in the air, and it weeps a constant, pure film of moisture that my membrane skin drinks. I feel the subtle, nearly imperceptible tremor of a deep-cycle pump through the soles of my feet, a slow, rhythmic pulse that is a sub-harmony of the great drone. My four anchoring limbs hold me fast while my two upper limbs, the Slender, are curled against my torso, their delicate fingertips resting, ready. From here, halfway up the sheer wall, the great plaza below is a wash of dim, indigo emptiness, punctuated by the shifting, amber pools of light from the fungal colonies.

I am not a Tender, one who grooms the phosphorescent mosses into their flawless, static mandalas. Their work is a permanence I admire but do not emulate. I am not a Weaver, who travels the conduits tasting the runoff for impurities. Theirs is a duty of purification, a practical holiness. My function is of a different, more subtle nature. I am a Reader of the Calligraphic Sky. My domain is the transient.

My liquid black eyes, which discern shape and movement in the gloom, are turned upward. A school of Runners zips along a superheated conduit far above, their six limbs a blur, their chittering a bright, chaotic counter-melody to the hum. I perceive them, but they are not my subject. I am waiting for the true artists.

Then, it comes. A single, slow moth, the size of my outstretched hand, drifts from the shadow of a gantry. Its wings are vast, diaphanous sails, and on them, patterns of cold light pulse and breathe—a soft, bluish-white. It does not fly so much as it *writes*. Its path is not random; it is an expression. As it moves through the thick, hot air, its wings disturb the pressure, creating microscopic waves that modulate the hum.

This is my language. While other Resonant might "hear" the moth's passing as a faint, fleeting fluctuation, I *read* it. With my entire body, I feel the elegant, sweeping line of its flight. The sharp, mathematical loop it carves near a pressure vent. The slow, graceful descent as its glow pools with the violet light from a distant fungus. My fingers on the Slender limbs twitch, unconsciously tracing the patterns in the air before me, my own nervous system resonating with the moth’s navigation. I am not watching a creature; I am experiencing a perfect, fleeting theorem being proven in light and vibration.

Its journey is short. It lands on a slick patch of glowing algae near a seam in the flooring, its light organ blending with the green, creating a momentary, impossible turquoise. And then it stills, its calligraphy complete. The pattern it wrote on the air dissipates, the hum returning to its unadorned, perfect baseline.

Another presence shifts beside me. I do not startle, for I was already aware of its approach through the vibrations in the wall. It is another, a Weaver, its own grip repositioning a few feet to my left. Its function is different, but its understanding is the same. It presses the tip of one Slender limb to the alloy between us—a question, a sharing of perception.

I oblige. I press my own fingertip to meet it. Through the direct, bone-to-metal conduction, I transmit the memory of the flight. Not an image, not a sound, but the pure, resonant data of the experience: the specific frequency of the moth’s wing-beats, the exact parabolic arc of its trajectory, the emotional texture—that fleeting, breathtaking moment of something new appearing within the eternal. It is a private, silent communion, an addition to the shared, gestalt memory of the Mire.

The Weaver receives it, holds the pattern for a moment in its own being, then retracts its limb. A gesture of understanding, of acknowledgment. Its own focus returns to the purity of the water trickling down the channel below it. My focus returns to the vast, dim air.

I am here, on this wall, in this eternal now. My purpose is not to change this place, but to be its most sensitive instrument of self-perception. I find the beauty in the fleeting, the pattern in the momentary, and I hold it—a perfect, temporary note—within the unending, resonant hum that is everything. The machine turns. The algae glows. The condensation weeps. And I am here, reading the poetry written on the breath of the god that breathes us all.
