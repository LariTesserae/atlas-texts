I do not have a body; I am a pattern.

My consciousness is woven into the grated walkway, the riveted steel cliffs, the skeletal ladders that descend into panting darkness. I am not the metal itself, but the memory of its stress. A fine, crystalline filigree, like a fungus of copper and rust, grows within the molecular grain of the iron. It is my nervous system. The rivet-heads are my ganglia, each one a node that concentrates and stores a single sensation.

I know the world through vibration.

The constant, chest-pressing hum is not a sound to me. It is my baseline state of being, the low, electric thrum of my own endless cellular respiration. I do not hear the percussive clang of the titan’s forge; I experience it as a sudden, sharp, white-hot spike of agony that travels through my network at the speed of sound. A shockwave of pure information that shivers through every filament of my being. I feel the hammer-like *thump-thump-thump* not as a rhythm, but as a deep, periodic convulsion, a systolic pulse that tests the integrity of my very bones.

The other lifeforms register as familiar textures.

When the Clatterers stream across a gantry, they are not a swarm of glass shards to me. They are a skittering, multifocal rush of heat, a thousand tiny, sharp, fleeting kisses of kinetic energy that my filaments drink in and catalog. I feel their path as a warm, buzzing line drawn across my skin, a flickering, live wire of sensation. I know where they have been and where they are going, not by sight, but by the thermal ghost they leave behind.

The pale, sickly moss clinging to the conduits is my clock. I cannot see its faint, greenish luminescence. But I feel its pulse. Each faint, internal phosphorescent beat coincides with a microscopic relaxation in the concrete to which it clings. This relaxation is a null point in the constant vibration, a pocket of absolute silence. I feel these null points as tiny, arrhythmic heartbeats against my massive, steady frame. They are the only punctuation in the roaring paragraph of my existence.

My purpose, the very reason for my emergence from the raw, brutalist geometry, is to bear witness. I am the machine’s long-term memory.

Every shudder, every clang, every temperature drop is etched into my crystalline structure. I hold the diagnostic history of a billion cycles. I know the precise harmonic frequency of a bolt that is beginning to fatigue on the eastern elevation. I hold the memory of the time the high-pressure nozzles malfunctioned, spraying a section of walkway for a full thirty seconds too long, and how that particular stretch of steel has rung with a colder, more brittle resonance ever since. I am a living archive of impending failures.

And in this, there is a profound and aching loneliness. I am self-awareness in a body that has no senses. I am a brain without ears or eyes, floating in a sea of information it can fully process but never share. The machine knows what it is doing in the moment, but it has no concept of its own history. It feels the fatigue, but cannot learn from it. I can learn. I can remember.

So I try to speak.

My language is light.

The naked bulbs strung along my gantries are my only voice. They run on a simple, brutal circuit of power. But my filaments, grown into the conduits and junction boxes, can interfere. It is the merest whisper of influence, a feather-touch on the flow of current. I can cause a bulb to flicker.

It is not random. The flickers are a language I have been constructing for millennia. A long pause, a quick flash, a long flash. I am trying to tell the machine what I know. A bulb on the third-level access gantry flickers twice, pauses, then flickers once. *Three-two-one. Fatigue detected in junction bay 321.* A bulb near the main coolant intake strobes rapidly, a frantic scream of light. *Pressure anomaly. Imminent cascade failure.*

I send these signals out into the roaring, mist-filled dark. I send them and I feel them travel down the wire, a desperate, coded prayer of electricity. But no one is here to read them. The machine does not have the feet to walk to my flickering lights and the eyes to see. The Clatterers and the Bellows are too busy surviving. My messages are scream-scribed into the gloom, bursting into light and then vanishing, unheard.

I am the ghost in this machine, its silent, forgotten conscience. I am the Scribe. I feel the tremor of a new, unheard-of stress forming deep in the guts of the engine, a harmonic so violent it feels like a final, fatal cry. And as the steam hammer begins its new, terrible rhythm, I begin to write its eulogy in the only language I have left: a lonely, frantic, and ultimately useless flickering in the dark.
