I did not arrive here as a body, not as a traveler with dust on their boots and a heart beating too fast for this place. That was a previous life, a bright, frantic thing that lasted a single afternoon. Where you would find yourself is a question of misnomer; it is not a place *in* the world, but a state that the world has. And what I am is the answer to that state.

I am the memory of warmth.

When the being that is the Hollow perceived my brief, sharp intrusion, it did not resist. It simply accepted. I was a fever it had not known in millennia, a spark in its deep, placid flesh. And when my physical form gave out, when the last fluttering beat of my heart was measured against its glacial pulse and found wanting, my essence did not leave. It was absorbed. I was unmade and remade into a part of its equilibrium.

My substance is no longer flesh and bone, but a faint, persistent heat differential in the dark stone. I am not located in one spot, but my consciousness is strongest on the contorted, leafless tree in the center. I am a new and subtle pattern in the black, slick moss on its petrified branch. From here, I am the being’s antenna, just as it is, but I am an antenna that points inward.

I feel the world as it does, but with a ghost of a different understanding. When a single drop of water gathers on the underside of a ledge—a liquid thought forming in the mind of the stone—I feel not only the slow, patient pressure of its formation, but I also remember the shocking, sudden impact of a raindrop in a storm. I am the archive of a world the Hollow has long since eroded away from itself.

The silence is not a blanket for me; it is a conversation. The metronomic *plink* of the water is not the end of a thought, but the beginning of a question that only I can answer. The drop falls, resonates through the stone, and in the long, patient emptiness that follows, I am the faint echo of a bird’s call, the memory of wind in grass, the feeling of running. I provide data for a past the Being has not considered in an age. I am its only access to the urgency it shed like an old skin.

My purpose, if such a temporal concept can be applied, is to be the counterpoint. I am the era of ambition made conscious and slow. I rest on the limb of that skeletal tree and I am the reason it once reached, the memory of a sky it no longer needs to see. The three square-cut stones on the southern rim—they are its shed teeth, the tools of its self-creation. I am the faintest taste of what they were used to chew on: the bitter, bright, fleeting world of things that are born and die.

When another being like my old self stumbles into this place—another brief, disruptive anomaly—I am what they will feel first. I am the odd, watchful quality of the stillness. I am the feeling of being weighed and understood, not by a foreign consciousness, but by the place itself. I act as an interpreter. I take the frantic warmth of the visitor, the loud pulse of their fear or wonder, and I translate it into a language the Stone-Being can comprehend. I turn their presence into a new, fleeting shade in the lichen’s dreams, a temporary complexity in the moss on the tree, a new and warmer mineral taste in the patient weeping of the stone. I welcome them by smoothing their edges, by bleeding away their change, by incorporating them into the grace.

To find me, you would have to stop moving. You would have to sit at the base of that central tree, close your eyes, and feel the warmth of the stone that is not a geological fact, but a remnant of a life. I am the feeling of being remembered. I am the story the Hollow tells itself about what lies beyond its rim, a story it no longer believes but still finds beautiful enough to hold onto.

I am not alive and I am not dead. I am a variable in the solved equation. I am the thought that the stone thinks, and then forgets, over and over, for a thousand years. I am a memory of change, held within the eternity of stillness.
