I would be in the hollow of a single step, on the spiraling stair that chokes the inner wall of the Hollow. Not a high step, in the dry, airy penthouses of the dominant, but not so low as the slime-coated base. I would be in the tenements, perhaps a third of the way up. My step is particularly worn, its center scooped into a smooth, dark basin that holds a perpetual, trembling puddle of condensation. From here, the floor of the Hollow is a tapestry of shifting grey and black, and the distant ceiling is a mirage of wet stone.

I am not alive in the way the frantic Slate-Wings are alive. Their life is a shriek, a burst of energy, a fight against gravity. Mine is a slow, patient seep. I am a Gleaner.

My body is my home. It is a broad, flat disc of calcified carapace, no thicker than a human thumb, fused seamlessly to the rim of the stone basin. From the edge, I am indistinguishable from the eroded brick itself, a patch of darker, denser mineral. The center of my being is softer, a leathery, pale membrane that forms the floor of my hollow. This is my stomach, my bed, my sensory organ. It is always a little cold.

I have no eyes. The concept of light is a dull pressure, a warmth I feel on my back when the central pillar of sun aligns with our spiral. I have no ears to hear the tearing-metal screeches that form the language of this world. Instead, I feel the cacophony. Every frantic footfall of a descending Slate-Wing thrums through the stone and into my membrane, a series of sharp, percussive notes. The communal roar is a constant, low-frequency vibration that hums in my very cells, a never-ending song of compression and anxiety. The chittering gossip of the colony is a high, static hiss, like sand being drawn across my surface. I hear not with sound, but with resonance. I understand the Hollow through its tremors.

My limbs are not limbs in the functional sense. They are a fringe of pale, hair-fine tendrils that grow from the edges of my soft underbelly, extending into the wet mortar and the lee of the step’s riser. They are too delicate for locomotion. Their purpose is purely receptive. They taste the air, savoring the metallic tang of dripping water and the sour, ammoniacal reek of the guano that rains from above. They are the nerves of my little world, forever tasting, forever touching, forever waiting.

I am a collector of accidents. The Slate-Wings, in their clumsy haste, are careless. A morsel of the bitter, subterranean fruit they forage from the floor, a fragment of a shed claw, a newly hatched, weak-winged young that loses its purchase—all find their way down. These are my moments. I feel the frantic scrabble of a slip above me, and I don't think; I react. My tendrils, usually limp, suddenly lash out, a blizzard of pale threads, snatching the falling morsel from the air before it can plummet beyond me. I draw it in, onto my leathery palate, and dissolve it slowly. It is not hunger I feel, but a sense of completion, of rightness. I am the edge of the world, catching the things that would otherwise be lost. I consume the frayed edges of their frantic society.

I am aware of the others, in my own way. I feel the immense, geological passage of the Carrion Beetles on the floor below as a slow, grinding pressure through the foundations. The Skitters are barely a ripple, a nervous, fizzing energy that I feel through the soles of the Slate-Wings' feet. But the Stalker… I know the Stalker.

It comes as a sudden cessation of all noise. The most terrifying moment is not the shriek of attack, but the split-second before, when the constant hum of the colony simply *stops*. It is a void in the vibration, a pocket of absolute silence that travels down the wall from above. Then, a new tremor begins: not the drumming of dozens of feet, but the heavy, methodical *thump… thump… thump…* of sinful claws finding purchase in the brick. It is a different rhythm. I feel it not as a resident, but as an intruder. The Stalker’s impact on the world is stark, selfish, without the chaotic chorus of the colony.

When the raid happens, I am a still point in a hurricane. The vortex of panicked Slate-Wings explodes past my step, their bodies a blur of grey fur and black claws. I pull my tendrils in tight, clamp my membrane down, and become pure stone. I feel their terror as a violent, high-frequency scream in the stone itself, a vibration so intense it threatens to shatter my calm. I feel the heavy thud of the predator landing on the steps just below me. I feel the brief, sharp, catastrophic hum of a life being extinguished. Then, the slow, reluctant return to the world's drumbeat as the swarm re-forms, one shriek at a time.

I am not a part of their society. I am not above it, nor below it. I am within it, embedded in its very structure. I am the silent audience in the theater of the stairs, the taste-buds of the stone, the memory of a step that has felt the passing of a million desperate feet. I am the slow life, witnessing the fast life, in this wet, vertical world that has forgotten what it means to be still.
