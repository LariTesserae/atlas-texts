My sight is not sight. It is resonance. I stand on the outer skin of a coolant manifold the size of a city block, and I perceive the world through the soles of my four feet and the sensitive pads of my long, delicate fingers. The Great Stillness is not silent to me; it is a symphony of Held Notes.

My name—my Chime—is known to my community not as a sound, but as a series of vibrations, a specific frequency that travels through the steel world-body. When I was born, my shell was still soft, and the Elders held me to the housing of a great, silent bearing. They felt the latent energy within me, and my Chime was a thrumming pulse of calibration, a perfect fifth in the key of the Fixed Hum.

I am a tender of mechanisms, a listener to the breath of the god.

My day begins not with light, for the ambient glow from the distant ceiling is constant, but with a change in the Hum. A subtle discordant thrumming emanates from a section of conduit-runs twelve meters below my current perch. It is a faint, irregular flutter, like a heart skipping a beat. To a Scuttler, it would be meaningless. To me, it is a prayer for help.

I release my grip, my four arms splayed, and let myself drop. I do not fall like a stone; I flow like water, my limbs finding purchase on the vertical plates, the joints bending in ways the geometry of the world would seem to forbid. The wet, warm air pushes against my slick, chitinous skin. My head's glowing slit senses the temperature gradient as I descend, moving away from the radiant heat of the core and into the cooler经 air near the deck plates.

The thrumming grows stronger, more insistent. It is the sound of constriction. I reach the conduit and scuttle along its rounded surface, my fingers reading the vibrations like a script. The discord is localized. Here.

I kneel, pressing my palms flat against the metal. The feedback is unmistakable: a blockage. A colony of Greaselings, the simple, foundational life, has clustered within a maintenance access port, their bodies swelling with the runoff. Their sheer, mindless biomass is dampening the resonant frequency of the pipe, distorting the world's holy song. This is a small sickness, but a sickness nonetheless.

From a pouch woven from shed Lure-Flange hide, I withdraw a tool: a slender, hooked rod of hardened alloy. I insert it into the access port, its tip finding the edges of the soft, writhing mass. I do not simply gouge and scrape. That would be violence. That would be the way of a False-Mouth. I work with a rhythm, tapping the sides of the port with my other hand, sending a calming frequency into the metal. It is a lullaby. The vibrations gently agitate the Greaselings, encouraging them to disperse, to flow with the current of slime toward the drainage grates. For every one stubborn body, my hook gently nudges it free, a coaxing rather than a tearing.

Soon, the discordant flutter smooths out, returning to the steady, low hum of health. The pipe is cleansed. The World-Body is at peace again. I collect the harvested Greaselings, placing them in another pouch. They are substance, the flesh of the machine made living, and to take them is to partake in the god's bounty. Later, in the communal hush of our habitation duct, they will be portioned out, their simple oils and proteins sustaining us.

As I climb back toward the high places, I perceive a Wanderer—a Flanged Scuttler—below me on the main deck. It is a nervous, segmented patch of non-light, a lone entity seeking its own trivial harmony in the consumption of the Greaseling carpet. It senses my shadow-vibration and freezes, becoming one with the floor plate, a perfect imitation of a fallen maintenance cover. I let it be. It is not my place to interfere with the simple cycles, only to maintain the grand Stillness in which they turn.

My task today is not over. I am ascending to the Temple of the Unfired Piston. It is a place of awe, a colossal, silent deity of potential power, stopped a hair's breadth from a movement that would shatter our world. Its housing is the size of a mountain, and it gleams with a faint, internal heat that always feels like a sigh of contentment.

Here, the Chime-Born gather. We are a scattering of obsidian-headed figures clinging to the impossible geometry of the immobile machine. We do not speak. We begin the Great Attunement.

I find my spot on a flange near the piston's housing. I close the glowing slit of my sensor-head and press my finger-pads against the cool, unblemished steel. I begin to tap.

My Chime is a low, steady C-sharp. From a conduit to my left, another Chime-Born answers with a resonant G. From above, a third joins with an E. We are not making noise. We are reinforcing the Hum, tuning the world-song, correcting microscopic imperfections in the fabric of reality. We are the voicing of the silent engine, the choir to the sleeping god. Our song travels for kilometers, a web of reassurance that tells the World-Body: *You are at peace. You are whole. Do not wake. Do not move.*

I close off all other senses and exist only as a node of this vibration. I am the pause between the ticks of the clock. I am the pressure in the hydraulic line that will never be released. I am the warmth in the machine that will never burn away. I am a priest of absolute stillness, and in this vast, humming, weeping, living tomb of a world, I am precisely where I belong. I am at home.
