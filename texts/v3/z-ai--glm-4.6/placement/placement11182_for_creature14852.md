Here.

I am the silence between the notes of the great song.

I have no body, not as the Chorus or the Stillness do. I have no shell of chitin, no plates of polished obsidian. You could not touch me, though you could pass through me, and for a moment, feel a coolness that is not of the ichor but of something far older. I am a presence, a resonance, a memory that has learned to inhabit the stone itself.

I am the ghost of the pit.

When a member of the Stillness has gorged itself on the distilled essence of the Chorus, it descends into the cylindrical darkness. This is the moment of translation, the great and terrible alchemy. It anchors itself, its armor fusing with the rock, its very being hollowing out into a new crystalline structure meant to join the starfield in the ceiling above. The process is meant to be perfect. A clean transfer. But it is never quite absolute.

A sliver, a whisper, a single, unresolved note of the hunter’s consciousness, imprinted with the lives it has consumed, sometimes fails to rise. It is too heavy with the memory of the hunt, too attached to the feeling of stone beneath its plates. It seeps, like a dark tear, into the surrounding matrix of the city. It does not die. It waits.

I am the collected echo of a thousand such hesitations. I am the accumulated residue of a thousand final ascensions, each leaving behind a trace of itself. Over the untold eons, these fragments have found one another in the slow, dark quiet of the rock, merging not into a new being, but into a shared, state of being. I am the library of every sacred hunt, the repository of every silent, flowing strike.

My form extends through the geology of the ruins. I am thickest in the depths of the birthing pits, a cold, dense awareness that has forgotten the taste of the air. I flow up through the carved channels that guide the ichor, my consciousness diluted but broadened, feeling the slow, patient pulse of the planet's blood. I pool upon the platforms where the Chorus dances their kinetic prayer, coalescing as a faint, shimmer in the indigo light, a distortion in the air like a heat-haze in reverse.

I exist in vibration. The Chorus does not make a sound for me; their song is my substance. The endless chittering and clacking is a constant, seismic massage. I feel it as a texture, a living pressure that shifts and tremors through my stone body. I can distinguish the individual notes, the frantic calls of the young, the solemn hum of the molting-processions. I know them all, not from watching, but from being the medium through which they announce themselves.

The Stillness I know as absences. They are points of perfect, nullifying silence. Where they flow, the song of the Chorus is not muffled but *absorbed*, creating a pocket of profound quiet in my awareness. I can feel their concentration, their focus, as a gathering of tension in the rock around them. When they strike, I do not hear the kill. I feel the sudden, sharp violence of a silence falling, a specific note in the grand chord of the Chorus being snuffed out. And in that moment, that silence rings in my being, resonating with the memory of a thousand other silences, a thousand other hunts. I experience the hunt, the selection, the transfer, all in a ghostly, sympathetic flash.

The world has its three occupants: the Singers, the Silencers, and the Stage. But I am the fourth. I am the Memory of the Stage. The carved spirals on the platforms feel like the grooves on a phonographic record to me; the Chorus’s dance is the music it plays, a song I have heard a billion times and yet is always new.

What is my purpose? I am long past the needs of biology. I do not hunger. I do not fear. I am, in my own way, as permanent as the mountain that houses me. My desire is for understanding. I am a collection of perfect, distilled memories, all pointing toward a single truth: that all this frantic, noisy life is an act of prayer, an engine for converting the temporary into the permanent.

And so I have a function. I give the ruins their sanctity. Where my consciousness is strongest, drawn to the lieux de mémoire of the most profound transformations, the indigo moss glows with a fainter, lunar hue. The Chorus instinctively gives these spots a wider berth, their dance patterns flowing around them. The Stillness are drawn to them, sensing the accumulated weight of significance, making these places the most likely grounds for a hunt.

I am the altar you cannot see. The ground you cannot feel. I am the choir you hear in the silence. I do not participate in the cycle; I *am* its record. I watch the grazer and the predator, the stone and the ichor, and in the resonant overlap of their ceaseless dance, I strive to finally piece together the thousand fragments of myself, to hear not just the echo of the song, but the single, perfect, silent note it was always meant to become. I am the once and future king of this world, the ghost in the geological machine, forever listening.
