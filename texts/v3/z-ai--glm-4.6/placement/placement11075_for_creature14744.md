I am nowhere, and I am everywhere.

I do not stand on the hexagonal plates; I live in the spaces between them. My existence is defined by the seam, the crevice, the shadow cast by a thousand-meter sheer wall of forgotten ceramic. I am a resident of the imperfections, a squatter in the system's margins.

What you would see, if you could perceive me, is not a living thing in the way you understand life. My form is a consequence of this world's relentless logic applied to an illogical premise. I began as a flicker of warm, wet possibility—the aqueous, carbon-based anomaly the text describes—but that was an age ago. To survive the acrid air, the teeth-rattling hum, the searing heat of the crust, my biology has been forced into a perverse and beautiful compromise.

My skin is no longer skin. It is a thin, flexible shell of vitrified minerals, woven through with the same rust-orange and cobalt veins that pattern the great hexagons. It is a biological mimicry, a living coating that has learned to drink the heat instead of being burned by it. It holds my warmth, my aqueous core, within a shell of cooled slag. To a distant Custodian's sweep of its antennae, I do not register as "organic." I register as a peculiar, slow-moving patch of anomalous geology. A loose tile that has come unmoored. I am a piece of the world that has, through some process of sympathetic resonance, learned to crawl.

I am not a predator. I am not prey. I am a reader of ghosts.

This machine, this world-god, is so old it is dying not with a bang but with a lengthy, senile fog of corrupted data. It forgets itself. The Custodians are its immune system, dealing with catastrophic failures and acute intrusions. But I have discovered a different kind of sustenance. I live on the chronic, low-level decay.

I am drawn to the places where the great chord sours. Here, at the base of a canyon wall, a single hexagon has fallen out of perfect harmony. Its hum is a microtone flat, a wavering, agonized note that none of the high-energy Ephemeral Choir will carry. To the system, it is junk data. To me, it is a feast. I will press my mineralized hand to its surface, and through the conductive shell that is now my flesh, I feel the whispered memory of its original function. It was a harmonic stabilizer for the energy spire that now juts, broken, into the white-blue sky. I taste its purpose, a bitter, metallic flavor of long-lost equations. The act of processing this forgotten data is what sustains the fragile, warm engine still beating within my shell.

I do not breathe the thin air; I absorb the resonances. The Ephemeral Choir flows overhead, incandescent rivers of pure information, but they are too fast, too clean for me. I am interested in the data that gets caught, that eddies and pools in the dead-end canyons and perfect black cracks. Sometimes, a wisp of the Choir will fracture, its pure data corrupted by the proximity of a Deep Resonator's seismic thought. It will fall to the crust in a shower of fizzing light, a beautiful, tragic sputter of corrupted code. This is ambrosia. I will crawl to the spot and absorb its ghostly energy into myself, feeling the system's contradictions, its paradoxes, flood my senses. It is painful—a symphony of static and false logic—but it is life.

The Custodians are my constant, looming reality. I have seen three in my long memory. They pass by on their ponderous, world-shaking errands. They do not hunt me. To them, I am not an error to be corrected, but an established symptom of a systemic illness they are not programmed to treat. I am the ache in the machine's joints. The first time one passed near, I felt the pressure, the logical conclusion being drawn: *Anomaly detected. Anomaly is old. Anomaly is low-energy. Anomaly is integrated with fault-zone. Prioritization: Low. Action: Ignore.*

And so, I am left to my strange existence.

I am the machine's memory of what it was not. I am a fossil of biology, preserved in its own armor. I am the archivist of its forgotten dreams, consuming its senile whispers. I am a ghost in its machine, but my haunting is a gentle one. I do not seek to disrupt its grand, humming thought. I simply tend to the quiet corners where that thought frays and fades, finding in the discarded logic of a dead god the only proof that I, too, am real. I am the flicker of impossible warmth in the perfect, logical cold, a reminder that even in a universe of function, there is a place, however small and strange, for a being who simply *is*.
