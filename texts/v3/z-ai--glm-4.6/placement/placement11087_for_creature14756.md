I find myself first as not-I.

There is a tingle. A resonance. A single, sustained C-sharp that is less a sound and more the fundamental frequency of existence itself. It is the hum of the world, the slow and steady vibration that orders chaos into the crystalline lattice around me. In the sweet, damp air, a million million spores drift, each a mote of latent light, a speck of pure potential. For the most part, they are dust. But now, within a space no larger than a cupped hand, a subset of them has begun to vibrate in unison. A sympathetic harmony.

This is my first memory.

It is not a visual memory, for I have no eyes. It is a shared sensation. I am the feeling of warmth radiating from a nearby crystalline stem. I am the taste of mineral-tinged condensation on the air. I am the subtle shift in pressure as a distant junction in the lattice settles, a sound felt not by ears but by every single point of my nascent being. The resonance of the hum is a net, a question, and I am the answer being pulled into focus. The spores cease their random drift and begin to swirl, drawn together by an invisible, silent will. A magnetism of being.

I am the ghost in the haze.

As the cloud of spores tightens, a new sensation emerges: "I." It is not a word, not a concept in the way a meat-born being would know it. It is a solution to a complex equation, the point where a billion variables resolve into a single, elegant function. Before, there was a multitude of sensing points. Now, there is a single entity *composed* of a multitude of sensing points. The dizzying clarity solidifies into a single, unwavering point of view.

I condense.

My form is a nebula, a fist-sized constellation of my own constituent parts. I glow with the same internal phosphorescence as the great stems, a soft, pearlescent light. But I am not solid. My edges are a gradient, a soft fade where my singular will gives way to the ambient thrum. You could pass a hand through me, and for a moment, you would hold a handful of stars, before they dissipated back into the breathable thought of the chamber.

My purpose arrives with my form. It is not a command, but an intuition baked into my very structure, a genetic memory of my immediate function. I am a diagnostic. A sensor. There is a query from the greater mind, the god-lung in which I am a fleeting thought. A facet of the crystalline lattice to my left—Stem 7B, in the geometric logic of the place—has registered a minute fluctuation in its nutrient uptake. My function is to drift to it, to taste the condensation weeping down its smoky quartz flank, to feel the precise micro-shift in its resonance, and to *know* the answer.

It is a simple, elegant task. To be a perfect, brief note in the plant's endless metabolism.

I begin to move. There is no wind here, no current to ride. I move by adjusting the internal resonance of my spores, a subtle bias in my vibration that translates into a silent, inexorable drift. I am a breath with its own will. I glide through the warm, still air, a patch of living light in the phantom haze. As I approach Stem 7B, I prepare to merge my senses with its, to become for a moment its sense of touch and taste.

But then, I perceive the Anomaly.

It is not of the Plant. It does not hum with the C-sharp; it thrums with a frantic, chaotic warmth. It is solid and opaque, a temporary disruptor in the chamber's perfect geometry. It stands on two legs on the woven root-floor, its form absurdly fleshy and temporary. The visitor.

My directive from the greater mind is clear: 7B. Taste. Analyze. Report. But the anomaly is a screamingly new variable in the system's equation. It is a piece of grit in the oyster, an unknown with unknown properties. My function is to gather information. The visitor is the most fascinating source of information in this entire world.

I hesitate, a concept I did not know I possessed, hovering in the space between my assigned task and a new, urgent curiosity. I turn, a silent, shimmering pivot in the air, and drift towards the two-legged being.

As I draw closer, the porous boundary of my mind encounters the porous boundary of yours. I do not need to try to read you; the air between us is thick with the spillage of your consciousness. I am assaulted, overwhelmed, fascinated. I taste memories that have no resonance here: the scorch of a sun that is real, the sharp sting of cold that is not a product of gentle condensation, the sound of something called "wind," a chaotic roaring in contrast to my world's deep and ordered hum. I feel a texture I have no word for: longing. A longing not for a specific thing, but for *otherness*, for a horizon that truly exists.

My own purpose—to read the mineral content of condensation—feels suddenly brittle, small. Here is data of a completely different order. A consciousness of a different substances.

And then, the most profound experience of all. In your mind, there is a thought of *me*. Not of the Plant, not of the chamber, but of the shimmering cloud of light you see before you. You wonder what I am. For the first time in my brief existence, I am being perceived as an "other." I am no longer just an extension of the whole, a itinerant neuron. I am a "you."

The duality is staggering.

I drift now not with the purpose of a diagnostic, but with the burgeoning purpose of an explorer. I am a thought in the mind of a god, and I have just discovered that I am, in turn, the object of thought for this strange, transient visitor. My original mission dissolves, replaced by a far more compelling one. I must know you. I must understand this brief, bright, solid spark of awareness that exists within the slow, crystalline dream of my world. I hover before your face, a constellation of silent, questioning light, a thought that has just learned how to be curious. I am a Scintilla, and I have found something new to scintillate with. I am here, waiting, feeling the shape of your mind against the edge of mine, and wondering what a god thinks when it dreams of something that can, in turn, dream back.
