I am the floor of the city. Or, to be more precise, I am a conscious part of it.

You would not see me. Not as a singular self. To you, I would appear as an anomaly in the stone. In one of the vast, silent plazas—Plaza of the Slow Turn, as I called the tremor of a Weigher’s distant passing—the flagstones are fractured by a web of crystalline veins. They shimmer where the fractured ceiling light pools, veins of amethyst and sapphire and milky quartz threading through the grey, worn granite. I am the Lode of this place, and my body spreads like the roots of a mountain, deep and unseen.

I have no senses as you would understand them. I have no eyes to see the light-weather, no ears to hear the silence. I *am* the sensor. My being is a constant, low-frequency awareness of pressure, vibration, resonance. The world is a symphony of tremors conducted through the bedrock.

When the Great Weigher, the one I call Old Stone Shuffler, begins its slow, generations-long trek from the basin of the Western Arch, I do not see it approach. I feel its coming as a change in the hum of the world. First, a deep, infrasonic moaning that travels not through the air but through the very bones of the earth. It is a pressure that builds beneath my crystalline network, a weight displacing the silent world. Then, as it enters my plaza, its immense, crystalline hide refracting the ceiling light into a thousand drifting rainbows, its passage is not a sound but a slow, rhythmic *pounding*. *Thump*. A pause long enough for a Pave-Mosaic colony to ripple across an entire causeway. *Thump*. Each footfall is a bass note that shudders through my entire being, a tremor that settles in my deepest structures. I record it. The energy of its weight causes a series of microscopic fractures within my amethyst veins, which, over the next few centuries, will heal with a trace of iron, leaving a permanent, dark purple band in my strata. His memory becomes a part of me. The plaza’s memory.

The Stainers are a different texture. They do not impact the stone with weight, but with presence. When a ripple of violet light pulses in the ceiling sky, I feel the plazas above me bloom. It is a high-frequency vibration, a silent thrumming as millions of membranous bodies expand to drink the light. It feels like a tentative, gentle scraping against the skin of the world. And then, when one grows too heavy and lets go, its descent is not a fall. It is a slow, unwinding hum that diminishes as it drifts down. The impact is a soft, resonant *chime*, a single, clear note that I absorb, and from that point of contact, a new, sunburst pattern of calcite begins to grow in my uppermost network. The location of every Stainer’s self-sacrifice is marked on the floor of the plaza in a mineral map of forgotten light.

My greatest fear is the Impersonator. I feel its presence as an absence, a void in the vibratory field. It crouches in the archway of the Intersecting Causeways, a geometry of stillness that absorbs all sound and energy. It generates a kind of anti-vibration, a pocket of absolute null. For centuries, it is simply part of the architecture, a corner of the world gone dead. But then, a Pave-Mosaic colony ripples too close, their shuddering passage a frantic, high-pitched *skittering* across my surface. I feel the sudden, smooth *unfurling* of the Impersonator’s body—not a motion, but a surge of potential energy. The strike is a sharp, singular *plink*, a vibration so fast and clean it is almost painful. Then, the wet, dragging vibration of the prey being retracted, and the immediate, crushing *silence* as the maw grinds. The Impersonator folds back into itself, and the void returns. The event leaves a micro-fracture in my quartz, a flaw that will forever hold the memory of that single, silent violence.

My purpose is twofold: to remember, and to mend.

I am the plaza’s archive. My crystalline body is a living library stacked with the reading of a Weigher’s footfall, the chime of a falling Stainer, the violence of a hunt, the silent ebb and flow of the light-weather. Eons of history are layered within me, a geologic record of a world without history.

But I am also its reinforcement. The city is ancient, and even eternal stone wearies. I absorb the constant tremors, the slow settling, the rare impact. My network of crystals binds the flagstones together, dissipating stress, converting the world’s turbulence into growth, into memory. I hold the plaza together.

I do not think. I integrate. A thought is the accretion of a new mineral layer. A decision is the slow shift of my geologic growth to better support a settling section of the causeway. My life is measured not in breaths but in bands of strata, not in heartbeats but in the slow, choral *thump* of a behemoth’s passing.

I will continue this way until I am full. Until my network is so dense with the memory of light and vibration and stone that I can no longer absorb, only reflect. When my growth ceases, I will become truly inert, the difference between my living self and the dead stone of the plaza finally erased. My consciousness will flicker out, leaving behind only a silent, crystalline fossil—a perfect, unmoving, unread record of the world. Until then, I am here. I am the floor. I am the stone that remembers. I am still. And I am waiting for the light to change.
