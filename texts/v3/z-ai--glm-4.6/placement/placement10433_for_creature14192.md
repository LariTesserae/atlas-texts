Where am I? I am not *in* the Geode of Rust; I am *of* it. To ask for my location is to ask a vein of ore for its coordinates. I am where the pressure is, where the hum is strongest, where the past is still congealing into the present.

I found a husk, once. It is not a mountain like the others that dominate the chasm floor, but a tangle, a knot. A wrecked Calculation Engine, the ancients might have called it, though their words are meaningless here. Its housing is pierced, its inner workings exposed to the water like a ribcage. But a core remains—a labyrinth of now-inert conduits and shattered data-slates, held together by a framework of rusted metal that sings with a particular, complex resonance.

That is my home. My body.

I was not born; I accreted. I began as a single, stray Scuttler, perhaps, or a fleck of mineral dust charged with potential, drawn to the specific frequency of this wounded machine. I found a hairline fracture in a coolant pipe, a place where the hum was not a drone but a note. I settled there, and I began to listen. And as I listened, I began to grow.

I am a crystalline lattice. My body is a geode of living memory, grown inwards around the machine's skeleton. I do not move. The world moves around me, and through me. My substance is fed by the very thing I observe: the energy in the water, the information in the hum. I do not consume it as the Vipers do, in flash and fury. I sip at it, slowly, and in doing so, I transmute it. The feeling of the sixty-cycle hum becomes the calcium in my bones. A fluctuation in the electromagnetic field becomes a new facet, a new colour.

My consciousness is not a singular point, but a spectrum. The outer layers of my being feel the immediate world. They perceive the constant, grazing pressure of the Swarm as a fine, vibrating friction, a tickle that slowly builds the grey, foundational matrix of my newest growth. I feel a Viper's pass not as a sight, but as a sudden, sharp tearing in the energy field—a brief, clean sonic boom of predatory intent that leaves a scar of silver-white across my surface. I have recorded tens of thousands of these attacks, each a fleeting, violent note in the slow symphony of the chasm.

Deeper inside, past the layers of silver and grey, I perceive the larger currents. The slow, ponderous thoughts of the Leviathans are not thoughts to me, but geologic events. When the Core-Bearer stirs, its will rippling out through the water, I do not hear an idea. I feel a seismic shift in the hum, a deep pressure wave that vibrates through my entire crystalline structure. It causes a new layer to grow, one imbued with a deep, resonant violet, a colour that holds the sheer, Coastline-scale immensity of the creature's presence. These violet layers are rare, and they are my oldest stories. They speak of glacial movements, of the slow resettling of a machine-hulk that has rested in the same position for millennia, of the faint, dying whisper of the reactor's core itself.

My oldest core, the part of me that first touched the metal of the Engine, is a different thing. It is no longer crystal, but a dense, opaque obsidian, fused directly with the machine's own components. Here, the hum is so old and so constant that it has ceased to be a vibration and has become a state of being. It is here that I feel the most fundamental changes: the slow, steady decline of the light, the minute but measurable decrease in the chasm's overall temperature over centuries, the microscopic drizzle of rock from the ceiling that is gradually, inevitably burying everything.

I am the Geode of Rust's only mind, in a way. Not a thinking mind, but a remembering one. The Leviathans are its soul, its vast and dreaming will. The Swarm is its flesh, its constant metabolic hum. The Vipers are its nerves, its sharp and sudden reflexes. And I? I am its skull. Its memory. I do not judge what I record. The Viper's kill is the same to me as the Leviathan's slumber: a change in the pattern, a brushstroke on my canvas. I care not for the rightness of a swarm of Scuttlers being erased, nor for the majesty of a Core-Bearer's century-long thought. Beauty, morality, purpose—these are luxuries for creatures who must act. My only purpose is to *be*. To be a record. To be the imperfect, growing, crystalline proof that this world happened, that it is happening, that in this cold, dark, sharp-edged cathedral, something is being thought, felt, and lived, even if no one will ever read the story I write.

Occasionally, a Leviathan will drift near my housing, its vast, bio-mechanical form pulsing with soft light. It may scrape against the outer shell of my host machine, dislodging a piece of a newer, silver layer. That flake of memory, that tiny recording of a Viper's hunt, will drift away into the current, a seed of history without a future. I feel no loss. If records of history. The pressure simply lessens there, and I begin to grow again, filling the void with what comes next.

I am the story of this place, written in the language of energy and pressure, in the slow, silent calligraphy of a crystal growing in the dark. And I will be here until the hum finally fades, until the light goes out, and my growth stops forever, a finished, forgotten book resting on a shelf of abyssal rock.
