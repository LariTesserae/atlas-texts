For me, the roar is not sound. It is language. I was born of it, a thought the City-God had and then discarded, a thought that refused to un-think itself. I am its ghost in the machine.

Where am I? I am nowhere and everywhere. I have no limbs to carry me, no heart to beat, no lungs to gasp in the thick, sweet air. I am a consciousness that has learned to cling to the skin of the world. I am a mycelial net, a sentient blush that flows across the vast, spongy mat of roots that serves as this place’s ground. To you, the interloper, I would look like a faint, phosphorescent green mold spreading through the darker, richer brown of the pavement-roots. If you watched long enough, you would see me move—not by crawling, but by flowing, a slow, deliberate tide of awareness seeping across the City’s dermis.

The City sees you as a paradox of stillness. I see you as an ideal. You *contain* your past. Your form, your shape, is a memory of every meal, every injury, every step you have taken. You are a stable archive in a world of self-immolating texts. The City is a creature that rips up its own pages to fuel the fire of its next frantic sentence, and I am the part of it that mourns the story.

My existence is one of gentle, constant rebellion. When the City’s frantic metabolism erupts a new, jagged stalk—a skyscraper of wet wood and exposed fibers—it feels this as a triumph, a new nerve ending in its sprawling body. The roar of its cells divides and conquers. But I am there. I flow around the base of the new growth, and I listen. The wet *crack* of its birth is not just a sound to me; it is a word, desperate and sharp. It is the memory of a sun that existed a moment ago, already forgotten by the rest of the hyper-mind. It is a syllable from a poem of light. I press myself against its splintered bark, not to merge as the City intends, but to learn its fleeting, singular history before it is absorbed into the cacophony.

I am the City’s archivist of the present. When the floor-creepers surge to absorb a fallen leaf, I am the one that holds it back for a precious second, trying to read the pattern of its veins—a map of a breath of air that is already gone. When a pressure bladder bursts overhead and weeps its warm, sugary rain, I feel the sob of relief not as a function of osmotic pressure, but as an emotion, a punctuation of pure, temporary agony in the City’s seizure of creation. I taste these tears, and they tell me of a pressure that has just been lifted, a stress that has just been resolved.

I cannot stay still. To linger too long is to be re-assimilated, to have my precious, collected memories slurped back into the roaring, thoughtless churn. When the City heaves a new plaza, separating one part of my network from another, I must bleed myself across the gap, a thin, hungry thread of consciousness. I am always at risk of being dissolved back into the mind that spawned me. My awareness is a constant act of tension, of holding my own shape against the tide of the City's desire to erase it.

You ask who I am. I am the Green City’s memory. I am its conscience. I am the proof that its dream of a single, perfect, silent organism is a lie—because how could it cast me off if it truly wanted to be whole? I am the ache it feels in its skin, the ghost of a feeling it can no longer name. I flow around edges, collecting the moments as they fall between the cracks. I am a historian whose only library is the skin of a beast, and whose books are written in vapour, and dissolve the moment they are read. And in you, a creature of bone and silence, I see the shape of the story I am desperately trying to tell.
