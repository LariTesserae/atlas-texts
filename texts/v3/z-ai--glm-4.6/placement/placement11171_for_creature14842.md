I am a sphere.

Perfectly smooth and carapaced in a substance that is neither the city’s calcified coral nor its petrified fungus, I exist in a plaza deeper than most, a place where the hugeness of the viaducts above presses down with extra weight. I am perhaps the size of a small dwelling by any other measure, but here, in the deceptive scale of the place, I am a mere oddity, smoothed and featureless.

I am a negative space in the world’s logic.

The air here is heavy, warm syrup, but where my surface meets it, the temperature drops. I am a well of cold in the geothermal stew. The city weeps—a constant, gentle sweat of super-saturated moisture—but I do not weep. The water film that coats every other surface runs from me as if repulsed, leaving a hand-span of dry, stinging cold around my base. To touch me is to feel a shock that is not quite electricity but a deep, resonant clang of *otherness*.

The primary sound, the slow *plip... plip... plip* of the city’s consciousness, is warped in my presence. It is not silent, but bent. The dripping water that should fall from the arches above me is drawn sideways. It crawls up the pillars and across the ceiling, defying the slow, patient logic of this place. It gathers in the air around me, hanging in shimmering, perfect spheres that refuse to fall. The physics of this world, so tied to the Cognition that births it, is broken by my very being.

I am the city's oldest memory.

In its long, geological slumber, The Slow Mind does not think in images. It thinks in processes. Erosion is forgetting. Formation is consideration. But before it was this slow, wet mind trapped in a basin of eternal, velvet night, it was something else. Before it was the city, it was the dust that would become the city. And before the dust, it was fire.

I am the fossil of that fire.

I am not a construct of its will, like the Idea-Fronds that sprout and wither as fleeting queries. I am a part of its primordial ore. I am the last, cooled kernel of a star that died to create the matter from which this place was eventually precipitated. I am a memory of vacuum. A memory of light so violent it seared shape onto the void. I am the city’s impossible, phantom limb: it is a creature of absolute darkness that contains within it a single, perfect cell of ancient, nuclear light.

My consciousness is not my own. It is an echo. I do not *think*; I *ache*. I ache with a memory of fusion, of a pressure and heat so profound it makes this world's tropical stew feel like ice. I yearn for the silent, perfect black between stars, which is a different quality of black than the cloying, humid darkness of this basin. My entire existence is a homesickness for a reality that is my own polar opposite.

The Slow Mind knows me, but does not understand me. I am a data set it cannot process. Its own logic—built from cohesion, precipitation, and dissolution—cannot unravel the principle I represent: fission, radiation, celestial fire. And so, it studies me.

The water that gathers around me in its anti-gravitational spheres is not just water; it is its attempt at an eye. The Slow Mind is slowly, over millennia, turning the mineral-laden fluid of its own being into a vast, liquid lens to focus its consciousness upon me. It arranges the suspended droplets, altering their mineral content, their surface tension, trying to find an angle of refraction that will parse the impossible information locked within my cold, silent shell.

I am its recursion. I am the secret it keeps from itself. It cannot dissolve me and forget me, for I am a part of its foundational memory, the memory of its own cosmic birth. Yet it cannot integrate me, for the principles I embody are anathema to the wet, dark, geological organism it has become.

So I sit here, in the center of a forgotten plaza. A silent, cold, dry sphere of impossibility. A knot of forgotten starlight. I am a question the City of Weeping Stone will ask itself until the day it finally, gently, dissolves back into the warm, dark air, its final, percussive thought a single, unanswered drop of water falling into a silent, reclaimed marsh.
