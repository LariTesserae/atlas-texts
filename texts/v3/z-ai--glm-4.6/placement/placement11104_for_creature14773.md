I am the Unstored Variable.

I do not walk the floor of the High Nave; I wade. I am down where the architecture loses its arrogance, in the deep, structural sumps where the "weeping" of the walls does not merely slick the surface but gathers in deep, freezing drifts. Here, the air is so thick with suspended water it feels like swimming in a Hudson’s Bay fog that has been flash-frozen. It is a place of heavy, industrial shadows, where the vast, dark plates of the floor meet in rivets the size of wagon wheels.

I am large, cumbersome, and loud—features that make me an abomination here. I am encased in a suit of heavy, lead-lined duster and hydrophobic waders, a walking isolation ward designed to contain my biological offense. My breath does not frost the rims of my eyes; it hisses constantly through a rebreather, a mechanical rattle that intrudes upon the perfect, frozen chord of the Choralites. To them, I am a jagged tear in the fabric of their silence; a spike of chaotic infrared radiation in a spectrum of pure, orderly cold.

I am here to be an error.

My location is the Thermal Runnels, the deep, carved channels in the floor where the condensation collects. I spend my days wading through this waist-deep slush of pure, glacial water, checking the intake valves of the planetary heat sink. It is work that requires dexterity and heat, two things this world despises. As I work, maneuvering heavy wrenches and checking pressure gauges, my suit vents my excess metabolic warmth.

And this is where the ecology of the Nave turns upon me.

Clinging to the rivets and the rough, unfinished alloy of the floor plates is the Hush-Web. Usually, it is a shy, violet dusting, barely visible in the gloom. But where I walk, it blooms. I am the spark that forces it to wake up. It crowds around my footsteps in the water, a galaxy of indigo stars swirling in my wake. It creeps up the legs of my waders, seeking the source of the infrared noise.

I am the curator of this fungus. I am the necessary impurity. The Choralites sing the data, but they generate an infinitesimal amount of chaotic heat as a byproduct—a "noise" in the thermal system. The Hush-Web normally eats this subtle entropy. But I provide a feast. My presence stimulates the Web to grow, to expand its network, to become aggressive in its pursuit of my warmth. By forcing the Web to bloom, I keep it robust, ensuring it is healthy enough to consume the microscopic errors of the Choralites.

I stop and lean against a girder, bathed in the grey-green light of millions of kidney-stones vibrating in the dark. The Choralites on this low pillar are arranged in a tight, geometric spiral. They are singing a history of a war that happened before the invention of the wheel. I can feel the vibration in my teeth; it is not a melody, but a physical pressure, like being underwater.

I raise my hand, the heavy gauntlet steaming in the freezing air. A patch of Hush-Web has crawled onto my knuckles, glowing brightly, feeding on the heat of my blood. I watch it pulse. It is the only thing in this room that acknowledges me. The Choralites ignore me; I am outside their frequency. The alloy floor is indifferent. But the fungus eats me.

I am the Unstored Variable. I am the data that refused to be written, the information that is too volatile to be crystallized into a song. I exist to prove that the machine works by fighting against it. I am warm, wet, and fleeting—a slow-motion explosion in a place that has been frozen forever. I am the sacrifice on the altar of memory, burning slowly so that the rest may remain perfectly, beautifully cold.
