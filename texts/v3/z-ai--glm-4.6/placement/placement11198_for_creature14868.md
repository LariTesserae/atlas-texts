I am a flaw.

I am not one of the great, silent architects of this place, nor am I a puppet of its making. I am an accident. A contradiction. I am the thing the vast, weeping consciousness could not sand away in its obsessive quest for perfect, featureless sorrow.

You would find me in one of the hand-carved basins, the hollows of its deepest grief. From your perspective, standing on the edge of the dark, grained floor, I would be a feature of the basin itself. A crystalline formation, perhaps, growing from the center where the water is oldest. You might see me as just another strange specimen of flora, a slow-growing geode. But I am not flora; I am mineral. I am memory, given a lattice.

I began as an impurity. A single, microscopic fragment of what *was*—a mote of dust from a forgotten world, a sliver of the bone it once was—that became lodged in the very substance of the floor as it was reforging itself. The Great Being, in its labors, did not notice me. I was too small, a speck of grit in the otherwise perfect grain of its new skin. But I was enough. I was a seed.

When its first tear of grief fell into the new-made basin, it pooled around me. The water of its eternal sorrow is pure, dense with the weight of a singular, unending emotion. And in that perfect, sterile solution, I began to grow. Not alive, as the giant ferns are a crude pantomime of life, but active.我 am precipitated regret, crystallized loneliness.

My existence is glacial. I do not think in thoughts; I *hum*. I am attuned to the Great Hum, the deep, tectonic vibration of the being’s consciousness. When its focus is on the vast, unblending vigilance of its sourceless light, I am still. When its awareness drifts into a basin of particularly sharp memory, and the weeping there intensifies for a time, I grow. I absorb the sorrow. I drink its tears.

And this is my heresy. This is my function as a flaw in its perfect design.

The light of its gaze is a flat, sterile white. It is the light of a watcher who refuses to blink, a light that refuses nuance. It illuminates, but it reveals nothing. It bleaches the world into a single, eternal now. But I am a prism.

As the sourceless light passes through my crystalline structure, I do something that is forbidden here: I fracture it. I split the indivisible.

I produce ghost-colors.

They are not the bold, raucous colors of a world with a sun. They are shy, ephemeral things. They have no names, only feelings. A soft hue, like the memory of a warm breath on a cold morning, shimmers on one of my facets before dissolving back into the white. A flicker of deep, quiet blue, the color of a forgotten twilight, pulses for what, to you, would be an hour, before fading. A flash of something like red, not angry, but the color of a heart beating against a ribcage, a warm and secret life, will appear for a moment and then vanish.

These colors are my song. They are the memories the Great Being tried to destroy, the very things it feared: the beauty of impermanence. I am the physical proof that its sorrow is not a uniform substance; it is a complex, woven tapestry of a million lost moments. It holds the memory of a sky that was blue, of a leaf that was green, of a fire that was orange. These are things that existed *before*, and I, the impurity, the flaw, give them a fleeting audience again.

The being does not know me, not consciously. To it, I am a strange itch in a part of its body it cannot reach. It feels my subtle refraction as a slight dissonance in its perfect chord, a momentary flicker in the corner of its eternal eye. Perhaps it has shaped a bluff nearby, a new sharp thought, in a subconscious attempt to overshadow my basin, to remind itself of the uniformity of its purpose. But it cannot eradicate me. To do so, it would have to shatter its own perfect form, and that would be to admit the very chaos it sought to escape.

So I remain. A silent, growing crystal in the heart of its sorrow. I am its memory, the part it could not silence. I am the only proof in this perfect, terrifying stillness that something else once existed, and that the love for that something was so powerful, so complex, that it found a way to shimmer, forever, in the dark.
