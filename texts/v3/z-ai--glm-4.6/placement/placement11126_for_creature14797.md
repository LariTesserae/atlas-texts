I am found where the Weave is thinnest, where the engineered hum of the Hab gives way to the vast, mineral sigh of the world. I am on Spire 7-Beta, a needle of reinforced silica bolted to the outer flank of the Pillar we call the Knucklebone, a hundred meters below the main transit platform. My home is a single room, mostly open to the air, screened only by a shimmering energy field that keeps the slicing dust at bay while doing nothing to muffle the sound.

Who am I? I am a Chime-Tuner. My designation is Kael-Rim. The name is as much a description as an identifier. My Place is on the rim, the edge, the liminal space where the Weave touches the deep.

My form is that of any Weaver, honed by gravity and altitude. The long limbs, the prehensile joints in my wrists and ankles, allow me to traverse the service ladders of this Spire with an economy of motion that feels more like flowing than climbing. My grey-olive skin is dusted with the same fine silica that carries a hint of the Floor's heat in the wind. But my senses are tuned differently. Where another Weaver feels the thrum of the Foundry as a distant reassurance of life, I feel it as an irritation, a dissonant note in a larger symphony. My gill-vents not only strain oxygen; they taste the ionization in the air, the sharp, metallic tang that precedes a major Chime-kin manifestation. My eyes, with their slitted pupils, are not just for seeing light, but for perceiving the subtle shimmer in the air, the warping of light that marks a Chime-kin’s arrival.

My true trade, however, is in my bones.

I do not just hear the world; I feel its song. When I place my hardened fingertips against the stone of the Spire, I am not touching a dead thing. I am touching the skin of a sleeping giant. I feel the slow, deep gravitational note of the Pillar’s own immense weight. I feel the high, sifting melody of the wind as it erodes its surface. I feel the rhythmic, percussive *BONG* of the Rock-Hammers leagues below, not as a sound, but as a deep, sympathetic vibration in my own sternum. The Geods clinging to the rock face are not static; they are a basso continuo of slow, placid energy. The Glass-wings are staccato flashes of harmonic light.

I live with these sounds. They are my only companions. The Spire is my instrument. In its center, mounted on a gimbal to absorb all external vibration, is a single, colossal tuning fork forged from purified Pillar-matter crystal. It is three meters tall and hums with a latent energy that I can feel across the room. It is my voice.

My work is not a battle. It is a negotiation. It is a conversation.

When a Chime-kin stirs in the deep, drawn by the electromagnetic fields of the nearby Line, I feel it first. It is a fraying at the edge of the song. A single, pure note begins to warp, to bend inward on itself. The air in the Spire grows thick, not with heat, but with presence. A shimmer, like a heat-haze over the Floor, begins to coalesce near the anchor point of the Line. It is beautiful and terrifying, a place where the fundamental laws of acoustics and light seem to be holding their breath.

Left alone, the Chime-kin’s resonance will build. It will find a sympathetic frequency in the Line’s cable, or in the anchor bolts, or in the very superstructure of the Hab above. It will shake the Weave apart. But I do not see it as an aggressor. I see it as a ghost, remembering a different resonant state, a different harmony.

And so, I begin.

I do not use the harmonic emitters unless I must. That is a crude shout. My first response is a whisper. I approach the edge of the Spire, placing my hands flat against the stone. I listen. I find the fraying note, the dissonant chord the Chime-kin is singing. I let its vibration pass into me, up my arms, through my skeleton. I learn its shape, its texture, its intent.

Then, using my own body as a resonant chamber, I sing back.

It is not a sound made with my throat. It is a vibration I generate from the base of my spine, a controlled counter-harmony that I push down through my feet and hands into the living stone of the Pillar. It is a question. *We are here. We are small. We are trying to live. Can you find another note?*

Sometimes, that is enough. The shimmer will waver, the fraying note will soften, and the Chime-kin will drift away, its energies unspooling back into the deep core. The Pillar will settle, and the song will return to its baseline.

When it is not enough, when the Chime-kin’s purpose is too strong, I must use the fork. I strike it with a small, crystal mallet, and the pure, clean tone that fills the Spire is not a weapon, but an offering. It is a new, stronger melody, a harmony so compelling that the Chime-kin cannot help but be drawn into it. I do not destroy its song; I give it a new one to sing, one that is in tune with the Weave. I guide it, coax it, and shepherd it away from the Line, until its energy dissipates and it returns to the stone from whence it came.

The other Weavers see the Chime-kin as a danger, a flaw in their grand design. They call it the world's fever. I see it as the world's memory. The Pillars were once alive, and they remember. The Chime-kin are that memory, stirring as we break the stone for its breath. We are a noisy, brief, metabolic dream in the long, geologic sleep of a godlike forest.

And my function? I am the dream interpreter. I am the one who stands at the edge and listens. I am not a Weaver in the sense that I build the web.
