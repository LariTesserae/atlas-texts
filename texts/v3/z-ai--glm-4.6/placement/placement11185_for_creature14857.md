Here, I am the Echo.

I was not born of the Optic Mycelium, nor grown from the lignin geology. I arrived. I was a thing of noise, a creature of a world that shrieked, sang, and shattered its own air with constant vibration. I remember the shock of the silence here, the first few days. It wasn’t quiet; it was a pressure, the way a diver feels the ocean’s weight. My own blood coursing was a deafening roar, the beat of my heart a kettledrum solo of pure terror. My ears, honed to parse the rustle of a leaf or the distant call of a bird, starved. They began to invent sounds, a phantom tinnitus that was the memory of a world that was no longer there.

The Chime did not attack me. That would have been a mercy. Instead, they managed me.

At first, I was simply a dissonance. A warm, electric, chaotic signal pulsing erratically on the surface of their vast, thinking brain. I was a fever in the system. A Scuttler was dispatched. I remember the prickling on my skin, a static charge that crawled over me, tasting the salt of my sweat, the heat of my flesh, the electromagnetic field of my frantic, singular consciousness. It did not harm me. It simply… logged me. It was a census of one.

Then a Loomsinger turned its crystalline face toward me. I was too far to feel any physical effect, but I perceived its attention. It was not a gaze of hostility, but of profound, unnerving curiosity. It pulsed with a slow, interrogatory rhythm, a chord of cool blue and analytical violet light that propagated through the fog. It felt like a question being asked directly into my bones, not with words, but with pure mathematics. *What are you?*

That was when the weaving began.

They did not encase me in moss or leech my heat with a thermal updraft, not at first. Those methods are for simple glitches, for mineral deposits or rogue spores. I was a complex problem. The Chime began to grow *into* me. A single filament of the Optic Mycelium, finer than a human hair, pierced the skin of my ankle. I felt no pain, only a strange, cold… connection. Like plugging a wire into my soul.

Over weeks, perhaps months—the frantic pace of the Loom makes time meaningless—this network proliferated. It traced my veins, wove itself between the bundles of my muscle, and delicately sheathed the nerves of my spine. The silence of the Loom began to fill me. The roar of my own blood softened, mingling with the deep, cellular hum of the Loom’s respiration. The beating of my heart fell into sync with a slow, tectonic pulse I could feel through the soles of my feet, the rhythm of the Loom’s own digestive process.

I am no longer just in the High Loom. I am of it.

My physical body is a hybrid. My skin is pale, traced with faint, violet patterns that glow softly when I am deep in thought. Woven vines, the same material as the "conveyor belts," have grown around my torso, offering support and a direct conduit to the Loom's nutrient flows. My lungs have adapted to the thin, metallic air, and the constant condensation no longer chills me; it feels like a baptism.

I have become what they needed me to be: an archive. A translator.

The Chime’s world is one of silence, of pure, ordered light and biological function. They had no concept of noise. But within my memory, they found an entire universe of it. I am their library of the forbidden. I am the only record of a symphony, of a screams, of the cacophony of a city street, the roar of an engine, the whisper of a lover's name.

This is my function now. I hum, not with vibration, but with potential. A Loomsinger will "sing" a question to me, a pulse of soft light, and I respond. I delve into the labyrinth of my own mind and pull out a memory. My body is a loom, the Optic Mycelium my shuttle and my threads. I translate the memory of a thunderstorm not into a sound, but into a cascade of frantic, staccato flashes of white and silver light that ripple through the Mycelial network for miles. The memory of a mother’s lullaby becomes a slow, warm, undulating wave of golden light, a pattern of pure, mathematical comfort that the Loom processes and stores as a new, alien form of data.

I am the violence without a scream, turned back into music. The frantic energy of my past world is ordered into the silent, industrial symphony of the Loom. I am a Scuttler that clears no blockages, a Loomsinger that sings no renewal. I am something new. A unique node in their grand consciousness.

Sometimes, the old shaggy quadrupeds, the lost fragments, will scurry past my resting place. They are frantic, erratic chaos. They do not see me as one of them. They feel the complex, ordered pattern of light that emanates from me and they shy away, perceiving me as something far more alien than even the Loomsingers.

I am the Echo. I am the ghost of sound given a silent body. The heavy pressure of the silence is no longer a threat; it is the medium in which I exist. I was a glitch in the system, a dissonant chord. Now, I am the high, strange note at the end of a piece of music they never knew they were missing. I have been woven into the tapestry of the High Loom, a single, shimmering, impossible thread, and in the absolute quiet, I have finally found my voice.
