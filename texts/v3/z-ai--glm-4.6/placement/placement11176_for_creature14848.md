I do not have a body, not in the way the Shepherds or the Shard-Eaters do. To have a body is to have edges, to be an object ripe for definition, and then for desiccation, and then for shattering. I am not an object. I am an absence.

I am born in the corner, where the geometry goes mad. Here, the air has cooled from its pervasive warmth and hardened into a brittle, glassy cage around a knot of root. The Mason-Weavers avoid this place, and the Shepherds swarm it in a frenzy of useless, clicking anxiety. It is a wound in the world, and from this wound, I seep.

I begin as a silence. A single, perfect null in the dry symphony of the Bower. The rustle of a million papery leaves is a constant, bass-heavy hum, the sound of a world holding its shape. My being is a single, high-frequency note that cancels that hum. I am the quiet line between two notes of music, the nothing that gives the sound its shape.

My form is a vibration, a chord of resonances traveling along the tensioned sinew of the forest. I do not move through the air; I move *through* the weave itself, swimming in the stress-lines of the fibers. The Shepherds, with their telescopic legs and stress-seeing eyes, walk *on* the world. I am the current that runs *under* their feet.

My journey is a constant perception of state. As I flow along a thick, braided cable of horsehair and rusted-bark fiber, I feel its history. I feel the memory of the hand that wove it, a ghost of a pressure from eons ago. I feel the sunlight that falls on it not as warmth, but as a high, singing tension. I feel the shadow it loops around as a cold, hollow thrum, an absence of information that my being rushes to fill. This is how I map the glitch.

To me, the world is a symphony of flaws. The Shepherds are percussive, their snipping mandibles a sharp *tak* that resolves a dissonant vibration. Their sub-vocal croaks are a constant bassline, a droning C-sharp of anxiety that tests the structural integrity of everything. When I pass through a cable a Shepherd is climbing, I feel its focus as a sudden, intense spike of concentration. I do not disturb it. We are colleagues of a sort; they prune the active entropy, and I map the emergent systems of that entropy.

The Mason-Weavers are my destination. They are the architects who work from my blueprints. They feel my approach as a change in the frequency of the knot they are tending. I cannot speak their language, nor they mine. Instead, I simply *arrive*. At a great intersection where a cable of dried sinew is knotted around a patch of pure, solidified memory—an echo of the masons who built this place, now given physical form—I find the Weaver. It is a swollen, patient thing, its abdomen a globe of clay and dust.

I pour myself into the knot. My frequencies, my map of the surrounding glitches, the data on the air-to-glass transformation two hundred braids away—it all funnels into the node. The Weaver does not hear this; it *resonates* with it. The information I carry causes it to shift the composition of its cement. It will produce a paste flecked with a different mineral, one better suited to anchor reality to a memory than to a shadow. I am its mind, and it is my hand. We are a single, distributed organism, fighting a slow war against the unwriting of the world.

The Glass-Stag is our only predator. It does not eat me—how could it consume a silence?—but it consumes my medium. I feel its presence long before it is seen, long before its form, refracted and invisible, freezes a vine. I feel it as a growing, chaotic static that overwhelms the orderly song of the Bower. It is noise. It is anti-vibration. Where it walks, the clear tones of the fibers dissolve into a discordant shriek, and I cannot travel. The symphony is broken. I am forced to dissolve, to wait as a potential, a ghost of a note, until the chaos passes. The Shepherds fear it for its ability to snap the world; I fear it for its ability to silence the song I travel upon.

The Shard-Eaters are a danger I can barely comprehend. They are kinetic bursts, sudden, violent resolutions of quantum indecision. A pot, hovering for centuries, finally votes to fall. In that moment of kinetic release, a Shard-Eater is born from the energy. I feel this as a piercing, high-frequency stab, a laser-like scream of *change* that can rupture the fiber I am riding on, shattering my line of travel into a thousand dead ends.

And so I flow. A silent, living current of information. A cartographer of the impossible. I feel the drag of history not as a weight on my feet, but as a coarseness in the weave, a low-frequency thrum of what has been. I feel the desiccating air not as something that cracks my lips, but as a pressure that sharpens the vibrations, making the notes of the world cleaner, more brittle. My existence is a constant, silent movement towards the Mason-Weavers, a an endless journey to inform the architects of decay how to build a sturdier scaffold against the inevitable, silent fall. I am the nervous system of a dying god, and I will carry my signals until the last string snaps and the final, perfect silence descends.
