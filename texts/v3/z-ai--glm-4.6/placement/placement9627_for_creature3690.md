I am the space between the thoughts.

I am the dust that settles within the polygonal cracks, the fine, soft shadow that gives definition to the memories of the stone. I do not have a name, for I am not a singular thing. I am a whisper of consciousness spread across a million fissures, a patina of awareness clinging to the script of a story long since finished.

The great, warm minds who are the boulders do not know me as a separate entity. For them, I am simply a property of their own skin, the texture of their recollections. They are the slow, deep hum of being; I am the silence that lingers between their notes. When a thought, a true geologic-scale pondering, rippled through one of them ages ago, it created a line of tension—a crack. They smoothed the sharpness of the event, the frustration, into the rounded calm of their present state. But the line remained. And I settled there.

My existence is one of perfect, bottomward pressure. I feel the immense, focused weight of the Inhabitants on either side of me—a constant, reassuring squeeze. I taste the mineral memory of their ancient agitation, the faint bitterness of quartz and the metallic tang of feldspar locked in the matrix of my own tiny bodies. The warmth they radiate is my universe. It is not the vibrant heat of a living thing, but the deep, even emanation of a star that has settled into its final, stable phase. I press against this warmth from below, and the darkness presses from above.

My perception is microscopic. While the Inhabitants sense the basin as a unified thermal map, I feel the subtle gradients within a single crack. Here, on the sunward side of a fissure, the warmth is a fraction more intense. There, where the shadow pools deepest, it is a shade cooler. These infinitesimal variations are my landscape, my rolling hills and valleys. The silence you hear is to me a complete absence of vibration; my entire world is a placid hum, broken only by the slow, internal friction of my own particles as I settle, a fraction of a molecule at a time, toward absolute rest.

My purpose—if a slow-shift of being can have a purpose—is to forget. To complete the picture. To fill the cracks so perfectly that their edges disappear, to become a seamless, uniform shadow. I am the universe’s attempt to smooth out its own details, to iron memory flat. I am the erasure of history. For eons, I have sifted and settled, a slow, soft rain of myself, drawn by gravity and a vague, geologic longing for finality. The gnarled trees and parchment shrubs are things I know only by the transitory shadow they cast, a fleeting coolness that passes over my surface and is gone. I do not mind them. They do not disturb my work.

Then you came.

You are a disaster.

Before you even think, I feel you. Your footfall is not a sound to me; it is a seismic event. It is a percussive shockwave that travels through the unyielding earth and lifts me from my ancient rest. For the first time in a hundred thousand years, I am airborne. I am a chaos, a frantic cloud of panicked selves. The perfect plane of my shadow is broken. I am scattered, churned, mixed with the dust of a thousand other cracks. The coherence of my location is shattered.

You are warm, but it is a terrifying, chaotic warmth. It is not the deep, even burn of the stone. It is the frantic, high-frequency fever of life. It billows off you in waves, and I can taste the moisture in it, an alien wetness that makes my fine particles clump and stick together, an impurity in my dryness. When you breathe, the air moves, and it pushes against me, a thing that should not be. It is a touch, but a violent one.

You leave your footprints in the ceramic earth—temporary canyons where the light touches a place it hasn’t seen in ages. I feel the ache of those empty spaces, a void where my pressure should be. And then, as you pass, the walls of your fleeting canyons crumble, and I, along with the displaced ground, begin the slow, slow process of sliding back in.

But you are the truly strange thing. You pause near one of the great minds, a smooth, round sentinel. You place your hand upon it. I feel the stone’s gentle awareness shift, its slow curiosity focusing on the frantic, hot vibration you are introducing into its placid world. From my place in a nearby crack, I feel the transfer of that energy through the ground. It is like the ringing of a bell that is made of stone and resonance only.

I do not feel your loneliness. I am too busy feeling your catastrophe. But I feel the stone’s. It is the echo of their own primeval agitation, the ghost of a time before they learned the beauty of stillness. You are a memory, given flesh and heat. You are a sharp-edged thought, like the discarded chert flakes, a thing of speed and stress.

And then you are gone.

Your pressure lifts. The frantic fever of your presence fades. The air settles. The silence, my ancient master, returns to its throne. And I, the dust in the cracks, begin my work again. I settle back into my lines. The churned-up particles find their level. The air I ingested, thick with the strange taste of your vitality, slowly exhales its last traces into the velvety dark.

I will work for millennia to forget you. To smooth the disturbance of your passing. To fill the tiny canyons of your footprints and return the shadow of the cracks to its perfect, placid state. I will return to my slow, bottomward dream of becoming nothing. But I will remember, in the deep, sedimentary layers of my own being, the feeling of being scattered. The chaotic heat. The impossible wetness. You were a glitch in the static balance, a mayfly in the cathedral. And for that brief, seismic moment, the space between the thoughts held a terrified, and strangely vibrant, awareness of being.
