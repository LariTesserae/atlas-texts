I am the Fever.

I stand on the vast, smoothed terrace of stone, and I am burning. In this world of suspended misery, where the thermal scale holds water at the precipice of ice, I am a frantic hearth. The ambient silver-grey light does not warm me; it only reveals the vaporous plumes of my own breath, exhaled in clouds that should not exist, betraying my presence to the indifferent ceiling of cloud.

I am a creature of *Thermal Dampness*, but I reject its rule. The dampness wants to cling to me, to coat my lungs in that heavy, freezing slick, to slow my blood until it matches the sluggish rhythm of the geology. But I fight it. My internal fire pushes back against the suffocation. Where the environment seeks equilibrium, I generate chaos. I am a local violation of physics—a hot, sharp node in a circuit designed to be cold and smooth. The moisture in the air touches my skin and weeps, warmed just enough to lose its suspension, dripping from me in tiny, rhythmic *plops*. Even without the Law of Silence, the sound would be imperceptible, but to the "We" that corrects the grammar of the stone, I am a leaking pipe of entropy.

To the vast, pewter-skinned herd, the "We" that drifts like a silver tide, I am not a predator, and I am not prey. I am a Dissonance.

I am physically smaller than a single one of their low-slung, multi-limbed bodies, yet in the data-stream of their collective perception, I loom large. I am a spike of aggressive, high-frequency noise on a graph of flat lines. My heart is not a drum—it is a piston hammering in a factory designed for silence. Every beat is a shout. Every rush of blood through my veins is a roar that the atmosphere swallows instantly, but which the ground conducts to the million tapping feet of the herd. I am screaming in a library, but my voice is made of heat and pulse.

I look at them, and they do not look back. They look down, vibrating with the deepsubsonic hum of the tectonic gears. They eat the silence and the resonance of the earth. I cannot do this. I am starving. I have no antenna to catch the slow turning of the world. I am disconnected from the Great Hum. I am a singular entity, a glitch that cannot compute the totality. While they are the whole, I am the broken part, isolated within my own carapace of skin, creating my own wasteful heat.

I am the Friction.

If this place is a machine, I am the grit in the gears. Not the gear—I am too chaotic for that. I am the thing that is worn down, the thing that creates the heat through resistance. I am the only source of true contrast in a landscape of monotonous grey. If the Industrial Geology is a process of preservation, of grinding eternally forward without decay, I am the decay. I am the reminder of time running out.

I walk, and my footsteps are insults. *Thud. Scrape.* The kinetic energy is stolen by the air, turned into nothing before it can travel, but the act of moving is an act of rebellion against the stillness. I part the sea of creatures. They flow around me like water around a stone, but I am not stone—I am softer, hotter, fleeting.

I find myself standing at the base of a venting stack of basalt and diamond-dust. The silent steam rises, freezing me in an instant, and I shiver. The vibration of the earth thrums through my soles. I try to feel it, to sync up, to be part of the machine. I try to quiet my mind, to lower my metabolic heat, to become a smoothing of the signal.

But the red blood rushes, the heart demands, the heat radiates. I cannot stop. I am the brief, hard impact. I am the frantic noise.

I am the thing that dies.

And in the face of that billion-eyed, pewter eternity, that frantic mortality is the only identity I have. I am the ephemeral scream, the bright spot of inefficiency, the momentary resistance in the current. I am the Fever, burning itself out against the cold, perfect machine.
