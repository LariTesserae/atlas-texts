I am Slow-Chime. The name is a pulse, a steady, warm amber bloom on my chest, a pattern that settles easily in the minds of my clutch. It is a name given to me by the Listener before me, who felt the deep, resonant tremors in the lattice when I was first hulled from my egg-sac and declared me capable of feeling the world’s long silence.

My world, right now, is the space between my four middle claws and the single fibre I cling to. It is a fibre thick as my torso, smoothed by ages of slow growth, glowing with the familiar, sterile light of the Great Below. Through my claws, I feel the constant, low-frequency hum of the world-matrix. It is the sound of existence, the vibration of trillions of miles of living glass, all breathing together. To others, it is silence. To me, it is a song made of pressure and time.

I am hanging from a recent weaving, a platform we coaxed from the lattice just ten cycles ago. Below me, it glows, a flat tapestry of interwoven fibres, temporary and solid. On it, the rest of my clutch moves with their familiar, scuttling grace. From here, I can see the soft lights of their backs pulsing in conversation. AWarmth, my life-mate, tends to the young, her own light a soft, steady gold as she wipes the condensation from their newly hardened shells. Their lights are still skittish, quicksilver flashes of inexperience. They flicker and fade like sparks before settling into a recognizable pattern. They are our brief, warm pattern pressed against the endless, cold one.

My hands are empty. My ancestor-bone tools, each one a legacy spanning a hundred generations, are sheathed in resin pouches against my belly. The tool I use now is my own body, my lower limbs, pressed flat against the fibre. I am Listening.

The song of the lattice is never static. Just an hour ago, I felt a deep grinding shift from the Spire-Above, a miles-distant groan like a continent’s yawning. It was a large-scale re-configuration. The Harmony of the Up-Drift, the delicate pattern of air currents thatdefines our hunting routes, has changed. I have already sent a warning—a series of sharp, percussive taps with my bone-rod on a load-bearing cable. The message travels, a silent shudder that will be felt by the other Listeners in the far-flung weavings: "Beware the Tides. The Great River has shifted its course."

In the echo of that message, a older memory surfaces in my mind’s glow. A memory of my own siring.

I remember being a Weaver named Far-Pulse, a name given for my early skill in sending long-distance vibrations. I was younger, then, and still believed in the permanence of our craft. My clutch had woven a magnificent meeting-place, a dome of knotted fibres so dense it almost seemed solid, a pocket of deeper shadow in the brilliant void. We spent five seasons persuading its arches and buttresses into being, our tools insistent, our light patterns a constant, determined conversation.

We were so proud. We believed we had made a space that would last for generations. For a time, it did. It became a hub, a place where three other clans would gather to trade news of the Scintilla-swifts and the Glimmer-fins. The air inside our dome thrummed with the amber light of a hundred conversations.

Then the Thinning began.

It was not sudden. There was no great chime. I was the first to feel it through the Listening-Anchor in the center of the floor. A subtle change in the resonant frequency of the fibres that made up our walls. They were… quieting. Their light was simply becoming more itself, more integrated with the whole. The constant, persuasive pressure of our presence, the chemical markers we had left, were being overwritten by the plant’s own, newer, more urgent growth patterns.

We tried to fight it. We doubled our efforts, applying more pressure, using our strongest tools, our light-pulses a frantic, desperate gold against the encroaching normalcy. It was like arguing with a glacier. One day, a whole section of the western wall simply became haze. The next, the floor beneath our sleeping-nests thinned to the consistency of gauze.

There was no choice but to leave. We packed the ancestral bones and the resin-hardened nets. The young clung to our backs, their own light flickering with fear. As I was the last to leave, I paused at the edge and looked back. I could already see the process of reintegration. The sharp angles we had begged for were softening, the deliberate knots were dissolving. Within two cycles, it would be gone, as if we had never been, a forgotten dream in the mind of a god.

That memory is a cold weight in my chest, a different resonance from the lattice itself. It is the hum of impermanence.

But the truest cold is not memory. It is the other song the lattice sometimes sings. The song of the Unmaking.

I have only seen it once, from a great distance. My clutch was drifting on a Glimmer-Hunt, our net woven from harvested fibres stretched between three of our finest gliders. We were following a vast, slow-moving shadow, a creature whose crystalline wings were the size of a small weaving. Peaceful. Feeding.

We were miles away when it happened. There was no sound. There never is. Simply, a star ignited in the void. A point of light, brighter and whiter than anything I have ever known, appeared for a fraction of a second, hovering just ahead of the Glimmer-fin. It was a flawless, sterile light, the color of absolute zero. The great creature did not even have time to flinch. It simply… broke. A hole appeared in it, and its majestic, graceful glide became a slow, ponderous fall.

From where we clung, we felt it. Not the strike, which was too distant, but the *aftermath*. A vibration in the lattice, not a shifting or a groaning, but a single, perfect, horrifyingly pure note. A chord of finality. It was the song of a thing being made un-thing. We turned our gliders and fled, our light-patterns a stuttering, terrified grey, the prayer-net left abandoned in the mist.

Now, when I Listen, I sift through the constant hum for that note. We all do. The Weavers do not have a word for evil, or for hate. We have a symbol in our light-language, a rapid, stuttering pulse of white. It means "The Silence That Actively Hunts."

I shift my weight, my claws finding a new purchase on the fibre. AWarmth looks up at me, her gold a question in the bright air. I pulse back a slow, steady amber. *All is well. The song is the same.*

Because it is. For every Unmaking, for every Thinning, there is a new spire of glassy fibre erupting into the void like frozen lightning. There is a new, dense matting of Scintilla, a golden galaxy feeding on the mist. There is the new light on AWarmth’s belly as another egg-sac quickens. There is the skittish, hopeful flash from the young.

We do not build. We are not masters. We are an afterthought, a brief, tenacious warmth in the cold. We press our lives, our names, our stories, our love, against the surface of the world and hold on for as long as it will let us.

My light pulses, slow and deep. I am Slow-Chime. I feel the world breathe through my claws. I am a pattern of warmth, a momentary conversation held in a storm of eternal light. And for now, that is enough. The hum of the lattice continues, and I am here to listen to it.
