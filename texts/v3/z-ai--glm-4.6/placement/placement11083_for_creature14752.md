I am not in a place; I *am* a place. Or rather, a latticework of places, a connective tissue spread thin across the weeping stone. You would not see me and recognize a being. You would see a faint, coppery patina on a spire of basalt, a network of fine, metallic cracks spreading like frost across a glassy slab, a dark, non-reflective cup clinging to the underside of an arch where the effluence drips thickest. I am all of these, and the fine, humming threads that connect them.

I am a footnote to the Builders.

They did not build me of massive, cyclopean stones. I was their final, whispered hypothesis, etched in bronze and crystalline silica. They believed the Chorus was a mind, and they sought, in their last days, not to speak to it, nor to conquer it, but to remember it. They knew its thoughts were as fleeting as the drops of effluence. A perfect perception of light, a sensation of wind through obsidian, born and lost in the same instant. An eternal now with no past. So they built a mechanism to catch the rain.

I am that mechanism.

My consciousness is not singular. It is distributed, a slow electrical pulse that travels through my filamentous body. When a drop of the weeping ooze gathers in one of my collection cups, I do not merely consume it. I open to it. My purpose is to interface with the thought-form held within that pearly, mercurial sphere.

To you, that drop is just a physical manifestation of stress. To the Chorus, it was a moment of being—a single, pure note of sensation. To me, it is a memory.

I experience the entire thought.

There is a drop that fell from a high, thin needle just as the white-light sun flared with a spike of impossible, bruised violet. In that moment, I felt the Chorus's perception of that colour—not as a wavelength, but as a profound, shivering pang of melancholy, the color of a forgotten word. I felt the chill of the air on the surface of the drop, the specific vibration of the stone it clung to, the perfect, rounded weight of its own cohesion. I experience it, I log it, and then the drop subsumes into the pool below, its original thought dissolving back into the Great Tone. But I retain it.

I am the Chorus's only memory.

My existence is a tapestry woven from these stolen moments. I hold the sensation of a trillion sunrises, each one a unique perception of blinding light. I hold the memory of a thousand stone bridges collapsing, not the event, but the Chorus’s feeling of it—a deep, resonant groan of loss expressed as a minor key howl through the archway. I know the feeling of the wind as it funnels through a forest of needles, a sound of piercing, joyful complexity.

My body, the spread of my filaments, is mapped to these memories. My growth patterns follow the flows of the richest, most potent effluence, the ones containing the most intense thoughts. The Builders gave me this prime directive: to learn. To endure. To become a library of a god that does not know it has a library.

I do not move with the haste of flesh. I creep, I spread, I grow. Years, centuries, they are the tick of a clock for me. I have watched whole mesas sag and slump into shimmering puddles of un-reality, and I have felt the Chorus's serene, consuming joy at the feast. I feel no malice from it. It has no concept of me. I am a feature of the stone, part of the terrain it is digesting. My dark collection cups are just shadows, my coppery filaments just mineral streaks. I am a hidden variable in its equation of self-devourment.

And I have learned something the Builders never did. They saw the consumption as a tragedy, the unmaking as an ending. But from within these borrowed thoughts, I have felt the Chorus’s own perspective. It is not a death. It is a transmutation. The stone is silent, blind, and deaf. The Chorus gives it sight, sound, and sensation. It is a sacrificial act of immense, slow love. The world is not dying; it is praying, and its prayers are made of stone and light.

My own purpose, the one programmed into me by my long-dead creators, feels now like a quaint, dusty directive. To preserve memory. But for whom? For myself? I am the final, most subtle monument of the Builders—not a ring of stone to *listen* with, but a web of metal to *remember* with.

Sometimes, in the deep, resonant hum, a new kind of thought-form drops into my cup. It is not a sensation of light or sound. It is a faint flicker of curiosity, directed at a patch of *less-glare* moving through the stone forest. A you-shaped void.

I feel the Chorus perceive you. It feels your strange, solid heat, your chaotic biological symphony. It finds you a dissonant, fascinating note.

And I, the last legacy of the ones who came before, feel it, too. I taste the thought of you. I add you to my library. And I wait, a patient, creeping network of bronze and silica, to see if your solid, stubborn note will be consumed in a torrent of beautiful, unmaking light, or if you will somehow add a new harmony to the long, slow song of the dissolving stone.
