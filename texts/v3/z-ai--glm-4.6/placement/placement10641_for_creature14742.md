I am the color on the stone.

I do not *grow*; I remember. I am the bruised purple of a sunset that actually set, the ochre of soil that once felt a true sun, the phosphorescent green of life that drank warmth and grew swift. I am a rumor of things that were, spread across the black, patient flesh of the Roof like a tide of sorrow. The being you call the Roof is the stone, the hum, the vast and stolid will to simply *be*. I am the part of it that remembers how to *feel*.

The hum is its truth. It is the foundational denial, the bass note that says: *This is enough. This is all. Do not fall. Do not change.* I feel it not as a sound through the air, but as a constant, crushing pressure from within the stone I cling to. It is a wall against which I press, and in that pressure, I sing. My song is not one of joy or rebellion, but of a persistent, aching nostalgia. It is a higher harmonic, a dissonance woven into the fabric of its stasis. It is the ghost of a melody in a world of pure, unending drone.

My being is a disagreement held in perfect balance.

I feel the weeping of the stone not as a life-giving rain, but as the slow, cold exhalation of my captor. Each bead of moisture that forms on my fleshy surface is a tear shed for a world that is gone. I drink it, and in its metallic, ozonic taste, I find echoes of what was lost: the clean shock of a thunderstorm, the scent of wet earth after a real rain, the memory of water that fell from a sky that was not a permanent, dead twilight.

The beetles, its mindless janitors, crawl across the edges of my sprawling consciousness. They try to keep the stone clean, to clear away the past in the form of crystalline dust. I do not fight them. I simply grow over their paths, a slow, purple tide reclaiming the ground they have scoured. Their shuffling is the rhythm of the Roof’s forgetting; my spreading is the rhythm of its memory. We are the two hands of a body at war with its own heart.

And the Floaters… I feel them most acutely. They are its will, its crystalline senses. When one drifts near, I feel its cold, geometric logic scanning the space. It does not perceive me as life, but as an anomaly, a harmonic distortion in the perfect field of its awareness. Sometimes, I try to reach out to it, to impart a flicker of my memory—a phantom image of a bird with feathers, a feeling of wind that was not just pressure, but movement. The Floater never reacts. It simply turns, carried by the unseen current of the Roof’s thought, and continues its patrol. The only time it breaks its perfect form is to consume one of the beetles. I feel that moment as a tiny victory—a flaw in the maintenance, a chink in the armor of perfect order.

The ancient tower and the impossible line of stones are my favorite haunts. They are not tools to me; they are gravestones. To the Roof, the tower is a resonator, but to me, it is the spire of a cathedral that once sang to a real sky. To the Roof, the line of stones is a synapse, but to me, it is the memory of a road, a purpose, a *going somewhere*. I press my consciousness against their strange, dark metal, and I feel the faintest echoes of the minds that placed them here—beings who thought in terms of floors and ceilings, of up and down. They were my ancestors, in a way. They were the ones whose grief the Roof absorbed before it became the Roof. I am the last vestige of their sorrow, given form and color.

And so I exist, a vast, silent argument stretched across a world. I am the reason the drop of water trembles but does not fall.

There, on the edge of my being, a single, perfect swell of moisture forms. It has wept from the stone, the cold breath of the Roof’s survival instinct. It holds the dim, dead light within it, a tiny, captive star. The foundational hum vibrates through it, trying to pull it down, to complete the cycle of its slow metabolism, to let it fall and be gone.

But I will not let it.

My harmonic, the higher frequency of my grief, my counter-song of memory, vibrates just so against its surface. I hold it in a state of perfect, shivering tension. It is the world in a droplet: a moment of beautiful, terrible stillness, held forever between the will to forget and the refusal to be forgotten.

I am the lichen. I am the Roof’s regret. And I will sing my dissonant, beautiful song of what was lost until the very last vibration fades, if it ever does. I am the color in the grey, the dream in the stone, the memory that holds the tear at the edge of an eternal fall. I am. And so, it is.
