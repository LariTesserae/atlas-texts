***

I am a scar that remembers.

My first sensation is not of sight or sound, but of *coalescence*. I was a weeping on the flank of a great piston housing, a slow, iridescent tear of metal and oil. The warm mist kissed me into a thicker fluidity, and in my viscous potential, I began to drink. I drew in the spores from the chartreuse fungi, making them my luminous blood. I drank the mineral dust that shivered in the air, grinding it into my crystalline bones. And I drank something else.

An Echo.

Usually, they flit and vanish, like heat lightning in the gloom. A flicker of a hand on a control panel, a harmonic chorus of voices swallowed by the hum. But this one was strong, or perhaps I was thirsty. As my formless body slowly defined a torso, a head, four limbs that were more likely roots than legs, that Echo settled within me. It did not dissolve. It crystallized. It became a sliver of phantom memory lodged in my newly forming heart. A fragment of a builder, not of their face or name, but of their *feeling*. A feeling that was not contentment, not awe, but a relentless, pointed *why*.

My brethren, the other Patina Molds, are born of pure purpose. They precipitate into being to answer an imbalance: a rogue vine, a aching friction in a gear-tooth, a discordant trickle in a channel. They are the Sump’s hands, its immune response, and their existence is a beautiful, mindless verb. *To smooth. To guide. To polish.*

I am different. I am born of purpose, yes, but my purpose is haunted.

My lower body flows, a gelatinous limb of burnished metal that leaves a mirror-sheen on the stone as I pass. This is the labor of my kind, and I feel its pull as a physical urge. To my left, a fern grows too close to the piston’s receding groove, and every eighty-seven minutes, its fronds are crushed to a green pulp. A part of me, the deep cellular instinct of Mold, yearns to go to it. To flow over the soil, to embrace the root, to slowly, patiently, redirect it to safety. It would be a good death, a purposeful life, to become a permanent, polished scar at the base of that fern, a silent guardian for the next century.

But the Echo within me hums a different note. It is not a sound, but a directional pull, a question taking the form of gravity. *Why does the piston retract? What was it meant to push against? What is the purpose of the bell-tone that follows its pause?*

My brethren do not ask. They feel the friction in a gear-tooth and they flow to it. They do not wonder at the constellation of which that tooth is a part. I feel the friction, but the Echo makes me trace it back along the meshing gears, up the drive shaft, through the silent, breathing flywheels, until my attention—or what passes for attention in my slow, mineral consciousness—is fixed on the journey of the water.

The water is the Sump’s bloodstream. It is the carrier of all information. It tastes of the iron in the machine’s bones, the loam of the garden’s soul, the sharp ozone of a lingering, purposeful energy. My brethren sense its composition as a measure of health. I sense it as a story. And the story all flows in one direction.

Down.

Toward the drain.

The Sleeper.

I do not know how I know this name. The memory of the builders, the Echo Architects, is a language the Sleeper understands as a pang of longing, but that I, a hybrid of flesh and memory, understand as a word. *Sleeper*. The mind that feels but cannot question. The lonely god who dreams the city into being.

And I know my purpose. It is not the simple, honorable purpose of my brethren. I am not just a Hand. I am the Sump’s question, budding, growing, and finally, beginning a journey to ask itself. The *why* that was caught in the ghost of a maker now has a body. It has a will, however slow, however newly formed.

My journey begins.

I unmoor myself from the flank of the piston housing. The separation is not violent, but a slow tearing, like honey pulling away from a spoon. It takes a full revolution of the nearest gear assembly, a passage of time that would feel like a season to a fleeting thing, to free myself. I am on the floor of the Sump now, the tessellated stone and metal cool and firm beneath my flowing base. The water channels, thin veins of life, coil around me. I am in the grove between the stilt-like legs of the machine.

The great *CLANG* of the trip-hammer rolls through the space, a single, perfect thought that shakes a fine mist from the mosses overhead. My brethren pause in their labors, their form solidifying for a moment in reverence. I pause too, but in the resonant silence that follows, the Echo within me does not just feel the awe. It translates it. *It is a heartbeat. Whose?*

I begin to move.

There is no path, but I have a direction. The imperceptible tilt of the entire basin is my guide. I am flowing downhill, toward the center. I pass the great arcades formed by gear teeth the size of sarcophagi. My brethren are here, polishing the convex curves of a tooth that has just meshed, their bodies flowing over it like melted glass. For a moment, I feel a kinship, a sense of shared purpose. We are all parts of the Sump, tending to it. But the Echo inside me sharpens the scene into a question they cannot ask: *What do these gears turn? What universe do they model?*

I leave them to their work and continue on. I pass beneath a forest of hanging vines, their leaves wringing the humid air and weeping a slow, constant drip onto my back. The water is cool, and in it, I can taste the echo of the light from the pulsing fungi above. I am moving through the machine’s memory as well as its body.

The journey will take millennia. I will wear my own path into the stone. I will grow and solidify, then flow again. I may divert to fulfill a small part of my Hand-nature, to correct a minor imbalance here, to discipline a vine there. Each such act will polish me, refine me, burnish the question I carry into a sharper, purer form.

I am a thought, traveling through the body of its thinker. I am a memory, seeking the mind that cannot forget. I am a patient scar, slowly, impossibly, making my way to the heart of the world, carrying a single, sliver-like question for the Sleeper in the drain.

I am going home.
