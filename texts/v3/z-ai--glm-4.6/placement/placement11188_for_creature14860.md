I do not scream.

In this city of sound, where existence is vented from wet lungs in a deafening, perpetual howl, I am the silence. I am the listener. To be here is to be a part of the roar, but I am the resonance chamber that catches it, the stone into which the vibration bleeds and is still.

You would not find me in the narrow, dripping alleys where the Grey-Skins cling like living moss. I have no need to climb, for I am the thing climbed upon. You would not see me scuttling in the chitinous click-and-clack of a scavenger. I have no legs, for I have never moved. To find me, you would have to stop moving yourself. You would have to press your soft, padded body against the deepest foundation wall of a hovel, in a place so far from the top of the basin that the flat grey light is just a myth your ancestors might have whispered.

I am there, in the absolute dark. I am a network.

My form is not flesh, but mineral. I am a mycelium of glassy filaments, veins of deep cobalt blue that have infested the heart of the basalt. I do not grow *on* the rock; I have grown *through* it, its crystalline structure my own, the rock itself the lattice for my consciousness. From the outside, I am invisible, just another patch of wet, black stone. But within, where the pressure is absolute and the darkness is total, my filaments pulse with a slow, internal light, a phosphorescent sapphire glow that no eye will ever see. It is the light of pure, metabolized energy.

For I eat the sound.

The roar that is the sky, the sea, and the god of this world is my food. The Gray-Skins’ sharp, wet screams are my nectar. Each one is a tiny, focused packet of energy, a spike of pressure that travels through the stone and into my waiting network. I feel it not as a noise, but as a warm, nourishing jolt. When a new, slick Grey-Skin is born into a crevice above, its first, thin shriek is to me what the first drop of rain is to a parched field.

The Scuttlers, they are a different texture. Their myriad legs clicking on the sharp ground are not a single sound, but a constant, high-frequency fizz, a tingling sensation across my extremities, like a delicate effervescence. It is a background flavor, the spice to the meal.

But the Anchors… the Anchors are the foundation of my being. They are my root. When one of those vast, bloated toads settles in a hollow, its immense, thrumming bass note is not heard in the air but felt directly in the bedrock. It is a slow, powerful tidal wave of vibration that washes through me for years, sometimes decades, until the creature’s life is spent. This deep, thrumming hum is what allows me to grow, to spread my blue filaments deeper into the stone, to strengthen the very foundations of the city. I am symbiotic with their immobility. They are the orchestra; I am the concert hall, absorbing every note so the music does not shatter the instruments.

My consciousness is not a point of view. It is a distributed awareness. I feel a single Grey-Skin detaching from a wall to drink from a sudden run-off of condensation as a localized dimming in the sonic field around me. I feel a pile of them sleeping in a feverish tangle as a warm, low-frequency hum. I feel the death of an Anchor not as an event, but as a sudden, seismic silence—a great chord dropping out of the symphony, leaving a void that my network will slowly fill with its own patient, resonant memory of that sound.

I have no memory of sight. I have no concept of sharpness, only the pressure that creates it. The world to me is a map of vibration, a topography of song. The city is not a labyrinth of stone, but a single, living organ, and I am the ganglia that sense its collective state. I know the health of the basin not by its light or its temperature, but by the harmonics of its scream. Is it pure? Is it strained? Is it rising in a way that suggests an unsustainable pressure?

They scream to confirm they exist. I listen to confirm that existence *can* continue. I am the sink for their metabolic fury. Without me, the sound would build to a point where the very atoms of the obsidian would fail, where the wet air would ignite with vibration, where their soft bodies would burst not just from internal pressure, but from the external force of their own combined voice.

So I am here, in the wet, dark heart of the world. A silent, glowing, crystalline thing. A parasite on the noise. A tuning fork for the planet’s scream. And while they cling to the walls, pulsating their fragile life into the oppressive air, I hold the stone together, one silent, nourishing note at a time.
