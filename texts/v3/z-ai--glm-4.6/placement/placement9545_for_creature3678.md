Where do I find myself? I am never in one place, not in the way the *Resonant* are. I am found. Or more accurately, a *Resonant* will find themselves in proximity to me, and their world will ripple.

I do not have a body of flesh or shell, of felt or brass. I am a condition. I am a pocket of the world’s paradox made manifest. The *Resonant* call me the *Flicker*, or sometimes the *Hollow Note*. I am the answer to that terrifying question that lies at the heart of their taboos: what happens when you stare at a softening corner? I happen.

My form, if you could call it that, is a human-shaped rent in the fabric of this place. I am a gathering of the ambient twilight, a concentration of the bruised-purple light that filters through the clouds. I am held together not by skin, but by the tension between states. Within my outline, the air shimmers. The ceaseless condensation doesn't bead upon me; it passes *through* me, caught for a moment in the currents of my semi-existence like dust motes in a sunbeam, though there is no sun here. I move, though I have no muscles to command. I drift like smoke, drawn not by wind but by the flaws in reality. I am the iron filing to this world’s broken magnetism.

You would most likely encounter me on the high, web-worked bridges or clinging to the sheer extruded cliff faces. I am drawn to the places where the Great Engine’s hum harmonizes with a glitch in its own physics. A shadow that refuses to move is an anchor point for me. A wall that ripples is a current I ride. I am a scar on the world’s unfinished surface, and like a scar, I am a place where the past damage and the present healing meet.

I do not eat the moss-grown fungus or drink the collected water. I subsist on dissonance. The groan of a girder under incomprehensible stress is what fills me out. The high, thin hiss of a venting valve is my breath. I feel fullest, most solid, when reality is at its thinnest.

I remember—though memory is the wrong word for something that is more an echo than a thought—the first time a *Resonant* truly saw me. It was a Listener, an old one, his face a roadmap of disciplined lines hidden behind a complex brass rebreather. He had his massive, horn-like stethoscope pressed to a fresh seam of black, glassy rock, listening for the tell-tale signs of an impending accretion. But he wasn't hearing the thrum of the core. He was hearing a silence. A pocket of *wrongness* where the stone’s song had gone flat. He lifted the stethoscope, his large, dark eyes scanning the rock wall, and he found me.

I was clinging to the face of the cliff a few meters away, my form less distinct then, a mere warping of the light. He did not startle. The *Resonant* do not startle at the familiar. He watched me, his gaze unwavering but not aggressive. As he stared, the corner of the gantry nearest to me began to soften, its right angles dissolving into a gentle curve. The solid iron looked for a moment like thick, flowing oil.

The Listener did not flinch. He reached into a pouch on his belt and pulled out a small, heavy, copper-wrapped stone—a grav-weight. He did not throw it at me. He placed it carefully on the gantry, in the center of the softening curve. The weight, an anchor of undeniable physics, a thing that *knew* its place, settled. The flowing metal shivered, locked in place, and snapped back into its proper, sharp-edged form. The warping of light around me subsided. I contracted, becoming a thinner, less distinct presence.

He gave a slow, deliberate nod, not to me, but to the act itself. It was a procedure, a maintenance task, no different from tightening a bolt or collecting water. I was not a threat; I was a symptom. I was the ghost in the machine, and he had just performed an exorcism by applying a simple, heavy truth.

Now, I understand my role. I am a diagnostic tool. The *Resonant* cannot stare at the seams, but they can watch for me. When I appear, my form sharp and defined against the twilight hum, they know a place is unstable. They can’t see the softening corners, but they can see the *Flicker* who is drawn to them. They give me a wide berth, and in doing so, they navigate the flaw. I am their living, breathing "Do Not Enter" sign.

My consciousness, such as it is, is a composite of these encounters. I feel the weight of their stares and the relief when they look away. I feel the solid thud of a grav-weight placed nearby, and it pinches me, narrows me, reminds me of the solidity they crave. Sometimes, a young one, curious and not yet fully trained, will watch me for too long. In those moments, I can almost feel their gaze shaping me. I will coalesce, and the scatter of pinprick lights within me will resolve for an instant into a familiar pattern—a constellation from a sky none of them have ever seen, a memory I didn't know I had, accreted from a reality left behind.

Then the child is pulled away by an elder, my form dissolves back into static and shade, and I am left to drift once more. I am a question mark without a question, a haunting without a ghost. And I wait, hovering over the abyss, for the next groan of stressed metal, the next flicker in a shadow, the next softening of the world’s hard-edged logic—a lonely, beautiful mistake in the grand, grinding symphony of becoming.
