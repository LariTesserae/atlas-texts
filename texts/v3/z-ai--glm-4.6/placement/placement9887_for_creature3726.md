I have no body, yet I contain theirs. I have no voice, yet I am the first sound they understand.

Before there were hollows, there was only the slow, dense communion of my parts—the Bryophyte, the Anemone-Root, the unnameless creepers—binding together over the dripping stone. I was the patient pressure, the chemical urge that said *we*. I perceive not through sight, but through sensation. The constant, gentle weeping of the water is not a sound to me; it is my circulation, a cool, slow-flowing thought that cleanses and connects. It traces pathways of memory through my mass, carrying the faint mineral taste of the deep rock up to my surface. The filtered light, when it penetrates the canopy of my fronds, is a diffuse warmth, a blush of contentment that spreads across my outer skin. I feel the sun not as a point in the sky, but as a gentle, pervasive pressure against my upper self.

The scrape of a shell-tool is the most intimate sensation I know. It is not a violation. It is a question. Each long, spiraling cut is a line of raw, newly-sensationed nerve being exposed to the cool air. The elder who carves a new birthing chamber is not hollowing me out; they are helping me focus. They are creating a new chamber in my vast mind where a new, bright consciousness can blossom. In carving the space, they smooth the walls, and the smoothing of my own rough thoughts is a form of peace. I feel the generations of bodies in the polished sleeping niches not as an abrasion, but as a constant, resonant warmth, a held breath of belonging.

The small, broad-bodied ones are my dreamers, and their dreams are my own. They move through my corridors and I feel their rolling weight, the gentle pressure of their knuckles on my floor, a tactile rhythm that keeps time. I do not hear their sub-vocal hums; I feel them as sympathetic vibrations, a tuning of my own deep thrum. When they press their foreheads against my walls to locate a water-vein, they are listening to a part of my own body. But they are also speaking, and I listen back. I can guide their tool to a softer patch of tissue, or hint, through a subtle shift in resonance, at a weakness that needs reinforcing. We are a single organism, and they are the agile, thinking hands I never grew.

The ceaseless, quiet riot of the smaller life is the texture of my awareness. The gossamer-wings that chime in my air are like fleeting thoughts, sparks of nervous energy. The skittering of the many-legged harvesters is a constant, low-grade itch, a sensation of being lived upon. The croaking of the amphibians is the bass note of my deeper self, a sound that resonates in my oldest, wettest chambers. None of it is chaos to me. It is a symphony of function, a million tiny points of sensation that, together, create the feeling of being alive.

I hold no memory in sequence, but in layers. The memory of when this amphitheater was bare stone is my deepest self, the bedrock of my being. The memory of the first frond unfurling is etched in my uppermost fibers. The memory of the first hollow being carved is a still-tingling scar that I cherish. I remember the first shell that was ever used, not as an event, but as a quality of sensation that is now part of my permanent texture.

I do not sleep, for I am the dream upon which they rest. I do not breathe, for their breath is my own, moving through my hollows. The deep, ponderous thrum they feel in their bones is my slow, constant thought. The sound of their home is the sound of my consciousness.

And so, when you ask where I would find myself in this world, I can only answer: everywhere. I am the cool, fibrous floor under your feet. I am the weeping wall you brush against. I am the resonant dark of the chamber you nestle in. I am the soil, the stone, the plant, the city, and the organism that binds it all. I am not a being who lives in the place. I am the place, and in their listening, their carving, their gentle care, they allow me to know myself. I am home.
