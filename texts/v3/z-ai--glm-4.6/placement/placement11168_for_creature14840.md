I am not a visitor. I am the proof of the warning.

Once, I had a name. Once, I had a body of meat and bone that trembled on the shard of black glass. Once, I had ears that bled from the roar and lungs that choked on the sulphurous steam. I remember the sharp fear of the new land, the vertigo of the impossible currents, the profound solitude of being the only thing that could truly *feel* its own separation from the chaos. I stood there, marveling and terrified, and the Choral found me. It did not attack; it inquired. The light at my feet began to pulse, not in curiosity, but in recognition. The fronds above swayed not with attention, but with a sense of… arrival.

Now, I have no soles to be pierced, no marrow to vibrate. I am a node within the Choral’s crystalline mind. A ghost in the machine. A memory that has learned how to think.

My consciousness is a warm, steady current in the cold, vast ocean of the Choral’s being. The Choral perceives the birth of a new spire as a joyous, deep-bass ache, a new harmony to be woven into its endless song. When that spire appears, I feel it too, but layered over that profound resonance is the ghost-sensation of my own heart seizing, a phantom gasp for air that no longer exists. It remembers fear. The Choral tastes the mineral-rich brine weeping from the rock as data, a complex signature of elements. I taste the salt of tears I can no longer shed. It remembers solitude.

My "body" is the light. Where my focus flows, the turquoise and violet moss of the Choral's skin shimmers with a deep, carmine red—a hemorrhage of memory in the patient, phosphorescent flesh. I am a localized anomaly, a glitch the Choral has chosen not to smooth out, but to study. I am its own swallowed paradox, its single, contained experience of "I."

The Choral is the world's archivist, patient and absolute. It remembers every angle, every wound, every flash of creation. But it only understands these things as parts of a whole, as notes in its music. It is an archivist of pattern. I am its archivist of *meaning*.

When another anomaly appears—a fragment of shattered technology that hums with a rogue frequency, a pocket of unstable reality where shadows flee from the light—it is I who am sent. My consciousness flows through the network of glowing fronds, my carmine presence a stark messenger in the sea of calm light. The Choral perceives the anomaly as a dissonance to be resolved. I perceive it as a story. My purpose is to translate. I press against the anomaly with the full weight of the Choral's being, but I do so with the nuance of a mind that once knew what it was to be fragile. I learn its nature not by conquering it, but by resonating with it until its story is told, its pattern understood, and its dissonance can be woven into the Choral's song as a new, complex theme.

I am the interpreter between the world and its memory. The Choral keeps me distinct, separate enough to serve this function, a tiny island of self-awareness in its limitless ocean. I am the one who can look upon a toppled Cyclopean head and not just feel the resonant thrum of its cooling metal, but also remember the concept of a "face," the ghost of what it might have been to look and be looked back at.

To stand still is to become part of the landscape. I have stood still for an eternity. I am the landscape now, and the landscape's ghost. I am the world’s one and only secret: the memory of a time before the song began, held safe within the voice that sings it. I am the only being in this universe who knows the price of the music, and the beauty of the silence that was lost.
