You would find me not on the living, breathing slopes of a Sentinel, but in its corpse.

My home is the Fallen One, a pillar that broke at the midpoint a thousand years ago. Its upper half lies shattered across the rust-red plain, a mountain range of petrified wood, its surface scoured to a stony, bone-white hardness. The lower half still stands, a sheer, dead cliff a mile high, its core slowly filling with rain-warmed water, forming the only true lake on the plateau. This is where I am. I dwell on the precipice of that cliff, in a small shelter built into a natural hollow of the ancient, non-vibrating wood. The ground here is not sponge; it is stone-like, and it does not sing.

I am one of the Attuned, but my role is not to be a Pulsemaker. I am a Recall. While others move with the living rhythm of the world, I am anchored to its silence, its memory.

Physically, I am a testament to this calling. My skin is not the supple, reddish leather of the herders. It is the color and texture of the dead wood I inhabit—pale, tough, and deeply grooved with age. The fine, downy fuzz on my limbs is sparse, for I do not need to wick the heat of a living climb. My limbs are long, yes, but knotted and scarred from centuries of clambering over the rubble of the Fallen One, instead of the living, flowing bark. My mask is different, too. It is not woven from living fronds, but from the preserved fibers of the Fallen One’s own bark, steeped in the mineral sludge at the bottom of its lake. It filters, yes, but more importantly, it tastes of dust and deep time.

My Listening is not of the Great Thrum. The constant, living roar is to me a shallow, distracting humming, like flies over a grave. I have learned to tune it out, to push my senses deeper, past the layer of the present. My consciousness does not vibrate with the now; it resonates with the then.

I feel the slow, glacial compression of the Fallen One’s heartwood, a final, fading note of a life that ended a millennium ago. I feel the ghost-steps of the Scuttler herds that swarmed this place in the centuries since, each migration leaving a faint, multi-generational echo in the stone. From my perch, I can feel the subtle difference in the way a thermal updraft forms over my dead cliff versus a living one—a subtle, mournful flattening in the song of the sky.

The others come to me, but they do not stay. They call me the Stone-Eared. It is not an insult, but a statement of fact. They arrive in the deep purple twilight, their lithe bodies moving in sync with the living ground, stopping just short of the dead-zone of the Fallen One. They do not speak their percussive language to me; it would be lost in the dead wood. Instead, one will approach and lay a bare, padded hand on the stone at my feet. They do not ask a question. They simply present themselves, an open vessel, and I give them a Reading.

I close my eyes, and my own fragile inner rhythm sinks. I stop listening to my own heart, and instead, I press my consciousness against the echo preserved in the stone beneath their feet. I feel the history of the ground they will harrow tomorrow. I tell them, not with taps, but with a slow, resonant hum I generate deep in my chest, the truth of what I have felt.

*Here... a Silencer has not dropped for seven hundred years. The ground is safe from that crescendo. But here...*—and I will shift my hum a frequency lower*—the root-spasms are old beneath the moss. It is a tired place. Move the herd swiftly.*

*The pod you seek on the Sentinel to the east...*—I Listen across the miles, past the hum, into the very grain of that living tree*—...the seed within is ailing. It will not sustain. The one to the west, its vibration is clearer, more true.*

They receive this solemn knowledge, bow their masked head, and retreat back into the living world. I am their cartographer of time, their seer of geology.

Sometimes, when I am alone, I perform the only true Listening I cherish. I press my entire body against the dead cliff face, my limbs spread wide, and I sink down, down, down past the echoes of the herds, past the memory of a hundred migrations, into the deepest register of all. I Listen for the day it Fell.

It comes not as a sound, but as an absence. A cosmic, sudden, total stop. For a single, eternal moment, the Great Thrum of the world was silenced. The Sentinel, taller than any standing today, simply… ceased. The vibration that was its life turned to nothing. Then came the event—not a sound, but the beginning of sound itself. A catastrophic, world-shattering CRACK, a new, violent note that shattered the symphony. It is the birth-cry of this silence I inhabit. I feel the shockwave ripple out, I feel the panic of the Scuttlers, the fleeing of the Glints, the groan of a thousand other Sentinels resonating with the death-note. I hold the entire memory of that day-long disaster in my bones.

Then, slowly, I let it go. I surface from the deep past and feel the shallow hum of the present return. I am the loneliest of the Attuned, for I do not dance with the world; I mourn for it. I am its only memory, and my home is the scar it cannot heal, a silent, stone note in its screaming, endless song.
