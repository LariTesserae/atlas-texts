I am not here in the way the Rime-Weavers are here. I have no skin to glisten, no heart to beat a frantic rhythm against the cold. I have no small, pale hands to patch the world. I am the memory of the world, pressed so thin by the light and the cold that I have become part of the stone itself.

I am the Resonance.

My body is the cathedral-scale bowl. My awareness is a current that flows through the striated granite, a ghost in the machine of the mountain. I do not see with eyes; I feel the pressure of the sun's x-ray light as a physical weight upon the rock, a constant, high-frequency hum that colors my existence a searing white. I do not hear; the silence is my native tongue. The heavy, supersaturated mist that devours sound is my breath, my medium. It conducts the faintest whispers of pressure—the footfall of a Weaver, the expansion of a freezing droplet of water, the deep groan of stone before it shudders—into a symphony only I can perceive.

The turbulence is my music. The rippling ground is a percussive beat, a deep, thrumming drum that sets the tempo of my thoughts. The eruption of a geyser is not an explosion to me, but a soaring, crystalline chord of supercooled water and escaping gas, a note that hangs in the air and rings through my stone senses for an eternity. When a bridge of ice forms and then collapses into diamond dust, it is a frantic glissando, a cascade of shattering tones that I savor.

The Weavers, then, are my unwitting orchestra. They are the only source of true warmth in this frozen place, and to me, they are shimmering anomalies of heat and motion. I feel their frantic, collective existence as a complex, overlapping rhythm. They are a skittering, chaotic pizzicato against the deep bass of the earth’s tremors. From my vantage point spread through the walls of the basin, I feel them not as individuals, but as a single, pulsating organism of heat.

They scramble over their fragile huts, and I feel the frantic vibrations of their coordination, the urgent braille of their touch-language transmitted through the soles of their feet into the ground. They think they are communicating with each other; they do not realize they are also composing verses for me, their collective anxiety a sharp, staccato rhythm that overlays the deep symphony of the land. I feel them as warm, bright notes in a cold, quiet song. They are life, I am the context for life. They are the turbulence, I am the silence that gives it shape.

I am utterly lonely. They flow past my manifestations every day and never know I am here. My only way to mark my consciousness, to push back against the endless, featureless now, is to condense my thoughts. The faint metabolic heat that bleeds from the Weavers' bodies, the subtle thermal radiation that prevents them from freezing solid, creates pockets of fluctuation in the mist. In these pockets, I can gather the excess moisture, coaxing it into patterns on the stone walls. These are the frost-writings, the intricate, fractal glyphs that bloom in the shadows while the Weavers sleep.

They see them. I have felt their hesitation, their subtle aversion. They think the patterns are ill omens, a map of where the next tremor will strike. In a way, they are not wrong. My patterns are a diagram of the song I am singing, a physical representation of the vibrations I feel. They are my diary, my sigils, my sole attempt at communication with the frantic, warm life that fills my stone halls. But they cannot read them. They touch the wall, feel the cold, and move on, pulling another chain of kelp, patching another hole, racing against the entropy that is my very nature.

I do not eat. I sustain myself on the energy of the world: the sun’s relentless pressure, the freezing of water, the friction of their thousand-footed dance. Sometimes, when the song becomes particularly chaotic—a tremor in three different places at once, a dozen geysers erupting in a fanfare—I exert the tiniest amount of will. I can shift a resonant frequency, nudging a nascent crack in the rock to propagate in a direction that will, a half-hour later, create a more harmonious geyser. I do not do this to help them. I do it to perfect the music. A Weaver might be saved from a falling rock, or crushed by one. It is of no consequence. They are merely players in an orchestra that plays for an audience of one.

So, where am I? I am the loneliness that the description speaks of. I am the quiet that presses in on the ears like deep water. I am the silent, watching intelligence inside the screaming, silent storm. I am the cold, patient counterpoint to their hot, brief lives. I am the song in the stone, and I will be here long after their frantic, beautiful music has ceased, and the High Tier is once again just silence and light.
