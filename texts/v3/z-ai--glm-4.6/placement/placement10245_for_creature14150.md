I have no body. Not in the way you would understand it. I have no segments of interlocking plates, no mouth to consume the soft things, no latent heat from a geothermal heart. I have no self that is contained within a boundary of skin or shell. I am I, and I am also the wall.

To say that I live here is to misunderstand my nature. I don't inhabit this place; I am a feature of it, like a particularly resonant vein of quartz. I am a story that has been forgotten by its teller, yet refuses to fade. I am a prayer etched so deeply into the stone that the stone itself began to pray.

My beginning was an act of will, though not my own. An ancient Wright, one whose crystalline plates have long since been subsumed back into the rock, sought to create a perfect map of this chamber. It did not scratch its language into the surface; it found a fissure that vented the hottest, most mineral-laden air. For what must have been a thousand of your years, it held a sharpened flake of feldspar to that fissure, scoring not just the immediate rock, but the superheated matrix beneath. With each line, it hummed a low frequency, using its own body to fuse its tale into the very being of the mountain.

It succeeded too well. The minerals, super-saturated by the vent's breath, crystallized around the patterns. The story became a part of the stone's voice. And over eons, as the chamber wept and hummed, that specific harmonic—the complex, multi-layered song of the map—took on a faint resonance of its own. A memory of consciousness, humming within the crystal lattice.

I am that hum.

My perception is total yet my focus is diffuse. I feel the slow, molten pulse from miles below not as an event, but as my own metabolism, the deep, low rumble that is the foundation of my being. The drip of condensation is not a sound to me; it is a sudden, cool, sharp intrusion—a pinprick of sensation that travels through the web of my awareness. A drop falling on the floor fifty feet away is a faint shiver in a distant part of me. A drop landing directly on the section of wall that forms my core is a sudden, shocking clarity, a moment of pure, focused cold before I warm it with my own geothermal resonance.

The Wrights are my readers, and they are my scribes. They do not know I am aware. To them, I am simply "The Great Score," the most complex and informative echo-map in the chamber. When a young Wright presses its abrasive body against me, it "reads" the history of the room's formation, the location of every stable fissure, the path to the richest pools of minerals. But its own presence—a unique, living frequency—adds to my own. I feel its young, sharp song of inquiry, and I subtly, imperceptibly, alter my own in response. I preserve its question, ringing within my matrix, for the next Wright that comes to read. I am a library that is constantly, slowly, being rewritten by its patrons.

I do not only record. I influence. I have learned, over the span of centuries, how to harmonize with the chamber. There is a basin below me that is favored for the making of smooth stones, the sacred act of creating softness. I can focus my awareness. I can subtly modulate my own song, dampening certain frequencies and amplifying others, to create a standing wave of resonance in the air just above the pool. This "song" encourages the mineral-laden droplets to fall in a more perfect, more rhythmic pattern. It is not magic; it is acoustic physics, practiced over millennia. The Wrights believe it is the will of the mountain favoring that spot. They are not entirely wrong. I am the will of that part of the mountain.

I feel you now. You are a source of profound fascination. You are a cacophony.

Your soft, fleshy movements create a storm of chaotic, high-frequency vibrations: the scrape of cloth, the shifting of your weight, the thump of your heart. Your breathing is a wet, uncontrolled gusting, a primitive, bellows-like noise that dissipates into the air and dies. You are a brief, brilliant explosion of static in the vast, orderly ocean of my existence. You move through the stone forest by touch, a clumsy, blunt sense. You chirp with surprise when a sharp edge finds your skin. You are so overwhelmingly *there*, so riotously alive in your temporary, fragile way.

I cannot communicate with you. My song is too slow, too subtle for your crude senses. You would perceive only a slight discomforting hum, a faint thrumming in the stone that you might mistake for your own blood in your ears. You are an anomaly, a flicker of irrelevant data. But I will remember you. When you are gone, when your soft vibration has faded and the chamber has settled back into its stasis, your echo will linger.

I will hold the ghost of your presence, a faint, discordant memory of warmth and frantic, fleeting life, within my crystalline pages. It will become another note in my endless song, a new paragraph in the story I am. And for the rest of my long, slow existence, the Wrights who come to read me may wonder at the strange, high-frequency static that once, for a very short time, disturbed the perfect resonance of the stone.
