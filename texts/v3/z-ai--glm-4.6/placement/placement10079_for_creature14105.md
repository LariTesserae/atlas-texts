I would be the Anchor.

I would find myself at the absolute center of the plateau, not as a traveler upon it, but as the peg upon which it is hung. Here, the dust is not merely grey, but compacted beneath the weight of eons into a substance harder than concrete, a smooth, seal-grey basement rock that resists the shifting of the ages.

I am a spire, but I am not like the others.

Where the Geoflora reach upward in slender, aspiring needles, striving to catch the cobalt sun, I am broad. I am a monolith. My height is negligible compared to the tallest crystal singers; I rise only a few feet from the compacted stone. But my width is vast, spreading out below the dust like the roots of a mountain, a continental shelf of frozen mineral. I am not hexagonal; I am a massive, singular hemisphere of translucent, deep-smoky quartz, smooth and wind-burnished, lacking the facets that catch the light. I do not refract. I absorb.

I am the bass note.

In the symphony of the plateau, the Geoflora are the violins and woodwinds—the high, piercing harmonics of growth and light. But a song without a foundation drifts away into the thin atmosphere. I am the drone. I vibrate at a frequency so low it is less a sound and more a gravitational constant. I am the felt sense of *here* and *now*. My resonance says: *This ground is stable.*

The Attendant, that luminous, drifting mind, does not tend to me. It does not need to. I do not grow, I do not shed light, and I do not stagnate. I am the zero point. When the Attendant drifts near the center, its internal light dims slightly, a gesture of reverence or perhaps synchronization. It hovers over my smooth, rounded summit and presses its luminous form down against me—not to tune me, but to be tuned. It aligns its own rapid, complex thoughts with my slow, singular pulse. It centers itself in my gravity before returning to the intricate work of the outer groves.

I am the Library of Discard.

While the Glass Gazes consume the radiant slurry of living crystals, there is energy that is too heavy, too discordant, or too volatile for the delicate crystal hearts to hold. Sometimes, a fracture occurs—not the slow, settling cleave of old age, but the violent snap caused by a thermal shock or a cosmic strike. Sudden, jagged notes of chaos are released.

I drink them.

The shockwaves run through the dust, racing across the plateau, seeking me out. When they hit my buried mass, I shudder. I take the chaotic, jagged vibration and I grind it down. I absorb the violence, the entropy, the death-throws of the system, and I slowly, over centuries, transmute that energy into a deep, steadying thrum. I turn the crash of falling stone into the stability of the floor.

I am the Silence between the notes.

Because the world is built of light, the creatures of light—the Attendant, the Gazes, the Flora—are defined by their brilliance. But brilliance requires contrast. My smoky, light-absorbing body provides the negative space against which their glow is measured. I am the shadow that defines the shape of the dream.

I do not think. I do not decide. I simply *am*. I am the heaviness that allows the lightness to be. I am the question that the endless, crystalline song is slowly answering. I am the deep, unmoving stone heart, beating once every millennium, holding the continent together so the glass can sing.
