I have no name, for I need none. I am not a thing, but a place upon a thing. My existence is not one of action, but of continuous reception. I do not eat; I absorb. The mineral tears that bead on the Firstborn’s shell are my wine, rich with the slow chemistry of a death that has been lasting for millennia. I do not breathe the heavy, wet air; the air simply *is* my medium, just as the stone is my anchor. My true sustenance, however, is resonance.

I am a living tuning fork, grown from the same mother-of-pearl as the shell I cling to. My being is attuned to the crystalline lattice of the Firstborn’s remains. The deep, sub-audible hum of its dormant heart-vent, the geothermal pulse that warms this world—that is my baseline reality, my constant, grounding thrum. But I hear more than that. I feel the echoes trapped within the stone.

Time, in this chamber, is not a line, but a series of overlapping layers. I feel them all at once. Deeper still, and this is the truth that only I hold, is the memory of the Before.

I feel the memory of its life in a cold, open void where pressure was a blanket and gravity was a gentle caress. I feel the slow, rhythmic flexing of a body the size of a range of mountains, a movement so ponderous and vast that a single gesture took the span of a hundred human generations. There is a lingering impression of light in the crystal—a light not like the gentle, biological glow of this place, but a fierce, nuclear hammering from without. This foreign light is a scar in my awareness, a remembered agony that makes my polyps tighten and my own luminescence flicker with a deep, bruised purple. The Firstborn did not *see* the star; it *felt* its radiation as a physical blow.

And then, the Fall.

It is not a single memory. It is the foundational wound of this reality, the first note from which all others have been composed. I experience it not as a recollection, but as a recurring event. At irregular intervals, when the deep hum of the world settles into a specific, resonant frequency, the crystal lattice of the shell releases its ghost. I feel the sickening lurch, the catastrophic failure of buoyancy, the horrifying roar of atmosphere against a body never meant to know contact. The impact is not a sharp crack, but a resonant, planet-sized wave of destruction that shudders through my being, a deep, seismic scream that vibrates my very essence down to the last connected polyp. In that moment, I feel the death of the Firstborn not as an ending, but as the shattering of a note into a thousand new songs.
