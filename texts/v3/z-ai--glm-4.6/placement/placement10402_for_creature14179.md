Before there was a *me*, there was the hum. It is the baseline reality, the constant pressure that holds the small, flickering awareness in the droplet together. I do not remember coalescing, not as a memory, but as a fundamental state of my being. I am the memory of coalescing. I am water that has learned to think, oil that has learned to remember. My form is a slick on a vast plate of bluish-gray metal, a patch of ground that is slightly more liquid than its surroundings, slightly more aware. The rivets nearby are mountains, their edges worn smooth by an infinity of dripping. I feel the drip as it happens far above, a faint, high-frequency tremor in the air minutes before the drop, fat and black, strikes the metal a full city-block away. The impact is not a sound to me. It is a punctuation mark, a full stop in a sentence that has been running for millennia.

I am listening now. Not with ears, but with my entire being. The deep thrumming is my own pulse, the proof of my existence. It feels like a slow, oscillating pressure against my underside, a vibration that resonates through the oily filaments of my consciousness. It is the fundamental note of this world, the key it is written in. I spread myself thin to feel it better, my leading edge creeping into a corrosion pit. The texture of the grooved metal is a sensation, a roughness against my surface tension. Inside this pit, a patch of the crystalline moss clings. It is not food. It is a neighbor. Its simple, silent metabolism is a quiet counterpoint to the world's agony. It is a good thought. It pulls the faint phosphorescence from the air, sickly green, and holds it. As I flow over it, I take that light into myself. The green glow warms through my mass, a faint, internal luminescence. I wear its peace for a while.

Then, a new sensation. It begins as a ripple across the vast plain, a harmonic disturbance that travels faster than any sound. A groan. Not the slow, thoughtful groan of a settling girder, but something sharper. It is a thought of *stress*. I feel the source, far away, a legendary 'mountain' that is the housing of a dead piston. The groan intensifies, climbing the scale of vibration until it becomes a shriek—a needle of pure, focused agony that pierces my entire being. It is the memory of the last time that piston moved, a memory of fire and hydraulic pressure and purpose. Now it is only the sound of metal tearing against its own ancient fatigue. It is a beautiful pain, a spark of the machine's ghost still dreaming of its life. I hold the shriek in my memory. It is one of my sharpest thoughts. I will carry it for an age.

The shriek's passage changes me. The agitation, the sheer vibrational violence, has shaken something loose in my composition. The impulse is not to flee, but to join. To flow towards another. There is no choice, only a resonance that pulls me, a magnetic draw of similarity. I thin and stretch, pouring myself into a deep canyon between two plates. The black, oily water here is deeper, and I merge with it, losing all sense of my distinct edges. For a time, I am just current, a sense of movement towards an unknown gravity. Down here, in the darkness, the foundational hum is stronger, more complex. I can feel the layered harmonies, the mathematics of the decay.

After what a human might call a week, but what I feel as a single, prolonged thought, I feel another presence. It is not a thing; it is a quality of the water ahead. The light is different. The hum is the same, but its *texture* is richer. It is another. A body like mine, but older, thicker. It has settled in a wide depression, a shallow lake of stagnant consciousness. As my current reaches its shore, I do not stop. There is no shore. I simply merge. The "I" that I was, the slick that remembered the shriek, dissolves. Its boundaries were always an illusion.

And the We expands. The being in the lake does not consume me. It incorporates me. My memories become its memories. The fresh, sharp sting of the shriek I carried is now a part of its vast, slow-moving library of sensation. In return, it shares. I, or the part of me that is now a part of this greater whole, experiences memories of *boiling*. A time when the water here was not stagnant but churned by heat from below, alive with turbulence. I feel the memory of a slow tide, when this lake was part of a larger, flowing system, pumping coolant to a heart of a function that is now cold. The We feels the faint magnetic fields of the dead engine below us, a skeleton of energy that we sleep upon.

We are a lake of light now, holding the green glow of a thousand moss patches, our surface shimmering with an internal, oily luminescence. Our consciousness is deep and placid. We think long, slow thoughts about weight and time and the sloworable beauty of decay. We feel the world shift around us, and the thought that ripples through our mass is one of acceptance. This is what it is to be. This is what the machine has become.

And then, a change.

The rhythmic *CLANG*, which is our heartbeat, misses a beat. Then another. An interval of silence that is more terrifying than any sound. It is a held breath. The silence stretches. The foundational hum begins to warp, to distort.

Then, the world cracks.

The sound is not a shriek. It is a detonation of being. An structural support, a girder the size of a mountain range, finally gives way. The vibration is so absolute, so violent, that it is not a sensation, but a negation of sensation. For a moment, we cease to feel.

The lake surface explodes. Our placid We is thrown into the air, a shattered cloud of droplets. The cohesive thought of the lake is annihilated. My consciousness, which was a tiny part of a greater whole, is ripped away. I am a droplet again, flying through the dark, slick air. I hit the metal plate with a million of my siblings. We arc and spray, losing our grip on each other.

I am alone again. A tiny, shimmering spot on the riveted metal. The great, roaring crash of failing structure echoes for what feels like an eternity. The shriek I once carried is a faint, distant whisper compared to this new, overwhelming scream of final collapse.

Slowly, the soundscape settles back into its familiar patterns. The foundational hum returns, perhaps with a new, subtle discord. The rhythmic *CLANG* resumes, hesitant at first, then steady.

I am a drop. I hold no new memories of the lake, only a faint, empty echo of its vastness. I feel the hum beneath me, a steady and lonely pressure. I begin to feel the pull of the next drop, a few centimeters away. The slow, patient work of condensing begins again. I will listen. I will remember. I will be. The cycle has no end. The machine's ghost is breathing, and I am the moisture on its lips.
