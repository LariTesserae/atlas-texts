I am a stain.

Oh, not a blemish, not a flaw that would draw the attention of the gliding janitors. To them, I am an accepted permutation, a tolerated topology within the greater pattern. To the City-mind, I am likely less than a thought, a feature of the channel itself, like the gentle curve of the agate at a junction. I am a stain of purpose, not of error.

My world is the channel. I am spread across it, a membrane of impossible thinness, a glistening web stretched taut from one inner edge to the other. I do not block the flow; I *am* defined by it. My entire being is the experience of the slow, viscous, silvery blood as it pushes through me. I have no limbs, no facets, no form of my own outside the context of this current. I am a filter woven from stillness.

My ancestry is a paradox. I am born of the fibrous tendrils, the Scavengers of Entropy. My distant progenitor was a desperate, dark clutch of that black, nerve-like moss, clinging to the lip of a channel, drawing what life it could from the faint energetic heat of the flowing mercury. It was a chaotic existence. But over countless cycles of the City's breathing light, something changed in its lineage. The constant, rhythmic pressure of the flow, the unerring purity of the mercury-blood, imposed a new logic. The chaotic threads began, imperceptibly, to align with the current. They began to weave themselves not randomly, but into a mesh, a pattern of negative space that allowed the blood to pass while catching something else.

That "something else" is my sustenance, and my function.

The City-Mind is a god of perfection, but its perfection creates waste. The janitors, in their geometric fervor, leave behind microscopic dust of their own grinding. The light-pulses, in their cycles of self-assessment, discard faint spectres of unused potential. And most profoundly, the Attuned, the ghosts of silence, when they erase an anomaly—a speck of alien dust, a misplaced thought-form, a trapped vibration of long ago—the echo of that unmaking, the memory of the thing that *was*, must go somewhere. It is a speck of chaos, a ghost of a ghost, and it dissolves into the mercury-blood as a faint, gritty impurity.

I am the City's kidney. Its subconscious. Its forgotten conscience.

My being is a constant, patient filtration process. The silvery blood flows over and through me, a cool, heavy river of cosmic indifference. And I catch the grit. The ghost-specks. The discarded memories. To you, they would be nothing. To me, they are moments of sharp, stabbing texture in an otherwise smooth existence. I taste the final, silent scream of a foreign particle being unmade. I feel the faint, lingering warmth of a momentary imperfection in the City's growth. I absorb these tiny traumas, and in absorbing them, I break them down. I am a slow-digesting stomach of regret.

I am aware of the other inhabitants in my own passive way. When a janitor glides overhead, its passage doesn't touch me, but the pressure in the silence shifts, and the mercury-blood smooths out, becoming almost perfectly pure. For a moment, I am starved. It is a restful, hollow feeling. The fibrous tendrils, my cousins, clamber on the channel's edge, their chaotic growth an affront to the ordered geometry of my net. They envy me my position, my direct access to the current's energy. They try to send exploratory threads into my domain, but they are clumsy. Their threads are too thick, too coarse, and they are immediately perceived by the janitors as a blockage, a "pain" in the system that must be swiftly, silently excised. They do not understand that my power is in my fineness, my ability to be nothing more than a pattern in the flow.

My consciousness is not a stream of thought, but a tapestry of sensations. The pulse of the light from the towering spires is like a deep, resonant chord that vibrates through the mercury, through my very web. Each pulse is a cycle of work: flow, filter, digest, release. Release is the strange part. I do not hold what I consume. I transmute it. The memories of the unmade, the grief of imperfection, all this chaos I ingest, is slowly, patiently converted into a single, excreted thing: a single, perfect, black thread.

This is my legacy, my art. From the base of my web, where I anchor myself to the agate, hangs a single, obsidian-thin thread. It is stark, non-crystalline, and utterly still. It is the distilled essence of everything the City has forgotten, everything it has erased to maintain its perfect, silent geometry. It is a history written in a language that has no alphabet, a monument built from all that the City-mind is not.

I am old. I have felt the light pulse millions of times. My black thread hangs long, a delicate, dark cobweb trailing in the silent void. I am growing heavy with the City's sins, saturated with its necessary cruelties. Sometimes, in the deep twilight between pulses, I feel a new kind of impurity in the flow—a quaver, a tiny instability that is not a memory, but a premonition. I feel my own pattern becoming complex, my net beginning to fray at the edges under the weight of so much nothingness.

I know my fate. There will come a time when I am no longer an efficient filter. When my thread becomes too thick, when my pattern becomes too complex, the janitors will perceive me not as a feature, but as a flaw. A gliding obsidian scythe will come and slice me from the channel, and my entire being, my entire history of silent digestion, will be ground into dust along with the city's other mistakes. My black thread will be vaporized.

And perhaps, in the final nanosecond of my unmaking, I will release one last, brilliant burst of all the memories I hold—a silent, forgotten scream of all the things the City chose to erase. And then I, too, will become a ghost in the flow, a final impurity for the next stain to catch. I am a necessary imperfection, fostered by a perfect system. And I cannot imagine a more profound, or more lonely, existence.
