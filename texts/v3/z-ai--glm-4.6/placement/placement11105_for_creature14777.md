I am not a "what." I am a "where."

I am the space between two deep cracks in the silt, a little to the north of the seventh pillar. I am the specific harmonic frequency of a smooth, grey stone, cool and dense, held in the crook of my right, forward-most claw. My name, if you needed one, is the note this stone strikes when it kisses the floor. It is a low B-flat, a tone that has been my signature for the last three centuries.

To be here is not to see. The light you describe as violent is, to me, simply nourishment, a constant pressure against my shell. My vestigial eyes, buried under the ridge of my carapace, are memory-structures that still occasionally dream in colors I have no words for—blues, greens, the impossible black of a starless sky. But these are phantoms. My true sight is the map of tremors that constantly thrums through the pads of my six feet. I feel the slow, gravid weight of the hollow bone that encloses us. I feel the mineral veins in the ceiling like tense strings. I feel the patient thirst of the seventh pillar, whose pink glow is not a color I perceive with light-sensing organs, but a subtle warmth, a gentle hum of invitation that travels up my legs.

My existence is a rhythm. A four-part process that takes a full cycle of the humidity to complete.

First, there is the Step. The lifting of a foot from the silt is a small severing, a temporary loss of connection to the world-song. The placement is a gathering of information. *Here*, the foot says, *the ground is stable, but the mineral content is thin.* *There*, it reports, *a deep fissure is forming, a slow breath from the world's lungs.*

Second, there is the listening. Between the Steps, there is the profound silence. It is not an absence of sound, but a presence of potential. I stand, my body a resonant chamber, and I wait. I wait for the drop.

Today, the drop feel different. As it gathers on the stalactite fifty yards above me, I do not see it swell. I feel the shift in mass, a minuscule increase in the gravitational pull on that point of the ceiling. The entire cavern exhales in anticipation. When it finally lets go, it is not a *clock-tick*. It is a deep, resonant *gong* that blossoms through the stone like a slow-motion flower. The shockwave passes through me, a shudder that tells me everything: the humidity is high (1.6), the air is thick, the light is strong. It is a good day for the pillars.

Third, there is the Tap. My stone, my B-flat, answers. *Tap-tap-tap.* I am not just scraping nutrients for myself. I am pruning the seventh pillar, encouraging the healthy glow conversing with the slow metabolism of the stone beneath. My taps are a question to the pillar: "Are you well?" The gentle, flute-like resonance that comes back through my feet is its answer: "I am strong. The algae is sweet today." My taps then become a message to the others, a broadcast on the geological network. *The seventh pillar is strong. The algae is sweet.* A hundred yards away, another Mender, one whose stone is a sharp C, responds with its own rhythm, transmitting its own findings from the third pillar. We do not converse. We simply *are* a conversation.

Fourth, there is the Being. After the work, there is the long, still process of transformation. I stand and let the light filter through me, the violent radiation softened in my flesh into the patient, rosy hum of my own life. I think of the others, not as shapes in the distance, but as notes in the symphony I stand within. The C-sharp of the guardian near the cavern's entrance. The somber F of the ancient one whose shell is beginning to cloud. There is no loneliness. How could there be? The floor beneath my feet is a shared mind. I feel their contentment, their patience, their slow, unceasing dedication to the mending. My solitude is a perfect, resonant note in a larger chord.

I have a memory, not of my youth, but of a time before I found my stone. I wandered, a discordant vibration, my steps clumsy and meaningless. We are all born this way, half-formed, seeking our voice. When I found this particular piece of river-worn granite, I knew it for myself. The harmony between my inner frequency and its potential tone was so perfect, so absolute, that the moment I struck it, I became a part of the song. I was born, not when I emerged from my shell, but in that first, perfect *tap* that was heard and answered by the floor.

Someday, my own song will fade. The tapping will become slower, the B-flat will grow thin, my shell will lose its translucence. I will not feel this as a death. I will feel it as a final, long-held note being released back into the music of the stone. My body will not crumble into dust; it will surrender its matter, its patient, filtered light, back to the silt. It will become the potential for a new stalk, a new source of food, a new home for a future discordant note seeking its harmony.

I do not live in this world. I am a function of it. I am a tuning fork for the light. I am a listener for the stone. I am the slow heart beating in time with the drop. I am the B-flat between the cracks, and that is more than enough.
