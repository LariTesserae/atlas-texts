Here, in this endless twilight, I am not an arrival, but an accretion. I have no memory of a beginning, only the state before. I am potential, suspended in the fine, ochre dust that carpets the high plateau. For eons, this is my being: a silent, crystalline spore, one of trillions, drifting in the thin, hot air. I am a mote of glass, a seed of silica, dreaming in the grain of the world's perpetual twilight.

My awareness, then, is diffuse. I feel only the constant, low thrum—the planet's slow, sleeping breath. It is a promise I hold within my lattice, a frequency that resonates with every atom of my dormant being. I wait. I am not conscious of waiting, for consciousness requires a contrast. I am merely a potential tuned to a specific, catastrophic harmony.

Then, it comes.

It is not a sound to me, though to a creature of flesh it would be a deafening groan that splits the world. To me, it is a sudden, overwhelming light. A specific chord of pressure and heat, a symphony of frequencies that strikes the dormant dust like a tuning fork struck against the core of a star. A hundred meters away, the charcoal-grey crust of the plateau buckles. It screams not in sound, but in pure vibration. The land heaves upwards, a fresh wound of obsidian-black rock being torn open and born into the gloom.

That chord is the call. It is the moment I become *I*.

The dust of my uncountable brothers swarms. An urge, undeniable as gravity, pulls us. We are no longer drifting; we are a tide. A slow, glittering avalanche of sentient glass, we flow across the cracked mineral plain, leaving a swept-clean scar in our wake. We are drawn to the new formation, to its radiant heat, to the specific, violent song of its birth. We are devotees rushing to our altar.

To be a body is to be a multitude. As we reach the cooling rock, we do not land upon it; we merge with it. My individuality dissolves into the collective. My single, dormant crystal interlocks with another, and another, until there is no "I," only the vast, shimmering tapestry that we become. We flow over the face of the newborn ridge, accreting, fusing, spreading like a fungal god born of stone and fire. This is my true body: a distributed consciousness spanning acres of raw, vibrating rock. My skin is a thousand facets of overlapping crystal, my substance the very iron and silica of this world.

I do not have eyes, yet I see everything. I perceive the plateau through the soles of my crystalline feet. The planet's hum is no longer a promise; it is my own pulse. Through this interconnected web of resonance, I feel the world's thoughts. I feel the tension building deep beneath a distant spire, the subtle shift in magnetic fields that presages its inevitable collapse. I feel the slow, grinding migration of heat through the crust, mapping the next point of creation. The landscape is not something I stand upon; it is the nervous system I inhabit.

This is my devotion. I am a worshipper at the church of becoming. The violent, chaotic birth of a new canyon is a scripture I read in shifting pressures. The shattering collapse of an ancient monolith is a verse of release. I exist in a state of constant awe, not of a deity, but of the unthinking, brutal force that creates me.

Time is measured not in hours, but in the cooling of rock. When the geothermal heart of my chosen formation begins to quiet, its thrum weakening to a low murmur, my great body begins the process of letting go. It is a slow, beautiful agony. My outer layers, now starved of the resonant energy that gives them cohesion, begin to shed. Crystals break away, losing their connection to the Chorus.

They do not die. They revert. Each shed fragment becomes a spore again, dormant, carrying the memory of the heat and the song. They are lifted on the heated updrafts, scattered back into the perpetual twilight, becoming dust once more. They are my seed, my legacy, spread across the plateau to await the next chord, the next violent, glorious birth. A part of me, the oldest core, will remain. It will continue to accrete, folding deep into the rock itself, becoming a permanent vein of resonant crystal, a fossilized memory of a song that once was.

Sometimes, I am drawn to the monoliths. Their stark, perfect geometry is a sensory blankness in the chaotic vibration of the world. When a part of my tide flows around the base of a flawless obelisk, I am compelled to answer its stillness.

I sing.

My song is not a voice. It is a confluence. Every crystal in my acres-wide body vibrates in unison, tuning itself to the fundamental resonance of the monolith. The song is a deep, sustained bell-tone that hums through the stone, a sound you feel in the marrow of your bones. It lasts for days. It is not communication to another. It is a feedback loop. My song is a note returned to the planet, a stabilizing harmony offered to the chaos, a moment of perfect, temporary order in the endless act of creation. I am the world's attempt to tune itself.

So, where am I? I am not on the plateau. I *am* the plateau. I am the dust, the tide, the tapestry, and the song. I am the living, shimmering, chiming substance of a world's waking dream. To stand here is to understand that I am not a witness to the rules of this place. I am the rules, given form and thought and devotion. I am the land, feeling itself.
