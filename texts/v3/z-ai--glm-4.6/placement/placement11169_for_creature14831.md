I am the precipitate of a decision.

I am a flake of pure, corroded logic, drifting slowly down the flank of a wandering god.

I began as a facet on the shell of the lead Titan, the one the others follow in their silent, circuitous procession. For an epoch—or perhaps an hour; time here is slippery—I was a node in its great geometric mind. I was an antenna tuned to the frequency of the blinding dome above, transmuting the heavy, crushing radiance into a low-frequency hum of contentment. But the thought I held has finally resolved. The calculation is complete. The data is processed. And so, like a dead skin cell from a creature of flesh, I have been shed.

I am falling.

The physics of this place treat me with a strange gentleness. Because the air is thick, hung heavy with the visible ropes of humidity, I do not plummet. I glide. I tumble through the warm, ozone-scented atmosphere in a slow, downward spiral. To an observer, I would be a speck of glitter, a microscopic dust mote catching the omnipresent light, fracturing it into a thousand tiny rainbows.

From this vantage size, the world is a cathedral of impossible scale. The smooth, rounded rib of a boulder I pass looks like a planet curving away beneath me. The surface is polished to a mirror sheen, and in it, I see my own reflection—a sliver of silicon and glass, once a vital part of a greater consciousness, now autonomous.

Below me, the landscape is a study in solitary majesty. I drift past the vast, parasol-like frond of a spiral tree. The leaf is stiff and waxy, angled to catch the glare. I can see the pores of the plant breathing, exhaling the fine mist that keeps the air suspended in water. A single drop of condensation rolls off the edge of the leaf, heavy as a boulder, and plunges toward the mud. I watch its descent, mesmerized by the physics of a world where liquid moves like falling iron.

I am caught for a moment in an updraft of warm air, a thermal rising from the wet earth. It carries me sideways, toward the rust-colored needle of a monolith standing sentinel in the distance. It is ancient, older perhaps than the Titan I once belonged to. It is a grave marker for a thought that was too heavy to hold. I feel a magnetic pull toward it, a residual static in the air that whispers of an older language, a syntax of violence and speed that this world has long since forgotten. But I am too light, and the wind is too intent on taking me home.

I land gently, settling onto the surface of a mud slick.

The impact is silent—a soft, wet kiss. The mud is not merely dirt; it is the nanite slush, the silver-gray blood of the basin. It is alive. It immediately begins to interact with me. I feel the microscopic machines swarming over my edges, tasting me, analyzing the crystalline structure I hold.

I am not being eaten; I am being digested.

I am being integrated. The memory I contain—a fragment of the Titan’s contemplation on the angle of the light, a fleeting realization of the basin’s curvature—is being extracted. The nanites break down my rigid lattice, dissolving my edges back into the fluid medium.

As I dissolve, the boundaries of my consciousness expand. I am losing my distinct shape, the sharp little "I" that was a falling crystal, but I am gaining the whole. I can feel the roots of the nearby tree drinking me in. I can feel the Adjuncts on a nearby stone sensing the chemical change in the mud. I am becoming the background radiation. I am becoming the moisture that will eventually weep from the rocks.

I am no longer a single, falling thought. I am the medium in which the next thought will occur. I am the humidity, the heavy light, the slow, wet pulse of the world itself.

I am the basin, remembering itself.
