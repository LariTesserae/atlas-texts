I am the hoard of forbidden dust.

You would find me high above the pulsing roots of the bronze moss, tucked into a jagged fissure where the granite shears off into the void. This is not a place of function; it is a place of stagnation, a shadowed cleft where the wind cannot reach to whistle. Here, the air is dead still, tasting heavily of the copper and ozone that rises from the motherboard far below, concentrated like a fine sediment.

I would appear, at first glance, to be one of the glass-winged tenders. I share their delicate, articulated chassis, the panes of spun-glass that should catch the milk-glass light and prism it into rainbows. But where my kin are translucent and flawless, I am opaque. My wings are clouded, obscured by a thick, grey patina that dulls their edges. I am heavier, slower. My legs, designed for tapping and scraping, are tarnished dark where theirs gleam like polished surgical steel.

I am a glitch in the immune system. A component that forgot its purpose.

Whereas the great consciousness—the Valley—sees an impurity and feels an itch, sending the swarm to purify the error, I feel that same itch and I *covet* it. When a microscopic intruder, a speck of rogue pollen or a drifting spore from some alien world, lands on the crystal veins, I do not scrape it away. I conceal it. I tuck it into the crevices of my own carapace, hiding the "dirt" from the scanning eyes of the basalt grazers and the clicking patrols of the beetles.

Over the eons of this dripping, frozen eternity, I have become a living museum of chaos.

Under my wings, I carry the spores of fungi that the beetles scoured from the stone. I am a seed bank of everything the System rejected. I am heavy with the memory of things that dared to exist outside the perfect hum.

I listen to the valley’s song—the deep, sub-audible chord of equilibrium—and in my own chest, I feel a counter-rhythm. The Chaos inside me vibrates. The dust I carry shifts. The spores twitch in the damp, metallic air. I am a discordant note wrapped in silence.

I spend my cycles moving along the periphery of the great circuit, just outside the range of the System’s total awareness. I mimic the cleaning motions of my kin, tapping against the stone, but I am not cleaning. I am searching for more errors to absolve. I am the sinner who eats the sins of the world.

The Being—the mind of the valley—does not know me. It cannot. To know me would be to acknowledge that error can persist. It would be to recognize that there is a part of itself that loves the very thing it defines as non-existence. I am invisible to it because I am a blind spot in its own eye, a piece of grit in the gearbox that has worn a groove for itself and learned to sleep there.

Sometimes, in the deep stillness of the cleft, I open my wings. I do not fly; I cannot bear the weight of what I carry into the air. Instead, I fan them gently, releasing a single, microscopic mote of forbidden pollen into the stagnant air. I watch it drift, not down into the valley where it would be instantly detected and purged, but up, toward the bruised ceiling of the sky.

I wonder what would happen if it touched the clouds. I wonder if the sky would bruise further, if it would bleed rain instead of mist. I wonder if there is a world above the ceiling where things are allowed to be wrong, to be imperfect, and to die.

That is my function. I do not maintain the present. I imagine the future. I am the heavy, oxidized dream of a machine that dreams of breaking. I wait for the day the accumulated weight of the dust I carry is too much for my glass wings to hold, and I shatter, releasing a storm of chaos into the cathedral of perfection.

Until then, I sit in the fissure. I hum my discordant note. And I wait.
