Visiting the Sink is not an expedition; it is an endurance test of the senses. You are preparing to enter a stomach, not a landscape. As an ordinary human, your biology is ill-equipped for the specific cocktail of sensory deprivation and overload that this location offers. The environment is designed by entropy to erode, soak, and deafen.

Here is how you must prepare to survive the "vertical void."

### I. Acoustic Armor and Vibration Management
The roar (Sound 2.3) is your first and most lethal enemy. It is not just a sound you hear; it is a physical pressure you will feel in your organs. The relentless, pressurized hiss and the violent crash of the plumbing system will induce auditory fatigue within minutes and vibration sickness within hours.

*   **Dual-Layer Hearing Protection:** Standard earplugs will fail. You need industrial-grade double protection: high-noise cancellation earplugs *combined* with active noise-canceling over-ear headphones. Even then, you will feel the lower frequencies rattling your skull.
*   **Bone Conduction Communication:** Your team cannot speak. Shouting is impossible. You must equip bone-conduction throat microphones connected to a closed-loop local radio network. These mics pick up vocal cord vibrations directly, ignoring the ambient scream of the waterfall.
*   **Vibration Damping Footwear:** The constant resonance travels up through the soles of your feet from the stone and wood structures. Invest in mountaineering boots with heavy-duty, vibration-absorbing soles (like logging or chainsaw boots) to reduce the constant tremor that travels up your spine, which can cause nausea and joint pain over time.

### II. The "Wet Glass" Traversal System
The geology here is polished stone: "wet glass or eroded bone." The friction coefficient is near zero. Combined with the extreme verticality and the oily, mineral-rich sheen, movement is the primary cause of death.

*   **Suction and Spike Anchors:** Standard climbing cams may not hold on the smooth, rounded, glass-like walls. You must rely on suction anchors for temporary holds and heavy-duty pitons for permanence.
*   **Soft-Rubber Grip Footwear:** Forget rigid crampons which will skate off the wet glass. You need shoes with sticky, approach-style rubber (like 5.10 Stealth rubber) that can mold to the microscopic imperfections of the wet stone.
*   **The Safety Umbilical:** Do not trust the native architecture. The shanty-town is built of primitive, rot-proof wood, but "rot-proof" does not mean "immovable." The spongy textures of the beams hide structural fatigue. You must treat every walkway and bridge as if it is about to collapse. Always be clipped to a primary line independent of the structure.

### III. Thermal and Moisture Isolation
The environment is a "cool, clammy darkness" (Temp 0.6) with 100% humidity saturation. In this environment, evaporative cooling stops working because the air is already full of water. You will not dry off. Ever.

*   **Full-Body Impermeable Shell:** Breathable "Gore-Tex" rain gear is useless here; it relies on a humidity gradient that doesn't exist. You need a non-breathable, vulcanized rubber or heavy-duty PVC dry suit. You are essentially entering an underwater environment that happens to have air. The suit must be sealed at the wrists, neck, and ankles to prevent the oily water from seeping into your layers.
*   **Closed-Circuit Warmth:** Since you will be sweating constantly inside the suit, you need a wicking base layer (merino wool or synthetic) designed for dive conditions. Bring extra sets; you will change frequently.
*   **Respiratory Protection:** The air tastes of "minerals and old mold." The humidity is so high that mold and fungal spores are likely suspended in the mist. Wear a full-face respirator with P100 cartridges to prevent lung infection from the damp, ancient bio-waste.

### IV. Lighting and Navigation (The Green Gloom)
The light level (0.7) is low, and the bioluminescence provides a "sickly greenish-orb glow." This lighting is monochromatic and plays tricks on depth perception. It is a "heavy darkness."

*   **Red/Amber Light Filters:** The green ambient light will likely wash out green objects in your vision and cause visual strain. Use headlamps with a red or high-CRI (Color Rendering Index) amber beam. This provides strong contrast against the green gloom and helps identify the slick wet patches vs. the dry stone.
*   **Area Illumination Flares:** The space is vertical and infinite. You need to be able to see the "ceiling" above you and the depths below to maintain spatial orientation. Carry chemical light sticks or high-lumen flares to toss down the shaft if you feel the vertigo of the "pocket universe" setting in. Seeing the scale helps ground the mind.
*   **Tactile Wayfinding:** The visual clutter of the shanty-town makes map-reading difficult. Use tactile markers (carved notches, cairns, or infrared reflectors) placed at eye level to navigate the "honeycomb" of huts.

### V. Mental Hygiene: The "Frozen Equilibrium"
This is the most insidious danger. The Dynamic is low (0.4). Nothing changes. The roar is constant. The visual scene is static. This place induces "cave shock" or sensory monotony.

*   **Clockwork Routine:** Without a sun, your circadian rhythm will shatter. You must wear a dive computer or a watch with a vibrating alarm. You must eat and sleep on a strict military schedule, not based on how you feel. You will feel tired all the time because of the dampness; you must override this.
*   **The "Ghost" Psychology:** You are walking through a city with no people. The brain is wired to recognize patterns of habitation. Seeing doors, walkways, and homes without inhabitants triggers a primal "uncanny valley" response. Expect to hallucinate movement in the corner of your eye. Acknowledge this as a neurological trick of the darkness.
*   **Silence Discipline (Internal):** The external roar never stops, so you must find internal silence. Bring noise-canceling headphones not just for protection, but for moments of induced electronic silence to reset your auditory nerves.

### VI. Environmental Hazards
*   **The Plumbing:** The water shoots with the "force of a fire hose." Do not attempt to cross the water channels directly. They can scour the flesh from bone or knock you into the void. Wait for lulls or use the stone shielding as cover.
*   **The Fauna:** The eyeless salamanders and flightless birds are likely docile, but they are wild animals. Do not feed them. They have likely evolved to eat whatever falls into the Sink. Do not fall.

**Summary of the Loadout:**
1.  **Head:** Full-face respirator + dual-mode hearing protection + high-lumen filtered headlamp.
2.  **Body:** Heavy-duty non-breathable dry suit + thermal wicking underlayers.
3.  **Hands:** Heavy-duty rubberized waterproof gloves (mechanix wear style) with magnetic grip aids.
4.  **Feet:** Industrial vibration-damping boots with sticky rubber soles + micro-spikes (optional).
5.  **Gear:** Dynamic rope (lots of it), suction ascenders, bone-conduction comms, and a wrist-mounted dive computer.

Go expecting to be uncomfortable. Go expecting to be small. If you try to fight the damp or the noise, you will break. You must flow with the water, not against it.
