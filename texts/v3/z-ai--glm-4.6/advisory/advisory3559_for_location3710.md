Traveler, listen closely. If you are used to the warm, green, slow-spinning worlds of the comfy multiverse, leave those habits at the portal. You are not going to a nature reserve; you are reporting to the floor of a living, open-pit mine located in the stratosphere. This location is a factory floor where the workers are made of granite and the product is erosion.

Here is how you prepare to survive the "Weeping Rocks."

### 1. The Acoustic Protocol: Your Life Depends on Earplugs
This is the most critical safety rule. The ambient quiet here is a lie. The silence is just the space between the working shifts of the Strata-Mills.

*   **Sonic Liquefaction Hazard:** The Strata-Mills do not chew; they resonate. When a Mill engages a cliff face, it emits a sub-sonic hum that vibrates rock into soup. At close range, this resonance will turn your internal organs into jelly long before the heat of the friction kills you. You cannot hear the deadly frequency, but you will feel it in your teeth.
*   **The Gear:** Standard passive earplugs are useless. You need a set of active, spectrum-filtering acoustic dampeners. These must be calibrated to hard-block low-frequency vibrations (sub-100 Hz) while allowing you to hear mid-to-high frequencies—specifically the *cracking* of the freezing verglas and the *scuttling* of the scavengers.
*   **The "Bone-Check":** If your dampeners fail and you feel the hum in your marrow, you do not wait. You run. The liquefaction radius is wider than you think.

### 2. Respiratory and Thermal Engineering
Forget "layers." You need a pressure suit.

*   **The Atmosphere:** It is thin—high altitude hypoxia is a constant threat. Your suit needs a pressurized oxygen supply or a significant rebreather capability. Furthermore, the air is not just empty; it is chemically "active." The Strata-Mills exhale superheated silicates and mineral salts. Walking into the exhaust plume of a feeding Mill without a heavy-duty particulate filter will coat your lungs in cement.
*   **The "Wet" Cold:** The description mentions water that freezes as it falls. This is a supercooled environment. Your outer shell must be hydrophobic on a molecular level. If moisture touches you, it freezes instantly. If your gloves get damp from the "weeping" rocks, the resulting ice will bite through to the bone.
*   **Eye Protection:** The "tarnished silver" light is dim, but the reflection off the wet, frozen basalt is disorienting. Wear goggles with photochromatic lenses tuned to high-contrast amber or yellow. This will help you distinguish the "steel-wool" grass and the obsidian weasels against the grey stone.

### 3. The Footwear: Mastering the Mirror
The ground is a treacherous mix of slick ice and frozen mud that shatters like spun sugar.

*   **No Crampons:** Do not bring standard mountaineering crampons. The ice here is not glacier ice; it is delicate, pipe-organ thin, and covers vertical faces. Standard spikes will shatter the verglas curtains, causing avalanches of sharp glass that will cut you to ribbons.
*   **Suction and Micro-Grip:** You need footwear with a soft, sticky compound sole (like climbing approach shoes on steroids) combined with retractable micro-spikes for the frozen mud patches. You must move silently and delicately. Every step you take sounds like a gunshot in the heavy silence; do not announce yourself to the weasels.

### 4. The Fauna: Industrial Machinery and Ambush Predators
You are stepping into a biological supply chain. You are neither the consumer nor the producer; you are a malfunction.

*   **Strata-Mills (The Background Hazard):**
    *   **Behavior:** They move glacially. They do not hunt you. However, they do not see you. You are dust to them.
    *   **Safety Zone:** Never approach the "head" of a Mill. If the ground vibrates rhythmically, the machine is engaged. Keep a distance of at least 500 meters from the sonic source.
    *   **The Exhaust:** If the wind shifts and you smell hot sulfur and wet stone, hold your breath and move perpendicular to the wind. The "fertilizer" clouds are chemically aggressive and will blind and choke you.

*   **Scavengers (The Cleaners):**
    *   Dog-sized beetles with chitin and metal parts. They are generally skittish but will bite if cornered. They are attracted to the heat signature of your suit. Keep a portable flare or chemical heat pack to throw as a distraction if they swarm you.

*   **Obsidian Weasels (The True Threat):**
    *   These are the assassins of the plateau. They move at "terrifying speed."
    *   **Camouflage:** They are black stone in a shadowed valley. Your goggles (mentioned above) are your only defense against spotting them before they pounce.
    *   **Defense:** You cannot outrun them. Do not carry a ballistic weapon; the recoil will knock you off balance on the ice, and the noise will trigger a sonic stress reaction in the local geology. Carry a "sonic horn"—a compressed air blast that mimics the distress call of a Mill. It might startle the weasel long enough for you to retreat.

### 5. Mental Preparation: The Psychology of the Empty
This is the hardest part to pack for.

*   **The Color Palette:** It is dim, grey, and silver. For prolonged stays, this absence of color can cause depression and visual hallucinations. Pack a brightly colored piece of fabric to attach to your pack—not for rescue, but for your own sanity. Remind yourself that the universe has colors other than slate and oyster-grey.
*   **The Sound of Creation:** You will hear the "ticking and cracking" of freezing water constantly. It is like being trapped inside a freezing clock. Do not try to block it out; accept it as the heartbeat of the world. Meditate on the sound. If you fight the silence, the silence will crush you.
*   **The Scale:** You are standing on a mountain that hasn't existed long enough to have a name. You will feel insignificant, temporary, and fragile. Lean into that. You are a tourist watching a forge. Do not touch the anvil.

### Summary Checklist for the Pack:
1.  **Active sonic-dampening headset** (Sub-bass blocking).
2.  **Pressurized rebreather** with heavy particulate filtration.
3.  **Thermal suit** with hydrophobic, razor-resistant outer weave.
4.  **High-contrast amber goggles.**
5.  **Sticky-sole boots with retractable micro-spikes.**
5.  **Chemical heat distractions** (for scavengers).
6.  **Sonic deterrent** (for weasels).
7.  **A strip of bright orange cloth.** (For sanity).

Move slowly, breathe shallowly, and never, ever interrupt a Mill while it is working. You are visiting a factory; stay out of the gears.
