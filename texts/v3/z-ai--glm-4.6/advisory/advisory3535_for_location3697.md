**Travel Advisory: The High Tier / The Cathedral of Frozen Glass**

This is not a destination for the casual sightseer. This is a hostile, high-energy anomaly that resembles a habitat but functions as a chemical reactor. If you intend to visit the "High Tier," you are walking into a paradox of flash-freezing hydrology and seismic violence. The environment is operating at a pace and intensity that human biology was not built to withstand.

Here is your comprehensive survival guide.

### 1. Respiratory and Atmospheric Protocol
The air here is deceptive. It looks clear, but it is trying to kill you the moment you inhale.

*   **The "Iron Lung" Risk:** The atmosphere is thin (High Tier hypoxia) and saturated with a metallic taste, likely ionized mineral dust or ozone from the friction of the ice. Oxygen concentrators are mandatory.
*   **Flash-Freezing the Lungs:** The humidity is at 2.5, but the temperature is absolute freezing. If you breathe the unfiltered air, the supersaturated moisture will flash-freeze upon contact with the warm mucosa of your throat and lungs. You will suffocate on internal frost within minutes.
*   **Required Gear:** A **heated, closed-circuit rebreather**. Do not rely on standard cold-weather masks that warm air with your own breath; the exhalation valve will freeze shut instantly from the inside-out due to the external mist. You need an active, battery-powered thermal element heating the intake air to at least 20°C (68°F) before it enters your mouthpiece.

### 2. Ocular Security: The "Absolute Light"
The lighting here is pathological. It is an x-ray saturation without the mercy of an atmosphere to scatter it.

*   **Snow Blindness on Steroids:** The "whiteness" is not just reflected sunlight; it is ambient, all-penetrating radiation. The glare reflects off the wet backs of the fauna, the polished granite, and the rime-armor with 100% albedo.
*   **Shadow Confusion:** The light source is positioned to eliminate shadows or create harsh ink-blots. This destroys your depth perception.
*   **Required Gear:** **Category 5 welding goggles or a full-face HUD with auto-polarizing visors.** Standard glacier glasses are insufficient. You need to filter out the UV and x-ray spectrum. Furthermore, your HUD should have high-contrast edge-detection software turned on to help you navigate the "ink-blot" shadows so you don't step off a ledge thinking it's a flat rock.

### 3. Thermal Protection: The "Burning Cold"
It is not enough to be warm; you must be sealed.

*   **The Wet-Freeze Paradox:** Normally, -40°C is dry. Here, it is wet. The mist acts as a contact-welding agent. If a single patch of your skin is exposed, the mist will settle, flash-freeze, and bond your flesh to whatever you are touching.
*   **Required Gear:** A **hazmat-rated, waterproof exosuit** with positive internal pressure. You need insulation that is also a vapor barrier. If your suit sweats (moisture inside), and you stop moving, that sweat will freeze. Your suit needs to be powered (heated wiring) to maintain a constant shell temperature.
*   **Joint Lubrication:** Your suit’s joints must be sealed with dry lubricants. Standard grease will freeze into "glassy beads" (as the description notes) and lock your arms in place.

### 4. Mobility in a "Silent Storm"
The ground is not static. It is a rippling, twitching entity that builds and destroys itself every thirty seconds.

*   **The Traction Problem:** The terrain is "polished to a mirror sheen" by sliding ice, then coated in a gel-mist that instantly turns to frictionless glass. Ice crampons will likely snap off in the explosive freeze-thaw cycles.
*   **Recommended Tech:** **Mag-lev boots or micro-spikes with sonic vibration.** You need a way to grip the obsidian-like stone without relying on friction. If you fall, you will slide until you hit a thermal fracture or fall off the shelf.
*   **The Seismic Shudder:** Every 30 seconds, the ground ripples. Do not stand under the "stack-stones" or the bone-and-gut shanties. They are structurally temporary. When the ground ripples, the ice snaps.
*   **Strategy:** Do not run. Movement here causes friction; friction attracts the flash-freeze. Move with a fluid, gliding motion. If you run, the air displaced by your body might freeze on your visor.

### 5. Sensory Management: The Silence/Turbulence Dissonance
This is the most dangerous psychological hazard. Your brain expects sound. If you see a geyser explode six feet from your face, you expect a roar. You get silence. This disconnect can cause nausea, vertigo, and hallucinations.

*   **The Hallucination Risk:** Your brain may invent its own sound to fill the void (auditory hallucinations), or it may fail to process the visual turbulence, leading to "strobe-induced" seizures from the flashing ice.
*   **Countermeasures:** **Bone-conduction audio feed.** Pipe in white noise, brown noise, or calming music directly to your inner ear. Do not let the silence bite you. It creates a sense of isolation that can lead to panic attacks. You need to ground yourself in the reality that you are still alive, even if the world feels dead.
*   **Vertigo Pills:** The combination of a shaking horizon and lack of auditory cues will scramble your vestibular system. Antiemetics are non-negotiable.

### 6. Interaction with the "Pale Swarm"
The inhabitants (Newt-Moles) are numerous, chaotic, and indifferent, but they are essentially living biological machinery.

*   **Do Not Touch the Fauna:** They are wet. If you touch one, your suit hand will freeze to it instantly. They will likely thrash or panic, damaging your suit.
*   **Do Not Hinder the Flow:** These creatures are repairing a fragile chaos. If you block a doorway, they may attempt to "patch" *you* with mud and ice. They view everything as part of the architecture that needs stabilization.
*   **Avoid the "Town Hall":** The central tower vibrating violently is a structural hazard. The tension holding it up is the ice. If your body heat radiates near the base, you could melt a critical bond and collapse the structure, burying you and thousands of them.
*   **Camouflage:** The environment is cobalt, white, and stark black (shadows). Wear white. If you wear dark colors, the shadows won't hide you—instead, you'll become a "black hole" in their visual field, attracting attention.

### 7. Emergency Procedures
*   **If a Geyser Erupts Beneath You:** It creates a pocket of dense humid air. This can actually buoy you up. Do not swim; let the air cushion you, but brace for the instant freeze when the pocket collapses.
*   **If Your Suit Breaches:** Do not try to patch it from the outside with tape—the glue won't set in the mist. You must engage the suit's emergency "seal-stitch" (magnetic clamping) or inject instant sealant foam *internally*. You have seconds before the localized mist flash-freezes your limb.

### Summary of the Experience
You will feel like a ghost in a frenzied machine. You will hear your own breathing, the hum of your suit, and maybe the music you piped in, but visually you are in the center of a hyper-kinetic explosion. The disconnect will make you feel heavy and slow, while the world moves at double speed.

**Stay low. Stay sealed. Do not trust the ground to stay still.** This place is beautiful, yes, but it is a beauty that wants to turn you into a glass statue.
