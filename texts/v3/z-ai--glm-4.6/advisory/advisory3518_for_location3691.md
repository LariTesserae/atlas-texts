### **Traveler's Advisory & Preparation Guide: The Chime-Deep of Khos-Adun**

**Threat Classification: Omega-Prime (Hostile Environment, Incompatible Biology, Extreme Geophysical Instability)**
**Recommended Experience Level: Expert only. Minimum 10 prior deep-enviro missions.**
**Travel Permit Required: Kelos-Verb Gi-7. All applicants must submit a psych-evaluation and a simulation run score of 98% or higher.**

Let's be brutally clear: this is not a destination. It is an ordeal. To visit the Chime-Deep is to be an unwelcome guest at the planet's private, ongoing funeral. The beauty is incidental, a side effect of processes that would erase you without a thought. If you are reading this and still considering the journey, your preparation must be meticulous, your mindset perfect, and your respect for this place absolute. Failure is not an option; it is the default state.

---

### **Part 1: The Philosophy of the Deep - Mindset and Intention**

Before you even think about packing a bag, you must prepare your mind. The greatest danger in Khos-Adun is not the heat or the toxic air; it is the psychological assault of profound alienness. You will be a lone, soft, carbon-based anomaly in a world of hard, slow, mineral life.

**Your Core Operating Principles:**

1.  **You Are a Ghost.** Always. The original inhabitants are gone, and the new tenants do not acknowledge you. Your goal is to observe without leaving a trace. Do not touch what you do not have to. Do not take samples unless your mission parameters demand it, and then only with the most extreme caution. You are an observer, not a participant. The city is not a ruin to be explored; it is a tomb that is still being occupied.
2.  **Embrace the Hum.** The constant, low-frequency vibration will be your companion. Some novices try to filter it out, to block it with their suit's systems. This is a mistake. You must learn to live *with* it. Meditate on it. It is the planet's pulse. If it changes, you will be the first to know. Learning to differentiate the foundational hum from the harmonic shifts of the crystal-forest or the ominous prelude to a major vent eruption is your most vital survival skill.
3.  **Patience is Survival.** Everything here moves on a geological timescale. The crystals grow, the city settles. Your fast, frantic human metabolism is an aberration. Move slowly. Deliberately. Every action should be measured and considered. Rushing leads to falls on the glass-shard ground, to stumbling into a vent, or to triggering a catastrophic equipment failure. The planet's heart beats slow. Yours must, too.

### **Part 2: The Shell - Environmental Suit and Life Support**

Your suit is not armor; it is your entire ecosystem. It is the only thin layer of "Earth" you will have with you. Off-the-rack exploration suits will not suffice. You will need a custom-rated **Class V Thermo-Chemical Hazard Encasement System (TC-HES)**. Here's your checklist:

**A. The Suit Itself:**

*   **Material:** A multi-layered composite.
    *   **Outer Layer:** Ablative ceramic platelets fused to a carbon-mesh substrate. This is for dealing with razor-sharp slag and the intermittent, high-velocity spray of molten mineral droplets from the vents. The material must be rated for impacts up to 2000 Kelvin.
    *   **Middle Layer:** A self-sealing, chemically reactive gel. If the outer layer is breached, this gel will instantly neutralize the superheated, corrosive vapor buying you precious seconds.
    *   **Inner Layer:** A full-body capacitive cooling network. You cannot rely on passive radiators; the ambient heat will overwhelm them. The suit must actively pump heat away from your body and shunt it into external, disposable heat-sinks mounted on your back.
*   **Joints and Seals:** All articulation points must have double, triple-sealed magnetic-baffled locks. No standard bearings or lubricants; they would be cooked or corroded in an hour. Movement will be stiffer, less fluid. Get used to it.
*   **The Helmet:** Your viewport cannot be simple glass.
    *   **Visor:** A multi-spectrum sapphire laminate, with integrated layers for: **Thermal Imaging** (to see invisible heat plumes), **Passive Low-Light Amplification** (to resolve the phosphorescence), and **LiDAR Mapping Overlay** (to constantly update the terrain in your HUD). Manual polarizing controls are a must to handle the glare from the faceted crystal-stalks.
    *   **Seal:** The neck-seal must be an independent, redundant system. A primary magnetic lock and an emergency physical clamp.

**B. Life Support:**

*   **Atmosphere:** A strictly closed-loop system. You cannot scrub this air; it's not air. You will carry a full oxygen/nitrogen supply, monitored by triple-redundant sensors.
*   **Atmosphere Analysis:** Your suit MUST have an external sniffer. It needs to be able to analyze the vapor composition in real-time: heavy metal particulates, sulfur compounds, unknown silicates, etc. This data is crucial for understanding immediate environmental threats. The sniffer's port must be self-purging and made of a non-reactive alloy.
*   **Water and Nutrients:** A closed-cycle reclamation system. You will be sweating profusely. Every drop must be captured, filtered, and rendered drinkable. Pack high-calorie, low-bulk nutrient paste. You will not be eating for pleasure.

### **Part 3: The Senses - Tools and Instrumentation**

Your own senses are useless here. Your eyes are weak, your ears will be flooded, your skin would be flayed. You must become a cyborg, a walker of sensors.

**A. Navigation and Awareness:**

*   **Audio:** **Disconnect your external microphones.** The raw soundscape would deafen you and overload the system. Instead, use a **Seismo-Acoustic Array**. Pick up the vibrations not through the air, but through the soles of your boots and the hull of the suit. Feed this data into your computer to be interpreted. It should give you three critical feeds:
    1.  **Geological Stability:** A real-time graph of the foundational hum. A sudden, sharp spike means the ground is about to crack or a new fissure is opening.
    2.  **Vent Prediction:** By analyzing the harmonic "tremors" that precede a superheated jet, your system can give you a 5-15 second warning to move.
    3.  **Bio-Acoustics:** Identify the resonant frequencies of the crystal-forests. This is how you "see" them through sound and map their density and health.
*   **Movement:**
    *   **Footwear:** Magnetically powdered soles. You can engage them for short periods on stable, ferrous rock to anchor yourself against a potential vent-blast. Otherwise, they should be cleated, tungsten-carbide tips for biting into the glassy ground.
    *   **Grappling System:** A launcher-harpoon system with a winch. Do not think of it for swinging like an action hero. Think of it as the ultimate safety harness. Anchor your line *before* you cross a chasm. Test the anchor point. Then test it again. The bridges look "impossibly thin and graceful" because they are. They are not for you.
    *   **Probe Staff:** A collapsible, sensor-laden staff. Use it to test the ground ahead of you. Tap it. Is it solid? Is it a thin crust over a hollow? Does it chime back with a dull thud (stable) or a brittle, sharp ring (fragile crystal growth)?

**B. Communication:**

*   Radio waves will scatter uselessly in the dense, ionized air. Your solution is two-fold:
    1.  **Primary:** A **lased-line-of-sight tight-beam communicator.** You need a team for this, and you must maintain constant visual contact. The moment you break that line, you are alone.
    2.  **Backup/Emergency:** A **tactile transponder** on your suit and a **bone-conduction receiver** in your helmet. You can't talk, but you can send pre-set vibrations: "DANGER," "MY POSITION," "VENT COMING," "SUIT BREACH." It's Morse code for a world of stone. You must practice until the signals are instinct.

### **Part 4: The Procedure - How to Walk and Live in the Chime-Deep**

Your arrival will be via high-altitude drop onto one of the few stable obsidian plains. The ramp of your dropship will open, and you will step into a cathedral of heat and sound. This is how you act.

*   **Your First Hour:** Do nothing useful. Stand still. Kneel. Plant your probe staff. Listen through the seismics. Calibrate your senses to the baseline hum. Let your suit's thermal regulation find its equilibrium. To act before you understand the local rhythm is a death wish.
*   **Movement Protocol: The glacier walk.** One step at a time. Place your foot. Shift your weight. Pause. Listen for a change in the ground-tone. Withdraw your foot, plant the probe. If the probe gives a stable reading, commit your weight. Three points of contact at all times (two feet, one probe/staff or one hand on a wall). This will be maddeningly slow. It is the only correct speed.
*   **Interacting with the Flora:**
    *   **Maintain a two-meter minimum distance from all crystal-stalks.** They are not plants. They are complex mineral structures under immense geophysical tension. Touching one could cause it to shatter, sending razor-sharp shards at ballistic speed, or it could trigger a vibratory cascade in the entire forest.
    *   **Do not step on the white moss.** It is a "slow, patient form of devouring." It is chemically active. While your suit is sealed, your boot soles might not be. It could degrade the cleats or, worse, adhere and make it impossible to pull your foot free. It is a beautiful, deadly trap. Go around.
    *   **Quarantine Discipline.** Upon returning to the dropship, you will spin-dry in an external decontamination chamber using a combination of abrasive inert-gas particulate and high-frequency vibration. Any material you bring back—sample, broken gear, a fleck of stone—goes into a Level 4 bio-chem containment crate. We do not know if the moss can "breathe" vacuum or if a crystal spore can gestate in a different atmosphere. Assume it can.

### **Final Preparations: The Psychology of the Self**

Spend weeks in a sensory deprivation tank, focusing on a single, low-frequency hum. Practice navigation in a cave system, by touch and feel alone, with a flickering red light. Memorize the sound of a thousand kettles boiling at once, until it no longer startles you. Study geology, not biology. Think in terms of pressure, heat, and erosion—not life and death. For here, they are the same thing.

The Chime-Deep is not hostile. It is merely *other*. It is a machine; a beautiful, terrible, musical machine of stone and heat. It was not made for you, and it will not change for you. If you are prepared, you may be privileged to hear its song for a short while. If you are not, you will simply become another silent, unnoticed part of its slow, crystalline symphony.
