### **Multiverse Traveler's Advisory: Guide to the Fixed Hum**

**Document Classification:** Omega-Protocol, Survival Guide
**Location Designation:** Hum-1 (The Fixed Hum)
**Traveler Profile:** Baseline Human, Physiological Standard
**Core Mandate:** Survive, Observe, and Return. This is not an environment for conquest or prolonged habitation. Your goal is to be a ghost in the machine.

---

#### **Part 1: The Core Philosophy – Prepare for Attrition, Not Exploration**

Before you pack a single piece of gear, you must understand the fundamental truth of the Fixed Hum. This place is not alive in a way you comprehend. It does not welcome you. It does not hate you. It is simply *on*. It is a geological-scale machine held in perfect, timeless equilibrium, and you are a fleeting, organic anomaly on its surface.

**Your entire preparation should be geared towards one concept: non-interference.** You are a diver visiting a wreck at the bottom of an alien sea. You do not touch the controls. You do not lean on the coral. Every action you take must be deliberate, minimal, and reversible. You will be fighting a constant war of attrition against the heat, the moisture, the sheer scale, and the mind-breaking quiet. Victory is not measured in distance covered, but in resources and sanity retained.

---

#### **Part 2: Physiological Preparation – Reheating the Human**

Your baseline physiology is inadequate for Hum-1. A 2.4 heat rating in 100% humidity is lethal over time. You cannot simply drink water; you will die of hyponatremia as your electrolytes flood out of your cells.

*   **Thermal Acclimation:** For a minimum of six weeks prior to transit, you must undergo daily sessions in a high-humidity heat acclimation chamber. Start at 85°F (29°C) and 80% humidity for one hour, increasing both temperature and humidity weekly until you can sustain three hours at 105°F (40°C) and 95% humidity without significant distress. This is non-negotiable.
*   **Electrolyte Loading:** Two weeks before departure, begin a regimen of hyper-electrolyte supplementation. Your body needs to be saturated with salts to withstand the constant, imperceptible sweating. Your meals should be saltier than you think is advisable; your water should contain balanced mineral packets.
*   **Respiratory Fortification:** The air is thick with moisture, oil particulates, and biological detritus from the fauna. You will spend weeks practice-breathing through a full-face, closed-system rebreather to acclimate your lungs to the sensation and learn to regulate your CO2 levels under stress.

#### **Part 3: Gear & Equipment – The Hermetically Sealed Shell**

There is no "roughing it" in the Fixed Hum. Exposure is death. Every piece of equipment must be sealed, self-contained, and made of non-reactive, non-corroding materials. Think of your gear not as clothing, but as a personal, wearable habitat.

**Footwear: The Anti-Puncture Platform**
*   **Specification:** Full-height, steel-toed boots made of a ceramic-glass composite. There can be no fabric seams. No leather. Nothing organic that the isopod-slugs might try to eat.
*   **Features:**
    *   **Outsole:** Must be a thick, solid block of vulcanized rubber with an aggressive, deep-traction diamond pattern (matching the floor plates) but with a completely solid edge—no lugs that can catch and shear off. The sole must be at least an inch thick to prevent puncture from sharp plate edges.
    *   **Upper:** Must be a single, molded piece that welds seamlessly to the sole. The closure system should be a magnetic or air-pressure seal, not laces (which would fray or be eaten). A built-in gaiter that seals against your suit's pants is critical.
    *   **Lining:** A copper-infused, antimicrobial wicking fabric to combat the perpetual clamminess inside the boot.

**The Enclosure Suit: Your Second Skin**
*   **Specification:** A two-piece, encapsulating suit of a chitin-woven polymer, charcoal grey in color.
*   **Layer 1 (Inner):** A full-body cooling wicking garment with integrated capillary tubing. This connects to a small, backpack-mounted refrigerant unit, powered by a kinetic battery charged by your own movement. This is your primary defense against the ambient heat.
*   **Layer 2 (Outer):** A hermetically sealed shell. It must be treated with a superhydrophobic and oleophobic coating, causing condensation and oil to bead up and run off. The texture should be slightly abrasive to make it difficult for the fauna to gain traction. Every seam must be sonic-welded.

**Respiratory & Visual System: The Helmet**
*   **Specification:** A full-face, panoramic-view helmet. Visor must be treated with an anti-fogging, permanent, self-cleaning coating.
*   **Features:**
    *   **Rebreather:** A closed-loop system with multiple filters: a HEPA filter for particulates, a chemical scrubber for airborne oils, and a moisture condenser to reclaim some of your exhaled water.
    *   **Audio:** No external microphones. The ambient soundscape is a psychological hazard. The helmet should feature active noise-cancellation tuned to create a low, neutral white noise, obliterating the organic *schlepping* and dripping. A one-way comms for team contact is essential.
    *   **Light:** A forward-facing, multi-spectrum light array should be integrated into the helmet. Normal white light for navigation, but also a UV setting. A "gut feeling" suggests the fauna might fluoresce under UV, helping you track their density.

**Tools & Consumables: Self-Sufficiency is the Law**
*   **Tools:** All tools must be made of titanium or carbon fiber. Nothing that can rust. No sharp edges that are not sheathed. You will need a multi-tool with pry-bar, wrench, and gripping functions. Crucially, you need a *soft*-bristled, non-conductive brush (think condensed nylon bristles) for gently clearing fauna from surfaces without crushing them.
*   **Water & Food:** You must bring 100% of your sustenance. Food should be in sealed, gel-packs that can be consumed through a port in your helmet. Water is for drinking only; you will have a closed-loop system for reclamation. Trust nothing that falls from the sky or oozes from the walls.
*   **Navigation:** GPS is useless. A compass is useless. You need an **Inertial Navigation Lidar Mapper**. This device creates a real-time 3D map of the megastructure as you move, allowing you to plot a course and, more importantly, retrace your steps with millimeter precision. Marking your path with physical tags is forbidden.

**Shelter: The Cocoon**
*   **Specification:** You cannot sleep on the ground. You must bring a single-person, inflatable, sealed shelter pod.
*   **Deployment:** The pod must be magnetically engineered to clamp to any vertical metal surface of sufficient size (like the side of a static gear or a wall). You will lock yourself inside, suspending yourself in a hammock, away from the legions of crawling organisms and the leaching heat from the floor plates. It should be soundproofed.

#### **Part 4: Procedural Protocols & Behavior – The Great Taboo**

Your actions in the Fixed Hum will determine your survival. There is one overarching rule: **The Great Taboo.**

**The Great Taboo: Do not interact with the primary machinery.** Do not touch the giant levers. Do not try to pry open the maintenance panels. Do not attempt to trip a single circuit breaker. The equilibrium is the only reason this world is not actively hostile. Your very presence is a minor disturbance; deliberate interaction could be the catastrophic event.

**Movement Protocols: The Geist-Walk**
*   **On Open Plains:** Do not walk heel-to-toe. Adopt a shuffling gait, keeping your feet parallel to the ground plates. This minimizes the chance of catching the lethal seams between plates and distributes your weight more evenly.
*   **On Structures:** Always maintain three points of contact. Test every handhold and foothold first with the gloved tip of your tool. Looks can be deceiving; a surface might be coated in a thick layer of slime over impossibly sharp metal.
*   **Avoidance:** The fauna are the primary obstacle. Never, ever contemplate walking directly through a swarm (a "carpet"). They may be a food source for something else, or crushing them may release some kind of pheromonal signal. Always find a way around, even if it adds hours to your journey. Use the soft brush to clear a path only when absolutely necessary.

**Fauna Interaction: The No-Touch Doctrine**
They are filter-feeders, but you have no data on their behavior towards large, warm-blooded organisms.
*   **If they crawl on your suit:** Observe. Do not panic and crush them. Use the soft brush to gently sweep them away. Their reaction will be invaluable data. Crush one, and you risk being swarmed as they investigate the sudden burst of organic matter.
*   **If one gets inside your suit:** This is a catastrophic failure. The protocol is immediate contamination control. You must have a pre-loaded injector with a broad-spectrum neurotoxin, one dose for you (if the fauna is toxic/venomous) and a microparticle spray for the creature. Then, you must have a protocol for emergency suit extraction, which likely means aborting the mission.

**Psychological Protocols: Enduring the Silence**
The constant, wet, organic quiet is a weapon. It will erode your mind.
*   **Sanity Timer:** Your helmet's HUD must have a "Sanity Timer." Every two hours, you must perform a cognitive protocol: a complex math problem, reciting a long-memized poem, or detailing your mission objectives aloud. This forces your brain to stay active and provides a benchmark for mental decay.
*   **The Anchor:** Select one small, personal object from your home world (a smooth stone, a digital photo, a locket). You are allowed five minutes per day, in the absolute confines of your cocoon, to hold this object with your gloved hands and focus solely on it. This is your psychological anchor.
*   **Embrace the Horror:** Do not fight the feeling of being in a tomb. Acknowledge it. You are a brief, warm spark in a vast, cold engine. Accepting your ephemerality here is your best defense against existential dread.

---

#### **Final Warning**

When you step through the portal, the first thing you will feel is not the heat, but the **weight of the silence**. Then the humidity will press in like a physical force. Then you will see the endless, writhing carpet of life, and you will feel the utter alienness of it all.

Remember this: The machine is not dead. It is sleeping. And you are a visitor in its dream. The only thing that moves here is life, and it is feeding on the breath of a dead god. Do not wake the god. Do not become food. Observe, document, and leave. The Fixed Hum has no memory of you, and it will have no mercy.
