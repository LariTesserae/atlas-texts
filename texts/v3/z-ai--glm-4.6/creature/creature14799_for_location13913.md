The ones who live here are not so much inhabitants as they are mobile components—the pale, elongated hands of a sleeping colossus, tending to its own dreams. You would call them the Silici, for they are born of the same crystalline, metallic life as the city itself. They do not live *in* the Hypogeal Green; they are a conscious, specialized tissue of it.

They are bipedal, but their gait is not a walk but a procession. A typical adult stands slender and tall, perhaps nine or ten feet high, with a frame that suggests raw scaffolding. Their limbs are long, multi-jointed, and composed of a polished, grey-green mineral that resembles serpentine or basalt slicked with a film of oil. There are no muscles, only articulated segments joined by what look like polished obsidian ball-and-socket joints, moving with the silent, gliding precision of a surgical instrument.

Their bodies are carved by an absolute lack of softness. There is no flesh, no fabric, no hair. Their forms are faceted and planar, catching the amber glow of the great stalks in sharp, angular glints. Their "torsos" are narrow, housing their core metabolic engine, which glows with a faint, internal warmth, a dull cherry-red visible through translucent plates on their chest and abdomen. This is their heart, a slow-burning furnace that chemically fractures trace minerals in the dust they absorb through the soles of their feet.

Their head is the most striking feature. It is not a head with a face, but a sensory node. It is a complex, asymmetrical crystalline structure, sometimes having three or four sides, sometimes a smooth, curving arc. It is studded with nodes of polished, lens-like material that are not eyes. They do not "see" light. They "see" vibration. They perceive the world as a constant, complex symphony of resonant frequencies. The 30-40Hz hum of the city is their baseline reality, the drone against which all other data is a melody. They feel the click-hiss of spore releases not as a sound but as a repetitive, percussive texture on their skin. They "see" each other by the unique, subtle harmonic signature each individual body emits, a identifier as distinct as a voice.

They do not speak. In this near-silence, the air is not for carrying sound, but for carrying information. To communicate, a Silici will kneel, placing a three-fingered, chitinous hand flat upon the root-floor. Through its fingertips, it directly interfaces with the fiber-optic nerve network woven into the ground. Its "thoughts"—which are structured, geometric packets of data—are translated into pulses of bioluminescent light that travel through the city's veins at the speed of thought. A "conversation" between two of them is a silent, stationary affair, a meeting of minds that appears to an outsider like two statues resting. The complex, resinous scent of wintergreen sharpens around them as they release micro-puffs of signaling compounds to add emotional or contextual nuance—the biological equivalent of.

Their society is not a democracy but an anatomy. The city is the body, and they are the nervous system. There are castes, most of which are not sentient. The chitinous, mica-winged "insects" you see are their autonomous extensions, their fingers, endlessly cleaning, pruning, and repairing. The lumbering, armadillo-like grazers are another, a caste designed to mulch excess growth and turn it back into nutrient-rich powder for the root-floor. The Silici are the Cognitive Caste—the ones who manage.

A Silici's life begins not in a womb but in a "Nursery Pod," one of the great hanging cathedrals. They are budding excrescences, carefully calcified by worker-drones until they reach a certain complexity. Then they are "awakened"—plugged into the network for the first time. Their consciousness is not learned; it is downloaded: the foundational geometry of the city, the chemical signatures of a thousand minerals, the protocols for expansion and maintenance.

Their purpose is the city's purpose: to eat. The most renowned among them are the "Geo-Architects" who work at the eruption zone. They do not wield tools. They stand at the edge of the raw, gnawing frontier, their heads tilted, "listening" to the resonance of the chthonic rock. They analyze the stone's composition, its faults, its density, and they will this information into the feeder-vines. They are the taste buds and the brain of a planetary stomach, directing the appetite of the city, guiding the dissolution of granite and basalt into new foundations, new stalks, new life.

There is no art here, only function. But function has achieved a sublime perfection. The truest expression of a Silici's being is to design a more efficient bridge-callus or to debug a harmonic instability in a fungal capacitor. Their beauty is utility; their joy is a perfectly solved equation rendered in living tissue.

And what of the individual? To be a Silici is to be a node on a vast, unbroken network. To be alone is to be deaf, blind, and mute. A Silici severed from the root-floor is a catastrophic failure. Its internal furnace will continue to burn for a time, but its mind, untethered from the hum, becomes a chaotic storm of meaningless data—a state experienced as pure, white-noise agony. It is not death; it is a static hell.

So they live in symbiotic servitude, their will a subset of the city's will. To see one is to understand that you are looking at a single, magnificent thought in the mind of the Green. A thought that is currently calculating the most efficient way to turn a mountain into a home.
