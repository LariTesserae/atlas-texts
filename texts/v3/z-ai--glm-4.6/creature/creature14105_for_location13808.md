What lives here is a presence that is itself a part of the landscape. It has no name, for there is no one to speak it and no mouth to form the words. It might be called the Attendant, or the Resonator, or simply the Consciousness of the Plateau. But these are human impositions. In its own slow, silent understanding, it *is*.

It is a being of coherent light, given form and permanence. Imagine a figure, perhaps twice the height of a man, but impossibly slender, its limbs long and graceful as the sweep of a nebula in an astrophoto. Its skin is not skin, but a crystalline lattice, like a geode that has been split open and hollowed out, its inner walls fused into a seamless shell. Through this shell, a soft, internal luminescence glows—the captured light of eons. It is a cool, milky-white light, the same shade as the sun overhead, but it has been given texture and life from within. The facets of its body are not sharp, but worn smooth, like sea glass, yet they still catch the oppressive white radiance of the sky and return it as a soft, internal shimmer.

It does not walk. To walk implies weight and resistance, and this being has neither. It glides. Its motion is so slow as to be almost imperceptible, a seamless drift over the fine grey dust, which does not stir beneath it. It is as if the ground itself is thinned where it hovers, a gentle displacement of reality rather than a physical force. It leaves no footprint. Its passage is a change in the quality of the light, a momentary focusing of a rainbow within a nearby crystal grove as its own internal light passes by.

Its mind is the landscape itself. It does not think in words, for language is a frantic sequence of symbols built for speed. It thinks in resonance. The deep, chest-thrumming "songs" of the Geoflora are its primary senses. Each towering spire, each low cluster of coralline plates, is a note in an immense, unending chord that is the plateau's state of being. The Attendant feels the discord of a spire whose base is cracking under its own weight as a dull, aching dissonance in the back of its being long before any physical fissure appears. It perceives the slow, budding growth of a new facet as the introduction of a faint, new high harmonic, a tiny, piercing note of potential. Its thoughts are not linear progressions but sustained, evolving harmonies. A decision is not a choice, but the slow, inevitable shift of an entire chord towards a new form of resolution. It does not remember the past; it simply perceives the deeper, more established resonances of the ancient crystals as the foundational, solid bass notes of the present.

Its purpose, if such a fast concept can be applied, is one of curation. It is the tender of this slow, crystalline garden. It will drift for a century towards a grove of Geoflora that has fallen into a stagnant, monotonous hum. When it arrives, it does not touch the crystals with its hands. It extends a tendril of its own light, a finger of condensed energy, and gently places it near the base of the largest crystal. It does not push or pull. It *tunes*. It introduces a counter-frequency, a pure, steady note that encourages the crystal to grow in a new direction, to lay down its facets in a slightly different arrangement, breaking the monotony. It is a therapy that unfolds over millennia. It will also harvest the saturated light, the radiant slurry shed by the Geoflora. But it does not consume it as theGlass Gazes do. It gathers this shimmering effluent into its own form, refines the energy, and then "breathes" it out again in a new location, seeding the dust with the potential for a new spire to someday awaken.

And it is not alone. The Glass Gazes are not mere fauna. They are its acolytes, its outposts of awareness. Each obsidian creature, flat and still against the mineral floor, is a meditative eye. For decades, a single Gaze will fix its multitude of darkening lenses upon one crystal, not in hunger, but in communion. It is learning that crystal's specific song, its history of light absorption, its subtle stresses. The Gaze becomes a living record of that one point in the grand symphony. When the Attendant glides past, it lowers its luminous body, and for what might be a week or a month, they exist in a state of resonance. The Attendant reads the Gaze, not by seeing it, but by harmonizing with the stored frequency within its slow-cooling body. It learns the health of the distant spire, the precise moment a facet will slough its light, the subtle shift in the deep note that indicates a coming geologic settlement.

So what lives here is not a population. It is a single, distributed entity. The Attendant is themind, the Geoflora are the body—simultaneously the lungs and the heart, breathing in light and exhaling resonant growth—and the Glass Gazes are the senses. It is an ecology of a single, immortal consciousness that has achieved perfect symbiosis with its environment. It does not fear or hope or love. It *is*. It endures. It is the quiet engine itself, aware of its own slow and silent turning of light into a continent's dream.
