To understand the life of this place, you must first unlearn the rhythm of breath and pulse. There is no heaving of chest, no thrum of heart. Here, metabolism is a glacial crystallization, and movement is a geological process.

The most abundant life begins as a trick of the eye. On the polished flutes of the causeways, where the grey stone gleams like dull pearl, the ground appears to ripple in the peripheral vision. If you stare directly, it is still. But glance away, and the reflection of the fractured sky seems to swim. These are the **Pave-Mosaics**.

They are not singular creatures, but colonies of trilobite-esque entities, each no larger than a dinner plate, comprised of interlocking plates of slate and silicate. They fit together with such seamless, aerodynamic precision that they form a living carpet over the stone. There are millions of them in a single plaza. They do not walk; they shudder. A vibration passes through the colony, a micro-seismic event, and the plates shift, sliding over one another in a synchronized, fluid wave. They are the grazers of the microscopic dust, their undersides armed with fine, etching tongues that scrape the mineral film from the stone. When the "light-weather" above turns turbulent, pulsing with a sudden violet ferocity, the Mosaics hunker down, their plates locking tight to seal in moisture, transforming the plaza into a solid, inanimate floor of grey ceramic.

Higher up, inhabiting the verticality of the cyclopean structures, are the **Stainers**.

To the casual observer, the great blocks of the city are stained with vertical streaks of rust, copper, and obsidian—natural run-off from the ceiling eons ago. But these streaks are alive. They are flat, membranous organisms, resembling manta rays crafted from flexible parchment. They cling to the sheer faces of the ancient buildings, possessing no limbs, only a muscular, adhesive "foot" that grips the stone. Their bodies are photosensitive, reacting to the slow-burn turbulence of the ceiling. As the veins of light in the crystal sky expand and contract, the Stainers expand and contract in turn, blooming from dark, dormant sludge into wide, translucent sheets that catch the luminescence. They are the Filters. They drink the damp air and the suspended motes of light, growing thicker and richer in color over centuries. When they become too heavy to cling, they simply let go. They do not fall; they drift. Their bodies are so light and the air so dense that they spiral slowly to the ground, where they shatter into mineral-rich shards, feeding the pavement below.

In the shadows of the deepest archways, where the light is clinical and white, dwell the **Impersonators**.

These are the hunters, though their existence is a testament to the lethargy of the world. They are roughly the size of a draft horse, but their geometry is jagged and angular. They possess a carapace of flint and shale, fractured into facets that mimic the very architecture of the city. An Impersonator will crouch at the intersection of two walls, folding its legs beneath it, aligning the ridges on its back with the tool-marks of the ancient masons. It can remain there for a hundred years, indistinguishable from a fallen column or a heap of rubble.

It does not chase prey. It waits for the grazers. It waits for the unwary. It has no eyes; instead, it has a line of vibration-sensitive pits running along its flanks. When a Pave-Mosaic colony ripples too close, or a drifting Stainer touches ground, the Impersonator unfolds. This is not a sudden pounce, but a terrifyingly smooth unfolding of stone. It extends a neck that is segmented like a telescope of granite, and a tongue of sticky, quick-lime snaps out to ensnare the victim. There is no struggle, no scream. The prey is simply engulfed, retracted into a maw of grinding obsidian plates, and silence returns to the hall.

But the true architects of this silence are the **Great Weighers**.

These are the behemoths mentioned in the lore of the light, the "boulders" that move. They are colossal, dwarfing the Impersonators, their bodies the size of small hills. They roam the vast emptiness between the city’s structures, traversing the smooth, worn basins of the world. Their hides are not flesh, but a dense, shaggy mat of crystalline fibers—millions of thin, glass-like needles growing from a bed of tough, leathery skin. This "fur" refracts the ceiling light, breaking it into rainbows that drift across their backs as they walk.

The Great Weighers are the agents of erosion. They move with the momentum of a landslide. Their legs are pillars of bedrock, ending in feet that discard layers of themselves as they walk, leaving behind smooth, polished paths. They are reverent of the silence. If two Great Weighers meet, they do not butt heads or roar. They stop. They align their massive, milk-quartz eyes and stand perfectly still. They communicate through deep, infrasonic frequencies that travel not through the air, but through the soles of their feet, vibrating into the bedrock of the city itself. They exchange the memory of where the light is falling, where the crystal-rain is heaviest. Then, they turn and plod on, their passage so gradual that the dust settles on their backs before they have taken the next step.

Finally, there is the rarest of all the life here, the ones who watch the watchers.

Perched on the highest pinnacles of the tallest towers, where the air is thin and the light is most intense, live the **Prisms**. They are avian in shape, though filled with helium rather than muscle, their bones hollow chambers of gas. Their feathers are panes of thin, transparent mica. They do not eat dust; they eat light. They anchor themselves to the stone with hooked beaks of iron and float horizontally, their wings spread wide to catch the photons. They are fragile, exquisite things that look like hanging lanterns. They live solely to absorb the excess energy of the turbulent ceiling, regulating the light levels of the world below. When a Prism dies—usually from a structural failure in its brittle wings—it does not rot. It crystallizes instantly, turning into a permanent, glittering statue attached to the city’s skyline, becoming just another fixture in the crystal stonework.

The life here is a closed loop of mineral-eating, light-drinking, and stone-making. There is no waste, only the rearrangement of the world's infinite cold matter. They are the city’s immune system, its maintenance crew, and its ghosts, all rolling slowly across the floor, waiting for the light to change.
