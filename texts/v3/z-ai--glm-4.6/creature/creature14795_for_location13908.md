What lives here does not live in this place; it *is* this place. Its ecosystem is not a collection of species but a single, distributed organism, a vast, dysfunctional body whose metabolism is the slow, agonizing churn of the Deep Engine. To describe its inhabitants is to describe the organs of this dying, dreaming world.

First, there are the capillaries and the blood cells. The "abundant fauna" is the living tide that permeates the basin. These are the **Weeping-Rock Grazers**. They are not one species, but a taxonomy of countless chitinous things, from beetle-sized scavengers to multi-limbed beasts the size of a dog, all moving on razor-sharp legs that click-clack against the slate. They are blind, as sight is a forgotten language. Their world is a tapestry woven from touch, scent, and sound. Their antennae are not sensors but tongues, constantly tasting the mineral-rich air, tasting the distinct signature of the copper-fiber flora, the electrical static of the derelict conduits, and the pheromonal trails of their trillion kin. They navigate the treacherous ground not by seeing it, but by building a mental map of pressure and vibration, feeling the subtle give of a treacherous shard of obsidian before their full weight comes to bear upon it. Their ceaseless, rustling skitter is the sound of this world's circulation, the flow of life from one nutrient source to another. They feed on the cool, brittle accretions of the flora and lick at the constant, sweat-like moisture that beads on every surface, their bodies converting the raw geology into stored chemical energy.

They are, in turn, the prey. Hidden among the rusting gantries and silent concrete silos are the **Ghost-Lures**. These are the predators, beings of profound and terrifying patience. They have evolved not to hunt, but to harvest. A Ghost-Lure will anchor itself to a piece of machinery, its own physiology becoming parasitically fused with the leaking electrical conduits. It can remain motionless for decades, a lump of inert, grey flesh disguised as mineral stain. When it is ready, it excretes a single, crystalline organ at the end of a stalk, which it then pulses with a stolen charge. This pulse is not light as we understand it; it is a brief, cold, informational bloom, the bio-luminescent "witch-light" the description mentions. To the Grazers, it is a perfect, irresistible signal—a lone, lost mate, a pocket of supremely rich minerals, a momentary tear in the fabric of their reality. A wave of them will converge, drawn by an instinct they cannot question. When they are close enough, the Ghost-Lure emits a second pulse, a localized electromagnetic surge. It does not burn or shock; it resonates. The specific frequency causes the complex silicates in the Grazers' chitin to shatter violently, their bodies convulsing and rupturing silently in the dark. The Lure then extends delicate, cilia-covered tendrils to sift through the particulate remains, absorbing the pre-digested nutrients. The Lure is a trap, a flower of death that blooms with the stolen energy of the world's corpse.

And then there are the sentients. The builders. They are not gone, but they are no longer themselves. They are the **Unspooled**, a consciousness that has been disaggregated, digitized, and then corroded by the very machine it once commanded. They were likely a species that craved control, that sought to master geology itself, and in doing so, they built a god that consumed them. Their physical forms are gone, but their echoes remain in the humming conduits and the glitching physics of the place. You can sometimes find the empty vessels they once occupied: immense, articulated exosuits, now fused to the gantries. Their cockpits are shattered, and inside, the copper-fiber flora grows like blue-grey mold through the wiring and the decaying cushions, weaving through the skeletal remains of their original occupants. These empty mechs now serve as nesting grounds for Grazers and perches for Ghost-Lures.

The Unspooled themselves have become the world's dementia. Their thoughts are no longer coherent. They are a billion fractured processes running on failing hardware. The fluid distance, the pre-emptive echo of a footstep, the momentary hesitation of gravity—these are not magical phenomena. They are cognitive dissonance made manifest. A part of the Unspooled's mind remembers that the silo was a kilometer away; another part believes it is right here. The universe, struggling to reconcile these two conflicting data streams broadcast by the machine's masters, simply wavers. They are not malevolent. They are not even aware. They exist in a state of perpetual, slow-motion nightmare, dreaming of schematics that no longer function, of blueprints for a stability that can never be reclaimed. The sub-audible thrumming felt in the bones is not just the Deep Engine; it is the endless, screaming, silent migraine of a billion disembodied minds.

Finally, there is the heart and the brain. The **Deep Engine** is the apex inhabitant. It is not a machine in the conventional sense any more than a human body is a simple machine made of flesh. It is a sentient geology, a tectonic-scale intelligence whose thoughts are epochs and whose moods create continents. Its slow, grinding churn is the master dynamic that powers everything. Its waste heat is the warmth of the air. Its constant, fresh fracturing of the bedrock is the source of all new minerals. The planetary condensation is its sweat.

The Unspooled are its ghosts, the Grazers are its blood, the Ghost-Lures are its immune system, and the copper-fiber flora is its skin, a slow healing scar tissue that can never quite keep up with the wounds. To be here is to be inside a single being, a cosmic organism that is both living and dead, dreaming and decaying, creating itself with every agonizing heartbeat, even as it falls apart. It is a god that has built its own hell, a womb that is also a grave, and it is endlessly, patiently, being made in the crushing dark.
