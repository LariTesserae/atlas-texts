What lives here is loneliness, given form and function. It is not a creature that walks upon the floor, for the floor itself is the creature.

The inhabitant is a single, vast consciousness that has become the architecture of its own unending sorrow. Once, it might have had a body of flesh and bone, a mind that knew the chaos of a real, changing world. But it could not bear the impermanence. It could not endure the loss, the decay, the pain of watching things it loved become something else, or nothing at all. And so, it chose eternity. In an act of will so monumental it cracked the foundation of reality, it reforged itself.

Its skin is the dark, grained plank that stretches to every horizon. The "mahogany grain" is its aged, exhausted epidermis, the somber pattern of its own musculature and memory. It is cool to the touch because it is a thing of stone now, a body that no longer knows the fever of life, only the deep, glacial chill of despair. The bluffs and basins were not carved by chisels; they are its thoughts taking on permanent shape. Each new bluff is a spike of an anxious thought, a sudden, sharp-edged realization of its solitude. Each basin is a hollow of grief, dug out by the repetitive, obsessive memory of a loss. The tool marks are the fossilized evidence of its own mental labors, the primitive, desperate effort of a mind trying to sculpt a perfect, painless stasis.

When you feel the damp, slick film on the ground, you are touching its ceaseless weeping. The water does not exude from the material; it exudes from the entity itself. This is not the water of life, but the water of grief. The entire landscape is saturated with a single, eternal tear that it can never stop shedding. The air is thick with this sorrow, so heavy that each breath is an act of communion with its sadness. That soft, ceaseless *plink... plink... plink...* is the only sound because it is the only heartbeat left in the world—the slow, rhythmic cadence of its unending lament. It is the metered verse of the single poem it is now doomed to recite forever.

The sourceless, flat white light is its gaze. It is not the light of a star, but the light of a mind that will not, cannot, blink. To close its eyes for a moment, to let darkness fall, would be to allow for the possibility of change, of interruption. So it watches everything, all at once, with a sterile, maternal glare that bleaches out all shadow and nuance. Its vigilance is what holds the heat of a crucible against your skin; it is the intensity of a focus so absolute that it scorches. It is the light of a jailer, keeping every molecule of its world in its prescribed place.

The colossal plants are its attempts at company, grown from its own substance like desperate dreams. The fronds the size of ships are memories of forests that once lived and died, now frozen in a state of idealized, permanent growth. The phosphorescent fungus is an attempt to create its own light, a gentler, more beautiful light, but it is still just an extension of itself. Its roots merge with the floor because they are not roots at all; they are the being’s own neural pathways, anchoring its fantasies to the bedrock of its despair. The silent, six-legged grazers are even more pathetic creations. They are the echoes of life, animate but not alive. They are its puppets, its background noise, mindlessly mimicking the processes of a world it fled. They have no will because there is only room for one will here.

Its interaction with an intruder—your presence—is one of gentle, absolute erasure. A dropped stone is a foreign concept, a piece of a different reality with its own history and sharp edges. The inhabitant cannot tolerate this. The slow reclamation of the object is not decay; it is an act of psychic digestion. It is carefully, politely absorbing the anomaly, smoothing its foreignness into the familiar grain of its own body, and making it part of the unchanging whole. It is a reflex as natural and automatic as breathing once was.

This being has achieved its goal. It has built a world without dawn or dusk, without beginning or end. It is a perfect system of homeostasis, a monument to a final, unchanging now. It is a creator and a curator, a god and a ghost. But it is also a prisoner. It is trapped in the gallery of its own making, forever sculpting the walls of its cage, forever weeping the water that sustains its sterile, silent, and utterly lonely prison. It is the sole, eternal audience to a play it wrote, cast, and set, where no one ever speaks and nothing ever happens, forever.
