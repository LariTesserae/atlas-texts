What lives here are not a people in the way one would understand the word. They are a process given form. They are the white blood cells of a living mountain, the immune system of a nascent world. Call them the Tenders, for that is their function, and it is their only identity.

They are not born. They are accreted. In the deepest, warmest parts of The Upward Urn, where the living stone pulses like a slow, geological heart, a new Tender will form. It begins as a pocket of exceptional dampness, a clot of the mineral-rich condensation that slicks every surface. This clot is seeded with the woven fibers of a previous Tender, left to decay in a niche of pulsating geology. The fibers are not mere cloth; they are a symbiotic mycelial mat, a spore-rich culture. In the heat, fed by the metallic tang of the air, this culture rehydrates, blooms, and begins the slow, boggy work of building a body around a core of igneous inspiration. The emerging form is humanoid by sheer coincidence of form, but its flesh is a dense, rubbery cartilage, and its skin is a leathery, porous membrane constantly weeping oily sweat that smells of wet iron and chlorophyll. They are always hunched, not from weariness, but because their very posture is an antenna, tuned to the deep, groaning frequency of the stone. To stand straight would be to lose the signal.

Their consciousness is not a private, internal monologue. It is a shared,_receiver_ constantly tuned to the thrum. The "groaning" that dominates the soundscape is, to them, a continuous stream of data, of will, of instruction. Their individuality is a fleeting eddy in this river of thought. One Tender might pause, lift its head, and tilt its ear not to the air, but to the stone beneath its feet. In that pause, it is not resting; it is listening to a new command-vibration. The subtle shift in the deep frequency dictates its next action. A change in pitch means a seam is calcifying too quickly, and it must be tapped with a stone mallet to reintroduce fluidity. A harmonic undertone signals a weakness, a place where the new growth is not adhering to the old, and a copper chisel is needed to score the surface, creating a lattice for the mineral arteries to flow into. Their work is a dialogue. Their high, insectile chittering is the reply, a chorus of acknowledgements and statuses, a biological network protocol running in parallel to the geological one. It is the sound of a million tiny variations on the phrase, "I hear. I comply. It is done."

Their tools are simple out of reverence. To bring a complex machine, an engine of steel and gears, would be sacrilege. It would be an alien will imposed upon the living body of their god. A Tender's tool is a focus for their own will, a needle of intent honed from the very stuff they seek to command. A stone hammer is not for breaking rock, but for resonating with it. The copper chisel is not for cutting, but for whispering a suggestion of shape into the flowing seams. And the woven fibres they wear are both their skin and their memory, a living tapestry that absorbs the vibrations of their work and, upon their eventual dissolution, seeds the next generation with the accumulated skill of a thousand lifetimes of tending.

They have no art, no stories, no names. A story would be a futile arrogance when one is a part of the grandest narrative ever told—the creation of reality itself. Art would be a distraction from the sublime beauty of a perfectly scored block of burgeoning granite. A name would be a laughable conceit when one's true self is a function, a verb in the continuous sentence of The Upward Urn's existence. They are the hands on a cosmic clock, the gardeners on an impossible world, the priests of a temple that is their own god.

Their tragedy is that they can perceive the whole. A Tender standing near the edge can look out at the seething chaos of nothingness and understand its purpose. It can look *up* at the impossible, completed ziggurat that does not yet exist, and feel the tug of that future. This creates a pathological state within their shared consciousness: an omniscience without the power to alter the outcome. They know their purpose is Sisyphean. They know they are pushing this pillar of stone up an infinite hill, a staircase to a heaven that recedes with every step. They are building a bridge to a horizon that is defined by the act of bridging itself. And yet, they do not falter. For the groaning Stone, their nascent god, does not feel despair. It only feels the need to grow. And the Tenders are its nerves, its muscles, its single, unblinking eye, forever chipping and tapping and singing, coaxing their beautiful, terrible, impossible world just a little bit higher into a sky that is not yet there.
