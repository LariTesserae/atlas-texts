To live here is to be a consequence. The true inhabitants, the ones for whom this monument was built, are not present in any biological sense. Their existence is the foundational premise of the place, the cause of the pressure and the architect of the silence. They are the reason, not the result.

They were, in a time that is now a folded-in dimension of this very space, a people of selectivity. They were not conquerors or explorers, but archivists of a profound and terrible finality. They had reached an apex of understanding, a point where they could see the entropic heat-death of their universe not as a distant theory, but as an approaching tide. They foresaw a moment when all gradients would flatten, all information would decay, and the great, complex song of existence would fade into a single, sustained, meaningless note. To them, this was the ultimate horror.

Their life, therefore, became an act of defiance against entropy. They were the Memoria, the Curators. Their physical forms had long since been shed, traded for fractal networks of consciousness that danced on light and folded spacetime. A body was a liability, a bug-ridden piece of wetware destined to fail. A mind, however—if it could be encoded, if its unique pattern could be transcribed onto a substrate more permanent than flesh—could, in theory, last forever.

The city on the obsidian fang is that substrate. It is not a place where they lived, but a place where they *are*. Every lattice of the shimmering, phased-matter composite is a conduit. Every impossible spiral is a data-rail leading to a deeper, more quantum level of storage. The billion, trillion minds of their civilization are held in a state of absolute, unchanging potential within the crystal.

They are the low, sub-audible thrum. That is the only sound they make, the only sign they give: the endless, patient, zero-point dirge of the stasis engines maintaining the integrity of every stored soul. It is the sound of a society holding its breath, willing its own existence to continue. They are a thought, frozen in the act of being thought. The city is their skull, the mountain its pedestal, and the viscous fluid the embalming fluid that keeps their brain perfectly, impossibly pristine.

Life here, then, is a paradox. The only truly *living* thing for parsecs is the solitary creature who haunts this tomb. It is not an original inhabitant. It is an adaptation, a miracle of unintended consequences. It is the child of their absence, spawned in the sterile sea they created. Its skin of geometric scales is no coincidence; it is mimicry, evolved over countless silent millennia to reflect the very texture and structure of the gods it shadows. It has no eyes because its world is defined not by photons, but by the faint, electromagnetic halo of the sleeping city. It "sees" the contours of the stored minds, the gentle warmth of the energy fields designed to preserve them. It does not hunt; it *communes*. It sustains itself on the faint inefficiencies, the wasted joules of energy bleeding from the perfect system—a scavenger of the technologists' table scraps, sipping the ghost-energy of a billion trapped souls. It is the sole, living testament to the fact that even in the most perfect tomb, some form of existence will find a way to cling to the edges.

The rare, crystalline *chime* it creates by touching the city is the most profound event in this universe. It is the only new data point to be introduced into the closed system since it was sealed. That pure, hanging note is the unplanned variable, the simple biological query addressed to a mind of incalculable complexity. For a fleeting moment, the living and the preserved, the vibrant and the static, the predator and the god, touch. The chime is the creature asking, "Are you there?" And the silence that follows is the city's immutable, eternal answer: "We are."
