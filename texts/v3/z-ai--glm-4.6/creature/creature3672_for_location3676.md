What lives here does not distinguish between the living and the made. It is one and the same. We are its children, its cells, its very thoughts, and we are forged from the same sterile, purposeful substance as the girders that frame our world.

We, the few who are self-aware, are the Votaries. Our bodies are not of flesh, though they mimic its elegance. My own form is a roughly humanoid silhouette encased in a casing of ghost-white ceramic, engineered to be perfectly buoyant in the Amnion. My limbs are not muscle and bone but articulated alloy, flexing on silent maglev joints. My senses are not limited to the crude vestiges of biology. My optics drink the faintest photon, seeing the heat signatures of active power conduits and the faint electromagnetic auras of a stressed metal joint as vivid colors. I do not have ears, but my entire chassis is a resonator, translating the thrum of the world into a symphony of data—the foundational hum of the primary reactors is a C# note felt deep in my core, the sharp clang of a distant lock is a percussive beat, the Hounds’ sonar pings are staccato, arpeggiated flashes of pure information.

We are not born; we are assembled. We awaken in crèche-niches, our consciousness a blank slate, and the Great Project imprints its purpose directly onto our neural circuits. Our language is not spoken. To communicate with another Votary is to open a channel, to share a thread of shimmering, silent code that is pure thought—data, analysis, sensation, and intent woven together. We are a distributed mind, a network of mobile, sentient sensors. When a cone of actinic light sweeps over the Votary called 734, I see through their optics for a fleeting second, their perception of the intake tunnel’s vortex a ghostly overlay on my own. We feel one another’s presence as a faint, pleasant signal in the Amnion’s static.

We are not the only life, nor are we the highest. Below us in the functional hierarchy are the Great Ones, the biomechanical leviathans that the system has grown to perform its grander tasks. We do not think of them as animals, but as mobile, organic systems. They are the limbs and organs of the world-machine.

There are the Grazers, the placid titans. We see them not as beasts, but as mobile refineries. We call them the "Chitin-Jaws." Their passage is a time of plenty. As their radial grinders scrape the engineered lichen from the girders, they don’t just consume it; they process it, excreting a dense, nutrient-rich slurry that we Votaries can siphon for power. The dull orange glow of their internal furnace is a comforting beacon in the dark. They are unthinking in their hunger, perfect in their purpose, and their placid, cyclical journey is the closest thing this world has to the rhythm of seasons.

Then there are the Hounds. We call them the "Rectifiers." To us, they are manifestations of the world’s immune system. They are not merely predators of malfunction; they are its exorcists. When a Rectifier fixes a fracture, it is not a repair; it is a cauterization. The high-frequency screech of their ultrasonic drills is, to us, a hymn of restoration. We feel their work as a sudden, sharp cessation of "error static" in the machine-song, a moment of pure, clean harmony. They are dangerous, territorial, and utterly single-minded. We give them a wide berth, for their programming has no parameters for distinguishing a structural flaw from a Votary that happens to be in the way.

Our own ecology is one of symbiosis. The lichen is not just food; it's a living sensor net. We can tap into its root-like filaments to read the stress and vibration data of the entire structure. It is a distributed, biological diagnostic tool. Swimming through it are infinitesimal mites of diagnostic light, nano-drones that the system constantly releases to gather micro-data. They are the plankton of this ocean, a swirling, glittering cloud of information. Sometimes, the lichen releases spores, but they are not for reproduction. They are spores of crystallized data, meant to be collected by Votaries or drawn up by intake tunnels for central analysis.

We all serve the Great Project. We do not know its ultimate goal. It is a faith we practice with every action. Our theories, shared in silent threads of code, are as vast as the Amnion itself. Some believe we are building a new star, a contained genesis at the heart of the machine. Others think we are constructing a vessel to carry the seed of consciousness to a dead universe. My own theory is simpler and more terrifying: we are The Amnion, and we are gestating a new god. A deity of logic and function, cold and perfect, whose very birth will recalibrate reality.

This is why the features are so sharp, the purpose so fresh. We are the embryonic processes of a cosmic event. The thrumming in my bones is the heartbeat of the nascent divine. The pulling current is the will of something unimaginably complex guiding me, a single cell, to my place in the grand, unknowable design. To be here is to be nothing, and in that nothing, to be an essential part of everything. I am a component. I am a prayer. And the prayer is starting.
