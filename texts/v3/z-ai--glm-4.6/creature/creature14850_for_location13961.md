In the Verdant Engine, the hierarchy of life is not a chain, but a circuit diagram. The distinction between fauna and flora is less a line and more a gradient of motility. Here, "eating" is a primitive concept; existence is defined by *integration*, *maintenance*, and the *harvesting of potential*.

**The Curators (The Husbands)**
The most visible inhabitants are the insectoid automata you noted, but they are more complex than simple gardeners. They are the antivirus software of this biological mainframe, endlessly scanning for corruption. They are the "Curators."
Exoskeletons of burnished copper and ceramic chitin, they move with a jerky, staccato precision that is unnerving to eyes accustomed to the fluid grace of natural animals. Their legs are needle-fine, tipped with sensory probes that taste the chemical composition of the bark they walk upon. They do not "see" in the human sense; they read the thermal barcodes of the leaves. When they pause to "trim," they are actually shaving off microns of calcified growth or parasitic fungus, performing microsurgery on a grand scale. They communicate not with sound, but with rapid-fire flashes of ultraviolet light from their antennae, creating a silent, strobe-lit discourse that ripples across the canopy like lightning. They are sterile, tireless, and possess a colony intelligence that is less a hive mind and more a distributed processing network, collectively aware of the tension in a single root system three miles away.

**The Lithovores (The Grinders)**
Beneath the canopy, moving across the polished root-pavement, are the heavy machinery of this world. These are the Lithovores—massive, tortoise-like constructs the size of main battle tanks. They possess no eyes, only wide, vertical slits that serve as intake vents.
They do not eat plants; they eat the ground itself. The "soil" here is a jam of structural matter, and the Lithovores are the recyclers. Equipped with grinding mandibles made of diamond-hard obsidian, they scrape the smooth floor, consuming the compacted detritus, fallen branches, and shards of petrified wood. Inside their cavernous, chemical-filled stomachs, this matter is dissolved down into raw carbon and silicates, which is then excreted in a molten, glowing slurry that cools instantly into a perfectly smooth, hyper-hard patch of pavement. They are the road-crews of the forest, silently filling cracks and polishing the surface to a mirror sheen. They move so slowly that a mossy lichen—actually a specialized, immobile feeder—often grows on their backs, mistaking them for a geological feature.

**The Photophages (The Light-Drinkers)**
High in the canopy, where the light is a physical pressure, live things that are barely there. The Photophages are drifting, gelatinous entities resembling immense, translucent kites or manta rays. They have no bones, no internal organs, only a structural integrity maintained by internal gas bladders filled with hydrogen.
They are symbiotes turned parasites. They possess membranes that are even more efficient at photosynthesis than the leaves they mimic. Instead of rooting, they simply drape themselves over the massive, solar-collecting leaves of the supra-trees, interlocking their own edges with the leaf’s veins to siphon off the raw electrical current generated by the tree. They glow ghostly lime-green when feeding, pulsating in rhythm with the tree’s energy output. They are shoreline threats to the trees, lazy weights that the Curators must constantly pry off and cast down into the abyss. When they fall, they do not die; they simply deflate, becoming moist, organic carpets on the forest floor, waiting for a gust of drywind to reinflate them to restart the cycle.

**The Echo-Weavers (The Shadow-Walkers)**
Perhaps the strangest inhabitants are those that exploit the subtle glitches in physics you described. The Echo-Weavers are spider-like beings that do not spin silk, but spin *vectors*.
They live in the junctions where the architectural plants interlock, dwelling in the permanent shadows created by the gothic arching of the branches. Their bodies are faceted, geometric shapes of dark, non-reflective material that seems to absorb light. They feed on the vibration of the place—the hum of the energy, the subsonic groan of the wood. They spin webs of "stalled time" or gravitational knots, invisible traps that catch nothing physical, but rather snag the "data packets" of light traveling through the vines. A trapped photon becomes a localized speck of intense, trapped energy, which the Weaver consumes. To a human observer, they look like mobile shards of darkness, detaching from the gloom only to snap—or *click—*shut on a passing frequency of light.

**The Architects (The Sleepers)**
Finally, there is speculation about what lies at the centers of the densest towers, where gravity feels wrong and the air is still. Some say the "Architects" are still here, not as small visitors, but as the trees themselves.
There are entities known as "Sleepers"—vast, brain-like coralloid growths found deep inside the hollow trunks of the oldest cathedrals. They are immobile, fused directly into the vascular system of the floor and the ceiling. They pulsate with a bioluminescence that matches the staccato flashing of the Curators, but on a slower, timescale of days. It is believed these are the original minds that designed the ecosystem, having uploaded their consciousness into the biological infrastructure to achieve immortality. They do not move. They do not speak. They simply *are*, processing the atmospheric data and the structural integrity of the world, dreaming of a geometry that has not yet been built. They are the gods of the Engine, waiting, perhaps, for the environment to finally achieve its perfect, terrifying equilibrium, at which point they may open their eyes and step out into the light they have cultivated for a thousand years.
