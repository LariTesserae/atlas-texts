They are not merely residents of this place; they are extensions of its circuitry, biological components wired into a geological machine. You see them first as silhouettes against the violet gloom—tall, attenuated figures wrapped in heavy, felted garments spun from the mycelium of the organ-pipe fungi. Their movements are dictated by the rhythm of the world. They do not walk so much as they pendulate, shifting their weight in time with the deep, sub-audible *thump* that echoes from the core. To move out of sync is to stumble, for the gravity here shifts with the heartbeat of the engines; they have learned to ride the waves of upward pressure and downward drag like surfers on a heavy, invisible tide.

Their faces are mostly hidden behind complex rebreathers, masks of brass and glass that condense the moisture of their breath before it can be lost to the damp air. The skin that is visible—around the eyes or the backs of hands—is pale, almost translucent, with a faint, bluish tint that speaks of a life lived in the perpetual twilight of the cloud layer. Their eyes are large, adapted to the bioluminescent green of the moss and the angry flares of the deep chasms, and they possess a nictitating membrane that slides shut when the pressure drops or the wind carries a spray of oily scald.

They call themselves the *Resonant*, though they have no word for it in any spoken tongue you would know. Their language is a mix of signs and low-frequency hums that vibrate in the chest, designed to be felt through the soles of the feet rather than heard over the screaming metal. They do not shout over the noise; they harmonize with it.

Society here is vertical and sparse, clustered around the "veins" of the world—the exposed vents and gantry junctions where heat and power bleed out from the planetary core. Their dwellings are not built; they are *grown* or *grafted*. Using a mixture of quick-setting slag and the adhesive secretions of the local fauna, they anchor their homes to the cliff faces, hanging suspended over the abyss like barnacles on the hull of a sinking ship. The walls of their homes are layered with the thick, phosphorescent moss, providing both insulation and a dim, living light.

The *Resonant* are defined by their relationship with the strange physics of this extruded plateau. They are the masters of the "thin spots." You will see them navigating the bridges that span the deep valleys, walking with a confident, heavy stomp that anchors them to the metal grating. They carry "grav-weights"—pendulous stones wrapped in copper wire—that serve as countermeasures against sudden shifts in density. A child learning to walk wears a harness of lead beads, dragging them along the ground until they learn to intuit the fluctuating pull of the earth.

Their economy is one of maintenance and harvest. They are the Stewards of the Great Engine, though they did not build it. Their primary caste, the "Listeners," spends entire lifetimes pressed against the cold iron of the cliff face, wearing massive, horn-like stethoscopes made of bone and brass. They diagnose the health of the world by interpreting the groans of the metal and the rushing of the magma deep below. When a Listener signals a shift in the strata—that a new layer of rock is about to be extruded or a drill-bit is preparing to descend—the entire community mobilizes. They pack their belongings, seal their moss-gardens in water-tight canisters, and ascend the web-worked bridges to higher ground with a glacial, practiced urgency.

They practice a strange form of agriculture. They do not plant in soil, for the ground is constantly being replaced. Instead, they cultivate the *scavengers*. They farm the chitinous, rivet-patterned beasts in corrals of scrap metal, feeding them on industrial runoff and the slippery moss that grows everywhere. In return, the creatures provide the *Resonant* with eggs rich in protein and shells that can be ground into a paste used to patch leaking pipes. They also harvest the "vent-moss," carefully shaving the water-saturated slime from the rocks to distill into a potent, clear liquor that burns the throat but settles the nerves against the constant vibration of the floor.

But the most defining feature of their lives is their discipline regarding the "Seams"—the places where reality wears thin. The *Resonant* adhere to a strict code of sensory denial. To stare at a corner is a taboo, an invitation for the geometry to fail. They train their children to look *through* things, to focus on the horizon or the floor, never at the peripherals where the walls might blur. They navigate by touch and memory more than sight, trusting the friction of their hands on a railing to tell them it is solid, even if their eyes tell them the iron is rippling like water.

There are legends among them of the "Lost," those who stared too long into the softening curves of a glitching pocket dimension and simply became unstuck. The *Resonant* say these souls didn't die; they were simply accreted into the walls, their voices added to the background hum of the machinery, another vibration in the endless symphony of the world.

At night, if such a thing exists in this bruised purple twilight, they gather in the common halls. The air is thick with the steam of cooking fungi and the metallic tang of recycled air. They sit in silence mostly, swaying gently to the rhythm of the planet’s heartbeat, drinking moss-water and listening to the subtle changes in the ambient noise. They are a people waiting, living in the pause between the *thump* and the echo, content in the knowledge that they are small, fragile things clinging to the skin of a god that is still learning how to be a world.
