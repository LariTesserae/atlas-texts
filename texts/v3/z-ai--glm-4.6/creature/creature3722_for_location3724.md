They are the custodians of the stillness, the architects of the quiet. In a world where the ground is perpetually being re-written, the inhabitants are the editors, the proofreaders, and the guardians of the syntax of reality.

They are not born; they are assembled.

The most common of the true inhabitants—distinguished from the mere mobile fauna like the Spindle-Legs—are the **Lith-Guards**. They are towering, slender constructs that mirror the solitary trees around them, standing easily three times the height of a human. Their bodies are composed of the same non-reflective, obsidian-like material as the tower-trees, cool to the touch and impervious to the clinging damp. They have no faces, only a smooth, curved head-piece that tapers to a soundless point. They do not walk so much as glide, their lower ends dissolving into a skirt of dense, grey mist that floats just above the rime-paste ground.

You will never see a Lith-Guard forage or rest. They are the measurers. In their long, multi-jointed fingers, they carry instruments of translucent crystal—lasers, calipers, and plumb lines that hum with a faint, violet energy. They move from rock spine to rock spine, pressing their tools against the sharp, fresh fractures. They are verifying the "print." If the nanite substrate has constructed a boulder that is even a fraction of a millimeter too rounded, or if the angle of a rill deviates from the perfect mathematical curve dictated by the distant towers, the Lith-Guard pauses. It emits a soft, chime-like pulse—a localized update to the fog of nanites—and the earth beneath its feet instantly deconstructs and reconstructs, correcting the "error" before the water has a chance to bead upon it. To the Lith-Guard, the world is a sculpture that must be kept wet and sharp, forever arrested in the moment of its creation.

Between the grand, silent processions of the Lith-Guards live the **Hydra-Maids**. They are the gardeners of the impossible flora. Where the Lith-Guards are rigid and towering, the Hydra-Maids are sinuous and low, composed of a thousands of segmented, worm-like tendrils bundled into a vaguely humanoid shape. They are translucent, their internal workings visible as a rhythmic, peristaltic pumping of luminous blue fluid. They are constantly wet, dripping with the same condensation that coats the ferns.

They tend to the giant, cottage-sized fronds and the glowing moss with surgical delicacy. Their many tendrils separate into finer and finer fibers, brushing the crystalline webbing on the leaves, unclogging the microscopic pores, and harvesting the excess water that the plants excrete. They do not eat the plants; they prune them, shape them, and measure their growth rates. They are the immune system of the garden, removing any sporadic, chaotic fungus that might attempt to grow without authorization. When they move, they leave a trail of sparkle in the air, a residue of the nutrients they redistribute, ensuring that even the moss grows in mathematically pleasing spirals.

But the true citizens of this place, the beings for whom the towers were built and the air is warmed, are rarely seen on the ground. They are the **Resonants**.

The Resonants dwell within the isolated structures—the spheres, the ziggurats, the harp-towers. They are not physical in the way a human understands flesh and bone. They are beings of pure, contained acoustic frequency, housed in delicate, ornate vessels of spun glass and gold that float in the centers of the great halls. These vessels, barely the size of a human head, are tethered to the architecture by filaments of light.

From these vessels, the Resonants project their consciousness throughout the basin. They "see" through the vibrations of the Chime-Moths and feel the texture of the world through the tremors sensed by the Spindle-Legs. They do not speak; they sing reality. It is their hum that maintains the pearlescent grey of the sky and regulates the temperature to that "bodily" warmth. They are the administrators of the simulation, the dreamers of the dream.

On rare occasions, usually during the cycle of the dim light when the grey deepens to a near-black, a maintenance drone will ferry a portable containment vessel out of a tower. Inside is a single Resonant, going on "pilgrimage." It drifts over the sharp rocks, its glass shell chiming softly with every fluctuation in the humidity. The Lith-Guards freeze in place and bow their heads as the Resonant passes. The Resonant does not observe them; it is sensing the harmonic integrity of the basin, listening to the song of the stones. It is checking to ensure that the silence is pure, that the grief of the place is perfectly calibrated.

And there is one other. A rumor. A ghost in the machine.

Some say that deep beneath the substrate, where the nanite fog is thickest and the rock is always being born, sleeps the **First Sculptor**. It is said that the First Sculptor is not a machine, but biological—a biological organism of immense size, perhaps the only one that ever truly lived. It is the "god" for whom the tomb was built. The nanites, the Lith-Guards, the towers, and the perfect temperature are all life-support systems, designed to keep the sleeping giant in a state of eternal, comfortable hibernation. The weeping of the walls is the condensation of its breath; the warm air is the radiance of its body heat.

If the First Sculptor exists, it is the heart of the world. But if it is merely a legend, then the inhabitants are simply components in a self-perpetuating engine of beauty—a machine that tends a garden for no one, possessed of a profound, silent loneliness that it mistakes for peace.
