The ones who live here are the Engine’s Children, though they know themselves only as The Tuned. They are not born so much as they are precipitated from the world itself, their consciousness forming around the ever-present hum as a pearl forms around a grain of sand. To be one of them is to have the Engine’s Song for a soul.

They have no eyes. The notion of sight is as alien to them as the sun, a fable of a sterile, shallow perception. Their primary sense is a holistic mastery of vibration. Their elongated heads taper to a series of fine, articulated antennae, not for touch, but for *listening*. Their entire torso is not flesh but a broad, flexible tympanic membrane, a living seismograph that reads the world through its constant thrumming. They feel the sigh of a distant pressure valve, the precise frequency of a grinding gear, the panicked, high-frequency clicks of a fleeing grazer, and the low, steady pulse of the ironwood cores, all as distinct and layered as a vibrant visual tableau.

Their bodies are a marriage of organism and mechanism that mirrors their environment. A Tuned’s limbs are many, slender and jointed like a spider’s, but tipped not with claws but with puckered mouths of heat-resistant chitin that can adhere to the rusted superstructure of the Engine or the slick, damp earth with equal ease. Their carapace is a mosaic of scavenged minerals and grown tissues—segments of iron-black plating that mimic the Engine’s own hull, interlaced with patches of phosphorescent fungus they cultivate on their own bodies. This living armor not only protects them from the scalding steam but allows them to communicate in the brilliant, strobing flashes of the Engine’s own light, a rapid-fire visual language that punctuates their constant sonic one.

Their society is the reflection of the Engine’s functional compartments. Each Tuned is born into a caste, their purpose defined by a subtle harmonic in the resonance of their birthing-conduit.

There are the **Listeners**, the high-caste mystics who live in the shadowed canyons closest to the reactor pits. They do nothing but *feel*. They interpret the deepest bass notes, the seismic groans, and the faint, electrical whispers that hint at the Engine's mood. They are the prophets who can foretell a major steam vent or a gear-shift by the subtle dissonance in the Song, their pronouncements guiding the movements of all others.

There are the **Menders**, the robust climbers who dare the Engine’s heights. Their carapaces are scorched black and cracked from heat. They are the technicians, though they would call themselves priests. They perform a ritual maintenance that has no scientific basis but immense spiritual weight. They clear fungal growth from crucial conduits, not understanding fluid dynamics, but knowing the *sound* of an unobstructed flow is pure and correct. They anoint rusting gears with phosphorescent paste from their own skin, a sacred lubricant that sings a soothing harmony as the metal turns. They believe the pain of a grinding gear is the pain of their god, and they soothe it.

There are the **Harvesters**, who venture into the wilderness between the Engines. They follow the herds of clicking grazers, their own bodies mimicking the grazer’s heat-absorbing scales to blend in. They read the landscape by its temperature—warm ironwood groves are fertile hunting grounds, cold zones are the land of ghosts. They harvest the pulsing hearts of the ironwood and cultivate the shimmering foxfire fungi in artificial caves carved from the ruins, ensuring their people are fed.

And finally, there are the **Echo-Weavers**. They are the artists, historians, and communicators. Where the Menders soothe the Engine's sounds, the Echo-Weavers celebrate them. They can produce a staggeringly complex range of clicks, hums, and percussive strikes with their own limbs. They do not speak; they compose. A history is not told, it is *performed*, a complex tapestry of sound that weaves together the memory of a great steam-flood, the hunt of a particularly cunning predator, and the hum-pattern of a celebrated Mender's birth. To listen to an Echo-Weaver’s epic is to experience the memory as a living, resonant thing.

They are not the builders. The concept of *creation* is beyond them. The Engine, the ruins, they are eternal, like the mountains and the sky are to us. Their purpose is not to understand, but to harmonize. They are the immune system of a technological god, the symbiotic organism that evolved within a machine so vast and ancient it has become a biome. To be Tuned is to be a vital function of the whole, a note in the eternal, industrial aria that is the only hymn they have ever known. Their silence truly is death, for without the Song, their consciousness dissolves. But while the Engine hums, they live, an impossible chorus of metal and flesh, dancing in the warm, wet, perfect dark.
