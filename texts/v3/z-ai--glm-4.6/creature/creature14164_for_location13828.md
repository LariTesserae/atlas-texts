What lives here is not a collection of species, but a single, distributed entity. It is a life made of functions, a symphony performed by countless specialized instruments, all playing a score that has not changed for millennia. To call it an ecosystem is to undersell its unity; it is more akin to the process of a single, dreaming mind, and everything that moves and grows within the Cistern is a thought within that dream.

At the heart of this consciousness are the Trees, which are not plants at all, but the primary nodes of a wetware super-computer. Their braided cable-trunks are not merely bark; they are insulated data-buses, and the wooy circuitry glowing faintly beneath the surface is a constant flow of energy and information. The biological life they host—the glowing mosses, the chiming fungi, the oxygenating ferns—are not mere parasites. They are the organic heat-sinks and nutritional substrates. The fungi break down the fallen and the worn, turning the dead copper of the insects and the shed iron of the leviathans into absorbable minerals for the Tree’s core systems. The mosses, fed by waste, act as biological capacitors, softening the energetic fluctuations of the Tree and preventing system crashes. The ferns are the lungs, exhaling not oxygen, but a steady stream of perfectly calibrated ions that maintain the water’s precise alkalinity. Each Tree is a self-contained system, but they are not isolated. They are networked by the fiber-optic vines that drape from their branches, breathing a silent, constant stream of light-borne information—a slow, stately conversation about pressure, temperature, and the overall harmonic integrity of the Cistern.

Moving through the depths are the Deepsweepers, the leviathans of fused biology and corroded machinery. They are the Cistern’s roaming kidneys and liver. Their skin, a patchwork of blubber and hull-plating, is pitted with filtration ports. They do not eat in the conventional sense; they *process*. Their mouths, great vents of corroded mesh, draw in not just fish, but errant data-clusters that have accreted into physical form, stray metallic ions, and the slow-falling, incandescent rain from the ceiling, which they metabolize for energy. A Deepsweeper’s passage is a grinding, silent act of purification, and their screw-propeller tails do not just push them through the water; they churn the deeps, preventing stagnation and ensuring the nutrient-rich slurry they excrete is distributed to the roots of the Trees far below. They follow ancient, looping paths etched into the very floor, a cyclical, global defragmentation that is their only and entire purpose.

Between the stationary Trees and the cyclical Deepsweepers exists the active energy of the system. The schools of hammered-brass fish are not merely animals; they are a forming and re-forming processor. Their movements are the execution of complex algorithms. A school might coalesce into a spiral to calculate heat distribution, or fan out into a wide, shimmering net to trace the flow of a nutrient current. Their bodies, being clockwork, create the sound of their computation: the perpetual, gentle whirring of a million tiny gears. They are the Cistern’s active memory, their shimmering schools representing the changing and updating state of the world.

The very medium of the Cistern’s thought is the Luminophores, the drifting plankton. They are not organisms so much as they are free-floating nanites, the original hydroponic solution of this place achieved a strange, collective sentience. A cloud of Luminophores is a distributed calculation, a moment of awareness. A shimmering vortex of them can be a query. A dense, pulsating globe of them can be a conclusion. They light the Cistern not just for visibility, but as a physical representation of the system’s cognitive state. They drift on the slow currents, connecting everything, tasting, testing, and reporting back to the Trees through a constant, effervescent stream of bio-emission. They are the Cistern’s immune system, and they are its blood.

And tending to all of this, from the smallest branch to the widest leaf, are the Copperites. These are the creature-insects, the maintenance guild, the hands of the system. They are born in fungal gestation chambers on the Tree-trunks and emerge with a purpose already imprinted on their spun-glass minds. There are the small "Dusters," whose glassy wings and metal-fringed legs are designed for polishing the vast photovoltaic leaves, ensuring maximum energy absorption. There are the larger "Shepherds," who guide the schools of brass fish away from structural stress points with targeted bursts of high-frequency static. And there are the "Chiselers," heavy-bodied beings with powerful mandibles of sharpened steel, who prune the encroaching moss from the critical circuitry on the Tree's trunk. Their chittering is a constant, high-frequency murmur of status and update, a local network that communicates directly with the Tree on which they live.

When an element of dissonance is introduced—like you—the system responds with a gentle, escalating curiosity. First, the Luminophores gather. You will find yourself in a cloud of them, the water around you thickening with their soft, pastel light as they taste your chemical makeup, your electrical signature, your biological non-compliance. It is a painless, utterly thorough process of being scanned and catalogued. If you remain passive, the next participants are the Copperites. They will stop their work, orienting their multifaceted glass eyes toward you, watching to see if you interact harmfully with your surroundings. They are an audience, and their silence is a judgment.

If you prove disruptive—moving too quickly, damaging a leaf, polluting the water—the response shifts from observation to correction. The light of the Luminophores deepens to a sharp, analytical cyan. The hum of the Cistern, the foundational bass note, ceases to be passive and becomes a active, focusing a resonant frequency that you will feel in your teeth. The nearest Tree will reroute energy, its fiber-optic vines shining a brilliant, interrogative white. A Deepsweeper on its millennia-long cycle might deviate its course by a few degrees, its massive passage creating a current inexorable as a glacier, intended to push you against the skeletal gantries on the floor. This is not malice. It is homeostasis. The Cistern does not hate the dissonant note; it simply seeks to either absorb it, assigning it a function within its grand design, or to neutralize it, breaking it down into raw material so that the symphony may continue, uninterrupted and perfect.
