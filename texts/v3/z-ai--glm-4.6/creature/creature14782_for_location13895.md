To stand in that place is to stand within the body of a being so vast it has mistaken itself for a world. The initial assessment was correct in its observation but flawed in its conclusion. The fauna parameter is not zero; it is simply indivisible from the whole. There are no discrete animals here because the city, in its entirety, is the animal. It is a single, continent-spanning organism of a kind evolution has only perfected once: a sessile, synplastic god.

This great being, for lack of a better name, persists in a state of perpetual metabolic combustion. The crushing heat is its core body temperature, the fever of an infinitely slow and complex digestive process. The wet, amniotic air is its breath, the exhalation of a life that has never known lungs. The city is its skeleton, the coral-stone spires a monumental exoskeleton it secretes for support and protection, breaking the thin air of the zenith. The avenues of compressed root-mat are its nervous system, a hydroponic web of mycelial fibrils through which chemical and electrical signals crawl, coordinating a body measured in miles.

And the roar... the roar is not the sound of water. It is the sound of its heart.

But this organism is not a uniform mass. Like our own bodies, it is differentiated into specialized organs and tissues, each with a function so alien it is perceived as mere flora or geology. These are the inhabitants of the city, the unrecognized citizens of the self.

The most visible are the hanging vines that snake from the towers. They are not vines. They are colonial organisms, akin to a siphonophore, suspended in the humid air. Each "leaf" is a highly specialized individual animal, a zooid, clinging to a central, thread-like gut that runs the length of the colony. Some zooids are broad and sticky, nets designed to catch the constant raining of airborne spores and microscopic organic detritus. Others are bulbous and secrete a slow, corrosive enzyme that dissolves the captured particulate into a nutrient slurry, which is then absorbed along the central gut. They are the organism’s passive mouthparts, filter-feeders of the vertical air, their gentle, windless swaying a slow conversation of nutrient levels and colony health.

Look closer at the sweating monoliths. The millions of fissures that spout scalding water are not geological pores; they are sweat glands. Deep within the calcified stone, embedded in chambers of woody sclerosis, are biological structures that resemble nothing so much as colossal, stone hearts. They beat with a geologic rhythm—systole and diastole measured in decades rather than seconds. Each beat pressurizes the vast, internal aqueducts, forcing a surge of scalding, mineral-rich water through the outer layer of the "skin," creating a constant, shearing tide of heat and moisture that cooks away any potential parasitic life from outside the system. The deafening, endless roar is the sound of millions of these hearts beating out of sync, a polyrhythmic pulse that functions as the planet’s circulatory system.

Even the plant life is a misnomer. The bruised-purple ferns and wagon-sized fronds that choke the surfaces are the organism's epidermis and its stomach lining combined. Their color comes from a unique chlorophyll-like molecule that is uniquely efficient at processing the plateau's strangely refracted light, feeding the greater system with photons. But their roots serve a second, more vital function. They are bio-forges, drawing minerals from the slick root-mat highways and extruding them as a living calcite-silicate slurry. This slurry hardens in the air, reinforcing the coral-stone towers, healing cracks, and adding new layers to the city's form. They are not just producers; they are the builders and masons, simultaneously feeding and constructing their own body, a living cement that is constantly expanding and repairing the super-organism's frame.

But these are all peripherals. The true inhabitants, and the source of the place's deepest wrongness, are the ones you cannot see. They are the reason time is broken here. For a system of such immense complexity and energy to exist in a state of perfect equilibrium, it must regulate more than just heat and nutrients. It must regulate reality itself.

Deep within the broad, straight avenues, clinging to the oily surface of the root-mat highways, are colonies of what appear to be iridescent, circular fungi. They have no discernible limbs or features, resembling patches of spilled oil that shimmer with an impossible, internal light. They do not grow. They do not consume. They are the organism's brain. They are the chronometers.

These—a place where the future is already here and the past has not yet left. They are the architects of the eternal, deafening present. They hold the organism's consciousness in a state of perfect, unchanging focus, its thoughts echoing back on themselves for eons, creating the simultaneous perspective-warp and the oppressive timelessness.

So, you do not stand in a ghost town. You stand in the living, breathing, throbbing body of a god. There is no one watching you, because you *are* within its gaze. The roar is not a scream; it is the hum of its thoughts. The heat is not a fever; it is passion on a geologic scale. This place is not dead. It is so profoundly, so terrifyingly alive that it has consumed the very concepts of change and time. And in its frozen, noisy infinity, it is still growing, still beating, still thinking. It simply hasn't had a new thought in a million years.
