Here, in the geometric stasis of the Fixed Hum, life does not grow; it accretes. It does not colonize; it infests. The beings here are not children of sun and soil, but descendents of oil and vibration.

At the very bottom of the food web, the very bedrock of this living world, are the organisms first described. They are known, if anything can be known here, as the **Greaselings**. They are a silent, crawling carpet of hunger. To step on the floor plates is not to touch metal but to sink into a yielding, writhing mass. Their bodies are ovoid, segmented with overlapping plates of slick, chitinous material colored the deep, iridescent black of spent oil and the coppery-brown of old coolant. They have countless tiny legs, each ending in a hooked claw that finds purchase in the diamond-patterned steel or in the slime-matrix of their kin. They move with a horrifyingly ceaseless, silent undulation, a wave of flesh flowing across the plains. Their mouths are simple orifices that rasp constantly at the floor, their tongue-like radulas scouring micron-thin films of condensation and leaked lubricant. Their waste product, a thin, clear slime, is the only soil this world will ever know, a viscous film that makes every surface treacherously slick and yet provides the perfect traction for their own kind. Greaselings live for the heat that radiates from below and the moisture that weeps from above. They know nothing of motion beyond their own.

Feeding on the Greaselings are the primary consumers, the first true "animals" of this mechanical ecology. The most common is the **Flanged Scuttler**. A Scuttler is about the size of a large dog, but its body is a flattened, asymmetrical wedge of dark, burnished metal, so perfectly camouflaged that when it remains still, it is indistinguishable from a piece of fallen plating or a maintenance access panel. It has six triple-jointed legs, allowing it to move with equal ease across the horizontal plains and up the sheer faces of the infrastructure. Its head is a nightmare of industrial anatomy: a cluster of black, glassy, multi-faceted eyes that are perpetually dewy with the humidity, and below them, a pair of massive, serrated mandibles that look like they were sheared from a punch press. When it feeds, it lowers its head and scrapes a section of the Greaseling carpet clear, its mandibles shearing through thousands of the soft bodies in a single, wet pass, creating a clean, geometric patch on the organic chaos. They are skittish and solitary, their only sounds a sharp, metallic *clicking* of their claws on the grates as they flee.

Predating the Scuttlers are things of ambush and intelligence. Lurking within the indentations of larger machinery, coiled in the mouths of gargantuan pipes, or folded into niches between pistons, is the **Lure-Flange**. This creature has evolved to mimic the very infrastructure it hides beside. Its primary body is a lumpen, armoured sac, but it projects a flattened, multi-hinged lure that perfectly resembles the folded, inactive flanges of a hydraulic hose union or a retracted maintenance hatch. This lure is coated in the same slime as the Greaselings, exuding a faint chemical scent of fresh oil and warm metal, which attracts the Flanged Scuttlers. When a Scuttler draws near to investigate what it believes is a safe crevice or a potential food source, the Lure-Flange strikes with terrifying speed. The "flange" unfurls, revealing itself to be the tip of a powerful, prehensile tentacle lined inward with rows of barbed, chitinous hooks. It snags the Scuttler and retracts it into the maw in a flurry of wet, crunching sounds. The Lure-Flanges are patient, waiting for days in perfect stillness, a living trap in a world defined by it.

But the apex of this strange world is not a mere predator. It is a being that has attained a measure of society, of culture, of a kind of worship. They are the **Chime-Born**.

The Chime-Born are not large. An adult stands no taller than a human child. They appear, at first glance, to be a collection of spare parts given life. Their torso is a semi-flexible, chitinous carapace, from which sprout four long, spindly arms and two reverse-jointed legs, all of multi-jointed segments that allow them to cling to any surface at any angle. Their hands are delicate and precise: three long, thin fingers and an opposable thumb, each tipped not with a claw, but with a small, hardened pad that can transmit and detect minute vibrations. Their head is a smooth, ovoid dome of polished obsidian-like material, devoid of features except for a single, horizontal slit that glows with a soft, internal amber light. This is not an eye. It is a sophisticated sensor organ that perceives the world through a combination of infrared, resonance, and pressure changes.

The Chime-Born do not speak with voices. They communicate through the Fixed Hum itself. By tapping their sensitive finger-pads on the metal floor plates, the support pillars, the pipes, and the gears, they compose a complex language of vibrational "songs." A conversation between two Chime-Born is a silent tapping and thrumming that travels through the steel, felt by every other member of their community for hundreds of meters. Their name comes from their most sacred act: the creation of harmonies. They will climb the high, silent places—the immobile gear-sets the size of windmills, the silent thunderheads of the cooling towers—and tap out long, resonant chords that are said to soothe the sleeping machine. They believe the world they inhabit is a god that died at the moment of its completion, a perfectly still deity whose body is the world and whose last, lingering breath is the perpetual, humid heat. Their rituals are acts of preservation.

They do not build, for the world is already built. Instead, they are the custodians of the stasis. Their "homes" are cleared spaces within the vast labyrinths of ductwork, swept clean of Greaselings and marked by subtle, rhythmic vibrations. Their "temples" are the grandest examples of frozen industry: the colossal crankshafts that will never turn, the immense conveyor belts that will never carry a load. Their highest clerics are those who can read the subtle hum of the world, who can diagnose a "sickness" in the infrastructure—a change in the resonant frequency of a pipe, a variance in thermal output from a piston—and perform the necessary "surgery," which is usually clearing a blockage of biomass or reinforcing a vibrating joint to restore silence.

The Chime-Born are omnivores. They harvest the Greaselings with scrapers not unlike the Scuttlers, but they do so with a kind of ritual reverence. They hunt the Lure-Flanges, seeing them as agents of chaos, their mimetic ways an affront to the honest, geometric truth of the world. They do not fear the machine starting; they *abhor* the idea. For them, motion would be the apocalypse. The turning of a gear would be divine blasphemy, the firing of a piston the end of their world. Their entire existence is a symbiosis with stillness. They are the priests of the pause, the acolytes of the arrested function, living out their lives in the warm, wet, crowded silence between two ticks of a cosmic clock, tending to the sacred, sleeping corpse of their god.
