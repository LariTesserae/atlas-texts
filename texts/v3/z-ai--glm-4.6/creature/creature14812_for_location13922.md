What lives here are the Accreted.

They are not born; they are assembled. There is no single mother or father, no cradle or crib. A new consciousness begins in a quiet niche high on a Gantry, a small, protected cradle woven from the strongest steel-strands and the most dense, unyielding mosses. Into this cradle, the denizens of the city place the nascent elements of a being: a core of nutrient-rich slurry, a seeding of neuro-conductive fungal spores, and an intricate lattice of metallic thread, salvaged from a sunken structure. It is a gestation of assembly, not of biology. Over cycles, as the basin thrums and the city shifts, these elements merge, finding their own resonance until a tremor of awareness begins to coalesce.

Their form is a direct reflection of their world. An Accreted has no single, homogeneous body. At their core is a dense, resilient mass, something like peat, something like gristle, rich with the basin's heavy metals. This core is soft, pliable, and retains the deep, foundational thrum of the world like a tuning fork. This is the seat of their consciousness, their emotional memory. From this core, their true body grows outwards in layers, just like the city itself.

Their outer shell is a hybrid of chitin and recycled alloy, plated in segments that overlap like the gills of a bracket fungus. It is not one solid piece, but a constantly maintained and repaired accretion of scavenged materials. An elder Accreted might have strata of corroded brass from a forgotten generation visible beneath a fresh layer of polished, sintered chrome. Their limbs are not fixed. They can extend serrated, multi-jointed legs tipped with the same screw-like pylons that anchor the city, allowing them to clamp onto a platform and feel its stresses, or to anchor themselves against a sudden seismic shift.

And woven all through this mechanical shell is a living sheath of mycelium. It is a unique, cultivated strain, a symbiotic partner that is as much a part of them as their metallic bones. This living skin pulses with a soft, cool light, mirroring the larger fungi of the basin. It provides insulation from the cold, aids in processing the damp, metallic air into something they can metabolize, and most importantly, connects them to the world. Through this fungal network, an Accreted does not merely *see* the phosphorescent carpets; they *feel* them, tasting the mineral content of the soil miles away, sensing the health of the Sinterers through the vibrations they cast into the ground.

They are the nervous system of the living engine. What we would call their "senses" are vastly different. They do not rely on sound. The profound silence of the place is the canvas upon which they paint their reality. Their primary sense is proprioception on a colossal scale. They feel the constant sag of a bridge, the slow sinking of a district, the subtle torsion in a pylon deep beneath the grey water. They communicate not with voices, but with a complex language of resonant frequencies transmitted through the metal walkways and even the fungal mat itself. A conversation might be a series of low thrums that travel for a kilometer, a rapid-fire patter of vibrations like a drum solo, or a shared, sustained pulse of harmony that signifies agreement or communal purpose. When they need to express more complex, localized data, their fungal skin can flash in intricate patterns, a silent, luminous syntax of data and emotion.

There is no concept of individual ownership or permanent dwelling. An Accreted *is* its function, and its function can change as the city needs. One cycle, they might be an Adjuster, their limbs recalibrating the sinking pylons of an old habitation cluster. The next, they could be a Forager, silently moving through the vast, silent forests with their six-legged skittering beasts, harvesting the most potent fungi for communal sustenance or for seeding new systems. There are Harterrs, who coax new alloys from the Sinterers, and Weavers, who integrate those materials into the city's ever-growing structure.

An individual’s memory is not an abstract thing in their mind. It is a physical record, a part of their layered body. The deep-etched scars from a time a platform collapsed are kept as a mark of experience. A sliver of metal from a legendary, long-vanished structure might be deliberately integrated into their shell, a physical link to the past. To know an elder Accreted is to read the history of the city in their very form.

They do not die in our sense. When an Accreted’s core becomes too saturated with heavy metals, when their body becomes too large and heavy to navigate the shifting gantries, they do not fight it. It is the final adaptation. They make their way to a designated Anchor Point, a nexus of bridges or a foundational platform that requires strengthening. They extend their pylons deep into the spongy ground, lock their joints, and merge their fungal network with that of the greater city. They become a living pillar, their bodies slowly dissolving into the structure, their consciousness dissolving into the basin's hum, their accumulated knowledge and strength becoming a permanent part of the city's skeleton. In this way, the dead are not mourned but read; their fossilized forms are the strongest parts of the city, their physical presence a lesson in adaptation and permanence.

They are not conquerors of this embryonic world. They are its stewards, its immune system, its evolving consciousness. They are the ones who feel the basin's desire to become a planet, and they are the hands that guide the Sinterers, the cities, and the fungi in that chaotic, collaborative dance. They are the will living within the violently active peace, the thoughts forming inside a prototype world, waiting for it to decide on a shape.
