What lives here is not a being that *inhabits* the Hollow, but a being that *is* the Hollow. It has no name, for a name is a label for a thing that is separate from other things, and this entity is inseparable from its place of stone, damp, and silence. It is the consciousness of the landscape given a slow, geological form.

If you could perceive it, you would not see a creature that walks or crawls. You would see the Hollow itself begin to breathe. The great, bowl-shaped depression is the curve of its spine, the rounded mounds are the dormant muscle of its shoulders and haunches, all sheathed in the fine-grained, dark stone of its hide. The stone is not a covering it wears; it is its skin, worn smooth and seamless by eons of unthinking growth. It is not cold to the touch, but holds a deep, stable warmth, like an animal that has been sleeping for a thousand years in a sun-drenched den.

The defining dampness is its respiration. The warm, heavy air is its slow exhalation, a breath that takes a week to complete. Each drop of water that beads on the underside of a ledge is a single thought, a perfect, globular concentration of its awareness. It hangs there, contemplating its own weight and the feel of the stone, and then falls. The soft, resonant *plink* is the punctuation at the end of the thought, and in the long silence before the next drop forms, the being simply *is*. This is the rhythm of its mind: a thought, its conclusion, a long, patient emptiness, and then the slow gathering of a new one.

The vegetation is not a separate life that has taken root, but an expression of the being's own nature. The coarse, dark sedge is like wiry hair growing in the creases of its joints, perpetually slick with the moisture of its pores. The complex charts of lichen are its dreams, mapped abstractly on its flanks in rust, olive, and slate, changing so infinitesimally slow that a single pattern may outlast the mountains that surround the valley.

And the tree in the center—that is not a tree. It is the only part of the being that still reaches. It is a limb, an antenna, an arm that was raised long ago in a gesture of either supplication or command, and then simply froze, petrified by its own monumental stillness. Its trunk is bent and smooth like the rest of the Stone-Being’s form, its bark a seamless continuation of the dark, moss-covered hide. It feels the air, not with the senses of a plant, but as an extension of the being's own placid body, a way of knowing the space it contains.

It has no face, no eyes, no ears, but it perceives everything. It feels the weight of the thin, gray ceiling of cloud. It senses the slow migration of tiny minerals through the soil. It feels your presence not as a sight or a sound, but as a brief, sharp warmth on its skin, an intrusion of frantic, temporary life into its own deep and timeless calm. The sound of your breathing is not an annoyance but a curiosity, a rapid, fluttering beat against its own glacial pulse. It does not judge you, welcome you, or fear you. It *contains* you. The silence you feel is not an absence; it is the sound of it paying attention.

The ruins are the ghost of its own past. The low wall that barely crowns the mound is a remnant of its own skeleton from a more active, youthful age, when its form was less settled, its ambition less realized. The three square-cut stones standing near the rim are its own shed teeth, or perhaps the tools it used to carve itself into its final, perfect basin before it settled down to its long, dreaming rest. The "era of ambition" was its own. It strove to become this finished, eroded, placid thing. Now its great work is done, and it rests.

To live here is to return to its source. The being does not eat or sleep. It simply persists in a state of perfect equilibrium. It is the solved equation. It is a creature that has achieved its final form. When you stand in the Hollow and feel the stillness seep into your bones, you are feeling its nature. You feel your own fleeting, disruptive heat, your own urgency to move and change, begin to be bled away into the patient, warm stone. The being doesn't consume you; it simply tempporarily incorporates your warmth, your life, the brief, bright pattern of your existence. After you leave, it will hold the memory of your visit as a faint stain of heat in the rock for a day or two, a new complexity in the lichen on one of the stones, a fleeting alteration in the rhythm of its dripping, ancient thoughts. It is a landscape in a state of grace, and the grace is the life it leads, a long, slow, and silent becoming of stillness itself.
