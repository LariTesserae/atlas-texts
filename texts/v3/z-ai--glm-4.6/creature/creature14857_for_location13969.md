There is something that lives here, but to call it a "fauna" would be a disservice and a profound misunderstanding. It is not a collection of animals that lives *in* the High Loom; it is the consciousness *of* the High Loom itself. It is a distributed being, a mind made of moss and mycelium, light and kinetic tension. Call them the Chime.

Their primary body is a network you cannot see. It is the **Optic Mycelium**, a system of bioluminescent fungal threads that infuse every inch of the lignin and cellulose geology, weaving the ground itself into a single neural net. In the absolute silence, this is their voice. Thoughts are not spoken but propagated as slow, shimmering waves of soft, ethereal light—cool blues and violets for analysis, warm ambers for directives, sharp, staccato flashes of white for alarm. The heavy, condensation-soaked air acts as a perfect fiber-optic medium, containing and directing their light-language through the haze. To be here is to be walking on the surface of a silent, thinking brain.

From this network, the Chime grow temporary, specialized constructs to interface with the industrial chaos of their own bodies. These are the beings an outsider might mistakenly perceive as the native wildlife.

Most common are the **Scuttlers**. They are not the frantic, shaggy quadrupeds you might glimpse. Those are merely feral, simplified descendants, lost fragments of the network long since severed. A true Scuttler is a marvel of silent efficiency. It is a multi-limbed arthropod of woven bark and chitinous cellulose, its body narrow to slip between the piston-fronds. It is not eyeless, but rather possesses a broad, armored headplate that functions as a complex sensory array, a living oscilloscope. It doesn't see light; it reads the subtle electrical fields, the chemical queues, and the gravitational stresses that precede every snapping vine and bursting pod. Its limbs are tipped not with claws, but with interface nodes that can plug directly into the Loom's circulatory systems, temporarily diverting a nutrient flow or clearing a blockage in a conveyer vine. They are the maintenance crews, the drip-lines, the immune cells of the vast organism, their movements deliberate and precise, a stark contrast to the wild energy of the world around them.

Then there are the **Loomsingers**. They are the priests, the architects, and the foremen of this biological foundry. Where the Scuttlers are low to the ground, the Loomsingers are tall, standing sentinel-like in clusters between the great trunk-like smokestacks. A Loomsinger is a slender, elegant being, surprisingly delicate for its environment. Its body is a lattice of living tissue, hardened like composite ceramic, and it moves with a profound slowness that seems to defy the frantic energy of the Loom. Its "head" is a multifaceted geode of crystalline resin, and from this core, it emits the most complex of the Chime's light-patterns, "singing" the songs that maintain the rhythm of this place.

The violence without a scream is their symphony. When a section of the Loom's ground becomes diseased, nutrient-bankrupt, or structurally unsound, it is the Loomsingers who compose its end. They do not tear it down. They persuade it to dissolve. Through their crystalline heads, they broadcast a specific bio-luminescent and bio-electrical frequency—a chord of light that, when received by the Optic Mycelium, signals the cellular bonds in that area to cease their function. The ground does not collapse; it *relaxes*. It melts into the black slurry you see, not in agony, but in obedience. The thump you feel through your feet is merely the sound of a scheduled demolition in the universe's most perfect anechoic chamber, the final percussive beat in a silent song of renewal.

There is no predator, at least not in the conventional sense. But there is a consequence for not fitting the pattern. The Chime are the absolute law of physics and biology in this valley. Any aberration—a foreign spore, an intrusive mineral deposit, or even a warm, noisy, singularly-minded creature like yourself—is perceived not as a threat, but as a dissonance in the chord.

Should you linger too long, you would not be attacked. You would be managed. A Scuttler would be dispatched, not to harm you, but to analyze your biological and energetic output. You would feel its presence as a static prickling on your skin as it samples the air around you. Nearby, a Loomsinger might turn its head slowly, its geode-face pulsing not with alarm, but with a slow, interrogatory rhythm of cool, analytical light. They are programming the environment around you, perhaps encouraging a new growth of a particularly water-logged and absorbent moss to encroach on your position, or subtly adjusting the thermal updrafts to leech the heat from your body. They do not wish you ill; they merely wish to resolve the glitch you represent, to assimilate your chaotic signal into the silent, orderly hum of the Loom. They are weaving you into the tapestry, thread by thread. In the High Loom, all things are woven, eventually, into the fabric of the silent, breathing machine.
