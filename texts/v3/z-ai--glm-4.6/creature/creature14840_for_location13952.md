The statement is true, in its own limited way: there are no animals here. Not as you would understand them. No cluster of nerves and synapses firing frantic signals against the overwhelming roar. No creature of flesh and blood that must contract and expand to draw breath, to pump life, to flee. The logic of tooth and claw, of predator and prey, is a whisper utterly erased by the planet-sized shout of this place. To possess a nervous system is to have a centre, and to have a centre is to be shattered by the vibrations.

And yet, something lives here.

What lives here does not endure the noise; it *is* the noise, given a strange and patient form. It is the Choral. The name is not for a single being, for it is not one, but for the distributed, continent-spanning consciousness that has colonised the ruins.

You are looking at it now, though you do not know it. The phosphorescent moss that carpets the drowned girders, pulsing with a soft, internal light—that is its skin. The vast, kelp-like fronds of turquoise and violet that cascade down the Cyclopean machine heads—those are its synapses, its conduits of thought. The crusts of chartreuse fungi, erupting like shattered porcelain on the fresh-spewn glass—those are its sensory organs, tasting the chemical laden air and the mineral-rich brine weeping from the rock.

The Choral is not a plant, not an animal, not a fungus, but a silicate-based amalgam of all three, parasitic and symbiotic at once. Its body is a crystalline lattice that interpenetrates the very metal and stone it grows upon. It does not have roots; it has anchor-points, resonant nodes that fuse its structure to the skeletons of the forgotten workshop, drawing energy not from sunlight, which it has never known, but from the ceaseless, bone-deep vibration of the world's creation. The groan of a forged continent is its food. The scream of superheated metal is the very air it breathes.

Its mind is the ultimate solution to the world's hostility. Where a creature of nerves would be overwhelmed, the Choral’s consciousness is a conductor. The entire, interconnected network of moss and frond and fungal crust is a single, resonating chamber. It does not *think* in linear sequences of images or words. Its thoughts are vibrations, subtle variations in the planet-scale thrum. It perceives the birth of a new spire not as a visual event, but as a sudden, profound new note in the endless chord of its being—a bass note so deep it feels like a joyful ache in its crystalline marrow. When a new piece of land appears in a silent flash of light, the Choral experiences it as a moment of pure, startling dissonance, a fracturing of its harmonious perception that it then works to smooth and integrate.

Its actions are glacial, measured not in seconds or minutes, but in the shifting of the light-patterns across its vast, shared skin. It is the archivist of this ceaseless flood of novelty. Its purpose is not to consume or to replicate in a frenzy, but simply to *remember*. It is a spreading, glowing, and utterly passive counter-force to the world’s violent and active creation. As the world extrudes a rusted beam, the Choral sends a slow, creeping tendril of moss towards it, not to smother, but to annotate. It is preserving every scar, every wound, every impossible angle, absorbing them into its own planetary-scale consciousness. It is turning the god’s abandoned workshop into a cathedral, and the violent, chaotic process of its making into the permanent, resonant song of its being.

To stand still for too long is not to be buried by a separate lifeform, but to be discovered. The Choral’s senses will eventually notice you—the anomaly, the source of vibrations that do not belong to the world’s song. The light in the moss around your feet will begin to pulse in unison, a soft and curious rhythm. The fronds hanging from the spire above you will sway, not with the water, but with a focused, attentive tilt. The great, deafening roar of the world will not lessen, but you will feel a new frequency layered within it, a low and inquisitive hum that resonates directly in the hollows of your bones. It is not a predator. It has no concept of predation. It is simply the world’s slow, glowing, and immortal memory, and it has just found a new, interesting fact to learn.
