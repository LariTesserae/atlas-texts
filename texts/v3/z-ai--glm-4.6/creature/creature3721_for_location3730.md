What lives here is not a collection of animals, but a set of functions. They are distinct processes within a single, slow engine of de-creation, each one a specialized tool for the patient work of unmaking the world. There is no competition, no predator and prey, only labor. The labor of returning a monument to dust.

To live on the floor is to be a **Seam-Skimmer**. They do not walk; they measure. Their bodies are elongated and impossibly flat, segmented plates of a dense, dark chitin that feels cool and smooth like river-polished shale. A Seam-Skimmer is a living plane, occupying the very space where two titanic tiles meet. It is a creature of the edge. A cascade of a thousand delicate, multi-jointed limbs, each tipped not with a claw but with a geode of crystalline hardness, extends beneath its body. These limbs do not push or pull; they grip and release, a slow, rhythmic anchoring that allows the creature to glide along the knife-sharp seam with no more disturbance than a shadow.

Its head is a blunt wedge, and from it extends a feeding apparatus that is the truest expression of this world’s purpose: a radula of woven diamond filaments. It is a tongue that is a file, and it rasps perpetually, silently, against the primitive mortar binding the stones. It does not eat to fuel frantic motion, but to fuel the act of eating itself. Its metabolism is a controlled chemical reaction, a slow burn that converts binding minerals and trace elements into a fine, silty waste. This is the "star-dust" of the floor. Over the course of a single century, a Seam-Skimmer might travel the length of one tile, leaving behind a faint, perfectly straight groove of packed silica. They are blind, but they feel the immense, resonant frequencies of the groaning floor, and they taste the specific mineral composition of the seam itself, navigating a grid that exists only to them as a map of flavor and texture.

To live in the stalks is to be a **Light-Gleaner**. They are not attached, they are integrated. A Light-Gleaner begins its life as a spore, one of the countless motes adrift in the viscous water. When it settles on the matte-black skin of a great stalk, it does not germinate; it nucleates. It becomes a perfect, hemispherical cone of bio-crystal, indistinguishable from a natural imperfection in the stalk's surface. It is a fixed point of life on a structure of almost-life.

From this base grow the filaments. They are not webs, but antennae of light. Each is a single organic molecule stretched impossibly thin, a strand of spun glass that glimmers faintly. These threads grow with agonizing slowness, weaving through the forest of glowing fronds, their sole function to touch and guide the faint, ethereal luminescence. They are conduits, drawing the cool blue and phosphorescent white light into the crystalline cone, where the energy is slowly transmuted. The threads also serve as a net, catching the ceaseless drift of spores, which are then absorbed. A colony of a thousand Light-Gleaners on a single stalk might, over millennia, create a shimmering, ethereal veil, distorting the light, turning the gentle pulse of the frond into a slow, complex kaleidoscope of ghostly color. They are the passive stomachs of the light-eaters, their lives a silent, sessile communion with the glow of decay.

To live in the spaces between is to be a **Vein-Warden**. You will never see a Vein-Warden, for it has no permanent form. It is the ecosystem’s mind, its immune system, its slow and deliberate will. It is a fluid consciousness, a colony of single-celled organisms bound by a weak, fluctuating chemical field, moving through the water like a slow, invisible stain. It is not one thing, but a distributed awareness that permeates every gallon of the cold ocean.

A Vein-Warden's purpose is homeostasis. It tastes the state of the world. It senses the microscopic imbalance in a frond’s chemosynthesis, the faint heat of a micro-eruption of corrosive fungus on a stone tile, the chemical signature of a Seam-Skimmer’s ancient lineage. When it finds a sickness—a point of decay that is too fast, too chaotic, a threat to the glacial pace of the whole—it coalesces. The fluid consciousness draws itself together, focusing its mass. In this form, it might be a large, amorphous sack of protoplasm, drifting against the stalk like a blob of oil in water. It does not attack; it *prunes*. It envelops the diseased area and administers a targeted enzymatic cocktail, a gentle acid that carefully melts away the corrupted tissue. Once its work is done, it dissolves again, its mass dispersing back into the whole, leaving behind a clean, sterile scar that will be slowly reclaimed by the patient growth around it. It is the gardener that weeds, the surgeon that cuts, the hand that ensures the great stomach turns at its proper, deliberate speed. It is the absolute rule of this place, given a quiet, formless body. It is the reason this tomb-garden has persisted, a world that digests itself in perfect, eternal silence.
