In this churning, light-breathing womb of a world, to be a single thing is a defiance. To have a name, a shape, a boundary between your skin and the suffocating, living air, is to declare war on the chaos. Here, the inhabitants are not so much discrete beings as they are temporary eddies in a hurricane of flesh and chitin.

The ones who call themselves the **Kith** are the most desperate and most conscious of this defiance. They are the creatures the world would call Reclaimers, but that name describes only their relationship to the dead. The Kith see themselves as that: family, the only cluster of ordered self-awareness in a realm that seeks to dissolve them. They are built for clinging. Their bodies are slender and elongated, possessed of a fluid, boneless grace reminiscent of a stoat or eel, but sheathed in a tough yet porous hide that sweats a constant, cooling, saline slime. They have six limbs; two prehensile arms ending in four delicate, mutually-opposable fingers, and four powerful legs whose splayed, gecko-like feet can find purchase on the slickest, most vertical fungal flesh.

Their faces are smooth, almost featureless, save for two large, dark, forward-facing eyes perfectly adapted to the pearlescent gloom. There is no nose, only a series of tremor-sensitive pits along their jawline. They do not breathe the air so much as filter it, a constant, shallow flutter of a gill-slit behind their head, tasting the spore-heavy atmosphere for warning and for sustenance. Communication is a private thing. It is a language of light and pressure. Along their flanks, rows of bioluminescent photophores can pulse in complex, shimmering patterns, a light-speak that is invisible against the backdrop of the Great Bloom but intimate at close quarters. Their true conversation, however, is tactile. They press their damp, flat palms against the stone, the mycelium, the very bones of their coral-platforms, "listening" to the symphony of the valley. They feel the Skitterer-rivers, the distant crunch of a Geode-Maw, the deep-lunged grind of a Tuneller beneath them. It is a language of vibration, of interpreting the world's roar as a detailed narrative.

The Kith are perpetual mourners. They are born, live, and die in the shadow of their own impermanence. Their platforms of pressed mycelium and bone are not homes; they are statements. They build them on the massive, inactive fungal shelves because the shelf itself is a legend—a Great Bloom that has died. To build upon its corpse is to acknowledge their own fate. They are the planet's gutters, its butchers. When a Geode-Maw falters, succumbs to a Maw-Jewel infestation or is simply torn apart by a coordinated frenzy of its own kind, the Kith descend. They do not fear the frenzy; they enter it like monks entering a sacred font, their slender bodies weaving through the carnage. They retrieve not just flesh, but the indigestible geode-core of the beast, the immense armored plates, the colossal tendons. These are the building blocks, the reclaimed materials from which they shore up the brief, fragile wall of their existence.

But the Kith are not alone in their intelligence, only in their self-awareness. The valley is full of other, stranger minds.

There are the **Glint-wings**, who are not a species so much as a single, distributed consciousness. They operate in shrieking, razor-like squadrons, their movements so perfectly synchronized they seem less like a flock of birds and more like a single entity shattered into a hundred glittering parts. They communicate via the frequency of their own wingbeats, creating a localized, high-pitched thrum that directs the swarm's attack. Their minds are simple, predatory, and united; a test of this is to isolate a single Glint-wing, whose frantic, suicidal impulses immediately prove it is nothing without the whole.

The **Geode-Maws**, too, have a semblance of society. They travel in immense, ponderous herds, their six-leg gait shaking the ground. Their young, no bigger than a Kith, are soft-shelled and kept in the center of the herd, protected by the living bulk of their elders. They are not merely stupid beasts of prey; they are farmers. They cultivate within their own multi-chambered guts a particular strain of phosphorescent mold. When they defecate, they leave behind glowing, nutrient-rich trails that the Skitterer currents follow, herding their food source and maintaining the "soil" of the valley floor. Their roar is not just a threat, but a territorial signal, a deep seismic broadcast that declares their grazing grounds.

Deeper still are beings for whom the word "animal" is inadequate. The **Tunnellers** are geology given will. They are colossal, blind, soft-bodied things, miles long, whose passage through the cavern's basaltic bedrock is the true agent of erosion. They are sieve-vores, swallowing entire regions of rock, soil, and life, metabolizing the silicates and trace minerals, and excreting a fine, nutrient-poor sand from pores along their back. No Kith has ever seen one; they are known only by their wake—the sudden opening of a sinkhole, the deep, infrasonic hum that vibrates through the soles of your feet for days, the fresh, clean-smelling scar of stone left in their path. They are the world's great slow subconscious, digesting its own bones.

And then there is the **Lure-Fungus**, a creature that straddles the line. It is sessile, a permanent resident of the high ledges. Its main body is an insignificant, greyish lump, but from it dangles a single, hundred-foot-long filament, as fine as a hair and stronger than steel. At the end of this line is its lure: a smaller, detached body that mimics a juvenile Glint-wing or a fat, luminescent Skitterer. It pulses, it flits, it mimics the distress calls of its prey on a strand of vibrating silk. It is the ultimate patient hunter, not competing with the frenzy below but simply waiting for one of it—the swooping Glint-wing, the incautious Kith—to become entangled. Its victim is drawn slowly, inexorably upwards, into the waiting maw of the fungal colony that is the Lure-Fungus's true body.

Finally, pupating within the very strata of the ground itself, are the **Chrysalis-Things**. The "soil" is not just dead matter; it is an incubator. For every million Skitterer shells, there is the dormant, nigh-invulnerable ovoid of something else. They are the future, dormant and unpredictable, waiting for a specific combination of spore-rain from the Great Bloom, pressure from a passing Tuneller, and the chemical trigger of a Geode-Maw's death. When they emerge, they will be new, their purpose unknown, their place in the chaotic hierarchy unwritten. They are the world's evolving imagination, and every Kith knows, with a primal, inherited dread, that one day the ground will not seethe beneath their feet. It will hatch.
