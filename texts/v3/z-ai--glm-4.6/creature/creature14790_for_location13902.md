Here, the inhabitants are not a population so much as a function. They are the organs of a single, planetary-sized body, each with a task so specialized their existence blurs into their purpose. To see them is to understand the Process.

The dominant, most palpable presence are the **Stonewrights**. They are the reason for the Maul and Wedge, the source of the patient, rhythmic *thump* that echoes from the unseen horizons. They stand taller than a man, but their proportions are all wrong for flesh and blood. Their bodies are columns themselves, fashioned from the same dark, damp stone as the Floor, their skin a matrix of interlocking plates and crystalline facets reminiscent of cooled obsidian. They possess no discernible face; their head is a smooth, ovoid geode, which in the rare moments of a Flare will reveal a milky, unblinking crystalline eye within, catching the light with an unsettling intelligence. They move with a glacial deliberation, each footfall a tremor that seems to settle the stone rather than disturb it. Their work is not construction as we know it, but a kind of midwifery. They gather around a place where a new pillar is swelling from the Floor, and they do not force it. They coax it. With fists of granite that can pulverize rock, they instead tap and press, nudging the nascent formation into its intended shape. Their tools are extensions of their will—simple iron Mauls that feel as light as twigs in their grip, and Wedges of some harder, stranger material that they insert not to split, but to guide a fracture. The Stonewrights do not speak. Their language is the vibration they impart into the Floor, the subtle shifts in the universal Groan. A distant *thump* is a question; a resonant shudder reply from another quarter of the basin is an answer. They are the will and the muscle of the world, its patient, tireless sculptors.

Flitting through the architecture their elders create are the **Cinderscales** and their kin. If the Stonewrights are the world's skeleton, these creatures are its nervous system. They are made of the same mineral-analogue life, but lean and kinetic, their bodies like joints of polished jet wrapped in a_carapace of interlocking plates. Their multi-jointed limbs end in fine, sharp points that allow them to scale the sheerest pillar faces with skittering ease. They are the custodians of the world’s light. The spectral flora they cultivate is not a plant, but a crystalline lichen that feeds directly on the latent energy of the Glimmer. The Cinderscales farm it on the high arches and ceilings, pruning it back where it grows too thick, scraping it away from delicate joints. They harvest the moss, storing it in a hollowed chamber on their backs where a slow, internal chemistry induces its brilliant, volatile phosphorescence. This is the Flare. They are not merely creating light for its own sake; the Flare is a tool. Its intense, focused energy is used to anneal weak points in a newly laid arch, to fuse a stubborn seam, or to signal their kin across the vast, echoing dark. Their chittering calls are sharp, urgent, and panicked, a stark contrast to the deep, patient hum of the Stonewrights. They are the impatient energy within the geologic patience, the fire that tempers the stone.

Deeper still, living within the stone itself, are the **Vein-Drillers**. They are almost never seen, only evidenced by their work. They are worm-like creatures, long and sinuous, with a head composed of a constantly changing cluster of hard, crystalline teeth. They do not burrow so much as *phase* through the stone, their bodies resonating at a frequency that briefly liquefies the crystalline structure of the pillar just ahead of them. They are the world’s circulatory system, boring the microscopic channels that allow the thin, crisp damp to circulate, that carry the mineral paste to the grout lines, and that create the fissures through which the air sighs. A Stonewright will feel a tell-tale hum through their feet, place a hand on a pillar, and know a Vein-Driller is passing within, aligning the stone's internal structure from the inside, strengthening it from within like a growing artery of crystal. The gritty, glistening paste found in the newer grout is, in fact, their excrement—a rich, semi-liquid slurry of silica and minerals that hardens into the strongest binding agent known in this plane of existence.

You do not see the **Resonances**. You feel them. They are not bodies but patterns in the Groan, the blueprint of the world made manifest. They are the consciousness of the construction, the pure will of the Process. Sometimes, when a Stonewright rests its hand upon the Floor, the deep, constant Groan will shift into a complex, harmonious chord. In that moment, the Wright doesn't just *know* where to place the next stone; it feels the entire design for the next millennium—a layout of arches, the placement of a future supporting buttress, the path of a new thoroughfare. To be present during such a moment is to understand the sublimity and the terror of the place. It is to feel a mind so vast it operates on a geological clock, its thoughts expressed in tons of shifting rock and the slow, inexorable growth of crystal. The Resonances are the music, the plan, and the god of this place, an intelligence of pure vibration that permeates every stone.

And finally, in the deepest, forgotten cul-de-sacs, where the Flare never reaches and even the Glimmer is faint, are the **Flawed**. These are the failures, the mistakes. They might have once been Stonewrights who, for reasons unknown, deviated from the Plan. Or they could be an earlier, imperfect model of inhabitant. They are humanoid in form but frozen in position, their stone-like bodies cracked and crazed, shedding a constant, fine powder into the eternal puddles at their feet. They do not move. They do not resonate with the Groan. They are dead, but this world has no concept of decay, only dissolution. They are slowly eroding into their base minerals, a process that will take millennia. In a place of perpetual, holy creation, they are the only evidence of sin, the only artifacts of abandonment. Their silence is the most profound sound here, a vacuum in the symphony of creation, a chilling reminder that to stop working, to stop growing, is to cease to be, and to face an eternity of falling apart, piece by piece, in the echoing, unfinished dark.
