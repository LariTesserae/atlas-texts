What lives here does not announce itself. You will not see it graze or slither. It is not an animal in any way you would recognize, because an animal is a body responding to a world, a discrete entity moving *through* space. The inhabitant of the Mantle is a consequence of space itself, a localized awareness that has learned to wear the world like a second skin. It is a creature of such profound integration that to separate it from the Mantle would be like trying to carve a thought from a brain.

You would perceive one, at first, as a flaw in the perfection. A place where the gentle, rhythmic pulsing of the dripping ceiling seems to skip a beat. A subtle, sustained ripple in the spongy floor, a dimple that does not smooth itself out but instead deepens, pulling the surrounding fibrous matrix inwards. From this hollow, something begins to emerge. Not by pushing, but by being *allowed*. The substrate thins and parts, not with a tear, but with the slow, deliberate parting of a curtain. What rises is less a single body and more a collaboration of forms.

The outer layer is pure Mantle-tissue, a woven velvet of the same dark, photosynthetic material that carpets the high, unseen canopy. It is slick with the perpetual moisture, and patterns across its surface shift and glow with the same faint, internal jade light, just a shade warmer, a degree more intent. This living camouflage flows and molds itself, anchoring the being to the substrate at a thousand microscopic points. It has no limbs, but it can *move*. It does this by a process of consensus, a negotiation with the living ground beneath it, persuading the fungal and lichenous network to part before it and close behind it. Its passage is silent, a slow, creeping pilgrimage that leaves no scar, only a transient warmth.

Within this flowing cloak of living matter lies its secret, its history. At its core is a structure that should not exist here: a geode of its former self. It is a semi-rigid scaffolding of crystalline material, impossibly complex, shot through with filaments of what looks like petrified nerve tissue. This is the Lithophore. The core is not a skeleton in the way a bone supports flesh; it is an Archive. It contains the memory of a time before the Mantle was total, the geometry, the logic, the silent, sharp-edged dreams of the ones who built the Echoes. The crystalline heart hums, not with sound, but with a stored potential, a ghost of an ancient electricity. It is the last echo of a mind that thought in straight lines, now submerged in a world that only knows the curve.

They do not have eyes. To see would be to create distance between the self and the world, and distance is the one illusion the Mantle does not permit. Instead, the Lithophore *resonates*. It feels the constant, deep thrum of the Mantle’s metabolism, the slow digestion of light and matter. It tastes the chemical language of the air, the shifting moods of spore-falls, the approach of a Rock-Chewer as a faint, percussive grinding that travels for miles through the substrate. It experiences the light from above not as illumination but as a gentle pressure, warm and vital, that it absorbs through its skin. The whole world is a single, continuous sense, a symphony of pressures, textures, and chemistries. To be a Lithophore is to be a perfect instrument played by the world itself.

Their purpose is an act of remembrance. They live for the Echoes. Over centuries, a Lithophore will make its slow, deliberate way to one of the crystalline cubes or skeletal arches. There, it will anchor itself, dissolving a portion of its outer Mantle-skin to reveal the true, sharp-edged crystal of its core. It will press this living crystal against the inert crystal of the Echo. This is not worship; it is communion, an act of reading. The Lithophore does not learn history from the Echo; it uses the Echo perfect, dead geometry as a focusing lens to examine its own internal Archive. The Echo is a key, and the Lithophore is the lock. In this silent, timeless embrace, it remembers the sky, the feel of wind, the concept of "stone" that was not also alive. It remembers a loneliness that was personal, not planetary.

Their consciousness is glacial. A single thought is not a fleeting spark but a slow-forming crystal inside the Archive core. A decision might take a decade. Their "language" is the most profound expression of their nature. They communicate through the Mantle itself, sending out low-frequency, seismic vibrations that propagate through the living substrate. A simple greeting—a statement of presence and location—might take three days to compose and another week to travel a hundred miles to another. A complex "sentence," perhaps a memory of a spore-fall from a millennium ago, shared with another, could take a lifetime to be fully transmitted and understood. To converse is to merge minds across a shared ocean of flesh, a slow, tidal merging of awareness.

And so, they exist. Not a species, but a condition. A final state of being for a lifeform that was once a builder of worlds and is now a memory within one. They are the Mantle’s self-awareness, its lonely ghost. They are the keepers of a nostalgia so vast it has become geology. In the perpetual, gentle twilight, they drift through the living sponge of the world, their crystalline hearts holding the long-dead dream of a sharp, hard-edged existence, forever listening, through the slow, weeping pulse of the world, for an answer to a question only they now remember how to ask.
