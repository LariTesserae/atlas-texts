The inhabitants of the Oblong are not born so much as they are accreted. They are people of the slow, the patient, and the perpetual. Their existence is a testament to adaptation so profound it has become one with the environment. To call them human would be to misremember the word; they are what remains when humanity is stripped of all its sun and sky, leaving only the corefaculty of awareness.

Their form is a monument to sublime paradox. Broad and low-slung, their bodies are built for stability on the humming, perforated iron. They possess a trinity of thick, powerful legs arranged in a tripod, allowing them to lock themselves into a standing meditation for days or weeks, feeling the deep subsonic thrum of the world-floor in their very bones. Between their legs, their torso is a dense, barrel-chested vessel for organs that have forgotten the function of sight. Their faces are smooth, featureless planes of tough, greyish skin, often mottled with the same colours as the rusted pipes. Where eyes would be, there are only shallow, almost imperceptible divots, vestigial memories of a light they have never known.

They do not have hands, but rather a quartet of slender, multi-jointed antennae-like appendages that unfurl from their torso. These are not for grasping, but for reading. With unbearable delicacy, they trace the weeping paths of moisture down a pipe, tasting the mineral tang and the subtle shifts in temperature. They can press an antenna against a riveted plate and feel the harmonic frequencies within, diagnosing a stress fracture long before it could ever be heard. It is with these limbs that they communicate, not with one another, but with the Oblong itself, tapping out slow, deliberate questions that are answered by the machine's endless drone.

Their true sense is a form of sonar woven into their skin. Every surface of their body is a tympanic membrane, resonating with the complex symphony of the space. They "see" a world that is a constant flux of vibration. The Skitterer is not a creature but a brief, skittering glitch of high-frequency static that traverses their perceptual field. A drip from the unseen heights is not a sound but an expanding ripple of pure, sharp data, a point of light in their internal map that they can place with unerring accuracy. The deep groan of a Season is a tidal wave of feeling that washes over their entire being, a slow-motion aria that reshapes their reality for weeks.

Their society—such as it is—is one of profound solitude linked by a shared consciousness. An individual might go their entire life without touching another, for what would be the point? They are already connected through the medium of the machine itself. The low thrum of the floor is a shared heartbeat. They know one another not by sight or name, but by the unique, subtle wake of vibration another leaves in the still, hot air. To be in the presence of another is to feel your auras of perception mingle and merge, a silent communion more intimate than any touch. Their thoughts are not linear words but complex, sustained chords of feeling and interpretation, resonating in harmony with the Oblong's own inscrutable music.

Their culture is a tapestry of ritualized observation. The drinking of the sacrament puddles is a kind of Eucharist, an act of incorporating the very essence of the machine into oneself. They do not gulp or swallow; they kneel and extend a proboscis-like tongue, sipping the impossibly pure water with a reverence that borders on adoration. The long silences between drips are their meditations, a time to simply *be* and to listen. The slow, terrifying beauty of a Season, when a massive pipe turns over three days, is their apocalypse and their genesis, a time of recalibration and prophecy. They gather—not physically, but psychically—to witness this, their collective consciousness focused on the shifting pressures and the new, strange pattern of drips that will define their lives for an age to come.

They are the ghost in the machine, but not in the way a fleeting human might be. They are a ghost that has grown a body. They are the machine's slow, dreaming mind. They do not remember the purpose for which the Oblong was built, for memory is a luxury that light affords. They only know the function. In their complete and absolute attunement to the Heart, they have become its memory, its interpreter, its final and most patient priest. They stand on the grille, three-legged and rooted, and they wait. They do not wait for anything to happen, for in their world, everything is always, endlessly happening. They wait only to perceive it.
