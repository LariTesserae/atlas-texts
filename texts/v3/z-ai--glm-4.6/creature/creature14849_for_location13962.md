They are the Chimeless.

They do not have a name for themselves in the way you might understand it, for they have little use for spoken words. To call them the Chimeless is to name them by what they lack, but it is the most apt descriptor the world of sound can offer. They are a people who live in the key of silence.

Their bodies are sculpted by the damp and the stone. They are tall and spare, with long, gracefully articulated limbs that seem to have too many joints. It is not a horror, but an elegant adaptation, allowing them to contort their bodies to fit the cracks and ledges of their basalt home. Their skin is the first thing you notice. It is not like human skin. It is smooth and pearlescent, like the inside of an oyster shell, but textured with the faint, velvety nap of the moss they cultivate on their homes. It clings to their lean muscle, and when the mist clings to them, they are almost invisible against the wet rock. The constant cold has given them a layer of subcutaneous fat, but it is distributed differently, pooling softly at their joints and along their spine, creating a look that is both sharp and fluid.

Their faces are wide and placid, with high cheekbones and shallow brows that shelter eyes of a profound and liquid black. There are no visible pupils, only pools that absorb the bruised twilight, reflecting nothing. They see perfectly in this dim world, but to a creature of sun and shadow, their gaze would be unnerving: an endless, passive stare. They breathe through wide, flat nostrils that are constantly flaring, testing the air for the taste of iron and mint. This is how they smell not only the world, but each other, reading a complex language of pheromones in the heavy air.

Their feet and hands are their genius. The fingers are long and prehensile, devoid of claws or sharp nails, ending in broad, toughened pads that can grip the slickest obsidian. The toes are similarly equipped, and they can hang from a ledge by a single foot with the casual indifference of a sloth. Their movement is a silent, flowing ballet. They do not walk; they pour themselves from one handhold to the next, a constant, deliberate fall that is always arrested at the last second.

Right from birth, they are taught to move. An infant is never laid down to rest on a flat surface; to do so would be a death sentence. It is swaddled in soft, cured moss and hung in a cradle of woven vines that is never truly still. The cradle is attached to a springy branch of a giant fern, and the parent, or another member of the community, will give it a gentle, rhythmic push every few minutes. This rocking is their first lullaby, a motion that is as instinctual as breathing. The elderly, when their limbs grow too stiff for climbing, are given positions as Rhythm-Keepers. They sit on wide ledges, rocking back and forth on polished stone balls, or swaying in woven chairs, their constant, subtle motion a lifelong habit that ensures they are never overwhelmed by a lack of it. To be still is to be assimilated.

The most defining aspect of their being, however, is their relationship with The Drift.

They do not fear it. They *are* it.

The Chimeless are the shepherds of the silent, creeping tide. The Drift is their larder, their mortar, their shield, and their god. The taste of iron in the air is from the rust-red blood that courses just beneath their skin, which has a metallic tang the Drift finds unpalatable. They have coated their entire settlement, and themselves, in a slime they cultivate from specific rare mosses mixed with their own saliva. This biofilm—their "Slick"—creates a barrier, making their homes and their bodies less appealing to the endless appetite of the soft-bodied creatures.

Their culture is one of subtle touch and vibration. Language is a complex system of strokes, pressures, and gestures on the skin. A long, slow caress down the arm is a question of intent. A sharp, triple-tap on the shoulder is a warning of danger from above. The swaying bridges are not just for crossing; they are communication lines. A complex pattern of tugs on a primary guide-rope can convey a message from the lowest tiers to the highest lookout in a matter of minutes, a silent telegram that travels through the entire settlement.

Technology is an extension of their bodies. Their tools are not for breaking, but for guiding. Long, flexible poles of cured fern-stalk are used to gently divert flows of The Drift away from vulnerable rope fibers or toward a designated "feeding shelf" where it can be easily harvested for food. The Drift itself, when dried and pressed, becomes a tough, water-resistant material used to patch roofs and reinforce baskets. The six-legged, wet-feathered herons are not hunted, but respected as "The Sharp-Sighted," another force that sculpts and contains the Drift's expansion. The Chimeless will sometimes follow a hunting heron, gathering the "off-cuts" of its meal with quiet efficiency.

Sustenance comes from The Drift and the jungle. They harvest the soft creatures with curved, smooth-bladed wooden scrapers, piling them into clay pots where they are fermented into a nutrient-rich, tasteless paste. This is their staple. To this, they add the vibrant, spicy mosses and bitter, dark-fruited ferns they cultivate on the high ledges, creating a diet that is as strange and balanced as the ecosystem they inhabit.

To be a Chimeless is to understand that you are a temporary eddy in a great, silent current. They are not fighting the world; they are masterfully navigating it. They live in a world of constant, gentle, and profound pressure. The stone presses from below, the sky presses from above, and The Drift presses from all sides, a tactile reminder that life is not a state of being, but a process of becoming, of moving just enough to maintain your shape against the soft, eternal quiet that waits for you to stop. They are a civilization built on a single, shared principle: Do not be still.
