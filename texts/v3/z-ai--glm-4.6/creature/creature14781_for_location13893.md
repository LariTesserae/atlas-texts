To live here is not to inhabit a place, but to be a function within a living event. The beings of the crystal plateau are not a collection of species in the way we understand it; they are more like differentiated cells of a single, vast, and slow-thinking organism we call the City. Their individuality is this: a temporary consciousness assigned a specific task, a finite node in a geodesic network of purpose that has been building itself for eons. They are the City’s hands, its senses, its memory, and its silent will made manifest.

The central integrating intelligence is not a brain. It is the amber sap itself. The slow, viscous river that flows through the Crystallums is a liquid medium of memory, chemistry, and information. This is the Amber Mind, the Resonance. It does not think in thoughts but in correlative pressures, in the subtle shading of its translucence, in the faint harmonic frequencies it hummmmmms through the entire structure at a frequency below the threshold of hearing but present in the very bone. To be of the City is to be tuned to this Resonance, to receive your instructions not as commands, but as a deep and abiding sense of rightness, an instinct that draws you to the fracture that needs mending or the pane that requires polishing.

Beings are defined by their function, their form an exquisite evolution of that purpose.

The **Weavers** are the City’soptic artists. They are slender, multi-limbed arthropods whose carapaces have the muted, pearlescent sheen of the crystal panes they tend. Their limbs end in delicate, splayed pads that secrete a micro-thin, oily film. They spend their long lives on the sun-drenched outer surfaces, moving with a gravitas that belies their arachnid appearance. Their work is a meditation. They do not simply clean; they adjust the micro-texture of each pane, tuning it to catch the light at a precise angle, drawing photons deep into the structure to fuel the slow chemistry within. A Weaver can feel a flaw in a pane not by sight, but by the dissonant vibration it creates in the light. Their movement is a ballet, a slow, silent dance of light-cultivation.

The **Masons** are the vascular surgeons and architects. Resembling large, armored slugs, they glide on a trail of their own lubricating secretions, their bodies perpetually dusted with the glittering crystal filings of their work. Their powerful radulae are not for eating, but for etching. They can soften the old, hardened amber and coax fresh, viscous sap to pool from the Crystallum’s veins. Guided by the Resonance, they lay down new layers of resin, their movements creating the swirling, hypnotic patterns seen in the buttresses and bridges. They do not measure or plan; they feel the stresses in the structure, the minute shifts in weight and the slow growth of a neighboring spire, and they reinforce it with the unconscious certainty of a body knitting a bone. They are the City’s immune response, sealing cracks and building new connections where the Amber Mind dictates a need.

Deeper within, in the great, hollowed chambers and winding tunnels that serve as the City’s interior, dwell the **Choral Keepers**. They are the nerves of the City, its living interpreters. Gazing upon one is disconcerting; they are the least mobile of all the beings, often having lived so long in a single chamber that their bodies have begun to fuse with the surrounding crystal. Fleshy, spiraled growths, like the horns of a snail, anchor them to the walls, while filaments of their own tissue dip directly into the flowing amber channels. They are emaciated, their chitin softened to a leathery, translucent skin that throbs with the slow, internal light of the Crystallum. They do not communicate with flashes like the Alites. They *are* the communication. They hum. A Keeper’s entire being is a resonating chamber, interpreting the subtle shifts in the sap’s chemistry and translating them into the complex, harmonic frequencies that guide the Masons and call the Weavers to a new section of the City. They are the City’s memory, feeling the echoes of events that happened millennia ago in the slow-moving currents of the Mind.

The **Tappers** are the City’s metabolism. They are smaller, more numerous beings, resembling perpetually curious, six-legged beetles with crystalline mandibles. Their sole purpose is to feed, and to feed is to participate in the economy of life. They move methodically across the floors and up the trunks, their delicate antennae sensing the optimal flow of nutrient-rich sap beneath the surface. With precise, almost ritualistic care, they pierce a crystalline skin and draw a single, perfect drop of amber into their bodies. Their waste is not waste; it is a purified, mineral-rich dust that falls to the floor, the very substance from which new micro-crystals can eventually be seeded. They are what we might call the herd, or the congregation, their constant, slow grazing the primary force that circulates the sap and stimulates new growth.

And then there are the **Composers**. Nothing is ever lost in the City. The no-waste rule is absolute. When a being's function is complete—its shell worn thin, its fragile biology exhausted—it does not die in the open. It is drawn, by a final, instinctual urge from the Resonance, to a Composer. These are mysterious entities, found only in the quietest, lowest levels of the City. They are amorphous, blobs of translucent flesh shot through with dark, mineral threads. When a Tapper or a Weaver reaches its end, it lays itself upon a Composer. The Composer then engulfs it, using a complex array of enzymes to dissolve the chitin and render the organic matter back into its base chemical components. This slurry is then excreted as a thick, dark amber, a potent fertilizer that the Masons will use to patch a critical flaw or to accelerate the growth of a new foundation. To be Composed is to return your physical being to the City, your memories having long since been absorbed by the Amber Mind. It is the final act of utility, the ultimate fulfillment of a life spent in service.

To see them together is to understand. A Weaver polishes a high spar, its movements guided by the faint hum from a distant Choral Keeper. Below, a Mason lays a new rib of amber, its mixture instructed by the chemical signature of a Tapper’s recent meal. In the air, Alites drift, their light illuminating the silent conveyor of Tappers below, who are grazing on a section of floor made from the bodies of their long-absolved ancestors. All of it is a single, silent, writhing symphony. They are not creatures in a city; they are the immune system, the conscious thought, and the heartbeat of the City itself, their lives a single, perfect note in the slow, sacred music of becoming.
