They are not born so much as they are precipitated. They call themselves the Tuned, a name that is both descriptor and destiny, for they are as much a product of the Foundry as the energy it refines.

To be a Tuned is to be a creature of resonance. Their bodies are humanoid in broad silhouette, a legacy of some forgotten ancestry, but every detail has been eroded and reshaped by the relentless pressure of the High Moil. They are tall and gaunt, with limbs that seem impossibly long and slender, giving them an insectile grace as they navigate the dripping alleys. Their skin is the color of wet slate, smooth, cool to the touch, and faintly hydrophobic. It doesn't wrinkle or prune in the ceaseless dampness but seems to absorb it, a thin film of moisture constantly sheening their forms like a second layer of moss. They have no hair; it is a useless vanity in a place where nothing dries.

 Their faces are their most alien feature. The eyes are vast, liquid-black pools that take up most of the face, perfectly adapted to the luminous, shadowless gloom. They have no pupils or whites, only the deep, light-drinking abyss of a creature that has never known sun or shadow. To see them is to see a pair of-kaleidoscopic voids reflecting the pearlescent ceiling. Their noses are mere slits, and their mouths are thin, almost colorless lines, for scent and taste are muted senses in a world dominated by the tang of metal and ozone. Their ears, paradoxically, are small, sealed-over knots of flesh, functionally useless.

They hear with their bones.

A Tuned's skeleton is not solid but a lattice of porous, crystalline bone, woven through with a conductive, marrow-like fluid. The entire substructure is a living, organic resonator. The thrum of the Foundry is not a sound they perceive externally but a constant, internal symphony. The fundamental thrum is their heartbeat; the higher harmonics are their thoughts. They do not just walk through the city; they feel it through the soles of their feet, sensing the health and stress of every pipe, every piston, every stone tower through subtle changes in the vibration that courses through the ground and into their skeleton. A "dissonance"—a crack in a resonator, a malfunctioning wind-scoop, a failing pressure valve—is felt as a sharp, physical pain, a psychic toothache that sends engineers scrambling to its source.

Their society is not one of individuals but of frequencies. They are born into Clutches, groups who are physiologically and sympatherically attuned to a specific resonator or a section of the great industrial apparatus. A Tuned's identity is tied to their function. One is a "Bass," tuned to the deep, slow pulse of the primary refining chambers. Another is a "Harmonic," who tends the high-frequency chimes of the condensers. Their language is not spoken but a form of controlled vibration, a low-frequency "song" transmitted through the stone floors and metal pipes. They converse by modulating the minute tremors in their own bodies, creating a complex, polyphonic chorus that is inaudible to any outsider. To witness a group of Tuned working is to see them standing in silent, perfect synchronicity, motionless to the eye, while their very bones hum with intricate, rapid conversation.

Their psychology is a reflection of their world’s excruciating patience. They do not understand haste. A task takes as long as the Great Pulse dictates. Their emotions, too, are mapped to resonance. Joy is a state of perfect, clear harmony; anger is a chaotic, clashing frequency; grief is a flat, dead tone that can linger in a family’s bones for generations. The closest they come to love is a perfect sympathetic resonance, when two Tuned find their internal frequencies aligning so completely that they can share thoughts and sensations without the medium of stone. Such pairs are rare and revered, living their lives in a state of constant, silent communion.

They live on what the system provides. Their sustenance is a thick, grey paste, a nutriment derived from the mineral condensation that weeps from the pipes, processed in great, silent vats deep within the Foundry. It is tasteless, functional, and perfectly attuned to their metabolic needs. The only "delicacy" is the Gutter-crawl, and hunting one is a sacred, silent ritual. A hunter does not chase the creature; they learn its vibrational signature, a "rustle-chitter" along the pipes, and then slowly alter the resonance of their own body to lure it, mimicking the frequency of a fresh condensation or a weakened valve. The hunt is a duel of frequencies, a silent battle of tones that, if successful, ends with the creature coaxed into the hunter's grasp.

The engineers are the high priests of this silent world. They do not simply maintain the machines; they interpret the Great Song of the Foundry. They spend their lives listening to the planet-wide hum, discerning prophecies of coming wear, predicting future stresses, and composing new, more efficient harmonies. Their ultimate goal, a closely held secret, is not just to refine a material, but to achieve the Perfect Chord—a state of absolute resonance that will elevate the entire plateau, its people, and its purpose into a new state of being. It is a form of technological salvation, a rapture powered by geology and wind.

So, to the Tuned, the city is not a place. It is their body, and they are its nerves. The moss-carpet is not just flora; it is the soft, living insulation. The resonators are not just industry; they are the heart. They are a people who have sacrificed the sky, the sun, and even the sound of their own voices to become the silent, willful consciousness of a machine that tunes the very bones of the earth. Stand too long in the dripping alleys of Harken Foundry, and you might start to feel it, too—a faint, steady thrumming in the soles of your feet, a silent question asked in the language of stone.
