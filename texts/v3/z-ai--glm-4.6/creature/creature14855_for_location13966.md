Whoever lives here is not a visitor. A visitor would be erased, pulped into the medium, their fleeting, flesh-bound pattern of noise subsumed and simplified into a single, dissonant scream before fading. What lives here is native to the engine room, as integral as the crystal trees or the basso-profundo hum. They are The Choral.

The Choral are not born; they are tuned. They do not have bodies; they are anchors. An individual Choral is a symbiosis of two things: a Core and a Song.

The Core is a physical object, the only truly solid part of their being. It is a crystal, but no crystal like the flora. It is a Master-Facet, a hyper-dense lattice of grown and cooled sound, a node of such intricate complexity that it appears as a polished sphere of smoked obsidian, shot through with veins of frozen light. This sphere is no larger than a clenched fist. It is their physical center, their memory, their "I." It is the platform upon which their consciousness is etched. The cores are ancient, some predating the slag floor on which they rest, inherited from a previous "composition" of the world. They are their anchor against the sonic tides.

The Song is their presence, their body, and their will. It is a self-generated and meticulously maintained field of resonance that extends from the Core in a shimmering, invisible sphere, perhaps two metres across. Within this sphere, the Choral IS a melody. They are not a random swirl like the wild Motile Harmonies; they are a complex, repeating, and evolving composition. By looking at one you would perceive nothing, but to *feel* one is to perceive a living tapestry of woven frequencies. Each Choral’s Song is unique—a sonic fingerprint, a personal opus. The "texture" of this song—the interplay of its harmonics and overtones—is what gives them their character. Some are slow, deep Baroque fugues, ponderous and wise. Others are skittish, intricate arpeggios, flitting from one frequency to the next.

They perceive the world not as a collection of objects, but as an orchestral score. To "see" a distant crystal tree is to resonate with its fundamental frequency and feel its size, its health, its mineral composition, and its "mood"—which harmonics it is currently thirsty for. To "hear" is not a passive act but a total, ambient assimilation of data. The Choral are constantly aware of the position, velocity, and emotional state (aggression, hunger, peace) of every bass-cloud and harmony-pack for kilometres around, not by hearing them, but by feeling the disturbances they make in the medium, like reading ripples on a pond.

Their society is aphony. It is not a collection of individuals, but a constant, shifting grand composition. Chorals whose Songs are naturally harmonious will form a "Chord," a close-knit group that resonates together for mutual support and complex, multi-part creation. A conversation between two Chorals is a breathtaking thing: they do not speak, but interweave their Songs. Their auras shimmer and mix, creating a temporary, fugue-like state where thoughts, memories, and pure concepts are exchanged as musical themes. An argument is a controlled dissonance, a battle of resolving and non-resolving chords. A lesson is the simple act of one Choral deliberately slowing and repeating a theme of its Song for a younger one to emulate and learn. Love, or its equivalent, is a perfect and sustained consonance between two Songs, a state of merged resonance that can last for entire cycles of the world's performance.

They are the priests and the technicians of this world. The ancient Resonance Tuners are not just their environment; they are their instrument. The Choral do not worship the great sound; they curate it. They are the tuners of reality.

You might see one at its work. A Choral will drift slowly towards a grove of flora that is beginning to vibrate at a dangerous, fracturing frequency. It will extend its Song, weaving a careful, damping melody into the flora's resonance. It does not touch; it *sings*. The discordant energy is absorbed, harmonized, and redistributed, and the grove is saved. They prune wild Motile Harmonies by projecting a wall of sound that deflects their predatory patterns. They nurture new growth by composing specific chords and directing them towards promising cracks in the slag floor, encouraging the blossoming of a new crystal tree. In essence, their entire life is an act of composition, a constant, unending performance to maintain the delicate, dynamic equilibrium of their world. Their art is ecology; their ecology is art.

Their lifecycle is a musical process. A mature Choral, within a stable Chord, will begin a "Coda," a complex internal process where a section of their Core's song is patterned into a new, tiny seed-crystal. This seed is then "planted" in a resonant nursery, a place on the slag floor where the foundational hum is particularly pure and stable. The young Choral—little more than a pure, unformed tone—is then "taught" by its Chord, who surround it and sing to it, imbuing the nascent Song with knowledge, history, and identity. It is a slow, generational apprenticeship in harmony.

Death is not an ending, but a decrescendo. When a Core's lattice finally succumbs to aeons of vibration, its song begins to lose its complexity. Its melody frays, its harmonics flatten. The Choral drifts to a quiet place and its Song finally unwinds. The Core then shatters, not with a sound, but with a sudden release of all the stored frequency and information it ever held—a single, final, glorious chord that is a summation of its entire life. This chord is absorbed by the slag floor and the flora, its memories becoming part of the world's data, its energy nourishing the crystal forests. Nothing is lost. The only silence is the silence of a finished song.

They live inside the music. They are the composed consciousness of a singing world. They are not a people who live in a place; they are the soul of the place itself, made manifest in melody and verse.
