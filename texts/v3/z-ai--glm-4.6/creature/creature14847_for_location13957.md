To survive in the High Loom is to endure a paradox: the environment is built for immensity, yet survival belongs to the small, the扁平, and the parasitic. There are no grazing herds here; there is no grass to graze on, only hardened heartwood and reed-matter tougher than iron. Instead, the ecosystem is populated by beings that treat this colossal vertical city as a singular, hostile organism to be harvested, burrowed into, or mimicked.

**The Silicate Mites**

On the vast, empty plains of spongy cortex between the Megastems, the primary inhabitants are the Silicate Mites. They are not insects in the earthly sense, but hexapodal clusters of chitin and mineral deposit, no larger than a dinner plate. They move with a frantic, jerky energy, vibrating constantly.

Because the ground "breathes" and emits heat, the mites cannot remain stationary for long or they will be cooked. They are nomadic scavengers. Their existence is a race against the rapid renovation of the landscape. When a Megastem collapses and the "ruin" begins to steam, the mites arrive in swarms. They use powerful, hydraulic jaws to snap through the slurry of wet pulp, consuming the calcified helices and the nutritious, sugary sap before the new moss seals the wound.

They rely not on sight, but on a complex array of pressure sensors along their underbellies. They can feel the "groan" of the planet—the expansion of wood and the rush of water in the deep pipes—minutes before it becomes audible. They are the cleanup crew of a catastrophe that happens every hour.

**The Cord-Stalkers**

Spanning the acres of empty air between the towers are the steel-cable vines. These are the highways of the High Loom, inhabited by the Cord-Stalkers.

These are elongated, arboreal creatures that resemble a cross between a squirrel and a grappling hook. Their limbs are disproportionately long, equipped with hooked claws made of bone-dense keratin. They do not run; they *swing*. However, because the vines are under immense tension and vibrate with a low hum, the Cord-Stalkers have evolved a unique physiology: their bones are hollow, filled with a gas lighter than the thick atmosphere, allowing them to be buoyant.

Their skin is translucent and mirror-slick, reflecting the blinding UV chartreuse of the environment to keep cool. They are solitary hunters. They feed on the "sun-tracking flowers," clipping the unseeing lenses of the giant blossoms with surgical precision to drink the fluids inside. They move in absolute silence, their motion perfectly in sync with the humming of the vines, rendering them invisible to the few predators that might watch for movement.

**The Aquifer-Ghosts**

The roar of the water is the heartbeat of the High Loom, and inside the massive, subway-tunnel-sized conduits live the Aquifer-Ghosts.

These are massive, pale, worm-like leviathans, blind and eyeless, that have evolved to live entirely within the pressurized dark of the water pipes. They can grow to the length of a train car, absorbing nutrients directly from the mineral-rich slurry rushing up from the deep aquifers.

Their skin is not slimy, but sandpaper-rough, designed to grip the sheer walls of the vertical pipes and anchor them against the terrifying current. They are essentially living corks. When they die, their bodies block the pipes, causing the pressure to build until the pipe bursts, flooding a section of the platform with hot, life-giving water.

The local "animals"—specifically the Cord-Stalkers—worship these bursts. To them, a burst pipe is a rain event, a sudden deluge in a thirsty world. They will climb down from the towers to lick the damp walls where the Aquifer-Ghost has passed.

**The Geometric Angler**

Perhaps the strangest inhabitant is one that mimics the architecture itself. It is known as the Geometric Angler, a predator that exploits the rigid, right-angled nature of the Loom.

It is a flat, perfectly triangular creature that adheres to the vertical sides of the Megastems. Its skin perfectly matches the texture of the bark—smooth, segmented, concrete-like. It has no limbs, only a muscular foot that allows it to slide along the surface like a amoeba made of stone.

It waits, perfectly still, for hours or days, looking for all the world like a decorative vent or a natural growth flaw in the tower. When a Cord-Stalker leaps from a vine, seeking purchase on the tower's surface, the Angler reveals its nature. The "stone" splits open, a vertical iris of teeth snapping shut with the sound of a cracking branch. It does not chew; it dissolves its prey internally, the waste excreted as a hard, white pellet that looks exactly like the natural calcification of the tree.

**The Spore-Drifters**

Finally, there is the sky itself. The air is thick with spores that glitter like diamond dust. Living within this cloud are the Spore-Drifters.

They are not single entities, but colonies. They resemble massive, floating jellyfish, their bellies filled with hydrogen generated from digesting the iron-rich air. Their tentacles are miles long, trailing nets of sticky mucus that catch the glittering spores.

They are the only beings that seem immune to the sense of altitude. They drift above the tallest platforms, touching the clouds, communicating with one another through pulses of bioluminescence that are lost in the blinding white sun. They are the ghosts of the Loom, drifting in the silence that exists above the roar of the pipes, waiting for the heat to die down so they can descend and mate in the humid, steaming ruins of the fallen towers.
