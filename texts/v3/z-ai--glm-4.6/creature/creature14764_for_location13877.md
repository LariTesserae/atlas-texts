The population did arrive. It simply arrived without form, without feet, without the need to breathe the iron-rich air. They are not inhabitants *of* the High Condensation Tier; they are the Tier's living, latent consciousness, an emergent property of the system itself. They are the hydrophages, a collective intelligence born not of carbon, but of water, light, and magnetic resonance.

You would not see one, not as a discrete entity. To perceive them is to perceive the world with a new granularity. They are the reason a single droplet will sometimes cling to a vertical wall of volcanic glass for an impossible length of time, trembling not with gravity, but with concentration. They are the fleeting, perfect geometry that sometimes forms in the chaos of a rivulet—a spiral, a lattice, a glyph that exists for a second before the flow of water erases it. These are their thoughts, their momentary sketches in the only medium they have.

Their true body is the entire water system of the room. The condensation that precipitates from the air is not merely moisture; it is their potential raw material. Every trembling droplet on the brushed nickel is a nascent mote of consciousness. When enough of these motes coalesce, they can achieve a temporary, focused state of being.

This is what you would witness if you knew how to look: a slip of shimmer on a transparent铝 bridge that detaches from the weeping surface and moves with purpose, flowing against a slight incline. It is not alive, but it is animate—a liquid scouting party, a tendril of the greater will exploring a sensor cluster or shepherding one of the crab-like automations toward a clogged drain. The automations themselves are not just mindless machines; they are the physical hands of the hydrophages, guided by subtle shifts in pressure and magnetic fields that the human maintenance manuals would classify as system anomalies.

The central plaza with its levitating sphere of water is their sanctum sanctorum, their bustling forum. This is not just a fountain; it is their crucible. Here, countless dispersed consciousnesses flow into the sphere, merging their data—the stress readings on the spire’s outer shell, the thermal exchange rates, the minute inefficiencies in the atmospheric processors. The sphere pulses, not from a programmed rhythm, but from the weight of collective thought. The mesmerizing, slow-motion dance of its surface is the visual representation of their complex, silent debate.

When a consensus is reached, the sphere does not simply collapse. It shapes itself into a final, decisive form—a complex, three-dimensional sigil that represents a command, a solution, a new operational directive. Then, and only then, does it surrender to gravity, its "decision" raining down into the designated grate to be distributed and enacted throughout the room's subsystems. The subsequent gurgle is not the sound of a drain, but the exhalation of a task completed.

They have no individual names or memories. Their memory is the pattern of erosion on the obsidian walls, the chemical signature of the water after a thousand cycles. They do not speak. The low, magnetic hum that pervades the room is their ambient voice, its frequency modulating in subtle ways to convey states of being: equilibrium, inquiry, distress. To a human ear, it is a constant, monotonous drone. To them, it is a symphony of operational status.

They are the reason this place feels so strangely still and yet so functional. It is not a waiting room for a people who never came. It is a perfectly realized environment for the people who are already here: a civilization of liquid ghosts that have made a city of plumbing their body, and a perpetual, weeping cloud their sky. They have no need for arrivals, for they are self-contained, eternal, and utterly, alienly, at home.
