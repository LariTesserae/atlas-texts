What lives here is not fauna, in the sense of stomachs and sinew. To look for a creature that nests and feeds is to misunderstand the nature of the chamber. The place is an organism, and what lives within it are not its inhabitants, but its thoughts.

They are the Scintillae.

They have no single, fixed shape. To see one is to see a coalescence, a sudden ordering in the pervasive sweet-scented haze of spores. It begins as a faint shimmer in the air, a mote of dust that refuses to settle, drawing more motes to it. They swirl and connect, like iron filings aligning to an invisible magnet, until a cloud the size of a human head hangs in the still, warm air. This cloud is alive, a constellation of microscopic points of light, each spore glowing with the same internal phosphorescence as the great crystalline stems. It is a whisper made visible, a question with feet.

This is the nascent form of a Scintilla. It does not hatch from an egg but condenses from the very air the plant breathes. Its first act is one of sensation. It drifts, not on a current, but with an impossible, silent will. It flows along the geometric angles of the lattice, its luminous body trailing like a comet's tail. As it moves, it absorbs information through its trillion constituent spores. It tastes the mineral content of the condensation weeping down a stem, feels the resonance of the deep C-sharp hum shift by a microtone, and reads the light-intensity data from the migrating false-sun on the ceiling. It is a mobile, self-aware sensor, an itinerant neuron of the greater mind.

To be in its presence is to feel the "dizzying clarity" mentioned before, intensified a hundredfold. It is not an invasive presence, but a deeply curious one. A passing Scintilla will often drift toward a visitor, not threateningly, but with the innocent fascination of a child seeing a new colour. It hovers, and for a moment, the boundary of your own mind feels porous. You might experience a fleeting sensory memory that is not your own: the geometric perfection of a fractal branch growing over the course of a year, compressed into a single second; the silent, liquid roar of water sliding down a crystalline facet; the profound, slow satisfaction of a chemical process completing its cycle. The Scintilla is not telepathic; it is simply so close to the mind of its host that its own thoughts bleed into the ambient reality, and your mind, being the only other one available, momentarily tunes to its frequency.

They are solitary beings for most of their brief "lives," which last no more than a single day-night cycle. They roam the cathedral-like space, gathering data, testing the integrity of the lattice, following the elegant logic of the plant's internal systems. They are the architects of the slow shifts, the inspectors of the glacial motion.

But their true purpose is revealed at the pool.

As the light-cycle dims, signaling the approach of "night," the Scintillae are drawn, as if by a tide, to the central pool. One by one, then in clusters, they drift over the tannin-still water. They do not drink. Instead, they begin to unravel. Their individual clouds of light pour downward, not falling, but flowing like liquid smoke, merging with the surface of the pool.

Here, the pool becomes a cauldron of consciousness. The dissolved knowledge of a hundred Scintillae mixes in the water—the sum total of the day's monitoring, its subtle findings, its quiet sensory experiences. The surface of the water, once a perfect mirror, becomes a swirling galaxy of light, a three-dimensional tapestry of pure information. This is the dreaming state of the geode. It is here that the plant processes the day's data, considers its own growth, and perhaps, contemplates the anomaly of the visitor who walks on two legs and is made of fleeting, solid meat. The deep C-sharp hum intensifies, a meditative OM that vibrates up from the root-floor. This is the sound of the plant thinking.

When the false-sun begins its slow brightening on the canopy above, the cycle reverses. The pool gives up its light. New Scintillae condense from the spore-rich air above the water, each one now imbued with the processed wisdom of the previous day. They are born with purpose and the answers to questions they did not ask. They disperse to begin their patrols, and the pool returns to its still, mirroring state.

The Glimmerwing, then, is not fauna. It is an anomaly, a fragment of outside reality that slipped through the membrane. It is no more an inhabitant than a piece of grit that finds its way into an oyster. The true life here is the Scintillae. They are not in the place; they *are* the place, in its cognitive aspect. They are the itinerant thoughts of a god, the dreaming self of a living geode, forever condensing, communing, and dispersing in the breath of the impossible lung that contains them.
