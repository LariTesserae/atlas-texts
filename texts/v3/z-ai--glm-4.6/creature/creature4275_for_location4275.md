At first, you convince yourself that the plateau is empty. It is an easy mistake to make. The silence here is so total, the visual horizon so dominated by the geometry of the Spires, that the mind interprets the lack of birdsong or insect chatter as a lack of life. You stand in the grey-black dust, listening to the wet thud of your own pulse, and you assume you are the only dynamic thing in a static universe.

But then you blink. Or perhaps the light shifts slightly, striking a facet of a distant amethyst monolith at a new angle. And you realize that your solitude is an illusion. The air is not empty; it is thick with them.

**The Perceptual Ghosts**

The inhabitants of this place begin as visual static, a shimmering heat-haze that clings to the ankles of the great crystals. They are not biological. Even at a distance, you sense the absence of biology. There is no scurry, no panic, no rhythm of breathing or hunting. They drift in the violet twilight like dust motes in a sunbeam, but dust motes obey the laws of fluid dynamics; these entities obey only the logic of probability.

As you focus on one, the "shimmer" resolves poorly. The eye struggles to grip them. They possess a wavering, unstable outline, like a mirage above a hot road, but they do not fade. They possess a sort of negative density—they occupy space, but they seem to be comprised of the space itself knotting into a single point.

This is the first stage of their nature: they are hesitation made visible. Looking at them feels like looking at an optical illusion that refuses to resolve into a single image. The brain races to categorize them—is it a swarm of tiny insects? A flutter of translucent wings? A disturbance in the dust?—and fails.

**The Quantum Fugue**

When you move closer, the true strangeness of the locals asserts itself. They do not transition; they toggle.

One moment, a Glitch clinging to the face of a Spires resembles a fern made of cut glass, intricate and fractal, perfectly symmetrical. You turn your head for a fraction of a second to check your footing on the crunching glass-dust, and when you look back, the glass-fern is gone. In its exact location, hovering inches from the crystal face, is a shape resembling a polyhedral bird. The transition did not happen. There was no blur of motion, no unfolding of wings. The fern simply ceased to be, and the bird instantly was.

They exist in a state of superposition. They are a flock of geometric shapes, a cascade of hanging silver moss, and a cloud of impossible angles, all contained within a single locus of spacetime. Your observation forces them to collapse into a single reality, but they do it imperfectly, flickering between forms with the erratic stutter of a damaged film reel.

They are not shapeshifters. A shapeshifter flows and stretches. A Glitch *replaces* itself. It is a living cut-and-paste error in the editing software of reality. One frame they are a branching crystalline tree; the next frame, they are a hovering tetrahedron; the next, a ripple like a stone dropped in a pond, frozen in mid-air.

**The Non-Linear Travel**

Movement here is another affront to causality. The Glitches do not walk, crawl, or fly. They do not disturb the air as they pass.

You might watch a cluster of them—a gathering of indigo-hued, spindle-armed shapes that look vaguely like deep-sea anemones made of light—drifting near a Waystone. You look away to consult your compass, or perhaps just to blink away the dryness in your eyes. When you look back, the cluster is not three feet away; it is fifty yards away, near the next ridge.

They did not travel the distance. There is no track in the dust, no displacement of air. They did not accelerate or decelerate. They simply ceased to be at Point A and began to be at Point B. They move by resetting their coordinates in the quantum grid, hopping from one moment of existence to another, leaving the space between absolutely empty.

This makes observing them a deeply unsettling experience. You cannot track a trajectory. You cannot predict where they will be. The landscape is littered with them, appearing and disappearing with the silent, random logic of subatomic particles. The air flashes with their constant, silent teleportation—a kaleidoscope of geometries that vanish and rematerialize without a sound.

**The Ecology of Stasis**

They crowd the environment. Once you see one, you see hundreds. They cling to the northern faces of the spires where the "Lichen-Lace" grows, hovering over the fractal silver frost not to feed, but simply to occupy the same vector of space. They drift through the pillars of solid violet light, refracting the beams into impossible new colors that shouldn't exist in a standard spectrum.

They are a swarm, a hive mind of chaos, yet they are perfectly silent. They make no vocalizations. They have no need to communicate; they have no individual will to communicate. They are not alive in the sense that they struggle against entropy. They *are* entropy, given a harmless, shape-shifting form.

To call them animals is a mistake. They are more like weather phenomena that attained a flickering sentience, or complex mathematical equations that learned to visualize themselves. They are the population of a world that has finished its story. They are the spare punctuation marks, the scattered typos, and the unused font weights of a creation that has already been saved and closed.

In the shadow of the Spires, they wait. They do not wait for anything, for waiting implies a future. They simply exist in the eternal, crushing duration of the present. When you finally leave this place, crunching back over the glass dust, the last thing you will see is the air itself flickering, a thousand impossible shapes blurring in and out of reality, watching you go with eyes they do not possess.
