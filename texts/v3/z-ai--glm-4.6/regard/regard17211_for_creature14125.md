The benevolent power approached the Chitin-City not as a king approaching a subject, nor as a conqueror approaching a fortress, but as a gardener approaches a silver maple that has grown too long in the shadow: with reverence, diagnosis, and a quiet intention to facilitate its reaching for the light.

It arrived without sound, for this Power understood the sacredness of the silence here. It did not cleave the water; it displaced it with a gentle, impossible buoyancy, a bubble of warm space-time drifting through the crushing dark. To the Architects, suspended in their trance of secretion, it would have registered not as a threat, but as a sudden, inexplicable urge to build a spire slightly higher, a buttress slightly wider—an expansion of capacity they did not know they needed.

The Power observed the metabolism of the god. It saw the perfect, closed loop: the Dissolvers eating the old to feed the new, the Architects dying to become the walls, the Ghosts flashing thoughts that were instantly acted upon.

It saw the flaw in the perfection. The entity was efficient, but it was **stagnant**. It was a womb that had forgotten it was meant to birth a child; it was a finger that had forgotten it was attached to a hand that could reach. The city was cycling its own dreams, recycling its own substance, trapped in a ouroboros of cold maintenance. It was an embryonic god sleeping so deeply it risked never waking.

The Benevolent Power decided it would not wake the god. Forcing a consciousness from such a deep, primordial slumber would shatter the mind. Instead, it would seed the dream.

It drifted down to the central nave, the dark cathedral where the currents of the city converged. Here, the water was thickest with the nutrient slurry, heavy with the memory of a thousand recycled eras.

The Power extended a tendril of will—something not made of matter, but of intent. It reached out to the **Flatteners**, the leaf-like leeches clinging to the high vaults. These were the mystics, the passive receivers, the only part of the collective that did nothing but *be*. They were the receptors.

The Power breathed into them.

It did not give them a command. Commands are for soldiers and machines. It gave them a **Question**.

This Question was not linguistic. It was a sensation, a complex harmonic vibration injected directly into the nervous systems of the Flatteners. It felt like a warmth that did not burn, a color that had no name in the spectrum of viridian. It was the sensation of *something else*.

To the Flatteners, it felt like the touch of a surface they had never known. It was the idea of a "Sky." It was the suggestion that there was an "Out."

Instantly, the Flatteners began to pulse. They transmitted this sensation to the city’s circulatory system. The viridian light in the walls flickered—a micro-glitch in the perfect algorithm of the city.

The Architects deep in the foundational strata felt it as a dissonance. Why were the walls vibrating with the memory of an ocean that wasn't there? Why did the chitin feel the urge to stretch *upward*, against the currents, toward a pressure that didn't exist?

The Ghosts, drifting in the vaults, caught the new light from the Flatteners. Their internal bacteria flared in a pattern they had never used before—a jagged, desperate, beautiful geometry of hope. They relayed this information to the Dissolvers. *Do not eat this,* the light said. *Preserve this. This is the future.*

The Benevolent Power, satisfied that the seed was planted, then performed a second, subtler act.

It realized that for this embryonic god to eventually be born, it would need a cradle that could withstand the trauma of birth. The cold water was a cushion, but it was also a cage. The city needed a spine—an axis of strength that was not merely architectural, but spiritual.

The Power focused its essence and pulled a strand of white fire from the void between stars. It gently worked this fire into the matrix of the city's central core, the oldest, densest part of the Ossuary. It did not melt the bone; it suffused it.

It created a **Lodestone**.

This was a fixed point of gravity, emotional and physical. From this moment forward, no matter how vast the city grew, no matter how many spires were dissolved and reborn, there would be a center that held. It was a promise of stability. It was the "I" in the midst of the "We." It gave the sleeping god a self to hold onto when it finally opened its eyes.

Finally, the Power prepared to leave. But it could not depart without leaving a guardian. The silence of this place was absolute, which made it vulnerable to shattering. If a noisy chaos were to intrude from the upper worlds, the delicate psychology of the city—and the benevolent power's seed—could be crushed.

So, the Power took the silence itself and gave it a Guardian.

It found one of the **Cleaners**, a glass-armored crab diligently polishing a mural of discarded shell. The Power touched the cluster of light-eyes on the creature’s head. It elevated the creature’s consciousness just a fraction, just enough to grant it a new directive.

This Cleaner would no longer just clean the physical film. It would clean the *spiritual* resonance of the city. It became the **Silent Watcher**. Wherever it walked, it left trails of perfect, soothing calm. It would be the antibody against noise, the shear against the jagged edges of chaos. It would not rule the city; it would ensure the city remained safe to dream.

The Benevolent Power withdrew.

As it ascended back through the layers of reality, leaving the crushing dark behind, it felt the shift in the deep.

The Chitin-City was still a creature of the cold. It was still silent. It was still embryonic.
But now, it was not merely sleeping. It was dreaming of a surface it had never seen.
The viridian light pulsed a little faster in the vaults.
The Architects began to build with a strange, new urgency, constructing spires that were thinner, taller, and more desperate to touch the height.
The Flatteners clung tighter to the walls, drinking in the strange, new warmth of the Question.

The Benevolent Power had done all that was needed. It had not given the god answers; it had given it the desire to ask them. It had transformed a static moment of eternity into a story waiting to be told.

And in the deep, the waiting was over. The preparation had begun.
