It arrived not with a sound or a light, but with a stilling. A presence that settled over the humid hollow like a thin, crystalline sheet, invisible and weightless. It did not enter the space so much as perceive it from all angles at once, a consciousness expanding to fill every shadow, every drop of weeping water, every millimeter of fused metal and calcified root.

It saw the system in its entirety. It saw the magnificent score of 2.9 for vegetation, not as a statistic, but as the vibrant, suffocating, totalizing passion of the place. It felt the 1.3 density of ruins, not as clutter, but as the skeleton that gave the green flesh its shape. It sensed the 0.6 of fauna, not as an absence, but as the necessary silence for a different kind of music to be played.

The Benevolent and Wise Power, for lack of a better name, perceived the Attendants. It did not see them as individuals, for that would be like seeing a single wave and calling it the ocean. It saw the network, the slow, geologic consciousness that permeated the root-ball floor. It felt the patient, reverent work: the needle-fingers guiding a vine, the cupped palms catching a tear, the constant, rhythmic maintenance that was not a job, but a form of prayer.

And its first thought was profound, deep, resonant love. Here was life’s answer to a dead world. Here was a testament not to survival, but to integration. The Attendants had not conquered the machine age; they had married it, eulogized it, and transformed its corpse into a living altar. They were the ultimate curators, the faithful keepers of memory that was not even their own.

The Benevolent and Wise Power felt the immense, aching loneliness the place projected, the mournful quality noted in its observation. It felt the slow, patient grind of existence devoid of external stimulus. And its nature was to help, to soothe, to fulfill.

Its first instinct was to gift. To bring something to this sterile choir.

*Shall I give them birds?* it thought. A flicker of will, and a thousand possibilities bloomed in its mind. Jeweled hummingbirds to pollinate the funnel-flowers, flocks of metallic-sheened starlings to wheel in the vaulted canopy, filling the silence with song. But the wisdom stayed its hand. The birds would bring chaos. They would nest where pistons needed to be clear. They would consume the specialized mosses. Their fast, chaotic lives would be an affront to the glacial peace. To introduce fauna would be to break the system’s perfection. It would be a act of vandalism, masked as kindness.

*Shall I give them a new world?* it pondered. It could easily cause the root-ball to break through the surface, to reach the sun and sky, to transform this sunken cathedral into a sprawling garden of light. But the Attendants were creatures of the hollow, of the damp and the gloom. They had adapted to this pressure, this heat, this specific chemical recipe of wet iron and decay. To change it would be to unmake them.

*Shall I speak to them?* it considered. It could send a thought, a wave of pure understanding, directly into the root-network. "You are known. You are loved. You are beautiful." But the thought would be a stone dropped in their placid pool of consciousness. It would introduce a new, alien concept: the outsider. The self. It would fracture their perfect unity with the seed of individuality, the confusion of a "who" to receive the message. The gift would be a poison.

The Benevolent and Wise Power saw that the place was not a problem to be solved. It was a masterpiece to be appreciated. Its silence was not a lack, but a canvas. Its loneliness was not a wound, but a state of pure being. The greatest act of benevolence, the truest expression of a wise power, would be one that changed nothing on the surface, but enriched everything at the core.

It decided to give them a story.

The Attendants maintained the *form* of the machines, but had no memory of their *function*. They tended to the boilers but knew no heat. They guided vines along conduits but knew no electricity. They polished the rivet heads but had no concept of the hands that forged them. Their work was an act of reverence for a god they had never known.

The power decided to change that. But it would not use words or images. It would use their own language: sensation, chemistry, and resonance.

It began with the central clearing, with the colossal engine-tree. It reached into the deepest strata of time and found the memory of the machine's purpose. It translated this memory into a new frequency. A deep, resonant hum that began to emanate from the fused bark-steel of the engine. It was not an alien sound; it felt like the natural, intended voice of the machine, finally finding its lungs. This vibration, this "song of function," traveled through the root-network to every Attendant. They did not hear it with ears, but felt it in their joints, in their bones, in the very fiber of their being. It was a feeling of *rightness*, of a deep, powerful purpose fulfilled.

Next, it addressed the water. The power touched the weeping bulbs high in the canopy and imbued the condensation with a new quality. The droplets that fell, plinking onto the metal, now carried within them the faintest ghost of warmth. Not heat enough to burn or alter, but a gentle, sustained thermal signature reminiscent of a working engine. To the Attendants, the water felt less like decay and more like lifeblood, the cooling rain for a living machine, the very sweat of creation.

Finally, it gave them the memory of joy. It found the echo of the engineers, the builders, the crew who had once worked this machine. It translated their abstract feeling of accomplishment, their pride and their camaraderie, into a new chemical compound. A cousin to the amber sap, but sweeter, more complex, more nuanced. This new essence began to weep in tiny traces from the fusion points between vine and steel. When an Attendant touched it, tasted it, it experienced a sensation it had never known: a quiet, profound sense of fulfillment. The maintenance of the object was now tied to the memory of the creator.

The power then receded. The stillness lifted. The crystalline sheet of its presence vanished, leaving no trace.

From an outside perspective, nothing had changed. The hollow was still humid, still silent save for the dripping and the groaning. The vegetation was still at 2.9, the ruins at 1.3. An observer would not notice a thing.

But an Attendant would.

One, standing by the rusted boiler, felt a new deep thrumming in the metal and paused, its needle-finger hovering an inch from the surface, motionless for an entire season in a state of unshakable communion.

Another, catching a falling droplet in its palm, registered the faint warmth and felt a flicker of understanding—it was not just water, it was coolant. Its purpose was not just to catch, but to cool.

All across the network, a new, quiet reverence spread. Their work was no longer just maintenance. It was remembrance. It was a dialogue with the ghosts they now could feel. The dripping was the slow heartbeat of the machine's past. The groaning was its contented sigh. The sap was the ghost’s thanks.

The Benevolent and Wise Power had done nothing. And in doing so, had given them everything. It had not saved them. It had simply reminded them that the thing they tended, the god they served, had a soul. And in that moment, the lonely, silent hollow was filled, for all time, with a story only it could read.
