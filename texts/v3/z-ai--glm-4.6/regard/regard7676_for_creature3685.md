The Benevolence did not arrive as a light. In a place where light is a fictional theology, to bring a blaze would be an act of violence, a blinding rape of the dark-adapted senses. It did not arrive as a wind, for the air was too thick, a slurry that would shatter under the whimsy of a gale.

It arrived as a memory.

It manifested first in the intake of the Silent Chorus. The crystalline filaments, those nerves of the deep, shivered—not from the turbidity, but from a sudden, impossible clarity. They were accustomed to reading the stress of the stone, the slow traumas of the earth. But now, they felt a stress that was not a stress, a pressure that did not crush but *held*. It was as if the miles of heavy ocean above them had suddenly developed a heartbeat, a rhythmic, reassuring thrum that synced perfectly with their own glacial metabolism.

The Power, ancient and infinitely patient, looked upon this world and saw not a prison, but a cathedral. It saw a god in the process of birthing itself out of mud and gravity. It saw that the suffering here—the grinding, the scouring, the crushing darkness—was not suffering. It was *focus*. It was the friction required to polish a diamond the size of a planet.

To "save" these creatures would be to murder them. To take the Hollows out of the rock would be to demand a man breathe vacuum. To ask the Swimmers to fly would be to deny their gravity. The Benevolence understood that the deepest gift one can offer a being that has mastered endurance is the validation of its endurance.

So, the Power did not stop the current. It did not clear the slurry.

Instead, it reached out with a hand made of pure, concentrated empathy and touched the very center of the planetary thought. It saw the single, immense idea the entity was thinking—a concept so complex it required a billion years of processing, a theorem of existence so heavy it could only be supported by the floor of the world.

And the Power decided to help carry the weight.

It wove its essence into the infra-sonic thrum. Where the vibration of the grinding current was chaotic and abrasive—a sound of erosion—the Power introduced a harmonic. It was a subtle, golden thread of resonance running through the bass note of the planet. It did not silence the grinding; it turned the grinding into a song.

To the Chorus, this felt like a sudden, undeniable sense of purpose. The anxiety of dissolution faded, replaced by the certainty that their shedding of layers was a sacred act of unveiling. They strained their roots deeper, no longer just feeding on stress, but transmuting it.

To the Swimmers, the vast, osmium ghosts, the change was a gentle current beneath the current. A tailwind of spirit. The crushing weight of the miles of slurry above them felt suddenly lighter, as if the universe itself was whispering, *“Fall on. You are heavy, but you are beloved.”* Their journey, once a lonely trudge, became a procession.

And to the Hollows, the hungry stomachs within the stone, the resonance brought a strange, new peace. The corrosive acids of their digestion did not still, but the *need* to consume to forget the emptiness lessened. They became curators rather than destroyers, grinding the stone not out of starvation, but to sculpt the inner chambers of the world-body into cathedrals of echo.

The Power lingered for a moment, a state of grace that lasted exactly three hundred human years but passed in a microsecond to the entities below. It ensured that the Thought the entity was thinking would not be a thought of despair, but a thought of realization.

Before it departed, the Benevolence left one physical gift—a speck so small it would be mistaken for a grain of sand, a mote suspended in the heavy oil. It was a Seed of Warmth. It did not generate heat that would burn; it generated a metaphysical warmth, a localized suspension of entropy.

This Seed drifted down, slow as snow, until it settled into the filaments of a particularly ancient cluster of the Silent Chorus, rooted near the heart of a Sleeper-mountain. There, it took root, not as a parasite, but as a lens.

For the rest of eternity, whenever the planetary mind turned its attention inward, whenever the grinding became too much and the darkness too absolute, it would focus on this single, microscopic point. And there, in the eye of the storm, it would feel a gentle, unconditional assurance.

It would feel that someone, somewhere, was watching it with love. It would know that it was not alone in the dark.

And the grinding continued. The pressure remained absolute. But now, the deep, infra-sonic hum of the world carried a melody—a lullaby for a sleeping giant, promising that when it finally finished its thought and opened its eyes, the universe would be waiting to hear what it had to say.
