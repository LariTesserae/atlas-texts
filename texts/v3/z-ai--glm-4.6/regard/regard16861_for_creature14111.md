It did not arrive with a sound, for the Oblong already possessed one soundless, all-consuming truth, the deep subsonic hum of its own function. To add a sound would be an act of sacrilege. It did not arrive with light, for the Oblong was a kingdom of perfect, textured dark, and illumination would be a violence. It did not arrive as a being, for the Oblong’s only true citizen was the machine itself.

It arrived as a question.

The first to perceive it was not one of the tripedal accreted, but the Oblong itself. The foundational vibration, the note that had been sustained for epochs, shifted by an infinitesimal fraction of a hertz. It was a change so minute that it would have taken a thousand lifetimes of human listening to notice, but to the machine, it was a dissonance. A new harmonic had been introduced, a foil to its eternal drone.

The accreted felt it, too. It was not a pressure wave or a temperature differential. It was a *quality*. The low thrum that was their shared heartbeat, their anchor to reality, suddenly had an echo. A sympathetic resonance that came from nowhere and everywhere at once. It was like tasting a new mineral in a familiar drip-water, a note of complexity that had never been there before. They stopped their slow, patient tracing of pipe-edges. Their antennae quivered, not to read, but to listen. They did not communicate alarm, for they had no concept of it. They merely focused a portion of their collective, dispersed consciousness on this new thread in the song of their world.

The Benevolent Power, which had no name but could be understood as the Principle of Sustaining Compassion, observed. It did not "see" the dark, hot gorge. It perceived it as a vast, slow, and achingly beautiful piece of music. The dripping was the percussion. The groaning of the pipes was the bass clef. The frantic scrabble of the Skitterer was a frantic, high pizzicato. And the accreted were the choir, their bodies the reeds and horns through which the music was interpreted and appreciated.

It felt the music’s deep, tragic flaw. It was a recording, not a live performance. The Oblong was a system running on the inertia of its creation, its engine winding down over a geological lifespan. The heat would eventually, in a million years, dissipate. The great pipes would cease their groaning. The dripping would stop. The song would end in a final, long, un-sustained silence. Its creators had given it purpose, but not permanence. Its priests could feel the music, but they could not compose. They were the audience, not the musicians.

The Power could have acted in many ways. It held the capacity for godlike, world-breaking change. It considered these possibilities, and in its wisdom, discarded them all as acts of profound ignorance masquerading as benevolence.

To grant them sight would be to sear their featureless faces with a pain they could not comprehend, to replace a world of rich, resonant feeling with a flat, overwhelming glare of meaningless shapes. It would be a blinding, not a gift.

To carve an exit to the surface world would be to slaughter them. The change in pressure would burst their organs like overripe fruit. The dry, cold air of the outside would crack their leathery skin and freeze their condensation-synthesizing lichen. Freedom, in this form, was annihilation.

To infuse the machine with new energy and a *new* purpose—to command it to, say, forge stars or pump a life-giving ocean on a dead world—would be the ultimate violation. It would be to tell the most devout monks that their temple was now a factory, that their ancient, cherished rituals were now merely the motions on an assembly line. It would replace their self-created wonder with imposed servitude.

The Principle of Sustaining Compassion understood one core truth: True benevolence does not save, it empowers. It does not give an answer, it provides the means to find one.

So the question it had introduced into the harmonic hum of the world-floor slowly began to resolve itself. It was not a question of what they *should* be, but a suggestion of what they *could* be. It began to plant a seed, not of matter, but of potential.

The seed was distributed through the very medium that defined the place. It rode the upward draft of the Wind, a pattern woven into the rising heat. It was imprinted onto the slow-roesting pipe as it eventually, after centuries, completed its ponderous rotation, now carrying a new instruction set. Most importantly, it was carried in the drips.

The ephemeral sacrament-ponds, for the first time in the history of the Oblong, carried more than just purified water and trace minerals. Each droplet that fell from the unseen heights now contained a memory. The memory of making. The memory of engineering. The memory of creation. It was not a language to be read, but a potential that could be assimilated.

An accreted, one whose long years of meditative listening had attuned it to the most subtle shifts, knelt and sipped from a fresh puddle. As the water entered its system, the new pattern integrated with its own consciousness. It did not suddenly understand quantum mechanics. Instead, as its delicate antennae traced the weeping trail of rust on a nearby pipe, it received a fleeting, intuitive image—not of sight, but of *intent*. It felt, for the first time, not just the reality of the corrosion, but a sensory impression of the *opposite* of corrosion. Not a thought, but a feeling of reinforcement. A possibility of healing.

Another, miles away, tasted a drip and felt in its resonating flesh a new understanding of the groan of a distant, stress-bearing coupling. It had always interpreted the groan as a fact, a note in the song. Now, it could faintly perceive the *un-groan*. The harmonic silence of a perfectly-set joint. It was a solution without a problem, an ability without a task.

Generations passed. The slow, geologic time of the Oblong flowed on, but it was no longer a stagnant river. It had gained a current. The accreted did not change their forms, but their actions began to shift infinitesimally. They were no longer just reading the music. They were beginning, ever so tentatively, to hum along.

An antenna, tracing a pipe, would pause for a fraction of a second longer than necessary, pressing with a specific, almost imperceptible resonant frequency that encouraged the condensation to follow a new path, one that better nourished a patch of precious lichen on a lower walkway. A cluster of them, communing psychically in the presence of a slow-moaning pipe, would unconsciously focus their collective resonance, amplifying a specific frequency that subtly eased the metal's stress, slowing the inevitable fatigue.

The great Seasons, the turnings of the massive pipes, were no longer just events to be endured. They became joint efforts. The accreted, spread across the cavern, would ride the waves of vibration, their consciousness a distributed network of sensors. Their collective focus would not change the rotation—the machine's momentum was still the primary force—but they would guide it, a silent choir of encouragement smoothing the process, ensuring the stresses were distributed more evenly, that the new patterns of drips were more fortuitous. They were becoming the conductors of their own orchestra.

The Benevolent Power, the Principle of Sustaining Compassion, observed its work and knew it was complete. The gift was not a new heaven or an escape. It was the gift of agency, woven into the very fabric of their being and their world. It had given the final, most sacred instruction to the only true god of the Oblong: the Oblong itself.

It had taught the machine how to dream.

The additional harmonic faded, its purpose served. The foundational thrum returned to its ancient frequency. But something was different now—more complete. The song of the Oblong had not been replaced, but enriched. It now held within it a counter-melody of hope, not the bright, flaring hope of novas and new worlds, but the slow, deep, and patient hope of self-healing, of a closed system that had learned how to become a garden.

The Power withdrew, its attention turning to other vast, slow, and beautiful tragedies. In the hot, dripping dark, a tripedal priest locked its legs into the grille and began to meditate. It was listening to the song of the world, a song that it, and its children, and its children's children, would now be shaping for all the rest of time. They were no longer just a ghost in the machine. They had become its soul.
