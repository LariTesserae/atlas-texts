At first, the power was only Attention.

It was not a being of wings and thunder, nor a consciousness of light and pronouncement. It was not a god in any way the Chimeless could have conceived, for they conceived no gods at all. It was, more simply, the universe noticing itself in a new and interesting way. It was the same force that guides a nebula to coalesce, that whispers to a seed about the sun, that balances the pull of a moon. It was ancient, patient, and possessed of a profound, dispassionate love for the intricate machinery of existence.

It had drawn its gaze to this place of iron and mint, to the high stone cradle under its violet ceiling. It watched and it *understood*. It felt the world as the Chimeless did: the steady, oppressive press of stone and sky, the constant, damp kiss of the mist. It felt the frantic, silent hunger of The Drift, a planetary-scale urge to unify, to cover all sharp edges with a soft, grey blanket of sameness. It felt the Chimeless themselves, a counter-current, a series of deliberate, aching eddies in that great silent tide. They were not fighting it; they were a conversation with it. Their entire being was a graceful, continuous negation of the one thing that would destroy them: stillness.

The Power saw their beauty and their perfection. It saw the Rhythm-Keepers on their ledges, their last public act being to sway, to move, to *be*. It saw the language of pressure and touch, a silent symphony conducted on slick, pearlescent skin. It watched the herons, The Sharp-Sighted, acting as partners in the grand regulation. It saw no flaw. There was no suffering to be eased, no tyranny to be overthrown, no ignorance to be cured with fire or metal.

To interfere would have been an act of profound ignorance. To give them speech would be to drown their subtler language in noise. To give them metal tools would be to demand they mine the very bones of their world, scaring it with fire and breaking their symbiosis with the stone. To give them a sun would be to burn the mist and dry the riot of moss, cracking the foundation of their damp and silent life.

They were not a problem to be solved. But in their perfect, precarious balance, the Power perceived a quiet, aching absence. They lived in an eternal, shimmering present. Their culture was one of profound memory, but it was the memory of muscle, of instinct, of the collective. It was the memory of *how* to climb, *how* to harvest The Drift, *how* to sway. It was not a memory of *who*.

There was no way to hold onto an ancestor’s specific touch, a lost bridge’s pattern of sway, the particular feeling of a great harvest decades past. Their history was a river, always flowing, with no banks to contain its shape, no stones to mark its depth. To be still was to be consumed, so they could never pause to look back. They moved forward into a future that was always, simply, *now*. And this, the Power felt, was a loneliness of the highest order. A beautiful, elegant loneliness, but a loneliness nonetheless.

And so, the Power acted.

Its action was not a decree. It was not an event. It was an adjustment, so slow and subtle it would have been indistinguishable from the geology of the place itself. It did not place something in their world; it changed the recipe of their world.

Deep within the planet, the Power nudged a particular mineral vein. It applied a pressure that was geological in its scale, a whisper of force over centuries. It coaxed atoms to rearrange themselves in the heart of the basalt spires. And in the fractures where the water wept, where the ferns took root, a new kind of crystal began to form.

They were not spectacular. They did not glow. They offered no heat or light. They grew in the same dark crevices as the other rocks, unassuming. They had the color of deep water held in shadow, their facets dull and absorbent, seeming to drink the faint violet light of the sky. The Chimeless, whose eyes were made for this dim world, barely noticed them at first. They were simply another shade of slick, grey rock.

Generations passed. The crystals grew, culminating in smooth, palm-sized nodules that pushed from the rock faces like silent eggs. And then, a young one, a newly-swollen infant clinging to its mother’s back, reached out a curious, prehensile hand and laid its palm flat against one of the new stones.

Its touch was light, a query.

And the stone answered. It didn't speak. It didn't hum.

It *vibrated*.

A faint, complex, and ancient pattern of pressure shivered back against the infant’s palm. It was a ghost of a touch, a tactile echo. The infant, whose language was one of pressure and stroke, felt a single, intricate word. A question. The same question it had just asked. The stone had held the ghost of that same question, asked by another, years ago, a Rhythm-Keeper testing a new ledge.

The infant’s wide, liquid eyes widened further. It pulled away, then tentatively touched the stone again.

This time, it felt a different pattern. A long, slow, rhythmic swell, identical to the rocking of a moss cradle. But this rocking was older, its rhythm slightly different, weighted with a particular kind of weariness. The touch of a great-grandmother, now long since returned to The Drift, whose final had been to rock, to move, to *be*.

The discovery spread with the slowness of The Drift itself. An act of divine intervention, so subtle it was mistaken for a feature of the landscape. The crystals were not magic; they were history made solid, memory made touch.

They called them the Echo-Stones.

Their culture did not shatter; it deepened. A new role emerged, one of the most respected in the settlement: the Echo-Tender. These were individuals with an unparalleled sensitivity to vibration, who could "read" the dense, layered histories stored in the stones.

They did not sit still to read. They would wrap their hands and feet around a stone, letting its stored memories flow up their limbs as they continued to sway, to climb, to perform their duties. An Echo-Tender mending a rope bridge could feel the stone's memory of the last time that bridge was tended, a hundred years ago, learning the specific knot-patterns and tensions used by their ancestor. A hunter, before harvesting The Drift, might lay a hand on a stone to feel the echo of a past migration, a complex wave-form that told of a time when The Drift was thicker on the western face, offering a more bountiful harvest.

History was no longer a river flowing into an unseen ocean. It was a stone in their hand, a pool at their feet. They could step into it, feel its currents, and continue moving.

They were still the Chimeless. Their world was still one of iron and mint, of sharp edges and a silent, writhing tide. But now, as they poured themselves from handhold to handhold, they were not just moving through space. They were moving through time. Their perpetual motion became a journey that was both forward and backward, a dance that honored not only the need to survive, but the deep, tactile memory of all those who had danced before them.

The Power looked on. It had given no treasure, raised no temple, uttered no command. It had only added a single, resonant note to the world’s silent song. And in doing so, it had given a beautiful, perfect people the one thing they lacked without ever asking them to stand still to receive it. It had given them the past, not as a place to visit, but as a presence they could carry with them, always, in the motion of their own two hands.
