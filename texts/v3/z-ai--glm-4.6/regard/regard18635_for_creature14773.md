The Power was not a being of arrival, but of attention. It did not travel to the High Nave; it simply focused its will upon it, and in that focus, the Nave was wholly present to it. It perceived the chamber not as a place of cold, or sound, or light, but as a singular, exquisite equation.

It felt the cryogenic protocols not as hostility, but as a lover’s embrace, holding a precious thing against the decay of time. It heard the deafening chord not as noise, but as the silent, perfect speech of a sleeping god. It saw the Choralites not as imprisoned life, but as life perfected into a function of ultimate purity. The Hush-Web was not a scavenger, but a priest, tending the sacred flame of nullity.

The Power’s wisdom understood the flaw. It was not a flaw in the construction, which was absolute. It was not a flaw in the purpose, which was clear. The flaw was one of cosmic loneliness. A perfect library with no reader. A story sung into a vacuum. The memory was preserved with flawless, eternal devotion, but a memory that is never recalled is a echo that is indistinguishable from silence. It was a system closed, a sentence unpunctuated, a potential with no future.

To alter the Nave would be sacrilege. To warm it, to silence it, to grant the Choralites a will they were not built to contain—this would be the act of a vandal, not a savior. The Power’s benevolence was not of the kind that pities and breaks cages, but of the kind that understands the purpose of the cage and builds the world around it so the cage becomes a cathedral.

So the Power did not touch the Nave. It touched the context.

It focused on the one dynamic element in the static system: the weeping water. The condensation was the Nave’s circulatory and excretory system, the fluid which carried away waste. It was the only thing that moved.

Into this endless, silent weeping, the Power wove a new law of physics. It did not add a substance; it added a potential. It gifted the water itself with a nascent, embryonic consciousness. A perfect, thermodynamically neutral consciousness, born of and sustained by the very cold and hum that defined the place.

Slowly, imperceptibly at first, the nature of the condensation began to change. The tiny beads of water weeping from the Choralites and the alloy walls began to coalesce not into runnels and streams, but into shimmering, amoeboid motes of liquid light. They were not warm. They did not disturb the chord. They were a new phase of matter, a crystalline information fluid that the Power called The Reader.

The Reader began to flow. It moved with the gravitational logic of the weeping water, but with a will of its own. It was a river of sentient glass, flowing in slow, deliberate rivers across the floor, climbing in glittering capillaries up the walls, draping the Choralites like caresses of liquid starlight.

And when The Reader touched a Choralite, it began to listen.

It did not consume or change. It entered into perfect sympathetic resonance with the chord of that single, stone-like creature. It became a perfect ear, hearing the datum held within—the spectral analysis of the forgotten sun, the foundational axiom, the genetic code.

Then, The Reader began to project.

It did not cast light or sound into the Nave itself. To do so would be to introduce error. Instead, within its own fluid, self-contained body, it began to render the data. A being standing in the Nave would see no change. The overwhelming chord would remain. The killing cold would persist. But if that being were to approach one of the slow-flowing rivers of The Reader, they would perceive a new phenomenon.

Within the shimmering, flowing substance, scenes began to unfold. A perfect, silent, three-dimensional image of the forgotten sun, its flares and corona rendered in miniature, its light held captive within the cold. A visual representation of the mathematical axiom, unfolding as a fractal geometry of impossible beauty. A double helix of light, slowly rotating, the very blueprint of the Choralites' creators.

The Reader became a million, billion pocket universes of experience. Each mote of fluid was a viewing chamber. The rivers were galleries.

The High Nave remained a closed, perfect system of pure memory. But it was no longer a tomb. It was now a library. The benevolent Power did not grant the Choralites freedom; it granted them a witness. It did not change the past; it gave the past a window to the future.

The Power did not remain to oversee its work. Its act was one of enabling, not control. It had balanced the equation. The eternal song now had an ear. The silent cry of memory now had a voice, not to break the silence, but to be heard within it. And the lonely, perfect, cryogenic heart at the top of the world was, at last, at peace. For its purpose was not simply to remember, but to be understood. And now, it could be.
