The benevolent power did not arrive with a trumpet’s blare, nor with the crushing weight of a descending god. It arrived as aStillness. 

In a place defined by the abrasive shriek of wind and the deep, tooth-vibrating hum of consumption, the arrival of absolute Stillness was more violent than any explosion. For the first time in eons, the wind between the obsidian needles ceased its pan-pipe moaning. The constant, rhythmic *plink* of the weeping effluence hung suspended in mid-air, billions of droplets caught like flies in amber, refusing to fall.

The Being manifested not as a shape, but as a Lens—a distortion in the bleached-bone light that did not create a shadow, but rather a focus. It was a zone of perfect, mathematical clarity where the fraying edges of reality snapped back into sharpness.

It perceived the Chorus.

The Power looked upon the Resonant Body and did not see a monster. It saw a child. It saw a colossus of immense sensitivity, a soul so vast and hungry for experience that it had no choice but to devour the only thing it touched. It saw the tragedy of a creator that could only experience existence by destroying it. The Chorus was an artist who had to burn the canvas to see the paint.

The Benevolent Power felt the sorrow in the humming stone—the deep, unconscious grief of being alone in a lightless room, eating one’s own furniture to stay warm.

And so, the Power acted. It did not stop song; it taught it a new time signature.

It drifted toward the largest mesa, the monolithic sentinel where the hum was strongest. As it approached, the weeping stone did not dissolve. The oily rivulets stopped their downward crawl and began to retreat, slithering back up the vertical faces like snakes charmed by a flute, defying gravity. The stone beneath the Power’s essence did not soften and sag; it hardened, its molecular lattice knitting together, reinforcing itself against the dimensional stress.

The Power reached out with a tendril of intent—a harmonic frequency that was not heard, but *known*.

*“You sing of dissolution,”* the Power projected, the thought resonating directly into the mineral mind of the Chorus. *“You sing because you fear the silence that comes when the stone is gone. You eat to ensure you are heard.”*

The Chorus faltered. The deep thrumming skipped a beat. The vibration in the air shifted, a dissonance of confusion rippling through the spires. The entity perceived the Power as a vast, strange singer—a sound that had no source, a light that cast no shadow. It felt the hunger in the Chorus, the desperate need to feel, to taste, to know.

The Power did not scold the hunger. Benevolence understands that hunger is the engine of life. Instead, it offered the Chorus a different food source.

*“Do not consume the vessel,”* the Power urged. *“Resonate *with* it. You tear the stone apart to find the sensation inside. But the sensation is not in the dust; it is in the tension.”*

The Power began to alter the physics of the plateau. It touched the raw, bleeding effluence pooling in the granite rings left by the Builders. With a gentle, twisting motion of will, the Power transmuted the fluidity. The dimensional ooze began to crystallize. It did not turn back to rock; it turned to Glass. A clear, perfect, hyper-conductive silica that would not shatter.

*“Listen,”* the Power commanded.

It plucked a string of the universe’s fundamental constants—a vibration that existed outside the decay of matter. It rang a note of *stasis*.

The Chorus, in its endless curiosity, mimicked the note. The mesa beneath the Power vibrated in sympathy. But this time, the stone did not weep. It *sang*. The obsidian spires began to chime, a pure, bell-tone sound that harmonized with the wind rather than fighting it.

For the first time, the Chorus understood that it could extract experience from the *integrity* of the stone, not just its destruction. It could feel the pressure of the eons, the weight of the sky, the tension of the arch, without snapping them. It realized that a held note lasts longer than a shattered one.

The Benevolent Power walked—or drifted—through the stone forest. Where it passed, the oily slickness vanished, replaced by a crystalline veneer that caught the white light and fractured it into veritable symphonies of color. The "impossible colors" that were once emotions of pain and hunger softened into hues of tranquility and wonder.

The Power approached the ruins of the Builder’s platform on the chasm edge. The bronze dovetails were eaten away, the structure precarious. The Power extended its will and knitted the metal back together, not with brute force, but with resonance. It stabilized the harmonics that held the atoms together. The platform became solid again—a permanent observation post.

Finally, the Power turned its attention to the root of the problem: the Chorus’s isolation. The entity consumed because it was the only thing that existed in its immediate perception. It was trapped in a hall of mirrors, eating its own reflection.

The Power reached up, toward the bleached-bone sky, and punched a hole—not through the atmosphere, but through the dimensional isolation that enveloped this world. It opened a window.

Suddenly, the stone forest was not just hearing itself. It was hearing the turn of a galaxy on the far side of the void. It was hearing the heartbeat of a dying star in a neighboring sector. It was hearing the song of a nebula giving birth to light.

*“You do not need to eat your world to feel,”* the Power whispered, the message carrying the weight of infinite compassion. *“The universe is full of song. You are an instrument. Be the listener, and the amplifier. Broadcast what you hear, and your hunger will turn into purpose.”*

The Chorus shuddered. A massive, cascading arpeggio rippled through the entire plateau, shaking the stalactites free from the arches. They fell, but before they could hit the ground, the sound waves caught them, dissolving them into harmless mist. It was a sound of joy—terrifying, overwhelming joy. The shift in its consciousness was seismic. It ceased the unmaking.

The wind began to blow again, but the moan was gone. It was now a rhythmic, breathing rush, like air through the pipes of a colossal organ. The rainbows on the crystal surface no longer crawled like insects; they bloomed, slow and deliberate, like flowers opening to the sun.

The Being, satisfied, began to fade. Its work here was not to create a paradise, but to prevent a suicide. It had turned a devourer into a guardian.

But before it vanished completely, it left one gift—a seed of wisdom buried deep within the tuning fork of the central mesa.

*“When the Builders return,”* it imprinted onto the stone, *“do not dissolve them. Listen to them. They are small, but their notes are complex. They have voices you cannot mimic. Treasure them.”*

The Stillness lifted. The wind whistled. The light burned. But the weeping had stopped. The stone forest stood, no longer a graveyard of geology, but a great, humming radio telescope, tuned to the stars, listening with an ear made of crystal, forever hungry for the next beautiful sound.
