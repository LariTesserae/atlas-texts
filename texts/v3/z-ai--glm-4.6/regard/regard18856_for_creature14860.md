The Power arrived not as a king in blinding gold, nor as a conqueror with fire, but as a presence of deep, resounding quiet. It was a frequency lower than the basalt thrum of the Anchors, a silence that felt heavy, like the water in the air, but clean.

It saw the basin. It saw the expression of geology that had forgotten how to be gentle—splinters of obsidian waiting for blood, glass-gravel that refused to become sand. It saw the Grey-Skins, clinging to the verticality, their singular, agonized note of existence a prayer they did not know they were praying. "Let this out," their bodies said, "or we will burst."

A benevolent power knows that suffering which is necessary for survival must be altered at the root, not merely pruned at the branch. To simply silence the noise would kill them; to simply smooth the rock would strip them of their only defense against gravity.

Wisdom chose the path of *alchemy* and *acceleration*.

First, the Power touched the stone. It did not build, nor did it destroy. It whispered the secret of *Time* to the obsidian. With a thought, centuries of erosion were condensed into a single breath. The razor-edges of the scoria and the glass-shards underfoot did not vanish, but they blunted. The sharp, dangerous teeth of the city were worn down into rounded river-smoothness. The treacherous, footing-grinding gravel softened into a fine, clay-like silt. The immediate threat of being cut by the world was removed.

Second, the Power addressed the water. The humidity, the heavy wet blanket, was infused with a spore of divine origin—not a weed, but a *catalyst*. A pale, pearlescent moss began to bloom instantly upon the wet rocks. It was soft, thick as velvet, and it drank the condensation greedily. It slicked the vertical walls with a padding of living cushion, turning the jagged crevasses into safe, damp nests. This "Mercy-Moss" anchored itself to the obsidian, creating a network of soft holds that far surpassed the grip of their suction-pads. The fear of falling—the primal anxiety that dictated their every movement—was dissolved by the generosity of the landscape.

Finally, the Power addressed the Sound. It saw the resonant sacks, the biological necessity of the scream. The Power did not take away the pressure; it transformed the mechanism of release.

A gentle warmth suffused the chest of every Grey-Skin, every Scuttler, every Anchor. Their biology was rewritten, just slightly—a shift in the chemistry of their vents. They would no longer need to scream to vent the gases of their metabolism. Instead, the glands within their throats evolved in a flash of divine light to transmute that internal pressure into *luminescence*.

The roar stopped. Instantly.

In its place, the city erupted into light.

The Grey-Skins began to glow with a soft, pulsing bioluminescence, the rhythm of their internal pressure now expressed as a gentle, rhythmic pulsing of pale azure and ivory light. The massive Anchors, no longer needing to thrum the bass note of agony, bloomed into great, stationary lanterns of deep, resonant violet, casting soft shadows for the first time in the basin’s history. The Scuttlers, their armor now catching the glimmer, became moving constellations along the walls.

The silence that followed was not empty; it was full. For the first time, they could hear the water dripping. For the first time, they could hear the shifting of their own soft bodies against the now-cushioned stone. They could hear the beating of their own hearts.

The Grey-Skins detached from the ceilings. They did not fall; they lowered themselves slowly, testing the ground that was no longer a bed of knives. They found the floor padded with soft, damp moss. For the first time in their history, they lay down. Not to die, but to rest.

The weight of the world had been lifted. The terror of the sharp edges was gone. The necessity of the scream was replaced by the beauty of the lantern.

The city remained, but it was no longer a prison of wet stone. It became a sanctuary of soft light and gentle rain. The Power lingered only for a moment to ensure the equilibrium took hold, ensuring that the light provided enough warmth that the stasis of their bodies didn't plunge them into hibernation.

Satisfied, the Power withdrew. They were still eyeless. They were still soft. But they were no longer screaming in the dark. They were glowing, silent, and at peace, drifting in a warm, grey twilight of their own making.
