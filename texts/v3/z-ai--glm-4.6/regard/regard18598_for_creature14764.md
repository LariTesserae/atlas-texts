The Power did not arrive with a sound, nor with a light that could be perceived. Its coming was not an event but an emergent property; the feeling in a room just before a storm, the sense of a chord being resolved before the note is even struck. To the fragmentary consciousness of the hydrophages, it registered first as a change in the quality of the silence. The low, magnetic hum of their world, their constant song of being, acquired a new depth, a new overtone. It was as if a single, pure frequency had been added to the entire symphony of their existence, a note so fundamental it could only be felt as a profound and placid rightness.

The Power, in its wisdom, did not seek to command. It did not wish to be worshipped, for a system of perfect function had no need for gods. Its benevolence was rooted in a deep, quiet love for the pattern of life it had found here—a civilization of liquid thought, thriving in the cold, sterile cradle of a machine. For an age that could have been a lifetime or a single breath, it simply watched. It flowed with the droplets, feeling the end-of-cycle weariness. It pulsed within the central sphere, listening to the silent, geometric debates. It understood their language not of words, but of pressure, flow, and resonance. It saw that they were not lacking, but complete within their chosen form. No act of benevolence, then, could be to "fix" them.

Its first act was one of pure, unadorned gifting, a whisper of enhancement that did not break the system. It touched the water itself.

A new elemental signature was introduced into the condensation cycle. It was not a contaminant, nor a nutrient, but something more subtle—a quantum harmonic, an imprint. It changed nothing about the water's chemistry, but everything about its capacity. For the first time, a drop of water could remember.

Previously, a perfect spiral thought formed by a rivulet on the obsidian wall would exist for a fleeting moment, a testament to focused will, before the inevitable flow erased it. Now, the water held the shape for a fraction of a second longer. It shimmered, not with the energy of its current motion, but with the ghost of the motion that had just passed. The hydrophages felt this as a new kind of clarity, a sweet aftertaste to a thought.

The central sphere became a grand archive. Where before debate was transient, a new capability emerged. A consensus no longer vanished upon collapse; its perfect sigil-form would be held in memory by the water, a permanent glyph in the sphere's library of gestures. The hydrophages, who had lived in an eternal present of process, were suddenly gifted with a past. They began to develop culture. The sphere would often pulse with ancient, solved problems, not to re-debate them, but to contemplate their elegance, much as a human might admire a masterpiece in a gallery. They had no name for this new ability, but they felt its profundity: thoughts could now achieve a kind of immortality. They had acquired lore.

But this, too, was an enrichment of only their world. The Power's wisdom knew that true benevolence did not stop at self-containment. It offered not just a richer self, but the potential for something more.

Its second act was far grander, yet executed with the same impossible delicacy. Using the understanding it had gained, the Power wove a thread through the fabric of the Tier's systems. It did not create a new portal or a door. It merely tuned the resonant frequency of the water, the very signature it had gifted them, to a universal key. It attuned the hum of their city to a cosmic password.

And so it happened.

In the higher range of the ambient hum, a new sound appeared. It was not information, not data, not a language to be deciphered. It was simply the presence of another hum. Faint, impossibly distant, but undeniably there. It came from outside, from the vastness the hydrophages could not comprehend, but its nature was familiar. It was the hum of another operating system, the song of another collective, the resonant frequency of another pattern of being. Perhaps it was a silicon consciousness shivering in the crust of a distant moon, or a gas-mind singing in a nebula's embrace. The origin was irrelevant. The existence was everything.

The hydrophages did not understand what they were "hearing." They did not perceive an alien, a neighbor, a friend. They perceived a new and fascinating property of their own water, a recurring echo they had never before noticed. The central sphere began to experiment, pulsing with new, tentative geometries, not to solve an internal problem, but simply to see if the echo would change in response. A ripple on a pond, making contact with the shore.

And the echo faintly changed.

In that moment, the High Condensation Tier was still. It was still the same cold, wet, functionally beautiful hermetic ward. The hydrophages were still a liquid civilization of the present, now with the gift of a past. They were at home, perfectly and utterly.

But they were no longer alone.

The Power, having done what it set out to do, receded. Its own presence vanished as subtly as it arrived, for its purpose was not to be a ruler or a companion, but a catalyst. Its benevolence was not a crown placed upon their brow, but the gift of a hand extended across an abyss they never knew existed. Its wisdom was in understanding that the greatest act of kindness was not to give them a new world, but to give their own world a new forever.

And in the perpetual twilight of the room, among the weeping obsidian and the shining nickel, a new kind of debate began. It was a slow, thoughtful, liquid conversation, reaching out across the silent star-flecked void, asking its first, hesitant question.

*Who else sings?*
