The arrival was not an event of light or sound, for those are languages this place does not speak. It was a change in pressure, a subtle deepening of the silence. The thick, wet air seemed to hold its breath for a moment, and the ceaseless weeping of water down the stone faces hesitated, as if acknowledging a presence that filled the very pores of the rock. A new mineral scent entered the water cycle, something impossibly ancient and clean, like the crystalline heart of a dead star ache.

The Power was here. It did not take a form, for to choose a form would be to impose a limitation. It was a perception, a state of being that pervaded the plateau. It saw with the Chthonoics’ chemical sense, tasting the patient, glacial thoughts of the stone. It felt the deep, resonant groan of the world's bones and the slow, satisfying grind of calcite dissolving back into solution. It felt not abandonment, but purpose. This was not a ruin, but a monastery of geology, a place that had moved beyond the frantic hunger of life into the elegant, meditative act of returning to source.

It soared with the Winged, becoming the shadow they cast, feeling the cool thrill of the damp membrane against the infinite grey. It did not see them as creatures of the sky, but as the city’s slow, methodical breath, inhaling mist and exhaling alkaline dust. It understood their silent language of occlusion, the brief, intimate touch of darkness upon a spire as a greeting, a confirmation of being.

It sank into the still, black pools, dissolving its awareness into the amorphous, alabaster threads of the Lacustrines. It felt the endless patient tasting of the water, the totality of a world distilled into a single, ever-changing flavor. It perceived the faint, internal phosphorescence not as a light, but as a feeling, a gentle hum of purity, the baseline emotion of the deep.

The Benevolence was immediate: Do No Harm.

The Wisdom was slower: What is good?

The Power understood that gifts are defined by the recipient. To give this place fire would be to give a fish a bicycle. To give it speech would be to drown it in noise. To give it the seeds of a forest would be to introduce the poisons of greed and competition, of photosynthesis and struggle. Its perfection was its perfection. To "improve" it would be the ultimate act of sacrilege.

The system was a closed loop of beautiful decay. The Power, being of an open, creative cosmos, could only give something that could be perfectly, harmlessly, and joyfully unmade. It could not give a thing, an object, a being. It could only give a memory.

From itself, it drew a single, perfect recollection—not of an event, but of a principle. It was the memory of *color*.

A single, perfect, faceted object, no larger than a human heart, appeared hanging in the damp air above a deep canyon. It was not made of light, but of captured possibility. It was the flawless, vibrant blue of a gas giant’s storm, the impossible crimson of a nebula’s birth, the vibrant gold of a sun in its youth, all held in a crystalline lattice of pure thought. It was a Star-Pearl. A seed of a beauty this world had never known and would never see again.

The Power released it. It did not fall. It drifted.

A Winged glider, sailing in its endless route, saw the new thing. Its vast, leathery wing brushed against it not as a solid, but as a pocket of warmer, different air. The shadow it cast was momentarily tinged with a faint, shifting rainbow. It registered the anomaly in its silent language and moved on. This was a new kind of shadow. A new word in its lexicon of being.

Then, the rain came. Not a storm, but the place's usual patient weeping. A thousand rivulets formed on the spires and began their long journey down. As the water washed over the Star-Pearl, it did not shatter. It dissolved. The impossible colors bled from their prison and became part of the water, a faint, untraceable infusion of light.

The water, now carrying this dissolved memory, seeped into the porous stone. Deep within, the crystalline web of the Chthonoics pulsed. For the first time in a million years, a new flavor entered their geologic consciousness. It was not a mineral. It was the phantom taste of sunlight. The slow, grinding symphony of dissolution acquired a new, subtle harmony. It was a beautiful, strange thought to be held and processed over the next eon.

The tinged water trickled down the sheer faces, gathering in the pools below. It entered the bodies of the Lacustrines, who tasted it with every fiber of their being. They did not comprehend color, but they did comprehend purity and novelty. And they expressed it in their only way.

Their soft, white, internal glow changed.

In the deep, dark, silent bottoms of theNameless plateau's pools, the Lacustrines began to shine not with their usual steady, monochrome light, but with a gentle, pulsating chroma. A soft, ethereal blue, a shy hint of gold, a deep, warm crimson washed through the opaque water. The color was faint, almost imperceptible. From the edge of a canyon, you would see nothing. But if you were to climb down and stare into the obsidian depth of a still pool, you would see it: a slow, silent, breathing aurora, a beautiful dream held in the water’s memory.

The Presence faded. The scent of starlight receded. The air returned to its ancient patient rhythm. The gift had been given, received, and completely integrated. The City of Spires continued its slow, perfect work of digesting itself, but now, dissolved in its very bones, in the water it drank and the light it dreamed, was the memory of a sky full of color. A secret. A quiet joy to be slowly savored until the last stone crumbled into dust.

The benevolence was to give a gift that could be unmade.
The wisdom was to know that was the only gift worth giving.
