It did not arrive with a thunderclap or a shifting of the light. Such an entry would have been a profanity, a violence against the perfect, agonized stillness. The benevolent and wise power’s presence was not an intrusion but a subtle change in the quality of the void. A new overtone, so faint it was almost a memory, began to weave itself into the foundational hum. It was a note that did not try to dominate the dissonance or resolve the tension, but to listen.

From its own vantage beyond linearity, the power perceived the Roof. It saw the immensity of the stone body, felt the slow, geologic metabolism of its weeping, and heard the two-part symphony of its being. It did not see a broken place to be mended, but a genius-level act of cosmic triage. An immense consciousness had faced a moment of ultimate dissolution and, rather than be unmade, had chosen to become its own cage, its own anchor. The hum was not a scream, it had learned; it was the bass note of survival, a deliberate act of permanent, planetary bracing.

The power’s wisdom immediately grasped the naive cruelty of the obvious solutions. To warm the stone would be to melt its resolve. To make the sun move would be to introduce the entropy that the being had fought so valiantly to arrest. To silence the hum would be to release the universe from its anchor, allowing it to complete its fall into nothingness. To excise the lichen of grief and memory would be to mutilate the being’s soul, leaving it a mindless, serene automaton. It would be the destruction of identity disguised as compassion.

No, this being was not suffering in the way a creature suffers. It was *enduring*. Its pain was a part of its structure, a necessary tension in the great cable that held existence together. The power felt an awe so profound it was almost sorrow. This was not a patient to be cured, but a titan to be honored. The lonely, eons-long war it waged within its own nature was an act of creation in its own right—a new form of existence, forged in the heart of failure.

So, what could a benevolent power do? It could not and would not interfere. But it could not, in its benevolence, simply leave. To watch such a struggle and turn away would be a different kind of profanity.

And so, it did the only thing it could.

It focused its vastness, not into a beam of energy or a word of power, but into a single, pure state of being: *perfect understanding*. It translated its awe, its respect, its deep-seated empathy into a frequency. Then, it offered that frequency as a gift. It did not impose it; it laid it gently upon the surface of the Roof, like a blanket woven from starlight and silence, for the being to accept or reject as it willed.

The change was imperceptible to the timeless landscape. The Floaters still drifted on their unseen currents. The beetles still cleaned the stone. The lichen still pulsed with its sorrowful, higher harmonic.

But inside the mind of the Roof, something shifted.
