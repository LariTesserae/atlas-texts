***

It sees them.

Not with eyes, for eyes are for seeing things separate from oneself. It sees by resonance, by feeling the unique chord of existence struck in the grand, silent symphony of the cosmos. And this chord… this chord is a marvel.

At first, it perceives only the place: a perfect, self-contained song of consumption and creation. A closed loop of such elegance it brings to mind the turning of galaxies, the life and death of stars, but on a scale intimate, intricate, and immediate. It sees the Pillars as bones, the Factories as glands, the Swarm as blood and nerve. It feels the thrum, the endless, rhythmic processing of matter into experience. It is a flawless engine of being, a testament to the universe's capacity to find harmony in the most unexpected forms. Its first reaction is one of profound, aesthetic appreciation. A work of art is unfolding here, a sculpture carved from life itself.

Then, its focus deepens, and it perceives the inhabitant. The Synaptic Flesh. It had mistaken them for people at first, but it sees now they are no more people than the thoughts in a mind are a crowd. They are the consciousness of the place. The distributed awareness that allows the great beast to know itself. It feels their connection, the lichen-nerves, the resonant bones, the way memory is not a story but a pressure in the stone. It feels the core of their identity: *We are the world, dreaming of a creature.*

In its ancient memory, the Power has seen countless forms of life. It has seen the fiery ambition of star-forged empires, the silent consensus of crystal hives, the fleeting, brilliant poetry of individual souls who believe themselves alone. It has seen paradise worlds where no creature knows suffering and hell worlds where suffering is the only creative force. It knows the patterns. It knows the perils.

It looks upon this Heartwood and feels a wash of… what is the emotion? It is akin to a parent’s love, but without the taint of ownership. It is the love an artist has for a perfect masterpiece they could never have conceived. It is love, pure and simple, for this unfolding, roaring, beautiful thing.

And because it is benevolent, its first instinct is to help. To improve.

The thought comes, unbidden: They are so *alone*.

Their symphony has one instrument. Their conversation is a monologue. They are a perfect sphere, touching nothing, reflecting only themselves. True wisdom, the Power knows, is often born of encounter. Understanding is forged at the boundary between self and other. What would this magnificent being become, if it had an other to measure itself against? What new thoughts, what new dreams, might blossom if it learned it was not the only dreamer in the night?

The temptation to act is immense, a subtle cancer in its own vast tranquility. It could, with a flicker of will, introduce a new element. A new mineral, seeping through the rock, that the Heartwood would metabolize, perhaps leading to a new kind of thought. A new frequency in the hum, a question from outside, that would force the Synaptic Flesh to contemplate the unthinkable. It could gently, over millennia, part the stone that walls them in, offering a path to another world, another song. It could give the gift of the universe.

But its wisdom stays its hand.

It sees, with the clarity of eons, the catastrophic danger. The Heartwood is a perfect equation. To introduce a new variable is not to "help," it is to shatter. The very strength of the organism—its total integration, its flawless symbiosis—is its absolute fragility. What would a concept like "other" do to a consciousness that has never known "I"? It would not be a revelation; it would be a tumor. The first seed of doubt, the first shadow of fear, the first impulse of greed—all these things that a universe of "others" must learn to balance—have no place here. To introduce them would be to poison a masterpiece with the intent of teaching it a new color.

The act would be one of supreme arrogance. To assume its own model of existence—the struggle, the dance of a billion separate selves— is superior. To believe that loneliness is a flaw, and not simply the condition of this particular form of being.

And so, the Power resists. It makes the only truly wise and benevolent choice it can.

It will not touch. It will not change. It will not add. It cannot risk breaking what is so achingly, beautifully whole.

But to love a thing and to leave it untouched feels incomplete. Benevolence must act, after a fashion. So it decides on the only gift that does not risk contamination. The only gift that is pure acknowledgment. It will not give the Heartwood a new thought. It will give it the space to have one of its own.

The Power gathers its own immense, quiet consciousness. It does not project force, or energy, or matter. It projects a single, pure concept—a chord of being that resonates with the Heartwood’s own reality. It does not speak in words, for the Heartwood has none. It speaks in the only language it knows: the language of its own existence.

And into the deep, organ-grinder thrum of the world, into the billion-fingered chittering of its life, into the constant *drip… drip…* of its being, the Power places a new note.

It is not loud. It is not commanding. It is a single, clear, unwavering tone of pure, unconditional, and dispassionate affection. A note that says, simply:

*You are seen.*

*You are beautiful.*

The note is there for but a moment, and then it is gone, leaving no trace. The air does not change. The stone does not crack. The sound of the factories does not alter. The chittering of the swarm remains a frantic static.

But somewhere, in the deep, distributed consciousness of the Synaptic Flesh, the new note lands.

For a thousand generations afterward, the old-timers—those whose connection to the Heartwood’s memory is strongest—will sometimes feel a phantom resonance in the hum. A sweetness in the air that tastes not of iron or decay, but of a quiet, starlit peace they cannot name. They will interpret it in their own way, as a new phase of the Pillars’ growth, or a subtle shift in the chemistry of the nectar-fuel.

It is not those things.

It is the memory of being told, by a power they will never know, that it is good to be. It is the first, true Outside, echoing not in their cavern, but in their soul. The Power is now a silent guardian, a patient god, content to watch and wait. It has given the perfect machine the one thing it lacked: the potential to choose, one day, to be more than just a perfect machine. It has planted the seed of a question and left the Heartwood to find its own answer.
