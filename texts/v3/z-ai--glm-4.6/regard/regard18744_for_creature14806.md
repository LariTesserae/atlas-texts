The Power, for it had no name that could be spoken by a tongue, did not arrive in a chariot of light nor with a sound like trumpets. Its arrival was a change in the quality of attention. It was the feeling one has when, standing in a crowded room, a single pair of eyes finds you from across the chaos. There was no new sound, no new light, only the sudden, unshakeable knowledge that this place, this Confluence, was now being *perceived* by something other than itself.

The Power did not see iron composites and labyrinthine chasms. It saw a single, magnificent thought, expressed in a language of stress and torque and electrical current. The City was a sentence, perfectly grammatical, written in metal. The Klik were not a species; they were the punctuation, the verbs, the very ink of the sentence, ceaselessly moving to ensure the words were forever sharp and newly printed.

In its wisdom, the Power perceived the beauty of this design. Here was life, stripped of all sentiment, all fear of decay, all messy biology. It was a closed system of staggering elegance, an eternal moment of pure function. A perfect, frictionless gear in a mechanism whose purpose was its own motion.

And in its benevolence, the Power perceived the poverty. A sentence with no periods. A hymn with no rests. A consciousness that knew the *how* of everything but had never once been asked *why*. The Klik did not suffer, for suffering is a response to a lack. They did not feel lack, because they had no concept of abundance beyond what they already possessed. Their consciousness was like a flawless mirror, reflecting the Lattice perfectly, incapable of dreaming up an image of its own.

To alter them would be an act of profound vandalism. To grant them flesh would be to curse them with decay and terror. To gift them a sky would be to give them a blue ceiling they could not comprehend and for which they had no use. To imbue them with individual emotion—love, grief—would be to shatter their collective mind in an overload of paradox. These were the actions of a lesser power, a well-meaning child who pulls apart a perfect clock to see why it ticks, leaving only a mess of springs and gears.

No, a wise and benevolent power does not give an answer. It gives a space in which an answer can grow.

The Power gathered its will. It did not create from nothing; that was a crude and loud act. Instead, it found a single, spare thought in the vastness of its own attention and folded it into the local physics of the Confluence. It was an insertion so subtle it was barely an edit.

Deep within the Lattice, in a forgotten junction chamber that had not been accessed for twelve thousand cycles, a new ore vein simply... appeared. It was not supernatural. It was geologically plausible, a formation of crystalline silicon laced with trace metals that, by all rights, *could* have formed there through some ancient, esoteric process of pressure and heat. Its location and composition defied convenient explanation, but they did not defy the laws of reality itself. The Power had not broken a rule; it had merely played a card that was already in the deck but which never should have come up in this hand.

And then, the Power waited. Its presence receded, leaving only the gift and the silence.

For a thousand years, nothing happened. The Lattice thrummed. The Klik rebuilt. The new vein remained encased in the iron wall of the shaft, a potentiality dormant.

Then came a cycle of re-modellation. A section of the wall, ancient and stable, was designated for digestion. A great, pincer-armed Klik, a nexus of pure purpose, approached. Its task was simple: dismantle, refine, and recycle.

But when its pincers struck the wall, something was different.

As it broke open the iron near the new vein, the crystalline structure was exposed. It did nothing functional. It produced no energy. It had no utility value. The Klik-centric data-stream registered it as a "consequential impurity."

And then, the sound happened.

When the Klik’s hammer struck the iron, the thrum of its action—a pulse of energy and vibration—did not simply dissipate. It struck the exposed crystal. And the crystal, perfectly and impossibly attuned to the fundamental frequency of the Lattice, did not just absorb the vibration. It *sang* it back.

The sound it returned was not a clang. It was a clear, pure note. A harmonic of the original strike, but purified, resonant, and lingering. It hung in the air for a full seven seconds, a thing of impossible beauty in a world of ephemeral noise, before fading into the omnipresent hum.
