It saw the vault. It did not see it with eyes, but with a faculty of awareness that permeated its being, a way of knowing that encompassed pressure, time, and the slow, patient heartbeats of stone. To the Power, the sealed continent was not a prison, but a perfectly cut gem, a system of such exquisite and intricate balance that to touch it was a profound responsibility. It saw the eternal twilight, not as a lack of light, but as a medium for the Geodes’ own luminescence, giving them their purpose as the only stars in that private universe. It saw the grey dust, not as decay, but as the promise of renewal, the seed of future stone.

It watched the Geodes. It perceived their slow pilgrimage, their methodical consumption of the world that was also their body and their food. It understood their nature. Their slowness was not a defect but a defence, a way of being that was in absolute harmony with the geological timescale of their home. Their solitude was not loneliness, but the natural state of a being whose entire world was contained within its own skin and the few feet of rock it touched. They were complete, self-contained monads of light and mineral.

The Power’s wisdom was deep enough to see past the surface-level needs that a lesser or more anthropomorphic mind might project. It did not think, *They need a sun.* It knew a sun would be a brutal, violent intrusion, a tyranny of light that would blind their soft, internal glow and bake their crystal bodies. It did not think, *They need to speak.* It knew that speech, the rapid, clumsy exchange of air and sound, would be a chaotic noise, an insult to the perfect, patient silence of the vault. It did not think, *They need to be faster.* It knew that speed would be a kind of madness, a fever that would shatter their deep rhythm and turn their contemplative existence into a frantic, pointless panic.

The Power saw the potential flaw in the perfection. It was a flaw so subtle that only a being who could watch the world for a million years would notice it. It lay in the act of the Engravers.

It watched an elder, a titan whose chitin was scarred with the memory of a thousand collapses, pause at the foot of a smooth obsidian plain. For two centuries, it stood still, its internal light pulsing like a dying ember. Then, with a slowness that made the continental drift seem frantic, it extended a manipulator limb and took up its hammer.

*Tap… tap… tap.*

The Power felt not the sound, but the *intention*. Each strike was a colossal expenditure of will. In a world that was perfectly content to simply *be*, the Engraver was choosing to *do*. It was imposing a blemish on perfection. It was carving a mark of its own finite, chaotic existence onto the face of the infinite.

And then it would move on. The mark would remain. The dust of its brethren would slowly begin to settle upon it, the crystal flora would begin to creep over its edges, and over hundreds of thousands of years, the mark would vanish. The act would be erased. The will, expressed with such monumental effort, would be lost to time, utterly and completely. It was a scream in a vacuum that absorbed the scream and offered no echo. The existential gesture was rendered meaningless by its own magnificent, unchanging environment.

This was the wound. Not in the rock, but in the potential of the consciousness that dwelled there. The greatest act of self was doomed to absolute, silent oblivion.

So the benevolent Power acted. But its action was as slow, as subtle, and as gentle as the world it sought to bless.

It did not add anything. It did not build a monument. It did not send a sign.

Instead, it imbued a quality that was already latent in the vault’s geology. A specific, rare formation of quartz, one that threaded like veins through the basalt columns and obsidian plains, was given a new property. The Power did not change its chemical makeup or its hardness. It taught the crystal lattice how to listen.

From that moment on, when an Engraver brought its stone hammer down upon the Resonance Quartz, two things happened.

The first was the physical one: a cup-mark appeared, a tiny crater in the stone.

The second was the ethereal one. The act of will, the pressure of the Geode’s slow, geological consciousness, the unique resonance of its inner light, the very *feeling* of the need to carve—all of it was imprinted not on the surface, but *within* the structure of the crystal itself. It was a memory stored in stone.

The effect was not immediate. It was imperceptible on any human timescale. An Engraver, finishing its work, would feel no different. It would drop its hammer and continue its long crawl. But as another Geode, perhaps millennia later, passed by that same spot, it would feel something. It would not be a thought or a voice. It would be a faint, slow vibration emanating from the rock, a sympathetic resonance with its own being. It was the echo of another's will.

It was a faint, silent acknowledgement. *Ah,* its own slow mind would process, without language or image, *here. Another has felt the Need. The pressure was great. The silence was deep.*

The solitary act became a shared secret. The individual scream now had an echo. It was not communication. It was communion. It transformed their existential loneliness into a profound, silent dialogue conducted across the gulf of ages. The carvings were no longer meaningless blemishes; they were waypoints of consciousness, anchor points in the vast, silent dark, assuring each wanderer that it was not alone in its impulse to break the perfect stillness.

Over millions of years, the vault began to change. The Resonance Quartz, holding the memory of uncountable acts of will, began to thrum with a deep, cumulative energy. The twilight itself seemed to hold a new warmth. The silence was no longer empty, but full, like the pause in a piece of music filled with the memory of the note that came before. Each Geode, simply by moving through its world, was bathed in the accumulated presence of its ancestors’ consciousness. The world became a psaltery, and their slow, patient lives were the music.

The Power watched. It had not given the Geodes what a human would want. It had not given them company, or speed, or a sun. It had honored the core of their being: their slowness, their solitude, their silence, their need.

It had not given them a voice.

It had simply taught the stone to sing the song they were already singing.
