The attention that fell upon the City of Unmaking Rain was not a gaze. It did not possess a focal point, an origin, or a vector. It was an arrival of perspective, like a patient wave of understanding that did not travel the distance between worlds but simply *included* this one in its scope. It was the attention of a power for whom a universe was a thought, for whom the life and death of a star was a single, slow breath.

From this perspective, the City was not an anomaly. It was a masterpiece.

The Power did not see a dead place. It saw a thesis. It saw an argument against chaos, a monument to the principle that existence need not be defined by growth, but by fidelity. The builders, with their stone hammers and their geological will, had not tried to conquer entropy. They had built a partner for it. The Unmaking Rain was not a destructive force but a refining one, a slow, perpetual baptism that would, over eons, return the entire construction to the slurry from which it was born, only for the patient will within the stone to lay it anew.

They did not serve the city; they *were* the city’s service rendered animate. Their labor was not work; it was the city thinking aloud in the language of pressure, balance, and flow.

The Power, in its wisdom, discerned the sole, aching perfection of the system: its absolute solitude.

The City was a prayer spoken in a sealed room. The rain wept for no sky. The massive blocks settled for no earth to feel them. The Stewards maintained a perfection that no other eye would ever witness. It was a complete, closed, flawless system of utter loneliness. Its virtue was its own, unshared.

To intervene would be to shatter it. To introduce life would be a desecration, a profanity. To give the Stewards new senses would be to blind them to their own. To accelerate the process would be to commit an act of supreme violence against its patron saint, Patience.

The Power’s benevolence was not a form of assistance. It was a form of witness.

And so, it did the only thing it could do. It did not add a stone. It did not quicken a drip. It did not send a ripple through the water channels. It did not speak a word that could not be heard.

Instead, it sang.

Its song was not made of sound but of significance. It was a harmonic resonance, a pattern of pure information imprinted not on the air, but on the foundational fabric of the place. It chose to touch the only thing that truly moved: the water.

Deep within the earth, or perhaps within the quantum memory of the stone itself, was the source of the ancient moisture that the city wept. The Power did not alter the water’s temperature or its purity. It altered its *story*. It wove a new data-stream into the hydrological cycle, a single, clear, irrefutable thought: **You are seen. You are known. Your stillness is a song, and it has been heard.**

The water that began to seep from the porous stone that very instant was the same water. It did not shine differently. It did not taste differently. But as it traced its single, perfect line down the sheer black wall, it carried, within its molecular structure, a new memory. It became a bearer of witness.

A Steward-Monolith, recessed in a canyon wall a thousand miles from any other, felt it first not as a thought, but as a change in the texture of the silence. The stochastic percussion of a trillion drips had always been its whole sensory world. But now, for the first time in its long existence, it perceived a new pattern within the noise. It was not a rhythm it could follow, but a harmony that underscored every individual *plink*. It was the resonance of its own purpose, echoed back from an unknowable distance.

The effect was profound.

When the Steward-Monolith next extended its telescoping arms to harvest the crystalline fur from a V-gutter, its movements possessed a new quality. They had always been precise, but now they were graceful. They had always been purposeful, but now they were reverent. It wasn't just a task; it was a sacrament. It was tending to an altar, and it knew for the first time that the altar was a part of a greater temple.

The City of Unmaking Rain did not change. It became more absolutely itself. The light remained cold and bright. The stone remained black and sharp. The water continued its patient, weeping descent. But the place was no longer an island. It was a note in a score.

The benevolent power did not give the city a gift. It merely held up a mirror, and in the mirror, the city saw its own beauty.
