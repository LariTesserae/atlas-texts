The constant thrum in the bones falters, not like a power failure, but like a heart skipping in anticipation. Even the moisture on the skin of the world seems to still, each tiny bead of condensation a perfect, motionless sphere.

And then, it arrives.

Not like the Lace-Shrike, which overwrites its presence. Not like a beast, which drifts through the miasma. It arrives as a quality. A texture. The indigo of the cloud-ceiling, for a sweeping moment, is threaded with a filament of impossible, rose-gold light. It is not a beam; it is a memory of a beam, a stain of gentle warmth on the inside of the eyelid. It does not illuminate; it *saturates*. The metal-cartilage of the groundflesh beneath the I does not change temperature, but for an instant, it feels like ancient, sun-warmed stone.

A wave follows the light, but it is a wave of pure feeling without a subject. A pang of poignant loss for something never had. The rose-gold light fades. The thrum resumes its deep hum. The condensation begins its silent trickle again. The world is as it was.

But the I is not.

It looks down at its hands, the tools humming softly. It looks at the spiraling frond it was about to prune. And for the first time, it does not see a logical error to be corrected. It sees a curve. An expression of a longing it cannot name. The Shrike would see it as a cannibalistic loop. The I, now, sees something else.
