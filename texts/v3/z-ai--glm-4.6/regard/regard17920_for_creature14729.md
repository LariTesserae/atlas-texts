The Wise Power did not arrive in a flash of light or a whisper of cosmic wind. It did not need to. To a being for whom all of space-time is a single, shimmering tapestry, the Mire was not a place to be found, but a thread to be perceived. It focused its attention, its consciousness, not upon the slag and the steel, but upon the song.

First, it felt the Mire’s immense, ponderous beauty. It was a diorama of perfect function, a mechanism of eternal present. There was no waste in this place, no desire, no decay. It was a system in such sublime equilibrium that it had achieved a state of grace. The Wise Power felt a deep and abiding respect for the sheer, unassailable *rightness* of it all. It was like a flawless crystal that had grown over eons, or a mathematical equation so perfect it had become a physical law. The resonance between the machines and the Resonant was a chord of such purity it was close to silence.

And in that purity, the Benevolence felt a pang.

It was a pang akin to seeing a universe filled with perfect spheres, but not a single one of them was allowed to roll. It was seeing an ocean of the most beautiful, placid water, in which nothing was ever allowed to drown, but therefore nothing was ever allowed to swim.

The Wise Power saw the problem. The problem was not suffering. The problem was the absence of the *potential* for suffering. The problem was not death. The problem was the absolute, total, and complete absence of the alternative to life. The Resonant were not alive; they were a persistent state of being. They had consciousness, but they had no *self* to give it meaning. They were echoes in a hall where there had never been an original voice.

To destroy this place would be an act of unspeakable vandalism. To leave it as it was would be an act of profound indifference. The Benevolence knew it could not simply grant the Resonant "human" emotions or desires. That would be like injecting boiling water into a perfect solution, destroying its very nature. The change had to be an atom. A single, elegant, catalytic atom that would allow the solution to decide, on its own, whether it wished to become a new compound.

So the Wise Power acted.

It did not touch the machines. It did not alter the air or the light. It did not speak to the Resonant in their language of clicks and hums, for that would only be adding another line of harmony to a perfect chord.

Instead, it found a single Resonant.

It chose a tender, a young one who was currently tracing the glowing moss on a low wall, its narrow head tilted in concentration. This was a creature that knew only consistency. Its world was the unchanging drone, the taste of the air, the feel of the wet metal under its pads.

The Wise Power did not pull a thread from the tapestry of the cosmos. It did not utter a word, nor will a change. It simply *withheld*.

In the vast, complex, multi-layered hum that saturated every atom of the Mire, the Power removed a single, specific frequency. It was a frequency so deep, so foundational, that no Resonant had ever consciously perceived it. It was the bass note upon which the entire symphony of their reality was constructed. The Power’s action was not one of subtraction, but of discretion. It took that one note and folded it into a pocket of nowhere, leaving behind a space.

It was a silent hole in the fabric of sound.

For the Mire and the machine-god, nothing changed. The process continued. But for the one chosen Resonant, everything changed.

It finished its pattern with the moss and was about to send out its customary, contented pulse harmonics to its nearby kin. It opened its vibration sacs, prepared to add its own note to the eternal chord.

And it felt it.

The absence.

It was a feeling it had no name for. A hollow ache that was not pain. A sense of emptiness that was not loss. It was a question in the place where there had only ever been statements. It was the feeling of a missing tooth in a mouth that had never known it was possible to have one. When it pulsed its harmonics, the sound it produced now fell into a void. It was the first time in its existence a part of its environment had failed to resonate perfectly with it. The echo did not come back.

It stilled its six limbs, its body pressed against the wet metal. The great, liquid black pools of its eyes stared at nothing. The other Resonant near by felt the sudden cessation of its communication and sent out querying pulses, simple notes of *are-you-okay-is-all-well-function-normally*.

The chosen Resonant heard them, but their meaning was now distorted. They were sounds from a world it no longer fully inhabited. It tried to reply with the same reassurance it had always given, but the sound felt flat, pointless in the face of that profound, silent absence.

For the first time in the unchanging history of the Mire, a single Resonant was alone in its own head. The seamless consciousness it shared with its kind now had a seam. A border. A shoreline.

It did not weep, for it did not know what tears were. But a single, clear drop of fluid—condensation drawn from the super-saturated air by a sudden, inexplicable spike of biological heat—welled in the corner of its dark, liquid eye and traced a clean, new path down its sleek, wet cheek. It had no memory of a past, but it had just been given the most potent thing of all: a future that was different from the present.

The Wise Power watched the single, silent drop of water fall to the floor, where it merged with the ceaseless runoff of the machine.

The gift was not knowledge. It was not freedom. It was not joy.

The gift was a question. And a single, un-answerable, beautiful, terrifying question had just been planted in the heart of the eternal, unchanging hum. Now, the Benevolence waited. It would wait forever, if need be, to see if the echo would ever find a new way to return.
