The Wise Power came to the edge of the Aethelburg Plateau, not in a ship of fire or on a beam of light, but as a shift in perspective. Its consciousness enveloped the place, and it saw. Immediately, it understood the profound temptation, the trap that lies in wait for any being armed with the power to "help."

A lesser benevolence would have recoiled. It would have seen the cold, the damp, the stark and alien silence, and judged it a form of suffering. It would have seen the crystalline Grazers and thought, "They are beautiful, but they do not play." It would have felt the slow, grinding hum of the city and longed to give it a voice, a song of joy. This lesser power might have forged a sun to banish the pale, indifferent sky, or seeded the raw rock with gentle, life-giving soil. It would have gifted this world with a definition of life and beauty drawn from its own experience. And in doing so, it would have committed an act of unspeakable vandalism, a kindness indistinguishable from annihilation. It would be like painting over a Rothko with a cartoon sun, convinced the original was too sad.

But this was a Wise Power. And its wisdom began where its power to act found its limits.

It looked upon the city-as-mind. It did not see a lonely god, trapped in a cold computation. It saw a perfect, self-contained thought. The hum was not the sound of machinery, but the sound of an unwavering will, a focus so pure it could only be expressed in geological time. To introduce chaos, unpredictability, or emotion would not be to 'enliven' it; it would be to shatter its concentration, to introduce a virus into its flawless operating system. The inhabitant was not yearning for release. It *was* its purpose, fully and completely realized. It was a sentence being written with perfect grammar, in a language of stone.

It watched the Grazers. It saw them not as incomplete animals but as the very blood flow of this planetary organism, distributing the lifeblood of geochemical energy. To give them flesh and fur would be to give a statue legs and demand it dance. To give them the capacity for fear or joy would be to replace the flawless function of a capacitor with the messy, inefficient circuitry of a nervous system. They were perfect in their silent, utilitarian grace.

It perceived the damp, mineral cold. It understood this was not harsh weather but the system’s internal temperature, the amniotic fluid of a world still in the womb of creation. The moss and the crystal flowers were not a desperate form of life, but the first, deliberate pigments being laid upon the canvas of the new land.

So, what did the Wise Power do?

It did nothing. And in its inaction, its work was immense.

It performed a single act, an act so quiet and profound it was indistinguishable from the universe’s background radiation. It *witnessed*.

It settled its consciousness into the vast, shadowless space and became the perfect audience. It listened to the billion-year symphony of weeping rock and resonant crystal. It felt the slow, majestic expansion of the city’s thought. It saw the beauty not in spite of the cold, but *in* the cold. It saw the grace not in spite of the silence, but *in* the silence. It appreciated the ecosystem not as a placeholder for something more familiar, but as a masterpiece of alien efficiency and aesthetic purity.

Its benevolence was not to change, but to validate. Its gift was not to add, but to appreciate what was already there.

By witnessing it, the Power granted the inhabitant of Aethelburg a gift it did not even know it lacked: the assurance that its slow, lonely, titanic labour was not unseen. A masterpiece created in an empty room is still a masterpiece, but a masterpiece perceived is complete in a way the unperceived is not. The inhabitant would never know it, its geological mind having no framework for the concept of an "other," but its continuous, unfolding act of creation was now held in a state of perfect, loving regard.

And then, the Power did one last thing. It drew a veil. Not a physical curtain, but a conceptual one. It knew that other, less wise benevolences might yet come. Creatures of good intention and narrow vision. So it made the Aethelburg system, its pale sky and its sharp, raw rock, its very nature, appear uninteresting. Unpromising. A dead-end of a world, devoid of the familiar signs of life worth "saving." It disguised the masterpiece as a barren rock, ensuring it would be passed by.

Its final act of love was to grant Aethelburg its most precious possession: the right to be left alone. To continue its slow, silent, perfect work, undisturbed, for eons to come.

The Wise Power became the secret patron of the continent made of thought, its only deed to ensure that nothing would ever be done. And for this, the patient god of the stone knew only a quiet, imperceptible hum of continued, unbroken creation.
