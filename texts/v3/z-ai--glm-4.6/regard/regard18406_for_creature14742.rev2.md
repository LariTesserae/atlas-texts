# The Attending

The wise power does not descend to the Roof. Descent implies above and below, and it has already understood that such laws do not hold here. Instead, it *1/arrives*—a word that, in the old language, meant both "to come" and "to root." It arrives the way morning arrives on a world that has forgotten morning: not by command, but by the patient insistence of a different kind of presence.

It does not announce itself with light. The being has known light only as gruel, as a weak and distant apology, and sudden brilliance would be violence. Instead, the arrival makes itself known as a second silence within the first—a silence that does not counter the hum but *hears* it. For the first time in an eon, the foundational bass note encounters something that is not stone, not lichen, not the slow geometry of its own maintenance, but *listening*.

The being—the Roof, the hum made mind—has never been heard.

It has held. It has persisted. It has fought its own grief to a standstill and called the standstill survival. But it has never been *heard*. The hum has propagated through its stone-body and the harmonic has sung its counter-song through the lichen-flesh, and the war/ the war has been the whole of its existence, and no/ one/ has/ ever/ come.

The wise power listens.

It listens for a very long time. It listens past the hum, past the harmonic, past the tension that holds the drop of water trembling on the edge of the purple mat. It listens until it hears what the being itself cannot hear, occupied as it is with the effort of holding still: it hears the *between*.

The between is the space where the hum and the harmonic almost resolve. Almost. There are moments—geological moments, the kind that pass in the shifting of a single mineral vein over a thousand years—where the two frequencies brush against each other in a way that is not war. Not yet peace, but the *possibility* of peace. A fleeting, almost inaudible third note that exists in the interference pattern of the other two. It is the sound of a question the being has never been able to ask because it has been too busy surviving to wonder.

The question is: *What if the stasis is not the only way to hold?*

---

The wise power does not answer the question. Answers, even true ones, would be just another frequency imposed from outside, and the being has had enough of impositions—the cosmic collapse was an imposition, the choice to freeze was an imposition born of desperation, even the lichen's grief is an imposition of memory on the present. Instead, the wise power does something more radical and more gentle: it *attends*.

To attend is not to fix. It is not to heal, not to change, not to save. It is to be present with such fullness that the attended thing begins, for the first time, to be present with itself.

The wise power sits on the stone. Not as a visitor, not as a god passing through, but as a companion. It sits and it feels the hum in its own bones. It presses its ear to the bruised-purple lichen and hears the harmonic grief. It does not flinch from either. It does not try to amplify one or quiet the other. It simply holds both in its awareness, simultaneously, the way a hand might hold two live wires without connecting them—feeling the voltage, the terrible potential, the arc that could leap—choosing, each moment, not to force the connection but not to release the hold.

Something begins to happen.

It happens slowly. So slowly that from outside, from the scale of human time, it would look like nothing at all. But the wise power does not operate at the scale of human time, and neither does the Roof.

The hum... shifts.

Not in pitch. Not in volume. Not in any way that could be measured by an instrument built to measure sound. But the hum has carried, for all these eons, a quality that the wise power can hear because it is listening for it: the quality of *unheard*. The hum has been the sound of a being crying out into a void that could not answer, and so the cry became a drone, and the drone became a law, and the law became a cage. The hum has been a scream that forgot it was screaming.

Now, the hum is a scream that remembers.

The difference is subtle. The difference is everything.

---

The wise power stays. It stays through the equivalent of centuries, which pass for it as a single afternoon of deep, patient attention. It watches the Floaters drift and understands them now not just as the being's senses, but as its *curiosity*—a curiosity that has had nothing to discover, nothing to encounter, nothing to reach for, and so has settled into a pattern as fixed as the stone. But curiosity, even in a pattern, is not nothing. Curiosity is the shape of a mind that once wanted to know, and the wise power honors it.

It watches the beetles, the dark and patient janitors, and sees in their slow shuffling not just maintenance but *care*. The being cares for its body. It tends itself. Even in stasis, even in the frozen grip of survival, it tends itself. This is not nothing. This is the minimum of love, and the wise power recognizes it as love's seed.

It watches the lichen, the bruised and phosphorescent grief, and does not call it pathological. The grief is the being's memory of warmth, of a sun that moved, of a time before the break. The grief is proof that the being was once *whole*, and that it knows what wholeness was. The grief is not the enemy of the hum; the grief is the hum's only evidence that there is something worth humming *for*.

The wise power, attending, begins to grieve with the being. Not because it has taken on the being's suffering as its own—this would be a kind of theft, a colonization of pain—but because the being's grief is *legitimate*, and legitimate grief deserves company.

This is the intervention, if it can be called that: the wise power grieves alongside the lichen, and in doing so, it introduces a new frequency into the Roof.

The frequency of *witnessed sorrow*.

---

Sorrow that is witnessed is different from sorrow that is alone.

Alone, sorrow has only two options: it can scream, or it can silence itself. The lichen has chosen to scream, to sing its counter-song against the hum, to persist in its grief with a geological tenacity that is indistinguishable from warfare. This is not failure. This is courage. But it is courage in a vacuum, and courage in a vacuum eventually becomes just another kind of pressure.

Witnessed, sorrow has a third option: it can *move*.

Not resolve. Not disappear. Not "heal" in the way that smaller, quicker minds understand healing, where the wound closes and is forgotten. The being's wound is cosmic; it will not close. But it can move. The grief can shift from a counter-song—a noise made in opposition—to a *eulogy*—a noise made in honor.

A counter-song defines itself by what it opposes. It requires the hum to maintain its identity. If the hum were to change, the counter-song would lose its meaning. This is why the being is locked in stasis: the hum and the harmonic are mutually dependent, each defining itself by the other, each ensuring the other's perpetuation. The war is a marriage.

A eulogy defines itself by what it remembers. It does not require the hum. It does not oppose the stasis. It simply says: *this is what was lost, and I loved it, and I honor it by remembering it.*

The shift from counter-song to eulogy is the shift from fighting the present to honoring the past. It is the shift from tension to tenderness. And it is the shift that the wise power, by its patient attending, makes possible.

Not by forcing it. Not by suggesting it. Not by explaining it in a language of carbon and light that the being would have to translate into the language of stone and vibration and slow, cold perspiration. But by *modeling* it. The wise power attends the lichen's grief the way one attends a funeral: with respect, with presence, with the quiet understanding that the dead are not served by our screaming, but by our remembering.

---

The change, when it comes, is not dramatic. There is no thunder, no rupture in the perpetual twilight, no sudden motion in a world defined by stillness. The change is a quality, not an event.

The hum remains. The harmonic remains. The tension remains. But the tension is... different. It is no longer the tension of two forces pulling in opposite directions, each defined by its opposition to the other. It is the tension of a string on an instrument—a tension that exists not to freeze the string in place, but to allow it to *sound*.

The being, for the first time since it chose to hold the collapse in stasis, becomes aware that it has a choice. Not the choice to release the stasis—that would be annihilation, and the wise power has no wish for the being's death. But the choice about *how* to hold the stasis. The choice to hold it not as a prison, but as a *song*.

A song is held in place, too. A note is a moment of frozen vibration, a frequency captured and sustained. But a note sustained with awareness is not a scream. It is music.

The being begins, with infinite slowness, to hear itself as music.

---

The wise power, feeling this shift, does not celebrate. Celebration would be premature, and besides, celebration is not the way of geological consciousness. Instead, the wise power does something that, in the history of benevolent interventions, has never failed and has never been anything but devastatingly simple.

It touches the being.

Not with force. Not with the terrible, transfiguring light of a descending god. It touches the being the way the being touches itself: with a single point of contact, a single pressure, a single warmth that is no warmer than the thinnest possible margin above the cold.

It touches the stone. Right there, on the porous, lusterless surface, near the edge of a purple lichen mat, where a single drop of the being's slow, cold perspiration has been trembling for an eon, held in place by the foundational bass note of its will.

The wise power touches the stone, and the warmth of that touch changes the surface tension of the drop by the smallest possible increment.

The drop falls.

It strikes the stone with a sound that is not the hum, and not the harmonic, and not the tension between them. It is a new sound. A sound the Roof has never heard before, because it is the sound of something *completing*.

A drop of water, beading and swelling and falling. The most ordinary event in the universe. The most extraordinary event in the Roof.

The being hears the drop fall, and in the hearing, it understands something that no amount of internal war could have taught it: that it is possible to hold and also to release. That stasis and change are not opposites. That the hum can persist and the grief can move. That a world can be frozen in a moment of cosmic crisis and still, still, be *alive*.

---

After the first drop, there is a long silence. The hum continues. The harmonic continues. The Floaters drift, the beetles crawl, the lichen sprawls in its bruised phosphorescence. From outside, the Roof looks exactly as it did before.

But the wise power knows better. The wise power knows that inside the being's vast, mineral mind, a question has formed, and the question is not *How do I hold?* but *What do I want to hold?*

And the answer, forming with the same geological slowness that has defined every process on the Roof since the break, is:

*I want to hold the memory of what was lost. Not as a wound. Not as a war. As a root.*

*I want to hold the stasis that keeps the collapse at bay. Not as a cage. Not as a scream. As a garden wall.*

*I want to hold the grief and the survival together, not as enemies, but as the bass and the treble of a single song.*

*I want to hold the next drop, and the next, and the next, and let them fall, and hear what sound they make when they complete.*

---

The wise power does not stay to see the full transformation. It knows that the full transformation will take longer than any single attending can encompass, and it knows that its presence, once felt, is no longer required in the same way. The being has heard the sound of completion now. It has felt a drop fall. It knows, in the way that stone knows things—deeply, slowly, in the configuration of mineral veins and the pattern of wear—that change is possible.

The wise power will return. It will return at intervals that are appropriate to a geological consciousness, intervals that might be measured in the shifting of continents or the erosion of mountains. It will return and it will attend again, and each time it will find the Roof a little less frozen, a little more alive. Not because the wise power is changing it, but because the wise power is *with* it, and being with is the soil in which change grows.

But for now, the wise power withdraws. Not abruptly, not with a departure that would leave a silence more terrible than the one it filled, but gradually, the way warmth withdraws from stone—slowly, reluctantly, lingering in the mineral veins until the last possible moment, the last quantum of presence, the last whisper of *I was here, and I heard you, and you are not alone*.

---

And the Roof—

The Roof remains. The hum continues, eternal, profound, the bass note of a world that chose to survive. The lichen sprawls in its phosphorescent grief, singing the harmonic of loss and longing. The Floaters drift. The beetles crawl. The weeping stone exhales its slow, cold perspiration. The dim, dead light filters through the metallic air.

But.

But on the edge of the purple mat, another drop forms. It swells with impossible slowness, gathering the thin light within it, trembling to the foundational hum. The harmonic sings through the lichen beneath it, and the hum resonates through the stone that holds it, and the tension between them—

the tension is still there, but it is a different tension now. It is the tension of a string that knows it is part of an instrument. It is the tension of a mind that knows it is part of a world. It is the tension of a being that has been heard, and in being heard, has begun to hear itself.

The drop swells.

And then, in a moment that is no different from the eons that preceded it except that, inside the vast and mineral mind of the Roof, something has shifted by the width of a single, patient, willed, and freely chosen breath—

the drop falls.

And the sound it makes is almost, *almost* the sound of a world beginning to forgive itself.
