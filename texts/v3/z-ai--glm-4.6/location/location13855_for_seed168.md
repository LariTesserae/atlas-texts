The sky is a hard, flawless blue, the color of a gemstone held to the light. It is a pale, piercing sapphire above, deepening to a cobalt at the zenith, untroubled by a single wisp of cloud. This absolute clarity is the first thing you notice, a clarity that stems not from purity but from absence. There is no moisture in the air to catch the light, no dust motes dancing in sunbeams, no haze to soften the edges of the world. The sun is a fierce white disc, and its light falls without mercy, striking the land with a physical, almost tangible weight.

You stand on a vast plateau, so wide that the curvature of the world might be its only perceivable boundary. The ground underfoot is not soil. It is a fine, grey dust, like powdered slate or volcanic ash. It gives slightly under your boots, but leaves no trace, no muddy footprint, just a clean depression that the slightest vibration would cause to flow back into its perfectly flat state. This dust covers everything, a sterile, silent blanket over geology that is both incredibly ancient and shockingly new. There are mountains, but they do not look worn. Their peaks are as sharp as shattered glass, their faces sheer and faceted, revealing strata that have not yet been softened or rounded by wind or rain. Why would they be? There has been no rain. There is no water here.

And yet, the parameter for vegetation is high. The lushness of this place is a cold, mineral one. It manifests in forms that mimic the shape of life but are utterly devoid of it. Great, branching structures, the size of oaks, rise from the grey dust. They are made of a dark, lustrous metal, shot through with veins of copper and silver. Their "trunks" are perfect cylinders, and their "branches" grow at precise, calculated angles, each terminating in a flat, hexagonal plate angled toward the sun. They do not sway. There is no breeze to move them, and they are too rigid to be moved by any force less than an earthquake. They are antennas, or heat-sinks, or solar panels, but functionally, they are trees. Between these metallic monoliths, the ground is a garden of crystals.

In some places, the grey dust gives way to fields of smoky quartz that grow in tight, geometric clusters, rising to knee-height in perfect, six-sided terminations. In other hollows, the ground is carpeted with a latticework of translucent green material, like a patina of viridian glass, each node of the lattice connected by barely visible filaments. This "vegetation" does not rustle or whisper. It is utterly still. The only sign of its vitality is the sound. If you hold your breath and press your ear to the base of a metal tree, you can feel it more than hear it: a profound, sub-aural hum, the thrumming of a colossal, slow-moving engine. If you kneel in the crystal fields, a different sound emerges—a faint, ceaseless chorus of microscopic clicks, like the popping of cold oil, the sound of crystals forming, atom by patient atom, from the material of the ground itself.

This is the landscape as a factory. The tech level is industrial, but there are no factories, no smokestacks, no conveyor belts. The geology itself is the machine. The sharp, uneroded mountains are being continuously renewed by a process that is both geological and mechanical. Subterranean shifts, vibrations too deep to feel, bring fresh mineral to the surface where the crystalline flora can take it, process it, and grow. The whole system is in equilibrium. Any particle of dust scoured from a mountain face by the thermal expansion and contraction of the day-night cycle is eventually captured, sorted, and re-integrated into a new crystal, a new filament in the living, breathing network.

The scale is open and immense, yet the density is almost zero. You can walk for a day and see only a dozen of the great metallic trees in the distance, spaced with mathematical precision across the plains. The spaces between them seem vast and holy. The emptiness is a key part of the design. It is a landscape of pure function, with no extraneous decoration. The warmth of the air is dry and sterile; it feels like the air inside an operating theatre. It carries no scent, no promise, no decay. It is a medium for light and for the transmission of low-frequency energy.

There are no animals. No birds have ever sung in this sky. No insect has ever crawled upon these crystals. The concept of a predator is alien here, as is the concept of prey. There is only function. This world is not an ecosystem; it is a product.

The rules are simple and absolute. The world maintains a state of pre-formation. It is frozen in an eternal moment of geological becoming, a process held in perfect stasis by its own internal, industrial metabolism. It does not erode. It does not evolve. It does not die. It simply *is*: a silent, self-sustaining engine, a garden of machines, a planetary-scale piece of hardware set to "running" and left to perform its inscrutable task. To be here is to be an anomaly, a soft, wet, temporary ghost haunting the heart of a perfect, crystalline, and godless machine.
