The sound has no source and no end. It is simply the condition of the air, a presence as fundamental as the warmth on your skin. It is a low, omnipresent thrumming, a vibration felt in the bones and the teeth, punctuated by the sigh of a billion slow leaks and the periodic, deep *gong* of something immense shifting somewhere far beneath the stone. This is the sound of the world in its steady state, the noise of a function that has never known a beginning and will never know an end.

This is a basin, a wide, low depression cradled by a distant ring of monolithic mountains that are sharp and dead against a normal, bright sky. The floor of the basin is not sand or soil, but a vast, flat pavement of dark, vitrified rock, worn smooth by aeons of unseen friction. It is the color of wet slate, and it is always, perpetually wet, though it never rains. A warm, humid breath hangs in the air, thickening around you. Every surface is beaded with a constant, gentle sweat of condensation, the only evidence of the intermittent water that defines this place. The air smells of ozone, hot metal, and clean, wet stone.

The landscape is a machine. This is its industrial nature, expressed not in artifact but in geology. Rising from the flat floor are titans of fused, pumice-like stone. They are not jagged or violent, but immense, rounded columns that taper towards the sky, like anthills built by a god. These are the Collectors. Their porous, mineral flesh is hydrophilic; they pull the moisture from the humid air with an unceasing, passive thirst. The condensation that forms on their dark skins does not fall, but is absorbed into the stone's internal structure, a process so slow that to watch it is to watch a mountain breathe.

From the bases of the Collectors, a network of channels, arterial conduits a few feet deep and perfectly smooth, webs out across the basin floor. They are not carved by water, but are the ancient, established pathways for something else. Following them leads you to the lowlands' other primary feature: the Vents. These are wide, shallow bowls, perhaps a hundred yards across, where the dark pavement gives way to a crust of delicate, crystalline growths, like rime frost made of silica and iron. Here, the sound is different. The hum coalesces into a tangible, rising pressure, and a silent, invisible gas, heavier than air, seeps from the ground and spills over the edges of the bowl, hugging the ground as it flows, inexorably, towards the Collectors to begin the cycle anew. It is a slow, planetary-scale engine of atmospheric chemistry, running on geothermal warmth and the physics of condensation. The process is in perfect balance, and so, it never changes.

There is no plant life. There is no green, no chlorophyll, no cycle of growth and decay. Life here is an anomaly, a rare intrusion upon this mineral perfection. On occasion, moving through the slow rivers of venting gas, you might see one of the Fauna. They are Grazers: slow, multi-legged things that resemble a mobile cluster of geodes. Their bodies are faceted crystals, translucent at the edges, allowing you to see the pulsing, silicate fluids within. They do not eat, not in any recognizable sense. They drift upon the currents of heavy gas, and as they pass over the crystalline fields of the Vents, their undersides emit a faint, acidic solvent that dissolves the mineral accretions, which they then absorb through their crystal skin. They are as much a part of the machine as the Collectors themselves, another component in the unchanging equilibrium. Their movements are glacial, their presence so sparse that days can pass without a sighting.

The scale is open. You can stand on a rise between two major Conduits and see for miles, the Collectors standing like silent, patient titans under a warm sun. The horizon is a hard, definite line. The density is sparse; there are vast, empty stretches of flat, wet pavement between the machine's major components, spaces that amplify the feeling of immense, patient emptiness.

To be here is to exist inside a functioning, artificial organ that nature forgot to stop building. It is loud with the sound of its own perfect operation, and yet utterly still in its unchanging routine. It is warm, damp, and devoid of any life you would recognize. It is a place of profound, unsettling loneliness, the loneliness of a conscious mind trapped inside the body of a god-like machine that is forever, and only, itself.
