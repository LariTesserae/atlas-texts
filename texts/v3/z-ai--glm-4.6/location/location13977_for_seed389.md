***

This is a place that builds itself. Not quickly, nor with any discernible haste, but with the immense, geological patience of a crystal forming in a silent, pressurized dark. You stand on a gantry of warm, dark metal that is not bolted to the ground but *extrudes* from it, a seamless part of the structure. This is the city of the High Caldera, the Choral Forge, a place of constant, silent becoming.

The terrain is the city and the city is the terrain. To call it built is a misnomer; it is closer to grown. Extreme elevation has you standing on a mesa placed atop a world of cloud, a plateau shattered into a thousand spires and deep shard-like canyons. But the spires are not of rock. They are alloys and crystals, born of forces deep below. Great, dark-grey latticeworks of what looks like basalt but rings with a metallic chime when struck rise thousands of feet into the thin air. Their surfaces are not uniform. They are glistening with slow intrusions of softer, brassy metals that ooze from fissures like cooling magma, hardening into veils and intricate filigree. Elsewhere, faceted crystals of quartz and amethyst, large as houses, push their way out of the metal matrices, their surfaces clean and sharp, as if just excavated. This is the rawness of the world: an emergent geology that is also architecture. The density is overwhelming; the canyons are not empty voids but chasms crammed with buttresses, bridges, and soaring, unsupported archways, all grown from the same source, leaving only narrow, vertical slits of sky.

Heat is the first and most constant of its gestures. The temperature is intense but not fiery, a dry, all-encompassing warmth that radiates from the very substance of the structures underfoot and overhead. It presses on you, thinning the air to a keen, sharp edge where breath comes short. The light, though from a normal sun, is transformed by this heat and altitude. It bleaches the uppermost spires to a stark white but is quickly absorbed by the darker city-body below, casting the depths into a cool, permanent twilight. The air is stained by a perpetual, faint, ochre haze, not of smoke or pollution, but of microscopic particles shed from the city’s constant growth.

And it is damp. The heat draws a constant, thick condensation from the thin air. Every surface weeps. Water gathers on the horizontal planes of the lattices, forming dark, mirror-pools before finding a microscopic crack to drip from. The sound of this place is the sound of dripping. A million, billion slow, rhythmic plinks and plops echo in the immense canyons, creating a percussive, ever-shifting symphony of moisture. This is the only sound that breaks the profound quiet. The industrial aspect of this world is silent. No great gears turn, no engines roar. There is only the deep, almost sub-audible hum that you feel in your teeth, a resonant frequency that seems to vibrate up from the core of the world itself, the thrum of the massive, unseen engine of its own creation.

There is no plant life. Biology never found a foothold here. The ecology is mineral. The rare fauna are things that skitter in the deep, damp reaches where the warmth is most concentrated. They are chitinous things, but their chitin is a ceramic compound of silica and iron. They have six legs, but move with the stutter-stop, lurching certainty of a machine. They are chemovores, feeding on the effluent of the city’s growth: the sludgy, metal-rich runnels of water that collect in the lowest levels, or the soft, newly-extruded metals which they scrape away with mouthparts of hardened diamond. They are as rare as precious gems, sighted only as a glint of motion at the edge of vision, a fleeting shape of daunting alienness. To see one is to know this world has its own life, however strange and peripheral it may seem to the central act of construction.

The true wonder, the true strangeness, lies in the rules of this place. Nothing here is ever assembled. It is all simply *arranged*. Huge, seamless sections of a newly-formed wall will detach and slide silently along an unseen track, fitting themselves into a new configuration over the course of a week. A spire will grow impossibly tall, then begin to gently curve, its apex lowering over decades to form the roof of a new cavernous space. It is a city designed by a mind that thinks in eras, not years. The industrial technology is not one of manufacturing, but of guidance—vast, planetary-scale resonators that tune the pressure and elemental flow from the world’s mantle, directing the city’s growth like a conductor guiding a silent, crystalline orchestra. The dynamic is so slow as to be nearly imperceptible from day to day, but over a lifetime, the map of the city is utterly redrawn. Nothing is ever demolished, only subsumed, re-grown, or built over in successive, patient layers.

To live here, if anything truly does, is to exist in a cathedral that is forever building its own foundations. You are dwarfed by a process so immense and deliberate it makes your own frantic existence seem like a brief, feverish spasm. The only constants are the heat on your skin, the taste of metallic moisture on your lips, the low hum in your bones, and the perpetual, dripping music of a world that is not yet, and is already, complete.
