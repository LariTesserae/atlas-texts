The air is the first thing you notice: a thick, warm press against the skin, heavy with the scent of ozone and something vaguely organic, like honey and wet clay. It is a dim twilight that holds no immediate source, a soft, grey luminescence that seems to emanate from the atmosphere itself. This light reveals a space that is both a room and a landscape, a high plateau suspended within a bowl of unseen sky.

You are standing on a floor that is not ground but raw material. It is a dark, metallic-grey substance, shot through with veins of a faint, pulsing lavender. It is called geolattice. Its surface is not weathered; it is violent and fresh. Edges are razor-sharp, facets are clean, and formations rise in impossible, angular spires without a single hint of a rounded corner. There is no dust, no soil, no sand. Here and there, the lattice weeps, glistening beads of a thicker-than-water fluid coalescing on its faces and tracing slow paths down the unblemished geometry before pooling in shallow, dish-like depressions. This is the wetness that defines the place. The fluid is slightly viscous, clinging to the fingers with a faint, cool tackiness, smelling of the same honeyed clay.

This is the only water this place knows, and it does not erode. The geolattice is either too new or too hard to be worn down. The construction is ongoing; you can feel it in the subsonic hum that trembles constantly through the soles of your feet, a note just beneath the edge of hearing. This is the sound of un-creation, of the world being born.

Vegetation is a Clasped, an afterthought. It does not grow from soil but directly from the lattice itself. Sparse, solitary things sprout like anomalies. A thicket of fluted, copper-coloured stalks, each no taller than a person, stands silent in a wide-open plaza of stone. They do not move with the still air, but their tips glow with a soft, intermittent amber light. Lichen, in patches of iridescent green, clings to a north-facing cliff, its colour deepening and fading in a slow, rhythmic pulse. There are no leaves, no grass, no softness of that kind. The only softness is the gleaming moisture.

The emptiness of the flora is starkly contrasted by the abundance of fauna. Life teems here, a constant, chittering, buzzing, skittering presence filling the sparse spaces with movement. They are all drawn to the water. In the shallow pools, dart-shaped creatures the size of a human thumb flicker through the viscous fluid, their bodies transparent lines of blue light, absorbing the nutrient soup directly through their skin. They are called Filters, and their silent, frantic gamboling is the base of a visible food chain.

Larger things hunt them. Flat, manta-like beings with wings of polished obsidian glide silently a foot above the pools, elongating their mouths into proboscises to sip the Filters from the surface. They move with an unnerving, silent grace, their only sound a slight *shlick* as they retract their feeding tubes. On higher ground, things built of chitin and articulated joint scuttle in the shadows, flinging themselves from spire to spire. They are Weavers, trailing gossamer threads that harden on contact with the air, building delicate, geometric nests among the sharpest points. Their sounds are a constant, dry clicking, like a thousand claws on a stone floor.

The loudest sound, however, is a low, resonant croaking that echoes from the largest structures. These are the Geophages, immense, toad-like beings whose warty,(semi-translucent) hides reveal the slow movement of the nutrient fluid within their own bodies. They press their broad, bellies against the weeping geolattice, humming a resonant chord seems to coax the fluid from the stone, drinking it directly from the source. Their collective croaking is a constant, throaty bassline to the entire soundscape, a biological process amplified to the level of ambient music.

Scattered amongst the raw geology are the built elements, but they are not ruins. They are functional, humming instruments of the world's creation. A delicate archway, made of light, stands in the center of one clear space, flickering as a Weaver flies through it. A single, black monolith rises from a pool, its surface a placid screen displaying scrolling glyphs of an unknown language. These are not remnants of a lost civilization; they are the operating system of this living space, the caretakers and the controls. They regulate the temperature, maintain the fluid's chemistry, and perhaps guide the emergent evolution.

The rules are simple and absolute, dictated by the unseen technology that suffuses the place. This is a Forgesphere, a nursery at the scale of a cathedral. Its purpose is not to support a pre-existing ecosystem, but to *generate* one. The emergent, ungeological nature of the ground is intentional—it is a substrate, a foundation still being written upon. The sparse vegetation is the beta-test for photosynthesis under a synthetic sky. The abundant fauna are the first draft of a food chain, experimenting with forms that can metabolise the artificial nutrient broth. The loudness is the sound of a world booting up. The high elevation is a detachment from any planetary norm; this is a place suspended, a petri dish for worlds yet to come. You are not a visitor in a landscape; you are an observer in a chrysalis, standing on the raw, damp, and humming edge of creation.
