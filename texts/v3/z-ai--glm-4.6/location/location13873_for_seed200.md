The air is the first thing you notice, not as a presence but as a substance. It is not a gas so much as a warm, thin broth, thick with the smell of wet earth, ozone, and a cloying sweetness like overripe fruit and honey. To breathe is to drink it, feeling it coat your throat and settle heavy in your lungs. The heat is ambient, geologic, a pressure radiating up from the ground itself, as relentless and even as a buried heart. It is a heat that does not come from a sun hidden somewhere far above, but from the world's own metabolism.

You stand on a floor that is neither stone nor soil. It is spongy, yielding, and thrums with a low, deep-frequency vibration that is felt more in the soles of your feet and the base of your skull than heard with the ears. This is the carpet of the world: a vast, interlocking, fibrous mat of what looks like a collision of fungus and kelp. Tens of thousands of fibrous stalks, each as thick as an arm, grow up from the substrate, intertwining to form a springy, damp surface. Between the stalks, a glowing phosphorescence pulses, a soft, chartreuse luminescence that provides the only light. It is the light of a deep sea, but on land.

This carpet drapes over a landscape of raw, uneroded forms. Through gaps in the living floor, you can see the true geology: a foamy, porous stone, like a colossal field of cooled pumice, but dark grey and shot through with veins of crystalline minerals that glitter in the bioluminescent gloom. These structures rise in spires, slump in hills, and crack into vast, shallow canyons, yet they show no signs of weathering. Their edges are sharp, their surfaces unfinished. It is a landscape that has only just been born, yet it is ancient. The slow, seismic swell from below is constantly pushing new land into being, a geologic exhalation that is the only engine of change in this realm.

And the living carpet meets it instantly. Wherever the mineral foam emerges, the fibrous life surges to cover it, a colonization so rapid it feels simultaneous. There is no period of bare rock, no slow lichen-and-moss succession. The world is a system in perfect, unchanging equilibrium, forever in the act of being born, forever clothed in its mature, living flesh.

Water seeps from everything. It is not rain, for the sky is an impassable, pearlescent ceiling of mist. The water simply condenses out of the soupy air, weeping from the mineral spires, running in rivulets down the stalks of the fibrous forest, and pooling in the hollows of the spongy floor. The sound is therefore a constant, wet music: the gentle *drip... drip... drip...*, the faint, distant gurgle of a hidden channel, and the pervasive, wet *squelch* of the floor itself under any weight.

But these gentle sounds are merely the baseline. The true soundscape is a cacophony, a wall of biological noise that never abates. It is a loudness without climax or crescendo, a constant, droning chorus of billions of creatures. A high-pitched, insectile sibilance that vibrates in the teeth. The guttural, resonant croaks of things moving through the wet, fibrous tunnels below. The percussive clicks and whistles of organisms navigating the three-dimensional space. It is the sound of an ecosystem so dense that it has become a single, roaring entity.

The fauna is overwhelming not in its diversity, but in its sheer volume and integration. This is not a world of distinct creatures hunting and fleeing in the open. It is a world that *is* creature. Swarms of small, pale, segmented things flow like water through the fibrous mat, constantly cleaning and consuming detritus. Gelatinous, slug-like beings the size of pigs glide through the wider channels, their translucent skins revealing the pulsating organs within. They are part-coral, part-jellyfish, part-mollusk, perfectly adapted to this humidity and dimness. Above, through the thicker stalks, long, sinuous shapes with vestigial wings maneuver with a series of sharp, clicks of sonar, their bodies covered in sensory hairs that detect the faintest shifts in the dense, moving air. They do not fly so much as swim through the thick atmosphere. There is no predation in the typical sense; there is only consumption, absorption, and reintegration into the whole. A creature does not die of old age; it is simply reabsorbed into the vibrant, living matrix that spawned it.

To walk here is to move through a being that is a place. Everywhere you look, the visual field is saturated. Layers of glowing fibres, layers of crawling life, layers of gleaming, wet mineral. Nothing is still, yet the overall picture never changes. A stalk might pulse its light brighter for a moment, a swarm might split around your presence and reform on the other side, but the composition is fixed. This is a captured moment in time, stretched into eternity. A landscape in a chrysalis, forever on the verge of emerging into something else, yet eternally, contentedly, frozen in this state of luminous, wet, and thunderous becoming.
