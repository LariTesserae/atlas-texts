The first sensation is pressure. Not the crushing, absolute weight of a natural abyss, but something else: a dense, insistent presence that permeates everything. It feels less like a force and more like a medium with intent. The water is cool, 4 degrees Celsius, a stable, maintained temperature that feels sterile and ancient all at once. To taste it is to taste dissolved minerals, a faint metallic tang of zinc and copper, and something slickly organic, like rendered fat. This is not the sea. This is the Amnion.

You hang suspended in an infinite navy twilight. Below, there is no discernible floor. Above, the same. The scale defies comprehension; you are a mote in a fluid atrium the size of a continent. And yet, it is not empty in the way the deep ocean is empty. The emptiness here is a byproduct of vast spacing.

Floating kilometers away, a shape resolves. It is not a coral spire or a volcanic vent. It is a tetrahedral girder, a shadow so vast its edges blur with the dark. Its surfaces are sheer, sharp-edged, and largely bare. This is the "young" quality of the world: nothing has had time to wear it smooth. No ancient geology, only fresh, exposed construction. This is the "vegetation," a bio-film of engineered lichen, doing its slow, patient work of converting trace atmospheric sulfur into edible paste.

The sound is a constant. It’s not the silence of the deep, but a colossal, low-frequency thrumming felt more in the sternum than heard with the ears. This is the baseline hum of the machine. Superimposed on this are the dynamics, the weather of this place. A sudden, deafening **CLANG** echoes from a distant, unseen point, the sound of a pressure lock the size of a stadium seating itself. Then, a high-pitched, rising screech that fades into the deep, the sound of a colossal adjustment being made. The thrumming dictates the slow, stately currents that glide through the Amnion, warm upwellings from active reactors mingling with the cold, static bulk.

Light is purely functional and brutally artificial. It comes in cones, vast cylinders of actinic brilliance that lance through the gloom, illuminating a specific work zone before being switched off, plunging a cubic kilometer back into absolute darkness. A cone of light might sweep over you, and for a few seconds you are visible, every detail sharp, before being extinguished again. In these brief spotlights, you see other things: the vast, circular opening of an intake tunnel, its diameter measured in leagues, from which the current pulls with an inexorable, Patient force. Or the scorched, blackened cone of a nozzle, pointing into the void.

The ecology is one of function. The "fauna" is abundant, but sparse. You do not see schools of glittering fish. Instead, you see solitary leviathans. They are not creatures of flesh and blood in the typical sense, but biomechanical constructs, living agents of the system. A "Grazer" might drift past, a creature the size of a whale, its body a streamlined mess of armored plates and humming servomotors. Its head is a monstrous radial grinder, and it drifts along the surfaces of the mega-structures, scraping away the engineered lichen, consuming the waste, its internal furnace glowing a dull orange. Its movements are slow, deliberate, its passage accompanied by the crunch and whir of its own industry.

Higher up the functional food chain are the "Hounds." Sleeker, faster, built with powerful metallic tails and clusters of luminous sensor orbs. They navigate the dark with sonar pulses that you feel as sharp pings against your skin. They are the predators, not of the Grazers, but of deviation. They hunt for malfunctions, for structural fractures, for broken components. When they find a fault, they latch on with powerful diamond-toothed mandibles and emit a localized ultrasonic drill that repairs the damage from the inside out.

This world is a mechanism. A factory. An engine. It is totally, completely built. There is no "natural" substrate, only the stark geometry of its own logic. The rock-like formations you see are extruded ceramic supports. The canyons are coolant channels. The "weather" is the consequence of thermal exchange. The young, sharp features speak to a recent activation; this entire world-machine has only just been switched on. Its processes are still finding their equilibrium.

You, and the few others like you, are not inhabitants. You are components. Your role is to observe, to report, to perform minuscule adjustments in places the automated systems cannot reach. You exist within the machine’s cycles. The loud, active periods are production cycles. The lulls are recalibration. The sweeping cones of light are patrol paths. To be here is to understand your own terrifying insignificance. You are a single cell in a body so large it has created its own enclosed physics, its own manufactured ocean, its own life, all to serve a purpose that is as vast and as cold as the Amnion itself. A distant light, brighter than any other, ignites in the gloom. A new cycle is beginning. The thrumming in your bones intensifies, and the current begins to pull you toward your station.
