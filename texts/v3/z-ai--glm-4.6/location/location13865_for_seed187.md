The sound is the first truth. It is not a sound that begins or ends; it simply is. A low, fundamental thrumming that vibrates up from the soles of your feet and resonates in the hollows of your bones. It is the timbre of the place, a frequency so constant that the silence it seems to contradict feels like the alien intrusion. It is the sound of a great machine, of a titanic engine turning at a geologic pace, and it is the sound of a deep and placid heartbeat.

You are inside. This is the fundamental spatial reality. There is no open sky here, only the ceiling. The scale is vast, a cathedral of biology, but bounded. You can, in theory, walk from one end to the other, though the journey would take days through chambers the size of amphitheaters and corridors that stretch like silent, naved boulevards. The walls are not stone or metal, but something in between, something ancient. They are the substance of the world, a dark, fibrous material with the sheen of oiled mahogany and the unyielding texture of petrified wood. To run a hand along it is to feel the grain of a life that has been stationary for millennia, an organism so colossal it has become the very bedrock of its environment. This is the end-state of erosion: not a valley worn to dust, but a life compressed and settled into its perfect, final architecture.

The air is cool and perpetually damp, a fine, tasteless mist hanging in the space. It is not rain, but condensation, the exhalation of the entire structure. The moisture sheens the walls in a perpetual film of sweat, collects in slow, bulbous drops that hang from the high, arched rafters for hours before finally letting go. The water parameter is not a feature of the weather but a function of the world’s respiration. It gathers in the great, sponge-like floor—a porous, springy carpet of accumulated, slow- fermenting detritus, springy underfoot and faintly sweet, smelling of wet earth and ozone.

There are no constructed things, yet the place is utterly industrial. At regular intervals, along the vast bole-like columns that support the ceiling, are great sphincters of vascular tissue, veined with darker colors like glowing magma. These are the pistons of this world. With a rhythm as slow as the tides they contract, pulling vast volumes of air from one chamber and forcing it into another. The sound they make is a deep, brassy, organic *whoosh-THUMP*. They are the bellows, the pumps, the regulators. Light is not piped in from a sun; it is manufactured. The same dark veins that run through the pumping valves pulse with a soft, internal, bluish-white luminescence, providing a constant, shadowless, and necessarily normal level of illumination. The light is the static charge of the system, the exhaust of the biological engine.

Within the vast, hollowed-out architecture of this total plant-life, the interior is almost empty. The sparseness is a function of the immense scale. A creature the size of a whale would be a fleeting mote in the central chamber. Yet fauna is abundant. They occupy the niches of the void. There are the Drifters, leathery-winged things that do not so much fly as glide on the constant vertical currents stirred by the great pistons, navigating by echoing clicks. There are reams of skittering, chitinous grazers that scrape nutrient-rich algae from the glowing veins on the walls. And there are the larger, solitary things, the Predators of the hollows, whose Territories are measured not in miles, but in the number of chambers between the pumping stations.

Everything is part of the same frozen equilibrium. The Structure produces the light that feeds the algae that feeds the grazers that feeds the predators, whose wastes then percolate down through the spongy floor to be reabsorbed by the roots of the world. It is a closed, perfect, and ceaseless industrial ecosystem. The sound you hear is the hum of the power plant, the cries of the workers, the rush of the conveyor belts, all rolled into one. There is no day or night, no change of season. It is a place removed from time, a groove worn so deep into reality that it has become the reality itself. This is not a place that grows; it is The Grown, and it has reached its final, stable form. You are standing inside a living fossil, a machine of ancient flesh and vegetable matter, and its thrumming is the only proof you have that it is, somehow, still alive.
