The sound is the first thing, and the last. It is not a thing you hear with your ears alone but a pressure that fills your lungs, that vibrates in the marrow of your bones. It is the groan of a planet’s tectonic plates being wrenched apart, the scream of superheated metal forced into impossible shapes, the endless, bass-heavy thrum of a heart the size of a moon. It is a deafening, constant note of creation happening too fast, a symphony of violence and manufacture without pause or crescendo. Here, silence is a forgotten concept.

You stand on a promontory that was not here yesterday. The ground under your feet is a shard of black, glassy rock, so sharp it threatens to piece the sole of your boot. It is young land. The erosion has not had time to soften its edges, to round its angles or blanket it in soil. It is fresh-born, still weeping a mineral-rich brine that stains the stone with streaks of iridescent oil. This is the rule of the place: everything new arrives with a wound.

The world is water. Vast, shallow, and unnervally warm. There is no horizon, only a gradation of light from a bruised plum-grey overhead to a near-total black in the depths. The sky is a low, heavy ceiling of steam and chemical haze, so thick that it blots out the stars and the sun, rendering the concept of day and night moot. The only light is a weak, ambient gloom, pierced by the world itself.

From this warm, churning sea, the land emerges. Not as islands or continents, but as a forest of spires. Thousands of them, erupting from the surface at chaotic angles, vast skeletal fingers of rust-coloured metal and dark, volcanic-looking obsidian. These are the artifacts of the built world, scattered like toys on a shore: the skeletal ribcage of a processing plant, its conveyor belts hanging limp like dead vines; the toppled Cyclopean heads of Unknown machines, half-submerged, their single unblinking oculi staring into the murk; solitary ventilation columns taller than any skyscraper, belching plumes of warm, sulphurous steam that merge instantly with the low ceiling.

The scale is open, yet the density is suffocating. You can see for kilometers across this churning, metallic archipelago, but every surface is saturated, layered, overgrown. This is the lushness. Vegetation clings to everything with the desperation of a drowning thing. It is not the green of forests on Earth. Here, life is a phosphorescent, electric efflorescence. Gnarled, kelp-like fronds, thick as a man’s arm, cascade down the iron spires in shimmering, silent waterfalls of turquoise and violet. Carpet-like colonies of glowing moss pulse with a soft, internal light, illuminating the underwater decay in rhythmic, silent breaths. Fungi, like crusts of shattered porcelain, sprout from the dripping rock, their caps glowing a sickly, chartreuse green. It is a jungle built of patience and poison, a silent, thoroughly alien ecosystem that has claimed the ruins of a god's workshop.

There are no animals. No birds to cry out against the roar, no fish to dart through the luminous weeds. The incessant, turbulent noise and the reality-warping energy of the place have proven too hostile for anything with a nervous system. The only motion, apart from the lapping of the chemical-laced water, is the slow, inexorable crawl of the flora, and the chaotic creation of the world itself.

This is the turbulence. This is the weirdness. The world is not stable. To look at the water for too long is to invite vertigo, as the currents knot and unknot with impossible speed. Sometimes, a new spire of metal will not rise, but simply *appear* in a flash of blinding, silent light, displacing a thousand tons of water with a thunderclap that is utterly swallowed by the greater roar. The geometry of the place feels fundamentally wrong; parallel lines drift apart, shadows don't fall where they should, and the air sometimes shimmers like a heat-haze in a vacuum.

You are a visitor to a process, a witness to a world being continuously, violently generated. The tech is industrial, but scaled beyond all comprehension. This is not a factory; it is the operating system of reality, stuck in a glitching loop of production. It extrudes land, it pumps the atmosphere warm, it creates the very light-filtering smog that steals its own view. The rules are simple: endure the noise, accept the newness, and don’t stand in one place for too long, lest you become part of the emergent landscape, another sharp, submerged feature waiting for the patient, glowing creep of the world’s only life to find you and cover you over.
