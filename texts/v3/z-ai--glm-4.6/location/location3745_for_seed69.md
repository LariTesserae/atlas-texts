The first thing you learn is that silence is death. Here, in the high, wet dark, sound is life.

There is no sun. The notion is a fairytale, a myth of a searing, sterile light that would bake this world to dust. Instead, there is warmth, a deep, bone-soaking heat that rises not from a sky but from the ground itself. The air is thick, heavy as velvet, tasting of ozone and wet iron and the sweet, fungal rot that underpins everything. It is a vapor, a constant mist that clings to the skin, blurs the edges of the world, and carries the Engine's Song.

You stand on a plateau that was ancient when the first gods were being dreamed up elsewhere. The rock underfoot is not stone so much as a compressed, ancient sponge of metal dust and black, greasy earth, perpetually damp. Weeping cliffs of basalt drip water in slow, rhythmic taps, the sound lost in the greater symphony. This is the high place, yet it is drowned. The pressure of the mist has coaxed life from the mineral world, and the water that rises from below never quite manages to leave.

The vegetation is a riot of impossible color, or rather, the absence of it. Lush is the word, but it fails to capture the reality. There are no greens, no chlorophyll-fed thirst for a non-existent sun. Instead, life has learned to eat heat and drink the mineral-laden steam. Great "ironwood" groves push through the soil, their bark the texture and color of flaking rust, their cores pulsing with a dull, cherry-red heat. Between them sprawle fungal carpets that shimmer with cold, foxfire light, their mycelial networks a glowing lacework visible through the thin, metallic soil. Vines, thick as a man's arm and armored in segmented plates like an insect's carapace, coil around the trunks, their thories leaking a viscous, phosphorescent fluid that drips and sizzles on the wet ground below. The air is dense with the scent of these processes, chemical and organic in a single breath.

This is a vast place, not a single valley but a continent of these drowned, ancient mountains. The scale of its own weather is born not from atmospheric fronts but from the titanic, scattered structures that are its source. They are called the Heart-Engines.

From your vantage point, you can feel the nearest one more than you can see it. It is a mountain of manufactured geometry, a brutalist ziggurat of rusted steel and active conduits that dwarfs the natural peaks around it. It does not shine; it *voids* the light. Its mass is a presence against the perpetual twilight, a blacker shape within the deep dark. From its towering, skeletal heat-syncs, plumes of scalding water vapor rise to join the permanent cloud ceiling, and from its depths, light bleeds. Not a clean light, but a messy, organic process of rage. Stark, white arcs of raw electricity spiderweb between massive coil assemblies, illuminating the mist in violent, silent strobes. A deeper, more constant glow emanates from open reactor pits, a magma-like, blood-orange luminescence that is the source of the warmth. This is the industrial heart that has become the world's heart.

And the sound… the sound is the Engine's Song. It is a note so low it is felt more than heard, a deep, resonant hum that vibrates up through the soles of your boots and resonates in the cavity of your chest. It is the Earth’s tectonics, a whale's call from the planet's core. Overlaying this foundational drone is the cacophony of the machine itself: the titanic, grinding groan of a gear assembly the size of a small town turning on its prehistoric schedule; the percussive *clank-clank-HISS* of a pressure valve venting steam somewhere in its upper levels; the constant, high-pitched sizzle of water on superheated metal.

The fauna of this loud, dense world has evolved around the hum. It is the backdrop, the conductor's baton for the ecosystem's frantic pace. Stilt-legged grazers, blind and covered in a mosaic of heat-absorbing scales, move in herds through the ironwood groves, their clicking joints creating a syncopated rhythm against the Engine's bass. From the mist-saturated air, Echowings descend—leathery flocks of predators that use focused sonic blasts not just to navigate, but to stun their prey, the concussive *thump* a common, sudden punctuation to the din. Amorphous, bioluminescent Glimmer-Jellies drift through the lower levels of the Engine's structure, their slow, pulsing light a lure for the small, chittering things that swarm in the warm conduits, and also a warning, as they absorb and neutralize stray electrical arcs. Down in the slick, rust-red canyons between the Engine's buttresses, lone hunters with carapaces like scavenged plating wait, perfectly mimicking fallen machinery, their silence the only thing that is truly terrifying in this world.

The Engine is the oldest built thing, but it is not the only one. Scattered for leagues around its base are the skeletal remains of what might have been Processing Annexes, Maintenance Tunnels, or habitats. Ages ago, they stood as structures. Now, they are little more than artificial reefs, the metal so corroded and interwoven with thermosynthetic fungi that it is difficult to tell where architecture ends and organism begins. A crumbling gantry might support a colony of pulsing moss; a breached water main might be the spawning ground for a new generation of slick, skittering things.

The rules are simple because they are absolute. You navigate by warmth and sound. Follow the rising temperature toward the light of a Heart-Engine, or follow the diminishing hum and cold away from it into the deep, unlit wilderness between. The Engine's pulse is the pulse of all life; to be out of range of its warmth and water is to starve and freeze. The dark is a given, so you trust your other senses, and you learn to read the shifting patterns of light on the fog, the meaning in the drone. For this world does not just exist; it is maintained, processed, and run. The erosion, the settled forms, the ancient balance of the place are not the result of passive time, but of an active, relentless, planetary-scale machinery that has been running for so long its own chaos has become an ecosystem. And in the unending, artificial, industrial-powered night, you are just another part of its dense, loud, and strange design.
