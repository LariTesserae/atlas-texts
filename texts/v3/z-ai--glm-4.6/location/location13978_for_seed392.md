The light has no source.

It is not the sun. There is no sun. There is only the brightness, a flat, unblinking white that emanates from the entirety of the sky-container. It is a perfect, maternal glare that bleaches out all shadows and holds the heat of a crucible against the skin. There is no dawn, no dusk, no variation. Time is not measured in light, but in the slow, unending drip of water.

You stand on a floor that is not earth. Beneath your feet is a material like a petrified plank, impossibly wide, dark with a somber grain like mahogany, yet cool and hard as stone to the touch. It is damp, always damp. A slick film of condensation covers everything, and the air is so thick with water vapor that each breath feels like drinking. The ground is not level; it rises into sharp, abrupt bluffs and falls into shallow, hand-carved basins. These features are young, their edges as crisp as if a gigantic adze just finished its work. There is no soil here, no sand, no natural substrate. It is all a single, continuous construct.

From the grain of the stone-wood, water weeps. It does not spring from a fissure or run in a channel; it exudes directly from the material, a slow, constant sweat that beads and grows until its own weight pulls it down in a solitary drop. The sound is the land's only voice: *plink... plink... plink...* in the basins, a soft, ceaseless percussion in the profound silence. There is no wind to rustle leaves, no distant animal cry. The quiet is an active presence, a weight that presses in on the ears.

The vegetation is lush, yet sparse. Great, solitary specimens erupt from the floor at wide intervals. There is no grass, no undergrowth, only the vast, empty, damp plains of litho-wood stretching between them. But the plants themselves are colossal. One might be a fern whose fronds are the size of sailing ships, their unfurling fiddleheads curled like giant fists towards the bright, static sky. Another could be a tree-like fungus with a vast, phosphorescent cap that sheds a gentle, internal glow onto the wet floor beneath. Their roots do not penetrate the ground; they seem to merge with it, the woody fibers of the plant interlocking with the grain of the floor as if they are all part of one continuous act of making.

Life moves here, but silently. Six-legged, chitinous grazers the size of dogs move in slow, deliberate processions up the giant fern-fronds, their mouths rasping softly against the vegetation. They are mottled grey and brown, the texture and patterning of their hides indistinguishable from the damp stone-wood. In the weeping-basins, gelatinous organisms pulse with a slow rhythm, absorbing the perpetually fresh water. Everything is adapted to the stillness. To make a loud noise would be a sacrilege, a rupture in the world's perfect, frozen equilibrium.

And it is frozen. The temperature never varies. The humidity is a constant. The water level in the basins is the same day after day after day. The light never fades. This is a system in perfect, unchanging homeostasis, a landscape locked into a single, eternal moment.

This is where the world feels thin. The weirdness is a palpable friction, a sense that the rules of existence are being actively enforced by a will you cannot perceive. Drop a stone, and it does not just lie there; over days you will see its edges soften, its form blurring as it is slowly, politely re-absorbed into the manufactured floor. It is not decay; it is reclamation. The sharp-edged bluffs and cliffs that define the open, regional scale of the place are not geological. Up close, you can see the tool marks, the primitive, repetitive scoring of hand-forged chisels. Yet they have the feel of something billions of years old and something created only yesterday, simultaneously.

This is an artifact. A built environment on the scale of a world, constructed with tools of stone and bone, yet held together by a reality so pliable that it requires no advanced engineering. The builders are long gone, or perhaps they are here, dissipated into the very logic of the place, their ghost a pressure that keeps the sky lit and the water weeping. It is a terrarium, a garden, a museum, a prison. It is a place that has escaped change, and in doing so, has become a perfect, beautiful, and terrifying monument to a final, unchanging now.
