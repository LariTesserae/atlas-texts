There is no beginning to the sound, only an immersion. It is not heard so much as it is *inhabited*. Your bones are the tuning forks, your blood the resonant cavity. The world is a single, deafening chord that has been sustaining itself for an eternity, vibrating in a state of pure, violent potential. It is a cathedral-sized pressure, a physical weight of pure noise that presses in from all sides, a liquid darkness that is both medium and message.

We are in a high place, though the concept feels abstract here. Below, the sound deepens into subsonic registers that churn the very substance of the world into an abyss of roiling potential. Above, the same. We are on a small, flat-topped spire of basalt, a mere platform adrift in an infinite medium of warm, viscous fluid that is neither air nor water, but something in between—a breathable, amniotic ocean. You are submerged, yes, but you are breathing it, a thick, sweet-tasting atmosphere that hugs the skin and mutes all but the Song. The temperature is that of blood, a constant, enveloping warmth.

This basalt spire is the ground, but it is not still. It is so young it has not forgotten the fire of its making. Its edges are razor-sharp, geometric facets untouched by the smoothing caress of age. Every surface is a fresh, glassy break. This is the nature of the erosion here: it does not wear away, it *happens*. It is an event, not a process.

And the sound is the event composer. Within the overwhelming, foundational drone, a strident, high-pitched piercing note will suddenly assert itself, a needle of pure frequency. Where it focuses, a point on the basalt glows with a furious white-hot light for a split second, then a shard of rock explodes outwards, vaporizing into the warm, fluid medium. Seconds later, a series of lower, more melodic arpeggios might cascade through the chamber, and from the broken edges, delicate, spidery structures begin to bloom. They are the vegetation here—sparse, crystalline growths that look like spun glass or frozen frost. They are not alive, but they are not inert. They are physical precipitations of melody, condensing out of the oppressive hum, growing quickly, glowing with a soft internal luminescence that matches the tune that birthed them. Then, a discordant crash of sound, a percussive slam, and they shatter into a thousand glittering motes, their brief life over. This is the turbulent ecology of the place. Life is a stanza in an endless, deafening poem.

The light is dim, a constant gloom perforated by these flares of sonic genesis. The chamber's boundaries are not walls but gradients, where the density of the fluid-air thickens into an opaque, humming darkness. You cannot see far; the experience is intensely claustrophobic, focused on this single small stage of creation and destruction. The scale is intimate, grove-like. You could almost shout to be heard, though your voice would be a meaningless pebble tossed into a tidal wave of sound.

There are presences, but they are rare, spaced out in the vast emptiness between the peaks. Sometimes, a larger, more complex melody will resolve from the chaos, a coherent entity held together by its own intricate harmony of tones. It might drift past, a momentary whale-song made manifest, a swirl of pressure and dim light that feels curiously aware. This is the fauna: temporary islands of order in the sea of noise. They do not eat or reproduce; they simply *are*, for as long as their song can sustain its shape against the dissonant tide. Then they unravel.

The "built" elements are artifacts of past songs. Etched into the face of the young rock is a perfect, logarithmic spiral, a fossil of a dominant frequency that held sway here for a minute, or an hour, or a millennia; time has no meaning against the eternal hum. In a nearby alcove, a bass note is trapped, looping, causing the air to shimmer and distort like a heat haze—a ruin of sound. These are the only marks, the only signs that anything has ever happened here before this exact, deafening moment.

The rules are simple because they are absolute: The Sound is. It creates and it erases. To exist is to be a specific pattern of vibration that, for a fleeting moment, resists the fundamental chord of dissolution. There is no solid ground, only temporary footholds in a reality that is constantly, violently being renewed. The weirdness is not the strangeness of it, but its absolute, uncompromising logic. A high-pitched screech means a crystalline tree will grow. A percussive blast means it will be destroyed. We are in the engine room of being, watching the gears grind, listening to the foundry forge and shatter worlds in the same breath. And the sound never, ever stops.
