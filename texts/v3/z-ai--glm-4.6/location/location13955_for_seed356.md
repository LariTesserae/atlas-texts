This is a place without a sky.

There is no up or down that matters, only a direction of pressure. You stand on a surface that is not ground, but root. Not soil, but substrate. It is a fibrous, interlocking mass of something that could be material if it weren't so clearly alive. The texture underfoot is yielding yet firm, like dense, waterlogged cork, intricate with geometric patterns that spiral into the darkness. Every surface you see is part of this single, contiguous growth. It is the floor, the walls, the ceiling the size of a continent, arching away into a gloom so profound it feels like a physical weight.

The air is thick and warm, a humid breath that coats your skin in a constant, fine sheen of moisture. It smells not of earth or decay, but of ozone and fermentation, a clean, sharp, and slightly chemical scent. This is the result of the water. It does not fall as rain; it *exudes*. From the very fabric of the world-mass, a slow, viscous fluid weeps. It beads on the immense, woven walls and traceles slow, silent rivers down the colossal, braided columns that hold up the nothingness above. It gathers in pools on the un-floors, clear and syrupy, catching a light that isn't there.

For there is no sun. illumination comes from the structure itself. It is a cold, blue-green phosphorescence, deep within the plant-matter. It is a low, steady glow, like LCDs breathing in a sleep mode. The light is not for seeing; it is a byproduct of function. It reveals a landscape of impossible scale. Gorges and canyons are not carved by water but are the gaps between colossal, slowly grown cables of the living-mass, their edges sharp and pristine. These are the features of a "young" land. They have looked this way for an eternity, sharp as the day they were formed, because nothing here erodes. Nothing changes. This is a moment, stretched out into forever.

This is the great, quiet engine.

The "vegetation" is the machinery. It is a world built by a process that could only be called industrial, yet it is entirely grown. You see conduits as thick as subway tunnels pulsing with a faint internal light, their fibrous walls thrumming with a vibration so low it's felt in the bones. Near them, the substrate is smoother, tighter woven, as if around high-pressure lines. Elsewhere, great membraneous sacs, the size of mountains, hang motionless in the dark, their translucent walls filtering the weeping fluid, changing its color from clear to a faint, milky white. These are the refineries. The factories.

There is no sound, save for your own breathing and the deep, almost sub-audible hum that seems to emanate from every atom of the place. It is the sound of a perfect, closed-loop system. It processes its own atmosphere from the weeping fluid. It powers its own light from a metabolic, internal source whose nature is as alien as the construct itself. It grows only to maintain its perfect form, any new filaments seamlessly interlacing with the existing, vast pattern.

There are no animals here, no birds, no insects. There are no seasons, no day and night, no wind. There is only the colossal, living, unmoving machine. It is a world that is utterly alien and utterly logical. A place that came into being fully formed, a finished product left to run in its eternal equilibrium, its "youth" locked in place by its own perfection. You are walking on the circuit board of a god, a factory that has completed its production and now simply *is*, humming softly to itself in the absolute, continent-wide dark.
