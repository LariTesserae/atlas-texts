It is not a sea that fell from the sky, but one that was lifted there.

This is the Cistern, a single, contained space, a reservoir of impossible scale held aloft at an elevation that scrapes the underbelly of the stratosphere. To look up is to see not open air, but a distant, groaning ceiling of interlocked plates, dark and corroded, weeping incandescent condensation that falls as a perpetual, warm rain. Down below, through depths of liquid so clear it seems like thickened air, rests the floor—an event horizon of fused metal, where the sagging skeletons of colossal gantries and the smooth, moss-overgrown heads of titan-rivets mark the final resting place of a machine whose purpose is now only to *be*. This is the world’s final state: an engine eroded into geology, a factory settled into its ancient, inevitable tomb.

The medium of it all is the water. It feels denser than pure water, slick with a faint alkaline taste and the faint, clean scent of ozone. It is uniformly warm, a bath-temperature cradle that never cools. This is the amniotic fluid of a forgotten age, a hydroponic solution that has become an ocean. Currents move with the glacial slowness of tectonic plates, driven by deep, resonant hums from unseen pumps somewhere in the structure’s bones. The hum is the bottom layer of the soundscape, a constant bass note felt in the bones more than heard with the ears.

Life here does not form forests or reefs. It is sparse, arranged with the intentionality of a grand, lonely design. The few vertical features are the Trees.

They do not grow from the floor so much as they are moored to it. Each Tree is a solitary Titanic biomanifold, its trunk a tightly braided sheath of cables as thick as redwoods, each cable wrapped in a bark of strange, wooy circuitry that pulses with a faint, internal warmth. From this trunk, leaves do not unfurl; they extend. They are vast, leathery photovoltaic panels, deep green and veined with gold, angled to capture a light that the world itself generates. Vines are not vines, but loosened fiber-optic threads that drape from the colossal branches, breathing soft, pastel data-lights into the liquid. And all over these structures—on the warm metallic bark, in the joints of the panels—gardens of true plants grow. Lush mosses that glow a soft blue, fungi with fleshy, bell-shaped caps chiming with their own bio-luminescence, and tenacious, oxygenating ferns that cling to every available surface. Each Tree is an ark of biology, grafting itself onto the industrial skeleton.

The fauna is abundant, but it is a life of the gaps, the inhabitants of the vast, empty stretches between the Trees. Schools of fish with bodies of hammered brass and fins of latticed clockwork dart through the upper levels, their movements creating a sound of a thousand tiny, perfectly tuned gears turning in unison. Deeper down, in the gloom lit only by their own slow, thumming bioluminescence, move the leviathans. They are whale-like things whose skin is a patchwork of blubber and rusted plating, and their passage is a silent, majestic event, displacing tonnes of liquid with a lazy flick of a massive, screw-propeller tail. They are the filters and the processors of this world, their pilgrimage through the depths the longest, slowest of the Cistern’s processes.

But the most dynamic life is on the Trees themselves. They swarm with creatures of intricate, mechanical strangeness. Insects the size of a man's hand, their bodies fashioned from beaten copper and spun glass, skitter across the photovoltaic leaves, polishing them with delicate, articulated legs. Their wings are like those of a dragonfly, but shimmer with an iridescence more akin to an oil slick. They are the keepers, the gardeners, the maintenance crew of this forgotten Eden, their chittering and the soft crackle of static from their bodies adding a sharp, active texture to the deep groan of the structure and the gentle slosh of the water.

There is no day or night, only a constant, pearlescent twilight. The light is the ecology itself. It comes from the glowing mosses, the data-pulses of the fiber-optic vines, the plankton that swirl in the currents in silent, shimmering clouds, and the internal luminescence of the leviathans. It is a normal light in its intensity, but alien in its quality; it casts long, dancing shadows from the Trees, making the water between them feel both sacred and menacingly empty.

To be here is to be a foreign element in a perfectly stable system. The Cistern is a machine that has achieved biological equilibrium, an organism that has forgotten it was ever built. Its rules are not those of survival of the fittest, but of harmony through function. The copper insects clean the leaves, which provide energy for the Tree, which shelters the mosses, which oxygenate the water, which nurtures the fish, whose waste feeds the fungi on the Tree’s roots. It is a single, distributed life, active and loud with purpose, even if that purpose has been abstracted to the simple, elegant act of continuing. You are a note of dissonance in a symphony that has been playing for millennia. The only question is whether the system will absorb you into its rhythm, or grind you into its foundations to settle into its final, ancient form.
