The air itself is the first thing you understand. It’s not breathed so much as worn; a wet, hot blanket that smells of ozone, wet stone, and an undertone of sweet, biological rot. The heat is profound, a geothermally-sustained fever that warps the air and makes every surface slick with a constant, weeping condensation. There is no dry ground.

You are below the surface, in what was once a crack in the world’s bones but has since swollen into a region of impossible scale. This is no mere cave. It is a cavernous, open-wound valley, miles wide and stretching into a hazy, chitinous horizon. Its ceiling is a distant, shrouded curve, lost behind the thick, living air.

There is no sun. There is, instead, the Heartlight.

A colossal, pulsating fungal growth clings to the cavern’s high, unseen ceiling, a continent of spongy, bioluminescent tissue. It exhales a steady, pearlescent glow—not the cold blue of deep-sea life, but a warm, diffuse light much like that of a cloudy day. This light is the only source of illumination. It breathes, in a slow, deep rhythm, and the entire valley brightens and dims in time with it. As it expands, it releases a shimmering, invisible rain of nutrient-rich spores that settle on everything below, fueling the ecosystem.

And what an ecosystem it is.

There is no vegetation in the terrestrial sense. No grass, no trees, no ferns. The entirety of the ground is a writhing, moving tapestry of fauna. The “soil” is not earth but a layered strata of shed carapaces, dessicated fungal tissues, and the fossilized excrement of a million generations, all compressed by the sheer weight of the living tide that crawls ceaselessly over it. This is a world that has bypassed the plant kingdom entirely, pouring all its energy into an explosion of animal life. Fungi, the only plant-analogue, grow like architectural features: towering, shelf-like brackets the size of tenements, veiny phosphorescent networks that crawl across the stone, and delicate, candle-like fruiting bodies that gutter with a soft, internal fire.

The density is overwhelming. The open space is an illusion; it is simply a large volume filled to saturation with life. The ground seethes. The primary biomass consists of Skitterers, iridescent, beetle-like creatures ranging from the size of a fingernail to that of a shield. They move in living currents, a glittering, chitinous river that flows around obstacles, pouring over ledges and through the smaller fissures in the rock. Their sheer number produces the loudest sound in the valley: a constant, undulating hiss of a million tiny legs on a billion polysaccharide shells, punctuated by the clicks and snaps of their mandibles.

This is the baseline noise, but the symphony of the valley is deeper. This is a turbulent, chaotic world. The Great Bloom’s pulse seems to trigger waves of activity. One moment, the Skitterer current is a placid flow; the next, it erupts into a frantic, boiling frenzy as predators join the hunt. Glint-wings, nervous, dragonfly-like creatures with crystal-faceted wings that catch the Heartlight in razory shreds, descend in shrieking squadrons. Their shadows flicker across the ground, causing the Skitterer waves to break and eddy. Larger, armoured beasts—the Geode-Maws—plow through the swarms, their massive, multi-hinged jaws scything through the biomass, consuming thousands in a single gulp. The sound of their passage is a rumbling crunch and a wet, grinding roar.

The geology itself is emerging, still being defined. The raw rock of the cavern walls is sharp, unweathered, and black, like cooled obsidian. But it is not static. In places, the rock is being actively eroded. Not by water, but by life. Colonies of acid-spitting molds etch swirling patterns into the stone, while immense, soft-bodied burrowers create new tunnels and sinkholes overnight. To stand in one place is to feel the ground tremble not with earthquakes, but with the slow, irresistible passage of a tunneller deep beneath your feet. The landscape is embryonic, its final form an unknown, constantly being rewritten by the chaos it contains.

Amidst this cataclysm of life, there is the built. It is scattered, ghostly, and desperate. Here and there, clinging to a steeper wall or built atop a massive, inactive fungal shelf, are the structures of the Reclaimers. They were here before. Or maybe they are still here.

Their technology is primitive. Scaffolding made from the bleached femurs of some giant creature, lashed together with sinew and woven fungal fiber. Platforms of hardened, pressed mycelium. Necklaces of Glint-wing carapace and beads of semi-precious stone, pried from the cavern walls. They are masterful hunters and gutters of the massive beasts, but they are not the masters of this place. They are survivors on an island of their own making, precariously perched in an ocean of overwhelming life. Their structures are inevitably reclaimed, the fungal platforms sprouting new, wild growth, the bone scaffoldings becoming nesting grounds for smaller, parasitic creatures. Everything they build is temporary, a brief assertion of order against a background of total, entropic chaos.

The rules of this world are simple and brutal. Life is fast, plentiful, and cheap. Death is constant, a background noise like the hiss of the Skitterers. Food is not an issue; you are simply a part of the food chain. Light is a gift, but it is a living thing that will eventually consume your remains. Shelter is a temporary illusion carved from the bodies of your enemies. And sound is a tactile force, a pressure that vibrates in your bones, the never-ending roar of a world that is still being born, even as it tears itself apart with the sheer energy of living.
