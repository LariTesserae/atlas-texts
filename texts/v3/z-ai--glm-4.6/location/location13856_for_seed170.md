You stand on a floor that is not stone.

The air is thin and smells of ozone and hot metal, a scent so sharp it feels like a file drawn across the inside of your sinuses. Each breath is a deliberate act, pulling in little substance but a great deal of heat. The sky is a hard, white-blue dome, so high and clear it feels like a vacuum. There is no haze, no moisture to soften the horizon; distance here is not a gentle gradient but a brutal, unforgiving line.

Beneath your feet is a vast, tessellated crust. It extends to every horizon, a cracked mosaic of hexagonal plates the size of plazas, each one a dark, vitrified material shot through with veins of rust-orange and deep cobalt. Walking on it is a disconcerting experience; it feels neither like rock nor like metal, but like the cooled-and-ruined casing of some impossibly vast engine. The edges of the hexagons are worn, rounded by eons of a wind that carries no grit, no dust, only heat and a low, pervasive vibration. This is theEstablished Erosion: the slow polishing of a machine by its own patient, endless operation. The cracks between the plates are not filled with sand, but with a perfect, resonant blackness that seems to drink the light.

There is no water. Not a trace. No riverbeds, no dry basins, no salt plains. The concept of fluid is alien to this place. The chemistry is one of heat and magnetism and slow crystalline growth. The Vegetation is zero: nothing green, nothing soft, nothing cellulose. The only things that grow are the mineral filaments that sometimes sprout like metallic whiskers from the centers of the hexagons, humming with a faint charge before sublimating back into the air.

This world is Open and Empty. The scale is regional; you can see for hundreds of kilometers. The landscape is not flat. In the distance, impossible mesas rise not from bedrock but from the fused and silhouetted forms of colossal, forgotten machinery. A ramp the size of a mountain range spirals up into the thin air, ending in a shattered ring that once held a lens the size of a city. There are canyons, but they are not carved by water. They are perfect, rectangular trenches, their walls sheer planes of that same dark ceramic, leading down into deeper, vaster halls of the dead machine.

The Sound is Loud, yet it is not a cacophony. It is a single, overwhelming chord, a symphony of low frequencies that you feel in your teeth and in the marrow of your bones. It is the sound of planetary-scale transformers, the hum of a billion-ton flywheel spinning imperceptibly in the planet's core, the thrum of vast electric potentials arcing between disconnected components. Sometimes, a new note enters the chord— a deep, resonant *bong* that seems to come from a specific mesa or from deep within a chasm, a sound that takes a full minute to fade. This is the world’s Slow Dynamic, the geology of sound. These changes mark the passage of ages, not hours.

And then there is the Fauna, which is both overwhelming and utterly absent. It is overwhelming because each creature is an event, a truth that reshapes the local reality. It is absent because you might go for days, for weeks, and see nothing but the humming hexagons and the distant, machine-made mountains.

You see one now.

It has been on the horizon for what must be a day, a speck of absolute black against the rust-colored mesas. It is closer now, and its scale is making a liar of your senses. It moves with the tectonic slowness of a glacier, its multiple, jointed legs—each a pillar of interlocking metallic plates—lifting and falling with a ponderous grace that sends tremors through the crust. Its body is not flesh but a walking cathedral of pistons, gears, and glowing, crystalline matrices. There is no head, no recognizable face, only a complex of sensory antennae that slowly sweep the air, crackling with static. This is not an animal that lives *in* the world; it is a component of the world's vast, industrial mechanism. It is a mobile maintenance unit, a living engine left to wander the fossilized factory floor. As it draws near, the Humming pitch of the world shifts around it, harmonizing with its own internal, rhythmic clanking. The air shimmers not just with heat, but with the Warp of its presence, the Reality Breakdown of a system so old and huge it has begun to produce ghosts in its own machine.

You feel an instinct not of predator and prey, but of a component that is out of place. You are an impurity in the system. Your biology—a fragile, aqueous, carbon-based anomaly—is a glitch. The creature, this living piece of tech, does not seem to acknowledge you, but you feel its presence like a pressure, a logical conclusion being drawn in a language you were never meant to understand. There are no buildings here, no artifacts of conscious construction, because this entire world *is* the building. And you, a tiny, fleeting flicker of warm, wet life, are trespassing on the slow, loud, and eternal thoughts of a machine that has long since forgotten its own purpose.
