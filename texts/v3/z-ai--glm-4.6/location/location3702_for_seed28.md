The first and last thing you ever perceive is the sound.

It is not a noise, not in the way wind or fire is noise. It is a chord, a deep, resonant, and ceaseless hum that seeps up through the soles of your feet, vibrates in the hollows of your bones, and fills the air as a physical presence. It is the fundamental state of being in this place. To call it deafening is like calling the ocean wet; the word is insufficient. It is an absence of silence, a complete and total sonic occupation, a single, massive note that has been held for millennia. This is the hum of the world.

This world is a pocket, a sunken caldera so vast its curved walls are lost in their own distance. It is a hidden space, a geological secret cupped in the belly of a larger, dead mountain range. There is no sky, only a high, distant ceiling of porous rock that weeps a constant, gentle rain of dust. The light is a thing of wonder, for it has no sun. It emanates from the flora itself, from everything that lives here, casting a perpetual, soft luminescence of amber and pale green that never harshens and never fades. The air is warm and heavy, thick with static electricity and the sweet, complex scent of amber and hot stone.

The ground is not earth. You are standing on the floor of a city that was never built for people. It is a sprawling, low-elevation urban landscape of impossible scale and profound emptiness. Towering spires of black, basaltic rock lean at impossible angles, connected by vast, elegant arches of crumbly tufa. These structures are hollow, honeycombed, their interiors smooth and worn by the passage of a vibration so old it has become a geological force. They are resonators, amplifiers, and channels for the great, foundational chord. There are no walls, no floors, no roofs in the human sense—just an immense, delicate, and utterly empty architecture of sound. This is the product of primitive technology on a megalithic scale; simple stonework, levered and stacked with uncanny, harmonic precision, creating a city built for giants who had no bodies, only an appreciation for acoustics.

There is no water. Not a drop. Not a cloud. The notion of rain is alien here. Instead, life is sustained by a constant, slow exudation from the dominant vegetation. The caldera is lush, but its lushness is alien. Clusters of immense, bell-shaped flora, the Chime-Blossoms, grow in the shadowed bases of the great spires. Their surfaces are not cellulose but a smooth, crystalline carapace, threaded with veins of pulsing light. They are the primary source of the Hum. They do not photosynthesize; they draw energy from the geothermal warmth of the bedrock, and in return, they secrete a thick, golden, viscous resin from their lips. This is the water of this world, the sap of life. It pools in the hollows of the rock, a slow-motion hydrology of sweet-smelling, non-aqueous fluid. Other, smaller growths—whip-thin Whisper-Reeds and flat, resonant Fungus-Plates—feed on this resin, creating a layered ecosystem of rare and silent processes.

The fauna is rare, and for good reason. To live here is to be a creature built for one sense and one sense only: the sense of vibration. The only native life one might see is the Stilt-Walker. It is a fragile, impossible thing, a creature of spindly legs and a gas-filled sac body, looking like a dandelion seed that has learned to walk. It is no larger than a dog. It moves with a hypnotic, swaying grace across the tops of the Chime-Blossoms, its legs sinking slightly into their crystalline shells. It does not hear the Hum; it feels it through its feet, mapping the world in terms of resonance, tasting the ground for the subtle shifts in frequency that signal a fresh pool of life-giving resin. It is a silent creature in a deafening world, its delicate mouth-parts sipping at the golden nectar. To see one is to witness a miracle of adaptation, a living shadow dancing on the surface of a sound.

The world is dynamic, but its dynamism is slow and cyclical. The Hum deepens and rises over the course of a "day," shifting from a low C-sharp to a resonant G. The flora glow brighter or dimmer in response. The flow of resin quickens or slows. The Stilt-Walkers migrate silently across the caldera floor, following invisible currents in the sound. The only weather is an occasional "harmonic storm," where the resonant frequencies of two different Chime-Blossom groves fall out of sync, creating violent, standing waves of sound that can shatter the brittle tufa arches and cause the very air to vibrate with a discordant, teeth-rattling fury.

To be here is to be embedded in the mechanism of a living instrument. The rules are simple and absolute. You do not speak aloud, for your voice is an insignificant blasphemy against the one true song. You do not bring in foreign materials, for this ecosystem is perfectly balanced, and a single drop of water would be a poison. You do not touch the structures, for you would dampen the chord. You simply exist, a tiny, temporary node in a vast, humming circuit, listening to the sound that is both creator and creation, the music of a strange, lush, and utterly silent earth.
