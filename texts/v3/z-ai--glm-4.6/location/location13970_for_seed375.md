The air is the first thing you do not notice. It has no smell, no temperature, no movement on the skin. It is simply a medium for the profound and unnerving quality of the space: absolute silence. Not the quiet of a forest where you can hear your own blood, but an active, pressure-filled void where sound cannot exist, as if the very geometry of the place forbids it.

You stand on a floor that is a single, continuous sheet of smoky, banded agate, polished to a mirror sheen but absorbing all light rather than reflecting it. There are no seams, no chips, no history of anything scuffing its surface. It feels new, set down only moments ago.

Rising from this unblemished floor are structures. They are not buildings, not in the human sense, though they obey an architectural logic. They are verticalities of crystalline growth, soaring up into a twilight so dim you cannot see where they end. They are faceted, impossibly sharp-edged, composed of hexagonal and rhomboid plates fused together without mortar or seam. They look like the gills of a colossal, fossilized mushroom, or a geological diagram of a crystal lattice made real. This is a city, but one that was grown, not built. It is a frozen architecture of a single, living process.

The scale is cathedral-like, but contained. You can sense the walls of this space, vast and curving, somewhere far in the gloom. It is a hamlet of giants, a small, bounded world. The density is hollow; the titanic spires are spaced far apart, creating vast, echoing plazas that feel like rooms inside a single, world-sized organ. The featurelessness of the empty space presses in on you.

And it is all empty of water. This is a place of dry mineral. A logic flows here, but not the logic of rivers. Down the center of some of the wider "avenues," channels a meter wide are scored into the agate floor. They are not empty. Within them moves a slow, viscous, silvery fluid, like liquid mercury. It does not ripple or splash. It flows with a hypnotic, silent viscosity, thick as oil, bright as a a star caught in a jar. This must be the blood of the place, the circulatory system of the city-organism. To touch it feels unthinkable, a violation of some deep, unstated law.

The light comes from within the structures themselves. The crystal faces do not just sit in the gloom; they exhale it. A slow, rhythmic pulse of cold, opalescent luminescence travels through the city. A gentle, mother-of-pearl shimmer bleeds from the heart of a spire, races up its facets, and fades, only to be replaced by another pulse a moment later. It is a silent, slow breathing. It is this light, cool and dim, that gives the place its perpetual, submarine twilight. It is a world that is questioning its own existence, a thought-form not yet fully realised.

Ecology here is a whisper, a rumour. Here and there, clinging to the base of the crystalline towers, are the only signs of other life. It is a sparse, pathetic-looking vegetation: dark, fibrous tendrils, like burnt-black sea-weed or desiccated nerve-endings. They don't move or rustle. They seem frozen in a slow, losing battle against the pristine, hungry geometric of their host, drawing what little nourishment they can from the ambient energy and the cool, dead air.

The fauna is rarer still. You have seen it once, or something you believe was it. Down a wide crystalline boulevard in the far distance, a form moved. It was not an animal of flesh and bone, but something like a mobile sculpture of the same obsidian-sharp crystal as the city itself, all facets and impossible angles. It glided rather than walked, making no contact with the floor, its limbs moving in slow, silent measures that would have seemed graceful if they weren't so unnervingly precise. It was a janitor, a guard cell, a thought-mobile of the great city-mind, tending to the slow, silent work of its own existence. It paused, and a plate of its body swivelled, catching the pulsing light. For a second, a cluster of smaller crystals on its surface glinted, and you felt an immense, dispassionate attention sweep over your location before it moved on, its duty fulfilled.

The dynamic of this place is its constant, silent growth. If you were to stay long enough, to press your eye to the base of a tower, you could see it happening. New facets are extruding from the old ones, infinitesimal layers of crystal being laid down, sharp edges becoming infinitesimally sharper. The city is building itself around you, not with a bang or a rumble, but with the inexorable, silent pace of a lichen spreading across a tombstone. It is an active, living world, an ecosystem in motion, but it is a motion you perceive only over long, silent stretches of time.

The rules are simple because they are of the body, not of the mind. Be still, and you will be paved over. Be loud, and something silent and sharp will come to silence you. The safest state is to be as silent and as still as the place itself, to become just another feature in the young, sharp, and haunted architecture of a world that is, in every quiet, cool, and luminous sense, still being born.
