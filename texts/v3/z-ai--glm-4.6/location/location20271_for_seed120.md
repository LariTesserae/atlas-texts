The air is a thick, hot broth you can almost chew. It weighs on the lungs, saturated with the scent of wet stone, crushed chlorophyll, and something else—a mineral, metallic tang like blood after it has been left in the sun. There is no skyline, only a ceiling of woven fronds and latticed vines so dense that daylight is a remote, theoretical possibility, diffused into a perpetual, humid twilight. You stand not on earth, but on a surface that is both made and being made.

This is a single, stepped terrace, a pocket world cantilevered over an impossible void. The ground underfoot is stone, but it is wrong. It’s not the inert, cold rock of mountains. This stone is warm, and it thrums with a slow, deep frequency, a pressure in the bones that you feel rather than hear. Its surface is a chaos of primitive craftsmanship: sharp-angled chisel marks, the scoop-and-gouge of adzes, an almost frantic, obsessive level of detail crammed into every square inch. Yet, between these tool-marked blocks, the material itself flows. Seams of not-quite-solid stone, glowing with a faint internal heat, pulse like slow arteries. Everywhere, water weeps from the rock—not seeping *from* it, but condensing *onto* it, beading and running in rivulets that are immediately drunk up by a carpet of vegetation so lush and aggressive it seems to be holding the structure together.

There is no bare surface. Everything is packed. Mosses with the texture of velvet grow in thick, humid mats. Ferns unfurl fronds the size of a man, their fiddleheads a violent, electric green. Orchids—strange, cup-shaped things the color of old bone—cling to the vertical faces of the blocks, their hollows perpetually full of distilled water. The vegetation is a saturated, suffocating presence, a silent, photosynthetic riot in a landscape that has no room to spare. It is the only true life here; fauna are a rarity, a ghost in the machine. An occasional flicker of motion might be a winged thing with too many joints, or a soft, pulsing shape in the water that vanishes the moment you try to focus on it. The air is the domain of sound, not sight. The dominant noise is a low, constant groaning, the sound of a continental plate giving way. It is the sound of the structure, of the world, being stretched and born. Overlaid on this is the cacophony of billions of dripping leaves, the hiss of water striking hot stone and instantly vanishing as steam, and a high, insectile chittering that has no discernible source.

This place is called The Upward Urn by its... tenders.

They are occasionally seen, always at a distance. Primitive, hunched figures wrapped in woven fibres, their tools starkly simple: stone hammers, copper chisels, mallets of dense, dark wood. They are, impossibly, building this place. They do not haul blocks; there is no quarry. Instead, they tend to the glowing seams. They tap them, chisel them, sing to them in tones that blend with the groaning stone. They are not architects but gardeners, coaxing the raw, emergent geology into shape, forcing the reality-warping growth into orthogonal forms. Their "construction" is an act of will, a continuous prayer made manifest by physical labour, shaping the unreal into the real.

The Urn is a ziggurat that does not rest on the ground but is *of* the ground, the ground itself folded upwards into the sky. Your elevation is extreme, yet you have not climbed a mountain; the mountain has been extruded beneath you. To approach the edge of the terrace is to understand the horror and the wonder. It is not a cliff face that drops away, but the raw, unfinished edge of creation. A few meters beyond the manicured, tool-scored stone, the material dissolves into a chaotic roil of semi-formed matter, a seething storm of rock and light and pure potential. This is the "emerging" landscape, the zero-erosion frontier. Below, there is no world, only a centrifugal blur of existence. Above, the same. The Urn is a pillar of reality, a temporary island of stability in a maelstrom, and it is growing.

The rules here are simple and absolute. The stone lives. The air is water. The light is a memory. The heat is the engine of existence. To be here is to be a component, a temporary speck of awareness on the skin of a nascent god that is being built by pre-industrial hands to pierce a heaven it can never reach. The condensation slicks your skin, your breath is lost in the humid roar, and the ground beneath your feet shudders as another ton of impossible mountain is born from the raw, screaming will of the world.
