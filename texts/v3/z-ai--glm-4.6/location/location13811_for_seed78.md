The air is thin and stings the lungs with a sharp, metallic chill—it breathes like a vacuum, pulling warmth from the skin before the body can register the cold. This is the High Rime, a vertical archipelago suspended in a darkness so absolute it feels heavy, like black velvet pressing against the eyes. The light here is an afterthought, a rare and bruised purple filtering down from unimaginable heights, or perhaps the ghost-luminescence of the clouds far below.

To call this a "forest" is to misunderstand the scale. This is a world of pillars.

The vegetation (2.8) is not merely abundant; it is the architecture, the earth, and the only solid ground. There is no stone, no soil, only the *Stranglers*. These are colossal, fibrous mega-flora, rising from the abyss like the columns of a ruined, biological temple. They are vast—tree-like in silhouette but mountainous in girth. Their bark is not wood but a compressed, spongy conglomerate of millions of years of moss, fern, and vine, calcified into a crust that holds the shape of the world. The sheer scale (2.6) of these pillars is dizzying; looking up or down yields the same result—a vanishing point into dark, misty infinity.

Between the Stranglers lies the terrifying emptiness (0.1). Vast, hollow gulfs of still air separate the pillars, drops measured not in meters but in kilometers. To fall here is to enter void space, drifting for hours before striking the membrane of cloud cover leagues below. The density of the environment is so low that sound behaves strangely; it travels unimpeded for miles, bouncing off the waxy surfaces of the giants with a crystal clarity that makes the silence between noises feel loud.

The moisture (1.3) is subtle but relentless. It does not rain in sheets here. Instead, the air sweats. Condensation gathers on the immense flanks of the pillars, forming rivulets that snake down the vertical landscapes, freezing into translucent stalactites of ice where the wind cuts deepest. The water is cold, tasting of ancient dust and oxygen.

Because the terrain is young (1.0), the top of the canopy is jagged and unforgiving. The Stranglers are still growing, still fighting for purchase in the thin air, and their peaks are sharp, shattered spines of fiber-wood. There is no weathered smoothness here, only the fresh, violent break of new growth and the constant shedding of dead layers that tumble silently into the dark.

The ecosystem is in constant, violent motion (2.5). This is not a static garden but a machine of predation and decay. The fauna (1.5) has evolved to traverse the empty gulfs—leathery, gliding creatures with immense wingspans that cling to the vertical bark. They move in clouds, shifting through the pillars like schools of fish. The sound (1.9) of the place is a cacophony of biological noise: the screech of hunting banshees echoing across the void, the groaning creak of the giant plants shifting in the wind, and the low, thrumming vibration of millions of insects boring into the inner layers of the pillars.

Life here is primitive (0.7), scratching existence from the vertical walls. There are inhabitants—small, scurrying figures seen only against the vastness of the bark. They do not build outward; they build into. The "built" environment (1.2) consists of shallow caves carved into the spongy dead-wood of the pillars, reinforced with woven webs of vine and thorn. Small, precarious platforms hang suspended by ropes of dried gut, offering a vantage point to watch the dark skies. Fires are rare and precious, fuelled by the resinous sap that bleeds from the Stranglers when they are wounded.

At night (or what passes for it, in this perpetual gloom), the weirdness (0.9) of the place manifests. The plants themselves seem to breathe, rhythmically expanding and contracting. The water trapped in the crevices of the bark freezes and thaws in cycles, creating a cracking, popping sound like static. And occasionally, seen from the corner of one's eye, the vast spaces between the pillars seem to ripple, as if the air itself is a thick, viscous fluid through which the world is slowly swimming.

It is a place of immense, cold indifference. A hollow, vertical cathedral of life where you are either clinging to the side, or falling through the dark.
