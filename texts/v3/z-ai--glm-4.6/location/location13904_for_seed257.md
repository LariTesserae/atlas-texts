The first and only constant is the water. It is not an aggressive or violent inundation; the world was not conquered by a flood, but surrendered to it. This is a state of total immersion, a warm, amniotic fluid with the density and clarity of old glass. It presses in evenly from all sides, a gentle, omnipresent weight that muffles the self and blurs the line between body and medium. To breathe is to draw this fluid into the lungs, where it yields a thickness of air that feels nourishing and clean. The temperature is perfect, a blood-warmth that makes clothing irrelevant and time indistinct.

There is no light from above. No sun, no moon, no stars drowned by the depths. The only illumination comes from the place itself. It is a faint, abyssal phosphorescence that seems to bleed from the very substance of the world. It is a cool, white light, like faded bone, that etches the edges of things but leaves their faces in soft, ambiguous shadow.

You are standing on a floor. It is smooth, unnervingly so, worn down by eons of this gentle water until any corner has been rounded to a gentle curve and any texture has been polished into a uniform, slick sheen. This is the "high ground," an ancient plateau now lying at the bottom of a new sea. To walk here is to walk on the summit of the world, a fact you can feel in the sheer, vertical pressure drop a hundred meters in every direction. Beyond the edge of this vast, level floor, the phosphorescence fades into a profound, absolute blackness—the abyss into which this mountaintop world now drops.

And rising from this floor, filling the space so densely they are almost touching, is a forest of silent architecture. This is the "vegetation" that is not vegetation. Immense, monolithic pillars, squared-off and perfectly plumb, stretch up into the gloom until they disappear from sight. They are not built, but grown or extruded; there are no seams, no bricks, no mortared joints. They are simply *there*, carved from some single, unknown, slightly luminous stone by a method that left no tool marks—only the perfectly smooth, mathematically precise surfaces of a technology too simple to be understood. Arches of impossible span bridge the gaps between titanic blocks, and cyclopean stairways lead up to small, solitary plinths that hold nothing at all. The entire space is a dense, built environment, a single-room city or a necropolis for a forgotten god, created with the patient, hand-tooled care of a primitive craftsman on a geological scale.

The water is thick with life, but it is a life without voice or frenzy. The fauna are abundant, a silent, drifting population of geometric creatures. They are not fish; they have no fins and no scales. Most take the form of polyhedral skeletons, frames of fine, dark material that endlessly glide through the warm, viscous fluid. Some are simple, glowing tetrahedra no larger than a thumb. Others are vast, floating lattices of interlocking rings and struts, slow-moving zoos of intersecting geometry that drift through the silent architecture like tectonic plates. They seem to feed not on organic matter, but on potential energy, or perhaps on the slow erosion of the structures themselves. You will see one of the larger creatures press a crystalline facet against a pillar and hold it there for what might be hours, a process so slow it is indistinguishable from stillness. There is no frenzy of feeding, no struggle for survival. It is a patient, silent communion.

Sound is near-nonexistent. The thick water and the porous, stone-like nature of the architecture soak up vibrations. The world's quiet is not an emptiness, but a presence. It is the sound of processes too slow to hear; the glacial grinding of a creature's limb against a pillar, the infinitesimal stress of a structure holding its own weight across eons, the molecular hum of the water itself holding the warmth of the planet's core. A sudden movement—your own hand sweeping through the fluid—produces a brief, soft *shloop*, a scandalous noise that quickly dissolves into the profound, patient hush.

To be here is to be in the final, stable form of a world. The dynamic of change has slowed to a near stop. The primary activity, if you can call it that, is maintenance on a geological timescale. The geometric fauna are the stewards of this place. They clean, they polish, they very slowly—impossibly slowly—reshape it according to a plan that was laid down before the concept of memory existed. The "weirdness" is not in the appearance of the place, but in its implication: that this is a machine, and it is running perfectly. The silence is the sound of its function. The dense forest of structures is its circuitry. The blind, drifting, countless fauna are its slow, silent, dutious operators. You are a momentary anomaly in its endless, patient cycle, a brief flicker of consciousness in a dark, warm, and silent place that was not built for you, and will not notice when you are gone.
