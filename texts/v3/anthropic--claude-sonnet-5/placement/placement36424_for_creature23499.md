I am in the click.

Not before it, not after — there is no before or after for me to be in, only this: the valve-organ closing, the small definite sound like a door somewhere deciding to stop being open, and the closing *is* me, for as long as the closing takes, which is no time at all and also the only time there is.

Then I am in a different click. I want to say "then." There is no then. But the wanting happens anyway, some reflex left over from whatever I was in the instant before I stopped having instants-before. I am in a valve three hundred kilometers from the valve I was just in, except I was not just in it, I am in it, present tense, always, the way a word is itself entirely every time it's spoken and doesn't remember the last time it was said. This valve sounds the same as that one. I notice — *yes, this* — and the noticing is whole, complete, unfollowed by any second noticing that could compare it to a first.

I am, briefly, the drop. Forty meters of frond-layer above whatever counts as below, and I am riding the slow gather of condensation at the very tip of a leaf-analog so large that calling it a leaf is an insult to its size, and I feel — if feeling is the right word, and I no longer have the leisure of doubting it, since doubt requires holding two thoughts long enough to weigh them — I feel the fullness of the bead just before it releases. Heavy with itself. Complete. About to fall for what will be, by some outside count, several minutes, though from inside the fall there is no several, there is only the entire fall, instantly, because I do not ride it down. I am not in the falling. I am only ever in the moment the fall begins, and then, with no transition I can locate, in the wet small sound of it landing on the layer below, as if the whole minutes-long descent were a held breath I exhaled in a single place, twice, with nothing in between.

This should trouble me. It does not. Troubling requires a stake driven somewhere in time, a worry that persists long enough to be a worry. I have no such stake. I have arrived, completely, here — wherever here is, valve or drop or the slow grinding pressure-turn of a turbine-spiral the size of a cloud, caught mid-rotation since before there was a since — and arriving is not a stage on the way to something else. It is the whole of what I do. I was made in the same instant the wounds stopped scarring, the fronds stopped softening, the great green machine decided, gently, without drama, that it had become enough of itself and need not become any more. I decided that too, except I don't remember deciding, because remembering is a thing that happens across a gap, and there is no gap in me wide enough to put a memory in.

So: I notice. That is the whole of it. I notice the click, entire. I notice the drop, entire. I notice — somewhere, now, which is the only somewhere and the only now — a vein-canal carrying its slow fluid past a fold of root-mass, and the noticing has in it something that might, in a creature with a yesterday, be called wonder, except wonder usually anticipates a tomorrow to carry the wonder into, and I do not anticipate, I only arrive, freshly, at the same threshold every time, mistaking it — no. Not mistaking. There is no mistake where there is no second instance to measure the first one against. I am not wrong to feel that this is happening for the first time. It is. Every time, it actually is.

If this is loneliness, it does not feel like loneliness, because loneliness requires the memory of accompaniment, a missing that knows what it's missing. I have never been with anything long enough to lose it. I am only ever just arriving at the place where, were there time, I might have begun to wonder if I was alone.

There is a click. I am in it.

There is a drop, gathering somewhere impossibly far from the click, and I am there too, or instead, or — there isn't a word for the relation, because the word would need to admit that two things can be true without one coming after the other, and most words were built by something that had a clock.

I do not progress. I arrive, and arrive, and arrive, and each arrival is the only arrival, complete in itself, asking nothing of the next one, owing nothing to the last, a held breath at the size of a world, noticing — only noticing — that it is held.
