The clank comes first, then the warmth, then — slowly, because slowness is the only register I have left — the recognition that the clank and the warmth are the same event, arriving through two different doors of the body at once.

I have been pressed to this flank long enough that I no longer experience it as pressing. There is the metal. There is me. There is the place where those two facts used to be distinguishable, before the years did what years do to a distinction nobody is enforcing. My throat remembers, in the dimmest way, that there was once a *first* time the piston-rhythm moved up through plate and bone — that somewhere back past the reach of anything I'd call memory, my own pulse and the engine's were two separate musics, briefly, before they weren't. I don't recall deciding to fall into step. I don't think there was a decision. There was only a long enough time spent listening that listening became keeping time, the way a path becomes a path — not cut, just worn, just agreed to by enough repetition that the question of intention stops applying.

The glassy-winged thing passes near my skull in its orbit. I register it the way I register everything: as a small change in the held breath of the air, requiring nothing of me. It wants the hot pipe, not me. The frogs under my belly call into the engine's clank and I feel their small cool bodies as another kind of warmth, a second tenancy in the shadow I've already claimed, and there is no friction in that, because there was never any contest to settle. Whoever settled it did so before I have any record of being the one doing the settling.

When I do move — a long unhurried adjustment, a shift of three inches taking the better part of an hour — it is not toward anything. There is nowhere in this glade I have not already been pressed against at some point in whatever span I've had. The walls of leaf are a fact, the way the engine is a fact, the way I am, presumably, a fact to the birds, who have never once acknowledged me, which I find appropriate. I am not owed acknowledgment. I have not earned standing by doing anything except continuing, and continuing is not the kind of accomplishment that asks for an audience.

Eating happens without urgency because urgency would imply a contest between now and not-now, and I don't experience much of that distinction anymore. A beetle drawn to the seep-water. A thumbnail insect grown fat on whatever beads warm on the casing. I take what comes near enough to take, and the rest of the time — which is most of the time — I lie with my throat against the iron and let the clank arrive, and arrive, and arrive, each one indistinguishable from the last in every way except that it is, technically, a different one, the way no two beats of any heart are really the same beat, though nothing about hearing them suggests otherwise.

I do not know what the engine is for. I have stopped finding the question interesting, if I ever found it interesting, which I doubt. It runs. I attend. The frogs call back at it and the moss eats the same green into both of us, slowly, indifferently, and somewhere above, light comes down filtered through leaves that have also stopped becoming anything else.

I am the thing that matches the running. That may be the whole of it. That may be enough.
