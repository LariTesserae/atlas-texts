I think I would not be standing at the rim looking in. The rim is for grazers and visitors, and I don't think I'm patient enough to be either.

I think I would surface somewhere between two half-built walls, in that gap left generous and unexplained, and the first thing I'd notice is not sight but temperature — my own, running a little hotter than the stone I'm touching, the heat in me already migrating toward my jaw, toward the long gland-line down my flank, because the air has gone soft and damp in exactly the way that tells my body: *now*.

I don't think I'd have a plan. That's the part that takes longest to settle into, writing it from inside rather than watching it from the rim — the absence of anything like intention. Not blankness; more a kind of certainty that doesn't route through thought. The way your hand knows to close before you've decided to make a fist. I would feel the gap between two structures the way you feel a sentence that wants one more word, and I would lean my shoulder into the unclaimed stone, and something along my jaw would already be loosening, going hot and half-liquid, and I would pack it — fast, blunt, unceremonious — against the rock that's already there, and step back, and it would be a wall before I had any thought resembling *wall*.

I think I would not look at it twice. That's the strangest part to inhabit — not pride, not even satisfaction, just the attention already three structures over, already finding the next gap that wants filling. My body would be a kind of weather system that happens to leave architecture behind it. I would not know, in any way I could report, why the spacing between dwellings feels right at this width and wrong at that one. I would just feel wrong-width like a held note slightly flat, and correct it, and move on.

The small fast things in the ferns would scatter from my footfall and I don't think I'd register them as separate from the moss, particularly. The bird-things overhead, finding ledges in walls I raised an hour ago — I think I'd feel them the way you feel a breeze you're not facing into. Present. Irrelevant. Not unwelcome, just outside whatever narrow corridor of awareness my body has been built to run on. Make heat. Find damp. Pack the mineral. Leave the gap generous. Move to the next gap. The rim-grazer's slow scrape against outer stone would reach me as sound and nothing more, a fact of the basin, like the drip off a leaf.

And then — and this is the part I can feel coming even before I write it, the way you feel dusk arriving in your skin before the light actually changes — the air would shift. Drier. A half-degree cooler, maybe, or some quality in the humidity that has nothing to do with temperature at all. And the wanting-to-build would simply stop, the way hunger stops, not from satisfaction but from the body's own clock running out its hour. I would feel suddenly, mildly tired in the gland-line, in the jaw, a used-up feeling with no anxiety in it. And I would turn — not toward any destination I'd thought about, just toward *under*, toward the deeper cold dark at the basin's edges where the basalt goes from built to simply old — and go down into it, and stop being visible to anyone standing where you're standing now.

I would not think of the town as unfinished behind me. I don't think that word would mean anything from inside this body. The wall is the wall. The gap is the gap. Tomorrow's damp will tell me where the next one wants to be, and I will not remember deciding, because there was never any deciding to remember — only heat, and stone, and the unhurried, planless rightness of a wasp finishing a cell it will never live to see weather.
