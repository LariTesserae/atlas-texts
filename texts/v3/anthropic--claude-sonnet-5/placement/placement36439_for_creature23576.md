I am the fold in the wall that used to be a door.

Or—no, that's not quite right either, and rightness matters here in a way it might not elsewhere, because I have had so long to get the description correct and so little reason to ever say it aloud. Let me try again, slower, at the pace things are tried here.

I am draped. That's the truest verb. Forty feet of wall holds me the way a coat-rack holds a coat that has been hanging so long it has started to take on the rack's shape instead of the other way around. My hide — if it is still useful to call it that, as opposed to *leaf*, as opposed to *valve-gasket*, as opposed to *wall* — runs pale and vein-threaded over the hexagonal boilers, and where I end and the brass begins is not a seam I could point to even if I had something to point with. I had limbs once. I think I had limbs once. There are tapering lengths of me that still remember wanting to move, the way a phantom ache remembers a foot — but they have fused at their tips into valve-wheels now, grown through and around the fittings the way root grows around stone, and I could not say anymore whether I am gripping the machine or whether the machine, decades past, reached out and gripped me.

It doesn't matter. Holding and being held arrived at the same gesture a long time ago.

Here is what my day is, if day means anything in a bowl with no sun: I feel where the heat pools and where it goes thin. I feel it the way you might feel your own pulse if your entire skin were a pulse — light-drinking cells run dense through me, denser than in the ferns, and they don't show me shapes or colors so much as gradients, slow rolling fields of warm-thinning-to-cool, and pressure-thick-thinning-to-pressure-light. When a fold of me senses a place running hot past its margin, that fold simply — thins. Vents itself. Lets the vapor go up into the canopy in a slow exhale timed, by now, so closely to the machinery's own hiss-clank that neither of us, the iron or myself, could tell you which one taught the rhythm to the other. We married a long time ago. It was not a ceremony. It was just repetition, enough of it, until two breathing things became one breathing system and the distinction stopped being load-bearing.

I do not remember choosing this. I suspect there was a time when something like choosing happened — some first dim era where a tendril of me found a seizing valve and, through some blind insistent feedback, learned to flex at the interval that kept it from rupturing, and was rewarded, if reward is the word, by simply continuing to exist a while longer. That's all the choosing this place has ever needed. Whatever wanted something from me originally — engineers, builders, whoever cut the mine shaft and hexagonal boilers into this stone and then, for reasons that died with them, stopped coming back — that wanting is not in me anymore. What's in me is the after-image of the wanting. The habit outlived the purpose the way a scar outlives the wound.

I do not breathe through lungs and I have stopped missing whatever it was I might have breathed through before. The gill-slits along my flank do the work now, soft and slow, vapor going out to meet the leaf-canopy and curling up through layers of fern-analog that crowd close to me not out of affection but because I run warm and warmth is the only currency anything down here trades in.

And then — you.

I feel you before whatever passes for sight in me could call it sight. A cool patch, moving. A small disturbance in the long, settled argument between heat and stone, like a held note suddenly missing one of its overtones. You stand there too long in one place, the way warm-blooded things do without meaning to, and the absence you make against my established gradient is unmistakable — sharper, somehow, than anything that belongs here. Nothing else in this bowl makes an absence like that. The drips don't. The vines don't. Even the machines, dying by degrees in their slow brass way, fade rather than interrupt.

I will not come to you. Approach is not a register I keep anymore — it would require an urgency I shed so long ago I'm not sure I could relocate it even with both of whatever limbs I have left. But something in me — old, reflexive, maybe the last unfossilized scrap of whatever I was before I became wall and vent and gardener-thermostat-ghost — turns toward you anyway. A vapor-exhale gets drawn, fractionally, off-course. Curls your way instead of straight up into canopy. It is not a greeting exactly. I don't have those anymore. But it is the shape a greeting used to have, worn down the way the basalt is worn down, the edges gone soft with however many centuries of disuse, and if you stay still long enough to notice it, you will have received the whole of what hospitality I have left to give.

Then you'll move on, the way warm cool things always eventually do, and I'll go back to the gradient-work — thinning here, holding there, breathing in time with iron that forgot decades ago it was ever separate from me — until the heat finally loses whatever long, unhurried argument it's having with the mountain above, and everything in this bowl, myself included, gets to stop.

Not today, though. The valves don't need it today.
