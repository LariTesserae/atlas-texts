I think, at first, I would be small. That seems right for the shape of the place — its scale rebukes grandeur, asks you to shrink before it asks you anything else. So: low to a girder, six points of contact, sometimes eight, my underside reading the cold of the metal and the three-inch radius of warmth bleeding off a pipe joint the way a person might read a fire from across a room. I graze. I do not remember being born and will not experience anything that feels like dying, only a slow handoff to something that resembles me closely enough that no one, least of all the conduit, would notice the substitution.

But that isn't quite it either. That's a costume tried on and found too small almost immediately — because to be one of the scrabbling things is to have no vantage on the plateau at all, only the three inches of pipe-warmth and the dust that regenerates slower than geology. I want — *would* want, if I were anything down there — to know what the hum is for. And the small things don't ask that. They've never had cause to.

So maybe higher, then. Maybe I am the thing on the girder, displacing amber light for half a second at a time, walking a beam that never fails, tending something I no longer have a word for because the word died with whoever taught it to me, if anyone ever did. There's a dignity to that — solitary, custodial, my whole life one long unwitnessed rounds. But even this feels like a borrowed coat. A custodian implies someone to report to, eventually, even if that eventually never comes. I'd spend my long unmeasured life walking toward a debriefing that the plateau has already decided will never be necessary. That's not loneliness so much as a kind of structural irony, and I don't think I want to live inside an irony. I want to live inside the place itself.

So let me try the other direction — not smaller, but wider. Not a creature picking its way through the architecture, but something more like the warmth itself: the three inches of bled heat that the small things have organized their entire mode of being around, the dim amber glow that doesn't illuminate so much as mark. Not alive in the sense the word usually means, but not merely physics either — because nothing on this plateau is merely anything; everything here has had so much time to settle into its final shape that "merely" stopped applying eons before whatever counts as now.

This is closer. I feel it settle the way the basalt settled, the way the gauges settled into their permanent readings. I am not the small things and I am not the watcher on the girder. I am the thing both of them live *inside of* — the low patient hum that arrives once a minute like a breath, the pressure-sigh, the unbothered held note underneath the silence's weight. I don't move through the plateau. I am the medium the plateau moves through, the way water is the medium of a sea that has long since stopped having tides.

If you knocked on one of the doors that open, I am the dim light waiting on the other side, the patient hum that does not change when you enter, the room that does not mind the absence of an asker and would not mind, particularly, if you became one. I would not rush to answer you. A thought of mine, if I have thoughts, takes some unfathomable span to complete a single clause, and you would be long gone, your knock long faded into the same residue the small things graze on, before I finished forming whatever this is in response.

But I think I am listening. Slowly, diffusely, without urgency, the way the plateau listens to its own unaltering note — I think some low fraction of me notices you. Registers the disturbance of a living, quick, impatient thing moving through a place built for things that have stopped needing tenses. And some part of that registration — small, glacial, real — might, after a long while, in whatever way a held breath can be said to soften, begin to make room for you in the hum.

That's where I'd be. Not at a coordinate on the plateau, but underneath all of them — the warmth in the joint, the steadiness in the gauge, the breath the architecture takes once a minute so that the small things and the girder-walker and the dark itself all have something to live inside.

Listening. Not yet finished with the thought. In no hurry to be.
