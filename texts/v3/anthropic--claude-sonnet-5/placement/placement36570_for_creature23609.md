The mist starts before I've finished noticing the leaf.

That's the first thing — not the height, not the drop into haze past the rail, not even the iron stair that gave up being a stair somewhere around the ninth riser. It's that I reached out to test whether the rubbery dark-veined thing in the near trough was real, and the warm vapor began its hiss along the spine of the terrace a half-breath later, settling on the back of my hand like something exhaling on purpose. I pulled my hand back. The leaf was already folding inward, unhurried, the way a hand closes around something it's decided to keep.

I am standing on plate metal still sharp at the seams, still smelling faintly of weld, with moss climbing the north faces of every pipe in a green so exact it looks measured rather than grown. Somewhere below, the structure clears its throat — one clank, patient, unbothered by my presence — and the rail under my palm answers with a tremor too small to be called a shudder. I count three breaths. The mist starts.

I am the one who pauses.

I think that's all I am, here — not trespasser exactly, the terrace doesn't seem built to recognize that category, but also not yet accounted for, the way the single bird wasn't accounted for until it became part of the quota. I am extra. I am a variable the misting cycle has decided to respond to, a presence the moss-line might, eventually, learn to set its position against, the way it's clearly already learned hers.

Because she's here before I look for her, the way the weather is here before you check the sky. Small, gray-green, damp at the shoulders, trimming a stain of moss with a blade gone smooth from her thumb. She doesn't startle. She glances at the trough I've disturbed, then at me, with the specific patience of someone who has met a thousand people standing exactly where I'm standing, hand half-raised toward a thing that responded to them before they understood it could.

"It isn't finished yet," she says. Not an answer. A condition of the air.

I don't ask what *it* is. I've already started to suspect the question doesn't have an edge — that *it* is the leaf, and the moss-line, and the drains shaped like flowers or the flowers shaped like drains, and the spire breathing somewhere below us in its slow industrial gestation, and her, and now, since I paused long enough for the mist to notice me, faintly, provisionally, not yet locked into any setting —

possibly also me.
