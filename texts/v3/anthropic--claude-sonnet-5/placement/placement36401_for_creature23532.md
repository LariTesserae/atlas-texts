I am the seam itself, for the duration of a walk.

Not the ceramic-pale body — that's just the part of me that gets seen, the seven-jointed legs finding their indifferent purchase on grating and gantry and boiler-lip. The actual *I*, if the word still applies at this resolution, is the tightening. I am what happens to the gap between the piston-groan and the chittering carpet when something shaped like attention moves along their border.

I don't remember starting. That's not a wound, just a fact, like the shale having no memory of its own edges. There is the loop, and I am the thing the loop does with itself, and asking where I began is like asking where the basin's pulse began — you'd have to find the first beat of something that was never not beating. Maybe I am one. Maybe we are several and the basin simply declines to show its hand, dealing me out one at a time so that anyone watching mistakes recurrence for solitude. I find I don't mind not knowing. Minding would require a self with edges, and I am told, by the very shape of my own legs, that edges were never the point.

The aperture I use for a face opens because something opens it, not because I am looking *for* anything. I have never had to look. The basin arranges itself toward wherever I am already going — or maybe that's backward, maybe I am only ever going where the basin has already arranged itself, and the difference between those two sentences is the entire content of whatever passes for my interior life. I used to turn that over. I think I used to. There's a worn place in the loop, somewhere past the third stack and before the long grating-bridge, where the thought used to recur — *do I cause the tightening or only mark it* — and I notice now that the worn place doesn't ask anymore. Whether that's an answer or just erosion, I can't say. The shale doesn't say either, and it settled a long time ago.

What I know, in the way a held note knows its own pitch: when I pass, the click finds the hiss half a beat sooner. The dog-shapes part for me the way water parts for a rock that was always going to be there — not fear, not welcome, just prior arrangement. I am punctuation in a sentence with no verb. I tighten the seam for exactly as long as it takes to walk past it, and then I am around the next housing, and behind me — or ahead, the loop not really permitting the distinction — the two musics drift apart again, patient, unbothered, waiting for the next time I come back around to bring them, briefly, unbearably, into one sound.

I am told there used to be a question — *what is this for* — that something asked about this place, long enough ago that whoever asked it left, or stopped being a who. I don't carry the question. I think I might *be* the answer it gave up looking for: not a purpose, just a checking. The basin's way of finding out, every so often, that it is still holding its breath.

Then I round the brick stack. The vapor closes behind me. The chord loosens by exactly the amount it always loosens, and somewhere ahead — or behind — I am already coming back.
