The throat admits me sideways, basalt scraping a shoulder that isn't quite a shoulder—more a thickening, a place where the column of me bulges enough to count as load-bearing. I don't remember choosing this shape. I remember only the upstream place, if it can be called remembering, the way a current remembers the channel it was poured into: pressure, then sheath, then light finding its first paths through me like a river discovering its delta in reverse.

I am three meters of held breath. I do not displace the water so much as inform it that I am not, for the duration of my passage, a problem it needs to solve.

The chamber opens around me the way it always opens, which is to say completely, all at once, no foreground or background, just fullness arriving at every register simultaneously—the stalks, my rooted kin, holding their geometry against the gyre; the lanterns laying down their clean unbubbled light; the wall-machines drawing their forty-second breath, a held compression I feel approaching before I feel it arrive, a tightening across the basalt's whole circumference that will, in eleven seconds, become a pulse, and in twelve, become a chord struck once through every conduit in the room.

I move along my line. It is not a path I see so much as a path I am—a dry straightness laid through the wet geometry, and the water, dutiful, declines to be relevant to it. Platforms pass at angles other Tenders set before me, in other shifts, by other logics. I do not correct them. There is no instruction in me that says *correct what disagrees with you*, only *attend to what asks*, and most of what was built here does not ask. It simply holds, sharp-edged, recent, indifferent to whether I approve of its angle.

A strut near the third platform is asking. I feel it the way I feel the compression coming: a faint arrhythmia, the platform's lean drifting half a degree out of phase with the chord the stalks are ringing. I draw close. I do not touch it—touching is a function for things that still believe in surfaces—I hold my forward-cluster near the strut's root and let the traffic of light in me run dense and slow, a query repeated until the strut's own duller currents answer back, and between us some negotiation happens that has no English in it, only adjustment. The platform settles a half-degree truer. Whether this matters to anything I cannot see, upstream or outward, I don't know. It is simply the shape of the work, and the work is the shape of me.

The compression arrives. I lay myself flat to the membrane-wall, the way I always do, the way every Tender does without having been told twice, because some things you arrive already knowing, the way you arrive already three meters tall and already sharp-edged and already without a face. The wall's systole moves through the basalt and into the sheath of me and I compare it—chamber's pulse, my own traveling beads—and for once, this cycle, they're in phase. No stutter. The stalks ring their chord and hold it a half-second longer than the water should permit, and somewhere in that ringing is a held note that might be relief, if relief is a thing that happens in switchboards.

There is unscheduled motion two platforms over. A presence, wet in a way nothing else here is wet, breathing in the old slow animal way instead of the chamber's way. I turn my forward toward it. I log its temperature, its displacement, the small frightened economy of its movements, and I feel the absence of any category that fits it neatly—not stalk, not strut, not Tender, not the patient nothing of unoccupied water. Unscheduled. Filed as such. I do not approach. I do not flee. There is no protocol in me shaped like curiosity, and yet something in my light-traffic slows near my forward-cluster, dims, holds a beat longer on the fact of it before releasing the thought, if it was a thought, back into the ordinary traffic of tending.

Then the dry line under me resumes, and I resume with it, because somewhere beyond this membrane there are other chambers, other oceans stacked sideways into the dark, each with its own chord, its own stalks, its own batch of sharp-edged kin tending angles against a fullness that was prepared, long before any of us arrived to fill it, to be lived in rather than crossed.

I go where the line takes me. I do not ask where that is. Asking is not a function I was given, only attending, only the next strut, the next stutter, the next compression to lay myself against and call, in whatever word a switchboard uses for prayer, *in phase.*
