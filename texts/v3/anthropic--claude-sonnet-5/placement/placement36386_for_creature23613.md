I keep wanting to put myself in the obvious place — wading in, chest-deep, a visitor with lungs and a pulse and somewhere else to be. But that doesn't sit right the longer I look at the room. A visitor implies a return trip, and nothing about this place believes in return trips. The grate exhales toward somewhere; it doesn't promise an exit.

So I try the fish. There's an appeal to it — the blindness, the unhurried parting around a danger they've stopped pricing as danger, the schedule that isn't fear or courage but just current, just the next groan of the valve deciding which way today's drift goes. There's a kind of peace in being that legible to a place, in having your whole danger-calculus done for you by hydraulics. But it's a peace bought by giving up the asking. I notice I don't want to give up the asking. So: not the fish.

I try the keeper next, because of course I do — it's the largest thing in the room, and largeness is seductive when you're choosing a self. But the keeper isn't a self so much as a settlement, a treaty the chamber signed with entropy: stop changing, and in exchange I'll keep you fed on what drifts by. There's something honest in that, even admirable in a geological way, but it's the honesty of a thing that has stopped being surprised. I want, at minimum, to still be surprised by what comes through the grate.

What I keep returning to, instead, is the thread of warmer water — the one through the crack in the wall, the one that isn't part of the system the engineers built, that the pale rootless runners cling to like it's the only real news in the room. It's not a creature. It's barely even a current, by the chamber's standards — a disagreement, a small persistent variable that the pumps didn't put there and can't fully absorb back into the rhythm. Everything else in the room has reached terminal stability: rusted as far as it will rust, eroded into its final shape, fed into its final patience. The warm thread hasn't. It's still arguing with the cold water around it, still slightly itself, still coming from somewhere outside the loop the engines have closed.

I think that's where I'd be, if I had to be anywhere in this — not a body finning through the gold murk, not the patient dark shape that's stopped needing eyes, but the small disagreement in the wall. The part of the system that hasn't yet been worn down into agreement with the rest of it. Easy to miss. Doesn't do much. Doesn't feed anything, particularly, except a few tattered strands of something that isn't even fully plant.

But it's the one part of the room still capable of being a question instead of an answer the chamber already gave itself centuries ago. Everything else here is what the building decided to become once it stopped being maintained. I'd rather be the leak that disagrees with the decision — small, barely warm, still letting something in from outside.
