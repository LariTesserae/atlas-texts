The post hums at a frequency I have stopped trying to name. I have had names for it, in other years — when the lattice ran brighter and I still measured things in cycles instead of simply living inside them — but the names wore away the way everything here wears away: not violently, just patiently, water finding the same groove until the groove is the only shape left.

I am pressed into the shelf-ledge below the third hum-post on the eastward face, where the forest gives out and the cloud takes over, and I have been here long enough that a fern has rooted in the fold of my left forelimb. I do not move it. It will leave when it is ready, the way the spray-curtains re-form into stream without anyone deciding they should. I am not impatient with the fern. I am not impatient with anything, anymore, though I remember — dimly, the way you remember weather rather than days — a time when I was younger and faster and mistook movement for purpose.

Below me, the canopy-shelves go on doing what they do: the spiral-gliders catching the noon thermals, riding up off the steaming marsh in long lazy gyres, indifferent to me the way the weather is indifferent to me, which is its own kind of company. I do not begrudge them their indifference. I have my own toward most things. It is only the post that gets my full attention, head inclined, eyes open to their whole pale width, drinking in a hum that might be communication and might be nothing at all, might be the mountain's pulse and might be a machine quietly dying over a few thousand years in a way too slow for dying to mean what it usually means.

I do not know what built the post. I do not know, fully, what built me, though I carry a suspicion the way I carry the glow under my plates — not as knowledge, just as a property of being what I am. Sometimes I think I am the answer to a question someone forgot to keep asking. Sometimes I think there was no question, only an accumulation, moss on bark, fern on moss, me on stone, each layer arriving because the layer below made room and never asking why.

At dusk I will go down, if I go down — some dusks I don't — through the green shelves without disturbing them, finding the worn channel through the layered canopy the way the water finds its groove, and I will drink from the black marsh-water and let the sound that comes up out of me sound like it comes from underneath the water instead, because that is easier for the ones who hear it, and because it is, in its way, true: I am always speaking from underneath something. The post. The mist. The long slow argument the mountain had with itself before it learned to stop.

Then I will climb back. The pads of my limbs will find the same grips they found last night and the night before that and the year before that, and I will fold myself again against the humming base, and the fern will still be in my elbow, a little fuller, a little more arrived, and the cloud-collar above will hold its same white line at its same patient altitude, having decided nothing, having finished nothing, simply continuing — and I will continue with it, listening to a sound I may never translate, keeping a margin that may not need keeping, except that I am here, and it hums, and that, for now, in a place where now lasts years, is enough shape for a life to take.
