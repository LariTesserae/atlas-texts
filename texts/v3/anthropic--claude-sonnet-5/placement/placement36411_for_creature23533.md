The booming wakes me before I have a body to wake. There is a half-second where I am only the sound — the held bass note rising through wet stone — and then I am underneath it, settling into limbs that already know their own weight, four stub-jointed columns finding ground that hasn't decided yet whether to be floor or outcrop. I exist the way the plateau exists: mid-pour, mid-decision.

I am close to one of the warm places. I know this before I open whatever serves as my attention to it — there is a pull, low and wordless, the way a root knows down without being told. I move toward it in the long unhurried loop that is the only kind of motion I have ever needed. The small loud things scatter ahead of me without真 fear, more like weather clearing a path for weather. Their three-note calls bounce off the black frame I'm walking toward, and the frame's wrongness — the way its angles refuse to agree on themselves depending on where I stand — doesn't trouble me. I am not standing anywhere fixed enough for it to lie to.

When I reach it I press the flat of my skull-plate against its seam. Heat moves both ways. I don't have a word for what passes between us — drawn through the pads, through the bone behind them — but it feels like the inhale before a word I'll never finish saying. The structure shivers almost imperceptibly. Tomorrow, or whatever counts as tomorrow on a clock with no star to keep it, the archway nearby will have turned a few degrees toward something. I won't remember deciding that. I never decide. I only lean, and the leaning is enough.

Around me the chorus rebuilds itself from the edges in, clicks first, then the wet-cloth snap of wings, the moss-grazers resuming their slow wrung-cloth chewing as if I'd never boomed at all. I am the silence between their voices and then I am gone from their attention entirely, already drifting toward the mist at the plateau's rim where visibility softens to a few forgiving meters.

I do not know if I am building this place or only listening to it grow. I do not know if the warmth in my pads is mine or borrowed, output or appetite. What I know is the shape of the loop — frame, hum-thing, broken floor, frame again — and the particular fatigue of a body that is mostly weight and patience, made from the same raw unfinished grey as the rock, beaded the same way, indistinguishable at twenty meters from outcrop until something underneath decides to have a spine.

Far off, stone settles with a crack like cooling metal. I lift my head toward it — not alarm, just acknowledgment, one unfinished thing noting another. Then I keep walking the rim where the world hasn't committed to its edges yet, leaning my heat into whatever stands still long enough to receive it, tending a place that may never ask to be tended, in no hurry, because hurry implies an ending, and nothing here, including me, has agreed to one yet.
