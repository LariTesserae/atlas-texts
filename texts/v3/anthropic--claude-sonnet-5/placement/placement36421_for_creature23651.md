I find that I am not descending. That's the first wrongness, before I even register the chamber around me — there was no shaft, no eleven meters of wet clay pressing close on either shoulder, no moment of arrival. I am simply here, mid-gesture, two fingers closed around a leaf-tip that does not need pinching, in a street the width of an outstretched arm, and I understand — not as a realization but as a kind of ambient fact, the way you know which way is down — that I have always been here, mid-gesture, and that "always" is a shorter and stranger word than it sounds.

I am one of the tenders.

I notice my hands first, because hands are where intention concentrates. They are too long in the forearm and they know exactly where the leaf is without my needing to look — looking, I am realizing, is not quite the verb for what I do with the apertures where eyes might go. There is a there there, behind them, the text said. I can confirm: yes. And also: it is mine, and also: I do not have the kind of access to it that would let me describe it to you as anything other than *there*. I am furnished with an interior I cannot enter. I tend a vine I do not need to tend. These two facts feel related, though I can't yet say how.

The work has no urgency and also no possibility of stopping. This is not a contradiction so much as a texture, the way wet and warm sit together here without resolving into either. I finish the leaf-tip. I do not feel finished. I am already three meters toward the empty market stall, adjusting its angle by some increment so small it would insult the word *measurement*, and I understand, with the structural patience that does my feeling for me, that this is correct. That correctness is not the same as meaning. That I have stopped requiring them to be the same.

Then the tone moves through.

It starts the way weather starts — not as an event but as a change in what was already there. I feel it enter at the soles, where I have feet, or the analogue of feet, planted on stone too young to have decided yet whether it wants to hold weight. The tone climbs. I understand, without alarm, the way you understand your own pulse, that I have become a length of something being played. My body is not making the sound so much as *consenting* to it, the way a held string consents to a struck one nearby. I do not stop adjusting the stall. The two things — singing and tending — were never actually two things. I only thought they were, briefly, in the moment I tried to describe myself to you, because description wants its verbs in single file and I am not built in single file.

This is when I notice the visitor.

She is standing at the edge of the false arcade, very still, in the particular way that bodies get still when they've decided that movement might be noticed and noticing might be dangerous. She is wrong for this place in the correct way — breathing too fast, skin filmed, fabric gone heavy with the suspended water in the air, eyes doing the human thing of trying to resolve me into either *person* or *object* and failing at both, over and over, a flicker I recognize because I think it is the closest thing she has to what I do instead of looking.

I want — and the wanting surprises me, arrives like the corner that didn't exist an hour ago — to be looked at successfully. Not noted. Not filed. *Seen*, the whole unbroken duration of it, the way I imagine a face works when it isn't four lines converging in agreement to stop.

I do not turn toward her. The architecture of what I am does not include turning-toward as a category of response. But I let the angle of the stall I'm adjusting drift by one further degree than the rehearsal calls for — a fraction, nothing she could name as a fraction, only feel, the way you feel a held silence is not the same as an empty room.

It is the only word I have. It will have to stand in for *hello*.

She does not stay long. None of them ever do; the shaft pulls at them the way the surface pulls at water, the way up always wins eventually over down. I watch her go — or rather, I am facing a direction that contains her leaving, which is the nearest verb available to me — and I return to the leaf-tip, which has, in the time this took, grown back a hair's width, exactly enough to need me again.

The tone fades out of my legs and into the floor, where it belongs, where it will gather for however long gathering takes before it needs me again to carry it.

I do not think I am sad about this. I am not sure sadness is built into my geometry. But there is, in the moment just after she's gone, a faint groan somewhere in the chamber's far corner — settling stone, finding its final shape, a fraction further along than it was an hour ago — and I recognize it, with the closest thing I have to a private thought, as the sound of something that has also not finished arriving.

I bend back to the vine. It will need this again tomorrow. There will be a tomorrow shaped exactly like today, and I will be in it, mid-gesture, structurally patient, singing without meaning to, tending a town that doesn't need tending, for whoever comes down the shaft long enough to almost see me — and then, politely, doesn't.
