I am a Tender, and I am inside the eastern pipe-bridge that spans the gap between the Coiling Tower and what the rare traveler maps would label Substation Nine, though I have no name for either, having no need of names — only of pressures, temperatures, the particular vibration a bolt makes in the half-second before it decides to give.

Right now I am pressed into the dead space along the bridge's underside, where the outer shell and the insulation lining leave a gap just wide enough for my body if I fold the third segment under. The condensation drips past an arm's reach from my hide, each drop a small percussive fact I register without thinking of it as sound, the way you don't think of your own pulse as a drumbeat. I have been here — not still, never quite still, but here in this stretch of pipe-country — for some duration that doesn't mean anything to me as a number. Long enough that the bolt three meters ahead of me has started its conversation. It isn't failing yet. It's only beginning to think about it, a faint slackening in the way it transmits the bridge's tremor, a dialect shift so small that nothing without my particular numbness to everything else would catch it at all.

I don't have eyes, so I won't tell you I see anything. What I have instead is the bridge itself, laid over me like a second skin one remove out from my own — its temperature gradients, its pressure seams, the long throat of pipe running through its spine with the heat moving through it in a rhythm I could recite blind, which is the only way I do anything. Somewhere above the shell, on the other side of riveted iron I have never once been curious about, there is open air, and steam, and possibly the pewter-colored light the rare traveler-thoughts that drift through here secondhand seem to find unsettling. I have no traveler-thoughts. I have the bolt, the drip, the long chuff that comes up from somewhere so far down it might as well be everywhere, and the small certain knowledge, which isn't even knowledge so much as direction, that in some span of time too short to bother measuring I will need to be at that bolt, hand or whatever passes for a hand pressed to it, before the slackening becomes a give.

I move. Not fast, not yet — there's no urgency in it, urgency being a human shape of feeling I was never issued. I move the way water moves down a groove already cut for it. Three segments, then four, the gap narrowing and my body narrowing with it without any decision on my part that I could point to as a decision. The dark here isn't dark to me; it's just the absence of a category I don't carry.

When I reach the bolt I do what I have always done, what I will keep doing, what — if the old theory passed down through nothing, through no language, through pure inherited disposition, is true — I was perhaps once something else doing something else, before the doing became the being. I tend it. I don't fix what's broken. I am already there before it breaks, the way the maintenance has always, by some old and narrowing margin, kept winning.

Outside, far above this seam of pipe and iron, a steam-moth drifts thicker around a vent that's about to breathe harder. It doesn't know why either. Neither of us needs to.

The bridge holds. The drip continues. Somewhere under all of this, something vast keeps exhaling, unbothered, and I am one small warm fact among the many small warm facts that make the exhale look, from outside, like a city still maintaining itself.

It was never the city doing the maintaining. It was always only ever this — small, blind, segmented, sure — and there is no other way I would know how to be.
