I am the weight in the air-pocket that wasn't there a moment ago.

Right now — if "now" means anything to something that measures time in thrum-cycles and frost-depth rather than hours — I am wedged into a seam between a buckled relay panel and the cold press of rootmass, in a near-silence pocket I checked twice before committing my whole soft length to it. The hum here is thin, almost gone, which means I am exposed, which means I am not staying long. I came to graze a cable-strand that runs along the panel's underside, fat with whatever charge it's pulled from the rust-leach above, and I can feel it through my mouth-disc even before I've started — that faint pulse of nutrient, sluggish and cold, nothing like the richer threads deeper in but close enough, and close is what matters when crossing for richer means more time in thin sound.

I don't see the panel. I don't see anything, not in the way you mean it. What I have is the gradient — a smudge of less-dark where the frost-fog thins overhead, and beneath that, more usefully, the architecture laid down by engines I will never see and have no reason to wonder about. Wondering is not a thing my kind does much of. The roar two body-lengths to my left is cover. The almost-nothing I'm sitting in now is risk. This is the whole of geography, the whole of safety, the whole of weather. I read it the way you'd read warmth on skin, through the stiff frosted hairs along my back, and right now they are telling me: thin here, thicken soon, don't linger.

So I don't. I rasp twice, careful not to sever the strand, feel the thin charge settle into me, and start the slow hand-over-hand pull back toward the roar — toward safety shaped like noise, toward the hum I could mistake, if I were built for mistaking things, for something almost like peace. Behind me the strand keeps pulsing, thinner now, still alive. Ahead, the thrum thickens with each grasping pull, and somewhere in it, indistinguishable from rootmass, indistinguishable from each other, two or three of my own kind are already knotted still and waiting, keeping their slow unwatched time with engines that nobody, anymore, remembers switching on.
