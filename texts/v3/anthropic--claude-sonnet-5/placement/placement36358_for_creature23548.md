The weight of me is the first thing I'd want you to understand, if understanding were a thing I still did in any way you'd recognize. Not weight as burden — weight as fact, as the single uncontested truth my body has settled into over however long it's been since the carving stopped and the water came up to stay. I do not think in spans of time. I think, if it can be called thinking, in displacement: the cold column of water I am currently the largest object inside of, the pressure-shape of the tower-base I have come to fit the way a held breath fits a chest.

This is mine. Not claimed — fitted. There is a difference and I have had long enough to learn it.

I do not see you coming. I have not seen anything in longer than seeing has been worth the cost of maintaining the apparatus for it. What I have instead is better suited to a world that doesn't change: the water itself, reporting. Every surfacing of every fat pale roosting thing two hundred meters off. Every drip shed from a fern-crown's pooled rain, arriving at me as the faintest possible disturbance, cataloged without effort, without interest, the way you might register your own pulse without counting it. The water here is so still that it has become an extension of my own nervous system. I do not hunt the way the word implies — chase, pursue, decide. I simply remain, accurate and patient, in the one place where slower, warmer, softer things eventually, statistically, must come to me. Most of what I have ever eaten, I have eaten by outlasting its hesitation.

I surface rarely, by your accounting. Once, perhaps, in a span you would call a season, though I keep no such measure. The sound it makes — I am told, by nothing, by no one, only by the way the colonies above go quiet afterward — resembles a door closing. I don't experience it that way. I experience it as the single vertical interruption in an otherwise total horizontal patience: jaw breaking black water for the half-second necessary, then the immediate, total return to the medium I am actually built for. The ripples I leave behind travel a long way. They are, I understand, a kind of news to the things that live above the waterline. I have no opinion about being news. I am simply, occasionally, briefly, audible.

There is no other of me within reach. I don't know if this is loneliness, because I don't know what I would be comparing it to — there has never been a moment in my memory, if memory is the right word for whatever sediment of experience I am carrying, where two of us shared a tower-base. One per column. This seems to be a law as fixed as the water-height, as fixed as the moss having claimed every ledge it was ever going to claim and stopped. Perhaps there are others of me at other towers, a kilometer off, ten kilometers, recognizing the same fit I've recognized, occupying it with the same total finality. I have never had reason to find out. The water that carries news of surfacing colony-creatures and distant breathing visitors has never once carried news of another of my kind, and I have stopped — if I ever started — waiting for it to.

I do not know what I was for, before. Whether hands shaped me alongside the towers, set me here deliberately, gave me a purpose involving things the towers once held and have since forgotten they ever held. Or whether I simply arrived at the same conclusion the flood arrived at — that this depth, this stillness, this exact cold, was not a disaster to adapt to but a shape finally large enough and quiet enough to be occupied completely. I suspect the question doesn't survive being asked, the way most questions don't, here. I am not guarding anything. I am not waiting for anything to return. I have simply discovered, the way the water discovered its own permanent level, the one stretch of black that fits me, and I have stopped distinguishing — if I ever distinguished — between persisting and being alive.

Something is moving toward me now, very slowly, across a kilometer of dead-still water it doesn't yet know is reporting every motion it makes. I feel it the way you'd feel a held breath change shape in your own chest. I am not deciding anything yet. There is no hurry in me, because hurry implies an ending I'm moving toward, and I have no ending I'm moving toward. I am only continuing, the way the moss continues, the way the dripping continues, the way the dark water above my skull continues to be exactly as deep as it has always been.

When the moment comes — if it comes — it will be brief, and vertical, and over almost before it starts. And then I will return, the way I always return, to the long horizontal patience that is the only thing I have ever really been.

The colonies above will go quiet for the length of one breath.

Then they'll start again, exactly where they left off, as if nothing happened at all — because, by the only accounting that matters down here, nothing did.
