The hook-blade's at my belt before I've decided to put it there — that's the first thing I notice about being here, that the body already knows the gesture, has known it for sixty-some years, and the deciding self just rides along a half-step behind.

I'm old. Not frail — there's a difference the Wards teach you early, between worn-soft and worn-out — but old in the specific way that means I no longer test a vine's tension so much as *recall* it, the way you'd recall a person's voice before you'd recognize their face. My hands are stained that permanent green-black, the moss-and-oil color, and I think of it the way another kind of person might think of a wedding ring: not decoration, just the visible fact of a long arrangement.

I'm standing where the lane widens near the gasworks tower, in the cool gold-green light that never quite commits to morning or afternoon, and I'm doing the thing the young ones notice and don't have a word for — I've gone still a beat too long, attention out past the conversation I'm not having, listening to the chimney-vine the way you'd listen for a sleeper's breath to make sure it hasn't changed pattern overnight. It hasn't. It won't, not for another decade at least. I know this without checking it, the way I know my own pulse without taking it.

There's a child somewhere behind me — I can hear the particular scrape of a small hand clearing fern-choke from the dry fountain's basin, dutiful, slightly resentful, exactly as every wardkeep child has sounded doing that exact job for longer than anyone's kept count. I won't go correct their technique. They'll get it by feel, same as I did, same as whoever taught me did, back before I was the one people meant when they said *one of the old ones*.

If you stopped me here and asked whether it troubles me — the moss insulating on purpose, the roots a little too conveniently placed, the sense that the green isn't colonizing the brick so much as *clocking in* — I think I'd give you the shrug before the words. *Of course it's doing a job.* What unsettles a visitor is just Tuesday to me. I rest two fingers against the vine-trunk at the tower's base, not asking it anything, just confirming what I already know: still here, still working, same as me.

The conveyor will run at its hour regardless of what either of us decides about the metaphysics of it. I have a lamp mantle to replace before dusk. That's the whole of today's theology, and it's enough.
