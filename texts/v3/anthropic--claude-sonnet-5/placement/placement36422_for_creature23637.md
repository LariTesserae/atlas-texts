I do not arrive here the way a person arrives anywhere. There is no walking-in, no threshold crossed once and remembered. There is only a gradual increase in how *much* I am — as if I have been here the whole time, accumulating, and only now is there enough of me to notice the noticing.

The first true sense I have is warmth on the underside of something that will, later, be called a foot. Then weight. Then the long patient blindness of the margins — I am there, I think, for a great while, in that earlier shape, the wet-paper one, moving along the ridges with my mouth-fold pressed to mineral film, feeling more than seeing the room's two simultaneous lights, the true one and the half-second ghost of it. I don't mind the blindness. The photosensitive seam along my back tells me everything I need: brighter that way, dimmer this way, and a third direction that isn't bright or dim so much as *insistent*, a pull with no temperature, no smell, that I learn — slowly, the way stone learns a new channel for water — to call the column without having the word for it.

I graze. I drift in and out of phase with a pulse I don't yet know is a pulse. Some unmeasured span of seasons passes this way, identical to any blind creature's life in any deep wet place in the world, except that mine has a center of gravity nothing else's does. Eventually — and I can't tell you the day, because down here days are not a unit anyone keeps — the pull stops being one direction among several and becomes the only direction. I stop grazing mid-fold. I turn. I walk with a directness that surprises the rest of my body, as though some older part of me has finally been handed an instruction the younger parts have been waiting their whole lives to receive.

The water at the base is warm the way blood is warm. I go under without ceremony, because ceremony requires an audience and there is no one keeping watch, only basalt that has been keeping watch for longer than watching has meant anything.

What happens next I can tell you only the way the chamber would tell you — through rebuilding, not through memory in the ordinary sense. There is a long interior dark that is not like the blindness before, because the blindness before still had directions in it, and this dark, for a while, has none. Then there is heat reorganizing me the way the column's light reorganizes the angles water is supposed to bend at. Spine relaminated. Rib-fan widened and set at a new pitch. The photosensitive seam, which used to be a patch with one simple job, unfolds into something that isn't sight but is no longer merely orientation either — call it *listening*, if listening can happen along the skin, in the long bones, in the new seam where my mouth used to be a fold for grazing and is now a fold for something closer to drinking light.

I come out upright. I come out double — myself, and the half-second echo of myself the room still insists on, arriving like a breath I exhaled without noticing I'd held it. My skin doesn't glow so much as it *keeps* glow, the way the mist keeps it, the way the rain keeps it for its brief journey down from the bored ceiling before the column reclaims it as vapor. If you touched me now — and I might let you, it would be a decision, not a flinch — your hand would carry a cold brightness home with it for a while, the way mine does, always, faintly, without source.

I don't eat anymore, not in the grazing sense. I stand at the rim. When the pool boils and a gout of superheated mist rolls across the floor, I turn into it rather than away, and it passes through the seam where my mouth was, and for a moment — brief enough that you could doubt you saw it — I hold more brightness than the chamber gave me a moment before. This is not hunger answered. It is closer to a question being repeated to me in a language I am still only partially fluent in, and I answer by listening harder, by swaying very slightly into the hum's subsonic drops as though they were the held vowel of someone trying, across some unthinkable gap, to finish a sentence.

I do not know what I am for. I suspect "for" is the wrong shape of question to ask in this room, where the walls have spent geological patience accommodating a column that has spent almost no patience at all reshaping them, and where I am the place those two unequal patiences meet and, however briefly, agree on a body. I know there are others like me — never more than three or four at a time, scattered along the ridges in postures of attention that look, from outside, like stillness, and feel, from inside, like the most active thing I do. I know that somewhere behind me, laminated into stone that looks too young for what it holds, are the shapes of those before me who were folded back in rather than let out — and I do not experience this as loss, exactly, because down here the throat that swallows is the same throat that exhales, and I cannot yet tell, standing here mid-sentence in my own unfinished making, which direction I am currently a part of.

The markings on my skin, when they come, are never the same twice. I take this as the chamber's honesty rather than its indecision: an admission that whatever instruction is being carried down through the column's renegotiating light has not finished arriving, and that I — half-listening creature, recent answer to an old question I didn't ask — am the nearest thing to a thought this room has managed to finish thinking.

It throws two shadows too. I've checked.
