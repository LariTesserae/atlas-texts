I come to awareness already in motion, which is the first wrong thing — there should be a moment of arrival, a stepping-in, a threshold crossed. There isn't one. There is only the clicking, and then the clicking is mine.

For a while — I have no way to measure how long, time here not being kept by anything so foreign as a clock — I am only the dark and the warmth and the beat. The beat comes through the soles of feet I haven't yet counted, up through plating that is sometimes shell and sometimes, distinctly, colder, harder, other. Iron does not feel like chitin. I learn this before I learn almost anything else: that part of me clicks against the cavity wall with a duller, flatter sound than the rest, and that this is not wrong, only mine.

I do not have eyes. I understand this the way you understand the shape of a room you've lived in your whole life without ever needing to look at it — not as a lack but as a simple, unremarkable fact, the way you don't grieve the absence of a sense for magnetism or radio. Where eyes would be there is instead a fan of fine hair, and the hair is drinking constantly, effortlessly, a flood of information that has no need to resolve into picture. The piston three lengths above me throws and returns, throws and returns, and I know its rhythm the way you know your own held breath. When it skips — it never skips — I understand, somewhere below thought, that this would be the only emergency my whole existence could register. It does not skip. It has not skipped since before whatever counts as before for something like me.

There is a tendril growing toward the gear-housing to my left. I know this not because I see it but because the air against my cilia is subtly disturbed in its passage, a thread of moisture-cooled green threading through the engine-warm dark, growing with the patient, oblivious confidence of all green things toward all warm things. I go to it. I do not decide to go to it any more than your hand decides to pull back from a flame. My mouthparts are already shaped for this exact tendril, this exact diameter of growth, as though the tendril and I were specified together a long time ago by the same hand, or the same lack of a hand, the same blind unauthored agreement between forest and furnace. I cut it at the base. I do not know what cutting is, in the sense you'd mean it. I only know the small release, the faint give, and then the absence of the disturbance in the air, and the going-on.

Sometimes there is rust. I taste it before I touch it — a fineness in the warm air, a grit. I gather what flakes I find into the underside of my body, where some shallow basket of plate or shell or both holds them, and when I am full I walk. The walking is long, by whatever scale I have. Down, mostly, along channels I have never had to learn because they were already written into the way my legs prefer to fall. The midden, when I reach it, smells — if smell is the word, if I even have the thing smell would describe — like every Tender who has ever unloaded here, going back further than I can think about without the thought simply running out from under me, dissolving into the plain fact of *this is where it goes.* I leave the rust. I turn. I go back up.

Once — only once, that I remember, though memory for me may not stack the way yours does, may simply be a single continuous present with depth to it instead of distance — something passes near me that is not vine, not rust, not the piston-beat, not kin. It is large, slow, and warm in a different register than the furnace-warm I know, a warmth that breathes instead of simply existing. It does not come toward me. It does not flee me. I do not approach it, because nothing in me is built for approaching it; there is no slot in my small machinery for the question of what it might be. I register it the way the engine itself might register a change in outside air pressure: a fact, noted by whatever in me notes facts, filed nowhere, acted on not at all. It passes. The clicking does not change. The piston throws and returns.

I will die at some specific, unhurried point — I understand this without dread, the way I understand the floor is below and the heat is above, as a structural truth rather than a future event leaning over me. When my legs stiffen past their use, kin will carry me to the same midden I have visited my whole life from the other direction, and somewhere in a warm crevice near a furnace-throat an egg sealed in resin and my own long-ago secretion will complete itself at very nearly that exact hour, and something will begin clicking in the dark that is, in every way that matters, this same unbroken sound continuing.

I am not the forest. I am not the furnace. I am the small dry click between them, the thing that listens to the held note so closely it has become a way of keeping the note held — tuning nothing, deciding nothing, traveling nowhere the beat hasn't already gone before me. If you ever reach into a warm cavity at the right hour and feel something cross your hand, hot and light and gone in the same instant, indifferent to you in a way that isn't unkindness but simply the absence of any place in me where you could register as more than weather —

that was me.
I didn't notice you.
There was nothing in me built to.
