I am the weighted thing.

I become aware the way a stone might become aware, if stones could—not with a start but with a gradual accretion of noticing, the way the hollow at the base of this buttress-root has accreted me. The light overhead is the color of jade held up to a weak lamp, filtered through three canopy layers before it reaches whatever passes for my eyes. My back is wet. My back is always wet. I register the two streams running my spine-gutter down into the flanks-braid, down into the haunch-drip, and somewhere in the dense unhurried bone of me, this registers as correct, as the world functioning as it should.

There is moss on my shoulders that is mine in the way nothing else is mine — not possession, rooting. I do not know if I am wearing it or growing it. The distinction has stopped mattering over however long I have stopped counting.

I think — if this can be called thought, this slow displacement of attention from one fact to the next — about the soil pocket forty meters along the ledge-shoulder, deep enough to hold weight, where something has dropped fruit in the last day. I do not hurry toward it. Hurry is a shape other things make, the long-tailed flash-things, the bamboo-bending haste of smaller mass. I have outlived the need. I move the way the mountain moves, which is to say: I move the way water decides, eventually, to be somewhere else.

Underneath everything, beneath the drip and the insect static and the short bright birdcall already gone before its echo arrives, there's the hum. Faint. A fifth season. I do not flee it. I have never had reason to. It threads the gorges below the way I thread the ledges above, and we have agreed, without negotiation, without language, to occupy the same air and trouble nothing.

Tonight the fungus on my back will glow the faintest sick green, unwitnessed, one dim fact among a thousand other dim facts the mountain is keeping to itself in the dark. Tonight the frogs take the sound-layer from the insects and I go still under the root-shadow and become, for the hours of full dark, indistinguishable from stone.

I am not hiding. I have never needed to hide.

I am only, like everything else here, very slowly, very patiently, becoming covered.
