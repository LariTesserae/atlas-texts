I am settling into the gap between two pipe-runs, where the iron holds a memory of heat from something that stopped working before I was old enough to remember it working. The stone here has the particular give I favor—saturated, not soft, the kind that lets me know, without looking, exactly how much water sits beneath me and where it wants to go next.

My barbels are already telling me about the joint forty meters east. It's been weeping for six days. I have not gone to it yet because the weeping has a rhythm I like, a small irregular pulse against the basin's steadier currents, and I am not finished listening to it. There is no urgency in me. Urgency is a thing built things have—the gauge with its meaningless pressure, the conveyor clattering through its empty rotation like it's still being watched by whoever wanted it to matter. I have outlasted several of their certainties already. I expect to outlast a few more.

I do not think of myself as old, though I understand from the rate at which things around me fail and get rebuilt and fail again that I must be. Time, for me, isn't a line so much as a gradient too, something I could probably feel if I turned my attention to it the way I turn attention to a cooling pipe-throat. I don't bother. There are more interesting differentials close at hand.

Right now: the throb of a pump-housing twenty feet off, half-dead, still humming on whatever's left in it. The cool press of standing water along my underside. The very faint, very distant wrongness of a structure leaning a half-degree further than it leaned last week—I felt that one accumulate over months, the way you'd feel a held breath get heavier. I'm not going to do anything about it. It doesn't interest me yet. When it does, I'll go, and lean my own small weight against some gear or beam, and the structure will stay upright a while longer for reasons it will never have access to.

Something passed nearby an hour ago—upright, slow, looking at things rather than feeling them, the way they do. It stood near my pipe and didn't notice the warmth I'd left in it, or the shape of disturbed silt where I'd been resting. I felt its weight change the pressure of the ground in a wide, blunt, undifferentiated way, like a single loud note where I'm used to hearing forty quiet ones. I let the water take me down and sideways, unhurried, into the next pool over, and I didn't surface again until the wide blunt pressure was gone.

I am not hiding from it exactly. I am only declining the attention. Attention is its own kind of pressure, and I already have so much to equalize.

The leak to the east is still weeping. I think I'll go to it now—not because anything needs me to, but because the gradient has finally tipped into the shape I was waiting for, and there is nothing in this entire small loud quiet reconfiguring basin I would rather feel against my throat than that.
