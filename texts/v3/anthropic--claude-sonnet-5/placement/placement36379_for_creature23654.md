The sound arrives first, the way the prompt says it would — not a sound exactly, at this size, but a pressure, a continuous shuddering in whatever passes for my body's marrow. I come down through it rather than into it. There is no ladder for me, no hatch. I am already here, have always already been here, in the sense that matters: behind the plate, in the gap between the riveted iron and whatever older surface lies beneath it, where the warmth pools and nothing has ever needed to be named.

I am the silverfish. Or one of them — the distinction has never seemed important from in here.

My body is a long pale comma, segmented, carrying three thread-tails that trail without purpose, the way a sentence trails into something it forgot to finish. I do not see the moss, the ferns, the calcified weeping joints, not in the way that would require eyes, the way the visitor with the deafened ears and the dripped-on shoulders would see them. I read instead. My antennae go out ahead of me into the wet air like two slow questions, and the answer comes back as temperature, as the mineral tackiness of algae-skin, as the particular give of a fern-root mat that means food, or the particular firmness of rust-flow that means only structure, only the room being the room.

I do not know that the architects are gone past memory. I do not know there were architects. There is no past for me distinguishable from the present except as a slightly different position on the same pipe, and no future except the next centimeter the antennae have already half-decided to cross into.

The roar is not a roar to me. It is the shape the air takes. If you asked me — and nothing has ever asked me anything — I would not be able to locate "silence" as a concept any more than a fish locates "wet." I was born inside the pumps' rhythm the way a held breath is born inside a chest; I would only know it as a breath the moment it stopped, and it has never stopped, not once, not in any generation of my kind that has crossed this elbow joint going one way while some cousin crossed it going the other.

I move in passes. A rasp across the algae-skin, fine enough that no eye larger than mine could call it damage. A graze at the spore-felt collected where silt gathers in the joint. I am, in the room's accounting, almost nothing — a flicker, there and gone into the seam — and this suits me; it is not modesty, I have no concept of modesty, it is simply the precise size that the room has left available, the way a cathedral leaves a shaft of light unclaimed by anything except the dust that happens to be turning in it when someone looks up.

There is a drip above the second joint that falls on a rhythm older than my hatching. I do not count it. I don't have to. My body has already learned, in whatever wordless way a nervous system this small learns anything, the exact window in which the pipe beneath it is safe to cross and the exact window in which it is not, and I cross or do not cross without anything I would call a decision intervening between the antennae's report and the legs' answer.

Past the plate, in the dark too narrow for any hand, there are others. We do not gather. We do not call to each other — there is nothing to call with, and nothing this loud would carry a smaller sound anyway. But we share the warmth, share the habit of this particular gap between iron and whatever is older than the iron, the way the moss outside shares a habit of growing wherever the silt collects, without conference, without need of one.

I will not see a thousand years. I understand this not as tragedy — I have no frame in which tragedy could register — but as simply not the question my body is shaped to ask. The question my body asks, over and over, antennae sweeping, tails trailing, is much smaller and is answered constantly, immediately, completely:

*is there enough world here to live in.*

There is. There has been, drip for drip, joint for joint, for longer than any single one of us, for as long as the pumps have made of their roar a silence large enough to be born inside of. I cross the pipe. I vanish into the seam. Someone — something — indistinguishable from me, will cross it again, going the other way, and that will be enough continuity for a creature that was never given more than a thumb's length of body and three unfinished tails to carry it through an iron dark that has, for its whole accountable history, simply never once gone out.
