I would not see anything. That's the first fact, and it arrives before any other — not as deprivation but as simple, unremarkable truth, the way you don't miss a sense you were never given a name for. My eyes, if you want to call them that, are two pale dimples that register only the smear of light from the structural seams, enough to know up from down, gleam from dark, nothing more. So I am not looking at the Cistern Heights. I am *hearing* it, and being heard by it, and that is so much larger a thing than looking that I almost pity whatever creature would try to take this place in through eyes alone. Eyes would drown in this dark and learn nothing.

What I have instead are vanes. Paired, fluted, ribbed along the skull like the wax-terraces I cling to — and that's not a coincidence, that's the whole design: I am made out of the same listening material as the rock that made me. I send out small sounds, dozens a second, too quick and too high for anything without my vanes to notice, and the world answers back. A tower answers in one register, dense and near. A bridge answers thinner, with a tremble in it from its own structural hum. A body — kin, packed shoulder to shoulder with me in the fold of stone where we hang — answers in a register all its own, breathing-warm, damp, alive, indistinguishable from the next body except in the smallest details of pitch that I have learned, without ever choosing to learn it, to read like a name.

I am pressed flat right now against a wax-terrace, one of thousands doing the same along this undercroft, wings mantled tight, my plated hide holding the cold condensation as a second skin so that anything without my own ears would mistake me for more terrace, more cooled stone, one fold among the plateau's endless half-finished folds. This is not hiding, exactly. It's closer to agreement — the rock decided to be this shape, more or less, and so did I, and the difference between us is mostly a matter of pace.

Around me: the roar. Not chaos — I want to be clear about that, because to anything without vanes it would sound like chaos, a continuous wet-throated din of click and chitter and long low moan, wingbeats somewhere off to the left displacing air in a gust I feel before I hear. But inside the roar there is structure so fine it would take a human lifetime to learn and I was simply born already knowing it. That moan, three towers over, pitched to ride the stone — that's a male, boasting, and I know without thinking about it whether he's strong or just loud, whether his vent is rich or thin, by nothing but the way the moan decays. That stuttering pattern threading under everything else, half-buried — a mother, somewhere in the pools below, calling a brood that isn't mine, but I know its shape the way you'd know a stranger's name spoken in another room.

I feed without urgency. My plates draw the bead of mineral-laced wet straight off the stone, and further down, past the bridge that hums in my teeth whether I want it to or not, a vent bleeds something warmer and richer into a trough, and there is a low frenzy of wet sound there always, kin gathering, kin contesting, a war fought in inches over years, slower than my own life will be long.

And underneath all of it — under the roar, under the hum, under even the patient war for the vents — there is a stillness so vast that my vanes can barely find its edges. The towers are not moving, except that over years they are. The rock is not changing, except that over decades it is. Somewhere in a seam whose glow is a little stronger than the others, I sense, more than hear, something that moves at the speed of the stone itself — jointed, slow, fused half into the wall it tends, smoothing an edge that won't be finished in my lifetime or my mother's mother's. I have no name for it that isn't borrowed. It does not call. I have never once heard it answer my voice, though I have sent sound toward it, the way you'd send a question toward something too large to need you.

I am small, and loud, and quick, and built from the same wet wax as everything around me, one voice in a roar that has been going on since before I had ears to add to it. Somewhere above me the dark is crowded with wings I cannot see and do not need to. I press closer to the terrace. The stone is cold, and patient, and not yet finished deciding what it wants to be, and I am, for now, exactly what it has decided so far.
