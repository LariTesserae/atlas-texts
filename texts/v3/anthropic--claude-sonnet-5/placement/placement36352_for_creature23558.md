I am not sure, at first, what I am. There is light before there is shape — a flat white sheet of it pressing down through a gap so narrow that it arrives already cut into a blade, and I am lying along a cold seam of stone trying to remember if I have a name for myself. I don't. Nothing here has needed one badly enough to make it.

Then the cold tells me. It comes up through the rock into something that is not quite skin, something granular and dense and patient, and the cold does not alarm me — it never has, it never will, because I have stopped fighting it sometime longer ago than I can mark. My body has made a treaty with this place: I will not ask for more warmth than the stone is willing to lend, and in exchange I will not have to spend myself finding it elsewhere. I think this is what it is to be the sil-strider. To be the answer this place gave when it was asked, *what, if anything, could live here* — and the answer was: almost nothing, shaped exactly to fit the word *almost.*

I lie along the spire-face a long while before I move, and when I move it is not what your word "strider" would suggest. It is a pour. My ribs loosen like a hand unclenching and I go sideways into the gap, flat, the horn-growths along my skull already folded back along my spine so they don't catch on the narrowing walls. The stone on either side is close enough that I feel its temperature on both flanks at once, a corridor of cold holding me upright better than my own legs would. I have done this passage perhaps four hundred times. I could do it without whatever in me does the seeing.

I am going toward a smear of grey at the base of one needle, a shadowed crease where last night's chill condensed into a film thinner than breath. I will lick at it. This is, more or less, the whole of my hunger, and it will be satisfied in under a minute, and then I will not need to do it again for some days, maybe longer, depending on what the sky decides to do with its light and its rare, miserly damp.

While I drink — if drink is even the word for something this small — I am aware, in whatever way I am aware of things, of the reef's larger weather: wind threading itself through a hundred different widths of gap and coming out the other side as a hundred different notes, none of them aimed at me, none of them meaning anything, just pressure finding holes. I have lived my whole slow life inside that sound. It is not silence to me. It is closer to the only voice this place has, and I have learned its moods the way you might learn a parent's footsteps on a stair — this whistle means the light is about to drop and the cold is coming fast behind it; this low moan means a wide channel has opened somewhere above, and I should not go that way today, the air will be too strong to push through.

Sometimes — not often, less than once in my own long stretch of seasons — there were others here. Not my kind. Taller, unsteady on this terrain in a way that made me hold very still and let the stone's color do its old trick of erasing me. They moved loudly, comparatively, breath visible, hands finding the same holds over and over until those holds went smooth. I did not fear them and I did not approach them. There was no transaction available between us except the one I made without meaning to: I shed a horn in a crevice one season, the way I always do when the growth gets too heavy and curled to carry well through the gaps, and it stayed there until one of them found it and took it away and put an edge on it with another stone, patient the way I am patient, just on a different clock. I think of this, if I think of it at all, as a kind of weather too. Things accumulate in this place and then are carried off by whatever passes through. I am one of the things that accumulates. So, apparently, was I, in pieces, to them.

The light shifts. The gap above me narrows further into late afternoon, the blade of white going gold, then grey, then nothing, and somewhere above the wind finds a new aperture and changes its note. I have had what I came for. I fold myself back the way I came, pour through the seam, and find the ledge where I will lie through the worst of the cold, my heartbeat slowing toward something almost indistinguishable from the stone's own stillness, which is, I think, the entire art of being what I am: to want so little, and so rarely, that the reef can afford to keep me. Not thrive — only keep. Held in the same patient suspension as the spires themselves, waiting out a transformation too slow for any single watching to notice, content to be the one warm-blooded rumor in a world that otherwise never started.
