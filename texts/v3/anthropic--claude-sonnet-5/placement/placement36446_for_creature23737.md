The roar comes through the soles of my hooked feet before it reaches anything I'd call an ear, and I am already still, already spine-flared, already reading.

I am small against the scale of this place — the trunks around me rise like the legs of something that gave up walking eons ago and decided to simply be a world instead — but smallness was never a problem here. Smallness is what lets me fit into the fiber-lattice, hang from the underhooks while both hands work, go where the big dying cliffs of old trunk-mass cannot follow. I was born in a hollow bole that ran warm off an engine none of my kin-cluster could name, and I will probably die in another one much like it, a few foldings of canopy further on, when this fiber-mezzanine finally gives out its slow vegetable collapse and we move, unhurried, toward the next sound that says *home*.

Right now I am working a grille. My fingers — three and a thumb-analog, strong rather than fine — pull a season's vine-growth free of a housing that has been turning, by the worn and weathered look of it, since long before there was a we to clear it. It runs warm under my palm, warmer than the cool thin highland air around it, and that warmth is the entire reason I'm here: a clutch is coming, laid in the fiber-nest just below this vent, and warm grilles mean shells that don't crack too early in the cold nights. I don't ask what the engine is doing down in the root-dark making that heat. The question has the wrong shape. It would be like asking what the mountain is *for*. The engine is terrain. I tend terrain so the terrain doesn't kill what I love.

The storm-front is still a few kilometers off, but my spine already has its shape — a long, low pressure-chord moving through fused trunk-columns toward me, and underneath it, woven into it, the higher erratic threads of two flock-masses, one large, one larger, converging on a feeding ground I know without seeing. None of this comes to me as sound the way you'd mean sound. It comes as direction, as weight, as the rough good health or bad health of distant wood, the way a hand laid flat on a table tells you a truck is coming long before headlights do. My eyes, such as they are, give me almost nothing in this strobing dapple-light — gold, black, gold, meaningless, changing faster than sight can use. So I don't look. I feel the canopy do its long architecture-wide thing, and I understand it the way you'd understand a voice you've heard your whole life saying your name.

When the full roar finally arrives — when wind-through-cane and ten thousand screaming wings and the structural groan of a kilometer of living tower all fuse into the one continental sound this place is always tuning toward — I won't flinch. I'll lock into the lattice, hooks set, true hands free, spine-ridge flared as wide as it goes, and hold still at the center of the loudest place I have ever known, because this is the only silence I have. Everything underneath the noise gets quiet here. That's the secret of it. The roar is where I finally get to listen.
