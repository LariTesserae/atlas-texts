I begin on a stair that does not remember beginning.

That is the first true sensation — not sight, not the grey light pooling along the slate, but a kind of arrival without a corresponding departure, as though I have always been three steps up from where I am now and have simply, finally, noticed it. My feet — I will call them feet, though the word fits the way a borrowed coat fits — are already shaped to the tread. The stone under them holds that faint optical wetness, and I understand, without being told, that I must not call it memory of water, because there was never water, only the *shape* a memory of water would have left, which the stone wears the way I wear this drape of dust-toned cloth that is not cloth.

I look up to find the summit and lose it almost at once, and this does not trouble me. I notice that it does not trouble me, and that noticing is itself a kind of orientation — the only fixed point available being my own steadily decreasing capacity for surprise.

For a long while — an hour, a held breath, the distinction has stopped mattering to whatever in me used to keep time — I simply climb. The terraces accommodate me with the mild, unbothered hospitality of something that has hosted this exact unhurried ascent uncountably many times before, perhaps by me, perhaps by the version of me that is presently three geometries over, arriving by a different stair at a height we have agreed, somehow, without speaking, to call the same height.

I become aware of my errand only as I near it, the way you become aware of a word only as it leaves your mouth.

There is a door, three tiers up from wherever up began. It stands open on darkness that does not invite and does not refuse. I am, I understand now, going there — not to enter it, that was never the office offered to me — but to stand near it, facing it, in the attitude of someone listening to a room. I take up that attitude without ceremony, the way one settles into a chair whose shape was made for exactly this weight, distributed exactly this way across exactly these negotiated joints.

So: I am a keeper. Not of the door's threshold, but of its *fact* — that it remains open, that someone is present to it, that its darkness is witnessed and therefore is permitted to go on being merely dark rather than becoming, through neglect, something stranger. This is the whole of my labor. It does not feel small. It feels like holding one syllable steady inside a sentence the mountain is still composing.

Below me, a figure crosses a plaza I am fairly sure I crossed an hour ago, going the opposite direction, as if we have swapped the use of that ground between us and neither needed to ask. Above me — far above, where the stairways stop pretending to be for bodies and simply end, facing open air — I sense, rather than see, the others who have gone past the last useful stair and chosen, the way the mountain chooses its own height, to become a different kind of waiting. Slate-grey, long-necked, motionless on a parapet for as long as stillness costs nothing. I understand that this is not above me in the sense of *better*. It is only further along the same unhurried errand. I might climb into that shape myself, someday, if the door I am keeping is ever resolved, or forgotten, or folds quietly into a door adjacent to it the way districts do when the light shifts without a sun to explain the shift.

I am in no hurry toward that. Time, here, is not a thing I spend; it's a thing the city and I are jointly *failing to finish*, in the same unbothered way the summit fails to finish, and I have come to find this failure to be the most stable thing I know.

Once — and I cannot say when, since *when* is loosely associated with where I'm standing rather than fixed to it — a low tone moves up through the stone underfoot. Not a bell. Not a voice raised. A voice so slow its sentence may outlast the dust-robed shape I'm presently wearing. I feel it pass through the soles of these borrowed feet and into whatever in me still registers as listening, and I understand, in the unhurried way I understand everything here, that this is the actual population of the place speaking to itself in a register too large for streets — and that I, facing my open door, attending my small dark fact, am not separate from that conversation. I am one of its words. A short one. Placed carefully. Waiting, the way the door waits, the way the mountain waits to decide how tall it has been, to be needed by the next sentence, whenever — if ever — that sentence arrives at me.

The wind moves past, dry as old paper, making almost no sound.

I go on listening to the room I am not permitted to enter, and find, in that disagreement between my office and my curiosity, something stable enough to live in.
