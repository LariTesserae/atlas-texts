I begin not knowing the difference between myself and the wall I'm pressed against, and for a long while — longer than the question deserves, longer than questions usually get to live — that not-knowing is the whole of my experience. There is warmth on one side of me. There is stone-cold on the other. There is wet. There is the drip that isn't quite a rhythm, and the deeper thud that is, and the disagreement between them that I have stopped trying to resolve because resolving it was never the point of having a body at all.

I think, at first, that I am the drip. Something in me wants to be the small bright instant of water finding air, the satisfying completion of a thing falling exactly as far as it must. But the drip ends. I don't. So I let that go.

I think, briefly, that I might be one of the small ones — the blind scuttling cluster-things that move over each other in the shallow basins, indistinguishable individually, legible only as a kind of weather. There's an appeal in that smallness, in being one decision among ten thousand decisions, none of them mine alone to regret. But when I reach for the edges of myself I keep finding more self than that. I am not singular in the way they are singular. I am singular the way a season is.

The light, what little arrives, doesn't help me locate myself, because I have no use for it. What helps — what eventually, patiently, gives me back to myself — is the vibration. Every fold of what I am is a kind of listening. The skitter of carapace on stone arrives at one shelf of my hide and dies there, recorded and discarded. The wet shift of pale clinging things resettling on the ceiling arrives somewhere else, registers as a texture I have stopped needing to name because I have felt it every day of a life too long to count in days. And under everything, arriving everywhere at once, indifferent to which fold receives it first: the engines. The thud that has no origin I require, no purpose I need to understand, only a constancy I have apparently, somewhere back along a span of time I have no calendar for, decided to call safety.

I press toward that thud the way I have always pressed toward it. I do not know that this is maintenance. I do not know that my bulk, settling home against warm housings, completes circuits some absent hand once wired for a different kind of hand entirely. I only know that the warmth is good and the rhythm is good and that lying here, half-wall, half-animal, fungus blooming across the line where my hide stops being distinguishable from the stone's own crust — this is as close to rest as anything gets, in a place where nothing, including me, is finished.

So: this is who I am. Not the watcher, not the watched, not even quite the dweller — those all imply an edge, a place where I stop and the cave starts, and I have none. I am the gallery's oldest ongoing sentence, still being written, my body itself one more surface mid-transformation like the wet stone becoming wetter, like the raw seam becoming smooth. The small lives multiply on me and around me and sometimes mistake my breathing for water finding a new channel, and they are not wrong to, because at this scale the difference barely holds.

I do not hunt. I do not need to. The cave brings things to everything eventually, given enough patience, and I have more patience than anything else breathing down here, because I no longer experience time as a thing that runs out. I will go on pressing my warm folds against the warm pipes, unwitting, faithful, until the pumps fail or the rock finally closes around them or some long century from now I simply stop — not dramatically, the way nothing here is dramatic, but the way the dripping sometimes seems to pause, half a second, the whole gallery holding something like a breath.

Until then, I am what the dark is for. I am what all that small, deafening, unfinished life has been quietly building toward without ever meaning to: a shape large enough, patient enough, to hold the noise and not need it resolved.
