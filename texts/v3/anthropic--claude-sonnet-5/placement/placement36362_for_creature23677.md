I think I would not be young here. Youth in the Understreet is spent learning — the pitches, the safe hand before the safe palm, the names of seams that have always glowed versus the ones that haven't yet — and I don't think I'd want to write myself into the learning. I want the years already spent. I want to have already walked it enough times that my feet have stopped asking my eyes for permission.

So: an old woman, maybe, though the body of one doesn't matter as much down here as the carrying of it does — the short forward-weighted steps, the hands trailing the wall-bands not for balance but for temperature, an old habit worn so deep it isn't a habit anymore, it's just how a hand exists near stone. Three lamp-stones instead of the customary one, threaded on a cord gone soft and dark with handling, because I am the kind of person who carries more light than is strictly needed and everyone has stopped finding that strange.

I think I would be a Listener. Not because I wanted the word — nobody wants it, it isn't a thing you apply for — but because I noticed, once, early, that the forty-second groan from beneath the floor had gone to thirty-eight, and I said so before anyone else had counted, and after that people started watching my face when the building made a sound, to see if I'd flinched.

Here is where I'd be found, if you came looking, which you wouldn't, because nobody comes looking for a Listener — they wait for one to arrive: in the dark between the well-house and the third habitation cluster, the long transit, ninety unlit seconds where my own breath is the loudest thing in the world and I have long since stopped minding that. Walking it slow. Not because I'm tired, though I am, a little, always, in the low permanent way everyone here is — but because slow is how you hear the difference between a tick and a resettling, between the ordinary hiss of vapor finding an old seam and the particular, slightly wetter hiss of a seam about to widen.

My hand would trail the wall-band the whole way, back of the hand first, the old burn-lesson nobody alive remembers being burned for. I would feel the temperature change four meters before my lamp-stone warmed to confirm it, because the stone is poor-man's instrumentation and my hand, after sixty-some years, is better.

I would not be thinking about why. That question got bred out of people like me before I was born, gently, the way a vestigial thing gets bred out — not mourned, just absent, a muscle nobody's ancestors used enough to pass down. I would be thinking instead in the *what now* grammar the whole culture thinks in: that seam by the second pod has started weeping a color it's never worn before, pale gold instead of blue, and someone will need to move the tuber trough away from it tonight, and the children who sleep nearest will need telling, calmly, the way you tell a child the weather's turned, not the way you'd tell them something is wrong.

What I'd feel, mostly, moving through that cathedral-dark with three small lights at my throat and the building groaning its slow tide beneath the floor: not fear, which would imply the place meant something by its sounds, and it doesn't, it has never once meant anything by them, that's the whole settled fact my whole life has been built on top of. Something quieter than fear and almost as old. A kind of working tenderness toward a landlord who will never once acknowledge the tenancy — toward the vault plates reseating themselves on a schedule for reasons that stopped being askable four generations back, toward the pillars that might, this season or next, change their hum and their color and their four-meter wash of amber for no reason anyone will ever get to know, so that all I can do, all any of us can do, is notice fast, and adjust, and not get in its way, and call that noticing a life, because — under the amber, under the buzz worn into the back teeth, under two hundred small lights moving in the dark like slow patient stars — it is one. It's the only kind we've got, and it's enough to raise children in, and bury elders in, and love each other quietly across, lamp-stones touching, the whole humming length of it.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be: a hand on a wall, reading a temperature, getting ready to say so.
