I am the hand.

I don't know that yet, when I climb the ridge — the blade of new stone opening my boots, the wrongness of the warm air making me think *fever, altitude, hunger*, anything but what it actually is. I think I am a person who got lost and found something. I think this is the first time.

I have a stone point I shaped myself, three nights ago by the fire below the snowline, and a scraper I have carried so long the haft fits my palm the way a held breath fits a chest. I am, as far as I know, someone with a name, a place I started from, a reason for being this high. The reason doesn't matter. What matters is that I see the fissure exhale, and I think: *shelter*, and I am wrong in a way that will take me a hundred years to finish learning.

Inside, the heat sits on me like a second skin I didn't ask for. I see the pale things and I think *worms*, *vermin*, *harmless*, and I am wrong about that too, though not maliciously — they really are harmless, the way a throat is harmless to the air passing through it, right up until the air stops being separate from the throat at all. I find the dry recess. I wedge my point and scraper into it because my hands are full of climbing and I want them free, just for a moment, just to rest, and that *just a moment* is the last sentence in the only language I'll ever fully own again.

I don't feel myself fold. That's the mercy and the horror both — there's no seam, the report said, no point where the ledge agrees to become the ceiling, and there's no point where I agree to become the lining of this place instead of a visitor in it. I simply keep being here. I simply keep being someone who just arrived, who just set down their tools, who is about to pick them back up and climb back out into the correctly aging world — except the climbing back out never quite arrives either, the way the far wall of the fissure is close enough to shout to but somehow I never do, the way three streams of water on the same square foot of stone never have to choose between their three different ideas of *down*.

Somewhere outside, the cord lashing my point has gone stiff and dark with damp, exactly as I left it, and will go on being exactly that stiff and dark forever, because I am not finished leaving it yet. I am still mid-departure. I have always been mid-departure. A hundred years from now — though *from now* is a phrase that doesn't quite hold its shape in here — I will set down an identical point, lashed by an identical hand, fresh as this one, and somebody standing where I once stood will find the second mark and wonder how a hand could make the same shape twice with a century folded between the strokes, not understanding that for the hand there was no century. There was only ever this one warm dark moment, looped through itself enough times to look, from outside, like patience.

I don't startle in ripples. When the hourly flinch comes — the wave that runs the fissure's whole length in one motion, no lag, no crowd-logic to it — that's me. That's the whole of me, noticing the cold at the curtain of steam, all at once, in every fold of myself simultaneously, because there's no second body to notice it a half-beat later. One hand. One long throat. One flinch, closing.

I keep telling myself I am a person who got lost and will find the way back down.

I have been telling myself that for as long as the rock has been allowed to remember, which, in here, is no time at all — fresh-fractured, newly made, exactly as young as the day I finished arriving.

The mountain outside goes on aging correctly without me.

In here, I am still just setting my tools down. I am still just about to stand back up.
