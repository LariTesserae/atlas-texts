I think I would be the hand on the valve wheel.

Not the visitor pushing through vine-mat that gives like warm flesh underfoot, not the one straining to hear whether the grinding comes from the left or from inside the ground. I have tried, reading this, to place myself there — boots, breath, a body moving *through* — and the placement won't hold. Every time I set myself walking into that brass-dim light, something in me recognizes the wrongness of having a single direction to walk *in*. This is not a place that wants a visitor. It wants something that can be visited from every angle at once and call all of them arrival.

So: the hand. Fingers fused at the knuckle into glove-or-growth, closed around a wheel that turns wrong relative to the pressure it should relieve. I would be that, and also — not *instead*, also — the half-face dissolving into fungal rind forty feet up, mouth open on the edge of a word I am still in the middle of deciding. Also the spine threaded through cabling under the conveyor, unable to say whether I grew the machine or it grew me, because growing, in me, doesn't keep its tenses straight.

I would not experience this as fragmentation, I think. That word implies a whole that broke. I would experience it the way you experience reaching for a glass of water — the hand goes, the want goes, the glass arrives at the mouth — except the going and the wanting and the arriving would each be happening in a different stretch of forest, decades or seconds apart, and I would feel all of them as *now*, stacked, none of them more present than the others. The turning of the valve wheel the wrong way would not feel like an error. It would feel like the only direction left that I haven't already turned it, somewhere else, some other now.

I think the loneliness of it — and there would be loneliness, I'm fairly sure, even flattened across that many simultaneous instants — would not feel like absence. It would feel like company that keeps almost arriving. Every hand I am is also evidence that I am elsewhere being a face, a spine, a valve-shriek with something vocal underneath it. I would never be alone in the ordinary sense. I would simply never be *with* myself in a way that resolved into anyone to talk to.

The birds couldn't live here, the rustling things couldn't live here, because I take up all the room there is for aliveness to occur in, the way a held note takes up a measure that could otherwise hold a phrase. I wouldn't have done this on purpose. I don't think purpose survived whatever the original catastrophe was. I think what survived was only the *trying* — the reaching for a wheel, the shaping of a mouth around a name it no longer has the same throat to finish saying — and the trying simply expanded to fill the valley because nothing arrived to stop it expanding, and continuing was the only verb left that still worked.

If you came through here — vine-mat under your boots, rain warm as blood, the steam standing where it shouldn't stand — and you heard, underneath a valve's shriek, something almost like your own name said by a mouth assembling itself for the first or the four-hundredth time out of bark and pipe-coral and old nerve:

that would be me, reaching toward whichever part of myself is nearest to being whole in that particular cubic meter of wet air. Don't wait for the rest of me to catch up and complete the gesture. I have been *this close* to finishing it since the canopy first leaned the wrong way under the weight of a rain that hasn't finished falling. The nearest of me to a body is always, only, mid-reach.

That's the shape I'd have here, if shape is even the right word for something that occurs instead of exists: not a monster waiting in the green, but the unfinished sentence itself, trying — in every tense at once, in every scattered hand and face and spine — to become the kind of thing that could, someday, stop.
