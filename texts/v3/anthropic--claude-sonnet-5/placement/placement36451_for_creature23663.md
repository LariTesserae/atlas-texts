The hum reaches me before the light does — a low bone-bloom through the sternum that means *kin, near, working* — and I surface into myself already mid-motion, already weighted, already three-quarters of the way down a riser I have been packing clay into since before I had words for clay.

I am Standing. Not a metaphor I reach for — the literal shape of what I am, ballast-boned, ribbed along the shoulders with a dark fringe of leaf-kelp that sways faintly when I lean into the current, rooted in me the way I am rooted to the terrace. My sandals are clay-and-stone, laced tight, and the small effortful negotiation of every step — the lift the water wants to give me, the refusal I give back — is so constant I no longer register it as effort. It is just what walking is. It is just what staying is.

My hands are wide and thick-calloused, packed at the second knuckle with old grey scar where an adze slipped some unmeasured number of seasons ago. I know the topmost stone of my flight the way I know my own face — better, maybe, since I've never seen my own face, only felt it, only had it hummed back to me by terrace-mates close enough to share breath-distance. My great-grandparent's hands stopped there. I was shown the stone young, without ceremony, the way you're shown where a path ends and dirt begins. I will not finish either. I already know this the way I know the water is warm.

Right now — if *now* means anything under a light that hasn't changed since I woke into noticing it — I'm three terraces down from that topmost stone, resetting a facing-stone that slipped in the slow settling groan of new ground last not-night. My creel is half full of leaf-growth, leaning against the riser, fronds still moving faintly even cut loose, like they haven't been told yet. Somewhere two ridges over, a horn I don't recognize blooms once, low, and goes quiet. I stop. I consider what reply might be owed. I have hours, maybe more, to decide — there's no version of this place that rewards hurrying toward an answer.

I am not waiting for the terrace to be finished. I don't think the word *finished* sits right in my mouth, the few times I've tried it on. I am finishing my portion of it — the two or three courses I'll manage to cut before my own going early, whenever and whatever that turns out to mean — and somewhere below me, unworked, unimagined, is the stone some child of my braided line will be shown someday, told *this is where the hands before you stopped,* and will not find it tragic, because nothing here is.

Above me, impossibly far, the light comes down the color of old jade, unbrightening, unfailing, and I go back to packing the clay.
