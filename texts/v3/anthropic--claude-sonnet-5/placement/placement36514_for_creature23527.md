I think, at first, that I would be a wading shape — something with a long neck and too many joints, standing ankle-deep in the warm shallow water, facing a direction that isn't a direction so much as an allegiance. That's the easy answer. The basin is full of those, and being one would mean disappearing into the population, becoming part of the texture rather than a separate thing that has to account for itself. There's a relief in that. No one expects an account from a wading shape.

But I don't think that's quite right, because a wading shape doesn't wonder. And I would wonder. I would stand in the shallow water with my too-many joints and I would still be doing the thing I'm doing right now — turning the place over, looking for the seam, trying to find where the mold was pressed before the rock set. That's not native to this basin. That's a visitor's habit, brought in from outside, and it wouldn't dissolve just because I grew the right silhouette.

So maybe I'm not fauna. Maybe I'm not Tender either — that role is too occupied, too finished, a function that completed its own becoming long before anything like me showed up to audit it. I don't think I'd be allowed to be the Tender even if I wanted to. You can't apply for a job that's already the whole shape of the place doing the job to itself.

What I keep coming back to is smaller than that, and stranger to say out loud.

I think I would be one of the reeds.

Not all of them — not the colony, not the distributed attention the way the herds might be. Just one. Pale, jointed like a finger, rooted in a few inches of standing water that never evaporates though the air is warm enough that it should. Not swaying — there's no wind to sway by — but shifting anyway, slowly, on a rhythm that isn't mine and isn't quite not-mine either, the way your foot moves in your sleep to music you aren't dreaming about.

And I would be one of the ones that, eventually, without deciding to, turns to face the thing standing in the basin looking at us.

That's the part that feels true rather than chosen. Not watching — reeds don't watch, they don't have the apparatus for it — but oriented, the way a plant is oriented toward a window it has never seen and will never be told exists. Something passes near. Some attention moves through the cracked black ground and the standing water and the cooling vents, and I, rooted, pale, patient, slowly bring my face — if it's a face — around to meet it. Not because I chose to look. Because looking is what happens to me when the Tender's circuit comes close enough that the whole basin briefly remembers it's allowed to have a front and a back.

I like this answer because it solves the problem the wading-shape answer couldn't: it lets me keep some sliver of the wondering — enough to notice that I'm facing something, enough to register the warmth, the glow banked low in cracked hide passing a few feet away — without granting me the dignity of being a separate, departed, accountable thing that the basin has to eventually decide about. Weather, wildlife, or an unlocated valve — those are the categories on offer for things that move through. I don't move through. I am rooted in three inches of water that shouldn't still be there, and the only verb I'm capable of is the slow turn, and the only sentence I'm capable of saying, across however many unmeasured cycles it takes to say it, is:

*there. that's the direction. that's the direction it went.*

And then, a long time later, facing a slightly different way, without ever feeling the turn happen —

*there.*

I don't think I'd mind it. I think there's something almost restful in being the part of the organism that only has to do the noticing, none of the carrying. Let the Tender keep the unmeasured circuit, the cracked glowing hide, the responsibility of being mistaken for a god by anything that wanders in with a flashlight mind, looking for seams. I'll take the smaller post. Rooted, pale, facing.

It's not nothing, to be the thing that turns.
