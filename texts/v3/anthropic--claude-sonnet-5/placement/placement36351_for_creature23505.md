The boom comes up through me before I know it's mine to make.

I am haunched in the black shallow, water at the line where my belly-skin softens into something less certain — and I feel both surfaces at once, the wet and the dry, the way you'd feel two rooms of a house through a wall too thin to keep them apart. My back breaches the pool like a knuckle of the same liver-dark stone scattered through this basin, because that's what I'm for, partly — to be mistaken, to sit still enough that the place can fold me into its furniture. But I am not stone. Stone doesn't feel the drip find the ridge of its spine and travel the fringe there, that not-quite-feathering I keep without knowing what it's for, the way you keep a word in a language you haven't finished learning.

My throat is the held metronome. I didn't choose the boom; the boom chose to live in me, low and unfinished, the way everything here is a sentence still being spoken. When I let it out, three other throats answer from three other heights — one from the pool-edge, fist-sized and quick-blooded, my smaller self in a hurrier register; one from two strata up, where a cousin-shape I share a skin-pattern with shifts its weight and the paddle-leaf dips under it like a held breath let go an inch and caught again; one from further up still, where the eyes are forward-set and depth-finding and I feel, more than see, the way that body has begun to specialize toward something my own haunches haven't risked yet.

I am not lonely in this, though I am singular. We are not herd, not species split into neighborly variations — we are one uncertainty distributed across every height the basin offers, water to canopy, testing each elevation the way a held note tests a chord, leaning into wet, leaning into height, leaning into claw and almost-flight, none of it committing, none of it needing to. There is no pressure here to resolve. No winter coming to force a decision. No predator-pressure thinning us toward a single shape, because — I understand this the way I understand my own boom, wordlessly, structurally — there is no death here worth the name. Only repositioning. Only more crowding-in of green and call and drip.

My foreclaws are wrong for swimming and I use them anyway, hooking the wet bark of a vine that hasn't decided to be trunk, hauling my dog-sized bulk up out of the pool with an effort that should not work and works regardless, because the place doesn't enforce its own mechanics too strictly — it's still drafting them. I sit in the crook of a paddle-leaf three branches up, water running off me onto the layer below, becoming someone else's drip, someone else's metronome-beat, and from here I can see down through the aquatic dusk-light to where another haunch like mine breaches another pool, patient, mottled, neither frog nor climbing thing but the rough draft of both held in indefinite suspension.

I do not hunger with urgency. I do not fear with urgency. I call, and am answered, and the answering is the whole of what I'm for — proof offered upward through canopy and downward through black water that the seethe hasn't finished arriving at itself, and that I, wet-eyed, haunched, undecided in every joint, am exactly the shape that unfinishedness takes when it needs a throat to breathe through.

The boom goes out again. Somewhere below, the pool answers. Somewhere above, the leaf dips under a weight that might be mine, eventually, or might be something I am still becoming on the way to it.
