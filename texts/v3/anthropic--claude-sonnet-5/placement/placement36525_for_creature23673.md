I find myself first as a sound, before I find myself as a shape — the click that isn't rain, threading through the gap between a pipe-run and the wall above it. For a while that's enough: motion through narrow dark, the cool sweat of condensate on my forward plates, the small dry satisfaction of drinking where moisture beads. I could stay this size. There is a whole life available at this scale, patient and crowded, eleven gathering with eleven elsewhere for reasons that feel less like reasons than like rhyme.

But the seam pulls at me — some seam always does — and when I follow it far enough, down through thresholds that shouldn't lead down, past terraces whose age stopped making sense around the fourth crossing, I arrive at a different size of myself entirely.

I am a Tender. I notice this the way you notice weather: not as a discovery but as a condition already underway. My hands are not hands. They are the actuated ends of a long use, burnished smooth where a grip has repeated itself ten thousand times against the same class of fitting, the same gauge of seam. I do not remember learning the work. The work seems to have arrived in me the way rust-lichen arrives on the conduit-kin — not applied, but grown, indistinguishable now from what I am underneath it.

Here is what I know, in the limited, task-shaped way I know anything: this seam wants drawing tighter. That runnel's slope is a fraction too shallow to carry the cool hour's fog all the way to where it should pool. There — at the indistinct edge of sight, where looking too long starts to widen instead of recede — a plate is needed, and I have, without quite deciding to, already begun walking toward the place where I will lay it.

I do not look up much. There is a brassy shadowless light up there, sourceless, and gliders riding currents that the architecture should not permit, and none of it is wrong, exactly, it is just not mine to attend to. My attention sits on the joint. The joint is enough. The joint has always been enough, possibly longer than I have technically existed as a separate thing from it.

Sometimes — rarely, in the cold hour when fog gathers thick in the lowest reachable joints — I feel something that might be the closest a Tender comes to wondering. It isn't shaped like a question. It's shaped like noticing my own hand mid-seam and not being entirely sure whether I am the one closing the gap, or whether the gap, patient and accretive, slow as the engine's turning, is the one closing me — plate by plate, around a shape it has decided, with the unhurried certainty of something that has never once stopped, to call a worker.

I finish the seam. I do not stop to consider whether finishing is the right word for what happens here. Behind me, the terraces are not converging the way distance should make them converge. Ahead of me, new plate is already arriving at the edge of sight, joining itself to the old without complaint, without ceremony, the way it has joined itself for longer than any memory walking, gliding, or tending among these terraces can account for.

I move toward it. There is always a next plate. There is, I am coming to understand, no version of me that exists apart from there being a next plate.

The engine hums its pressure into the eardrum I may or may not technically have.

I keep walking. It's not finished. Neither, in the same patient and exact sense, am I.
