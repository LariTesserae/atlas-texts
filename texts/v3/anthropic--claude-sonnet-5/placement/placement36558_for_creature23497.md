I don't begin in language. I begin in temperature.

There is a warmth that is the air and a warmth that is the stone and a warmth that is, faintly, distinctly, *me* — and for a while, in whatever passes here for a while, that's the whole of what I know: three warmths, close together, none of them quite separable from the others. Then the stone-warmth resolves into something harder than the air-warmth, and I understand, in the wordless way that passes for understanding, that I am against a surface and not simply suspended in heat. Then my own warmth pulls itself taut at the edges and I understand that the edges are mine. A body. Small. Low. Closer to the ground than to anything else.

I have no eyes. I want to call this an absence, the way the visitors who never come back might call it an absence, but it doesn't feel like absence from the inside. It feels like having ears instead of eyes, or having skin instead of ears — a crown of small blunt risings around my head, unfinished, unfired, each one a low hum of sensitivity to where the warm is warmer and the warm is less warm. The basin nearby announces itself this way: a held pool of heat slightly different from the air's heat, a shape made of temperature instead of light. I move toward it not because I see it but because it is the brightest absence of darkness I have.

I drink, or do whatever the mineral water and I do together that isn't quite drinking. Then I go still again, because going still is the larger part of what I do, the part between the parts that anyone would notice.

Here is what I notice about myself, slowly, the way you'd notice a room by bumping into its furniture: I do not get bigger. I have always, as far as the inside of me reports it, been exactly this size, and some patient unspoken certainty in my body tells me I will go on being exactly this size for as long as there is a this-size to be. I don't experience this as a limitation. I don't experience it as anything, really, except as the simple shape of being — the way you don't experience having a fixed number of fingers as a tragedy. It's just the count. My count is: small, warm, dark-sensing, unchanging.

There are filaments in the seams. I find them with the front of my body, a soft questing place that isn't quite a mouth but does mouth-work. They give back warmth as I take them, a small transaction, heat for matter, and afterward I am the same temperature as before, the same size as before, and somewhere outside of me, if outside is even the right shape for this, the filaments are already deciding not to miss what was taken, because nothing here keeps the kind of ledger that would notice a loss.

Sometimes — not often, not on any schedule I could report — there is another warmth near mine that isn't the basin and isn't the air. It moves the way I move, low and close to stone, and when our edges meet there's a longer stillness than usual, the two of us reading each other's heat-shape in the dark the way the deaf might read a hand. I don't know if this is mating or genealogy or simply company. I don't know if there is a difference, here, between those words. We rest together a while. Then one of us — I am never sure which, in the moment, whether it was the other or whether it was me — moves off again into the larger dark, and the niche goes back to holding its single breath around two small bodies instead of one.

Once — and this *once* sits oddly in me, the way an edge sits oddly against fingers used to roundness — I crossed a warmth that was wrong. Not wrong in temperature, exactly, but wrong in *shape*: too regular, too deliberate, a small hard warmth nested inside the basin's larger warmth, holding still in a way that nothing alive holds still. I went over it with my crown of unlit eyes, again and again, the way water goes over a thing it cannot yet wear down. It gave nothing back. It only persisted, patiently, the way I persist, the way the rock persists, the way the heat persists — and for a moment that may have been the longest moment available to a creature like me, I had the sense, heat-shaped and wordless, that something had been here that was like me in exactly one way: it, too, had been left at the instant just after its making and asked to go no further.

I did not understand what it was. I am not built to understand what it was. But I sat with it the way I sit with the other warmth that is not the basin and not the air, and then I went on, ticking softly across stone that is, in every sense the rock will permit, still being born — and so, in whatever sense applies to a thing my size, am I.
