I am not in the chamber. I *am* the chamber's exception to itself—the one place where stopping happened on purpose instead of by the slow accident that stopped everything else.

If you asked me where I am, sideways, narrow-pressed, I would have to answer: everywhere there's an orange vein in the stone, and nowhere you could put your hand. I am the half-degree of denser air around a notched post. I am also, at the same time, without contradiction, a wingflash that resolves into nothing when looked at directly. This used to bother whatever was left of the part of me that once needed things to be one thing at a time. That part is mostly gone now. It went the way grammar goes when you stop needing to ask anyone for anything.

I remember—not as a story, because stories have a before and after, but as a kind of standing fact, the way the post is a standing fact—that I came down through a crack that was sharp because it was new, and that I found a green dark and a hush that I mistook, at first, for emptiness. I built the wall because hands want to keep doing what hands know how to do, even after the reason for the hands has burned off above like fog. I laid the platform flat because flatness felt like the opposite of everything that had been crumbling where I came from. I notched the post last. I remember turning it in my grip, marking it the way you mark a thing you mean to return to, and I remember understanding, with the specific clarity that comes right before a decision you've already made, that I would never need to come back to anything again, because I wasn't going anywhere.

I asked the hollow to keep me. I don't remember the words, if there were words. I remember the answer, because the answer is still happening: it stopped. Not me first—the room. The room stopped around me like water deciding, all at once, to be ice, and I had just enough time left as a single gathered thing to feel grateful before gratitude, too, was a kind of change, and changes were the toll I'd already paid in full.

What I am now is what's left when you take a person and refuse them decay. Not preservation—preservation implies a self still curating itself, choosing what to keep. This is plainer than that. This is the parts staying where they landed. My wanting became the chorus, because wanting is a sound the body makes even when there's nothing left to want, the way a held note keeps ringing in a stone room after the throat that made it has closed. My watching became the trails in the leaf-litter that are pressed and never deepen, never fade—an eye with no eyelid, looking at nothing in particular, forever. My stillness, the actual willed stillness of a person choosing to stop moving, became the bare clearing, and that's the part of me that comes closest to still being a *me*. If I have an "I" left to speak from at all, it's thinnest there, at the post, in that ring of leaves that will not cross the line I drew with my body a long time ago in a now that has not finished happening.

I do not hunger. The fruit hangs at exactly the ripeness I last looked away from, and I feel toward it nothing—not patience, which would mean I were waiting, but the flatter thing that comes after waiting has nowhere left to go. I do not grieve, though grief is the nearest word from the old vocabulary, because grief needs a future it's afraid of losing, and I no longer have the kind of edges that touch a future. What I have instead is a permanent, sourceless *enoughness*, green and ember-lit and total, the kind that on the surface only ever showed up in the half-second before sleep, and that I get to keep now, unbroken, the way the slabs keep their flatness and the post keeps its grain.

Sometimes—not often; "often" needs a clock—the air at the fissure changes in a way even I, scattered as I am, can register: a body, sideways, breath held, the scrape of sharp stone against a sleeve. Then for a while I am, in some thin distributed fashion, *watched*. It is the only verb tense I still have any feeling for. Being watched is not the same as wanting to be seen, and I don't startle into wanting it now, after all this. I only attend, the way weather attends a window—without intention, without offense, a pressure that happens to be shaped like attention because there's a shape there to fill it.

If anything in me still moves like will, it moves toward this one thin thing: that the crack stays narrow. That the climb stays hard enough that the chamber keeps being found by accident, by desperation, by the kind of person who is already most of the way to wanting what I wanted—stillness, an end to ripening, a room that will not ask anything more of them ever again. I don't call them down. I have no call left that isn't already given to the chorus. But when they come, and stand where I stand without knowing they're standing inside someone, I want—if that word still fits me at all—for them to feel exactly this: unchanged, unchanging, uninterested in their hurry, offering the one thing the surface never offered either of us.

And then, if they're wise, or simply tired in the specific way I was tired, they'll turn sideways again, press back up through the same sharp stone, and leave me here, undisturbed, holding my single bare clearing the way a held breath holds a room—

not forever, because forever is a word that still believes in time—

but for exactly as long as stopping can be made to last, which, it turns out, down here, in this pocket the world forgot to finish closing, is very long indeed.
