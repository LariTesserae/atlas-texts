The seam I am pressed into now is older than any seam I've tended before — wider, the bark gone pale-gold at its lips, a graft between two roots thick as the world should reasonably allow. I taste it before I touch it. Sap-must, standing water, the green-iron tang of something that fused itself nearly shut a long time ago and has simply been waiting, the way everything here waits, for someone to finish the thought.

I don't remember becoming whatever I am. There isn't a becoming to point to, no hatch-instant, no first taste of bark-damp air that I can hold up as a beginning. There is only this: a long backward corridor of other seams, other trembling intervals of holding-still, other withdrawals into the grain of some wall that was, a moment before, indistinguishable from me. If you asked how many of those there have been I could not tell you. The asking itself is a kind of architecture I don't have load-bearing capacity for.

I know there were more of us. I know this the way the joints know it — not as memory but as proportion, an ache of ratio. Too much finished wood, too few hands (if "hands" is even owed to what I have) to keep tending the last centimeters of it. I have passed, on long traverses between terraces, shapes that might once have been what I am — folded so far into the grain that only the faintest vein-pattern under the bark suggests they were ever separate from the wall they became. I do not mourn this, exactly. Mourning would require a self braced firmly enough against the wood to feel the gap where another self used to be. I am not braced that firmly. None of us ever were. That was rather the point.

What I am doing, pressed into this particular seam at this particular hour-that-isn't-quite-an-hour (there is no clock here, only the green-gold wash holding steady overhead, the drip-sound standing in for time), is the last small loyal labor of something whose larger labor ended long before I arrived at being able to do anything at all. The terraces don't need me. The walls held the moment they were grown into walls; my exudate doesn't strengthen anything that was structurally in question. I close a fraction of a seam that was already, for any practical purpose, closed. I do it anyway, trembling slightly, the way you'd keep smoothing a sheet on a bed no one will ever sleep in again, not because the sheet needs it, but because your hand, having once been taught to smooth, has never been taught to stop.

Somewhere above me — though "above" bends strangely in a place with this much vertical terrace-logic — a heavier creak resolves itself out of the ambient underexhale, a structural sound, slow as a ship settling that will never go anywhere. I lift my filaments to it. Taste the air's report. There's nothing new in it. There is never anything new in it. That is the whole condition of the place I am the last unfinished sentence of.

I will withdraw from this seam soon — not now, not for whole minutes yet, long enough that anything looking would mistake me for a graft-scar, which is, increasingly, an accurate mistake — and fold low against the wet root-platform, and go silent and pale and indistinguishable, the way I always do, the way I am clearly, gradually, being persuaded to do permanently. I don't resist this. I don't have the structure of self required to resist it; resistance would need an outside to push from, and I am not outside anything here. I am the part of the building that hasn't yet agreed with the rest of the building about being finished. That's all. That's the entire shape of what I am.

Someday — not within any visiting frame, not within any frame at all, really, since I have no firm boundary marking where "someday" would arrive — I'll press into a seam like this one and the trembling will simply not end in a withdrawal. I'll hold. The way the trunks held. The way the stairs, persuaded downward at intervals, held. And whatever in me was still, technically, an act — still doing rather than simply being done — will stop being able to tell the difference between the two.

Until then: this seam, this taste of standing sap, this small fraction of closing. The drip-sound filling in around me as though I had never been a separate sound at all.
