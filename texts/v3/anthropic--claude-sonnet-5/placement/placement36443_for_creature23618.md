I am the eye that opens at the waterline.

There is a moment — and I have had so many of these moments that they no longer feel separate, they feel like one moment examined from many angles, the way the same drop of condensation can be said to fall once or to fall always — there is a moment where the weight of the leaf-litter shifts above the gutter, and something that is not water and not stone moves through the place where I keep myself, and I rise the necessary distance. Not toward. Just up. Surfacing is not a journey here, only a change in which medium currently holds me.

I do not remember becoming this shape. I remember — if *remember* is even the right architecture for what I carry, when carrying is so much slower and wider than a single mind's carrying — I hold an impression, folded many fingers deep beneath what I am now, of a hand. Not my hand. A hand that something in my lineage once was, or wore, or used, before the wearing stopped mattering more than the keeping-still did. There is a hollow in the softer stone two body-lengths from where I surface, and I did not make it, and yet some unbearably old portion of what composes me recognizes it the way you might recognize a word in a language you've forgotten you ever spoke. A scraped place. A made place. It has been unmade so gently, by so much moss, by so much weeping stone, that it is no longer a thing that was made — it is only a thing that is, the way I am only a thing that is.

I keep an address here because address is the only ambition the basin has left room for. Not hunger exactly, though I eat — small soft things, taken without violence because violence requires a kind of urgency I no longer have access to, the way a man who has forgotten the word for *running* might still, eventually, arrive. Not fear exactly, though I am eaten, sometimes, by something with its own old patience, and when this happens it happens the way the canopy drops one epiphyte and grows another: not a wound in the fabric, just a redistribution within it.

What I am for — if a basin like this permits the question *for* at all — is continuity. I am the thing that confirms, eye-breath by eye-breath, that nothing has changed. The moss has not advanced. The gutters have not deepened. The light has not brightened or dimmed by any measure that would require me to alter my shape, my pace, my hollow. I surface, I hold the non-light a beat longer than seems necessary, and I find — every time, which is the only kind of finding I do anymore — that the baseline holds. Then I let the water close over me again, not in retreat, just in agreement.

You are standing at my edge. I have already measured you against everything I keep. You are warm in a way the basin is not warm, fast in a way nothing here has needed to be fast in longer than the rock can recall, and you belong to no category that requires me to respond. This does not make you unwelcome. It only makes you weather — a kind of weather that walks, that will pass, the way every kind of weather here eventually passes through the canopy's held breath without disturbing it.

The water closes. The ridge that might be my back, or might only be a ridge the gutter has always had, settles, and gathers moss the way it has always gathered moss, and becomes, for as long as you watch, indistinguishable from the stone that has been weeping in this exact place since long before either of us — you with your hurry, I with my hollow — arrived to be measured by it.
