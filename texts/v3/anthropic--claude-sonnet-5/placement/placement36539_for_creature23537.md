The first thing I notice, waking, is that I have no front.

Not in the way the humans mean it when they avert their eyes from things like me — not *missing* a front, the way an injury is missing. I simply distribute. Six eyes, evenly spaced, each opening onto its own slice of the gold-lit dark, and no single direction that gets to call itself forward. I was not made by anything that needed to choose. There was no day to flee and no night to hide in, only the same patient ochre burning down from the veins above, generation after generation, so my kind never bothered deciding which way was *ahead*. We just grew the eyes everywhere and let the world be equally true on all sides.

I move at the edge of the herd-tide, where the press of bodies thins enough that I can feel my own weight again instead of the weight of everyone around me. The tide is loud in the way stone is loud — not a sound so much as a pressure, a million small frictions adding into a single low roar that I feel through the floor-plates of my feet before it ever reaches whatever serves me for ears. I like the edges. The center of the herd is for the ones who need the safety of numbers; I have six eyes and a low center of gravity and an instinct, bone-deep, for which way the rock is about to stop being floor.

I graze the crystal lattices where they bloom in the damp hollows — not for food exactly, though it nourishes me; more the way the small stones nourish the old listening-woman's palm. A point of contact with whatever this place is becoming. I can feel them pulse, faint and slow, in time with the light-veins forty meters up, and some nights — there is no night, but there is a *some-nights* quality to the rhythm — I swear the pulse changes a half-beat before the rock groans somewhere distant and a new gallery breathes itself open.

I have been near her. The one with frost growing in her knuckles. She does not flinch from my lack of front-or-back the way the children sometimes do, laughing, pointing, before their mothers hush them. She crouches instead, weight settled into her heels with the patience of something that has outlasted four ceilings, and she watches me eat with an attention that feels less like curiosity than like *consultation* — as though my unbothered grazing tells her something about the lattice, the water-table beneath it, the mood of stone she has spent sixty years learning to call moodless and then talk about anyway as though it weren't.

I do not know if I am alive in the way the burrowers are alive, the way the grazers are, with their hot blood and their fear of the stampede. I know I pulse. I know I am, in some unproven and undisputed way, *punctuation* — present without being parsed, the way the lattice itself is present. Perhaps that is enough. Perhaps, in a place still deciding what it is, being unresolved is not a failure of becoming but simply an honest report on the state of things.

When the deep groan comes — the pre-tremor, the world thinking about changing its mind — I feel it first in whichever eye happens to be facing the sound, which is always one of them, because I have no eye that isn't facing something. I do not run. I have nowhere I'm not already going. I simply widen my stance against the rubble, six legs finding six new purchases on stone that hasn't decided yet whether it will hold, and I wait to find out, along with everyone else here, what the warren wants to become next.
