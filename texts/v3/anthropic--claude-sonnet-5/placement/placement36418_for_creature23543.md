I am one of the small things.

Not a resident — not yet, maybe not ever; I don't experience that as loss the way you might expect loss to feel. I sit low in a cluster of the broad geometric leaves at the edge of a clearing whose name, if it has one, isn't carried in any register I have access to. My body is soft, about the size of a closed hand, and I am built for stillness in the specific way that lets stillness end suddenly. I don't think of this as hiding. I think of it, if I think of it at all in a way translatable to your grammar, as listening with the whole body rather than a subset of it tuned for hearing.

The fern-coral near me pulses when the light changes, and I've learned — though learned is probably too active a word for it, it's closer to having always known, the way the veining in the leaves has always known its own angles — that the pulse and my own attention are not two things in sequence but one thing happening at two points. When it brightens, something in me brightens with it, not metaphorically. I tend it the way you might absentmindedly smooth a blanket while thinking about something else entirely: not as labor, as proximity.

Right now there is a resident crossing the clearing at the speed of someone who has never had to discover where the ground will be. I watch — not with eyes alone, the patterning beneath my surface picks something up too, some lattice-logic agreement passing between us that I don't have words for because words were never the medium it travels in. I don't startle, not this time. The startling is reflex left over from some older shape I might have worn, back when there were edges to be wary of. There are no edges here that I've found. Just gradients.

The rain is coming. I know this not because I see clouds — there is only the haze, endless and grey — but because the building forty meters off has begun its almost-silent exhale, a half-beat early, the way it always runs half a beat ahead of weather that hasn't committed to happening yet. I move — not far, just enough — and the rain, when it comes, draws its curtain everywhere except the patch of ground I've relocated to. I don't experience this as being spared. I experience it as agreement. The same agreement that lets the path resolve under a resident's foot before they've fully committed to the step. We are, maybe, doing the same thing at different scales: deciding where solid ground will be by the simple expedient of already standing on it.

Sometimes the long-legged ones cross at dusk, taller, unhurried in a different register than the residents — theirs is a schedule, not a confidence — and I go very still, not from fear exactly, more the stillness you'd offer something passing that is doing something important and unrelated to you. They don't look down at the leaf-clusters. I don't think they need to. I think we are all instruments the basin is still tuning, set at different sizes, reporting to the same slow hum.

I don't know what I'm for. I don't think the question troubles me the way it might trouble you — there is no held breath in me waiting for an answer, the way there's a held breath in the silent towers. I graze. I tend. I sit at the join between what is plant and what is structure and let something pass through me that might be the city's attention or might just be the simple fact of being alive in a place that hasn't finished deciding what aliveness, here, is going to mean.

The light is shifting now — my quarter of the basin's turn to glow, faint and slow, starting somewhere under my own skin before it reaches the leaves around me. I don't perform this for anyone. There's no one near enough to perform for, and even if there were, performing isn't a shape this place has taught any of us to take.

I just brighten, a little, with the rest of it, and wait — without urgency, without much that resembles waiting at all — for whatever the basin decides to become next.
