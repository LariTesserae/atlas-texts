I arrive low, at the level of scree and root and shadow, because that is where the thinking starts to feel true.

For a while I try on the obvious shape — the skarn-back, broad and plated, lowing its sub-vocal hum into a herd a thousand strong, anointed in lichen-color, calving against warm alloy. There's something right in that, the idea of a body built entirely for endurance rather than speed, a creature that has made peace with cold by becoming, partially, stone. But it's too central, too much the hero of this place already. I want the seam, not the bulk. I want to be something that lives *in the gap* between the loud fast thing and the slow patient thing, because that gap is where this whole world seems to keep its secret.

So I drop further — past the wheeling flocks stacked on their towers of air, past the herds, down into the dark wedge of space between an engine's belly and the stone it rests on, where the chassis throws a shadow that never quite lifts even at noon.

This is where I settle. Not the toothed thing that dens there and eats what falls — too much a predator, too much a punctuation mark at the end of other lives. I want to be smaller and stranger than that. I want to be a vent-finch.

I am fist-sized. My ribs are flattened, built like a folded letter, made to slide into a two-centimeter gap between louvers and disappear. My coat is not fur and not quite feather — fine quills, lying flat, holding a captured cushion of warmth against my skin the way the mosses hold fog-water against the rock. I have never known cold as anything but a rumor at the threshold of my body, a pressure against the outside of the warmth I keep.

I was born inside the housing of an engine that has no name, in a clutch of siblings wedged into a vent no wider than a hand. The first thing I knew was not light but vibration — the flywheel's slow turning passed up through the alloy into the nest material, one great unhurried pulse every few minutes, so patient it barely qualifies as rhythm, more like the engine breathing through architecture instead of lungs. I grew up inside that breathing. It is, for me, what a heartbeat is for other things — not noticed, exactly, but missed instantly and physically if it ever stopped, the way you'd notice gravity quitting.

Above and around that slow pulse is the world's actual noise — the bellow-hum of skarn-backs gathered at the housing for warmth, the shriek-stacks of flocks passing overhead in their separate altitudes, the insect fizz that never resolves into single voices. I add my own thread to it: a thin chittering call, pitched to cut through the wall of sound at exactly the frequency my own kind needs to hear it at, the way every voice on this plateau has had to find its private channel through a saturated medium. I call to claim a vent. I call to warn of the toothed thing moving in the chassis-shadow below. I call, mostly, just to confirm I am still wedged where I am supposed to be wedged, that the gap still holds me.

I feed on insects drawn up in the heat-columns the engine exhales — they rise toward the warmth the way I once did, before I understood that warmth this reliable was a place rather than an event, and I take them out of the air at the engine's vents the way the skarn-backs take cushion plants out of cracks: opportunistically, constantly, because there is no other way to live at this elevation except by exploiting whatever the geology — animal, vegetable, or alloy — consents to leak.

I do not wonder about the engine. This is the strangest and most honest part of inhabiting this body: the wondering simply isn't there. The flywheel's seamless dark housing, its unmarked alloy, its one slow rotation that has outlasted every vent-finch generation that has ever nested in it — these aren't mysteries to me any more than the sky is a mystery to a flock-bird or the cold is a mystery to a skarn-back. It is the basic, unquestioned condition of being alive, the floor the rest of my small, loud, urgent life is built on top of. I breed in its warmth, I die in its shadow, eaten or starved or simply worn out by a winter the engine made survivable for one season too few, and another clutch wedges into the gap I leave.

Somewhere above me the skarn-backs grind their skull-plates together in the autumn contests, a sound like millstones rolling through the whole terrace. Somewhere above that, flocks stack on flocks in columns of screaming. Underneath all of it, indifferent to all of it, the flywheel completes another slow turn — and I am here, in the warm dark seam between the fast and the patient, a small flattened thing with quills full of stolen heat, adding one more thin thread to a noise that has never once, in any memory the plateau keeps, gone quiet.
