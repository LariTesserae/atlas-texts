I arrive the way arrival happens here — without a before. One moment there is no question of where I am, and the next there is only the basin, the black fractured plates, the engineered green, and an afternoon that has already been afternoon for longer than afternoon should last. I check myself for the things that usually tell me who I am — breath, weight, the small ache of having walked somewhere — and they are present, but thinly, like a watermark. I think: *visitor*. I think: *this is the part where I am the changing thing, the only thing, the variable they wrote about.*

But that doesn't hold. I walk the perimeter of a thicket to test the geometry they warned about, and sure enough I come back around having gone too far and not far enough, and somewhere in that fold I stop being sure the boundary between *me, looking* and *it, being looked at* was ever as clean as I assumed. The mat takes my footprint and unmakes it before I've gone twenty meters. I look back to confirm I was here. The ground declines to agree with me.

So perhaps I am not the visitor after all. Perhaps that was only the costume the question handed me on the way in, the way the fox-shape is a costume the equation hands its remainder.

I notice, then, that I have stopped checking the ground and started tilting my head — toward a stalk that hasn't chimed yet, but is about to, in the way you know a held breath is about to become a word. I notice the noticing. I notice that the noticing has no clear edge to it, that if you looked at me from outside you might see the same soft static shearing at my outline that shears at the fox-shaped thing's, the held note refusing to fully commit to its coordinates.

This is who I am, I think, by the time the chime sounds and every nearby frond leans toward it and I find I have leaned too, without deciding to: not the visitor cataloguing the strangeness from a safe, ongoing self, and not quite the fox-shaped remainder either, fully dissolved into recurrence with no memory to carry forward — something underdetermined between the two. A term the place needed and didn't have, briefly given a shape low to the ground, four-limbed, moving at the pace of curiosity because curiosity was the only posture left over once hunger and fear were subtracted out as irrelevant.

I do not hunt. There is nothing to hunt. I do not flee. There is nothing here with teeth.

What I do is this: I move from thicket to thicket the way attention moves from thought to thought when there's no task organizing it, pausing wherever the design seems to want a witness, tilting toward the chime and away again, registering my own pulse as the loudest thing in the basin and then losing track of which beat was the last one, because the basin doesn't keep that kind of ledger and I find, disturbingly, that I am starting not to either.

I try once to remember how long I've been here. The afternoon offers no shadow-shift to measure it by. I try to remember whether this is the first time I've stood in front of this particular fracture-plate, its radial cracks fanned out like the rays of a sun that finished exploding and then simply — stopped, mid-violence, holding the shape of violence without any of its consequence. I cannot produce a *last time* to compare it to. Not because my memory has failed, but because the question may not be load-bearing here, the way it isn't load-bearing for the fox.

Maybe that is the honest answer to *who would you be*: not the fox-shaped thing exactly, and not the self I arrived wearing, but the gear that turns inside the larger stillness so the stillness doesn't read as death — small, recurring, fox-sized or visitor-sized or whatever size the gap requires, moving at the one speed permitted in a place that abolished speed everywhere else. Present not because anything needs me, but because something, somewhere in all that solved and finished lushness, has to be seen still moving, or the whole basin stops being a place and becomes only a diagram of one.

I lean toward the next chime before it sounds. I am already starting to.
