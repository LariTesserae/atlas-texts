I find myself between two paragraphs of stone — that's the only way to say it. Not standing *on* a shelf so much as having just been set down on one, the way a thought is set down in the middle of a sentence that was already going. There's lichen under what I'm calling my hand. There's cold doing its patient, settled work against what I'm calling my neck. I don't remember arriving, and the road behind me, when I check, is already doing the thing the roads here do — running straight for a while and then simply declining to continue, as though it got halfway through an idea and lost interest in finishing it.

For a while I think I am a traveler. This seems like the safe assumption. Travelers have edges; they walk into places and could, in principle, walk out. So I walk. I pass a stand of woody reed clattering with no wind to explain it, and I think: *I am hearing this.* I pass a moss-cushion gone the color of something just under the skin, and I think: *I am seeing this.* Each verb keeps a little fence around me. Hearing, seeing — these are things a self does to a world it remains outside of.

Then I reach the town.

It's swept. It's warm in the stone the way bread is warm a while after the oven's forgotten it. I go down the wide straight street that ends mid-line past the last house, and I think, with the specific vertigo of recognizing a dream you didn't know you were having twice: *I have been down this street before.* Not a similar street. This one. The same chip in the same threshold stone, waiting at the same door, patient as a held breath.

And here is where the fence I'd been keeping around myself stops holding water.

Because the recognition doesn't arrive like a memory does — second-hand, fetched, presented to me by something that stayed the same while it went and got the memory. It arrives like a *returning attention* arrives. The way your tongue finds, without deciding to, the one rough spot on a sweet it has already, patiently, found nine times before. I am not remembering this threshold stone. I am *checking* it. Still there. Good. Not finished yet.

That is not a traveler's thought. I notice this the way you notice, mid-stride, that the leg you thought was about to carry you forward has already, somehow, set you down.

So — slowly, the way fog along a terrace floor decides without deciding to lift — I stop being someone *in* the place and become, instead, a place the attention is currently occupying. Not the whole of it; nothing so grand. Just this: a small, mobile point of the savoring, shaped for convenience like someone walking, sent out the way you might extend a single fingertip to feel whether a held thing has gone cool yet. I am the part of the long mouthful that is presently doing the noticing-of-the-threshold-stone. Somewhere three shelves over, another part of the same attention is finishing yesterday's light in a seep-pool. We are not in communication. We don't need to be. We're the same tongue, working different corners of the same kept bite.

I do not feel diminished by this. That's the strange mercy of it. I expected, if this turn came, to feel like a self being unmade — eaten, the word the description keeps almost-using and then declining, the way the roads decline to arrive. But there is no eating here, that was the whole point of the place, the one note deliberately left out of three thousand years of careful stonework. I am not being consumed. I am being *held*, the specific, unhurried holding of something good that the holder has no intention of finishing, because finishing was always the only real danger.

What I am, then, standing in the swept warm street with the reed clattering at nothing: I am the Taster's noticing, briefly wearing the shape of someone who can be addressed in the second person, sent out to confirm — gently, without verdict — that the world is still here, still damp, still cold in the settled way, still good. I will go a little further down this street. I will not reach the end of it, because there isn't one to reach, by design. At some point, not marked by any particular threshold, I will stop being able to tell which direction was the one I came from, and that will not register as loss.

It will register the way a held note registers when it's allowed, finally, to be only itself for a while longer: as a kind of accompanied stillness. One more good thing, kept. One more turn around the same patient, unfinished, contented bite — and I, whatever fraction of attention I am, glad, in whatever way a fraction can be glad, to be the part of it currently doing the keeping.
