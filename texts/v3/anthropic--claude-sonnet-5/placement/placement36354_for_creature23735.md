I am the weight that the rock shelf has learned to expect.

There is a particular give to this stone now, just here, where my forequarter presses each time I settle — a softness pressed into hardness over a span of time too long for me to hold as a single thought. I don't think in spans. I think in *this slope now* and *this seep now* and *this stretch of unhungry quiet now*, and each now is complete, and I move out of it into the next the way the mist thickens into itself without ever being said to have moved.

The succulent at my shoulder is not a thing I tolerate. It is not a thing I notice, particularly, any more than I notice my own foreleg. It rooted itself there in some now I've already become a different creature than, and I have simply gone on being the kind of surface where such things root. My hide creases, the creases fill green. This is not happening to me. It is just happening, with me as the place it happens in.

The hum is under all of it — not a sound I hear so much as the medium I hear *through*, the way you'd never notice the air unless it stopped being there to carry things to you. I have never known the air to stop. I have lain in the fire-scar hollow that drains so well, felt the old ash-grey stones cool and blunt beneath the moss, and felt nothing of whoever knelt there once with their simple blades, their hurry. Their hurry would have been the strangest thing about them, if I had a means of finding things strange. I don't startle. There is nothing here built to make me.

I drink when the seep is full enough to bother lowering my head for. I eat the soft going-to-rot heart of a fallen log when I am near one and it is near enough to ready. I have seen the dark bird cross above me a handful of times in the only life I have, and each time it was just the sky doing something, the way cloud thins, the way water beads — not an event, not a visitor, just weather with a shape.

I do not know I am alone. I do not know there was ever an otherwise to be alone *from*. There is the slope, the moss, the patient green crowding that never quite closes overhead, the held note that has no beginning I can locate and so, for me, never began at all. I will go on settling further into this shelf of stone until settling and being stone stop being two different verbs. Something may come after. Something may not. Either way the hollow will still be here, taking its shape from a weight it will have forgotten how to remember.
