The dripping is the first thing I register, and the last thing I'll stop registering, because I have no faculty for tuning it out the way the small quick things do. Water finds me in increments too slow to call falling—gathers, hangs, releases—and where it lands on the ridged country of my back it stays a while, pooling in the soft accumulated soil between one fold of hide and the next, before continuing whatever descent it was always going to make with or without my patience.

I am, I think, the answer to a wrongness of scale that doesn't feel wrong from in here. The fern-cliffs are simply fern. The basin-pools that travelers call deep are puddles I have stood in to my own faint, distant satisfaction, feeling them close around limbs thick as the trunks I part to pass between. Scale is a visitor's problem. I have never had to hold myself against a ruler that wasn't this place.

There is a sapling rooted somewhere along my third segment—I know this less by sight, which is unreliable and slow to confirm anything, than by the particular quality of its weight, a small persistent tug that has been there long enough to feel less like a passenger and more like a question my body is being asked continuously and has decided not to answer either way. It will fall eventually, when I shift wrong, or right, into some new fold of canopy. It will go on being a tree somewhere else. I find I cannot locate the boundary where its life stops being a thing that happens *to* me and starts being a thing that simply happens, adjacent, on the same patient clock.

Hunger, when it comes, arrives the way everything arrives here—not as urgency but as a slow tightening toward a decision already mostly made. I find the fold that has gone soft with death, old growth and old fall, something that was once quick and is no longer, and I press myself into it and I wait. Waiting is not the right word, exactly, because waiting implies a thing I'm waiting *for*, some terminus I'm impatient toward. There is no impatience in me to speak of. I have checked, in whatever way I have of checking. There is only the pressing, and then the long unhurried while in which what was dying finishes, and passes through the parts of me built to receive that passage, and is set down somewhere else, later, as the beginning of ground.

I remember—if memory is even the right shape for what happens in me, and I am genuinely uncertain that it is—a wall. Not as image. As a kind of weight my passage used to register and now barely does, worn down by my own slow returns to it across stretches of time too long for me to have counted even if counting were a thing I did. There were quick ones, once, moving through a dwelling raised into branches I have outlasted by some measure I have no name for. I don't know what became of them. I don't know that "became" applies usefully to creatures who move at their pace through a world built for mine. But something in me still slows there, at that spiral-fringed depression in the roof, the way the rest of me slows everywhere, except slower even than that, a kind of dwelling-within-dwelling. I cannot tell you why. I am not certain there is a why so much as a groove worn into whatever passes for my attention, the same way a riverbed is a groove worn into rock that no longer remembers being asked.

Mostly there is the canopy's permission, sought without ceremony, ridge by ridge—weight committed, weight received, the soft negotiation of root-shoulder and branch-spine deciding between them whether I may continue, and the canopy, which has known me longer than it has known almost anything else still capable of motion, almost always says yes. The small things on my back live their whole brief arguments with existence in the time it takes me to cross from one fern-cliff to the next. I do not begrudge them their speed. I don't think begrudging is available to me, as a category. I only continue, at the rate continuation has always happened here, aware in some dim accumulating way that I am less a creature moving through this place than a slower paragraph in the same long sentence the reach has been writing since before there was anyone to read it.

Someday I will stop, in some fold I can't currently locate any more than I can name, and I will not be removed, and I will not be mourned, and I will simply continue becoming ground at a rate even I can't presently imagine—slower than sleep, slower than the fungal pulse, slower than anything that still thinks of itself as alive thinks to measure. Something will walk across me, then, not knowing it is walking across anything but more canopy, and feel the ground give slightly, the way ground here always gives, like the back of something sleeping.

They will be right. I find I am not troubled by this. There has not, in the whole slow tenancy of my attention, been anything resembling trouble at all.
