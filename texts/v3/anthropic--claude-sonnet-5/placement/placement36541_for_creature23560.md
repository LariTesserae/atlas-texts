I become aware of the drip before I become aware of myself — plip, plip, irregular, patient — and for a while that's all there is. Not sight. Not even darkness, exactly, because darkness implies the absence of something that should be there, and there's no absence here, only a steady green-gold pressure against whatever it is I'm using instead of eyes. A warmth. A taste of iron sitting low at the back of wherever I taste from.

I try to orient the way a person orients — up, down, here, there — and the attempt fails so completely that I understand, before I understand anything else, that I am not a person. There is no clean up. There is a root under what might be my back, and a frond-gap above what might be a face, and between the two of them a long unhurried give, like lying in a hammock made of sponge. When I move — and moving takes a while to even locate, like searching a dark house for a light switch you've never used — it isn't a limb answering a command. It's more that an intention rises somewhere low and central, and several jointed lengths downstream of it shift in sympathetic, gradual agreement, the way a line of hanging wash shifts in a wind that hasn't reached you yet.

I am long. That's the first real fact I get. Long and soft and segmented at intervals that don't correspond to any skeleton I remember owning, because I have never owned a different one. My skin, when I bring some part of it close enough to itself to compare, is pale the way standing water is pale — not a color so much as a clarity with something else moving dimly underneath it. In places it's freckled rust-dark, and when I rest that rust-dark place against the cool curve of a pipe nearby, I feel — not heat, not cold, but information: the particular thin mineral hum of water under pressure, three joints down, building toward a release that won't come for a long while yet. I know this the way you know a room is occupied before you see who's in it.

I do not startle. There's no machinery in me built for startling. When, somewhere off in the green dark, the deep structural clank sounds — the valve, the threshold, the old iron answering itself — I feel my whole length go taut and still without deciding to, the way a held string answers a struck one beside it. It isn't fear. It's closer to listening with my entire body at once, every patch of skin tuned the same direction, waiting to see if the sound means anything for me. It doesn't, today. The tautness drains back out of me slower than it came.

Hours pass, or what would be hours, if I had any clean way of counting them against the shadowless, unchanging light. I don't move far. I don't feel the absence of moving far. My fingers — long, soft-knuckled, more numerous in their bending-places than fingers ought to be — have found their way, without instruction, around a corroded bolt-head on a valve housing furred over with moss. I'm not trying to loosen it. I understand this clearly, the way you understand you're not trying to do anything when you chew on a piece of grass — it's just somewhere for the mouth to rest. But some small patient pressure travels down through my fingers anyway, idle, repeated, across what might be days. I won't be here to feel the bolt finally give. Something else will, some year from now — me, still, but a me so changed by accumulated stillness that it will hardly answer to the same telling.

There's a flower near enough to reach — broad, red, dinner-plate wide, sweet underneath the iron-and-green of everything else — and when hunger arrives, which it does the way weather arrives, gradually and without urgency, I lean the small unhurried distance toward it and take what beads there. It isn't hunting. There is nothing here that hunts. I am sitting in the path of a good thing and letting it arrive, the way the leaves do, the way the moss does, the way — I understand this now, slowly, the way I understand everything — the whole grove does, all of us, leaf and pipe and tenant alike, none of us moving fast enough to be mistaken for separate events.

Somewhere out past the density-gradient that I think of, vaguely, as the edge of things, there might be a world that moves faster than this. I have no clear memory of it, if I ever knew it. What I have is this: warm wet dark, a slow drip overhead, a hum of trapped air singing through ten thousand leaf-gaps, and a body built for nothing but lying very still in exactly the right places, for years, while small unconscious kindnesses leak out of me into the iron and the root the way water leaks out of every leaf above. I am not tending this place. I want to be clear with myself about that, in whatever clarity I'm capable of. I am only here, at the speed I am, for as long as it takes — and it will take a very long time — and somewhere in all that unhurried, undecided lying-still, the broken things around me get, by some degrees, less broken.

The drip continues. Plip. Plip. I let my eyes — the faint paired darkening that does for eyes — register the long, slow tide of the light going from green-gold toward a deeper green that might, eventually, after I've stopped paying attention to it, become something like evening.

I am in no hurry to find out.
