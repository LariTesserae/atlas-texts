The wind reaches me before the shelf does — I feel it as a pressure in the long bones of my forelimbs, which are planted, all four, in stance, lifting the keel of my body into the column of moving air the way a held note lifts into a room. I am standing. I do not stand often. I am standing now because there is a boundary in question half a mile to the east, where the geometry shifted again at the last light-pulse and someone else's claimed quiet now overlaps mine by some stretch of unstill rock neither of us asked to share.

I am the caller. Not a caller — the distinction doesn't survive translation, there is no plural that matters to me, only the single throat I have and the map behind my small flat eyes of every other throat within a day's wind. I do not think in images. I think in distances that are also debts. The shape to the east has a register I know without ever having seen its body — a low, scraped, declarative sound, the voice of something that has held its ground before and means to again. My own chest, three lungs nested like the towers are nested in their leaning cluster, is already filling, already converting the thin sharp air into the only currency that settles anything here.

I am aware, in whatever diffuse way my brain bothers being aware of things it doesn't need to act on, of the ring — somewhere behind me, humming at a pitch that's crept upward since dawn, dragging its small glowing dependents into closer orbit. I am aware of the stalks I grazed an hour ago, three of them bitten at lengths I chose without choosing, the way the wind through their new stumps will sound different tonight, slightly sharper, slightly mine. I am aware, at the very corner of where awareness gives out, of a stillness near the towers that might be a shape and might be nothing, watching or not watching, tending or not tending — it makes no claim on my boundary so I make none on its reality.

None of this is urgency. There is no danger in losing — only the long undignified walk afterward toward whatever unclaimed silence the shifting shelf happens to owe me. There is only the held breath, and the wind passing through me like I am one more hollow stalk the architecture hasn't finished building, and the column of sound about to leave my body and travel half a mile to meet, or fail to meet, or outlast the other claim.

I throw my whole hollow weight into it. The note rises, splits across the wind's funnel into something that sounds like more than one throat, and goes out to measure, again, today, how much of this loud and unfinished room is mine.
