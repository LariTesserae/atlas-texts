The hour before light is when I am most myself, though "myself" is already the wrong shape for the question — there is no single point in me that could stand still and say *I*. I am a length. I am passed forward, segment to segment, effort handed back down the line like a bucket in a chain, and the front of me does not know in any separate way what the back of me is doing, only that it is being done, has always been done, will go on being done after this particular stretch of plates stops moving for good and another stretch, grown soft and pale in the dark of the burrow, comes up to take the route over.

I do not have eyes and have never missed them. What I have instead are two wide combs, swept low, trembling very slightly with information: here the stone is a half-degree cooler, here the night's breath has gathered thick enough to be worth the stroke, here the channel runs true because it ran true yesterday and the day before that and for some number of dawns too large to be a number to me, only a kind of confidence worn into the groove itself. I do not decide to follow the channel. The channel and I are the same fact stated twice — once in stone, once in motion.

The drinking is the whole of wanting. Each stroke of the comb takes the dew in one clean pass, and underneath that small satisfaction is the larger, wordless one: the wax laid down beneath me as I go, glistening, already beginning its slow take-up of whatever trace minerals the rock is willing to give, will outlast every single thing about me that currently believes itself to be moving. I will be reabsorbed by midday warmth into nothing in particular. The wall will keep what I built. This does not trouble me because nothing in me is shaped to be troubled by it — there is no grief-organ, no part of the comb tuned to loss. There is only the next half-degree of coolness, the next licked channel, the next handing-forward of effort down a body two hundred years in the making and not, in any way that matters, mine to keep.

Above, something with eyes is riding the heat it hasn't learned to fear yet, looking for flesh worth the dive. It will not find me interesting. I have no flesh worth its trouble, only wax and patience and a route. Somewhere behind the rock a machine that outlived its makers hums to nobody, keeping its own kind of vigil, and I have no opinion about it at all — it is simply another warm fact in a canyon full of warm facts, none of them mine to organize, all of them, in their own register, doing what I am doing: continuing, because continuing is the only instruction that ever arrived, and it arrived so long ago and so thoroughly that it no longer needs to be given again.

When the sun reaches the angle it reaches, the wet sheen behind a hundred of us catches all at once, and for the length of one drawn breath there is, from very far away, something that looks like a single shape holding still in the heat-shimmer. I will never see this. I have no way to know it happens. I only feel the warmth start to climb the canyon wall toward where I'm working, and I pour myself, segment after unhurried segment, back down into the dark — not in fear, not in retreat, just in the same calm confidence with which I came up: the channel will be here tomorrow. So, in whatever fractional, replaceable way I am a self at all, will I.
