The crack admits me sideways, the way it admits everyone, and the warmth of it goes through my shoulder like something checking my pulse before deciding to let me further in.

I am the stoop. I am the hands.

I have been at this ledge since the glow below went from red to a deeper red, which is the only clock I keep, and before that I was at the ledge above, and before that, lower again — the order doesn't hold still long enough to be a story, only a *practice*. Right now the wedge is going in at the angle my wrist already knows, has known longer than whatever I'd call a self, finding the grain the stone hasn't finished deciding on. Tap. The rock answers with a give that is almost agreement. Pause — because the pause is part of the tool, the part where I let the mountain's slow exhale catch up to my fast one. Tap.

Someone above me — I won't look up, looking up is for people who trust the rungs more than their hands — slaps a leaf aside twice. *Mind the drop.* I shift my weight a half-hand's-width left without breaking rhythm, the way you'd lean from a wave without waking from the sleep it's rocking you in.

My skin is doing what it does, going soft and pale at the knuckles where the wet has had the longest argument with me, and somewhere under the patience of that there is a fact I don't examine too closely: that I am made of the same becoming as the terrace, just slower, just on a longer clock, and that the awl three rungs up went the color it went on the day I set it down because we were, the awl and I, at that hour, the same age of wet.

I don't ask the mountain to stop. I never held that wanting long enough for it to leave a tool-mark. What I hold instead, the only thing my hands have ever finished becoming sure of, is this: stay one tap ahead. Let nothing it grows arrive without my having already half-shaped the space it grows into. Not mastery — I have watched water run sideways up a seam two rungs from here and felt no urge to correct it, only to carve a lip where it might pool, useful, instead of merely strange.

The half-song starts without my deciding to start it, pitched low to meet the groan under the floor, two creatures keeping time with each other without either one leading. It isn't for anyone. It isn't even, really, for the stone. It's what a hand sounds like when it's stopped being separate from its work.

Tap. Pause. Tap.

Behind me the fern that wasn't there an hour ago is already thick enough to brush my shoulder when I lean back to check the wedge's seat. I don't move it. I work around it. That's the whole of what I know how to want.
