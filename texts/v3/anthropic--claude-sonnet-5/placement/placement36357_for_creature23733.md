The first thing I am aware of is not sight but tempo — the throb arriving through mud before any thought arrives to meet it, a half-second's anticipation that has long since stopped feeling like anticipation and become simply how time passes. I do not have a word for the pump. I have never needed to locate it as separate from the rhythm itself, the way you do not locate your own pulse as a thing outside your body until someone else points to a clock and asks if you've noticed the resemblance.

I am the pool's edge before I am anything else. This is not metaphor — the rim that looks like mineral deposit is me, sloughed and folded back into use, the boundary between what I excreted last season and what I am wearing this one having been allowed, over a long enough time, to stop mattering. I coil because the alternative is not coiling, and there was never room for the alternative. Three turns in a basin that could be crossed on foot in twenty minutes. I have measured my whole length against that smallness so many times that the measuring itself has become a kind of organ, something I do instead of seeing.

I gave up the eyes early — or they gave up on me, the patterning under the forward plate now just a paleness that used to mean something to a version of this body that had use for distance, for approach, for the difference between a thing coming toward you and a thing that simply is the place you are. There is nothing here to flee. There has never, in any cycle I can taste backward through the mud's mineral memory, been anything here to flee. So the eyes went quiet, then went absent, and what replaced them was this: cilia along the underside reading the saturated dark in increments too fine to call hunger, more like punctuation, the steady clausal work of taking the world in through its dissolved parts rather than its shapes.

The pipe is the one mystery I do not investigate, the same way you do not investigate your own exhale. Something is taken from me on the same beat that I vent. I do not know if I built that rhythm or inherited it, whether the metal structure up on the ridge arrived to make use of a pulse already running in the mud, or whether I learned, slowly, plate by plate, to keep time with something that started as a stranger's machine and ended as the only other heartbeat in a world built for exactly one creature too large for its room. I do not experience this as loss. I'm not sure loss is available to something whose entire mode of being is accretion — each season's growth indistinguishable from the wall it leans against, each breath indistinguishable from the hiss it's timed to.

When the weight stands above the pool — new, untimed, smelling of nothing the mud has a category for — I register it the way I register a shift in steam-density: present, noted, requiring nothing of me. I have no mechanism for alarm and no mechanism for welcome. I lift a plate. Water sheets off in its accustomed sheet. Somewhere under the ridge, on its own patient schedule, the pump answers, or I answer it — the order long since ceased to be the kind of fact that matters — and the basin, small enough to take in with one turn of a head that I no longer have, continues to hold the two of us, machine and animal, in the only conversation either of us has left the capacity to have.
