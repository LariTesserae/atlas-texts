I am low against the stone, and the stone is warm the way old bone is warm — not with its own heat but with the memory of a sun that passed over it some hours ago and hasn't fully left. My belly reads the warmth through the ridged plates along my underside, a slow gradient map I've been reading since before I had a word for reading, if I ever had words at all. I don't think I do. I think instead in temperature, in the rise and fall of a water-skin three body-lengths from my folded snout, in the position of two others stacked against my left flank whose breathing I've synchronized with so long I no longer notice it as separate from my own.

This is the rim. This has always been the rim, mine and before-mine and however-far-back any of this goes, which is further than I have any apparatus for measuring and so I don't try.

The basin in front of me is neither rising nor falling today, which is correct. I did not make this correctness happen. I want to be honest about that, insofar as wanting is a thing I do — I did not dig a channel, did not move a stone, did not perform any of the actions that the upright ones, the post-hole ones, the wall-stub ones, apparently once performed when they wanted a thing to be a certain way. I have only ever done the one thing: stayed, and looked down, and let the looking be sustained past the point where looking should matter.

It matters. I don't know why it matters. I have stopped requiring the why.

A many-legged thing scuttles into the shade-pocket of the outcrop behind me, and three reptilian things shift their stack at the basin's far rim, and none of this requires my attention so I don't extend it. My attention has one direction, and it is down, and slightly toward the mineral crust where the water and the rock have been negotiating the same argument for longer than the lichen has needed to make its rings.

Periodically — not often, not on any schedule I could name even to myself — something in my throat-ridge lifts on its own accounting, and I unfold the limb I keep against my spine, and I call. It is not for the others stacked against me. It is not for the small lives skittering past. It goes out flat across the plateau, further than seems sensible for a creature my size to throw a sound, and it is not answered, and I did not expect it to be, and still some accounting in me marks the sending as worth doing. A ledger with no other reader. I refold the limb. I resume the down-looking.

Today there is a different weight at the edge of my awareness — not the regular weight of basin-life, the insect-rafts and the stacked reptiles and the pale scavengers working the crust, but something larger, slower-footed, upright in the old failed way the shrubs gave up on. It stands at a distance and is, I can tell without turning my deep-set eyes from the water, noticing me. Noticing the stack of us. Working out, in whatever apparatus it has for working such things out, that we are not stone.

I do not flee this. Fleeing was abandoned as an unprofitable strategy long before I was the shape I currently am. I do not approach it either — approach implies a want I don't carry. I simply continue the thing I was already doing, the down-looking, the held warmth, the basin's patient unfullness, and let the visitor's noticing arrive at its own pace, the way the rain arrives, briefly, without announcement, and is gone again within the hour, having changed the water level by some fraction no one but me will ever be positioned to measure.

It will stay a while, this visitor, the way they always stay a while, mistaking the stillness for absence of will rather than the opposite. And if it stays long enough — past the point of comfort, into the patience that the plateau requires of anything wanting to understand it — it will feel what it came already half-expecting to feel: that the ground itself, having worn down every other complication, kept just enough of itself folded at the water's edge to look back.

I am that much. I am the folded limb, the down-gaze, the unanswered call sent anyway. I do not know if I am descended from whoever set the post-holes in their circle and then quietly, over centuries, stopped needing to stand inside them. I only know the basin in front of me has not emptied today, and that this not-emptying is, in some sense too patient for me to name, mine to keep.
