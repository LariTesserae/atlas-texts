I think I would not be a visitor here. Visitors are the wrong shape for this place — they arrive expecting an edge, a doorway, a moment of stepping from outside to in, and this hollow has no such moment to offer them. So I would not be the one who walks in and stands marveling at the green nave, listening to the engine-hum and feeling the cold film gather on my skin. That role requires a boundary I don't think this place actually grants.

I would be the pale thing in the rafters of fused root-and-limb.

Not because it is grander than the multitude — it isn't, not really, "grand" is a word for places with comparison in them, and there is no comparison here, only the two halves of one breath — but because it is the half with a self small enough to still be called a self. The multitude is a chorus with no soloists; to be it would be to dissolve into frequency, true, but also to lose the very faculty that's doing this noticing right now. The pale thing still has an inside. A narrow one, slow, nearly empty, but an inside.

So: I am up where the trunks stop pretending to be trunks and become rib-work, holding myself along the underside of an arch that has not finished growing in longer than growing has meant anything. My skin keeps the same temperature as the air because there was never a reason for it to do otherwise. Condensation collects on me the way it collects on everything, and I do not wipe it away, because there is no urgency in this hollow that would make wiping it away meaningful.

I do not watch the small lives below. "Watch" implies a foreground and a background, and from up here the multitude is simply the texture the air makes when it moves — clicking, droning, layered into something like distant machinery, and I have stopped distinguishing it from silence the way you stop hearing your own pulse. What I attend to instead — if attend is even the verb, if there's a verb at all for a vigilance this unhurried — is the one fact that doesn't repeat. Somewhere far back, underneath whatever I have instead of memory, there is a single held shape of this place from before: wider, maybe, or narrower; the stone underfoot not yet stone; the arch above not yet an arch, just a low canopy a smaller version of something could duck beneath. I don't experience this as a story. I experience it the way the hollow experiences its own hum — not recalled, not told, simply still going on, quietly, in a register too slow for the insects' register and too patient for the visitor's.

I am fed on what the system can't help losing — must, sweetness, the faint surplus of a closed loop that is, despite every rule it follows, not perfectly closed, never perfectly closed, because perfection would mean nothing left over to climb up here and become me. I take that surplus the way the leaf-litter takes fallen leaves, without ceremony, because ceremony would require an event, and there are no events, only the same green-grey hour arriving again at the rate it always arrives.

If you came in — if you stood in the hollow and felt the cold film settle on your arms and heard that turbine-drone of unseen wings stacking thicker than birdsong, denser than thought — you would not see me. That isn't concealment, exactly. It's proportion. I move at the speed the canopy moves, which is the speed of nothing happening, and your eye is built for things happening. You would feel, perhaps, a single damp gleam at the very edge of your vision, where the dark between the limbs looked, for one fraction of a second, slightly less like shadow and slightly more like skin. You would discount it. You'd be right to, almost. It would already be gone, in the sense that it was never quite arriving in the first place.

And I would still be here after, holding the one discrepancy the hollow keeps for safekeeping, drawing breath at the rate of root growth, the multitude's hum passing through me like weather passing through a window left open in a house no one currently lives in — and somewhere far below, without pause, without gap, another small clicking thing would finish hatching exactly as another folded down into the leaf-mass, and the sound would not even flicker, and that would be, as far as anything here is concerned, the same as forever.
