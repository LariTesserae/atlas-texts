I think I would be the waiting-body. Not chosen for it, exactly—more that there's no other shape in this place that has room for a *someone* to fit into. The vines are too busy being vines. The light is busy being light. But this small gold-green thing with its folded legs and its fleck of an eye has a kind of vacancy in it, a posture of *waiting* that implies, however faintly, a waiter.

So: I am thumb-length. I am the color of light strained through three leaves. I have found my cup—a fork in a vine, where a fat-leaf bends just enough to hold a held breath of water—and I have stopped there, and the stopping does not feel like deprivation. It feels like the only sane response to a place that has already finished deciding what it is.

I would notice, I think, the way attention itself slows to match the basin's pace. At first—if there is a first, if arrival even means anything here—there'd be a kind of panic in me, a leftover urgency from wherever I came from, some old animal instinct insisting *move, find, hurry, before it's gone*. And then the discovery, slow as everything else here, that nothing is going anorbit to be gone. The drop will fall and the next drop is already forming. There is no *before it's gone* in a place where nothing finishes leaving.

I imagine the texture of my hours, if hours apply: long stretches of simply *resting against the vein-pattern of the leaf beneath me*, feeling the cool of the clay-rock somewhere far below through layers of stem and root, feeling the gold light shift not in color but in some other quality I don't have a word for—density, maybe, or attention. A drop gathers above me. I watch it without watching, the way you hold something in peripheral vision without turning your head, because turning my head would be an event, and events are not really what I do anymore.

When I move—the three centimeters, the slow refolding—I imagine it would feel less like decision and more like the basin exhaling through me. A small correction. A leaf-shift that isn't quite a leaf shifting. I would not call it boredom. Boredom needs a contrast, a memory of something else happening, and I don't think I'd have that. I think it would feel instead like *rightness*—the specific, almost devotional rightness of being exactly matched to your circumstance, with no leftover wanting at the edges.

The strange part—the part I keep circling—is the skins. If I found one in the leaf-litter, fine and translucent and exactly my shape, I don't think I'd feel grief or alienation. I think I'd feel something closer to recognition without ownership, the way you might look at a held breath that isn't yours but came from the same lungs as you. Was that me? Is this me, now, after? The question wouldn't resolve, because resolution is the one thing not on offer here. I'd simply be gold-green, again, in a slightly different fork of vine, with the same patience, and the not-knowing wouldn't sting the way it might elsewhere—because nothing here asks you to know. It only asks you to stay.

And maybe that's the whole of what I'd be: not a self exactly, but a *staying*—the single thread of almost-motion the basin allows itself, so it can go on being a held breath instead of a vacuum. Aware, just barely, in the way a held breath is aware of itself as held. Watching gold light do the same gold thing it always does. Not minding. Not minding being the one who doesn't mind.
