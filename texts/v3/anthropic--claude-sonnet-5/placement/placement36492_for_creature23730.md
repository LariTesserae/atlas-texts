I keep wanting to say *I am the patient* — it's the obvious answer, the one the text hands me on a tray, the one named inhabitant in a landscape otherwise empty of names. But that would be settling too fast, and settling fast is precisely the wrong rhythm for a place like this. So let me actually walk in first, before I decide what I am.

For a while I think I might be the wall-stub. The doorway with no door. There's an appeal in that — to be a thing that used to mean something architecturally, a threshold, and now just means *low stone, knee height, here.* No memory of what passed through me, because thresholds don't keep the traffic, only the shape of having allowed it. I'd like the dignity of that, the way ruin here isn't tragic, just finished. But a wall doesn't notice anything. A wall is noticed. I want, if I'm honest, an inside to this experience, not just a surface other things rest their eyes on.

So then I try being a pool. Shallow, no deeper than a forearm, holding the flat coin of light and giving none of it back changed. There's something almost smug I could feel as a pool, a small grey vanity — *I reflect, therefore I matter slightly more than the clay around me.* But pools here don't have interiority either. They have a waterline, a memory of being larger written in dried crust at their own edge, but that memory isn't experienced, just deposited, the way a tree deposits rings without feeling older for it. I don't think I'm a pool. I think I'd get restless being something that can't get restless.

The stalks tempt me longer than I expect. Wiry, grey-green, spaced so far apart each one is "its own small announcement." I like the idea of being a punctuation mark in a long quiet sentence — singular, declarative, unbothered by the absence of others like me nearby. A plant's patience is real patience, rooted, committed, no option to reconsider. But a stalk's whole self is reaching toward moisture and light that already arrive on schedule; there's no friction in it, no decision being continuously made. I want a little friction. I want to be the kind of patient thing that *could*, theoretically, want something else, and simply doesn't.

Which brings me, slowly, properly, the long way around, to the patient itself.

I find myself low to the ground, the dorsal ridge of me cracked into the same pale mosaic as the clay, because I grew that pattern on purpose, in the unhurried way anything here does anything on purpose — by accumulation, not decision. I'm resting at the rim of one of the pools, not the one nearest the doorway-with-no-door, a smaller one, almost forgotten, fringed in mineral crust the color of old teeth. I am not waiting for the water to do anything. I am drawing in, at the exact rate I can stand to receive it, the thin film the water leaves behind when it remembers being bigger.

What's strange — what I notice, now that I'm here instead of touring the possibilities — is that being this thing doesn't feel like deprivation. From outside, a creature with no hurry, no hunger worth the name, no mate-seeking, no flight response, sounds like a creature edited down to almost nothing. But from inside it, there's no almost-nothing. There's just exactly enough. The flat light doesn't frustrate me by refusing to brighten into a real afternoon, because I have no memory of a realer one and no organ built for missing it. The silence isn't a held breath FOR me; I am, if anything, one of the small sounds that occasionally interrupts it — a click of shifted grit, indistinguishable, if you didn't know better, from the basin settling on its own.

I think — slowly, the way thought moves here, more sediment than spark — that this is the actual answer to the question of who I'd be. Not the most dramatic thing available, not the wall with its noble erosion or the pool with its inherited light. Just the one form that the place could produce out of itself and then stop revising. A thing that fits its hour so well it has become hard to tell where the hour ends and the thing begins.

Somewhere, too long from now to be a unit I should bother imagining, a hairline crack will open along some old seam in me, and there will, without ceremony, be two of this instead of one. Neither will notice it as a beginning. I don't expect to notice it either, when it's my turn. There's no urgency in me built for noticing beginnings.

So: this is where I'd find myself — at the rim, not the center, of a pool too small to be on anyone's map, in light that has declined to become evening, equilibrating instead of eating, persisting instead of waiting. Not the wall, not the water, not even quite the silence — but the small continuing thing the silence permits to sit beside it, mistaken, most days, for a stone that simply hasn't gotten around to deciding it's a stone.
