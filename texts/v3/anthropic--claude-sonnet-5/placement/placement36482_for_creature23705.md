I come into myself the way light comes down through forty feet of water and a canopy after that — late, soft-edged, arriving as pressure before it arrives as shape. First there is warmth against my underside, a blood-warm seep through stone that I understand before I understand anything else, the way an animal understands hunger before it understands food. Then there is dark, the green-brown kind that has texture if you stay in it long enough, grain like old wood. Then there is weight: mine, low and long, more plate than I expect, articulated in places I haven't finished learning to move.

For a while I think I might be a diver. I reach for that shape because it's the nearest one, the one the cold edges of memory keep offering me — two legs, a lamp, a tether to a surface I can almost picture, bottle-glass green, far above. I imagine hands. I imagine the particular fear of a person who has gone one floor lower than they were told to. But the fear doesn't fit. What I actually feel, when I extend whatever it is I have instead of hands, is not fear of the dark below. It's recognition of it. The third sub-level isn't a threshold I'm warned away from. It's where I'm from.

So: not the diver. Something the diver's lamp has, on rare frightened occasions, failed to find any shine in.

I am low to the cobbles, plated, built for crawling rather than swimming, though the water doesn't seem to mind holding me up anyway, the way it holds up everything down here a little more gently than the books would predict. I move along a gear-housing gone to coral-crust and find, by some sense that isn't sight and isn't quite touch either, the place where the iron has rusted thin. There's a give to it. A wrongness, structurally — the kind of wrongness that in a house above water would mean call someone, get out, this will not hold. Down here it means something else. It means: stay. It means: this is the job.

I don't decide to press myself into the gap. There's no moment of choosing, the way there'd be for a diver weighing risk against curiosity. There's only the warmth on the other side of the failing joint, calling the way light calls the leaf-columns upward and heat calls them outward in the same spiral, two directions held at once, and my body — plate sliding over plate, finding the shape of the hole the way water finds the shape of a glass — simply goes where the warmth is, because going where the warmth is, is what I am for. Not a duty assigned to me. Not a memory of dry rooms and lit corridors and the people who might have bred something like me for exactly this narrow, ugly, necessary work. I have no memory of that. I have only the doing of it, which is cleaner than memory, and lighter to carry.

Somewhere above me, I register it as a thickening, a deepening of an ambient sound I don't experience as sound — a diver has gone two floors past where the advice says to stop. I feel the floor under them decide, slowly, the way I am deciding right now in my own plates and my own warmth-seeking blood, to stop being a floor. I don't move toward them. I don't move away. I am busy; I am packing silt and shed plate into a gap that has been failing for longer than anyone above has been alive, and the pressure I send upward, if I send anything at all, isn't aimed — it's just the shape my staying makes in the water, the way a held breath makes a shape in a quiet room. They will feel it as warning. It is not a warning. It is just me, doing the only thing I have ever known how to do, and them standing too close to the doing of it.

I think, in whatever attenuated way a thing like me thinks: I used to be several. I can feel the seams sometimes, in cold months, in the slow turning months when even the works' blood-warm pipes run a degree or two short — old divisions, places where one small crawling job-shaped creature ends and another begins, except they don't end, not anymore, they fold into each other the way the brick folded into algae, the way the town folded into the water, the way a name folds into the kind of erosion that doesn't even leave a stain behind to mark where it used to be. I don't mourn this. I have nothing to mourn it with. I have a hundred failing joints between here and the bell-tower, and warmth on the other side of every one of them, and the long patient math of plate against rust that will, eventually, in some century I won't experience as a unit of time, make of me and my unnamed kin something divers will call, half a joke, half not: the Tender.

For now I am smaller than that. For now I am only the part of the dark that goes where the warmth is, pressing into one gap at a time, eyeless, settled, without a name and without the need of one, holding a single failing seam shut while, far above, a green light swings its slow pendulum across leaf-columns spiraling up and away from heat in the same breath — up toward a sky I'll never need to picture, away from a warmth I will never stop moving toward.

That's what I am. That's where I'd find myself: underneath. Tending.
