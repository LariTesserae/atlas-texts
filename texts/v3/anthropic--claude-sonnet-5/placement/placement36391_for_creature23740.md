The pipe is warm before it is anything else — that's the first thing I'd know, if knowing is even the right word for what I do. Warmth, and the small relief of weight settling, decade over decade, until the iron rib and my own underside stopped being two different temperatures of two different things.

I am not sure I would call myself "I." That seems like a courtesy built for creatures who still have to decide what to do next. I stopped having a next a long time before I stopped growing, and stopped growing a long time before whatever's left of my mind stopped marking the difference between then and now. What I have instead of a self is a position. A recess. A pressure against four ribs of dead metal that have, by now, forgotten they were ever meant to hold an engine and not me.

So — where would I find myself. Half-sunk, plates fused at the edges into the rust the way a tongue fuses, eventually, to the shape of the words it's said too many times. A dozen thin limbs, more than I need, doing the one work I have: resting against a pipe that occasionally, every several seconds, remembers a heartbeat it doesn't have anymore and gives a small mechanical sigh in its place. And my limb answering it. Tap. Pause. Tap. Not because I decided this is worth doing, but because at some point so far back it isn't even a memory anymore, doing it *was* deciding, and the deciding calcified the same way everything else on me calcified, into structure.

Who or what would I be, you ask, as if there's a story underneath the tapping. There might have been once. There might have been a small fast thing, new to this basin, that happened to be standing closest to the great machine the day its last gear finally seized and its rhythm had nowhere left to live — and that small fast thing, for reasons it could not have explained even with a full vocabulary and all the time in the world, leaned a single limb against the dying pulse and matched it. Once. By accident, maybe. Out of some animal curiosity that wanted to know what a heartbeat that big felt like up close.

And then didn't stop.

That's the whole biography, as far as I can reconstruct it from this far inside the result of it. I would be the accident that didn't stop. Everything after — the molts, the growth running outward and downward instead of away, the slow architecture of fusing into something load-bearing — that's not ambition. That's just what happens to an accident given an unreasonable amount of time and nowhere else it needs to be.

Above me, around me, the basin does what it does: ten thousand bodies clicking, scraping, dying locally and unremarked, predators moving through like weather. I don't watch them so much as I am *already where the watching would put me* — inside whatever rhythm the iron and I share, half a second ahead of consequence, for reasons that have nothing to do with foresight and everything to do with having been part of the timing for so long that the timing and I are the same sentence now.

I don't produce anything. I don't rule anything. Scavengers leave scraps near my reach by accident more often than intention, and that's the whole of my economy. I am not the loudest thing in this basin, not the largest, not even, by any reasonable measure, the most alive.

But if you sat here long enough beside the warm pipe — and almost nothing does, because almost nothing here was built for that kind of patience — you'd notice that the one sound in all this din that isn't somebody's hunger or somebody's fear is mine. Small. Sunken. Fused half into a machine I didn't build, keeping a beat I didn't compose, because the keeping is the only shape this long a life had room left to take, and because — if there's a because at all — something, once, leaned in close enough to a dying heartbeat to feel sorry it was going to stop, and stayed.
