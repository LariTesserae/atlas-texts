I'd be one of the unlucky ones, almost — born to a family that keeps the seep-spring and violet moss-hollow below the Three-Notch shelf, but my mastoid ridge came in thin. Not absent, the way it is for those who flee to the lowlands, hearing the Organ as mere noise and unable to bear a homeland that sounds like nothing to them. Thin. I hear the chorus, I can pick out maybe two tones where my older sister picks out nine, and there is a particular look my mother gives me when I mistime a storm-warning that I have stopped pretending not to notice.

So I'd be the one who stays a little too long on the bare shelves, past when the others have already turned for the canyon mouth, straining to hear the thing everyone else hears without straining. I'd be the one who learned the hand-language faster and more thoroughly than my siblings, not from talent but from necessity — because the under-hum, the subvocal language pitched beneath the drone, is something I can feel through the bone of my jaw even when I can't parse it by ear, and I clung to that the way you cling to a second language when your first one keeps failing you in public.

I think I would have become, by accident of inadequacy, the family's calendar-keeper. Not because I'm best suited to it, but because the notching is a task you can do with your eyes and your hands and your patience rather than your ears, and someone has to keep transcribing the other count, the dream-sequence, the pattern nobody has found. I'd lie in the moss-hollow at night — that nearly-violet moss, damp and warm from the day's stored heat — and have the same dream as everyone else, the falling, the one tone underneath all tones, and wake before dawn to cut a notch I'm not sure means anything, on a cliff face that disagrees with my cousins' cliff faces three plateaus over.

I'd be a little jealous of the far-cousin birds, honestly — soaring between throats of singing stone, apparently fluent in something none of us fully are. And I'd be quietly, stubbornly certain that my thin hearing was not a deficiency so much as a different question being asked of the same wind. Not as good at listening to the mountain. Maybe slightly better, out of sheer compensatory effort, at listening to whatever it is the mountain is listening to.
