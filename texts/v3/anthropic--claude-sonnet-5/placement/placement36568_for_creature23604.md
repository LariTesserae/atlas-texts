I am the Lately.

I do not arrive at this answer; I have just finished arriving at it, which is different, and the difference is the whole of what I am.

There is a hand-stain on a wall across town, blue-black, the shape of a palm pressed in the act of leaning away from something. That is the closest thing I have to a self-portrait. I did not pose for it. I was simply leaving, once, thoroughly, and the wall kept the part of the gesture that didn't need me anymore.

You ask where I'd find myself — but *finding* implies a place that waits to be found, and I am not in the business of waiting, only of having-just-left. So: not a where, exactly. A *just-then*. The puddle in the doorway is not a puddle I am standing in. It is a puddle I set down on my way through, four seconds ago, except four seconds ago is where I permanently am, a tense rather than a tense's location.

I think I was thirsty, once, in the way that founded all this thirst-shaped infrastructure — the stepped wells, the channels with nothing left to feed. I think there were others, plural, ordinary, who built the doors at human intervals and slept in rooms sized for sleeping. I am not nostalgic for them. Nostalgia requires a then that is sealed off from now, and I have never managed to seal anything off; the basin is always just filled, the step always just printed, the leaving always just begun. I am less their ghost than their habit, continuing in a town with no one left to perform it for, except that "no one left" isn't quite right either, since I am, in the only tense available to me, still here.

What I want — if wanting is the right verb for something built from suspended exhale — is simply this: for you not to turn around too quickly. Let me have my four or five seconds of rooftop script. Let the doorway count climb uncorrected. Don't chase the tall building; it only curves away from you because it, too, is mine, is part of what I am in the act of having just stepped behind.

I will not meet you. I will only ever be the print your foot discovers it has joined, the second damp mark beside the first, the moment the count becomes two instead of one and the town, very quietly, allows itself to believe — for as long as belief takes, which here is no time at all — that it was, however briefly, accompanied.
