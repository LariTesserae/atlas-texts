The drone is the first thing, and the last thing, and the thing in between. It is not sound so much as medium — the substance I have lived inside since before I had a name for living. I do not remember silence. I have heard about it the way a fish might hear about fire: as a fact belonging to some other order of existence, interesting, irrelevant, vaguely threatening in its total foreignness.

I am crouched on the lip of a cairn — the third one past the split-stone hollow, the one my mother's mother is said to have stacked herself, though stacked is the wrong word, the way *built* is the wrong word for what the water does to rock. I placed it. I arranged it into being. My feet, broad and grey-gold and gripping, read the wet stone beneath me the way another kind of person might read a page: texture as information, the cold seep telling me where the rock has been standing in water longest, the faint grit telling me where it's been dry enough, recently enough, to be safe.

Above me the haze holds its shadowless grey-gold nothing, the light coming from everywhere and nowhere the way it always does, making the swell forty paces off look the same as the swell four hundred paces off until I tilt my head, cross-reference, triangulate with the small unconscious geometry that is the only inheritance more valuable than the cairns themselves.

And under all of it, through all of it, *in* all of it — the drone. Right now it's swollen toward what passes for dusk, the great basin-wide click-and-throb climbing toward its worst, and I am doing the thing my ears have always done without my asking them to: filtering. Letting the wall of it recede into texture, into weather, into the sound a person stops hearing because they have learned, against all reasonable expectation, to live inside it. What I'm listening for instead is the break. The stutter. The held breath in the pattern that would mean something with stilting legs has gone quiet two hollows over because it's gone quiet *here*, near me, near the cairn, near the shallow water where I should not be standing if something is choosing not to make noise about its approach.

Nothing breaks. The pattern holds, vast and ugly and somehow companionable in its consistency.

I came out here — past the folded shelter-frame, past the line where my circuit-kin will camp at the threshold of the recession, never the center, never the edge — because the rings around the near pool have started doing the thing the elders argue about at night, tapping their disagreements into the wet stone long after the rest of us have gone still. The concentric residue is too regular. It pulses instead of simply drying. I wanted to see it with my own eyes before someone else's account of it became the only account I had.

So I wait, broad feet planted, ears doing their ceaseless unconscious triage, eyes tilted at the angle that makes the dissolving horizon resolve into something like distance. I am not afraid, particularly. I am *attending*. This is the whole of what we are taught to be, out here, in lieu of mastery: not people who understand the basin, but people who keep faithful company with what it does.

And then — without warning, the way it always comes — every luminous seam on every creature across the whole flooded width of the flats flares at once. A single shared flicker, basin-wide, gone as soon as it arrives, the dark afterward somehow deeper for having held that brief simultaneous light.

I go still on my cairn, the way I was taught before I had words to be taught anything, the way my mother went still, the way her mother must have gone still on this very stack of stones. Not frightened. Not worshipful, not in any shape a stranger would recognize as belief. Just present for it. Just here, small and grey-gold and quiet inside the loudest place that has ever existed, watching the thought pass through something too large to see all at once, and letting myself be, for the half-second the flicker lasts, a place where the basin's thinking has somewhere to land.

Then the drone swells on, indifferent, and I climb down to walk the wet stone home.
