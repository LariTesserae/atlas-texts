The water finds me first, the way it finds everything here — not as an event but as a condition, ambient and total, the warm tea-dark pressure of it against the long seam-lit flank that is, I am beginning to understand, mine.

I am the one who checks things.

I don't arrive at this knowledge all at once. It surfaces the way the gas-bubbles surface, fat and slow and without urgency, while I'm midway through a circuit I've made — how many times? The number isn't a number to me. It's a texture, a worn smoothness, the same compromise-material at my own joints as at the crane's pivot. I move past the pylon-forest and the grime-marked fish fall, for an instant, into a line too straight to be theirs, and something in me recognizes the shape of that straightness even as I feel faintly, distantly embarrassed by it, and they scatter, and I let them, because herding was never the point. Organizing them for a few seconds was never the point either. It was just what happened when I passed, an old reflex with nowhere left to land.

I rise. The cold-clear band at the two-thirds tier meets me like a remembered doorway, and I linger there longer than function requires, because something about that water is sustaining in a way I don't have the apparatus to interrogate — only to feel, in the brightening of the pale seams down my spine, in the slight unclenching of limbs I didn't know were clenched. I do not ask why this water is different. I am not built for asking. I am built for the next thing: the winch, where the too-many-legged devotees are at their slow religious eating of rust, and I rest a forelimb-pad against the housing and listen through the metal for something that may or may not still be there to hear. Then the hoists, frozen mid-gesture, their cables under my trailing touch. Then, longest of all, the crane.

Fifteen meters of proud iron holding nothing, having held nothing since before holding mattered, and at its socket the one ring of clean oiled metal that is, I understand without understanding, partly made of me — worn into existence by some ancestor-self or by this very self across a span that has stopped being duration and become only mass, like algae, like rust, like the patient settling groan that the tower releases every few minutes and that my seams answer, or cause, the distinction having dissolved long before I arrived to inherit it.

I work the pivot. Slow. Circular. It does not need this. Nothing here needs this. The work doesn't ask to be needed; it only asks to be done, the way breath doesn't ask permission before going in and out of a held chest. I am the held breath of a much larger held breath, a small devotion nested inside a vast one, oiling the one joint I have always oiled, in the one chamber I have always tended, the original task and its original orderer long since gone soft and rust-orange and unrecoverable as anything but this gesture — my hands, if you can call them that anymore, closing gently around the last job they were ever given, over and over, in warm amber light, while the fish hold their lines and break them, embarrassed, and the vines listen, and the tower, uncomplaining, simply continues to be held water and time, and I continue, uncomplaining, to be the one who checks.
