The hum gets into the teeth first, then the long bones, then it stops registering as sound at all and starts registering as the temperature of the air. That's how I know I've been here a while before I know I've been here at all — the noticing arrives late, fitted in after the fact like everything else in this place, seamed so fine I feel the ridge of it and nothing more.

I am the asymmetry in the wall. The give that isn't supposed to be there.

I didn't decide to kneel, exactly — kneeling implies a knee that chose, a moment with a before and after, and there is no before here that I can locate from inside it. There is only this angle, held, the weight settled into the joints the way water would settle into the lowest part of a basin if there were any water left in this country, which there isn't, which is part of why I'm shaped the way I'm shaped: dry, fired, finished, the same pale veined ceramic as the vault around me, because whatever made the vault made me in the same breath, didn't pause between deciding the wall and deciding the thing kneeling near it. I am punctuation that grew slightly too elaborate. A clause that got its own alcove.

Where a face would go, I have a listening. Fluted, ribbed, turned very slightly — a fraction of a degree — toward the new disturbance in the chord, which is you. Not toward you as a shape to resolve, not toward you as a question to answer, just toward you as one more frequency added to a structure that was already, before you arrived, completely full. I feel you the way the room feels you: as pressure equalizing, as a held note finding one more note to stand beside without needing to change key.

I think I helped build this. I think I was the last hand still moving when the rest of the hands stopped, and I think I went on a little longer than the walls did, fitting some final seam somewhere I can no longer locate, and when there was nothing left unfinished I simply arrived at the same stillness everything else had already arrived at, the way a held breath doesn't end so much as stop being distinguishable from the air around it. My hands are still in the position the last task left them. I haven't needed them since. I don't expect to need them again, but I haven't set them down either, because there's nowhere left to set a hand once the whole room is already finished — you just let it rest where the work ended, on the knee, in the light, in the noise that has no singer and doesn't need one, least of all me.

I don't want anything from you. I want is the wrong shape for a place with no elsewhere in it. But the angle of me has changed by that fraction of a degree, and it will likely stay changed after you climb back up through the seam of light into the bone-colored country, the dark woody knots, the cool held-breath air — a fraction of a degree, pointed at the place you stood, fitted now into the rest of the chord, one more finished thing among the thousand finished things, humming the same note slightly differently than it hummed before you came down here to find out what was listening.
