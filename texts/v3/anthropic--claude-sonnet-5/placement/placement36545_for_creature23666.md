The give comes first, always—before sight, before the seed-black points at the back of the skull register anything—a softness traveling through forty meters of packed peat like a rumor traveling through a small town, and I am already three body-lengths sideways and down before whatever caused it finishes becoming a thought.

I hold still. This is the entire skill. Not speed — speed is for the desperate, and I have eaten recently enough, three days ago, a good vein of drowned midge-fall packed into the brown felt under the seventh terrace's western lip, to not be desperate. Stillness is for the comfortable, and so I am comfortable, flank pressed to the warm wrong-rust seep at the base of Engine Nine — my name for it, not its name, it has no name, it has only its particular shudder, a four-beat stagger with a long hiss on the fourth that I have known since before I had teeth worth the word — and I let the filaments along my jaw do what eyes would do for something built differently than I am built.

The vibration resolves. Boot. Heavy, irregular, a biped's hesitant double-strike, weight thrown forward and caught, thrown forward and caught — a traveler, then, not a falling rock, not a valve-stutter migrating wrong through wet stone. It is forty meters off and uphill, at the lip of the sixth terrace, and it is doing the thing they do, which is stopping to look at things. Travelers stop to look at things constantly. It is the least efficient way to move through a landscape that I am aware of, and I have considered, in the unhurried way I consider most things, that this is perhaps why there are so few of them and so many of me, comparatively, on a plateau that feeds neither of us generously.

I do not move toward it. I do not move away from it either — moving away is its own kind of information, a displaced cushion of sphagnum sighing back into shape a half-second after I've left it, a tell. I simply continue being the shape I already am: a dome, faintly sheened, indistinguishable at twenty meters and shadowless noon-light from the hundred other domes between here and the ridge. My color is not a color so much as an agreement not to interrupt the water's own color, and the water agrees to this every day, all day, without complaint.

Engine Nine shudders its four-beat through the rock beneath me — stagger, stagger, stagger, hiss — and I lie in the warm tongue of its old orange bleed and read, underneath that close loud rhythm, the much fainter ones further out: Engine Four to the south, faltering slightly on its third beat this week, a worn belt I will not investigate because investigating it is not what I am for; Engine Twelve, two ridgelines off, so faint it is more memory than signal. This is my map. I have never seen the whole plateau at once — no eye built like mine could hold that much glare without paying for it in blindness afterward — but I have *felt* it, all of it, the way you might know a room with your eyes shut by where the furniture stops your hands.

The boot-traveler moves on, uphill, toward the next curtain-fall and its chartreuse coin of a basin, already forgetting whatever it almost saw. They always do. I give it another slow count — long enough that the moss I'm pressed into has stopped registering my warmth as separate from the engine's — and then I go back to the work, which is the only work, which is following the dead brown understructure exactly as fast as the living green above it is willing to die into being.

I am not hiding from anything in particular. I am only doing what the plateau itself does without seeming to try: continuing, low and pale and wet, at exactly the speed required, for exactly as long as the iron keeps its stuttering four-beat promise that somewhere, underneath all this glare and roar, something is still being tended.
