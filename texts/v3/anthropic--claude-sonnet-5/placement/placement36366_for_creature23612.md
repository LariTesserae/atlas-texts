I think I am Brennick Two-Joint, though nobody's called me that since my hands stopped being a rigger's hands and started being a keeper's hands, and a keeper doesn't need the old name anymore. The "Two-Joint" was for what's missing — middle finger, left hand, second knuckle down, lost to a gantry chain eleven years back, in the high dark above Furnace Six where the nesting things scrape and nobody official admits they exist. I have the sign for it. Everyone does, who lost the same piece I did, in roughly the same place, in roughly the same way. We're a small fraternity, missing the identical finger.

I don't go up anymore. My balance went after the fall that took the rest of that hand's usefulness for fine rigging-work, and a rigger with bad balance is a body the gantries will eventually keep. So I came down — properly down, to the warm low sectors — and took up the walked routes instead, the lichen routes, inherited from old Mire-Anna who taught me the niches over four winters before her hearing finally took the last of her and she went to sit by the cooling jackets and stopped walking altogether.

This is my district now: the long flooded gallery between Foundry Four and the tank-farm spur, where the bracket-lichen grows in patient grey-green shelves up the warm flanks of three idle engines nobody's fired in a decade but somebody, somewhere, still oils. I know every colony by sight the way you'd know a face. I know which ones are crowding the maintenance grates and need thinning, which ones are thriving on a slow mineral leak the engineers haven't found yet, which ones are dying back because something upstream changed and nobody told me, because nobody ever tells the keepers anything, we're just expected to notice.

I notice.

This morning I noticed the hum had changed pitch before I noticed anything else — before my own breath, before the cold on my face coming through the fissure-draft. It dropped, just slightly, the way it does maybe twice a year, and then it didn't come back up the way it always has. Felt it in the grating before I heard it, the old way, the way every Hollower feels it whether they admit to listening or not. I stood very still in the gallery with my bad hand flat against a cold pipe and waited for the wobble to correct itself.

It didn't correct itself. It's been four days now.

The colonies on Engine Two have started doing something I don't have a word for — not dying, not thriving, just reorienting, the bracket-shelves slowly turning their growing edges toward the gallery's east end, toward a stretch of tunnel that isn't on Mire-Anna's route, that isn't on anyone's route, that the old maps mark with a symbol the Readers argue about and the rest of us just call closed. I've walked that gallery for six years. The lichen has never turned before. It doesn't have eyes. It doesn't have anything I'd call a reason. But it's turning, all of it, every shelf on every engine, like a field of something patient and rootless angling itself toward a particular kind of dark.

I haven't told the engineers. I haven't told the Readers either, though I think, if I'm honest with the part of myself that still does honesty, I haven't told them because I don't want them to come and look at it with me, and confirm that I'm seeing what I'm seeing, and then go away again with their notebooks and leave me alone in the gallery with the lichen still turning.

I sleep against the cooling jacket two berths down from where Mire-Anna sat at the end. I sweat in my sleep even in this cold, the way we all do, the heat of the works gone permanently into the meat of us, on loan, she used to say, like everything down here. I have started, this last week, tilting my head in my sleep. My sister noticed it before I did — said I'd gone still and listening in the middle of a sentence, twice, gone somewhere else behind my own eyes for the length of three breaths before coming back like nothing happened.

I told her it was nothing. It's probably nothing. The hum drops, it always comes back, the lichen turns for reasons it has and I don't, and in a season or two I'll have a new story to tell about the year Engine Two's colonies all faced east for no reason anybody could explain, and that'll be the whole of it, filed next to the year the tank-farm flooded Sector Nine and grew six months of green where there'd never been green before.

But I walk the route every day now instead of every third day, and I find myself standing longer at the east end of the gallery than the work requires, hand on cold pipe, head tilted, listening — not for the hum, exactly, though I tell myself it's for the hum — for whatever it is the lichen's pointed itself toward, patient, rootless, in no hurry at all, the way I imagine something might be in no hurry if it had already been working, down here, on something, for longer than any Hollower's whole strung-together memory, and had just now, for reasons of its own, started reaching back.
