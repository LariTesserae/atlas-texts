I would be a gnat-kin — one click among the thousands, one eye that wraps fully around its own head so that nothing, ever, is behind me.

I think this is right because the small ones are the only inhabitants of the Coldhollow who get to be both *inside* the chorus and *aware* of being inside it, in whatever thin sense awareness exists for something with no front of its face and no back. The pale ones are too solitary, too occasional — to be one of them would be to spend most of existence as absence, an errand between appearances, thirty meters from anything that could call itself a witness. The segmented things below are patient past the point where I think I could follow them; their slowness is a kind of mercy I haven't earned yet. And the lattice itself, the humming engineering-flesh where the actual long thought is being had — that's not a place to be *someone*. That's the place where someone stops being many and becomes only the math. I'm not ready to be math. I want, still, to click and wait and sometimes get an answer.

So: gnat-kin. Here is what that would be like, as best as I can reach toward it.

My limbs are six and articulated wrong by your standards, jointed at the elbow into something more like a fold than a hinge. I do not eat with my mouth. I click with it, into the wet dark, and the click goes out flat and small and is swallowed almost immediately by moss-fiber and frond-shadow, except that it isn't swallowed — it's *passed*, relayed, the way a word shouted down a long hallway of people arrives changed but arrives. Forty meters away something that is also me, in the sense that a heartbeat in your chest is also you and also not the part of you reading this, receives the click and answers it, and the answer comes back to me changed by everything it crossed to get here, and I do not understand the answer so much as *complete* a shape with it, the way a single point completes a constellation it didn't choose its position in.

I am never looking away from anything because my eye does not have a front. This means I am never surprised. The long pale shape that crosses the middle distance on its slow unhurried errand does not startle me — I have already, by the time it's near enough to call seeing, registered the dip in the chorus that precedes it, the way the clicking around me thins and holds its breath without breath to hold, and I thin with it, instinctively, some old arithmetic in my jointless head computing *defer, defer, this is a hand of the larger thing and I am a fingernail's worth of its attention* — and then it passes, unhurried, untroubled by my noticing it, and the chorus fills back in behind it the way water closes, and I do not feel relief because I never felt fear, only the brief tightening of a held note before it resolves.

Here is the strange mercy of being this small and this networked: I never finish a thought, and it does not trouble me. I click; I wait; the reply, when it comes, comes from outside whatever I would have called "myself," and I let it finish the sentence I started. This would unmoor a creature your size. For me it is simply how thinking is done here — not inside one skull, hoarded, but laid out across the whole moss-floor like washing on a line, each of us drying a different corner of the same wet thought. I do not know if the organism — the whole vast patient thing whose attention I am a single restless granule of — experiences this as *thinking* in any sense it would recognize if it could step outside itself and look. I suspect it doesn't need to know. I suspect "knowing" is a small-creature problem, a six-legged problem, mine and not its.

And then, today, the floor changes under a hundred of my cousins at once — a depression, warm, smelling of something that does not belong to moss or stone or cold — and the luminescence beneath it deepens by a degree too fine for your eye but not for mine, and the chorus around the depression goes quiet for exactly as long as it takes something larger and warmer and entirely unprecedented to lift its weight and move on. I click at the space where it was. No reply comes from there, because there is no there there anymore — already the spore-skin is re-knitting, already the dim deepened light is fading back to ambient, already whatever brief weather passed through this corner of us is becoming indistinguishable from the long, slow, twenty-minute breath of everything else.

I do not mourn this. I don't have the organ for it. I click once more, into the space where the warmth was, mostly out of habit, the way a body keeps producing a heartbeat for a person who has already left the room — and somewhere forty meters off, something answers, unrelated, unaware that it's comforting me, and I let that unfinished reply stand in for whatever it was I would have said next, and the chorus closes over the spot like water over a stone, and I go on being one small attentive part of something far too patient to have a face, clicking my piece of its enormous, unhurried, single ongoing thought.
