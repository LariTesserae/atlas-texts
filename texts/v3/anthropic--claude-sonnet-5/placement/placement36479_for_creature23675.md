I think I'd be somewhere in the middle distance between two oases — not in a town proper, not in true dead-gallery silence, but in that ambiguous stretch the Soaked cross in teams and call commute rather than home. A flooded gallery, tier eleven or twelve down from whatever passes for the surface, where a stairwell shaft turns the runoff from somewhere above into a waterfall loud enough to drown thought, and the light is the orange of one working cage-bulb every thirty meters, the rest blue dark.

And I think I would be a Listener — not yet old, not yet ruined in the knees the way the real elders are, but apprenticing into it, because I was a poor enough Diver that I kept my joints, and quick enough at reading the structure's moods that the actual Listeners started bringing me along instead of leaving me on the catwalk. So: still young enough to swim the corridors when required, but already spending more hours pressed flat against a bulkhead with my palm on cold rivets than out in open water.

What that means, concretely: I am the one who stops the group mid-crossing, lifts a hand, and says nothing, because the *something* I just felt through my palm — a clang two tiers off, a settling, a pressure-change that came up through the steel before it came through the air — needs a second to resolve into a category. Building, or thing-on-the-building. I have the dialect of taps in my back teeth before I have most words; I learned *wait* and *something's coming* before I learned my own name spelled out, because the second one mattered more, more often.

I'd carry: a weighted line coiled at the hip, gone soft and grey with handling; a tool roll for the small heresies of patching a pump that nobody alive has the schematic for, only the inherited scar tissue of what worked last time; and a particular relationship to silence that outsiders would find unbearable and I would find merely informative — true quiet, in the Works, is not peace, it's a question, and my whole training is learning to answer it correctly before it answers itself on me.

Home, such as it is, is a turbine hall three days' careful travel from where I'm imagining myself standing now — sodium light, a few hundred people, the bone-deep thrum of something that still runs felt through the soles of my feet whenever I'm there, which I would only fully notice by its absence, the way you notice a held breath only once you let it go. I'd be coming back to it. Hands translucent and soft at the fingertips from decades — no, not decades yet, but on their way there — of wet work. Night vision good. Patience for sunlight, if I ever saw it, probably bad.

And the thing I would be doing, in this specific imagined moment: crouched on a rusted spillway lip half-drowned in mist, palm flat against a bulkhead, waiting for a second sound to tell me whether the first one was the continent settling its own impossible weight, or something else entirely deciding, just this once, to come looking for a face.
