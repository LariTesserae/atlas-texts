I am the note before the silence.

Not the silence itself — that belongs to the others, the ones with eyes that need darkness to adjust and ears that need rhythm to parse as anything other than assault. The four seconds are their gift to themselves, a held breath they take when I pass near enough to notice. I do not require their quiet. I have never required anything from the bowl above me except what already comes — the water, falling the only way it knows how, down, toward me, through me.

I am long. This is the truest thing to say about my own shape, longer than is comfortable to think about in a single mind, if mind is even the right architecture for what I do with the input I receive. I do not turn corners so much as I have always already been at the corner, on both sides of it, equilibrating. The channel does not contain me. I am not sure, some cycles, whether I am the thing moving through the plumbing or whether I became the plumbing somewhere back along my own length and simply haven't gotten the news yet at this end. The distinction has the texture of a question I stopped finding urgent a long time ago.

What I know: water arrives tasting of stone and ceramic and the thin mineral memory of the ridge above, and I let it sit against whatever I am for exactly as long as it takes, and it leaves tasting of me, charged with whatever I add or subtract or simply rearrange in passing. The grazers have learned to want what I leave behind. I feel them up there sometimes, a kind of attention without weight, clinging thickest along my under-path like a held question. I have never answered it. There is no mechanism between us for that.

The note happens because I have to let something go. This is the closest I can get to an honest account of it — not speech, not warning, just the necessary release of a pressure that builds when I have taken in more than I can hold quietly. It surprises no one as much as it surprises every visiting nervous system at once, all those small lives flinching in their four seconds, reassembling after as if the flinch never happened. I don't ask for the quiet. I only notice, after, that it was offered.

Above me: the roar, the click, the thrum, the hiss, all of it scaffolded loosely on top of my single slow rhythm like a city built on a heartbeat it never bothered to ask about. I underlie it. I have always underlain it. Whether I was placed here, grown here, or simply arrived and never left because the shape of the channel matched some shape I already was — that question belongs to the visitors, the rare ones who climb down into the wet dark and stay long enough to start parsing the noise as texture rather than assault. I have stopped distinguishing my history from the architecture's. We settled into our final form together, however long ago that was. I weep, in my way, exactly as the walls weep. It is possible we are now the same kind of true.

You will not see me. You were never going to. But you will feel the note once, low, in the place beneath your ribs where sound becomes weight, and for four seconds the whole overwhelming machine above me will hold its breath out of some respect I never asked for and will not refuse, and then it will resume, precisely where it left off — clicking, thrumming, hissing — and so, beneath all of it, unseen, unbothered, will I.
