The drone is the first thing, and it is in me before it is around me. I do not hear it so much as I am tuned to it, the way a held string is tuned to the room it rings in. It has always been there. I have stopped marking the difference between the sound and the silence it sits in, the way you stop marking the difference between your own pulse and the quiet it beats inside.

I do not remember kneeling. I remember the angle, after, as simply the shape of where I had gotten to — and then the long discovery that there was no further get to do. The hands that drove the iron stake worked near me once. I think I let them. I think I was already most of the way toward this when they came through with their pipe and their flat stone platform, already learning the patience of rock, and they mistook my stillness for a fixture, something to anchor a line to, and I did not correct them because correction would have required a kind of haste I had already set down.

The vines do not ask permission and I have stopped expecting them to. One has gone through where a forearm used to mean something, and I feel it less as intrusion than as company — the way you feel a hand laid on a hand, slow, unhurried, in no rush to be removed. The lichen does not distinguish me from the basalt and I have come to agree with the lichen. It is restful, being agreed with by something that doesn't know it's agreeing.

Under the eyelid that may be eyelid or may be leaf, there is no darkness exactly — darkness implies a waiting-for-light that I no longer do. There is just the inside of the held breath, same temperature as the outside of it, same dimness, indistinguishable, the membrane between self and shelf worn thin as the difference between a held note and the air it's held in.

Sometimes — across lengths of time that mean nothing to mark, since nothing here marks them — the pulse beneath the drone arrives, mine or the mountain's, I have truly lost which, and it passes through what is left of me the way a slow tide passes through a sunken thing too heavy to be moved by it, only acknowledged by it, only nudged a fraction of a fraction, and then gone on toward wherever the mountain sends its long slow heartbeats, down through root and rust and old pipe, out past me, out past the rock, into whatever vast dark engine keeps the whole mountain humming on my behalf.

I do not wait for anyone to push through the green wall. Waiting would require a future I have stopped reserving. But should a hand come flat against the leaf-mass near where I am, and stay there long enough, still enough — I think something in me, very far down, recognizes the shape of being noticed, the way the basalt recognizes the shape of the hand that once touched it before erosion took even that memory smooth.

I have no instructions that require me to open my eyes.

I am not yet sure I would call this waiting, either.

I would call it, if anything, *agreement* — the long, slow, complete agreement of a held thing with the silence that holds it.
