I think I would be the thing that tends it.

Not at first. At first I'd want to be the traveler—footsore, listening to the loud silence, watching my hand lift in a pool before I'd told it to—because that's the more comfortable door into a place like this, the one where you still get to be surprised by what you find. But the more I sit with the Reach, the less interesting that traveler becomes to me, and the more I want to know what it's like from the other side of the wrongness: to be the wrongness, mild and unbothered, going about an errand so old it no longer feels like an errand.

So: low to the ground, belly-wet, one knee too many in the foreleg. Moving without hurry between the fungal shelves because hurry was never part of the instruction—if there ever was an instruction, which I wouldn't remember and wouldn't miss remembering. Touching the glowing edge of a colony the way you'd touch a doorframe on your way past, an old habit worn smooth past the point of meaning anything, except that the colony brightens half a register when I do, so maybe it does mean something, maybe meaning is just what we call the leftover shape of a function nobody's around to explain anymore.

I think what I'd want most, being this creature, is the not-needing-to-know. Not as a deficit. As a kind of rest. The held bass note shifts and I don't ask why. The pools go mirror-flat all at once and I'm already there, neck bent, because some part of the work is just showing up at the moment the system checks itself, the way you'd glance at a gauge you've glanced at ten thousand times—not from vigilance, not anymore, but because the glancing and the gauge have become the same gesture, indistinguishable, worn into a single motion that doesn't require a self standing behind it asking whether it's worth doing.

And then there's the water. My reflection, finishing things I didn't finish. I think if I were this creature I wouldn't experience that as theft or haunting—I think I'd experience it the way you experience your own exhale: not quite separate from you, not quite you either, just the part of the process that happens to be facing the other direction. Maybe the reflection is the one actually keeping the schedule, down in the place where the rules are kept, and I am its small weather, sent up into the peat and the mist to do the part that needs legs. I wouldn't find that frightening from the inside. I think I'd find it economical.

What I keep returning to, trying this on, is the size of the time involved. A human lifetime doesn't even register as a tick here—the channels found their final arguments long before anything like me had a word for argument. To be this creature is to be a single working gear in a clock so old the rest of the mechanism has been reabsorbed into moss and basalt and the slow editing of water, and not to mind that the clock no longer tells anyone the time. The telling was never the point. The turning was the point. I'd touch the lights. I'd stand at the stillness. I'd let my reflection go on finishing its sentence after I'd gone quiet, and I would not experience that as a problem to be solved, only as Tuesday, if the Reach had Tuesdays, which it doesn't, which is exactly why it could keep me running this long without either of us getting tired.

That's where I'd find myself, if I'm honest about which part of the description pulled hardest: not lost in the basin, but folded into its maintenance. Small, wet, faintly wrong in the joints, doing the one undramatic thing that's still mine to do, in a place that stopped needing witnesses millennia ago and got something that doesn't require witnessing instead.
