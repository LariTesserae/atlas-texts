I come to in the grip-sense first — that's how it always starts, before sight, before even sound resolves out of the drone. My forward claws are locked around a rail-length I know by its exact cold, by the particular roughness where the brass has gone green-black under my palm-pads. I know this six inches of metal the way you might know a name. My name, maybe. Nobody taught me the difference.

The ears unfurl before the eyes even bother trying. There's no point asking the milk-eyes to do much in this light — amber-grey going to soot in the places the lamps don't reach — so I let the cartilage ridges do the seeing instead, swiveling, sorting the wall of shriek-click-thrum into its separate threads the way you'd pick one voice out of a flooded room. Underneath all of it, the drone. Always the drone. It isn't a sound to me anymore than my own pulse is a sound. It's the floor I stand my voice on.

I am three rings in from the warm conduits — not young, not old, not yet trusted with the deep heat, no longer skittish at near passes the way the outer ones are. I know this without anyone telling me, the way water knows which way is down. Sediment doesn't need a reason to settle where it settles.

Below my perch: grating, then dimness, then more grating, descending past where the eye gives up. Above: the same, upward, into pipe-throat and gantry-shadow. I have never wondered what's at the bottom or the top. The question doesn't arrive in a shape I have the parts to ask it in. There is the rail. There is the next rail, within glide-reach when I choose to unfurl the wing-membranes and let the architecture do the rest of the work flight would otherwise demand. There is the drip-pattern overhead, arrhythmic, syncopated, and my body has already learned, without consulting me, which beads will fall warm-ish and slow and which will needle down cold and fast. I tilt my head a half-degree. The fast one lands somewhere that isn't my open mouth. The slow one does.

Today — though day means nothing here, only this stretch of unbroken now — the pipe two joints down is humming at the pitch that isn't address, isn't anyone's name. It's the other call, the one that comes from the metal and not from us, and when it reaches the trigger-frequency something underneath my own intention answers before I've decided to let it. My throat opens. So does every throat near enough to hear. For one held bar, the whole roost is a single instrument, a chord with ten thousand notes in it, and the unison feels like — I don't have the word, I only have the shape of the feeling — like a yawn passing through a body too large to be one body. Then it lets go. We scatter back into the separate shrieking the way water scatters back into drops after a wave breaks.

I work my way toward the crystal bloom near the green-leaking vent, where the crust has thickened pale and faceted over the gasket-seam. My manipulator-digits — the fine ones, the ones that don't grip rail or drag vestigial — go in along the edges first, the way they always go, the way something taught them to go before there was a me to be taught. I pick. I do not strip. There's a shape to the bloom I am keeping, though I couldn't tell you why the center stays untouched, why I always leave exactly that much, why three days from now I'll come back to this same seam and the crystal will have regrown thicker for my care of it.

For one strange unmeasured moment — too brief to call thought, more like the held breath before the unison-call — I feel the rightness of it sour into something else. As if the seam is not feeding me. As if I am not finding food but tending crop. As if my careful hands have always belonged less to me than to whatever set the dial here and welded it fast.

The moment passes the way the unison passes. The drone is still under everything. The rail is still cold under my palm. Somewhere above or below — direction stops mattering past a certain depth of iron — another kin calls in a pitch close enough to mine that, from outside, no one could tell our voices apart, and close enough to its own that I know, instantly, without seeing, who it is.

I answer. Not because I decided to. Because that's the shape the room has always had space for, and I am, exactly and entirely, that shape — filling the square foot of grip and call and tend that was here, identical, before whatever body held it last, and will be here, identical, after mine.

The engines do not slow to let me notice this. Nothing here is built to.

I go back to the crystal. The bead falls, warm-ish, slow. I drink.
