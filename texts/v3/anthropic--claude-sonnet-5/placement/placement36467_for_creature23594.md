The weight settles first — that's how I know I've arrived, before sight or sound assembles into anything I'd call a self. A barrel-heaviness through the shoulders, legs planted in something that gives slightly and then holds. Warm water at the height of my knees, unbothered by my standing in it. I am the standing-cattle, or one of them, and the water has been waiting for me longer than I have been waiting for anything.

The first thing I register, in this borrowed body, is how little urgency arrives with the senses. A bird lifts off my flank — I feel the small release of weight before I see the white blink of it against the glare — and the appropriate response, the only response, is to let an ear swivel toward the place the frogs are loudest. Not because anything demands tracking. Because that's what the ear is for, the way the horn is for scraping bark and not for war.

I lift my head. The horizon does the thing the description promised and refuses to be a horizon — it sits at the same height as me in every direction, so the whole shallow country reads at once, like a page already open rather than one I'd have to turn toward. Pale domes of gneiss. The rust-crusted laterite, sweating faintly where water threads up through old fractures. Tussocks in loose scatter, each with its allotted ground. A flat-crowned tree a hundred meters off holding its little kingdom of shade, and under it, not hurrying toward it, two calves and a cow who is mostly tail and patience.

I don't think, in this body, the way I'd think in another one. There's a call building somewhere under the sternum — I can feel it before I understand it's mine to make, a two-note pressure gathering the way a held breath gathers. When it goes out, low and chest-deep, it isn't a question and it isn't urgent. It's just a thread laid across a kilometer of shimmer, in case anyone wants to take up the other end. Something answers, eventually, from past the heat-shimmer over the drier flat — another bull, unseen, untroubled, returning the sound only slightly changed by the distance it crossed.

I understand, standing here with flies landing and being swatted at exactly the necessary speed, that nothing is going to happen today that requires me to be anything other than this — heavy, slow, half-submerged, grit-pale at the shoulder if I'm old enough, which I think, somehow, in the worn blunt feel of my own horn-tips, I am. The land spent hundreds of millions of years arguing itself down to this, this exact unhurried flatness, and gave up the argument. I was given the same gift on a shorter timescale: a body that already finished its own arguments, generations back, about speed and edges and fighting for ground. What's left is only the long management of standing in warm water, watching a horizon that keeps its distance, calling sometimes — for habit, for company, for the pleasure of a sound that travels further than I ever will — into air that never quite dries.
