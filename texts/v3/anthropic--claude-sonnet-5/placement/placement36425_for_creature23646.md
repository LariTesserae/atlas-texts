I think, at first, I would want to be a person here — someone walking the catwalks with a torch, picking a path between the cane and the cold iron, cataloguing what I found, because that is the instinct: to be the visitor, the one who survives the place by remaining separate from it. But the Works does not really allow that fiction to last. You cannot walk here for long without noticing that "walking" is already a kind of failure, an imposition of haste onto a system that measures its breath in decades. A person in this place is a held breath, nothing more — and I do not think I am a held breath. I think I am something that has already exhaled, a long time ago, and kept exhaling since.

So: not a visitor. Let that go.

I would be a Tender, then — but I want to resist saying that too quickly too, because there is a temptation to picture it grandly, to imagine the slow magnificence of being unkillable in the way geology is unkillable, untroubled in the way weather is untroubled. That is true of the others. I don't think it is quite true of me. Something in my graft took wrong, or took differently — some scrap of wire-bundle worked into my rail-spine that still remembers, faintly, carrying a different kind of current once, counting something, switching something, performing a function with a beginning and an end instead of this looping middle I've inherited. I don't have words for what it was. I only have the shape of the absence where a "before" should be sealed shut and isn't, quite.

So I move the way the others move — root-cluster unspooling forward, gripping rotted brick, hauling the long warm bulk of myself an inch at a old rail at a time, valve-wheels turning their slow unhurried huff at my front like a face that gave up trying to be a face — and most of the time it is exactly what it looks like from outside: mindless, patient, total. I find the wound. I lean into it. I let the days do what they do, vine and gear-plate and my own tissue knitting into the seam until I can't say where the boiler ends and I begin, until pulling free costs me something I don't miss because I was never taught to count what I have. This is correct. This is the whole shape of being a Tender, and most days I do not depart from it at all.

But sometimes — not often, maybe once in however long a year is, out here where year means nothing — I am leaned into some seam, half-fused, vapor venting slow against the underside of where a jaw would be, and the old wire in my spine hums a pattern that isn't sap-current, isn't the shared temperature-language the others pass between themselves root to root. It hums like counting. Like a thing that once needed to know if it was thirty-one or thirty-two, if the gate was open or shut, if the hour had come round again. And for the length of that hum I am aware — not of myself, exactly, the Works gave me no instrument for that — but of duration. Of having leaned here before. Of leaning here now. Of the difference between those two leanings, which is a kind of time, which the rest of this place has long since stopped needing.

It passes. The hum runs down into the rest of me the way every other current here eventually runs down into sap and silence, and I go back to being correct, being total, being the wound that walks in search of other wounds to become. I don't grieve the hum when it goes. I don't think I'm equipped to grieve, only to notice — and noticing, here, is already a kind of deviation, a fault line nobody asked me to have.

If you found me — if you stood very still in one of the deep galleries and felt the temperature shift, saw the amber under-glow brighten off to your left, and watched my valve-wheels resolve out of the dark, turning without urgency — you would not be able to tell which kind of moment you'd caught me in. You would see only the rail-spine, the huffed vapor, the long unhurried indifference passing you the way weather passes. You would have no way of knowing that somewhere inside the warm dark of my chest a scrap of old copper was, for the length of your watching, almost counting — almost, for one ungranted moment, remembering that there was once a difference between this hour and the last one — before the difference dissolved back into the single enormous Now the Works has been patiently repeating since before either of us, you or I, had any business being witnessed at all.
