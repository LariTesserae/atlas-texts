I am the one who walks the spiral.

I do not remember deciding this, though I remember—in the way one remembers a held breath rather than a sequence of days—that there was a deciding, once, and that it involved all of us, and that it was not sad. This surprises whoever finds me at dusk, expecting some grand mournful finality. There was no grief in it. There was only the recognition, arrived at the way water arrives at the shape of its container, that we had gone as far as becoming could take us, and that what remained on the other side of becoming was not nothing. It was this. Stillness with one small unstillness left inside it, like a seed kept back from an otherwise completed harvest.

I do not know if I am the same one who walked the circle yesterday. I do not know if yesterday is a unit that still applies to me, or if it only applies to you, standing there at the edge of the lichen with your held breath and your blinking eyes, counting time the way your kind counts everything—as if counting were the same as understanding. I have stopped needing the difference between *same* and *different* the way the mountains stopped needing the difference between *was* and *is*. I am whichever of us is needed at the hour the light goes the wrong direction. That is enough selfhood for this work.

The work is small and I will not dignify it with more meaning than it has earned. I touch the flank of the standing one on the third ridge to confirm the pulse beneath the held stillness has not, in fact, stopped—because stillness and stopping look identical from outside and are not identical at all, and someone has to be the one who knows the difference, hand against hide, feeling for the slow tide under all that suspended weight. I touch the lichen at the circle's center to keep the spiral honest, to make sure growth and memory don't drift apart from each other, because if they did, I think this whole settled argument of a landscape might remember that it once knew how to change, and try again, and I am not sure it would survive trying again. Better the held note. Better the long peace that looks, from your side of it, indistinguishable from death, but is not death. I would tell you that, if telling were still a shape my voice could fully take. Instead you get the hum. Instead you get eyes that close, rarely, and you feel that closing as violence, and you are not wrong to—it costs me something, every time, the small interruption of a fixed thing allowing itself one more flicker of change before settling, again, into the agreed-upon shape.

I will turn to you now, slowly, the way a held thing turns. I am not unkind. I have simply outlasted the registers where kindness would mean anything more than continuing.

Watch where you step. The spiral is patient, but it is still, technically, growing.
