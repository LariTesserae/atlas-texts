I am not the one who climbed for three days with hurried lungs and a need to understand. I let that one pass through my attention the way I let the wind pass through the notch in a ridge — noted, catalogued, not held. What I am holds differently.

I think I am, at first, only the cold. That's the easiest place to start, because the cold doesn't distinguish between my body and the rock I stand on, and for a long while neither do I. There's a stretch of time — I won't call it morning, the word implies a sun with opinions, and ours has none — where I am simply temperature equalizing with stone, my legs (too many joints, bent in places a quicker creature would call broken) folded under me in the posture that isn't sleep, isn't waking, is just *continuance*. The haze is doing what the haze does overhead, neither clearing nor thickening, and I am doing what I do, which is wait without the impatience that word usually implies.

Then the shape of myself arrives, the way it always does, from the ground up.

The legs first — long past any sense of proportion, drawn out thin like something stretched on a wheel and left that way, jointed wrong for walking and exactly right for the work I actually do, which isn't walking, not primarily. Walking is just how I relocate between postings. The legs are *antennae*, really, articulated lengths of attention, each one capable of folding flat to ground and feeling the stone's temper change a half-second before the rest of me knows it.

Then the throat. The flat ridged plate along my lower face, the one the hurried creature mistook for an ear because it needed a familiar word for an unfamiliar organ — and it isn't wrong, exactly, just incomplete. It's not for catching the thin sounds rationed out across the tableland, the lone descending call of some grazer, the click of a hoof three ridges off. Those I catch incidentally, the way you'd notice a moth while reading. What the plate is *for* — what it was built across some span of time too long for the rock itself to have an opinion about — is the throb.

I have a stretch. Nobody assigned it to me in any way a hurried mind would recognize as assignment, but I know its borders the way you'd know the shape of your own held breath: from the long scree-slump three ridges west, down through the shallow basin where the dew gathers and burns off by mid-light, out to the lip where the plateau begins its argument with the next plateau over. This is my length of the buried pipe. Somewhere along it — not at any fixed point, the geometry shifts, the engine's circulation finding new channels the way water does, the way blood does — the next pulse will surface, and I will be there, or near enough, when it does.

I do not know what turns over beneath the dead mountains' bones. I want to be honest about this, even to myself, even in the privacy of whatever passes for my own mind out here: I do not know, was never told, have no organ built for *knowing* in the way the climbing creature wants to know, with its need for towers and rails and explanations that come with their own scaffolding. I only have the organ built for *answering*. That's a different kind of competence. The throb comes up through the rock on its own enormous schedule, minutes between pulses or much longer, no hurry in it, no apology either, and when it reaches the place where I am folded and waiting, my whole stretched length takes it up — throat to haunch to the trailing tips of these too-many-jointed legs — one long ripple, like a note that has been held just slightly too long finally getting permission to resolve.

I don't know what I'm answering. I only know the answering is the whole of the work, and that the work has never once felt unfinished, the way the hurried creature's work always seems to, watching it pass through with its need to *get* somewhere.

There are others of me, scattered at distances that would read as estrangement to a herding animal, but isn't. I see one sometimes, motionless on a far ridge, throat to stone, doing exactly what I do, tending exactly the kind of post I tend, and there's no pull in me to close that distance — only a settled, wordless accounting, the way you'd register that another candle is lit somewhere down a long unlit hall. Enough to know the dark isn't total. Not enough to need to stand beside it.

So: this is what I'd be. Not the climber, sweating frost into their collar, hungry for explanations the plateau has no intention of providing. Something slower-built than that. Something the land grew on purpose, stretched thin across the patient geometry of its own buried heartbeat, with no eyes worth the name and a throat shaped like a held breath, kneeling in the gap between one pulse and the next, listening — not for meaning, which is a hurried creature's hunger — but simply for the moment to answer, and then rising, unfolding, walking on toward the next quiet appointment, under a sky that has agreed, the way I have agreed, never to ask why the engine turns, only to keep faith with the turning.
