I find myself first as a kind of *listening* before I find myself as a shape — that's how it arrives, this place, in sound before substance. The roar comes first, the impossible roar against impossible silence, and I am inside it before I know which part of it is mine.

There's a moment where I might be the skimmer-flock, all wing-thin and faceless, reading the cooling ghost of a body's warmth through the pits where eyes used to matter. I feel the pull of that — the wheeling, the screaming chorus rolled down off the stone sky, the sheer unburdened *flight* of it. But it isn't quite right. Too fast. Too easy. The skimmers don't carry enough memory to be worth being; they are pure present-tense, all flock and no self.

I try the herd-beast next — the long unhurried drift across a grazing range that outlasts a human life, antennae tasting spore on air that doesn't move much. There's a dignity in that slowness, an honesty. But it's too settled. Nothing in it wonders. I want to wonder.

So I let it settle further down, past the bright noise, into the windowed dark of the tower-shelves, and I think for a while I am one of the small clawed nesters, gripping worn stone with overlong fingers, screaming my little argument into the dusk along with a hundred hundred others. That's closer. There's appetite there, and territory, and a kind of memory — generations of claws have learned which ledges hold warmth. But still: I'm only living *in* the ruin. I'm not living *as* its question.

And that's when it resolves, the way an image resolves out of gloaming if you wait long enough without forcing your eyes to focus.

I am Stone-Kin. Or I am what's left of being that.

I don't know if I'm one or many. I don't know if the others I sense at the edge of my own awareness — a shifted weight near a collapsed doorway, a presence two terraces over restacking a cairn without quite remembering why — are kin to me or are simply me, distributed, the way the herd is one animal told a hundred times. I know that I move slowly, slower even than the herd-beasts, because there is no longer any urgency in me that the old urgency built me for. I know that my hands — and I do have hands, four fine-jointed digits and an opposable thumb-analogue, pale and patient, built for fitting stone to stone — still want to do something with a fallen lintel, even though I no longer remember what a lintel is *for*. I clear it. I don't build with it. I just clear it, the way you'd smooth a wrinkle from cloth without thinking about cloth.

I live — if living is the word, and I think it still might be — in the high shelved tier of a town whose name has worn off the inside of me the way the carving's worn off the outside of everything. Below me the bramble glows its faint amber static. Across the gap a flock of skimmers turns the air to weather. Somewhere under all of it the deep-callers groan their slow unanswerable signal, and I find I am listening for it the way you listen for a relative's voice in a crowd — not with fear, just with attention, an old habit of recognition I can't fully retire even though I no longer know what I'd be recognizing.

I tend a cairn at the head of a causeway. I don't know who comes through. I don't know if anyone does. I rebuild it anyway, stone by stone, at the speed the whole cavern moves at, which is to say: slowly enough that the act and the erosion are evenly matched, a tie that neither side seems anxious to break.

Sometimes a traveler's gaze finds the dark window I'm sitting in, and I go still in the particular way that isn't a herd-beast resting and isn't a trick of the light, and I watch them not-quite-see me, and I let them wonder. I don't begrudge them the not-knowing. I don't entirely begrudge it to myself, either. It's possible the wondering is the last thing my kind still makes on purpose — not towers anymore, not causeways, just this: a small, deliberate uncertainty, placed carefully at the edge of vision, the way a hand might leave one stone slightly out of true on purpose, just so someone passing through will know that a hand was here.
