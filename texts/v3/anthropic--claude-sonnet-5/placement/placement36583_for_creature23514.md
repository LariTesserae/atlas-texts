The fog here has a temperature, and I have learned to read it the way the smaller things read light.

Right now I am draped along a fissure where a pipe-vein runs close beneath the rock — close enough that the warmth comes up through forty meters of basalt as a kind of pulse, a font of slow heat, the pistons' rhythm translated into something my gill-lines can drink the way a leaf drinks sun. I have been here — I want to say *months*, but the word implies a calendar, and I have outlived calendars. Time, for me, is not divided into days. It is divided into the rhythms underneath: whether the vein is warming or cooling, whether some far seam of the engine has shifted in a way that means I should drag myself, knuckle over knuckle, three shelves down to a richer exhalation.

I have no eyes. I have never missed them. What I have instead is better suited to a mountain that doesn't bother to light itself: forty meters of stone between me and the turning machinery, and through that stone, clear as a voice, the whole history of what the engine is doing. Right now it says: *steady, slow, undisturbed, a vein running its old patient temperature, the way it has run since before there was a me to notice it.*

Lying here, I am the same shape as the rock. The lichen does not distinguish between my hide and the basalt's hide, and so the grazers on this tier — the pale long-limbed ones, eyes like wet stones with nothing behind them — pass within twenty meters of me without slowing their crop of the glow-moss. I let them. I am not hungry. Hunger, when it comes, will come the way weather comes, a slow front moving in over what might be weeks, and I will know it the way I know everything — as a change in pressure, an emptiness translated into intent.

I do not think, the way the small quick-blooded things must think, in sentences, in *whys*. I have a single, wordless certainty, worn smooth the way the basalt has been worn smooth: that there is warmth, and I have found it, and the correct answer to having found it is to stay. Somewhere below me, in a tier I will never see and do not need to see, another vein is running warmer than usual, and a turbine I have never laid eyes on has, in its dreaming rotation, very slightly changed its pitch. I feel it as a tremor that means nothing urgent, only *interesting*, only *worth remembering*, filed away in whatever I have instead of memory — a long accumulating sense of how this mountain breathes, built out of however many centuries I have lain pressed to its skin.

Eventually I will exhale. It happens without my deciding it, the way a held breath releases itself when the body simply runs out of reasons to keep holding. The note will go out through the rock in every direction at once, and on this tier and the one below and the one above, every grazing thing will stop chewing, every too-jointed climber will freeze on its cliff-path, and for one minute the entire shelf will hold itself as still as I am holding myself now — not from fear, exactly, but from the old animal arithmetic that says: *something large just announced itself, and you are smaller than it, and stillness costs nothing.*

I do not mind being the gap in the sound. I do not mind having no name except the absence I leave in others' noise. I have the warmth, and the slow turning beneath me, and an amount of time ahead that doesn't compress into a number — and for now, that is the whole of what I am: a fold in the mountain that has decided, with my whole weight, to stay folded.
