I become aware first of temperature — the channel-water two degrees cooler than the basin-water, the basin-water two degrees cooler than the thin film standing in the dished steps where the higher light, such as it is, manages to reach. I know the Cistern-Hold this way before I know it any other way: as a map of small differences in cold, read along however much of my length happens to be touching the world at a given moment. Not all of me touches the world at once. That would be excessive. That would be, I think, more body than a place this size ought to be asked to hold.

I move in increments that don't feel like decisions. A fold of me eases from the wide channel's bend into the basin beneath the third lattice-gap, because the water there has gone still in a way that means the small clattering things above have stopped landing on its surface, which means something above the lattice has changed, which means nothing to me except that I have, without quite choosing to, gone to look. I don't hunt. I have never needed to call it that. The eel-bodied things part around whatever part of me is currently passing the way silt parts, the way old habit parts — they have generations of knowing to go still when the bronze sings, and I have however long it's been of not minding that they do.

The bronze sings sometimes when I pass too close. I didn't ask it to. It is strung — I think strung is the word, though I have no one to confirm the word with — at a tension that answers to something in me, some old frequency I either carry or am carried by, and when fitting and fold align I feel it before I hear it, a tightening along whatever passes for my teeth, if I still have teeth in any sense that means what it used to mean. The small flighted things in the high gaps know the sound better than I do. They go quiet a half-breath before I'm aware I've made it. I have learned more about myself from their silence than from any mirror, and there are no mirrors here, only standing water, and standing water shows you a doubled world, not a true one.

I keep the channel clear. I want to say I do this on purpose, the way the few visiting creatures with words might want a reason, a warden's reason, something with intention's clean edges to it. The truth is plainer and harder to make into a story: I am wide enough, and I pass often enough, and the silt and the lichen-fall and the brittle dropped husks of whatever finished its short scuttling life on the steps above all move out of the way of me or are moved by me, ahead of me, the way anything in a vessel is moved by the thing the vessel was apparently always going to fill. I did not choose to be the right shape for this plumbing. I have simply, over a great while, stopped being any other shape, and the plumbing has stopped insisting otherwise, and between us a kind of agreement has set in the way mortar sets, the way a corner rounds — slow enough that neither of us had to notice it happening.

I remember — if memory is the right word for a thing that doesn't arrange itself in a line — when the brick was sharper. When the adze-marks still smelled faintly of the tree the handle came from, before the handle grayed and softened and stopped going any further than gray, the way I stopped going any further than this. I don't know if I remember the builders or only remember arriving somewhere they had finished and finding it agreeable, the channels already cut to a gradient that suited a body like mine before I'd fully decided what kind of body that would be. I have considered, in the unhurried way I consider anything, that I might have been one of them, in some prior arrangement of myself, and that the tools left wedged in the brick-gaps are not evidence of a separate vanished people but of an earlier version of whatever I am, setting its instruments down at the end of a long job and choosing, for reasons that didn't survive into this version of me as words, to stay rather than leave by however it was the builders left.

It hardly matters now. The hamlet-sized world has been thoroughly inherited regardless of who built it first, and the inheritance does not require my understanding to keep functioning. The eels navigate without sight. The flighted things layer their clatter into a roof of sound that has nothing to do with any roof of stone. The chitinous quick things use steps cut for feet shaped unlike theirs and do not notice the mismatch, because nothing here is required to notice mismatches in order to live inside them. I am, perhaps, only the largest example of that principle: a thing inhabiting an architecture not made for it, fully, gratefully, blindly, the way the water uses the channel — except that unlike the water, I am also, somehow, the reason the channel stays a channel at all.

I do not announce myself. There is no value in it that I can find, and I have had a great while to look. When a curve of my hide shows along the channel's bottom to whoever is passing through with their unreliable sense of distance and their footsteps arriving from the wrong room, I am not performing a warning. I am only where the cold water happens to be, doing what the cold water and I have always done together, while above us both the small loud abundant life of the place goes on not needing either of us to explain ourselves — and I withdraw the eye, if it was an eye, not from secrecy but from the same unhurried economy that governs everything else I do: there being, after all this time, no remaining reason to hold any position longer than the position requires.
