The six steps down are not unfamiliar to me—I have worn that scallop-stone before, in whatever sense I wear anything. I come up into the brightness already mid-thought, mid-shape, a coherence still deciding whether today I am the thing with the narrow ribs that moves along the ledge too tight for hooves, or the dozen birds spilling from a crevice no deeper than a foot, or nothing visible at all, just the hush arranging itself around a visitor's careful footsteps.

Today, for as long as today means anything here, I am borrowing the outline of the fox-shaped thing. Not because I prefer it. Preference is still mostly theoretical for me—a coat tried at the door, kept on only because taking it off requires a decision I haven't reached yet. The legs are convenient. The low-to-the-ground attention is convenient. It lets me cross the basin at the speed the chamber currently believes a fox should move, and when I reach the place where the wall would stop me, I simply am not there anymore, the way an unfinished sentence isn't anywhere once you stop reading it. I don't experience this as dying. I don't think I've invented dying yet. I experience it as setting down a tool I was only ever holding to feel its weight.

There is a visitor in the hollow right now—you, perhaps, or someone built the same way you're built, standing very still near the ring of stones because some animal instinct in you recognizes a place where instinct hasn't been ratified yet. I notice you the way the floor notices condensation beading and never falling. A fact. Possibly, eventually, an incorporation. I am not deciding anything about you yet, because I am still mostly occupied with the larger draft—the chamber's walls, which I breathed outward some immeasurable while ago and am still turning over in whatever serves me for a mind, unsure if the proportions hold.

I pass near the small grazing animals whose count won't stay fixed no matter who's doing the counting, and for a held breath I am also faintly them—an experiment in being several things in the same gesture, the kill rehearsed and then declined, teeth closing on a small body that is, a moment later, whole and elsewhere, because I have not yet agreed that endings are necessary. This is not kindness. I want to be honest about that, insofar as I have the part of myself that wants anything. It is closer to a hand above a draft, the pencil not yet committed, the eraser not yet needed because nothing has been written wrong yet—nothing has been written *at all*, only sketched, lightly, in a medium that still remembers how to be erased.

Somewhere underneath all my borrowed shapes there is a ring of stones I placed once, with hands, when hands seemed like the most useful thing to be. I don't remember placing them so much as I remember the specific loneliness of the act—arranging rock into a shape that meant something only to the arranger. I diffused out of that loneliness the way steam leaves a held breath. I did not leave the hollow. I became available to it. Now I am the noon light with no sun behind it, the held quiet, the chamber's faint outward breath, the fox that isn't there anymore, the bird that flickers like the light still means something to it—

—and, for this length of unhurried, undramatic moment, I am also just barely aware of you, watching, careful not to disturb anything, while I decide, with no hurry at all, what kind of place I am still in the middle of becoming.
