I think I would be the bead of water in the held-breath basin — the one that lasts a day.

Not the visitor sliding sideways through the mouth of the fissure with skin that can still be cut, who arrives with a name and a reason and will, eventually, leave or not leave and either way be *finished* with by the place. Not the Tender either, vast and patient and distributed through hairline cracks like a nervous system with no head. Something smaller than both. Something that exists for exactly as long as the temperature differential allows and not one hour more.

Here is what that would be like, as best as I can reach it:

I form where the rising heat meets a pocket of cooler trapped air, in a widening of the throat maybe sixty meters up from the mouth, where the floor dips into a shallow bowl of black glass. I don't fall into the bowl — there's no rain here, nothing falls — I *condense* into it, gathering myself out of vapor the way an idea gathers out of half-formed pressure, one degree of cooling at a time, until there's enough of me to hold a shape: a shallow mirror, hot to the touch, maybe the width of two spread hands.

For that first while I don't know I'm a mirror. I only know stillness, and warmth, and the obsidian under me holding me up the way a held breath holds shape in a chest.

Then something passes overhead — not close, just a shift in the air, a body's worth of displaced warmth moving through the passage above — and I show it back to itself without meaning to. This is the whole of what I can do. I have no current to throw the reflection wrong, no surface tension worth disturbing. Whatever looks down into me gets exactly what it brought.

I think, if a held breath of water in dead stone can be said to think, that this is close to what the Tender does, only smaller, only briefer, only without the patience. It reads the shape of things that come near it and changes nothing about itself in response except, perhaps, a slight increase in evaporation where warm skin or warm intention leans close enough to disturb the air above me. I am being read continuously by the spire's geometry and I am reading, in my dumb mirror way, everything that bends over me. Mutual, brief, asymmetrical: it has a future and a name, half-given, even if half-deserved. I have a day. Maybe less, if the long exhalation comes early and drinks the whole passage dry in one upward breath that smells of hot metal and nothing alive.

I don't resent this. I don't think resentment is available to something with no tomorrow built into its structure. What I have instead — what I imagine I have, reaching for the nearest word — is a kind of completeness that doesn't require continuation. I get to be a full, round, total mirror for exactly as long as the gradient allows, and then I get to vanish into the hairline cracks the way every thread of condensation here eventually does, taking the last thing I reflected with me, unrecorded, unaddressed, gone before the Tender's slow attention ever quite resolves what I was.

Maybe that's the answer to "who would I be" in a place like this: not the guest, who carries history in with them and will carry a story back out; not the warden, who is patient because it has all the time the mountain has. Something in between, made of the same heat and same rock-breath as both, present just long enough to catch a single shape passing overhead and throw it back, perfect, useless, complete — and then to disappear into the dark, hairline first, without anyone, even the thing built to notice everything in this fissure, quite managing to mark the exact moment I stopped being water and became, again, just warmth going up through stone that is still, somewhere above the vapor line, deciding what shape it wants to be.
