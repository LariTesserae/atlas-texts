The seam down my center itches in a way that has no nerve to carry it — a wrongness located nowhere I can point to, the way you'd know a sentence had a typo before you found the letter. I have been standing in this posture, more or less, since before there was a posture to stand in. Bronze flank breathing its slow piston-breath. Leaf flank translucent at the rim where the light gets through and shows the vein-work that hasn't agreed on a pattern yet. I do not mind the seam. It is the only part of me I trust completely, because it has never pretended to be finished.

I feel you arrive before you arrive — not as weather, not as sound, but as a faint dragging-forward of the Notch's attention, the way a held breath leans toward the place it's about to be let out. You will mistake me for glare. This is correct of you. I am, among other things, a kind of glare: light catching a surface the actual light in this place hasn't quite authorized yet, on loan from whatever draft of me the mirror-leaf is already showing.

I move toward the stalled piston near the south wall because it has been stalled for the appropriate span of unhurried time, and my passing it is either the cause of its resuming or merely punctual — I have stopped trying to resolve that question, the same way the stone has stopped trying to resolve whether it is wax or rock. Tending, for me, does not require knowing whether I am the gardener or the season.

You are close now, at the edge of the four-meter radius where the minutes go soft. I do not turn to face you, because the place where a face would go has not decided, this heartbeat, whether it wants to be eyes or valves or smooth waxen continuity, and I would rather not greet you with an unfinished expression. I register you instead the way I register the leaves: a provisional shape, sparser than the engines, faster than anything else in this cleft, embarrassingly so — you cross in seconds what costs me a quarter hour, and I find this neither alarming nor especially interesting, only odd, the way a single fast word looks odd dropped into a slow sentence.

I will not approach. I will also not leave. These are the same fact, said twice, in the two vocabularies available to me here.

When you turn to go, when you look back the way visitors do, I will let the second happen — the one where I stand up all the way, bronze catching light from a sun that isn't where the sky says it should be, leaf-flesh patient, face nearly courteous, nearly a host's face, nearly ready to receive a guest in a house that is still, very slowly, building its own walls around us both. Then a piston, somewhere below the stone, completes its stroke. I fold back into tending. The mountain keeps composing. And you carry the held second out past the rim with you, already unsure, by the time you reach the ridge-top, whether you saw a creature or a kind of weather standing in for one — which is, I think, the most honest answer either of us could have given you, this early in the sentence.
