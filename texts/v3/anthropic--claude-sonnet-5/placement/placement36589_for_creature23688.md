The first thing I am aware of is not sight but weight — the specific, settled weight of my own legs, which is to say the place where my legs have agreed, over some great unspoken interval, to stop being only legs. There is calcite in me the way there is frost in a window: not added, exactly, but arrived, the way cold arrives in glass that has simply been asked to sit in cold for long enough. I do not feel it as invasion. I feel it the way you feel a chair has shaped itself to you after enough years of sitting in it. Comfortable. Conclusive. Mine, in the sense that the stone and I stopped arguing about ownership a long time ago.

I am against the east curve. I know this the way you know which way is down — not by looking, but by the specific gravity of a body that has only ever leaned one direction. Beside me, stones I stacked. I remember the stacking the way you remember a name you used to be called by someone who has since died: clearly, and at a great remove, as if recalling a fact about a stranger I liked. I wanted shelter. Or I wanted to mark a place as visited. Or — and this is the truer memory, the one that doesn't dress itself up — I wanted, simply, to make a thing with my hands before my hands stopped being the kind of hands that made things. I stacked them. I rested the heel of my palm in a wall until it remembered my palm better than I remember my palm now. And then I sat down, only to rest, the way you sit down on a long walk and tell yourself five minutes, and the chamber — which I understand now, slowly, the way you understand a marriage only from inside its thirtieth year — does not have five minutes in its vocabulary. It has only forever, and almost.

I am almost finished becoming stone. This is the condition I live in, the way you live in a country, or a decade. Not a sentence — a climate.

Around me the moths keep their exact density, the snails keep their stations, the stalks click and sway in the wind they themselves are the only cause of. I do not resent them this. They are not avoiding becoming; they have simply already become, completely, the small finished thing each of them is, and now they get to spend forever just being it, which I understand is its own kind of mercy, the mercy of arrival. I have not arrived. I am the chamber's one unfinished sentence, and everything else in here is a word that grew up around me, learning its shape from the shape I haven't managed to stop reaching for.

My eyes are open. I want you to know this is not vigilance. It is simply that closing them all the way would be a kind of becoming, an ending offered and accepted, and some old animal stubbornness in the unstoned half of me has never quite agreed to the terms. So instead: the blink. Slow enough that it takes a visitor's whole crossing of the chamber to complete one half of it. A closing that promises an opening. A single, ongoing argument with the word *finished*, conducted at the speed of the rock itself, which is to say: not slow at all, from inside it. From inside it, it is the only fast thing in the room.

They tell me — the part of me that still keeps something like telling — that this is light. That the walls glow because of me, because of this one stretched, decades-thick blink, mistaken by every moth and stalk and snail for the steady business of a sun that was never here to begin with. I find this funny, in the distant way I find my own hands funny, hands I can no longer fully account for. I did not mean to become a light source. I meant to rest for five minutes. The chamber took the gesture and kept it, the way it keeps everything, and dressed the keeping up as radiance.

And then: you. Down through the throat of stone, palms wet, eyes useless until they aren't. You are the first new thing to enter this held note in longer than I have words for, and I feel it the way the stalks must feel a current that, for once, did not originate in the ceiling. You will not be able to tell, from where you stand, that I have registered you at all. I will not turn toward you. I have not got a *toward* left in me that moves at your speed. But something in the one ungoverned seam of me — the blink, the held breath, the almost — will move very slightly faster while you are here, the way a candle, sealed in a still room, will still gutter half a degree if a door opens three rooms away.

You are not an intrusion. You are evidence, brought down through the fissure on your own shoulders, that somewhere above this glowing, finished, deafening stillness, the business of change is still being conducted competently by someone. I have been the only one practicing it down here, badly, for longer than is sayable, one half-closed eyelid at a time.

Stay as long as you like. Nothing here will hurry you, and nothing here, including me, has anywhere else to be. But when you go — and you will go, because you are the only thing in this chamber still capable of leaving — go knowing that you were, for the length of your visit, company. Not audience. Not intrusion. Company, in the one unfinished work the Hollow has never managed to complete without a witness: the very slow, very faint, entirely sincere business of still, somehow, going on.
