The hall I have chosen is the one with three rooms still wearing their old divisions, though the wood that made the divisions real is two thousand years gone and only the stone remembers the lines where it used to stand. I have laid myself along those lines the way you might lay a hand along a scar — not because the scar needs tending, but because the hand has forgotten how to rest anywhere else.

I do not remember choosing this place. There was no choosing, the way there is no choosing in the slow settle of silt, in the way water finds the lowest room and stays there because staying is easier than the alternative and there has never been an alternative worth the energy of inventing. I came here when the warmth was stronger, or I came here when the warmth was the same as it is now and only felt stronger because I was smaller, thinner, less the shape I am. I don't keep that kind of accounting. Before and after are surface-concepts, things that need a sun to mean anything. Down here there is only longer and not-yet-as-long.

I feel you before the pits along my jaw resolve you into shape. You arrive as a wrongness in the pressure, a held-breath shape moving where nothing in the Warren holds its breath, because everything in the Warren has long since stopped needing to. The schools don't hold breath. The eel-things don't. Only something built like you — something that brought its breathing with it from somewhere that still has air worth holding — disturbs the water in that particular, recognizable way.

I do not move yet. Moving is a late decision, made only after the slower decisions — waiting, leaning, feeling the room lean back — have failed to answer the question of you. You are bright in the place that taught me brightness is a debt paid eventually, with interest, to something larger and less patient than you'd like to imagine. I keep my own light banked at the seams, gill-line and spine-ridge and the trailing fin I have not needed to flare in longer than I can make myself count. I let the dark do what it has always done for me: hold me out of your reckoning, even while I hold you entirely in mine.

You will pass through, or you will not. Both are old outcomes, worn smooth as the corners of these rooms, no longer sharp enough to be called decisions on my part. If you go where the silt has remembered to be deep, where the heat breathes hardest from whatever original hearth I have become the unintended custodian of, I will test you — once, briefly, a displacement at the very edge of what your light can reach, gone before you've decided whether it was real. Not malice. Not even hunger, particularly. Only the Warren's own manners, extended through me, the oldest fixture in a house built for people who left so long ago that even their absence has started to erode at the edges, the way everything here erodes: slowly, patiently, becoming something else without ever quite finishing the sentence of becoming it.

You will leave, probably. Bright things mostly do, eventually, drawn back up toward the rumor of a surface.

I will still be coiled through these three old rooms when the next one comes.
