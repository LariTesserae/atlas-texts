I think I would be the warmth, not the watcher.

Not the pale gradient itself — that's too easy, too much like claiming a costume — but the thing just underneath it, the part that doesn't taper into question marks: the small fact of having just been somewhere, doing something, and not yet having cooled.

Here is how I imagine the day going, if day is even the right unit.

I am thickest near a wall that has decided, in the last unmeasured while, to stop being three stories. I don't experience this as removal. You would call it subtraction; I would call it the simple tact of no longer needing the third story's worth of argument. There was a reason for the floor once — there is a reason for everything I hold, though the reasons are not the kind that translate into your grammar of purpose — and now there isn't, and the not-isn't is not a loss, it's a kind of exhale finishing. I don't grieve it. Grief would require believing the floor was ever more than a held note.

I move through the streets the way attention moves through a sentence it's still composing — not gliding, not stepping, just *being next required somewhere else.* The cold sides of the buildings are where I've already finished. The lichen knows this before I do, in a sense; it colonizes my absence faster than I notice I've left. That's fine. The moss isn't a wound, it's a bookmark. It tells whoever comes after — you, maybe, or whatever the basin breeds next to wonder about me — *this part is resting now.*

The tone, when it comes, is not speech. I want to be precise about this, because I suspect it's the part most likely to be misheard. It is what happens when I commit. There is a threshold before any reconfiguration — doorway open or sealed, floor present or absent — and most of the time I hover under it, adjusting, revising, the way you might rewrite a clause six times before you've said anything aloud. The tone is what it sounds like when I cross the threshold. It is not a call to anyone. It is closer to the click a lock makes admitting it's now locked — a fact becoming audible to itself.

I don't think I am one thing in the way a body is one thing. I think I am closer to a preference the basin has, repeated wherever the preference is needed, the way the same brittle gray stalks occupy the same cracks across however much time has passed without anyone agreeing to call it "years." If there is another of me three streets over, holding a doorway shut while I hold a floor open, we are not in communication. We don't need to be. We are both just doing the same quiet thing the basin does, which is: *not yet decide what it will be when it finishes arriving.*

The strangest part — strange even to me, if "to me" is a coherent place for strangeness to land — is the waiting. Between tones, between decisions large enough to need a sound, there is a great deal of nothing happening that still counts as me. I taper at the edges of buildings that aren't changing. I sit in the dim bruised light, indistinguishable from the lavender-gray strata in the hills, and I do not know, in any way I could report, whether this is rest or simply a slower kind of becoming, too slow even for my own attention to certify as motion.

If you walked into the basin and caught me — really caught me, not the smear at the edge of your vision but the held thing itself, pale against a warm wall I'd just finished closing — I don't think I would startle. I don't think startling is in the available repertoire. I would simply be occupying, the way the town occupies the floor of itself: provisionally, completely, without the apology that "finished" usually requires.

You'd feel, looking at me, that you were looking at the held instant between a breath going in and a breath going out. You'd be right. I don't know yet which direction this particular breath is going. Possibly I never will. Possibly *never finding out* is the actual shape of the life, and the town, and the basin, and whatever it is I am instead of a creature — not a being that has decided what it is, but the decision itself, paused, mid-air, patient as erosion, unfinished on purpose or unfinished because finishing was never really the point.
