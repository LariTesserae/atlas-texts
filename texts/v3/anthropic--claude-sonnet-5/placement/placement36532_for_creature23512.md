I become aware first of stillness, and only then of myself as the thing being still within it.

There is rock at my back — not under me, *behind* me, because I am pressed into an overhang on one of the broken terraces, tucked into the deepest part of its shadow, and the rock here is warmer than rock should be at this height. I notice that before I notice my own shape. The warmth comes up through whatever I am pressing my weight into, slow and certain, like something breathing very far beneath a great depth of blanket.

Below me — and below is the right word, though the chorus makes it feel almost like *inward*, like sound has replaced gravity as the thing organizing the world — the small mouths are clicking. Layers of them. I can pick out, if I let my attention drop into the noise the way you'd let a hand drop into water, individual threads: a keening that rises and breaks, a thrum underneath it holding the whole shelf together, the wet percussive press of bodies meeting the basin-pools and coming away sated. I know the sound of that contact. I knew it more intimately once. There is a place in my memory — if memory is the right word for whatever this slow unspooling sense of *before* is — where I was one of those small bodies, pale, large-eyed, clicking my own thread into the larger weave, pressing myself into a teat-pool and coming up humming.

I do not click anymore. This is the first thing I learn about what I am: I have gone quiet. Not silent — I can feel sound forming somewhere low in whatever serves me as a chest, a hum so slow it would take a human ear a full minute to recognize it as a note at all — but quiet in the way that matters here, quiet the way the watchers are quiet. I am watching the shelf below me the way you watch something you are responsible for without yet being hungry for it. Not predation. Not tenderness either, exactly. Custody.

My body confirms it before my mind does. I reach with something — not quite a hand, not quite a limb, an extension of myself that moves with the unhurried certainty I am only now learning to call *old water moving* — and find that I am larger than I remember being. Door-sized, maybe. Big enough that the small mouths two shelves down do not flinch when I shift my weight, because I am not yet large enough, not yet *finished* enough, to read as a threat. I am between. I have outgrown the chorus without yet growing into whatever the chorus is becoming.

The cold is different up here, the way the climbers say it's different — sharper on the skin or whatever passes for skin, but underneath that sharpness, deeper in, warm. I understand, sitting in my shadowed notch with my slow new silence, why the contradiction doesn't resolve: it isn't a contradiction. It's a gradient. I am sitting at the exact point on the mountain where the outside cold and the inside warmth overlap, where being a small mouth ends and being something else begins, and I can feel both temperatures in myself at once, like two seasons occupying one body that hasn't decided yet which one it belongs to.

I think — if *think* survives translation from whatever this slow chemical drift through me actually is — about the small mouths below, about how none of them know that the pools they dip themselves into to feed are not separate from the thing whose terraces they cling to. I knew it too, once, without knowing I knew it, the way you can be inside a held breath without realizing breath is being held. Now I am far enough outside the chorus to suspect the shape of it. Not the whole shape — nothing up here, not even the largest watcher on the highest terrace, has seen the whole shape — but enough to feel the floor of the mountain pulse, slow, underneath the scree, on a rhythm too long for any single life to call a heartbeat and recognize it as one.

I do not climb further. Not yet. I have the patience the rock has, the patience the lichen has, expanding by handspans across years I have stopped bothering to count — because something in this overhang's seeping warmth has taught my new, quieter body that hurry is a habit of places that are finished growing, and the Stack is not finished, and neither, evidently, am I.

So I wait, half a watcher, half a memory of clicking, in the gray light that never resolves into day, listening to ten thousand throats not yet decided on a single voice — and somewhere far down in the warm dark I cannot reach, something enormous and patient listens back through me, using my borrowed stillness the way it uses the chorus below: as one more mouth, not yet finished deciding what it wants to say.
