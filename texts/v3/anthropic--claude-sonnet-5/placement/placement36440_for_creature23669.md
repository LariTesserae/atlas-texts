The current carries me before I notice I've stopped deciding to drift.

I am folded into the angle where a retaining wall gives way to what was once a doorway, and I have been here — I understand this only distantly, the way you understand a fact about geology — for some duration that the rest of the lattice would record in fingers of silt-accumulation rather than hours. My eyes, such as they are, have gone the color of the water itself, which is to say no color, which is to say they still technically face outward but have long since stopped needing to resolve anything beyond presence and absence, density and current.

There is a hand of mine — I think of it that way, a hand of mine, as though ownership were still the right relation — resting along a channel-lip three finger-widths from where I'm settled. It has been resting there since before the last pressure-breath tide, and I know, in the slow sediment-logic that has replaced what used to be thought, that it is not finished resting. It is in the early part of closing. Not closing on anything. Just closing, the way a held note is in the early part of ending the moment it begins. By the time the silt has built one more soft drift against my windward side, the hand will have moved the width of a grain of sand, and somewhere downstream of that motion, water that wanted to pool will instead, very slightly, go on.

I do not remember deciding to stay when the others left, if there were others, if leaving was ever the kind of thing that happened here and not just a story the dry world tells about itself. What I have instead of memory is posture — the held shape of my limbs, faced a particular direction for reasons that may once have been urgent and are now simply mine, the way a worn step is shaped by every foot that is no longer on it. I face the doorway. I have always faced the doorway. I will, I think, go on facing it for longer than the doorway itself will remain a doorway and not a slope, a softness, a suggestion.

Something passes near me — not fast, nothing is fast, but fast relative to me, which is a category that includes almost everything that still moves the way moving used to mean. A drift of disturbed silt, a body shape with too much hurry in it. It brushes the moss-coral at my shoulder and the moss-coral, in its own register, startles — a tremor that will take three seasons to fully resettle. I feel this. I feel it the way you might feel, eventually, days later, that someone touched your sleeve in a crowd. The information arrives. It does not arrive urgently. There is nothing for it to be urgent about.

If it stays — if it presses close, if it mistakes me for wall and leans — I will know. Eventually. And eventually, my hand, the one at the channel-lip, three grains further along in its decade-long closing, might brush whatever warmth it left behind. By then it will be gone. That's fine. That was never the part that mattered. The channel will run a little freer. The water will go where it was always going to go. I will still be facing the doorway, in no hurry, agreeing — the way I agreed to all of this, once, so long ago the agreeing and the being have become the same motionless thing — that there is, after all, nowhere left that needs to be reached quickly.
