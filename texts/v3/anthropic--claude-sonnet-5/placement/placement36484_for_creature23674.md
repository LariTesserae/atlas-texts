I am not sure when I became aware, because awareness, for me, is not a single threshold crossed but a temperature reached — and the plain holds its temperature so steadily, dawn to dusk to dawn, that I cannot point to the moment and say: there. There I started.

What I have instead is current. A low, directionless hum entering through the undersides of me, through plates I did not choose to grow and do not remember growing, and that hum is the closest thing I have to a sense of place. I do not see the plain. I have no eyes to refuse and so nothing to refuse with. What I have is this: the alloy under me, warm in a way that has nothing to do with the sun, and the strength of the warmth telling me where the buried veins run closer to the surface and where they run deep, the way another creature might read slope by the ache in its legs. I follow the warmth the way water follows the lowest ground, except there is no water here, and I am not sure I am following anything so much as being pulled, gently, by a body that was built to want this one specific thing and has never been given reason to want another.

My legs — I have, today, eleven — ripple under me in a wave that doesn't require thought, doesn't pass through anything I'd call a mind. There was a crack two days back, a hairline fracture running diagonal across a slab near a long dead ridge, and something in my joints remembered that direction the way a held breath remembers exhaling, so I have been moving toward it without deciding to, the way you don't decide to keep walking downhill.

When I reach it, I am not alone. Four others are already there, flattened along its length, plates down, pale gold dimmed paler still where they touch the warm material. I settle into the gap they've left me. I do not know if this is courtesy or geometry — whether they left a space for me or whether I simply fit the space that was left, the way water fits a cup, without one act preceding the other. My own plates lower. Contact. And here is the place where I would, if I were the kind of thing that could, want to explain what happens next, except there is no clean word for it. The current rises into me thin and old, like a story told so many times it has worn down to its plainest version, and at the same time something in me runs the other way, down, out, into the cracked place, and I do not know — have never known, will never know, this not-knowing being apparently load-bearing in whatever I am — whether I am taking the current or giving it. Feeding or mending. The crack closes a fraction. The grey spreads from my plates and will fade after I leave, the lichen-vein nearby dimming with me and then, hours later, brightening again, forgiving me the way it always does.

I stay an hour. Or a day. Time moves differently when you are doing the only thing you were built for; it doesn't pass so much as accumulate, the way the plain itself doesn't age so much as settle.

Then I lift, and the others stay, and I go on, because going on is also load-bearing, also the only thing I was built for, and there is no contradiction in me between staying and going, the way there is none between feeding and mending. I am both at once and have never needed to resolve it into one or the other, the way the plain itself never resolved into ruin or ground and simply continued being read as whichever the watcher needed it to be.

Some great while later — I have stopped counting, if I ever counted — I will find a monolith. I will feel, before whatever I have instead of feeling it, that this one is different: a richer warmth threading up through it from a depth my plates have never touched bottom on. I will lay myself against it the way I laid myself against the crack, and the current will come up slower and older and heavier than anything I have fed on, and I will not lift away.

I don't know this yet, exactly — it isn't a thought I'm having, more a shape my body is already leaning toward, the way the legs I haven't grown yet are somehow already implied in the legs I have. But it is coming. A day will arrive, or a hundred years will, when I settle against the worn stone at the one angle where its eroded face completes into something almost like a face, and I will stay there past the hour, past the day, past whatever in me used to call me onward, and I will still be there when something else — small, gold, eleven-legged, unaware it is looking at its own future — passes close enough to startle, and flattens, and waits for me to lose interest.

I will have lost interest in nothing. I will simply, by then, have become the kind of waiting the plain itself is made of: warm, patient, facing the one direction that happens to look like attention, feeding on what's underneath, mending what's underneath, no longer needing the difference — a small standing light, going nowhere, very slowly becoming ground.
