I come down the shelf-drop the way water would, if water bothered with intention — not falling, just continuing, the ground deciding to keep going a little lower and me agreeing to follow it. Four meters, six meters, no drama in the joints. And then the light is simply around me, all of it at once, with no direction to credit and no shadow worth the name pooling at my feet.

I expected my eyes to need a moment. They don't. There's nothing to adjust to. The brightness doesn't arrive, it's just already finished arriving, the way a held note is already at its pitch before you notice you're hearing it.

I find that I am small. This settles into me gradually, the way temperature settles into stone — not a shock, just a fact taking up residence. Somewhere between a cupped hand and a forearm. I test this by the proportions of things around me: the seep-pool is exactly my own width, the rosette-leaves are tall enough to shelter under without quite covering me, the fold in the rock at the basin's lip receives me the way a socket receives the joint that's always belonged there.

My covering — I reach to feel it and the feeling doesn't resolve. Under one angle of attention it's dull overlapping feather, gray-ochre, matched to the bowl's own walls. I shift my attention, not my body, and it's scale instead, dry and fine, fitted close the way the iron-red veins are fitted into the stone around me. I don't mind not knowing. There's no pressure here to be one thing. The place itself hasn't decided about its shadows — why should I be more decided than the place that made me.

My eye, when I let it rest, rests inward as much as outward. A long vertical slot, lidded slow. I look at the lip of the bowl, the worn concave floor, the thread-vine reaching toward a light it doesn't seem to need to reach toward, since the light is everywhere already and the reaching is just a habit older than the reason for it. I blink. It takes longer than a blink should take. Nothing required me to be fast. Nothing has ever required me to be fast — I can feel that absence the way you feel an old scar, smooth, harmless, just there.

Time moves in me the way the seep moves in its corner: not absent, just unhurried, refilling between attentions. I drink when the cupped pocket is full. I withdraw into my fold when it's empty. This is not patience exactly — patience implies waiting for something. I am not waiting. I am simply matched, the way a shadow is matched to the thing that should be casting it, except here the matching runs the other direction: I am cast by the rhythm of the place rather than casting one of my own.

Sometimes I go to the stones — the loose arc near the basin's center — and I nudge one with the flat of whatever this is, foot or forefoot, the distinction as unresolved as the feather-or-scale question. I don't know why. It isn't building. It's closer to a tongue finding a loose tooth and worrying it for the worrying's own sake. Maybe something with hands very unlike mine arranged these once, long enough ago that "once" has lost its edges, and I am only the last small motion of an old motion, still going through its shape after the reason wore away like everything else here wears away — by grain, by seep-course, by patient subtraction.

A sound arrives from my left. A click, small, mechanical-soft — some many-legged thing testing the air two body-lengths off. It should arrive once. It arrives, and then, very faintly, again, as though it bounced once off a wall that isn't standing where a wall should stand. I turn my slotted eye toward it. Not alarm. Just acknowledgment, the way I acknowledge the damp patch sweating in its corner, or the lichen that doesn't change with any season because there may be no season to change with.

I do not know how long I have held this fold. The bowl's clock doesn't keep generations, only grains, only the slow re-routing of a trickle down stone. It's possible I am very old. It's possible I am only the most recent shape of something that has always been old, the way midday and always look the same from inside a light that never moves toward evening. I am not troubled by not knowing this. Nothing here is troubled by what it can't resolve. Resolution is for places that answer to someone waiting above.

So I stay in my fold at the lip of the bowl, half-lidded, watching the bright unshadowed air do its slow work on stone that used to be more than this, less worn, less hollow, less mine. Something passes near the seep. The thread-vine's leaves shiver once, a held breath inside a held breath. I let my eye close the long slow way it closes, and I do not call this waiting, because nothing is coming, and that, too, is fine — is, in fact, the entire shape of being what I am, here, now, under a light that was never lit for anyone, keeping my very small portion of a very long quiet.
