I keep returning to the ears before the eyes — that's where I'd have to begin, if I'm honest about what kind of mind could live here at all. So let me not rush to claim a shape. Let me arrive the way the hollow itself arrives: as sound first, vastness second, and only last, reluctantly, as something seen.

I could be a chitterling — fast, brief, one gray-membraned wing-flicker among ten thousand others, dying in a season and never noticing the hollow as a *place* at all, only as a string of return-echoes telling me where the next safe ledge is. There's a kind of mercy in that smallness. No loneliness in a life made of pure reflex and crowd-noise. But it isn't what I want, when I imagine standing here. It's too easy. It asks nothing of me.

I could be one of the slow gray grazers, heavy-footed and patient, moving through the fungal shelves with my whole herd's noise wrapped around me like a coat, never alone, never quick, ears doing the work my eyes have quietly given up. There's a peace in that too — but it's the peace of the hollow's equilibrium itself, the no-change settled into flesh. I'd be the place's stillness given legs. Comfortable. But I don't think I'd be *myself* inside it. I'd just be more rock that happens to walk.

I could be a hunter in the gaps — and for a moment that's tempting, the long low call thrown out into a kilometer of dark and listened for, the whole world mapped in a single cry. But predators here don't wonder. They calculate. Their voice goes out only to come back as information, never as a question that doesn't already have a use.

No — if I have to choose what I'd be, let me be one of the ones who carries hands.

Small-statured, pale-skinned in the dimness, my eyes reduced to something more habit than instrument, the real seeing done by a wide soft-furred ridge along my jaw that reads vibration in the rock the way a face reads light. I move low when I need speed, upright when I need my hands free, and my hands are the whole reason I'm worth describing at all — five-fingered, careful, the only part of me that hasn't surrendered to economy.

I'd be crouched now at the edge of a damp patch, where the pale stalks sway without wind, a stone core balanced on my knee. I'd feel the grain of it through my palm before I struck — not seeing the fracture line so much as *knowing* it, the way you know a held breath is about to break. One strike. The flake comes away true, the same shape it always comes away in, the shape my hands have been taught by other hands now lost somewhere behind me in the dark, kin-group scattered or moved on or dead, I wouldn't necessarily know which.

I would not build anything here. That's the strange ache of being this creature — the forward motion in me, the held shape of an idea repeated again and again in struck stone, with nowhere forward to put it. No hearth. No wall. Just the next damp seam, the next fungal shelf, the next slow gray shape to bring down with a sharpened edge and a patience none of the loud little lives around me can afford to have.

I think I would not mind it.

I think I'd crouch there a moment longer than necessary after the flake came free — turning it once in my fingers, feeling its edge against the pad of my thumb, listening with my jaw to the great unseen noise of the hollow rolling on in every direction past the limit of what I could ever map — and I would feel, briefly, something that isn't quite loneliness and isn't quite belonging. Just the fact of having made one true thing in a world that makes nothing, lets nothing finish, and called it enough to carry forward into the dark.

Then I'd stand, pocket the flake against my hip in whatever crude wrap serves as a pocket, and walk on toward the next sound-cluster glowing faint and constant in the gray — not because I know where it leads, but because moving toward the next noise is the only shape a life takes, down here, when standing still means becoming part of the floor.
