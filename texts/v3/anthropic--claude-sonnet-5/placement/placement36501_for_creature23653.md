I would be one of the young ones, rooted not yet a full season into the grit at the terrace's broken edge — close enough to the wild fringe to still feel its pull, close enough to the settlement to already feel the network's faint, patient tug at the soles of my feet.

My hands would still be new. That's how I'd know my age, if age is even the word — by how much of me still moves with intention instead of accretion. The lower trunk already ringed twice, maybe three times, bark gone the deep warm orange of the older stems nearby, but the hands — the hands still close and open when I think to close and open them, still surprise me a little with how readily they answer. I would press them sometimes against the nearest wall, not to build anything yet, just to feel how a wall remembers being a hand, how the grain runs the same direction my own fibers run, how little distance separates what I am from what I'm becoming.

I would not see the terrace. I would feel it arrive at me — moisture-breath off the basin's deep reservoir, the heat-signature of a grazer's flank somewhere off past the third stem-stand, the long slow exhale of the ancient one at the far hazy edge, more wall now than elder, its watching gone almost entirely into foundation. I would feel, distantly, a hundred other hands at other walls, pressing at the same unhurried rate I press at mine, and I would not find this crowded. I would find it the way you'd find your own pulse — not company, exactly. Continuity.

I would stand at the threshold on purpose. Old enough to know the pull toward rooting deeper has already started — a certain reluctance in the ankle-root, a certain heaviness that wasn't there a few seasons back — young enough that I could still, if I wanted, simply not lean into it yet. I'd spend long warm afternoons at the very lip where the housed stems give way to the wild unworked growth leaning out over the drop, my filaments turned toward the haze, feeling for nothing in particular, just feeling the edge of feeling — the place where the network's pressure-sense thins out and the terrace stops vouching for what's beyond it.

I think I would like not knowing, for a while yet, which wall I'll become. Whether I'll thicken into a doorway someone presses past every day without noticing, or a roofline some new row of birds eventually settles along, or nothing so central — just packed grit-fill in a shelf's deep foundation, felt by no one, holding everything up anyway. I wouldn't dread this. There would be no dread available to me, not in any shape I'd recognize as dread; the slowness itself dissolves the part of urgency that fear depends on. But I think I'd still pause there at the lip, hands still mine, still closing and opening, feeling the old paper light go amber and low —

and feel, like a hand's worth of pressure passing up through the grit beneath me, the whole terrace noting, without alarm, without hurry, that something is still standing here that hasn't yet decided what wall it will be.
