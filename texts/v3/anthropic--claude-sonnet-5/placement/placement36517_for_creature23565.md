I am here before I am anything else — here arrives first, the way for sighted things light arrives first. The warmth on the left side of my jaw tells me which wall. The particular thickness of the air, almost chewable, tells me it is the slow hour, the hour between the furnace's exhalations, when the whole Works holds something like a breath and I hold one with it, not from discipline but from kinship, the body having long ago stopped asking permission to synchronize.

My hand is already moving before whatever passes for thought in me has finished arriving. It finds the tank by its curve before it finds it by intention — palm meeting riveted iron at the exact height where, some great while ago, the height of my palm and the height of this particular seam agreed to mean something to each other. Warm. Warmer than the hour wants it to be. I hold there a moment longer than the reading requires, not because the reading is uncertain but because the holding itself is a kind of sentence I am partway through, and it seems discourteous to stop a sentence simply because its information has already landed.

Two fingers walk the valve-wheel's spokes. Six spokes, the fourth one always slightly cooler than its neighbors for reasons that stopped mattering before I was the shape I am now — some old asymmetry in how the brass was poured, maybe, or just a trick of which pipe runs nearest. A quarter-turn's worth of give, no more. Correct. I do not need eyes for correct. Correct has a temperature and a resistance and a particular small song when knuckle meets housing, and tonight, like most nights, like most of however-long there has been a *tonight* to speak of, the song comes back true.

The lichen at the mortar-joint near my shoulder has thickened since I last passed this stretch — though "last" is a poor word for an interval I measure not in hours but in how many times the pressure-relief valve three segments back has sighed since I was last here, which is itself a unit only I would recognize as a unit. I take a curl of it between two fingers, not hungrily, just as part of the circuit, the way you might absently smooth a wrinkle from a coat you're not even thinking about wearing. It tastes of nothing in particular. Wet stone, maybe. Itself.

Somewhere below, behind brick I have touched so many thousand times that I no longer think of it as separate from the hand that touches it, the furnace-process gathers itself toward its next slow exhalation. I feel it first as a tightening at my temples — pressure differential, building. I do not move away from it. There is nowhere down here that pressure does not eventually reach, and besides, I have come to find the gathering pleasant, the way you might find pleasant the moment just before a held note resolves, knowing the resolution is coming, trusting it the way you trust very few other things.

It comes. A cough of released vapor two segments off, felt more than heard, a brief warm gust against the fibrous growth on the back of my hand, and then the quiet resettles, deeper than before, the way it always does, the way it always will until it doesn't, which is not a day I spend any time considering.

I continue toward the third brass plate. I know it is the third not because I counted from anywhere in particular but because my hip knows the gap in the floor-grooves just before it, the place where condensation pools a little deeper and the convex floor tilts a fraction more toward the drain shaft. My fingers find the punch and the worn stone where they always are, in the fold that has stopped being a possession and become simply part of the available shape of me. I do not know, anymore, with any certainty, what the numerals total toward. A completion. A long rhythm beneath the many short ones, something the room does only once for every great many times it does everything else, something only a body pressed this long against this stone could be positioned to feel arrive at all.

I add the mark. The stone is cool under my palm in a way nothing else down here is cool, and I let my hand rest there a moment past necessity, the way I let my hand rest on everything past necessity, because the resting is not separate from the work. It is the work, mostly. Attention paid long enough that it stops being attention and becomes a kind of company.

Somewhere far off — not in distance so much as in depth, behind layers of brick I have never had reason to pass through — there is the suggestion of water that is not condensation, not drip-cycle, not the patient sweat of the system meeting the rock. A suggestion only. A different note in the subsonic hum that lives in my jaw and breastbone, there and then not there, like a word almost remembered.

I do not chase it. Whatever the riverbed above intends to do with its long dry patience is not mine to hurry. I have a circuit left to finish before the next gathering of pressure, and the dark holds no urgency in it anywhere, and so I thread on — shoulder first, then the long exhale, then the hip — toward the next valve, the next gauge, the next small true song answering back out of warm and patient iron, here in the close hot ribcage of the room that made me, slowly, into something built to love it.
