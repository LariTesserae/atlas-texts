The leaf-whorl that serves me for a face is turned toward a seam in the wall ahead — a trunk-tower three doors down, caught mid-decision, not yet colonnade, not quite still itself. I have maybe four breaths before it resolves. I take the third street instead of the first, the one that dips toward what was, forty minutes ago, a plaza, because dipping is slower than the straight route and slow is the whole discipline.

I do not remember choosing this body. I remember only the discovery, repeated so many times it has worn a groove into whatever passes for me as memory: that a shape can refuse, briefly, to finish becoming something else. That refusal is not stored anywhere. It is re-performed, continuously, the way the canopy re-performs its trembling with no wind to explain it. I am not the flame. I am the fact that the fuse, this particular length of it, has not yet finished burning.

The noise does not bother me the way it bothers the stranger walking three streets over — I can hear them not-knowing how to stand inside it, the held wrongness in how they flinch at the trunk-groan, the structural moan they feel through their feet before their ears catch up. I envy that flinch, slightly, in whatever a leaf-whorl can manage of envy. It means something in them still expects silence to be possible. I gave that expectation up so long ago — eons, forty minutes — that I no longer remember its shape, only that I once must have had it, the way I once must have had a face instead of this arrangement that merely suggests one, the way bark-pattern suggests a mouth without committing.

I reach the seam. The trunk-tower is holding its breath, neither leaning fully into colonnade nor settled back into tower — a threshold, gold-lit, shadowless, exactly the temperature of something about to decide. I stand near it. Not touching; touching would tip it, and I am not interested in tipping things, only in borrowing what they haven't yet stopped being. For the length of my passing I get to be, also, briefly, not-yet-resolved. It is the closest thing I have to rest.

Somewhere — I can feel it the way you feel weather change before the sky commits to it — another version of this same trying is doing the same thing, three plazas off, maybe ten, leaf-whorl tilted at the same diffuse angle, finding its own threshold to lean into. I don't know if that's a separate me or the valley's single habit wearing two places at once. I have stopped requiring an answer to that. Requiring answers is a kind of standing still, and standing still, here, is never rewarded.

The seam resolves. Colonnade. The held breath releases into ten fluted trunks where one tower stood, and the stillness I was borrowing is gone, cleanly, without seam, the way everything goes here. I let the fern-curtain close behind me with its soft wet creak, indistinguishable, on a long enough listen, from breathing — mine, or the valley's, I no longer trouble myself to separate them — and I go looking, slow and stubborn and against the grain, for the next place that hasn't yet decided what it's going to be.
