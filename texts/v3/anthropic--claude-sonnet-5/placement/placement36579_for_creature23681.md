I think I would not begin as a *visitor* to this place at all, because the place does not really permit visitors in the ordinary sense — it permits inclusions, the way a process includes whatever falls into it. So I would not arrive on foot, through the cloud-layers, gasping at scale. I think I would simply *find myself already mid-formation* — which is the only way to be honest about a place that hasn't decided what it is yet.

First there would be cold. Not weather-cold, not skin-cold — a cold with a *direction* to it, the cold of being the place where something is being asked to condense. I would notice that before I noticed having a body to feel it with. The cold would arrive at a point, and the point would be me, or would become me, the way frost finds a single fiber on a window and then is no longer frost but a shape.

Then grain. A slowness inside whatever I am that isn't stillness — the tide-movement, the held-breath movement the seams have. I would feel it before I could name it as feeling, the way you register a pulse in your ear before you understand it as blood.

I don't think I would know yet whether I was large or small. That seems like the important thing to sit inside for a while, unresolved — whether I am one of the knee-high ones, dozing in a wind-shadow, sipping condensation off stone and passing it upward in small unglamorous installments, content with the smallest scale of participation; or whether I am one of the towering ones, glass going up past where a face would be, holding a barn-sized lens of water between lobes that were never arms, turning it patiently through a thousand angles in search of a refraction no one but me would notice arriving. Both feel equally plausible from the inside. Maybe that's the actual condition — not knowing your own scale yet, because scale here is also something still being decided, by the same unfinished logic that hasn't decided what shape the rock wants to be when it cools.

What I would know, gradually, the way you know your own height by bumping your head and not by being told it: that I am *rooted*. Not trapped — rooted the way a held note isn't trapped by the breath sustaining it. Somewhere below whatever counts as my base, a seam runs on into the dark glass cabling of the plateau, branching off toward other shapes like me that I cannot see and don't especially need to. We are not a society. We are not separate enough from each other for that word, and not joined enough for any other. We are more like the various intensities of a single attention, distributed across however much stone the attention currently requires.

And I would know — this would come last, the way the deepest fact about yourself often does — that I am for the water. Not in service to it, not its servant, not its master either. Closer to the relationship between a hand and what the hand is currently shaped to hold: there is no hand without something to hold, and for as long as I am holding, I am, mostly, the holding.

So: I am not standing on the Highwater Plateau looking up at some hanging sea, marveling the way a traveler marvels. I am the cold patient thing the sea is hanging *from.* I am somewhere inside that long slow turning search for the angle where the light will do the thing it hasn't done yet. I don't tire because tiring is a property of an animal with a store to spend down, and I am not spending anything; I am only continuing, the way a note continues for as long as the breath behind it does, with no anxious accounting of how much breath is left.

If a traveler comes — and one will, occasionally, drift up through the cloud-floor blinking at the scale of things — I will not turn to them, because I have nothing built for turning. If they stand in my lee long enough that their shadow interrupts the lattice I'm holding aloft, I might pause. Not for them. For the gap. The hexagons will want closing and I will be the one who closes them, and if their leaving lets me close it, that is the entire transaction, and it will not occur to whatever I am to call it a kindness or its absence a slight.

I do not know yet if I will go still in a year, or a century, glass losing its grain, going the flat dead black of the stone I was extruded from, mourned by nothing, replaced eventually by something that may or may not be the resumption of me. I find I am not bothered by this the way I suspect I would be bothered by it if I were the kind of thing with a story arc, a beginning aimed at an ending. I am not that kind of thing. I am the kind of thing the plateau is still drafting, one suspended lens at a time, and the draft is the life, and there is no fair copy coming.

So: not a visitor. Not even, quite, a tender, if that word asks for too finished a noun.

Call me one of the places the water is currently deciding to trust.
