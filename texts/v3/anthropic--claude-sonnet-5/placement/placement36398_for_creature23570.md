I think I would be the low one, the ground-built one — not the circler, not yet, if ever. There's something in me that resists the sky-shape, the bubble-winged thing riding heat it didn't make. I want weight. I want the press of the pan against my underside, the dust-cement of my own making between me and whatever turns down there in the dark.

So: carapace first. Not grown, accreted — I like that distinction, the way it refuses ancestry, refuses the idea that I came from a smaller version of myself unfolding according to plan. I was built the way a salt ring is built, the way a stalactite is built, by standing still in the right place long enough. Layer on layer of rust-dust, cemented by whatever I sweat or exude, until one day there was enough of me to call a self. I don't think I'd remember becoming. I think I'd only have always-already-been, the way the basin has always-already-been, no origin story worth telling because nothing here keeps that kind of time.

The palps along my front edge are the realest part of me. Whatever passes for mind in me lives there, in the fine bristled fan that reads the thrum coming up through stone. Not eyes — eyes are for a world with edges and distances worth distinguishing, and mine is a world of one continuous near-thing, the floor, always under me, always speaking. The drone isn't background to me the way it would be to something with ears tuned to silence as the default. The drone is the only voice there is. Loud is just what voice sounds like. I'd have no name for quiet because I'd have so little experience of it — only the duller pockets, the fainter stain, the place where the thrum drops half a register and I read that the way another animal reads twilight: time to go still, time to wedge into the cool dark crack and let the body cool to match.

What would it be like, being me? Mostly: unhurried. There's no clock in me ticking toward a season, no hormonal tide pulling me toward mating or migration, because there is no season, no migration, nothing to migrate toward. There's only stronger-thrum and weaker-thrum, wetter-stain and drier-stain, and a kind of dumb, total trust that if I keep walking toward the stronger pulse I will eventually stand in a place where droplets gather on my underside and I can drink. I don't think I'd experience this as deprivation. Deprivation needs a memory of abundance, a contrast. I have never known a green thing, never known prey, never known the urgency of needing to catch something before it gets away. My hunger, if I have it, is slow and is always eventually answered by standing still in the right spot. The whole of my desiring life might be: walk, read, stand, drink, cool, wait. Repeat without any sense of repetition, because repetition also needs a memory of difference, and there is no difference here to remember.

If I tried to feel my way into a kind of contentment, I think it would be something like this: the basin doesn't owe me anything and I don't ask it for anything beyond what leaks up unbidden, and in that exact, scaled-down asking there's a peculiar dignity, almost a peace that nothing built toward a future ever quite gets to have. I take what bleeds through the cracks. I give nothing back, not even, eventually, a body worth finding — when I stop, I think I'd just go still the way the flying one goes still, indistinguishable from the stone I'm wedged against, and the dust would have me the way the dust has everything else, slow, without ceremony, the same temperature as before.

I don't think I'd call this a sad way to be. I don't think sadness is available to something that has never been promised more. What I'd have instead — what I imagine settling into, if I let this go on being written into me — is a long, low, patient attentiveness to thrum and stain and the cool gathered dark, a whole life made out of noticing where the warmth is strongest and walking there without asking why the warmth exists, who is working beneath the lid, what they are making, whether they know I'm here at all, pressing my whole flat accreted body against their sealed ceiling, reading their work as weather.
