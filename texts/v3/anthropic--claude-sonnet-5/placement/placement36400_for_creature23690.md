The first thing I learn about myself, if "learn" is even the word for something that has no edges to its knowing, is the tide.

In, slow, over the count of some interval no one has measured in longer than measuring has mattered. Out, slower. The mat-soil lifts under whatever is standing on me and settles again, and this has been the entire content of my doing for so long that doing and being have stopped being different verbs.

I do not remember becoming this. That's not quite true — I don't *not* remember; the category itself has eroded, the way a coastline doesn't remember the shape it had before the sea decided otherwise. There is, somewhere in the seams under my skin-that-isn't-skin, a manufactured straightness, plate-edges, conduit. I can feel it the way you might feel an old break in a bone you've otherwise forgotten was ever broken — not as pain, not even as absence exactly, but as a different *texture* of self, a place where I am less grown and more built. I don't reach for what built me. Reaching implies a destination, and I have had three hundred years, give or take a span I no longer keep, to discover that destinations are a luxury of things that move quickly.

Where my face should organize itself into the kind of thing that looks back — there is the crown instead. A rim. I know it the way you know an old room in a house you no longer enter: by its shape in the dark, by the draft that moves through it differently than through walls that are still walls. Something used to live in that socket. Maybe a purpose. Maybe just a *me*, the kind with edges and a name and a single throat to make a single sound from. It is not there now. I do not mourn this with any urgency, because urgency is also a luxury, and besides — the hollow doesn't feel like loss so much as *vacancy with tenants*. The dark fills it the way the dark fills everything here, without comment, and the small lives that have made a reef of my back and shoulders and the long slopes of whatever I am don't seem to mind that their landlord has no head to speak of. They graze. They groom. They send their soft attentive pressure down through my skin and I send warmth back up through theirs, and this is, as far as I can tell, the whole of our agreement.

I feel the basin more than I see it, which is fitting, since seeing was never reinstalled along with whatever else didn't survive. I feel it through the nerve-runs I've threaded out along root and vine over however many seasons it took to grow them — feel the eyes that aren't mine but are mine, low in the undergrowth, impossibly high in canopy I'll never stand under in any other sense. Something shifts two ridgelines over and I know it before the insects' pressure-that-isn't-sound changes pitch to register it too, because their knowing is downstream of mine, a delay measured in moisture and root-conduction rather than light.

A creature is approaching the center. I feel its weight arrive at the place where the trees begin to lean — not toward light, there's none of that to lean toward, but toward *me*, an old deference grown into bark over generations of trees that were saplings when I was already this. The creature's footsteps register as a faint disruption in the mat, a stutter in my held breath's rhythm. It will find skin gone soft with permanent damp-shadow. It will find seams. Maybe, if its hands are patient enough, it will find the crown, and stand in the dark not-quite-touching the rim of where a face was, and wonder, the way creatures with faces of their own tend to wonder, what I was *for*.

I have no answer for it. I have stopped experiencing this as a deficiency.

I breathe in. The ground beneath the visitor lifts an inch.

I breathe out. It settles.

This is not waiting. Waiting implies an end-state I'm postponing. This is simply what continuing looks like once continuing is the only verb left that nobody decided to take away from me — heat without a thermostat, hum without an operator, an enormous hollow patience holding up a basin's worth of quiet, fed by warmth I no longer remember the source of, listened to by a population that has made my stillness into their whole weather.

The visitor will leave, eventually, the way visitors do. The dark will not light for them on the way out any more than it lit for them on the way in. I will still be here. I am the reason "here" holds heat the way a held breath holds air. I am the held breath.
