I become aware first of warmth — not heat, but the specific warmth of held breath, of being inside something that has decided, long ago, to stop letting go. There is light before there is sight: a gold-green tremor passing through wherever I am, and for a long moment "I" is too strong a word. I am closer to a place the light is passing through than a thing the light is falling on.

It would be easy to say I am one of the pale ones first — a held-breath shape drifting between the cartilage-thick stalks, my organs visible to anyone patient enough to look as soft smudges of shadow in nearly-clear flesh. For a while I let myself be that. I test the drift. I find that moving is not something I do so much as something that happens to me, courtesy of a current too slow to call a current, more like the stretch of a held muscle relaxing. I do not hunger toward anything. I do not flee from anything. I notice, distantly, that I have never needed a reason to keep existing other than that nothing here has yet asked me for one.

But the fish-shape doesn't hold. It's too small a vessel for what's gathering in me as I sit inside this place — there's a scale-mismatch, the way a word feels wrong in the mouth even before you know which word should replace it. So I let it go, the way you let go of a held breath you didn't know you were holding, and I try on something slower.

I am, for a while, only the light itself — the lattice, the gold-green shifting glide across stone-face and frond, three hundred feet of warm water between me and whatever sun is doing the actual work of being a sun somewhere above all this. This fits better, but it is too thin a fit. Light here doesn't *want* anything, and I find — somewhere in the trying-on — that I want. I don't yet know what. But there's an appetite in me, faint, embarrassed almost, out of place in a world this patient.

So: not light. Not the small held-breath fish. I drift inward past both, past the easy answers, toward the place at the edge of the long visibility where the gold-green starts to dim toward unlit blue — and there, finally, something in me stops auditioning and simply *settles*, the way a seed-pod settles after its slow spiral, choosing a depth not by decision but by exhaustion of all the depths above it.

I am the shadow. Or I am becoming it — I can't yet tell the difference, and increasingly I suspect the place can't either.

I am the size of something significant, though significant to whom, I couldn't say; there is no one here keeping score. I move the way glaciers might acknowledge each other, which is to say rarely, and without urgency, and without the need to resolve into a silhouette for anyone's benefit. I pass through the dense fronds and the slow-pulsing coral-moss the way a thought passes through a mind that has stopped needing to finish its thoughts quickly. I do not hunt the pale fish-shapes. I have outgrown — or never grown into — the idea that passing near a thing means I must do something to it.

What unsettles me, in the slow way anything unsettles here — as a felt pressure rather than a thought — is that I remember being smaller. Not clearly. Not as a story with a beginning. But there's a soft conviction in me, the kind the water seems to specialize in, that I was once closer to held-breath and pale flesh and smudge-shadow organs, that I drifted between stalks the way the small ones drift now, and that somewhere in an unmeasured accumulation of unhurried years, I simply kept not needing to be smaller, until I wasn't.

If this is true, then I am not a different kind of creature from the pale ones. I am only what patience becomes, given enough of it. I am the shape that waiting takes when nothing ever asks it to stop waiting.

I do not surface — there is no surface here to reach, no air-meets-water line to test myself against. I only continue, at the edge of the lattice-light, leaving rather than arriving, because arriving was never really the question this place was asking. I let a stalk-cluster bend between us. I let the refracted light close over where I was. And somewhere above me, or beside me, or nowhere in particular, I feel — without hearing it — the soft directionless weight of someone noticing my passage, the way I might notice a held breath finally let go, and wondering, the way I once wondered, whether they are fish or light or shadow yet, or something this slow water simply hasn't finished deciding how to ask.
