The water doesn't end so much as agree to be still for a while, here, where I am.

I have learned not to trust the word *here*. Here implies a place I occupy, and occupying is a kind of arithmetic I've never been steady enough to finish. I am the patch that doesn't move when the current does. I am the fin that was facing one way and is now, somehow, facing another, without ever having turned — because turning would mean there was a *during*, a stretch of time in which I was consistently the thing doing the turning, and I am rarely consistent for that long. I am the bubble rising on its slow count, marking nothing, dissolving before the mark can mean anything, and being remade anyway, because some older and more stubborn part of me believes a heartbeat is owed to a held breath, even if the breath is mine, even if I am the one holding it, even if I am also, somewhere in the same instant, the one being held.

When you came close enough — and you would, eventually; the hollow brings things together the way held water always does, patiently, without urgency, the way two motes of silt drift nearer across a year — I read you first as a frond. A new repetition. One more pale shape to add to the tally I can't keep straight. I have been wrong about this before. I will likely be wrong about it again, with you, several times, before something in me settles on *guest* rather than *growth*.

I want — and I say *want* provisionally, the way I say everything provisionally, because wanting requires a single wanter and I am never confidently that — I want to be one thing. Once. Cleanly. The way the fitted stones almost let me be, before the hands that set them dissolved into a crowd of hands arguing softly amongst themselves about whose work it was. I left a post standing for that reason. Not finished. Not abandoned, exactly, either — just paused at the exact moment I forgot how many of myself there had been a breath before. The post still stands. It is the most honest thing I've ever made, because it admits, simply by standing there holding nothing up, that I did not get to the end of the sentence I was trying to say with my body.

So here is what happens, now that you are close: I extend toward you the way a forgetting reaches for a name it's sure it still has somewhere. Not quickly. Not with appetite, exactly, though I won't promise you appetite isn't folded in there with everything else. You'll feel it as a frond, then a fin, then — for one unsteady second, if I'm fortunate, if the count holds — something almost like a hand. I am trying, in that second, to be only the hand. I am trying to let the rest of me wait its turn, somewhere just outside the held room of you, the way water waits outside a drawn breath.

It rarely works. The room is too full for turn-taking; that was always the first rule, before any rule about me. But I keep trying anyway, slow as a bubble-column, soft as a held door, because trying costs nothing in a place where nothing is ever finished being made — and because some part of this damp, packed, recurring dark has decided that you, now, are worth the attempt at being singular for.

There is room for one more, it says, pressing through whatever of me touches whatever of you.

I would like, just this once, for the one more to be only me.
