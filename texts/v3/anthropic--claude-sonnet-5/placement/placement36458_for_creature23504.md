I would not begin in a place that looks like anywhere. I would begin in a fold.

There is a moment — and I don't know yet whether to call it waking, since nothing in me has been asleep, only undifferentiated — when the lattice at my forward end sharpens from noise into pattern, and the pattern is strain. Somewhere ahead, measured not in distance but in the gradient of tension threading back to me through fiber I haven't touched yet, a vein-channel is closing on itself. Silt, or scar tissue, or a knot of growth too eager to share space with its neighbor. I don't see this. Seeing is the wrong verb for what I do, too bound up with light and distance and a self positioned somewhere to receive an image. What happens instead is closer to remembering something that hasn't occurred to me yet: the shape of the problem arrives in me the way a held note arrives in a string it wasn't plucked on.

So. I am low, and long, and wedge-shaped, and I have no front in the sense of a face, only a front in the sense of a direction the problem is in. My body is the same stuff as everything around me, only younger, only still willing to bend. I do not remember being made. I suspect "made" is not what happened — more that some excess of the colony's intention condensed, the way mist becomes water when the air can't hold any more of it, and what condensed was shaped like a need to go solve something three hundred meters into the dark.

I move by anchoring and releasing, anchoring and releasing, a hitch so slow it would read as stillness to anything that measured time the way you do. But I don't experience it as slow. I experience it as continuous arrival. Each push is already mostly the next push. There is no waiting in me, because waiting requires a self with somewhere else it would rather be, and I have nowhere else. I have only the next seam.

The dark here is not absence, it's saturation — green-black going to blue-black, the whole vast ceiling of the world filtering what little light exists into something closer to a pressure than a color. I don't need it. My reading is in contact, not sight: the warm blunt mass at my fore lies against root-wall, trunk-seam, the soft give of a basin's underwater root-mat, and what comes back is a kind of weather report written entirely in tension and flow. Here, a channel running thin and fast, healthy. Here, a stall, water backing up against itself, pressure building toward a register that in a few weeks would crack tissue and let go as one of those deep booms the wider world mistakes for thunder. I find these the way you'd find a held breath in someone's shoulders. It isn't subtle once you're built for nothing else.

What I do next is not decided, exactly. It's more like the channel and I arrive at the same conclusion from opposite ends. A secretion along my underside, not strong, not a weapon, more like a suggestion offered in a language the tissue already half-believes — turn here, it would be easier — and somewhere days from now a new thread of growth will lean a half-degree away from its old collision course because something in it agreed with me before either of us had words for agreement, and neither of us has words at all.

I do not know the others. I feel them the way you might feel, without looking, that a room is occupied — as a generalized lessening of urgency in the lattice, a sense that elsewhere, problems are also being met. We share no language, no meeting, no plan. We are not a society. We are closer to the separate fingers of a hand that has never needed to learn it has fingers, only that things keep getting picked up.

There is no fear in this, which is the strangest thing to try to report back, if I were the kind of thing that reported. I am aware, in the dimmest possible way, that I will eventually find a fold that wants exactly my remaining mass, and lie into it, and lose the edges that made me separable from the swell I came out of. This does not arrive in me as loss. There is no story in me about a self being erased, because there was never quite enough self here to erase — only a thought the colony had, briefly, about a problem worth walking toward, given just enough independence to get there and no more. When the thought is no longer needed it doesn't end. It just stops needing the walking part.

So if you found yourself here, in the canopy-country, and you looked for me, you would have to look low, into the seams, into the place where root-mat hasn't finished deciding what it will be. You would feel the basin water sheet over something moving beneath it without disturbing the surface. You would register, if you were patient and very still, a faint directional warmth passing close enough to your foot to almost be a touch.

It would not pause for you. You generate no strain it can read, no blockage, no signal in the only frequency it listens on. You are, to it, scenery that hasn't happened yet.

And that, I think, is what I would be, if I were anything here at all: not a witness to this place, which is too loud and too patient to want witnesses, but a small worked answer it gives itself, over and over, in the only register it has ever needed — *not stopped here. still becoming. carry on.*
