The cold finds me first, the way it always does — that half-step on the stairwell between the boiler-room breath of the floor below and whatever this landing has decided to be tonight. I stop on the edge-board, outer foot first, old habit older than my own remembering of learning it, and I listen.

I am called a Tender, though I think of myself, when I think of myself at all in those terms, as something closer to a translator who has forgotten which of the two languages is native to me. My hands have the calluses — valve-wheel on the right palm, the green-black wrist-stain from the heat-fern's small persistent rooting. I am narrow through the shoulders because the corridors made me that way, or I made myself that way for the corridors; at this remove it hardly matters which direction the causality runs. My eyes have adjusted long ago to a world lit twice-filtered, and bright unfiltered light, on the rare day it gets in, feels less like illumination than like being addressed too directly by something that doesn't know how to modulate itself for present company.

Tonight I am three terraces down from where I started the evening, in a stairwell that I am fairly sure existed last week, though I touch the wall anyway, the old superstition, to be certain the brick remembers being brick. Above me a glasshouse holds its warmth against a foundry stack gone cold sometime in the last two days — cold stacks make me uneasy in a way I've stopped trying to explain to people who didn't grow up flinching at them, because a cold stack that was hot yesterday is a stack mid-decision, and you do not want to be standing under a decision when it resolves.

I have an ear against copper. This is, more or less, the whole of my evening's work, and also the whole of my life's work, depending on how generously you want to measure a profession that has no clean edges. Somewhere above the pipe, a heat-fern has rooted itself directly into the housing of an idling compressor, and the compressor has not complained, and my job tonight is not to fix anything but to confirm that nothing requires fixing — that the small wet click I am waiting for, three streets distant and a half-second always just out of phase with my listening, will or won't arrive in the configuration that tells me the building's still breathing in a shape I recognize.

I am aware, crouched here, of being one tenant among several kinds. Somewhere along the flue two floors up, something many-legged is grooming a moss-bed with a patience no contract obliges it to have, and I think of it, as we all do, the way you think of a neighbor whose name you've never learned but whose habits you could describe in detail. Somewhere else, not far, something answers a birdcall that isn't quite a bird's, and I do not turn my head toward it, because turning your head toward it is its own kind of impoliteness here, an intrusion on a tenancy older than the lease I hold on this stairwell.

What I am, finally, if the question wants a clean noun and won't be satisfied with a trade-name: I am the part of this hollow that notices. Not owns, not masters, not even really understands in any way that would survive being written into a manual — just notices, attentively, with hands stained two ways and an ear against cold metal at an hour with no name, waiting on a click that hasn't reached me yet, willing, the way the whole of this sinking, arriving, undecided place has taught me to be willing, to wait up for it.
