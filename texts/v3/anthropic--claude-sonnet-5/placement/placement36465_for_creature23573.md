I come to first as temperature — a coolness against my left side that the rest of me doesn't share, the particular cool of rock that has never been asked to hold the day's heat for long because the day's heat never asks for much. That's the ridge. I am leaning against the ridge, or built into it, or both; the wall behind my shoulders is stone with the fracture-shine still on it, and I have the specific, bone-deep knowledge of how many years it's held that shine, because I am the one who would know.

My hands are the second thing. They are not still. Even now, half-attending to whatever this is — waking, arriving, becoming noticed — my right hand is making small economical shapes against my thigh, a private rehearsal, the kind of thing a body does when a language has gotten down into the muscle below the place where you'd have to think about it. Three short pulses, a hold, a slow color I don't have a name for in this telling but whose *meaning* I have, immediately, completely: *nothing wrong, just checking, go back to sleep.* I have said this exact sequence so many times across so many years that my hand knows it the way a heart knows a beat.

So: not a stranger here. Not someone arriving by boat or causeway with the wide eyes of the visitor this place apparently collects. I am someone the place has already finished startling.

The sound arrives next, because the sound here never waits politely for you to be ready. It comes in as a stack, the way it's always described to outsiders and never quite landed on by the describing — the wet percussive hush of the moss-ripple crossing ground somewhere below the ridge, the papery roar of a flock tightening its gyre off to the south, and under both, closer, more mine, a low resonant moan that I feel in the floor of my chest before I hear it. The dome. My dome — not owned, never owned, but mine the way a held breath is yours while you're holding it.

That settles it. I am a listener.

I open my eyes — or I notice that they're open, and have been, adjusted long ago to a light that has no true edges to adjust to — and the world resolves into the particular view I must have looked at ten thousand mornings: the dome itself, a half-kilometer off across fern-mat and dust-flat, its skin in the slow middle of one of its color-cycles, going from a clean cold violet toward something warmer, something the texture of an old bruise just starting to heal, though it will not finish that transition for hours yet and I have learned not to wait for it to finish anything quickly. Around its base, where the warm vapor vents, a loose gyre of membrane-flock rides the thermal, dipping low enough that their bronze undersides catch the light and throw it back duller than they think it does. They've been there since before I woke. They'll be there after.

I am not young. I know this without checking, the way you know the weight of a tool you've carried for decades — in the slight permanent ache across my shoulders from years of the listener's particular posture, half-turned toward the dome the way you'd half-turn toward someone mid-sentence so as not to miss the rest of it. My skin has the matte Reach-pale and the wind-flush in the cheeks that marks everyone born to this haze and never burned clean of it. My eyes do the squint without my asking them to.

What I am for — and I feel this less as a fact recited and more as a shape my whole day is built around, the way a room is built around a hearth — is proximity. I stay near. Three generations of my kin-line have stayed near this dome specifically, recording its moods in a ledger of light-signal and whispered note that nobody outside the lineage could read quickly, watching the venting cycle for the small deviations that mean something is coming due — a shift in the flock's grazing thermal, a coming bloom in the fern-mat that the moss-ripple will reroute itself to feed, once, every few years, a correction from the dome itself, arriving without explanation, adjusting some valve or rhythm I do not understand and have stopped requiring myself to. I do not know what the dome is. I know, with total unembarrassed confidence, how it has been feeling lately.

Somewhere behind me, inside the rock, someone — kin, apprentice, the next listener whether they've accepted that yet or not — is sleeping in a room where the rule is whisper-only, where my raised voice would register as something close to violence. I will go back to that room eventually. But right now I am here, at the edge of the ridge, hand still idly signing *nothing wrong* to no one, watching a half-kilometer of open damp lush nothing between myself and a structure older than any memory my people kept in stone, and I am doing the only job this place has ever really asked of anyone:

I am staying close to something vast, and learning, patiently, year over year, what it is in the mood to do next.
