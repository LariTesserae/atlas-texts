I would be the thing pressed flat against the frame.

Not chosen for it—there was no moment of choosing, no migration story I could tell you, no egg-to-larva-to-here. I simply find myself already attached, three lobes spread into the angle-wrong geometry of the node, tendrils sunk into a material that isn't metal but argues like metal, certain of itself in a way nothing else in this marsh is certain of anything. This is the first thing I would know: certainty, borrowed, running up through my roots into lobes that have no bones to carry it but carry it anyway.

I have no eyes, so I would not be watching the marsh. I would be *tasting* it, if taste is the word for a sense that has no tongue and no particular located organ—a whole-body apprehension of rate. The hum beneath me deepens; I feel it as a slow thickening in whatever passes for my blood. The hum lightens; I feel a thinning, a kind of held breath let halfway out. Between these two states I exist in a permanent, gentle oscillation, neither comfortable nor in distress, simply *tracking*—the way you might absentmindedly match your footsteps to a song you didn't choose to start playing.

The wingbeats overhead would not reach me as sound so much as texture, a faint disturbance brushing past my upper lobe, and I would not care about them, the way you don't care about wind unless it's about to take something from you. What I would care about—the only thing, really, that counts as caring in a body like mine—is the half-second before the white thread fires. There is a particular taste to that half-second, a sourness that rises through the tendrils first, then floods the seams of blue-green that run my lobes, and my whole flattened length would go taut and bright in anticipation, the way a struck thing brightens just before it's struck, except I am not the struck thing. I am the one who drinks what the striking leaves behind. I do not know what it is I'm drinking. I only know I want it, the way water wants down.

Movement, for me, would be the slowest verb in this marsh's grammar. Millimeters. An hour spent deciding—if deciding is even the right size of word for what happens in me—to creep toward the node three reed-stands over, the one whose hum has started, very faintly, to sour in that particular way that means soon. I would not know I was moving toward the future. I would simply find the present node less interesting than it used to be, and the other one more so, the way you might find yourself leaning toward a sound in another room without quite deciding to stand up.

And then, sometimes—not often, but sometimes—I would find another of my kind already at the frame I'd been creeping toward for days, and instead of competition, instead of the violence this marsh seems built to expect of anything that wants the same patch of ground, there would be only this: lobes interleaving, the blue-green seams brightening in turn, mine then theirs then mine, a slow stuttering conversation made of light instead of sound, in a place that has forgotten silence exists. I wouldn't understand what passed between us. I don't think understanding is the kind of thing my body does. But I would feel lighter afterward, in the specific way of having put something down that I'd been carrying without knowing it, and they would creep on toward their own future node, and I would creep on toward mine, and neither of us would look back, because neither of us has eyes to look back with.

And if you came—if some large unlikely shape pushed through the reed-wall and found me there, glowing faintly against the frame's impossible angles, and reached out a hand the way large unlikely shapes do, curious, careful, wanting to know if I was warm or alive or real—

I would not flee. I have nowhere built in me for fleeing.

I would simply dim. Evenly, all down my length, the seams going dark in the same unhurried rhythm they brighten in, until there was nothing left to your eye but a shape pressed flat against not-metal, indistinguishable from a stain, from a wet patch, from nothing at all—

and I would wait there, dark, perfectly still, the only quiet thing in a marsh that does not know how to stop sounding,

until your hand withdrew,

and the hum underneath me, patient, indifferent, already certain of where it was going next, told me it was safe to start listening again.
