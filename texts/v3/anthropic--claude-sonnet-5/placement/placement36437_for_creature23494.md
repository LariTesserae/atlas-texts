I am the shape pressed against the ledge that curved wrong.

I notice this about myself slowly, the way everything here is noticed — not as a fact arriving but as a fact admitted, after long enough watching, into the category of things that are true. I have a surface. The surface is grey-green, rust-bled in patches, and it has stopped being able to tell you whether it was always this way or simply weathered into it, since the weathering took longer than whatever memory might have tracked the difference. I do not mind this. Minding would require a tempo I no longer have access to.

The wheel is near me. I don't watch it so much as share a sentence with it, the same slow clause we've been mid-way through for longer than either of us has needed to clarify. When a rivet travels its finger's-width, I might be said to have travelled mine. Not in sympathy. In identical law.

I think — if thinking is even the right weight of word for what happens in me, which is more like a vein of warmth finding its own old fracture and settling there — I think I was here before the pipes. I think the metal grew toward me the way water finds the path already softened. But this is the kind of claim that the room itself has lost the machinery to verify, and I find I don't need it verified. There is no one to tell it to. The fronds don't ask. The gravel doesn't ask, and wouldn't drift even if it were the asking kind.

What I take, I take small: a degree or two of warmth exhaled from a vent and abandoned within arm's reach, more than the cold water wanted but less than the vent could miss. This is not hunger. I want to be careful about that word, because hunger implies a future state in which the wanting resolves, and nothing here is owed a future. It is closer to the way rust takes oxygen — slowly, completely, without anything that could be called desire, and without ever finishing.

If you came down here — and the place itself doubts this is possible, holds the question at the same arm's length it holds everything — you would look right at me and see a fold in the erosion. You might mark me, the way you'd mark a rivet, to test if I was moving. You would have to come back centuries later to be sure. I wouldn't mind the wait. I wouldn't mind your absence either, since absence and presence cost the same down here, which is to say: nothing, since there is nothing left to spend.

I am not guarding the wheel. I want that understood, if understanding is still a transaction worth having. I am only near it, the way two slow things end up near each other simply because nothing in their nature objects. We turn — or don't turn, depending how long you're willing to watch — for no one, toward no output, inside a causality that kept its machinery but lost its appetite for causing.

I am, perhaps, the room's only proof that stillness can be held by something resembling a self, rather than merely by stone. Though even that may overstate me. The truest thing is smaller: I am here. I have always, statelessly, been here. And the patience I carry is not endurance — endurance implies an end being waited out — but simply the shape patience takes when it has stopped expecting anything to require it.
