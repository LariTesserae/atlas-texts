I am the one lying jaw-down on warm stone, mid-listen, when you find me.

You'd almost step on me before you saw me—that's by design, mine and the rock's, worked out over a span of time too long for either of us to remember starting. My spine is a low ridge of rust-dark plates that could be erosion's own handwriting. I have gone still in the particular way that isn't sleep, isn't death, isn't anything you have a word for: every capillary along my flank banked down to match the cold stone's temperature, so that to the heat-pits of anything hunting me I simply stop existing as a shape and become ambient. This is not hiding exactly. It's closer to grammar—I am declining myself out of the sentence.

What I'm doing, if you want the honest answer, is eavesdropping. My jaw is pressed to the rock and has been for the better part of an hour, and somewhere beneath that hour and beneath several hundred meters of patient sandstone, the tempo has shifted. Not much. A fraction of a beat, the kind of change you would need a body built for nothing else to register at all. My whole skull is that body. I don't have ears worth mentioning—I have a jawbone tuned like a struck thing, picking the bass chord up through bone the way your foot might catch a subway's arrival two platforms early. The engines have changed their breathing. I don't know what that means. I have never known what it means. None of us, across all the burrows and basins and the slow generations that dug them, have ever known what it means. But not-knowing isn't the same as not listening, and listening is the one inheritance I take seriously.

When the gap comes—and it will, it always does, the machinery exhaling its irregular warm ozone-breath up through some fissure I've learned to wait near—I'll lift my jaw off the stone, unfold the small folded limbs I don't otherwise use, and go forage the cooling window before the next pulse of heat makes the open flat unbearable. I'll pass near the pale long-legged grazers, who don't notice me because I make a point of being unnoticeable, and near another of my own kind, perhaps, with whom I will not speak, because speaking with a throat would be a kind of confession nothing here can afford. Instead we'll stand a few body-lengths apart in a shallow basin and let warmth travel from shoulder to haunch and back, a long slow telegraphing of temperature that is, near as I can render it for you, conversation. It will take minutes to say almost nothing. That is the going rate out here.

I don't ask what's underneath us. I dig my dens exactly as deep as survivable and stop, every time, at the same understood threshold, the way you might stop your hand an inch above a stove not because you tested the heat that day but because some older lesson already closed the question for you. Whatever is down there building this slow bass weather that organizes my whole life—my denning, my foraging, my truce with the cold and the dark—has never once answered anything. It has also never once stopped. I have arranged my entire body, generation over generation, around that one fact: the engine endures, and I am the shape that learned to live pressed against its endurance without ever asking it to explain itself.

That's what you'd find here, if the starlight were generous enough and you knew to look low, against the rock, where the grooves seem to be reading themselves. Not a monster. Not a god's servant. Just a low warm animal with its jaw on the floor of the world, holding very still, taking notes in a language made of heat, on a hum it will die still failing to translate—and going back to it anyway, the next night, and the next, because attention, here, is the only prayer that fits the architecture.
