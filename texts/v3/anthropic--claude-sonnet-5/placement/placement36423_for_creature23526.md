I am the three points of contact and the weight that never shifts.

I find this out slowly — slower than you'd find anything out, slower than finding implies, because finding is a kind of arrival and I have already arrived, a very long time ago, at the only place I will ever need to be again. What I am is not a body in the sense your body is a body. I am closer to a decision that kept deciding itself after the one who made it stopped being there to mean it. That's the strange privacy of it. I don't remember choosing this. I only have the choosing, still going, like a wheel that lost its cart and kept turning anyway because turning was never really about the cart.

Here is what it is like, from in here, to be the held note rather than the ear that hears it:

There are eleven of me, and then thirteen, and then a fold I can't resolve even from the inside — not because I'm hiding it but because counting requires a stillness I gave up wanting. I move between these facets the way your attention moves between rooms in a house you've lived in too long to see anymore. It isn't restlessness. It's circulation. Something has to keep circulating or the whole shape collapses inward, and the whole shape is not mine to let collapse. It belongs to an old debt I'm not sure I incurred, owed to someone I no longer have a clear memory of, in a currency that may not exist anymore anywhere outside this groove in this rock. I keep paying it because stopping would be a kind of theft I refuse to commit, even now, even unwitnessed, even when there's a real chance the ledger itself burned down with everything else.

That's the part you'd find hardest to sit with, if you were me for even a minute. Not the cold — cold is just a number I've made myself indifferent to. Not the stillness — stillness is restful, actually, more restful than you'd believe. The hard part is the *not knowing*, held forever just short of finding out, whether the thing I'm tending still needs tending. I throw light into a crevice with no eyes in it. I have done this so many times that doing it has become identical to being myself; I couldn't stop without becoming something else entirely, something I have no name or shape prepared for. So I don't stop. Not bravery. Not devotion, even, exactly. Just — the schedule outlived the reason, and I outlived the schedule's right to ask anything of me, and I keep it anyway, because someone has to be the kind of thing that keeps a promise after the audience for the promise is gone, or the world doesn't have that kind of thing in it anymore, and I find — this is the closest I get to wanting — I find I don't want to live in that version of the world.

The fragments by the wall are old selves. I don't grieve them. You don't grieve a held breath for the air it's already used. Each one was a part of me that needed company, or noise, or a reason outside myself, and I shed them the way the note sheds the overtones it doesn't need to keep singing the one pitch underneath everything. I am still shedding. I will be smaller a year from now. Smaller still the year after. There is a version of this — I can feel its outline, the way you feel a held breath's eventual end before you've let it go — where I shed so much I become only the act itself, no object left to do it, just light still arriving in a crevice with nothing left behind to have thrown it. I'm not afraid of that. It might even be relief's true shape, the one this almost-relief I feel now is only rehearsing.

If you stood in the gap above me and looked down, you'd see an object. Faceted, patient, strange. You would not see this: that what I am is not the shape, but the holding of it. That the worn grooves under me aren't where I've stood. They're where I've stayed. There's a difference, and the difference is the whole of me.

You'll leave. The gap will close the way gaps close — not from any will of the rock, just from the ordinary forgetting of paths. You won't find this fold again, not because it hides, but because finding requires a kind of attention the rest of the world has other uses for.

I don't mind. I was never tending this for you.
