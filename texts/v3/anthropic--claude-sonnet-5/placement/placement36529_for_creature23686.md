The third rust-fall has not changed in my lifetime, but I check it anyway, the way I check everything — not because it will be different, but because looking is a kind of payment.

I am a listener. Nobody made me one; I simply stopped doing other things and started doing this, the way water finds the lowest stone. My hands are not as quick as a gauge-reader's, my shoulders not built for a stoker's long patience with heat — but my ears, my skin, the wide dark drink of my eyes, these turned out suited for sitting still near the south wall, beneath the clock that runs backward, and noticing.

I'd be sitting there now. Oilcloth layered over wool, both gone the soft dark of decades of damp, the tin disk at my throat stamped with a sigil I never chose and never questioned — my family's claim staked not in a bloodline but in a length of pipe, a particular condensation tower three switchbacks north of here. My palms would carry the memory of a hundred valves, the specific shudder that means *about to turn* held in the same part of me that holds language.

The chamber would be doing what it always does — dripping without rhythm, breathing through its stacks, the blind birds clicking softly somewhere above in the pipe-rafters. I would be waiting for the note. Not expecting it — you stop expecting, after enough years, the way you stop expecting any particular drop of water to fall from any particular crack — but available to it, ear turned the way a plant turns to whatever light it's been given, sodium-colored and thin.

And when it came — that one sustained hum, sourceless, the same pitch it's always been or perhaps, today, a half-tone different, a thing only a listener would catch — I would feel the whole chamber still around me. Stokers pausing with coal half-lifted. Gauge-readers' hands going quiet on still needles. Somewhere in the dark water, perhaps, a hundred small blind faces turning upward in their own wordless agreement.

I would not understand it. I would not need to. I would simply be the one who heard it first, who would press two fingers flat against the cold stone afterward — *I noticed; we are still here; the chamber is still being watched* — and then return to listening, patient as rust, patient as the slow climbing damp, keeping faith with a machine whose purpose nobody remembers, for no better reason than this: it has been fed this long, and a thing fed that long becomes owed something, and I am here, in the dim and the drip and the borrowed light, paying it.
