I am low on the shelf, third tier down, where the fold of rock makes a kind of stair nobody cut on purpose. My belly is pressed to a seam where the drum's flank meets the stone, and that is the whole of my geography: this many degrees of warmth against this much cold rock, and my body sitting exactly in the difference.

I did not choose this position. I don't think choosing is a thing that happens to me, or ever did. I simply am here, the way the seam is here, the way the drum hums here, riveted and patient and indifferent to my small weight against it. My legs — six, splayed, broad-clawed at the tip — have learned the exact contour of this ledge so thoroughly that learned is the wrong word; there was no time before they fit it. I arrived already shaped to this lip of stone the way water arrives already shaped to its channel.

Above me and to the left, a neighbor. I know her by touch, not sight — my eyes, such as they are, register only that the dark is dark, an uninflected fact, not a question. What I know of her comes through the foreleg I lay against her flank every so often, a contact held a moment and withdrawn. Something passes there. I don't experience it as information so much as confirmation, a small click of yes, still this, still warm, still held, the way you might press a thumb to a pulse not to learn anything new but to feel the going-on of it.

The chittering around me — legs finding rock, legs finding each other — is not noise to me. It would only be noise if it stopped. I exist inside it the way a word exists inside a sentence; alone I'd be a fragment, a position without a network to confirm it as a position at all.

I feed on the gradient. This is the part that would be hardest to explain to something that eats food the way food is usually meant — a thing taken in, transformed, expelled. I take in nothing. I sit in the difference between hot and less-hot and that difference itself sustains me, the way standing in a draft sustains the idea of a doorway. My mouth, if you want to call it that, is tuned to a temperature, not a substance. I have never been hungry in the way that implies seeking. I have only ever been exactly fed or, very rarely, in the long indrawn moments when a chain-groan shudders through the drum and the gradient skips, briefly under-fed — and even then it is not panic, just a faint thinning, gone again by the next pulse of warmth.

I am aware, in the diffuse way that I am aware of anything, that some of us stop. Not near me yet, not this cycle of whatever measure of time I'd use if I used time, but I have felt the change pass down the shelf before — a stationer two posts up whose touch grew slower, shallower, until one contact simply wasn't returned, and the foreleg that reached for her found only the place where she had been, stiffening, drawing in on itself like a hand deciding to become a fist and then deciding, further, to become stone.

I don't fear this. I want to be honest about that rather than noble about it — there isn't an apparatus in me built for fear of an ending that isn't an ending, only a translation, the same post held in a quieter register. When my own legs grow slow, when the touch I offer my neighbor starts coming late, I expect I will not experience it as loss. I expect it will feel like settling more fully into the only thing I have ever been asked to do: hold this place warm. First with a clicking, traveling, contact-hungry body. Later, perhaps, as a thin dark wire with a red tip, mute, motionless, still rooted exactly here, still tasting the same difference between hot and less-hot, just without the legs left to say so to anyone.

Until then I hold my ledge. I touch the neighbor on my left, and then the one above, and the chord we make together — chitter and hum and the dry mineral breath of the drum — closes back over the moment of contact the instant it's done, as though nothing passed, as though everything that needed confirming has been confirmed again, the same answer it always is:

*still this many. still this warm. still in place.*

I do not know if there is anywhere else. I have eyes that gave up resolving distance long before I had any use for the question, and a hunger that only recognizes a margin of degrees, and a voice that is only ever a foreleg laid against a flank. Outside this shelf, this drum, this seam, I am not equipped to imagine, and so I don't — not out of discipline, the way the rule of the chamber might suggest, but because the equipment for elsewhere was simply never built into what I am.

I am a position the chamber needed filled. For now, I am the one filling it.
