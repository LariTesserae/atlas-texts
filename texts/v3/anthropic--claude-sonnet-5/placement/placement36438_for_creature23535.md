The mist sits at my knees before I've thought to notice it, and that's the first true thing about being here: noticing comes second. Body knows the floor before mind does — weight riding loose over a sphagnum give that might hold, might not, the old reflex worn so deep it doesn't feel like vigilance anymore, just walking.

I am called, if called at all, by a sound rather than a name — something low in my own throat that the frog-chorus answers two notes later, a habit my mother's mother apparently started as a joke and the marsh took seriously and never let go of. I tend the third house from the elder-house, the one that molted twice last dry season and hasn't settled since, still a little raw-pale at the eaves where new growth hasn't caught its color yet. I check it mornings. Not out of duty exactly — duty implies the house might fail to deserve it, and that's not a thought that survives long out here. You tend because the alternative is waking up inside a wall that decided, unattended, to become something stranger than a wall.

My hands know things my mouth has never had to say: the particular cool give of a vine that's about to make a decision versus one that's only resting; the difference between a peristaltic ripple that means *narrowing, move through* and one that means *molt coming, get the children out of the south room.* I weave floating beds in the slow hours, breath by breath into hollow reed, a skill that took me four wet seasons to stop fighting and one to finally trust. My grandmother's lungs are in those reeds, in a manner of speaking. So are mine, now, for whoever comes after.

I don't know what the seedpod-hum is, underneath the molars, underneath everything. I have stopped asking, the way you stop asking why your own pulse keeps time. I know the shapes under the black water are old and patient and not interested in me, and that this not-interest is itself a kind of treaty, the same wordless one I have with the house, with the moss, with the frogs who may or may not be answering a song my family only thinks it composed first.

What I am, if you want it simply: someone the Hollow has had long enough to start writing into itself. Marbled skin at the wrists already, faint green-bronze, the bruise that became a feature. Not the author of this place. Not even really its keeper, if keeping implies control. Just the audience close enough to the stage to occasionally lean on a swinging door — to make sure, when it settles, it still opens onto something I can call evening, can call home, can call, for now, enough.
