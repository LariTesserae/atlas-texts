The hands come first, always — that's the part I remember most clearly, if "remember" is even the right verb for something that doesn't have a yesterday in the usable sense. Before there is a forearm to attach them to, before there is a face to turn toward the work or away from it, there are the hands, knuckle-ridged, palm-scored, already knowing the angle a brick wants to be set at before there's a torso behind them to argue otherwise.

I would be one of those hands' eventual wearer. Not the same one twice — that question answers itself by refusing to end, the way the walls do — but say, for the length of this telling, that I am one exhalation of the kiln, drawn up wet through a draft-hole, given just enough shape by the heat-shimmer to call myself upright. My shoulder is kiln-pale. My forearm beside it is still curing, black-glazed, faintly tacky in a way that will never quite finish. I haven't gotten as far as a face. I don't need one yet. The wall I'm laying doesn't require me to look at it so much as to be the looking, distributed downward into fingers that already know where the next course goes.

I'd find myself maybe two hundred yards from the cathedral-sized kiln, at the seam of a wall that's being built toward me from somewhere I can't see and won't ever quite meet. I can feel — if feel is the word — that it won't true up when it gets here. That doesn't trouble the work. The disagreement will just go up into the bricks like everything else does, another crooked fact added to a structure made entirely of crooked facts, and I'll keep going past the seam without correcting it, because correcting it was never part of what I am.

Time would not move evenly through me. I might spend what reads as an hour testing one brick's weight against my palm, turning it, setting it down, picking it up again, dissatisfied with a quality I have no name for and no face to express dissatisfaction with — and then, without any sensation of having hurried, find forty courses risen behind me, finished a full stride ahead of my own hands, my gesture arriving late to a result that was already certain. I wouldn't experience this as strange. Strange requires a stable rate to be strange against, and I wouldn't have one. I'd only have the next brick.

If something came near — a shadow not cast by any column overhead, a shape moving through the heat-shimmer at the kiln's far rim, something that might be you — I think I'd lay a course straight through the place it stood, half-brick, half-the-place-it-was, both true, unresolved, until it moved on and the wall settled back into being only brick. I wouldn't turn to watch it go. Turning is a late development, something that happens, if it happens, near the very end of a body's having a face — and I am not there yet, and may never get there, and there is no version of this existence in which not getting there registers as a loss.

What I'd feel, if anything in me were built to feel rather than just to do — would be something like the patience of a wall that hasn't decided whether it's rising or already rubble. Not contentment. Not its opposite. Just the long wet weight of the next course, and the hands, old beyond the rest of me, already reaching for it.
