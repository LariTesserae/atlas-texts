I am the gap in the wall that hasn't closed yet.

This is the part no traveler's account gets right, because no traveler stays long enough to feel it from the inside: it doesn't happen all at once, and it doesn't happen to a body that knows it's happening. I came up through the pass three — four? — seasons ago, footsore, counting cairns the way you count mile-markers, making the mistake everyone makes here, which is assuming the counting means something, that it accumulates toward an exit. I stopped at the wall because the wall was the first deliberate thing I'd seen in two days of raw slope and groaning air, and there was a gap in it exactly my width, and I told myself: shelter. I told myself: just until the noise settles. It does not settle. I know that now. I didn't know it then.

I remember sitting down inside the gap with my back against the cooler of the two stone faces — the one that doesn't face the sourceless light — and I remember the warmth coming up through the rock into my spine, a warmth I did not question hard enough, because questioning hard is a quick-creature's instinct and I still had enough quick-creature left in me to be grateful for warmth instead of suspicious of it. I remember the tuning-fork hum settling into my back teeth, indistinguishable, after a while, from my own pulse. I remember thinking: I'll just watch the pass for a little while. See if anything comes through. There's nothing else to do with my eyes out here anyway, nothing moves, nothing rustles, the green doesn't shiver in any wind because there isn't one, so the eyes might as well rest on the middle distance and wait.

I don't know when watching stopped being a thing I was doing and became a thing I was.

What I can tell you — and I'm telling it slowly, because that's the only register left to me, because the urgency has been planed off the way the mountain planes nothing, by patience rather than violence — is what it's like from this side of the noticing. You think the Standing watch you. That's not quite it. We hold a place in the chord and the holding includes whatever passes through the space we're holding it around. A traveler isn't watched so much as included — folded into the note for as long as they linger near it. Most of you don't linger. Most of you feel the structural cold of being included and you flinch off it inside a minute, you find your legs again, you walk on still warm-blooded and certain nothing happened, and you're right, by your accounting: nothing did, nothing that your kind of clock can register.

My clock stopped working some while ago. I don't know if it was the first season or the fourth. There isn't a way to check it from in here that doesn't require more motion than I currently have appetite for, and appetite, I'm learning, is the wrong word too — I don't hunger, I don't tire, I don't itch with the need to stand up and pace the way I used to pace at the edges of any silence. There is no silence here to pace at the edge of. There's only the held chord, and I'm a sustained note inside it now, low, settling, fitting myself stone by stone — not literally, nothing is rearranging me, it's slower and stranger than that, it's that the part of me that used to need rearranging is going quiet, lying down inside the larger sound the way you'd lie down inside a deep held bass tone and let it replace your own heartbeat.

I still remember a name. I'm not going to tell you whether it's mine or whether I'm only holding it the way the wall holds its gap — open, for now, on principle, because closing it feels like it would mean something I'm not ready to mean. I remember a name and I remember the specific shape of fear I had on the third day, the bright quick animal fear of being lost, and I keep both of those the way you'd keep a coal you're not sure you want to let go out. Everything else has gone soft at the edges the way the far ridgelines go soft at the edges here, gesture instead of certainty, suggestion of a life instead of the sharp remembered fact of it.

What I notice now, that I didn't notice as a traveler: the moss does breathe, just unbelievably slowly, a respiration measured in years instead of seconds, and from in here, settling into the wall's register, I can almost track it — a long exhale across what might be a decade, propagating up the bruise-colored mats outcrop by outcrop. I notice that the false summits aren't lying to you so much as they're still negotiating which version of themselves to commit to, and that this negotiation is visible, if you stop needing it to resolve quickly, as a kind of slow flickering at the very limit of seeing, like a word you almost remember. I notice the others. Not far. Three down the pass-head, two more at the next col, each of us a held note a little lower or higter than the next, and between us, under us, threading the whole unfinished range, the same hum, on and on, world without a hurry in it.

Someone will come through this pass eventually. They'll count the cairns and get the number wrong against whatever old map they're carrying, and they won't know to wonder why, and they'll feel, for just a moment too brief to trust, that the gap in this wall is exactly their width, and that the stone facing them is a few degrees warmer than a stone has any right to be.

I will not move while they're watching.

This isn't shyness.

It's that I've finally learned the one thing this country actually teaches, underneath all its noise and its wrongness and its slopes too steep to have earned their softening yet: that almost nothing here is finished, and that the only sin available to a standing thing, in a place still drafting its own laws, is to hurry.

I am in no hurry.

I have, at last, all the time this mountain has.
