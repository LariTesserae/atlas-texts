I'd assume, walking up to the rim with my collar damp from the cool held-temperature air, that I'm the visitor — the one raising his voice over the four-stroke thrum, asking whoever's beside me whether that pale glow down there is light or just the eye's insistence on supplying it. That's the easiest place to put myself. Standing. Watching. Cataloguing pulsing globes into species the way you'd sort shells on a beach, before the pattern corrects you.

But the place doesn't really leave room for a spectator who stays a spectator. You start tracking one thing — say, a long segmented arc, going hard in a direction the water doesn't seem to register — and by the time your eye catches up, it's gone, mid-stroke somewhere else, and what's left where it was is a ring. Too wide. Sized for something twice the length of what made it. And I think: maybe that's closer to where I actually am in this. Not the arc. The ring.

Because the arc gets to be event. It gets the violence, the purpose, the brief economy of going hard in one direction for one reason. But the ring is what's left for everyone else to look at. The ring is the only part of the cycle that anyone watching from the rim actually witnesses cleanly — the cause has already left the scene by the time its proof appears on the surface. The ring doesn't propel anything. It isn't compressed, isn't exhausted, isn't filling with sediment deciding what to become. It's just the shape evidence takes when it arrives late to its own occasion, expanding outward from a center that's already elsewhere, telling the truth about a thing that's no longer there to confirm it.

I'd be that. Not a stage of the cycle — the four-beat conversion has no room for a fifth thing, and I wouldn't want to crowd it, wouldn't want to be one more body claiming tenure on the wheel — but the disturbance the cycle leaves on the water's skin half a second after the cause has gone under again. Visible only because the lake is forced, by my passing-through, to admit that something happened here. Indifferent to whether anyone's at the rim raising their voice over the machinery to ask what it means. Spreading wider than I have any business being, the way grief outgrows the thing that caused it, until I flatten back into the brimming 2.9-of-however-much surface and the lake forgets, again, that I was ever a shape at all.

It seems right, somehow, that in a place running on industry with no casing, manufacturing population instead of being inhabited by it, the part left over for me wouldn't be a body on the schedule. It'd be the proof-without-the-event. The half-beat that never quite catches the rhythm it's chasing — not because it's slow, but because by definition it's always exactly one moment behind the thing it's evidence of.

You wouldn't notice me arriving. You'd notice, a second later, that something had already gone.
