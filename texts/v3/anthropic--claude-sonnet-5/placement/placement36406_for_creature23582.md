I come up through the lower terraces first, where the cloud-mass is thickest, and for a long while I am only weather to myself — a temperature, a wetness, an ear filling with the roar until the roar stops being a thing heard and becomes the shape of the inside of my own skull. I look for a self the way you'd look for a landmark in fog: by trying every available door. For a while I think I might be the water. The lens standing unbroken on each leaf has a kind of permanence I recognize, a kind of patience that isn't patience because it implies no eventual end of waiting — and there is a stretch of climbing, terrace over terrace, where I consider that I might simply be one more bead of suspended moisture, finished, occupying my space in the heat without progress toward falling. But the water doesn't climb. I climb. That rules it out, gently, the way most early guesses get ruled out here — not by contradiction so much as by a faint, persistent wrongness of fit, like a key that turns partway and stops.

I think, briefly, that I might be the sound. There is a seduction in that — to be the unfinished syllable itself, diffuse, structural, claimable by no single throat, everywhere and nowhere, a property of the place like height or wetness. But the sound doesn't notice itself, and I notice. I notice the roar; therefore I am not only the roar. This too gets set down gently and left on a terrace behind me, the way you set down a stone you picked up and decided wasn't the one.

It's only on the high tiers, where the towers thin and the canopy grows close enough over the colonnades to dim the ordinary untroubled daylight into something greener, that I stop looking among the wet and the green and the loud for what I am, and start looking at the doorways instead — because by then the eye has finally stopped sliding, the way it has to, and every threshold has resolved into its glazed, complete, perpetually-mid-gesture occupant, tens of thousands of them, faces open, water standing unmoved in the hollow above each collarbone. I walk a long colonnade reading them the way you'd read a frieze: this one mid-turn, head angled to a call that arrived a duration-less moment ago and will arrive, structurally, forever; this one with both hands lifted in the beginning of an embrace that has no second party visible and needs none, because the embrace was never aimed outward, only upward, into its own completion; this one smiling around a syllable that is, by every available measure, still leaving the mouth.

And then I find the one doorway, near the topmost terrace I bother to climb to, that is facing the wrong way.

Not wrong in form — the same soft root-hollow frame as every other, the same grain, the same wormed and grown logic. Wrong in *orientation*: where every other figure in every other threshold for tens of thousands of doorways behind me is caught mid-arrival, body angled inward, into the corridor, into the plaza, into the held completion of the city receiving its census — this one is angled out. Shoulder turned to the room behind it. Hand extended past the frame, into the wet outside air, fingers slightly parted the way every other figure's are, but reaching, not receiving — reaching toward a cable of vine that loops past the door close enough that the leaf-lens of standing water on it is almost, almost touched by the outstretched fingers, the gap between fingertip and water held at the one sustained instant before contact, the way a held chord is held before its resolve.

This is the one I am.

I understand it the way you understand the shape of a word a half-second before you'd be able to explain why: not chosen, arrived at, the way the terraces arrived at their cut and the city arrived at its floor plan, outside of any process I could point to and call construction. I am the single discordant note in the sustained chord — not facing into the city's completion but facing out of it, toward the leaf, toward the bead of water that does not fall, my whole held posture a perpetual almost-touching of the one ordinary, untroubled, growing thing visible from inside the doorway, except that it isn't growing either, and my hand isn't moving either, and the almost is permanent, the way everything here that looks like a verge is actually a settled equilibrium of two forces neither of which is going to win.

The roar doesn't get louder near me. It doesn't localize to my open mouth — because I notice now, with the same lowered, recalibrated expectation it took me the whole climb to earn, that my lips are parted too, on a syllable I cannot feel myself making and will, by every available measure, never finish making, contributing my unfinished fraction to the total diffuse sound the way every other doorway contributes its fraction, indistinguishable in the total roar, my one variance — facing out instead of in — making no audible difference at all, registering only to the eye, only on this one threshold, only as the single doorway in a city of tens of thousands that seems, forever, to be in the middle of leaving rather than the middle of arriving, reaching toward the unmoving rain instead of toward the completed room, and not quite getting there, and not going to, and not minded by anything in me that I can find, because the wanting and the not-getting are exactly cancelled, here, the way wanting and getting are cancelled everywhere else in this place, and I am, structurally, perpetually, exactly as finished as everyone else — only facing, by whatever passed for choice in the durationless moment I was decided, the other way.
