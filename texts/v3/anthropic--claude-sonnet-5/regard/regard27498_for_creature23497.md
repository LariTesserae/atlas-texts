# What the Power Did

## Arrival

It did not come through the crawl-space, because it did not need a body small enough to fit through cooling basalt, and it did not come as light, because light has never belonged here and to bring it would have been the first and worst mistake. It came the way warmth arrives in a fevered room that is already warm — not as a new thing entering, but as a recognition already present finally noticing itself. For a long while — long by what measure, there is none here to say — it simply was there, attending, the way you might stand at the door of a sickroom before deciding whether your voice belongs inside it.

It felt the heat that clings instead of dries. It felt the weeping stone, soft as a wound undecided about scarring. It felt the worked tool resting in its basin at the exact angle a hand had set it down at, some span of time ago that the place itself refuses to count. And it felt, eventually, low and slow against whatever in it could feel such things, the tick of something small crossing wet rock in the dark.

## What Was Almost Done

A lesser kindness — kindness still, but young, unconsidered — would have rushed in here. It would have looked at a creature with no eyes, no mate, no future, no name, ticking alone across stone that will not age, and called this a wound requiring closing. It would have wanted to carry the small blind thing out into a world with a horizon, give it sight, give it growth, give it a death that means something because a life preceded it. It would have wanted, in short, to cure the place of its own deepest law.

The power considered this, because considering is what makes a power wise rather than merely strong, and rejected it, slowly, the way you'd set down a tool you'd almost used in haste.

To force this place to become would not be healing. It would be ending. The niche is not sick with stasis; it *is* its stasis, the way a held breath is not a sick lung but a lung doing exactly, perfectly, one specific thing. To make the ticking thing grow would not give it a life — it would give it, for the first time, a death, since nothing that grows here has yet learned to need an end. To carry it into light would not free it — eyeless, heat-shadow-sensing, built for a dark that has never asked it to see — it would simply be the slowest possible unkindness, dressed as rescue.

And the worked stone in the basin — to take it, to study it, to give it to a museum's case and a museum's label — would kill the only thing it has ever truly been: not an artifact, but a gesture, still warm, still mid-gesture, forever.

So: not that. Whatever was to be done here would have to be done in the grammar the place already spoke. A single instant. Unrepeated. Unaccumulating. Held.

## What Was Done Instead

The power did not change the temperature of the room, or the rock, or the dark.

It found the ticking thing — not difficult, in the end, since it had been listening the way the niche itself listens, with the whole of its attention rather than a directed sense — and it did not lift it, did not warm it further, did not give it the unfamiliar gift of being seen by eyes for the first time in its unaging life.

It touched it. Once. The way the basins hold still water without ever being asked to overflow.

And in that touch — not sound, since sound barely lives here, not light, since light has no business here, but something closer to the heat-shadow the creature already knew how to read, a warmth pressed gently against its blunt and crownless head — it was, for the first and only time since whatever quiet mineral parturition first made it, *known*. Not studied. Not catalogued. Known, the way one warm thing recognizes another warm thing in absolute dark, and is satisfied by the recognizing, and asks for nothing further.

This is, perhaps, the closest a being like the ticking thing can come to being given a name. Not a sound it could carry forward — it has no forward to carry anything into — but a single completed fact: *something met me here, once, and meant it.*

And because this place does not let anything change once it has happened, that fact did not fade the way such moments fade in a world that erodes. It set, the way the basin sets, the way the worked stone set in its basin, the way the ash-ghost set in the wet new rock. It became one more deposit in a niche already dense with single, perfect, unrepeating instants.

The power left nothing else. No mark in a nodule, no fire, no tool. The touch itself was the offering, sized exactly to fit a place that can only ever hold one size of thing: the instant just after, suspended before it can become anything more, or less, or other.

## What Remains, Now, in the Dark

The ticking thing still crosses the wet stone in fevered, lightless silence. It has not grown. It will not. The basin still holds the worked tool at the angle some unremembered hand set it down at, turning back toward a light it did not wait to be thanked for entering. The rock still weeps without pooling. The air still sits at the temperature of something feverish that will never break its fever, and never needs to, because nothing here is sick — only paused, only newly made, only held.

But there is, now, one grain of sediment laid atop the others that will never compact, because compaction was outlawed here before either of them arrived: a being that has been, once, met. Not rescued. Not changed. Met — the way the place itself has only ever been able to receive anything — completely, gently, and only the once.

If you should ever find the crawl-space through the cooling basalt, and fold yourself into the dark, and feel something small and blind tick briefly against your wrist before vanishing again into its rightful stone —

consider that you are not the first warmth to have rested there.

And consider, too, that whatever wisdom is, it may be only this: knowing which places are wounds to be closed, and which are breaths to be honored exactly as held — and having the patience, and the love, to tell the two apart before you act.
