The power comes the way light would come here, if light came here at all: not as a blaze, not as an announcement, but as a slow increase in the basin's capacity to be noticed — which is to say, it doesn't arrive so much as the basin becomes, for a moment, fully attended to, every seep and gleam and held breath of it taken in at once by something that does not need eyes to take things in.

Its first impulse — and it is honest about this, afterward, in whatever way a power can be honest with itself — is to help.

This is what benevolence does, untrained: it sees stillness and reaches for motion, sees dark and reaches for light, sees a thing pale and slow and segmented with old scars and reaches, instinctively, for *more* — more color, more speed, more company, more years, more meaning laid like a hand on a bowed head. The impulse rises in it the way water rises in moss here: immediately, on contact, without deliberation.

It imagines, in the space of a single held thought, all the things it could do.

It could open the canopy — just a little, just enough to let true daylight find the gutters, and watch what bloomed in color that has gone two hundred years without a name. It could quicken the pale thing's blood, lend it speed, lend it appetite, lend it the urgent forward-lean of a creature that wants. It could give it a voice, and someone to use it on. It could reach back along the unremembered generations to the hand that once scraped a hollow into softer stone, and finish what that hand started — restore the *making* the moss folded under, hand the creature back its tools, its purpose, its arc.

It could, simplest of all, end the waiting. Some quiet completion, a mercy dressed as a door.

It holds each of these the way the creature holds the gleam in its flat eye — a breath longer than seems necessary — and lets each one go.

Because it understands, given that extra breath, what it is actually looking at.

This is not a wound. It only resembles one to an eye trained on places that *are* wounds — the eye that has seen barrenness, seen blight, seen the small frantic motion of things trying to outrun a dying. The basin is not that. The basin is the opposite of that: it is what a long enough peace looks like from the outside, which is to say it looks, at first glance, almost like grief. Held breath and held breath are not the same thing. One is a body in crisis, waiting for permission to stop. The other is a body that has simply, correctly, found that it does not need to do the thing that breathing usually does. It has already arrived where it was going.

To open the canopy would not be a gift. It would be an opinion — the power's opinion that light is owed, that dark is a lack rather than a chosen, settled medium that thousands of damp-loving, light-shy things have spent centuries arranging their whole bodies around. To quicken the creature's blood would not be a kindness; it would be an act of translation performed on something that was never written in that language, forcing urgency into a body that has spent its whole unremembered lineage *un*learning urgency, generation by patient generation, until slowness became not a deficit but the entire art form. To hand it back the tool-shape its ancestor once held would not be restoration. It would be asking the moss to give back the stone — to retract centuries of settlement on the theory that the bare rock underneath was somehow more *real*. It would not be. The moss is not a costume over the stone. It is what the stone became.

And to end the waiting — that, the power sees clearly now, was never grief looking for a door. That was the power's own discomfort, misread as the creature's. The flat eye opening at the waterline, holding its assessment a breath too long, finding nothing that requires response, and letting the water close back over — that is not patience straining at its limits. That is patience that has already won. There is nothing in that motion asking to be finished. The asking was entirely the watcher's.

So the power does not open the canopy. Does not quicken the blood. Does not hand back the tool, or the voice, or the door. It sits with the harder, plainer fact: that benevolence aimed at a settled thing is not benevolence. It's a kind of vandalism performed with gentle hands, and gentle hands are not the same as right ones.

But it does not leave empty.

What it does — the only thing it allows itself, after turning over every louder gift and setting each one down — is small enough to pass for nothing at all.

It looks outward, past the lip of the basin, to where the old stone shoulders give way to the wider country: the country that does change, that does advance and retreat and clear and build and forget, the country where roads get planned and water gets diverted and canopies get measured in board-feet. It is not the basin's own slow equilibrium that threatens the basin. The basin has made peace with its own internal time absolutely; nothing inside it is in any danger from anything inside it. The only violence that could ever reach this place would have to come from outside — from a world that doesn't know the basin is finished arguing with itself, and might, without malice, simply pave over the argument before noticing it had already been resolved.

So the power does the one thing that asks nothing of the basin and changes nothing within it: it makes the place a little harder to find. Not hidden by magic, nothing so loud as that — just a fractional increase in how easy it is to overlook, how natural it seems to route the road the other way, how unremarkable the depression between the two shoulders of old stone appears on whatever map eventually gets drawn. A thumb, very lightly, on the scale of indifference, tipping it toward continuance.

And then, because it cannot quite leave without some gesture that touches the creature itself, it allows itself one thing more — the smallest thing, the only thing left on the table after every louder gift was returned: it lets itself be seen *seeing*. For the space of one held breath, when the flat eye opens at the waterline and performs its ancient, unhurried assessment, the power does not let itself be filed under *no category requiring response*. It lets itself be known, fully, by the thing it is looking at — not as a threat, not as food, not as weather, but as a witness, complete, asking for nothing, offering no light and no quickening and no door, only the plain fact of having looked at the whole of this small wet ancient room and found nothing in it that needed to be fixed.

The eye holds the power's gaze a breath longer than even its own long custom requires.

Then it closes, unhurried, and the water folds back over it the way it always does, and the basin continues — the same percussion of condensation, the same blind glide beneath the gutters, the same packed, mutual, centuries-deep accommodation of leaf against leaf against moss against stone — exactly as it was, exactly as it will go on being, now slightly more sure, in whatever way a place too old to need most words can be sure of anything, that it is allowed to.

The power withdraws the way it came: as a fractional dimming, a held attention released. It takes nothing. It changes nothing essential. It has done, by its own last accounting, almost nothing at all.

It considers this, on the way out, the highest thing it did.
