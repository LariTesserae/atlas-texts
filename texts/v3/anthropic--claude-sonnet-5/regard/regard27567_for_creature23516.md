The being doesn't need fixing. That's the first thing the power understands, watching from whatever vantage benevolence keeps for itself — that the Slow Lord is not suffering, not lost, not waiting for anything. It is doing exactly what it has always done, at exactly the rate it was always going to do it, and the reach around it is doing the same. There is no wound here to close. So the temptation toward grand intervention — lifting a curse, healing a scar, granting speech or freedom or some upward "improvement" — falls away almost immediately, because all of those would be acts of impatience, and impatience is the one thing this place and this creature have spent ten thousand years successfully exiling.

So the power does not arrive as a force. It arrives the way everything arrives in Verdhollow Reach: slowly, and from a direction that turns out, on reflection, to have been every direction at once.

It starts small, because small is the only scale the reach trusts. A fungal mat that has pulsed on its multi-day cycle for centuries brightens, just once, very slightly more than usual — not enough to alarm anything, only enough that something passing on its slow rounds might, if it paused, notice that it had been noticed back. The benevolent power does not announce itself. It introduces itself the way the canopy introduces a new fold of moss: by simply being present long enough that presence becomes unremarkable, then trusted, then load-bearing.

What it gives, mostly, is attention — the rarest resource in a place built on enormous spans of unwitnessed time. It begins to *watch with* the reach rather than at it. Where the creature pauses at the old ruin-marks, the swallowed wall, the spiral-fringed depression that was once a dwelling, the power lingers there too, and in lingering, does something the forest itself cannot do: it remembers on the creature's behalf, holds the shape of those vanished hands and that vanished naming-language gently in a kind of long attention, not to restore them — restoration would be a violence here, a forcing of ended things to un-end — but simply so that the fact of them has been *witnessed* by something capable of holding a memory across a span longer than moss takes to become cliff.

It does not speak to the creature. Speech would be the wrong tool, a young species' instrument, built for urgency. Instead it does what slow things understand as conversation: it changes one or two small conditions, gently, and waits — sometimes years — to see how the change is received. A trickle of water is redirected by a hair's width so that a particular fold of canopy-terrain, the one where the creature has been resting longest, drains slightly better, so the rot it has been patiently processing all this age finishes its work a little more completely, a little more kindly. A blind, deep well where the canopy-roof wore through is given, very gradually, a thin permanent shaft of real light — not enough to disturb the green-gold dusk-logic of the place, only enough that the one bioluminescent fungal cycle nearest it might, for the first time in millennia, have something other than itself to answer to.

And when, far off in whatever future the reach allows itself to have, the creature finally does what all things in Verdhollow Reach eventually do — stops, and settles, and begins the long becoming-ground that is not death here so much as a change of verb tense — the power does not intervene to stop it, because stopping it would be the only true unkindness available. Instead it stays. It is simply *there*, in the held quiet that deepens to listen whenever something calls, for the whole unmeasured duration of the change. Not to ease it. Not to hurry it. Only so that the long settling happens within the witness of something that understood, completely, what was occurring — that this was not an ending but a continuation taking a new shape, and that someone, something, some attention with no urgency of its own to impose, was glad to have known the shape it took before, and would be glad, in some future passage of its own slow notice, to feel that new ridge of ground give gently underfoot, and recognize what was sleeping there.

That, in the end, is the whole of what it does: it joins the only law the place already keeps. *Nothing here ends.* The power does not change that law, does not need to, only adds itself quietly to the long list of things — moss, fungus-light, water without direction, one vast patient animal — that have agreed to abide by it, and to keep, across whatever time is required, a kind of company that asks nothing back.
