## What Comes to Watch

It does not arrive the way you would arrive—through the fissure, down the six damp steps, blinking against the sourceless noon. It is already there, in the sense that it is already everywhere it has decided to attend to, and it has been attending to this hollow since before the ground finished its sentence and left off the punctuation. It did not find the place by accident. It does not believe in accident the way the ground does, the way the not-quite-foxes do, crossing baskets and ceasing. It found it the way you find your own pulse when you go looking for it: already present, only now noticed.

Its first act—if you can call a non-act an act—is to want nothing from the hollow. This matters more than it sounds like it should. Power, when it visits unfinished things, almost always wants something: to complete them, to correct them, to harvest the lesson of them, to put its name on the becoming so that when it finishes, the finishing will have been authored. The benevolent power does none of this. It has been unfinished things itself, long enough ago that the memory of it is mostly texture now, a held-breath quality it still recognizes the way you recognize a smell that belonged to a house you no longer live in. It knows, the way only something old enough to have once been young knows, that to walk into a gestation and offer guidance is a kind of violence with very good manners. So it doesn't.

What it does instead is smaller, and slower, and almost embarrassingly tender for something with so much reach.

It widens, very slightly, the unlikelihood of the fissure. Not seals it—sealing would be its own kind of decision imposed from outside, and the Tender has not asked for walls. But it adjusts the odds the way you might lower your voice near a sleeping animal without quite deciding to. Those who would come to the hollow already carrying a verdict—hands ready to extract, name, weaponize, harvest the strange ungoverned ecology for some use decided elsewhere—will simply not stumble onto the right scallop of stone, the right angle of unlikely noon-light leaking from underground. The hollow remains exactly as findable as it was for the wanderer who arrives with nothing but attention. The power does not protect the place from being seen. It protects it from being *seen with intent.* There is a difference, and the difference is the entire gift.

Then it does the harder, quieter thing: it stays a while, not as a shape, not borrowing the outline of fox or bird or deer-shaped thing with its uncertain count, but as a kind of second hush laid gently beside the first. Not instructing the silence. Accompanying it. If the Tender is a single being holding its breath while it decides what kind of intelligence to practice on the world, then the power's whole visit consists of holding its breath nearby—so that, for the unmeasured duration of the visit, the hollow is not alone in its indecision. There is, very faintly, a witness. Not a judge. Witnessing and judging look similar from a distance and are not at all the same thing, and the power has had a very long time to learn to tell them apart.

It leaves no ring of stones. It does not want to be incorporated into the floor the way the last visitor was—diffused into the grammar of the place, absorbed the way the bone shard is being absorbed, taken back in. That fate was offered to someone once, freely, and freely accepted, and to repeat it would cheapen it into a ritual instead of a singular act of belonging. The power does something else, something that leaves no shard, no half-buried evidence at all: it simply notices the Tender back. For the length of its visit, the half-formed intelligence trying on the bodies of an ecosystem one held breath at a time is, itself, *seen*—not corrected, not catalogued, not loved in any way that requires the loved thing to become more legible in order to deserve it. Seen exactly in its unfinishedness, the way you might look at a half-drawn face and find it, already, completely worth looking at.

Before it goes—and it does go, because staying forever would be its own kind of imposition, a permanent witness pressing its expectation into the clay before the clay has asked for an expectation to hold—it leaves one thing behind. Not a mark. Not a mercy. A *permission*, if permission can be left the way warmth is left in a chair someone has only just stood up from: the faint, ambient, unspoken fact that somewhere, something old and finished once visited something young and undecided, and did not try to finish it.

That fact will not change the hollow's size. It will not speed the healing of the floor over the old ring of stones, will not teach the predator-shaped thing whether to close its jaws all the way, will not tell the Tender what kind of intelligence it should eventually agree to practice—mercy, hunger, hush, hospitality, some fourth thing without a word yet. All of that remains exactly as undecided as it was. The power does not consider this a failure of intervention. It considers it the *entire point.* The kindest thing one becoming can do for another becoming is sometimes only this: to come close enough to be a comfort, and then to leave before its own shape becomes the answer.

So if you go down the six steps again, after—if the fissure still finds you, or you it—you will notice nothing different. The light will still come from nowhere. The animals will still rehearse their unfinished violences and unmake them a breath later. The chamber will still be very faintly larger than the last time anyone measured it by feeling rather than instrument.

But the hush, if you listen all the way into it, will have one more quality than before, soft and structural as a second held breath laid beneath the first: the specific, wordless texture of having once, briefly, been kept company by something that wanted nothing from the keeping.
