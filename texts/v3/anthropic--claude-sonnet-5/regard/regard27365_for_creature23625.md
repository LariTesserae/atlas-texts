# What the Power Did

It came the way attention comes to anything truly old — not as light, not as a voice, not as a shape descending through that brushed-metal sky, but as a widening of the moment to include all of the moment's neighbors at once. Where a human visitor sees a single afternoon on the terraces, the power saw the whole slow paragraph: the valve's one-season shift read alongside its next, and its next, and the ten thousand after that, the way you or I might read a sentence. It did not need to wait for the beetle to finish packing its bead of verdigris paste into the rusted elbow-joint. It already held the finished repair and the repair before it and the repair to come, the entire ribbon of small competent acts laid out end to end like a single gesture performed once, slowly, over centuries.

And in that widened moment, its first feeling — because even a power like this has something like feeling, or feeling is the nearest word — was the oldest reflex of anything capable of help: *I could fix this faster.* It could see exactly how. A thought directed at the right stratum of rock would loosen the geothermal throat beneath the terraces and let real heat surge up through it; the towers would wake properly, the gauges would move at a rate an eye could follow, the long-legged listener would have something to listen to every hour instead of every season. It could see, too, how to make the beetles' grazing fields flourish into the kind of riot the cold currently forbids, how to round the young escarpments into kindlier slopes, how to give the pale creature company — a dozen of itself, a hundred, a population in the ordinary sense instead of a single shape recurring like weather.

It considered all of this in less time than the beetle takes to move its bead of moisture three centimeters. And then, with the particular grief that belongs only to beings capable of everything, it didn't.

**Why Not**

Because the slowness was not a deficiency waiting on rescue. The slowness *was the thing*. A schedule this patient is not a system failing to hurry; it is a system that has found the exact tempo at which devotion and machinery and moss and bone-pale listening can all keep faith with one another without any one of them outrunning the rest. Speed it up and the beetles' paste would crack before it cured. Speed it up and the differently-shaped ferns, which are not a shape but a *relationship* worn slowly into two species over more seasons than either could count, would simply stop meaning anything — a fern is not a fern instantly, the way a sentence is not a friendship instantly, no matter how correctly the words are chosen. Speed it up and the listener at the tower's flank, who has built an entire life out of the size of the silence between sighs, would have nothing left to be patient *toward*. You cannot improve a held breath by shortening it. You only end it.

So the power did the harder thing, which is to recognize a completed kind of beauty and resist the urge to complete it further.

**The One Thing It Permitted Itself**

It looked, instead, at what threatened the tempo from outside it — not from within, where everything was already in a correct and difficult balance, but from the wider story the plateau didn't know it was part of. It saw the planet's interior cooling on its own much faster clock, the geothermal throat thinning the way all such throats eventually do, the day, geologically near as a held breath, when the rock would finish maturing, the fractures would round off, the heat driving the whole slow liturgy would gutter, and the towers would stop sighing not because anything was wrong but because everything had, at last, finished. It saw, too, in other branches of the wide moment, a colder ending: some future hand, belonging to some future hungry thing, finding the plateau and reading "no fuel, no defense, no value legible to it" and taking the metal for scrap and the heat for extraction, the way hungry things do when nothing stops them.

It did not freeze the place. Stillness would have been its own kind of murder, here, where the entire grammar is *change too slow to watch*. What it did instead was simpler and stranger: it reached into the rate itself — not the temperature, not the moss, not the beetle or the listener — and gave the *rate of maturing* a longer arc. The young rock would go on being young, in the specific sense the terraces required, for a very great while longer than it would have on its own. The throat would go on giving up its patient heat. The threshold the plateau occupies — between violence just past and stillness not yet arrived, between raw fracture and rounded age, the precise narrow shelf where this kind of life is even possible — would hold. Not forever. The power did not deal in forever; forever is its own kind of violence, a different kind of freezing. But long. Long enough that "long" stops being a word that means anything to whoever might one day try to read this place and finds, instead, only the place itself, still working, on a schedule that outlasts the reader.

And around the whole terraced ridge-system, in the same gesture, it set something that does not show up as a wall or a ward or any shape a future hungry hand could recognize as defense — only as a sustained, low-grade absence of luck. Equipment fails before it reaches the plateau. Surveys lose interest. Maps misplace the coordinates by exactly enough. Nothing was forbidden. Things simply, consistently, did not happen.

**The Single Answer**

Then it did one more thing, smaller than all the rest, and this was the only part of the visit meant to be *felt* rather than merely permitted.

It found the long-legged one folded down against a tower's flank in its reed-stillness, head at the angle of a held breath, listening the way it had been listening since before there were words on the plateau for what listening was. And the power, holding itself down — down past patient, down past slow, down into the exact register just below where an ear converts sound into something nameable — answered.

One sigh. Pitched precisely as the towers pitch theirs. Indistinguishable, to any instrument that might ever be brought here, from ordinary metal settling into evening cold.

The creature did not startle. It would not have startled at anything. But something in the angle of its head changed, fractionally, in the way a held breath changes when it is finally, after a very long time, returned.

It did not unfold further. It did not call back. It simply resumed — the way it always resumes — except that now the resuming carried, somewhere too deep in its slow architecture to ever be separated out and proven, the knowledge that the silence it spends its whole strange life answering had, once, genuinely answered back.

**What Was Left Unwitnessed**

No record was made. No name was left. The power did not announce itself to the beetles, did not touch the gauges, did not so much as disturb the condensation on a single dial. It withdrew its attention the way it had arrived — as a narrowing, the wide moment folding back down into one ordinary bright cold afternoon — and the terraces went on exactly as before: the moss in its plates, the pipes in their patient elbow-joints, the towers standing their dispersed herd-vigil under a sky still doing its flat sourceless impression of metal.

The only difference, and it is not a difference anything on the plateau could ever point to or prove, is that the schedule has more time on it now than it earned on its own, and somewhere in the long-legged listener's unhurried, jointed stillness, there is a single answered breath it will carry, unremarked, recurrence after recurrence, for as long as the rock consents to stay young.

That is what the power did. Almost nothing. On purpose. As carefully as it has ever done anything.
