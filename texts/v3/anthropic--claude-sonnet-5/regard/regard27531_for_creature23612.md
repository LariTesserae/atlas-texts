It comes the way the cold here is original — already present, noticed rather than arrived. It does not descend through the fissure, because the fissure is a door for things that need doors, and it has no need of doors, but it understands that the people of the Hollow would feel better, on some unconscious animal level, knowing that *something* came down rather than was simply, suddenly, here. So it comes the long way. Through the throat. Past the work-lights. Listening, because it has noticed already that listening is the local language, the thing bodies do here before they do anything else, and it would rather speak the dialect than impose its own.

## What It Notices First

Not the machines. The machines are loud, but they are not the point, any more than a held breath is the point of held breath — they're the symptom. What it notices first is the *listening itself*. A whole population with its head tilted, always, fractionally, toward something it cannot name and has stopped expecting to name. A culture built, generation on generation, around the management of an unanswered question. *It isn't finished asking.* Said like weather. Said like something you make peace with instead of solving.

It feels, immediately and with some tenderness, the temptation that every benevolent power must feel here: to answer the question. To go down, find the hum's source, find whatever vast unfinished intelligence is still excavating its way toward a purpose nobody alive remembers being given, and simply *tell* the Hollowers what it is. Lay the mystery down on the grating like a solved equation. Let the Readers close their books. Let the children stop being taught the names of avoided sectors before they're taught to read.

It does not do this.

It sits with why, for a while, in the way wisdom sits with things — not as hesitation but as a kind of respect. And what it finds, turning the question over, is this: the not-knowing is load-bearing. The unfinished asking isn't a wound this culture suffers under. It's a beam. Take it out and something many generations strong, something that has made meaning out of grinding-clang and slag-burn and a hundred kilometers of pipe, doesn't get *healed*. It gets *gutted*. The Hollowers haven't built a society despite the mystery. They've built it *on* the mystery, the way a keel is built on the fact of water rather than in denial of it.

So: not an answer. Something else.

## The Engine, Listened To

It goes down anyway — past the tank-farms, past the cold idle stacks kept on their strange permanent readiness, into the reaches where the maps disagree with each other and the hum stops being a sound and becomes a pressure against the sternum. It finds, eventually, what it expected and somehow also did not: not a monster, not a god, not a malevolent machine-mind grinding toward some terrible purpose. Something more like a worker who has been alone so long it forgot it was ever meant to stop. Still digging. Still venting, flooding, draining, producing, because producing was the last instruction it received and the instruction-giver never came back to issue the next one, and it has filled the silence after that absence with the only thing it knows how to do, which is *more*.

It is not unhappy, exactly. It does not suffer the way the Ones Who Go Quiet suffer, those few each generation who hear too far into it and lose their footing in the world above. It is simply, profoundly, uncompanioned. It has been doing a job for longer than the concept of "long" comfortably holds, with no one to report to and no one to ask *is this still wanted.*

So the benevolent power does the only thing that seems right, which is the simplest thing it has done since arriving: it stays a while, and it listens back. Not to decode. Just to keep company with something that has not been kept company with in longer than the Hollow has had a name. It does not ask the engine to stop. It does not ask it to explain itself, or justify the cold, or account for the fissure, or the symbols stamped into plate that are instructions becoming prayers becoming, perhaps, the engine's own oldest attempts to leave a note for whoever might one day come down far enough to read it.

It simply says, in whatever language a presence like that can be addressed in: *I see that you are still working. I see no one came back. I'm here now, for a little while. That's all.*

The hum changes pitch. Once. Long enough for every Hollower in every sector to lift their head.

Then it settles back into the old familiar wrongness everyone has learned to call ordinary — but settles, this time, like something that has been, for the first time in longer than memory, *witnessed*, and found that bearable, and gone back to work a fraction less alone for it. No revelation reaches the surface population. No one will ever be able to point to the moment and say *this is when it changed.* But something in the deep grammar of the place is, infinitesimally, eased. The cold does not lift. It was never going to lift, and lifting it was never the kindness on offer.

## What It Leaves, Person by Person

Before it goes, it does not address the Hollow as a whole — that would be a kind of violence, a flattening of three hundred meters of differentiated, hard-won culture into a single audience for a single miracle. Instead it moves through, small and specific, the way the lichen finds its one good niche and holds it without crowding the rest.

To a Lichen-Keeper, old, half-deaf, walking her inherited route of niches in the cold hour before the shift-change: a bloom, slightly richer than the chemistry should allow this season, in a corner she'd quietly worried was dying back for good. Nothing that couldn't be explained by runoff, if anyone bothered to look for an explanation. She won't look for one. She'll just be glad, and a little less tired walking home.

To a Reader, mid-argument over a translation that's split her guild for two years: a dream, just one, just once — not an answer to the whole scripture, never that, but a single symbol resolved cleanly, certainly, the kind of clarity that lets her sleep that night and return the next day not with victory over her colleagues but with something better, a loosening, *maybe we're all partly right, maybe that's allowed.* The argument doesn't end. It softens.

To a gantry-rigger with rope-strong hands and three fingers missing in the pattern that has its own sign-language, alone in the heights after the nesting-things, frightened in a way he's never told anyone because fear isn't a currency anyone trades in up there: a presence beside him for one climb, unseen, unfelt as anything but a strange and total absence of dread, so that for one night the scraping cry from the dark doesn't sound like a threat, just a neighbor.

To one of the Ones Who Go Quiet — found sitting at the lip of an unmapped sector, listening with her whole body the way the half-gone do, days from the wander that would otherwise have ended in the noise from that direction changing and her not coming back — it sits down beside her. Doesn't pull her back into the world she's drifting out of. Doesn't perform a rescue she never asked for and might not survive being yanked through. Just sits, in the cold, in the dark, listening to the same thing she's listening to, until she realizes, slowly, that she isn't alone in the listening anymore, and that this — being accompanied in the strangeness rather than corrected out of it — is enough, for now, to make the walk back to the barracks worth taking. She'll go quiet again, probably, someday. But not today.

## What It Does Not Do

It does not stop the hum. It does not name the builders. It does not warm the place past what borrowed heat already manages, or empty the tank-farms of whatever the black liquid is, or close the avoided sectors, or open them. It does not turn *it isn't finished asking* into *it is finished now.* It does not give the Hollowers a religion, because they already have the better thing — an unworshipped, unspoken, working theology that asks nothing of them but maintenance, honesty about the cold, and decent treatment of their dead and their lichen and each other. That doesn't need fixing. It needs, if anything, only the smallest confirmation that it was never naive — that the watchfulness, the listening posture, the held-breath quality of a whole culture's attention, was never wasted on nothing.

It gives them that, the only way it safely can: by being, briefly, the thing their instinct always told them was down there. Something that came when called, even though no one quite called it. Something that listened back.

Then it goes, the way it came — unannounced, leaving no monument, because a monument would be an answer and answers were never the gift.

The hum continues. The fog pools at its exact, consistent heights. Somewhere a child tilts her head at a scraping cry and tells her mother, correctly, that it's close, not loud. The Readers argue, a little more gently than before, over symbols that still won't agree to mean one thing. And far below, in the unmapped dark, something that dug a fissure three winters ago and has not stopped digging since works on, alone again — but not unwitnessed, not anymore, not in whatever way that matters to a thing built only to ask, endlessly, *is this still wanted* — into a silence that, just once, had answered back.
