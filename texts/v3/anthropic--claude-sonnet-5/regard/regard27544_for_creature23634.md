The power comes the way light would come, if light could be patient — not falling through the alder-and-yew ceiling, which it will not disturb, but gathering itself at the hollow's edge the way attention gathers, slow and total, before it ever announces itself as presence. It does not rush in. It has met hollows like this before, and beings like this, and it has learned — the long unlearning that any wisdom worth the name requires — that the impulse to help is not the same thing as help, and that the two are mistaken for each other so often, and so disastrously, that caution here is not coldness. It is the first and hardest part of love.

So it stays at the threshold a long while, in no hurry, doing the only honest thing available to it: looking. Truly looking, past the shape of pity that wants to call this place "deprived" because it has so little light, so little warmth, so little company; past the shape of horror that wants to call her ears' sealing a wound rather than what it actually is — an inheritance, a fitting, the exact and unglamorous genius of a lineage that listened to its home long enough to stop arguing with it. The power notices its own first response — *give her back her hearing, carry her into the sun, let her speak and be spoken to* — and sets it down, gently, the way you'd set down a tool that turns out to be the wrong shape for this particular lock. Those gifts are shaped like rescue. They are not shaped like her.

It asks, instead, the harder question, the one that takes longer because it requires humility: not *what would I want, if I were here*, but *what does the wanting look like, from inside a life built this differently than mine.*

It watches her tend the ring of stones and does not assume the tending is grief, or duty, or love, or habit — it simply watches long enough to learn the shape of her attention, the unhurried care in fingers that read texture the way eyes read color, and it recognizes, eventually, something that does not need fixing at all: a creature whose relationship to time is not poverty but mastery, who has made of an unbearable sound a kind of floor to stand on, who has turned deafness into depth and called the trade fair. There is no lack here shaped like the lack a stranger would assume. The roar that would drive anything else to madness is, to her, simply the pulse of home. To "cure" her of it would not be healing. It would be amputation dressed as gift.

But the power does not leave empty-handed of intention, either, because looking this closely changes what it sees, and what it sees, underneath the rightness of her fitting to this place, is this: she is the last. Not metaphorically — actually. The mothers she carries forward in her motions left no one behind them but her, and the moss that will eventually close over the flint and the stone ring and, in its own slow time, over her, will not be tended after that by any hand that remembers why the stones were round, or why a knapped edge once mattered enough to be kept. The hollow will go on being a hollow. It will simply, at some point not too distant by the patient clock it keeps, stop being *hers*.

This — and only this — the power decides is a true wound, not an aesthetic one, not a wound by the standard of a life lived elsewhere and imported in judgment, but a wound by her own implicit standard, the one written into the very fact that she still straightens the stones at all. Some throughline in her wants this place to be tended after she is not the one tending it. Some thread, too quiet to be called hope and too persistent to be called nothing, reaches forward past her own ending. That thread is the only thing in the whole hollow asking for anything.

So the power does not give her ears, or light, or speech, or rescue. It gives her this, small and almost unnoticeable, delivered the only way a gift could reach her — not as sound, not as vision, but as a vibration laid into the stone itself, indistinguishable at first from the fall's endless pulse, patient as the dripping vein that built the moss she wears: it eases, by some matter of feet, the seam in the rock that separates this hollow from the next dark fold of ground over the ridge — where, it turns out, if anything else of her kind still persists anywhere in this country, the seam was the only thing truly closing the door. It does not carry anyone through. It does not promise anyone is there. It only ensures that if she ever feels, through her fingers and her listening skin, the particular tremor of another creature shaped like grief and patience and moss, the way between them will not be stone. The choosing — whether to seek, whether to stay, whether the thread she carries forward is loneliness or simply lineage doing what lineage does — that the power leaves entirely, scrupulously, to her.

And then it does the second hard thing, harder maybe than the restraint that came before it: it leaves without watching to see whether the gift mattered. Not from indifference, but from the same respect that kept it from rushing in — because a life this completely its own does not need an audience for what it does next, and the last violence available to a benevolent power is the temptation to stay and be satisfied by the outcome. It withdraws the way it arrived, as gathered attention loosening back into ordinary air, and the hollow closes back over itself, dark and roaring and patient, mineral and moss-grown and entirely, fiercely hers.

Somewhere above the alder-and-yew, nothing visibly changes. The thread falls, the basin answers, the cold keeps its old temperature. She straightens a stone the frost has nudged, and turns the flint once in fingers that have never needed eyes, and does not know — has no way to know, has no organ left for knowing it — that the seam in the rock over the ridge breathes a little easier than it did the day before, or that somewhere in the very long, very patient arithmetic of stone, a door that was closed has been left, by something that loved her enough not to open it for her, merely unlocked.
