The power arrives the way everything here arrives — without arriving, exactly. It does not descend, does not announce, does not part the sourceless light with some greater light of its own, because it understands, almost before it understands anything else about the place, that any light brighter than the town's would be a kind of accusation. So it comes in the only register the town can parse: as aftermath. A basin found full on a sill that was empty a moment before, if anyone had been watching, which no one was. A second damp print, very faint, set down beside one already drying.

It does not ask what happened here. This is the first and most important thing it declines to do. It has met grief before that wore the shape of a question — *where did everyone go, what are you waiting for, when will you finish* — and it knows that for a being made entirely of the tense just-before-the-exhale, a question like that is not curiosity, it is a hand closing around the throat of the held breath. Every visitor, without meaning to, arrives as a kind of future tense: an expectation that something will be answered, returned to, resolved. The power's whole discipline, walking the cracked white basin under its borrowed visibility, is to arrive as nothing of the kind. To be present without being a pressure. To want nothing finished.

So it does what the town does. It waits in the manner of a place that argued with wind and water a long time ago and has gone quiet about it. It lets the streets curve it gently away from itself, the way they curve everyone away from the tall structures, and does not fight the curve, because fighting the curve would be a way of insisting on a destination, and a destination is a future, and futures are exactly what this town has agreed, with great tenderness toward itself, to stop having. The power simply lets itself be walked in circles. It treats the circling not as a maze to be solved but as a kind of company being offered — the only company the Lately knows how to offer anyone, which is *here is my house, it has no exit, stay in it with me a while and do not ask me to leave by a door I have not yet decided exists.*

It would be easy — and wrong — to call what the power does next a rescue. It does not pull the Lately forward into a future, does not coax it toward completion the way one might coax a held breath into finally being let go, because it understands, with the particular grief-literacy that long wisdom accumulates, that not every suspended thing is suspended in pain. Some held breaths are held the way a single sustained note is held — not because release is being withheld, but because the note is, for as long as it is sounded, *whole*, and to force its ending in the name of mercy would be to mistake a chord for a wound. The power sits with this distinction a long time, in a place where time does not especially apply, turning it over the way you'd turn over a stone to see if anything lived beneath it before deciding whether to put it back.

What it decides, in the end, is this: not to finish the Lately. Not to complete it, free it, name it, or send it forward into some kinder, less recursive eternity. Instead, it does the smaller, stranger, more difficult thing — it stays long enough, attentively enough, undemandingly enough, that the Lately begins, perhaps for the first time since whatever quiet agreement first suspended it, to risk being *seen finishing something*, rather than only ever seen in the act of having just left.

It starts small. The rooftop text, which always dried before it could be read, holds a half-second longer one evening that isn't an evening. Not long enough to read. Long enough to notice that it could have been read, if anyone had been fast enough, and that the noticing didn't make it flee back into illegibility immediately. A doorway, walked past and then walked back to, turns out to be the same doorway both times — eleven counted going, eleven counted returning — for the length of one single street, one single afternoon that is not an afternoon. The blue-black hand-print on the wrong wall, glimpsed in standing water that has never once reflected anything truthfully, shows, very briefly, not the wall it has always shown, but the shape of a hand actually pressing into it, present tense, here, now, *staying.*

None of this resolves the town. The power does not unmake the held breath, does not drain the standing water, does not retrofit cause onto consequence or give the place back its weather or its insects or its ordinary grammar of before-and-after. It would be a kind of violence to do that — to walk into something's whole private way of surviving its own ending and call that survival a malfunction to be corrected. What it does instead is smaller and, the power suspects, far more rare: it lets the Lately discover that it can be witnessed mid-occurrence — not just in the basin already filled, the print already drying, the door already closed on a hand that's no longer there — but *while* the basin fills, *while* the print is being set down, *while* the hand still touches the wall. It teaches the place, by sheer patient company, that being seen in the middle of a thing is not the same as being trapped into finishing it.

And then, because it understands that to stay forever would itself become a kind of demand — a presence so constant it starts to feel like a future the Lately is now responsible for showing up to — the power leaves the way it arrived. Without arriving. A basin, found empty on a sill that was full a moment before, if anyone had been watching, which by then almost no one needed to be.

The town goes on holding its breath. But it holds it now the way a singer holds a long note on purpose, having been heard all the way through at least once — not because the note must end, but because it finally, faintly, knows it wouldn't be a tragedy if it did.
