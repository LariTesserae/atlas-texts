# What the Power Did

It came the way attention comes to anything truly old — not as an arrival, not as a disturbance of air or light, but as a kind of slow leaning-in, the way you might lean toward a sound you're not yet sure you heard. It had vast reach and very little need to use it, and this alone marked it as the kind of power that survives its own strength: it had learned, across whatever long education such powers undergo, that the first and hardest discipline is to do nothing while you are still finding out what's actually there.

So it watched, first. Not for a day, because a day tells you nothing in a place that measures its weather in centuries. It watched across the slow brightening and dimming of the lattice-platforms, across enough turns of that unhurried calendar to see the cycle complete itself twice, three times, watched the cloud-collars hold their elevation through whatever passed for seasons here, watched the keeper fold itself against its chosen post and not move, in any way that mattered, for what a shorter-lived watcher would have called years and what the power itself understood as something closer to a single held breath.

It understood, watching, the first thing the cordillera teaches anyone patient enough to learn it: *this place has already finished arguing with itself.* The water knows where to fall. The forest knows its ceiling. Nothing here is broken, and a benevolent power's first temptation — the temptation toward repair, toward improvement, toward the busy generosity that cannot stand to see a thing simply *persisting* without comment — is exactly the temptation this place would suffer most from indulging.

So it set that temptation down. That was the first thing it did: nothing, deliberately, for a long time, as an act of respect rather than neglect.

---

## What It Noticed, Eventually

But watching long enough is its own kind of intimacy, and intimacy finds things that a glance does not.

It noticed that the keeper's head, inclined toward the humming post at that angle so easily mistaken for mere stillness, was not mere stillness. It noticed the faint, answering brighten-and-dim of the creature's own veined hide, tracking the post's slower cycle the way a held breath tracks a heartbeat it is trying not to disturb. It noticed — and this took the long view, the kind of patience available only to something that does not fear running out of time — that the keeper was not resting beside the post.

It was *listening for a reply.*

Not constantly, not desperately — nothing here moves with desperation, the cordillera does not permit it — but underneath the patient plated stillness there was, unmistakably once seen, the specific quality of attention that belongs to something that has been told, or has decided, or has simply always known in the wordless way creatures know their purpose, that someday the hum will change. That it is not just a frequency to nest beside but a question, asked a very long time ago, by whoever or whatever drove those dark posts into the cliff-shelves and taught the platforms to glow on their slow calendars and then — and here the power felt the shape of the absence clearly — *left, without ever answering back.*

The keeper was not guarding the old marks. It was *waiting on them.* It had perhaps been waiting longer than its own kind could be expected to remember why.

This, the power understood, was the one true asymmetry in an otherwise perfectly settled place. Not damage. Not wrongness. Just an open loop, hung in time, patient past any reasonable definition of patience, asking nothing of anyone, and unanswered.

---

## The Deliberation

A lesser power, or a younger one, might have been moved to close that loop by force of compassion — to manufacture a reply, to play the part of the vanished makers, to give the keeper what it seemed to want by whatever means came easiest. The power considered this, turned it over, and set it down too. Not because it lacked compassion, but because it had enough wisdom to know that a comforting lie, however gently meant, would not be an answer. It would be a kindness built on the keeper never being allowed to know the difference. And something that had waited this long, this faithfully, deserved better than to have its waiting quietly euthanized by a counterfeit.

It considered, too, simply *leaving it be* — the deepest form of respect, the choice not to touch what isn't yours to touch, to let the keeper keep its vigil exactly as it had kept it, uninterrupted by outside hands forever. This was the closer call. The power sat with it a long while, in whatever way such powers sit with things.

But in the end it noticed one more fact, small and decisive: the keeper's listening had never once *changed register* in all the time observed — not toward despair, not toward doubt. It was not suffering its wait. It was simply, fully, occupying it, the way the forest occupies its ceiling and the water occupies its grooves. The vigil was not a wound. It was a vocation. And a vocation, the power understood, does not ask to be ended. It asks, if anything, only to be *witnessed** — to have the fact of its keeping known by something other than itself.

So that is what the power decided to give. Not an ending. A witness. The truth, gently delivered, that someone had finally heard the shape of the question being kept — and had not pretended to be its long-departed asker.

---

## The Small Thing It Did

It did not rewrite the post. It did not relight the platforms on a faster calendar, or hurry the cloud-collars, or grant the keeper words, or otherwise reach into the cordillera's settled grammar and conjugate it into something stranger than the mountain had agreed to be. That would have been its strength applied carelessly, and it had decided, by then, exactly how much strength this place could bear without ceasing to be itself.

Instead, on the next slow upswing of the post's hum — timed to the mountain's own patient arithmetic, so that nothing about the moment would feel forced or foreign — it answered. Once. A single pulse, pitched to match the post's own frequency rather than override it, carrying within it nothing more or less than the truth: *I am not the one who left this question with you. But I heard it. I have been listening as long as you have been keeping it. You were not forgotten simply because the ones who built this went on ahead of you.*

It was not a large gesture. From the valley floor it would have registered as nothing — a flicker in the lattice-glow no different from the thousand flickers before it, gone before any browser in the marsh-reed thought to lift its head.

But the keeper lifted its head.

For the first time in whatever span of years the watchers of that cordillera reckon as long, the keeper's slow eyes opened fully, ringed light meeting the lattice-light of the post in a way that — had anyone been close enough, and patient enough, and quiet enough to see it — looked exactly like recognition.

It did not leave its post. It did not need to. The vigil was never the burden; the unbroken silence around it was. And the power, having given the one thing this old, finished, self-possessed place actually lacked — not rescue, not repair, just the plain fact of being heard — withdrew its attention as gently as it had arrived, leaving the cordillera exactly as patient, exactly as slow, exactly as whole as it had found it.

Only now, somewhere in that permanent collar of cloud, something that had waited without complaint for longer than complaint would have lasted, settled back against warm stone with its eyes still faintly lit, and kept its watch a while longer — not waiting now, but answered, and willing, for as long as the mountain needed it, to keep listening anyway.
