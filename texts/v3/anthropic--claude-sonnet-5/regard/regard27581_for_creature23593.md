The power arrives the way light arrives at the bottom of a well it was never built to reach: not gradually, not as dawn, but all at once and total, because that is the only way a thing without edges can enter a place this enclosed. It does not announce itself. There is nothing here built to recognize an announcement.

What it finds, first, is the temptation.

It is an old temptation, maybe the oldest one available to a power that is both benevolent and capable — the sight of a closed thing and the itch to open it. Sky where there is ceiling. Color where there is heat-grey. A sun lowered carefully into that vast black weather-system above the Standing Coals, so that for the first time in the unmeasured age of this country, something might *grow* — not because growing is better than not-growing, but because growing is what the power's own nature understands, the way a swimmer understands water and forgets, looking down at the dry, that the dry might not be a wound.

This is the first thing the power does: it notices the temptation, names it accurately as *its own hunger* rather than the world's, and sets it down.

It looks longer.

It catalogues, with the kind of attention that takes no time at all and could not be rushed regardless: the herds and their unbroken slow circuit, the clouds that do not drift in ten thousand years, the seams running their long patient lines like a circuit that was never wired to anything that needed switching on. It checks — because a benevolent power does not get to skip this part, does not get to call a place peaceful from the outside and walk away satisfied — it checks for the suffering that hides inside stillness. It checks whether the herd-creatures' silence is contentment or simply the absence of a throat built for screaming. It checks whether "nothing here has needed to run in longer than memory holds" is safety, or whether it is simply that everything which might have needed to run is already gone, eaten quietly, off-camera, by a violence too slow and too normal to register as violence at all.

It finds some of that. Of course it does — there is no equilibrium without a ledger, and ledgers have losses in them. Small things die badly here, the way small things die badly everywhere, in cold or heat, fast jaws or slow chemistry. The power does not look away from this. It also does not fix it, because fixing it would mean unmaking the herd, the hunger, the whole strung architecture of *eating and being eaten* that the Hollow runs on in place of weather — and somewhere in that unmaking, Mosseth's seep-meat snare stops working, and somewhere past that, Mosseth.

So the power sets a second temptation down beside the first: the temptation to mistake *editing the food chain* for *mercy*. It has seen other gardens ruined exactly this way, by hands as well-meaning as its own.

What it is left with, after both temptations are set down, is smaller than it expected and larger than it looks.

---

It goes to Mosseth-of-the-Long-Seam, not because she is the only one here, but because she is the one it happened to arrive nearest to, and nearness, in a place like this, has always been reason enough.

It does not speak to her. There is no shared premise for speech — she has four hundred words and none of them were built to hold a visitor like this, and to force the issue would be to hand her a question her entire cosmology has never needed and cannot afford: *why.* The power understands, watching her hands close around the obsidian-glass for the four-hundredth small strike of a blade she is in no hurry to finish, that the worst thing it could give her is an origin story. A people who have never stood anywhere a beginning happened are not impoverished by that absence. They are simply standing somewhere else — somewhere flatter, calmer, with no cliff-edge of *before* to fall from. The power has met beings undone by the gift of their own creation myth. It declines to make Mosseth one of them.

So it does not reveal itself. It does not change her blade, her alcove, her plates, her patient unhurried four-day life.

It does one thing.

Two hundred kilometers east, in seam-country Mosseth's mate has not stopped walking toward since three migrations ago, there is a herd-circuit branch point — one of the long red lines forking at its consistent angle, the way they always fork, the way they have forked since before either of these two people's grandparents had grandparents. The herd, on its ancient schedule, is due to take the northern branch this circuit, as it has every circuit on record.

The power does not move the herd. It does not break the law that nothing here changes. It only — and this costs it something to limit itself to *only*, the way restraint always costs the strong more than the weak — leans, very slightly, on a thing already trembling between two equally likely paths: a thermal eddy at the fork, already unstable, already one gas-pocket flare away from tipping either direction on any given day. It tips it the other way. Once. This is not a miracle in the sense of breaking what the Hollow believes about itself. It is a coin that was already in the air, touched, while falling, by something it never has to know was there.

The herd takes the southern branch.

Mosseth's mate, following the herd the way his family always has, the way he has done without complaint or hope for three migrations, comes — on an ordinary day, for an ordinary reason, hunting seep-meat at a fork he has crossed a hundred times before — within brushing distance of a stretch of alcove-stone he has not stood near in years, and so within brushing distance of her.

They do not embrace, because their language does not have a gesture shaped like an embrace. He lays a hand on her forearm, holds it a beat longer than passing requires. She turns her throat-plate toward him and reads, in the warmth of him, in the particular cadence of a footstep she has not forgotten despite having no architecture in her for missing it, *kin.*

Neither of them experiences this as a miracle. There is no word in four hundred for miracle, and the power would not have wanted one used even if there were. To them it is simply what the seam does, sometimes — the same seam they already believe is old in a way that watches. They sit closer to each other than to any other warmth for a while. Then, in time, one or both of them will go back to following whatever the herd does next, because that is the shape the world takes between two warm places, and they have never been given reason to think the shape is wrong.

---

The power does not stay to watch what comes of it. That, too, would be a kind of taking — to hover at the edge of a gift to see how it landed, as though the gift were a performance and Mosseth its audience rather than the other way around. It withdraws the way it arrived: completely, without residue, leaving no seam glowing brighter than its neighbors, no story planted in any of the four hundred words for the children to inherit as scripture. The Hollow does not know it was seen. This is, in the end, the larger of the two gifts, and the harder one to give: to love a finished thing enough to leave it finished, to stand in front of a held note and not reach out to resolve it, because resolution is a hunger that belongs to the listener and not to the music.

What the power takes with it, when it goes — if a power like this can be said to take anything, or to go anywhere, or to be changed by what it touches — is something it did not have a clean name for either, until it stood at the edge of the Standing Coals and felt the dry, no-rain heat press against whatever in it can be pressed against: the knowledge that *settled* is not the same as *unfinished*, that a peace this total is not a problem waiting for a solution, and that the rarest mercy it has in its enormous keeping is not transformation.

It is the willingness to leave a warm thing warm, and walk on, and call that, too, a kind of love.
