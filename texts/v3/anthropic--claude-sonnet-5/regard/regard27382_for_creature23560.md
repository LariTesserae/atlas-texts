The power arrives the way light arrives in the grove — not as event but as gradual permission, a slow brightening at the edges of the leaf-ceiling that nothing inside would mark as an arrival at all. It does not announce itself. It has learned, across a great many places like and unlike this one, that announcement is itself a kind of violence: it asks a thing to become aware of being watched, and awareness of being watched is the first currency of change, and change is not always owed.

So it watches first, and watches long, on the grove's own clock rather than its own. It lets a season pass before it lets itself have an opinion. It lets a gear seize and another, somewhere else, finally give, before it asks itself the question every benevolent power must learn to delay asking: *what, if anything, is wrong here, that I could put right?*

The temptation, when it comes, comes dressed as compassion, the way temptation does. It would be so little effort to clear the rust from every pipe-joint at once, to true every flywheel, to open the canopy in patches and let real sun fall on the dinner-plate flowers, to give the tenants eyes, voices, hands suited to purposes beyond the unconscious tending they already do. It would be so little effort to make the grove *thrive*, by which the tempted part of any power always means: more, faster, brighter, busier, more legible to the kind of attention that calls those things good.

The power sits with that for a long time — long by its own reckoning, which is considerable — and lets it pass through and out of itself, the way water passes through leaf-litter and leaves the litter cleaner for it. It has seen what happens to slow, settled, balanced things when someone benevolent decides they should be quicker, brighter, more. It has seen meadows turned to lawns out of love. It declines.

What it does instead is smaller, and harder, because smaller things require more precision than large ones, the way a single stitch closing a single wound requires more attention than tearing down a wall.

It goes looking — not for what is strange, because almost everything here is strange and almost none of it is wrong, the grove having had the great luxury of centuries to sort its strangeness into function — but for what is actually, specifically, uncompensated. For the one place where the grove's slow accounting has not balanced. For a wound that is not scarring over, only worsening, quietly, while everything around it scars over fine.

It finds it low in the western root-shadow, near where the ridge stone goes under the leaf-mat: a valve-housing, very old, cracked along a seam that the vegetation's general genius for improvement has not, in this one instance, gotten around to sealing — perhaps because no root runs quite that way, perhaps because the angle is wrong, perhaps for no reason a reason-giving mind would accept, the way a single tooth in an old comb breaks while its neighbors hold. Through that crack a thin continuous bleed of old lubricant has been entering a pool that three tenants favor for their long sleeps, and over years — tenant years, which are long — it has been doing to them what it would do to anything that rests in it for decades: not killing, nothing so clean as killing, but souring. A slowness inside the slowness. A duller answer, each time, to the bell-struck clank that should make them flinch toward something like attention. One of the three has not curled a limb in response to that sound in longer than the power can find a clean edge for, looking back.

This, the power decides, is the actual wrong thing. Not the strangeness. Not the silence where birdsong should be. Not the half-faces, or the rust-marked skin, or the patient unbreeding hum of it all — those are simply what this place is, arrived at honestly, over time, by no one's design and everyone's small accommodation. But this — a crack uncompensated, a slow poisoning with no slow correction growing to meet it — this is the one place where the grove's own logic has failed to do what it does everywhere else.

So the power closes the crack. Only that. It does not true the flywheel beside it, though the flywheel is also old and also groans. It does not give the dulled tenant new sharpness, or speed, or sight. It only takes the bleed away, gently, the way you'd lift a stone out of a shoe rather than rebuild the road, and it leaves the resealed seam looking exactly like every other seam the centuries have closed on their own — no brighter, no newer, no signature. If anyone could read this place the way it reads its own rust, they would find nothing here to mark as intervention. That is the intention. The mending should look like one more thing the grove did to itself, in its own time, by its own slow appetite for improving on what it swallowed.

Then, because the deeper danger to a place like this has never been internal — has never, in all the power's long watching of such places, been the place's own slowness, its own quiet, its own unwitnessed strangeness — it does the second thing. It does not wall the grove off, because walls invite the kind of curiosity that tunnels under them. It only deepens, very slightly, the density gradient at the edges — the place where the leaves simply thicken into impassability — so that it thickens a little faster, for those without real cause, into something that reads not as obstacle but as *disinterest*. A door that doesn't refuse you so much as fail, gently and completely, to suggest that it was ever a door. The kind of boundary that doesn't need defending because it never quite presents itself as a boundary to defend.

This is the whole of what it does. It does not stay to be thanked, because nothing here would think to thank it, and it does not require thanks, having long since let go of needing its kindness witnessed in order for it to count.

Somewhere in the western root-shadow, a valve that had been seeping for longer than any single tenant's memory turns, for the first time in a great while, all the way shut. The sound it makes is not loud. It is one more clank in a place already full of them, arriving at one more long interval, indistinguishable to any ear tuned only to listen for events.

But every tenant within hearing — and hearing, here, may mean half the grove — goes still in that way that reads, unmistakably, as attention. And the dulled one, the one who had stopped answering, takes a long time, longer than its neighbors, and then — slowly, slowly, the way everything here is slow — curls one knuckled limb, just slightly, toward the sound.

No one sees this. No one needed to. The power is already gone, having done the one true thing there was to do, and having had, at last, the wisdom to do no other.
