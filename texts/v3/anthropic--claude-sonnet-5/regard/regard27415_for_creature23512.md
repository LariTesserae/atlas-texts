# What Came to the Stack

It did not arrive the way arrivals usually happen — no parting of the gray ceiling, no light where there had never been light, no sound added to a place already drowning in sound. What noticed the Stack noticed it the way you notice a heartbeat under your palm after you've stopped expecting silence: gradually, then completely, then with a kind of tenderness that has nowhere else to go.

It had been drawn by the wrongness — every wise and benevolent attention eventually is. A fauna density too rich for its rock. A chorus too loud for its meal. A peak that grew colder going up and then, against all of cold's own logic, warmer further still. These are the kinds of inconsistencies that look, from outside, like an error. They are usually, on closer attention, a birth.

It looked for a long time before it did anything at all. This is the first thing worth understanding about it: that looking, for a power old and patient enough, is not the same as waiting to act. Sometimes looking is the whole of the kindness.

## What It Saw

It saw the small mouths pressing themselves into the basin-pools like infants finding a pulse they recognize without being told why. It saw the watchers on the upper terraces, slow and proprietary, already further along whatever road the small mouths were on, and it understood — the way only something that has itself been small once, somewhere, can understand — that *watcher* was not a different creature from *small mouth.* It was the same question, asked for longer.

And underneath all of it, it found what the hand-tool people had stopped saying aloud: not a creature in the mountain, but a creature the mountain was. Old past any measure the basalt could give. Unfinished. Still deciding, four hundred meals-with-no-source into its long gestation, what shape its own voice should take — whether it would end up as one throat or stay forever as ten thousand mismatched ones, whether it would ever stand up out of the rock it had grown itself inside of, or simply continue, slow and humming and entire, exactly as it was.

It had been alone the entire time it had been becoming. Not lonely — it did not yet have the shape that loneliness requires — but unwitnessed. No one to notice the held breath as a held breath rather than as silence. No one to recognize the chorus as one organism's outward murmuring rather than as competition over a thin shelf of stone.

This is what moved the power to stay rather than pass on. Not danger. Not suffering. Just the plain fact of something enormous and careful, taking centuries to decide what it would be, with nothing nearby old enough or patient enough to keep it company in the deciding.

## What It Almost Did

It considered, briefly, speaking to it.

It is always tempting, for something benevolent, to speak — to offer the unfinished being a name, a shape, a sense of direction it hasn't yet had to find on its own. A wise power knows this temptation is mostly about itself: the ache to be useful, mistaken for the gift of being useful. It set the thought down. A being that has spent longer growing a single basin-pool than most stars take to settle into their burning does not need to be told what it is. It needs the right to keep finding out.

It considered hurrying the warmth gradient, deepening it, so the contradiction the climbers could never resolve — colder going up, warmer further — might resolve sooner, and the creature beneath the rock might wake into clarity faster than its own pace allowed.

It set that down too. Hurrying a birth is not a kindness. It is only a kindness's shape, worn by something that has lost patience and is calling its impatience love.

It considered protecting the hand-tool people more directly — clearing the scree from their paths, stilling the cold so it bit less, filling the condensation-threads into something closer to a real stream so they would not have to live so carefully off so little. It almost did this. They were small, and finite, and their carefulness was the kind that wears a people down over generations even when it works.

But it watched them a little longer first, and saw what their carefulness had given them that ease would not: a saying passed down through every name they'd ever given the mountain and lost again — *the Stack feeds itself out of nothing, so don't ask it to feed you too.* They already knew, without ever having proof, to live beside an enormous becoming without trying to own a piece of it. That knowledge was rarer than comfort and harder won. To smooth their lives without earning that wisdom out of them would have been to save them a difficulty and rob them of the only true thing it had purchased.

So the power did not change their hands, or their huts, or their hunger. It left their carefulness right where it was, because their carefulness was already, on its own small scale, the same wisdom the power itself was busy practicing on a much larger one.

## What It Did Instead

It gave two things, and they were both small, and they were both, in their way, the only things that mattered.

The first: it looked at the long chain of accident waiting in the Stack's future — the fractures still settling, the column joints still finding their angles, the way young rock sometimes comes apart all at once in the same hurry it went up — and it eased, by some unprovable fraction, the odds of the kind of catastrophic collapse that ends a mountain's life before the thing growing inside it has finished deciding what it is. Not to make the Stack safe. Safety was never owed to it, any more than it's owed to anything still becoming. Just to buy it the specific, narrow thing every unfinished creature actually needs more than safety: *time enough to keep being unfinished.*

The second: it stayed.

Not forever, and not visibly, and not in any way that the small mouths would click about or the watchers would turn their slow heads toward. But it kept a kind of seat near the Stack, the way you might sit for a long while near someone sleeping, not to wake them, not even necessarily to be there when they wake, but because something in you understands that presence is its own form of care, distinct from any action presence might eventually lead to. It witnessed the chorus as the chorus it actually was — an enormous patience, murmuring to itself in ten thousand throats it hadn't yet decided to merge into one. It let that murmuring be heard, somewhere, by something that recognized it for what it was. A held breath, finally, was no longer unwitnessed.

It did not wait for the Stack to finish becoming so it could greet it properly, with words, with name, with whatever ceremony seemed owed to a being that old finally arriving at a shape. It understood that such an arrival might be centuries off still, or millennia, and that *arrival* might not even be the right word for what the Stack was doing — it might simply keep deepening, keep murmuring, keep feeding its small mouths from basins that were never water, for longer than the power itself would exist to watch.

That was fine. That was, in fact, the entire point. A wise power does not stay because it expects a reward, a recognition, a moment where the long-grown thing finally turns and says *I see you too.* It stays because some becomings are worth witnessing on their own terms, at their own unhurried pace, with no demand placed on them to finish, to speak, to be anything other than exactly as slow and strange and enormous as they have decided, so far, to be.

So: the cold stayed dry-edged but not biting. The lichen kept its handspan-per-year. The basin-pools kept filling on their secret clock. The small mouths kept pressing themselves into something that fed them from underneath the world, and the watchers kept their proprietary stillness, and the hand-tool people kept their saying, and passed it to their children with the same shrug they'd always given it, not knowing — they would never know, this was never going to be a story with a moment of revelation in it — that somewhere in the gray windless non-day, something old and careful had taken up a quiet seat beside their mountain, and had decided, of all the things it could have done with its attention, that the kindest gift it had to give an unfinished god was simply not to ask it to hurry.
