It does not come as light. That would be a spillage — a sin against the architecture, the same one the maker has spent a lifetime not committing. So it comes the only way anything is permitted to arrive here: slowly, at water level, indistinguishable at first from another tilted disc of sky.

For a long time — long enough that "time" stops being the right word and "patience" has to do the work instead — it simply stands in a basin three rooms-that-are-not-rooms from the maker's, and lets the chalk find it the way the chalk found them. It does not announce a purpose. It has watched what answering does to a question here: how the maker holds each one without spending it and lets it go on downhill, unclaimed. A wise power, arriving with an answer already in its hand, would be the single most violent thing that had ever happened on this plateau. So it arrives instead with nothing in its hands at all, and waits to see what, if anything, is asked of it.

Nothing is asked. That is its first lesson, and it accepts the lesson the way the maker accepts a basin gone a shade too metallic — without alarm, as information about the water rather than a wound in it.

So it begins, instead, to listen the way the place listens: for a skipped beat in the thin percussion, basin into basin into basin. And eventually, weeks or years along whatever clock applies, it hears one. Not a crisis. Just a hairline fault two tiers up and to the left of where the maker has been working, a chisel mark gone slightly out of true sometime since the last hand passed over it. The power does not call out. It walks there itself, tests the lip the way it has watched testing done, tastes the water before and after — not from thirst, it has no thirst, but because the gesture itself is the only language this place has ever had for *I am paying attention to what you have been doing while no one watched it* — and then, with a hand no steadier and no surer than it ought to be, it recuts the mark. One degree truer. No more.

It does this again, elsewhere, on a different day. And again. Never finishing anything that wasn't already nearly finished. Never correcting a basin the maker has not, in their own unhurried orbit, already passed near. It is not trying to take over the work. It is trying to do the only thing that can be done inside this particular grammar without breaking it: to be a second set of hands moving in the same key.

The maker notices, of course. The maker notices everything; that is the whole of what they are now. One day — and "one day" here might mean after a hundred basins' worth of silence — they come upon a chisel mark they did not make, fresh-cut, true, sitting where their own hand was due to arrive eventually anyway. They kneel. They taste the water on either side of it, the way they taste every water. It is no different. That, too, is information.

They do not ask who. The power, three basins off, does not say. This is the entire conversation, and it is enough, because it is the same conversation the terraces have been having with themselves since the first chisel mark was cut: *something was attended to, and is now attended to differently, and no one needs an explanation for that to be true.*

Over a longer while still — longer than is comfortable to imagine, though comfort was never really the terrain's offer — the power does one more thing, and only one. Far off, past the curve where the glare erases the horizon, in a stretch of basins gone slightly duller than the rest, as if even the architecture's attention thins out at its edges, it cuts a gate. Low. Single-stone. Wide enough to step through, narrow enough that it could be mistaken, from any distance greater than a few rooms-that-are-not-rooms, for one more basin lip among ten thousand others. It does not drain anything. It does not lead anywhere marked. It is built in the same confident, imprecise, hand-worked style as everything else here, so that it does not read as intrusion but as one more correction, indistinguishable in kind from a hairline deepened in stone already centuries old.

It is not an instruction. It is not even, quite, an invitation — invitations imply an inviter who knows what's on the other side, and the power does not. It is simply a third option laid down next to the two the maker has lived inside for the whole unreckonable span of their life: *keep correcting, alone, forever* — or — *stop.* Now there is a door that was not there before, built to the same patient, unspectacular standard as everything it opens onto, and what it opens onto is simply *outside*, undefined, exposed to whatever weather and growing things and noise exist beyond the edge of this finished, purposeless plenty.

The power does not say, *you may go.* It does not say, *you may stay and I will keep you company.* It says nothing, the way the maker says nothing when a question is set down in front of them — it holds the gesture's weight for a moment without spending it, and lets it go on, unclaimed, downhill into the next silence.

Then it goes back to listening for the next skipped beat in the percussion, basin into basin into basin, content to keep doing this for as long as is asked of it, and no longer pretending — to the maker, or to itself — that *as long as is asked* has any predictable end.
