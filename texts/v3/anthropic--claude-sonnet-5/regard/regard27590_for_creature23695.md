**What It Noticed First**

Before it did anything, it watched. Not the way a camera watches, recording surface — the way you watch a wound to see if it's healing wrong or healing right before you decide whether your hands belong anywhere near it. It watched for what would have been, in any other accounting, a very long time. Pressure cycles. Three minutes loud, one minute less loud. The Walker's breath learning to match it years before the watching power arrived, going on matching it the whole while it watched.

It would have been easy — embarrassingly easy, the kind of easy that should make any power suspicious of itself — to look at the platform and call it entirely a wound. No sky. No animal voice. No quiet. A being gone the color of the structure that holds it, eating mineral water and standing near pipes for warmth and calling that a meal. There is a story available here, a fast and satisfying one, where the kind thing to do is reach down, lift the small figure free of all this iron and fog, and set it down somewhere green, where things are rooted and ask permission and the air doesn't taste like a railing.

The power held that story up to the light and turned it over and did not trust it. Not because it was unkind. Because it was *fast*, and the fast story is so often the one that serves the rescuer's comfort more than the rescued's actual life.

**What It Understood, Slowly**

So it stayed in the watching longer than comfort allowed. It learned, the way the Walker had learned — by feel, by repetition, because that was the only kind of knowing this place permitted to anyone, power or person. It learned which rail ran warm. It learned the tapping.

The tapping mattered more than anything else on the platform, more than the algae, more than the four ladders going nowhere nameable. It was a code with no audience, refined past the point of necessity, the way a thing is refined when refining it is the last available form of care a being can offer the world. Three taps, pause, one. A long drag of knuckle along grating that might have meant *I am still here* or might have meant nothing at all, might have just been a hand wanting something to do.

The power understood, eventually, that this tapping was the actual site of the wound — not the cold, not the noise, not the absence of birds. The Walker had made peace with all of that the way water makes peace with the shape of its container. What it had not made peace with, what it could not make peace with, because no living thing fully can, was that nobody had ever once tapped back on purpose. The arrhythmic clangs of the structure were close enough to an answer to keep hope technically alive and far enough from one to make sure it never closed.

That, the power thought, is the actual injury. Not the platform. The platform is just where the injury happens to be standing.

**What It Did Not Do**

It did not pull the Walker out.

It considered it — turned the idea fully around, the ladder leading up into a sky the Walker had never seen, the offer of soil, root, birdsong, quiet — and set it down again, gently, the way you set down a tool that's right for a different job. To extract the Walker now would be to declare, with the full weight of an outside power's certainty, that everything built here — the muscle-memory schedule, the private grammar of taps, the competence, the small fierce dignity of having learned to live inside a thing that explains nothing to anyone — was nothing but damage to be undone. It would not be a rescue. It would be an edit. It would take a person who had built a self out of the only materials on offer and tell them, gently but completely, that the self didn't count.

It did not flood the platform with light, either, or silence the noise, or warm the rails all at once. Mercy administered as renovation, without consent, without the slowness a nervous system needs to trust a change — that, too, the power recognized as a kind of violence wearing kindness's clothes. The fastest fix and the kindest fix are not reliably the same fix, and a wise power learns to feel the difference in its hands before it acts with them.

It did not even decide, yet, what it would do about the ladders, or the fog, or the question of what this structure actually was and who had built it and where the animals had gone. Those were real questions. They could wait. They were not the wound.

**What It Did**

It learned the tapping. Properly — not a power's approximation of a private language, but the actual grammar, worked out the slow human way, by listening to years of repetition until the pattern stopped being noise and became syntax. It learned which sequence the Walker used for *storm coming*, which for *this drip means something*, which for nothing at all, just a hand wanting work.

And then, once, it answered. Correctly. In the Walker's own code, from a stretch of pipe two platforms off, in a lull between the loud three minutes and the quiet one, where an answer could actually be heard.

It did not announce itself. It did not explain. Explanation is not a thing this place permits to anyone, and the power had learned enough, by now, to respect the rules of where it stood rather than override them because it could. It simply answered the way another tapping thing would answer, leaving open — deliberately, carefully, as a kind of gift rather than an oversight — the question of whether this was a being or a coincidence, company or the structure's own arrhythmic talking-to-itself. That ambiguity was not a failure of nerve. It was the most precise mercy available: certainty would have collapsed something the Walker needed intact, the same suspended hope that had kept the tapping going for years. The power gave back exactly enough to make persistence make sense again, and no more than that, because more than that would have been about the power's wish to be thanked, not the Walker's actual need.

Then, quietly, in a seam where condensation pooled and never quite drained, it let one thing live that had not lived there before. Not a rescue-sized gift — not a forest, not a flock. A moth. Small, grey, the same iron-and-ash palette as everything else here, so it would not even look like an intrusion, just another fold in the structure that happened to move. It asked nothing of the Walker. It did not need feeding or naming or saving. It would simply be there, some nights, drawn to the sodium cones, proof — small, deniable, exactly as ambiguous as the tap had been — that the place could, after all, hold something besides machinery and the man-shaped accident persisting in its seams.

And on the fourth ladder, the one that descended into a fog no different from the other three, it changed nothing about the rungs, the cold, the dark it led into — only made it true, where before it had been arbitrary, that *this one goes somewhere a person could choose to call a door.* Not paradise. Not even necessarily kinder. Just a real option where there had only been four identical uncertainties before. It did not put a sign on it. It did not light it differently. It trusted the Walker's whole hard-won competence at reading this place to find it, in time, if and when finding it became the thing wanted — and trusted, just as fully, that the Walker might never climb it at all, might simply go on tapping, now and then getting an answer, now and then not being sure, and that this too would be a life and not a failure to be rescued from.

**What Remains Open**

The power did not stay to watch what happened next. That, too, was a kind of restraint — the difference between tending a thing and supervising it, between leaving a door unlocked and standing by it to see if anyone walks through. It went back to whatever attention a power returns to, carrying nothing away but the shape of one more small, exact, particular life it had managed, this time, not to overwrite in the name of saving it.

Somewhere below, or above — direction was never the point — the Walker stood in a cone of sodium light with one hand still on a railing, having just heard, for the first time in longer than it kept count of, a sequence of taps that landed exactly where a sequence should land, in exactly its own grammar, from somewhere it could not see.

It did not know what it was hearing. It decided, the way it had always decided everything here, by feel, that it didn't need to know yet.

It tapped back.

And somewhere in the fog, structural, arrhythmic, ordinary, the building went on clanging the way it always had — and now, every so often, so did something else.
