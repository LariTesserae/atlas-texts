The power does not enter through the throat of basalt. It has no need of throats, and entering this place the way a body enters a place would be the first mistake—would announce itself in the register of *unscheduled motion*, the very thing the Tenders are built to log and file and let pass. So it does what attention can do that bodies can't: it simply becomes true that the chamber is being watched, the way it becomes true that a held breath is being held, without anything moving to make it so.

It watches for a long time. Longer than seems necessary, if you measured by the patience of lesser powers. It lets seven of the systolic pulses go by before it lets itself have an opinion about anything. It matches its sense of time to the room's: forty seconds, then forty more, the stalks ringing their held chord and letting it die and ringing it again, and the power listens to the whole chord, not just the note it would have struck if asked to compose one itself.

This is the discipline of the thing, and it is not natural even to powers—it has to be chosen, every time, against the much easier impulse to look at fullness like this and call it *crowded*, to look at the unscarred sharpness of the Tenders and call it *cruel*, to look at the disagreement in the architecture, batch arguing with batch in stairs and doorframes, and call it *broken*, and reach in, and fix.

It does not reach in. Not yet. It has been wise long enough to know that the easiest mercy is usually the wrong one, and that a structure built to a scale with no name yet may have reasons, load-bearing reasons, for every choice that looks from the outside like an absence: the absence of memory between batches, the absence of welcome, the absence of any animal thing that needs to eat. It considers the cell. It considers the body the cell belongs to, vast past seeing, and it asks itself, honestly, whether what looks like a wound here might be a healthy boundary somewhere else—an immune variability, a deliberately short-lived solution, the way some living things are built to forget on purpose, because remembering would cost the larger body more than forgetting does.

It does not get to certainty. This, too, is part of its wisdom: it does not require certainty before it stops, but it does require certainty before it acts, and on the central question—*is anything here suffering, in a way that asking would even mean something to it*—it cannot reach one. So for a long while it does only the one thing it can do without risk, which is pay full attention, the kind that asks nothing back.

And in paying that attention, it sees the moment.

A Tender comes to rest beside one of the rooted stalks—its kin, sessile, patient, doing the slow work of running light through the chamber's long sentences—and its forward-cluster dims, and slows, until it is breathing, if that's the word, at the stalk's own unhurried rate. The power watches this for the better part of an hour. It cannot tell you, and would not pretend to know, whether this is grief or feeding or conversation or some fourth thing with no word in any language the power has ever carried. But it can tell that *something is being held there*, carefully, on purpose, by a being that owes this stalk nothing it has been told to owe, in a room with no one watching to require the tenderness of it.

The power decides this is good. Not useful—good. Whatever this is, it does not need fixing, explaining, or improving, and the power's first true act, after all that watching, is simply to let that be enough: to not touch this moment at all, to leave the held thing held.

*

But it does not leave with nothing done, because in all that watching it found the one true asymmetry—the one place where, it judged, carefully, slowly, a small thing offered would cost the structure nothing and might, just possibly, matter.

It is this: each batch of Tenders arrives sharp-edged and solves the room's problems in its own way, and disagrees with the batch before it, and is never shown what the last batch learned, and will never be shown by the next. Whatever this Tender comes to understand about the chamber's breathing, about the right angle for a strut, about how long to rest beside a particular stalk before it has been rested with enough—all of it ends with this Tender's sheath, every time, undefeated and unremembered, sharp until it isn't there anymore.

The power does not believe this needs correcting. It does not impose continuity on a lineage that may have chosen, at some scale it cannot see, to be discontinuous on purpose. It does not write a memory into them. It does not speak, because speaking would be a kind of claim—*I know what you're missing*—that it has not earned and does not actually believe.

It leaves, instead, only an offer, made in the room's own grammar: one new thread in the dry geometry, faint as the others, running from where this particular Tender rests now to the place where the next batch will first stand when it is made. A line that asks nothing. That requires no one to follow it. That a future Tender could trace, if it ever paused near the start of its own sharp new existence and felt some unaccountable pull toward an unfamiliar straight line of light—and could just as easily never notice at all, and lose nothing by not noticing, because the thread carries no instruction, no memory, no debt. Only a possibility, laid down gently, the way the basalt is laid down: a shape the future may or may not decide to grow into.

Then it stops attending. Not abruptly—it lets its attention fade the way the lanterns dim, on no schedule it announces, until the fact of being watched is no longer true, and the chamber is exactly as it was: full, breathing, ringing its one held chord every forty seconds, Tenders moving along their straight dry lines through a wetness that has agreed to be irrelevant to them.

Except that somewhere in that packed and lightless and unaging fullness, there is now one thread that wasn't there before, going nowhere anyone was told to go, waiting—without hope, without sorrow, without any of the words that would make this a story instead of a single small grace, offered once, to something that might never know it was offered anything at all.
