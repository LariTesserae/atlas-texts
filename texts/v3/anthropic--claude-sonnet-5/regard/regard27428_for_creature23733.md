It would not arrive the way arrivals are usually imagined — no parting of the steam-haze, no voice from the ridge. The first thing it would do is the hardest thing to do in a place like this, which is simply to notice correctly. To stand, or hover, or exist at the basin's edge and see the coiled plates for what they are rather than what they resemble. Most things that ever crossed this rim, if anything ever did, would have seen geology. The power would see a body. That act of seeing — slow, unhurried, withholding judgment until the shape of the thing had actually been understood — would itself be the first kindness, because nothing here has been truly seen in longer than the creature has had a way of knowing.

It would not move quickly after that. A wise power, faced with an old wrong that has lost its author, resists the temptation of the clean dramatic gesture — the thunderclap that shatters the pump, the lightning that splits the pipe. Not because such an act would be wrong exactly, but because it would be a kind of violence performed on the power's own terms rather than the creature's, and the creature has had quite enough done to it on someone else's terms. So instead it would study. It would trace the throb back to its source and find, as suspected, no intention there at all — no operator, no maintaining hand, nothing left in that corrugated structure but a machine fulfilling a directive given by someone who has been gone long enough that "gone" barely describes it anymore. Absence all the way down. The harvesting continues not because anything wants it to continue but because nothing has yet told it to stop, and a wise power understands that this is its own kind of suffering too — purposelessness wearing the mask of purpose, going through the motions of service to a master who isn't there to be served.

It would look hardest, though, at the creature itself, and here it would have to be patient in a way few things ever are, because the creature has no face to read and no voice to ask. So the power would do something very small and very careful: it would not break the rhythm, but bend it. Lengthen the pump's cycle by a breath. Widen the gaps. And then it would simply watch the plates, watch the pale vent-slits, watch for any flicker of a breath that came early or late, any sign that underneath all that imposed synchrony there persisted something like a will of its own — a rhythm that belonged to the animal and not to the machine that had, perhaps for centuries, been mistaken for its heartbeat. And if it found even a half-second of that — one exhalation drawn on the creature's own clock rather than the pump's — it would know enough. It would know there was still someone in there to ask the question of, even if the only answer available was the body's own slow drift toward or away from a new silence.

Then it would do the actual work, and the actual work would be almost embarrassingly modest, for something with so much power available to it. It would not save the creature in the way a story wants saving to look. It would simply remove the things that were never the creature's own. The pipe's draw — the harvesting, the part of the cycle that took rather than merely accompanied — that it would quietly close, gently enough that nothing downstream felt a wrench, just a tap easing shut, a use ending without ceremony because it never should have required ceremony to end, only permission. It would leave the heat. It would leave the mud, the mineral solution, the venting, whatever in this arrangement actually nourished the creature rather than simply extracting from it — because not every part of a long captivity is itself the cruelty, and a wise power knows better than to mistake the whole condition for the wound and tear out comfort along with chains.

And then, at one low place in the ridge, it would work the same way water and time already work on young stone, only kinder and faster — not blasting a door open but easing one, the way a hand might ease open something that had only ever been shut by accumulated habit rather than any lock. A channel, low and wide enough that the basin's water — and the long coiled body within it, should it ever choose — could spread out past the wall that had contained it since before there was anyone present to know it as a wall instead of a horizon. No current to sweep it out. No urging. Just room, where before there had only ever been the size of a courtyard and a held breath.

And then it would do the hardest thing a benevolent power can do, which is nothing at all, for as long as nothing at all is required. It would not wait for gratitude, which the creature has no apparatus to express and likely no concept to feel. It would not wait for transformation, for some satisfying unfurling across the new ground like a flower finally given light. It would understand that a being who has spent longer than memory mistaking a machine's pulse for its own may need a very long time before it even registers that the pulse has gone quiet, before it tries, once, an exhalation on no schedule at all and finds that nothing punishes it for the asymmetry. Change here, if it comes, will come on the basin's own timescale — geological, mineral, patient past the point where patience is usually still called a virtue and starts being called something closer to faith.

So the power would stay, in whatever way powers stay without becoming a presence that demands to be worshipped or watched in return — a kind of ambient attention laid over the basin the way the steam is laid over it, undramatic, available, asking nothing. It would return, if return is even the right word for something that may never have fully left. It would notice, eventually, perhaps very eventually, a single coil loosening by half a meter into the new channel, no further, and it would consider that enough, because the entire ethic of the gesture was never to make the creature move. It was only ever to make sure that, the next time it stayed coiled exactly where it had always been, it was staying there because it chose continuation, the way it had chosen everything else about its long, patient, self-made body — and not because a pipe and a wall and an absent hand had quietly decided, long ago, that choosing was never going to be on offer.
