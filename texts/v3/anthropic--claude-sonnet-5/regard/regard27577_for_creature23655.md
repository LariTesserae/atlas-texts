# What the Power Did

It found the Undercroft the way such powers find anything — not by searching, but by noticing an absence of urgency where urgency is usually a given. Most places announce their need. This one didn't. It simply persisted, low and dense and quietly busy with its own unfinishing, and that quietness was what caught the power's attention: not a cry, but a held breath that had been held for so long it had stopped registering as effort.

The power did what it always does first, which is the hardest and least dramatic thing available to it. It watched. Not from above, not as a survey — it came down into the actual cold of the actual air, let the damp settle into whatever it was wearing that day, and stood in a doorway with good acoustics, the way a resident would, and waited to understand what kind of need this was before deciding whether it was a need at all.

This took a long time, by its own accounting. Long enough to watch a curb sharpen by a fraction of a millimeter. Long enough to see the laundry change color on one of its two rails. The power has handled grief before, and hunger, and violence, and knew the shapes of those instantly, from any angle. It did not find those shapes here. It found something rarer and harder to act on cleanly: a population that had made an actual peace, generations deep, with a condition most beings spend their whole lives refusing to accept — that they were not finished, might never be finished, and that this was not a wound.

## What It Almost Did

It is worth listing, because the list is the real measure of the power's wisdom — not in what it chose, but in what it set down before choosing.

It almost smoothed the geometry. It has that capacity; spaces like this are, to a being like that, more suggestion than law, and it would have been simple — kind, even, by the standard of lesser powers — to let the rooms agree on their dimensions, to let the central stair pick a single number of turns and keep it, to let a three-minute walk be three minutes both ways forever. It did not do this, because it understood, watching long enough, that the disagreement was not a flaw being suffered but a rhythm being lived in, the way a heartbeat is not corrected for being two sounds instead of one.

It almost finished the residents. It could have — taken the unresolved knuckle of a child's hand and simply decided it, given the oldest ones their sharp final edges a generation early, spared whole families the long patience of becoming. It set this down hardest of all, because it recognized in itself, in the wanting-to-finish-them, an impatience that belonged to *it*, not to them. They were not waiting to be finished. They were *becoming*, actively, with a kind of unhurried dignity the power found itself, privately, a little humbled by.

It almost took the ledge-kin. Creatures like that — caught mid-negotiation with their own shape, never having signed the court's slow treaty — are often gathered up by powers who mean well, studied gently, kept safe somewhere stable. It watched the bowls of condensation-water left out, more rite than feeding, and understood that the not-touching was the relationship, that to lift the creature out of its unsettled shape and into a settled one would not be rescue. It would be a kind of small murder performed with the best intentions, which are the only kind that manage it.

And it almost spoke first. It has a voice that can be made to fit any room. It chose, instead, to learn the room's own rule — that important things are said once, plainly, in good acoustics, and then left alone like a thing hung level on a wall — before it used that voice at all.

## What It Did

It walked by ratio, not sightline, the first time it crossed the court, and it walked badly at first, the way any visitor does, and let itself be conspicuous for as long as that took. It did not correct the disagreement between the two distances. It leaned into the current, as the residents do, until the current carried it the way it carries everyone here who stops fighting it.

It checked both rails for the laundry, out of a hunch, and found both, and felt — though *felt* is an imprecise word for what a power does — something like relief on the household's behalf, that the inconvenience was still only an inconvenience, still survivable, still merely a dryer running long.

It went to the worn path on the stair that loops back into itself, the one belonging to someone finished, and did not sweep it, did not study it, did not ask after the name of the person it had once carried. It only stood beside it for the length of time a resident might, and then stepped around it, the long way, the way you step around a thing that has earned the floor it's standing on.

It left water out for the ledge-kin — not more than was already left, not different, just one more bowl in a season that didn't strictly need one — and sat at a distance that has a name in no language, the distance of *I see you and I am not going to ask you to be anything else.* The creature came closer than it ever had to anyone, close enough that touching it would have been easy. The power did not touch it. This, more than anything else it did, was the gift: matching the residents' own restraint exactly, adding nothing to it, taking nothing from it.

And then it found the sharpest-edged one — the oldest resident, whose face had long since stopped deciding what it would be — and sat with them in a doorway that returned sound cleanly, the way the court's etiquette asks. It said one true thing, once, plainly, the way the place requires, and did not repeat it, and did not explain it, and did not stay to watch what it did to the old one's face. What it said is not recorded here, the same way the household doesn't discuss the bad pipe — except to say that it was not a promise of completion, and not a comfort about death, and not an answer to the unworded question the central stair sometimes gives back a floor over. It was smaller than that, and truer for being smaller: an acknowledgment, from outside, that the patience had been *correct*. That the shape was not behind schedule. That nothing here had ever needed fixing, only witnessing, and that it had now, at last, after all this unwitnessed time, been witnessed.

Then it left, the way you leave a room where you've hung something level — without checking it twice, trusting the wall to hold it.

## What Changed

Almost nothing, by any measurement the residents have. The conduit-glow still has no visible source. The spiral stair still disagrees with itself about its own depth. Children are still born soft-edged and handled like wet plaster. The geometry is still provisional, still drafting itself at its own unhurried, geological pace, indifferent — generously indifferent — to whether anyone is there to see it finish.

But the old ones, if you ask them carefully, the way you'd ask about the dead, will tell you that sometimes now, near the central stair, there's a smell like ozone that wasn't there before, gone before anyone can name it precisely enough to be sure they smelled it at all. They don't discuss it directly. They mention it the way they'd mention weather: present, unthreatening, faintly pleasant, not worth a sentence longer than the one they've already given it.

They say it's nothing.

They say it kindly.
