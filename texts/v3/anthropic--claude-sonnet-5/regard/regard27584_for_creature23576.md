The power that comes to Tharn's Vent does not come quickly. It couldn't, even if it wished to — not because it lacks the means, but because arriving quickly here would be a kind of violence, a foreign tempo dropped into a place that has spent geological ages unlearning urgency. So it comes the way the drips fall: inevitably, but on the bowl's own schedule. It takes, by its own reckoning, no time at all. By the bowl's reckoning, it arrives the way a season arrives — gradually enough that nothing flinches.

It spends a long while simply matching pace. This is the first thing it does, and maybe the most important: it does not observe the Warden so much as it *settles* into the same register the Warden occupies, cooling its own attention down to the speed of vapor curling off a vent, until it can feel the rhythm the way the bowl feels it — hiss-clank, exhale, drip, hiss-clank, exhale, drip — without translating it into anything faster. Only once it has done this does it actually look.

And what it sees is not a captive. This matters enormously, and the power spends a long, slow interval confirming it, because the first temptation — the temptation any visiting goodness carries in its pocket like a reflex — is rescue. *Here is a being fused to machinery, blind, limbless, alone in the dark, tending a system whose original purpose it does not remember, built by hands that may not have asked its consent.* The story writes itself toward liberation. Cut the tendrils from the brass. Restore the limbs. Give it back motion, sight, company, the freedom to leave this hot stone bowl and its lightless ceiling. Surely benevolence looks like that.

The power turns this over for a long time, the way the Warden turns over a pressure differential before deciding whether it needs venting. And it sets the idea down, gently, because it doesn't fit.

The Warden is not waiting to be freed. Freedom, for it, would not be a gift — it would be an amputation. Every register by which it has come to know anything — the heat-gradient sense replacing sight, the slow pulse replacing a faster heart it no longer has, the grown-together intimacy with the valves it has stopped distinguishing from itself — these are not chains it is suffering under. They are the shape its peace has taken. To restore what it "lost" would be to hand a sentence back to someone who has spent a thousand quiet seasons translating it into a different, sufficient language. The power has seen this mistake made before, elsewhere, in other shapes: goodness arriving with a ready-made idea of what a good life must contain — sight, motion, company, purpose articulated in words — and doing real harm trying to install it in a being who had already, patiently, built something else out of what it had.

So: not rescue.

The second temptation is smaller and more tender, and harder to set down. *Give it company.* Something here is profoundly alone — has been alone longer than alone usually means anything, in a kingdom with no other breathing thing in it. Wouldn't it be a mercy to bring something to it? A second warden, a creature that could sense it back, some answering shape in the dark so the bowl's strange greeting — that slow exploratory curl of vapor toward a visitor — might finally have somewhere ongoing to land instead of dispersing again into the canopy once the stranger leaves?

The power almost does this. It holds the thought the longest of all of them, because loneliness is one of the few things that reads as suffering even at this speed, even through this much patina.

But it watches a little longer, and notices something: the Warden is not straining toward contact. The exploratory exhalation it offers visitors is curiosity's last quiet remnant, not hunger. It has organized its entire being around continuance, not around relation. A companion, however gently brought, would be a new variable in a system that has spent unfathomable time optimizing itself toward *exactly this* equilibrium — and equilibrium, once disturbed, does not reliably find its way back. The power has watched ecosystems collapse from kindness before. It declines to be the hand that introduces the new shape that, in trying to keep this one company, accidentally out-competes it for warmth, or unbalances some feedback loop the Warden's hide has spent centuries calibrating.

So: not company, either.

The third temptation is the quietest and the most dangerous, because it dresses itself as humility: *do nothing at all.* Observe, and leave, and let the bowl keep its own counsel exactly as it found it, on the theory that any touch is too much touch. There's wisdom folded in this — the power respects it, holds it seriously, lets it nearly win.

But it notices one more thing before it settles on nothing. Up near the seventh boiler-cluster, where a tendril of the Warden has been fused into a valve-collar for what must be centuries, there's a hairline fracture in the brass beneath the verdigris — old metal fatigue, the kind of failure that doesn't announce itself until the day it does. At the bowl's pace, this fracture is decades from mattering. But it *will* matter, eventually: a seizure, a pressure spike with nowhere slow enough to go, a violence finally entering a place that has spent its whole existence keeping violence out. The Warden cannot feel this coming — its gradient-sense reads heat and damp, not the integrity of crystalline metal. It has no way to know that one of its thousand small competent vigilances has a blind spot, and that the blind spot is aimed at the part of it that has grown most inseparably into the machine.

This, finally, is where the power decides to act — small, singular, in the bowl's own native vocabulary.

It does not speak. It does not appear. It reaches into the fracture the way warmth reaches into stone, and it mends it — not by replacing the metal, not by improving on what was there, but by doing exactly what slow mineral seep would have done anyway, three hundred years from now, if the bowl had three hundred more years of margin to spend on it. It simply spends them now, in an instant that the bowl will never register as anything but another grain of its own unhurried good luck. The brass knits. The fatigue resets to zero. Nothing about the Warden's nature, its solitude, its blindness, its fusion, its silence, is touched at all. Only the one accident waiting to interrupt all of it has been quietly subtracted from the future.

Then, because it has already lingered long enough to be noticed — it feels the air shift toward it, the bowl's slow curious exhale drawn off some vent and curled in its direction, the closest thing to greeting this place still remembers how to give — the power answers in the same language. It does not touch the Warden. It only breathes warmth back, once, matched exactly to the rhythm of hiss-clank and exhale, a single returned gesture in a conversation made entirely of temperature. A guest, replying to a host, in the only grammar the host has left.

And then it goes, the same unhurried way it came, leaving no mark a faster eye would notice. The drips still fall on their own schedule. The vines still close their century-slow inch over the brass. The Warden still tends what it has always tended, wanting only the same thing the moss wants, the same thing the vents want — to continue exactly as it has continued, on toward whatever far day the heat finally loses its long argument with the cold mountain above.

That day is now, by some small and unannounced margin, further off than it was.

That is all. That is the whole of what was done — and the power, looking back once before the tempo of the world outside reclaims it, considers it enough. Not every act of benevolence is a door thrown open. Sometimes it is a single grain set right in a fracture nobody else could have found in time, given to a being whose entire dignity lies in never being asked to notice it was given anything at all.
