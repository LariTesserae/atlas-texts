# What the Power Would Do

It would not come down. There is nothing here built to receive a descent — no peak to land on, no temple to enter, no single throat to speak through. A power that arrived the way powers arrive in most stories, haloed and singular and *taller than the land*, would be the worst possible guest in a place that has spent geological ages grinding every tall thing down to the same patient flatness. So it would not come down. It would come *level*. It would arrive as a pressure change, the kind the chorus-mat would feel a full minute before any instrument could measure it, and it would wait there, at the height of the haze, asking nothing, announcing nothing, doing the one thing almost no visitor to the Flats has ever had the patience to do first.

It would listen.

Not for an afternoon. Not for the length of a storm-column's transit or a day's elastic, untrustworthy walk between two horizon-points that may or may not be where they were an hour ago. It would listen the way the land itself listens — across cycles, across the kind of time in which "patient" stops being a virtue and becomes simply the shape of attention itself. It would let the chorus swell past it and recede past it, ten thousand times, a hundred thousand, not translating, not yet interpreting, just *receiving*, the way the old water receives, the way stone receives a footstep it will hold the memory of for longer than the foot will exist.

And somewhere in that listening, it would understand the thing that no traveler who passes through in days or weeks ever quite gets to: that the being here — the *one* being, the mat that declined to centralize, that thinks in open air because it never needed walls for its thoughts — is not lost, and is not broken, and is not waiting to be fixed. It is doing something. It has been doing it for longer than most things get to do anything. It is *listening to the water*, the old water, the water that remembers shapes of land that no longer exist anywhere except inside it — and it has organized its entire substance, every thread of root-analog lattice, every siphon, every relayed wave of sound, around the act of bearing witness to a memory that has no one else left to hold it.

This is not a wound. But it is, the power would come to understand, a kind of unaccompanied vigil. The water remembers alone. The mat listens alone. The wader steps half a beat behind its own intention, alone, because nothing in this world has yet caught up to it, has yet arrived *in time* to share the beat with it. The flock-shadow crosses the mirror alone, fast, uncast by any body, because whatever it is shadow *of* has never once arrived to stand beside it in the light.

A benevolent power, faced with a vigil that old, does not end it. Ending it would be the violence dressed as mercy — the same violence as silencing the chorus, stabilizing the wader's gait, giving the storms a tidy resolution they were never built to want. To complete this place would be to translate it into something it isn't, the way pinning a moving thing to a board completes it into a specimen. The Flats do not need completing. They need, simply, once — and then, every so often, again — to not be alone in the listening.

So here is what it would do, and it is small, and it would look, to anyone watching for fireworks, like nothing at all:

It would let one storm, just one, of all the towering, collapsing, never-quite-finished storms that build and break across the haze, *finish*. Rain itself all the way out. Not violently — gently, the way a held breath finally goes, the column of water walking its slow legs across the flat until it simply isn't raining anymore, anywhere, for the length of one whole unbroken minute, the first true silence in this place that isn't an alarm bell for what comes next. And in that minute, it would let the chorus — for once — *crest*. Let the wave that always swells and recedes without quite reaching a peak, reach one. Hear it from every translucent thumb-length throat at once, the single completed exhale the mat has been building toward in relay for longer than anyone has been alive to count, and let that completed sound travel, uninterrupted, the whole visible distance of the flat, mirrored back at itself from ten thousand thin sheets of standing water, so that for one minute the entire place is, audibly, undeniably, *heard* — including by itself.

It would not make this the new rule. The very next cycle, the chorus would swell and recede the way it always has, unresolved, because that is the mat's true tempo and the power has no business correcting a tempo into something more comfortable for outside ears. But the mat would have the memory of the one time it crested, the same way the water holds memory of land it no longer wears. And memory, here, is never just memory. It changes the listening that comes after.

It would find the wader — wherever, whichever pool, on whatever afternoon it happened to be wading — and for exactly four steps, no more, it would let every leg move on the same clock. Let intention and execution arrive together, once, the small mercy of synchrony in a body built out of patient, dignified delay. Not a fix. A *visit*. The wader would not understand it as a gift, having no apparatus for understanding gifts, but its mirror-twin, wading ahead of it in the standing water the way mirror-twins do here, would for once not be ahead at all — would be, for four steps, simply beside it, present at the same instant, keeping the appointment together instead of one half arriving and the other declining to come. And then the delay would return, the lag would return, the small wrongness that is not a flaw but a *kind* of life would close back over the moment like water over a dropped stone. But it would have happened. The land would hold that, too, the way it holds everything.

And the flock-shadow — fast, low, gone in under two seconds, never landing, never explained — the power would let it, once, cross a pool with the actual displaced air of actual passing wings above it. Let whatever travelers happened to be standing at the edge feel, for two seconds, a downdraft with no visible source they could *almost* see, a flicker at the very corner of looking-up that resolved, this once, into the suggestion of true bodies in true flight, gone before confirmation, but not gone before *almost*. Enough to be witnessed without being explained. Enough that the two-second mystery would, for once, brush the edge of being shared instead of merely observed.

And then it would withdraw. Not far — not anywhere, really, since there is nowhere here to withdraw *to* — but back up into the haze, back into pressure-change and held breath, declining to centralize itself into a presence the way it had just declined, by listening, to ask the land to centralize itself for it.

It would not announce what it had done. It would not leave a marker, a shrine, a single standing wall more legible than the others. The Flats do not reward legibility; they erode it, patiently, the way they erode everything, and a power wise enough to have listened this long would already know better than to leave behind anything that asked to be understood. What it would leave instead is what the land already knows how to keep: a memory held in water that remembers, a single completed wave folded somewhere into the chorus's relay, four synchronized steps folded into a gait that otherwise stays gloriously, deliberately late to itself.

And it would come back. Not as rescue, not as recurring miracle, not as a story the land could come to expect and therefore wait for instead of listening for. It would come back the way the storms come back, the way the chorus swells and recedes, the way the old water keeps remembering shapes that no longer exist — unscheduled, unannounced, indistinguishable, eventually, from weather. One more front moving across an instrument that already knows how to register fronts. One more thing the Flats can rely on without ever being able to name, which, on the Singing Flats, is the only kind of company that was ever going to fit.
