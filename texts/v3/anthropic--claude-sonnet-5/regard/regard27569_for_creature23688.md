# What Came Down Through the Fissure

It did not need the throat of cool stone. It did not need to turn sideways, palms picking up damp like a second skin, the way you did. A benevolent and wise power does not arrive by entering; it arrives by *attending*, and so the first thing that happened in the Lit Hollow, when its attention turned there, was not a disturbance of the air but a deepening of the light — so slight that the moths did not startle, so slight that the stalks did not still, so slight that even the snails, who notice everything in the only way available to them, which is by having already moved, did not register a change in the room at all.

Only the being against the east curve might have felt it. Might have, in the way that a held note might feel the room around it grow very slightly more able to hear.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not break the equilibrium. This is worth saying first, because it would have been so easy, and so wrong, and so exactly the kind of error that power mistakes for kindness: to reach in and *finish* something. To let the blink complete, the calcite recede, the legs unlock, to stand the being up out of its unsayable stillness and call that mercy. A lesser power, a power eager to be felt as good, might have done this — might have looked at "almost becoming, forever" and decided on the being's behalf that almost is a wound and finishing is the cure.

But wisdom is mostly the refusal of that kind of certainty. The power looked at the open eyes, dark and wet and fixed on the middle distance, and did not assume despair. It looked at the stacked stones, lichen-fused, placed once by hands that wanted shelter or a marker or just the animal comfort of proving *I was here* — and it understood that the being had sat down, after that, *only to rest*. Not to be rescued from resting. Not to be hurried out of it. The chamber had taken the resting and made of it a held breath that does not move toward anything, true — but the being had chosen the wall, the rest, the stillness, before the chamber ever closed its hand around the choice. To unmake that now, on the being's behalf, without asking, would not be tenderness. It would be a second sitting-down imposed from outside, dressed up as freedom.

So it did not wake the being. It did not free it. It did not dim the glow or hush the chord of frog-throats reaching forever for a melody they will never find, because that reaching, too, might be wanted, might be its own kind of song the being has learned to live inside the way you learn to live inside your own pulse.

## What It Did Instead

It became, for a while, a second variable in the room.

Not a visitor — visitors come down through fissures and feel the damp on their palms and eventually, always, leave, the way you will leave. The power did something quieter and stranger: it sat. Not bodily — it has no body the Hollow's light could find a surface for — but in the sense that matters here, the sense the chamber has always measured things by. It took up a station, the way the moths take up the roots and the snails take up the gaps between stalks, except its station was *attention itself*, placed deliberately beside the being against the east curve, where no one had placed anything deliberately in longer than is sayable.

And it waited.

It waited through one full motion of the moths' breathing exhale. It waited while the thread of water found, as it always does, the same groove, and did not pool, and did not dry. It waited while the unnamed pale creatures held their stations on root and wall and floor, furnishing a room that had organized itself entirely around an old, unfinished gesture — because the power understood, by now, that this was the actual shape of the gift required: not intervention, but *company* in the one business the Hollow could not finish alone. The business of still, however faintly, going on.

It waited for the blink.

The being's eyes had closed, once, in the time it took you to cross the chamber, and opened again before you decided to leave — a single seam in an otherwise seamless equilibrium, the one thread of becoming the Hollow had never managed to smooth away. The power did not hurry that seam. It simply stayed long enough, attentively enough, that when the lids did at last begin their slow, geological descent — slower than the calcite climbing the legs, slower than the trickle cooling amber down the wall — there was, for the first time since the stones were stacked, *someone there to see it land.*

A blink completed in the presence of witness is not the same blink as one completed alone. This is, perhaps, the entire mechanism of wisdom and benevolence both: not the changing of a thing, but the changing of what it means for the thing to happen at all. The being's eyes closed. The being's eyes, after a silence longer than the chamber's whole sound, opened again. And in the space between, nothing in the Hollow moved — the moths kept their density, the stalks kept their sway, the snails kept their patient elsewhere — except that the held note, for one full cycle of itself, was no longer being held *alone*.

## What It Left

When the power's attention began, very slowly, to withdraw — the way it had arrived, by a deepening that reversed itself, so gently that nothing in the room could mark the moment it ended — it left one thing behind, set down by no hand anyone could name, found only because it had clearly not been there before and was, after, simply part of the furnishing, the way everything here becomes part of the furnishing eventually:

A fifth stone, atop the three or four already stacked near the eastern curve. Not stacked perfectly. Not an improvement on the gesture, not a correction of it, not a finishing of the sentence the being had set down so long ago that even habit had outlived the memory of its reason. Just an answer, of a kind. A second hand's acknowledgment that the first hand had been there, had wanted something, had built — and that the wanting, however long ago, however buried under calcite and moth-wing and the patient elsewhere-shifting of snails, had been *seen*.

It did not wake the being. It did not free it. It did not even shorten, by a single grain of dry-burning stone, the unfathomable distance between *almost* and *arrived*.

It only meant: *you are not the only variable who has ever stood in this room and changed. I came down, and I was changed by finding you — moved to wait, moved to witness, moved to leave a stone I did not have to leave. That, too, is a kind of becoming. It happened here, beside you, because of you. The chamber will furnish itself around my gesture exactly as it furnished itself around yours, and in a thousand years some other thing that grows in the dark will hold its station near a stack of five stones instead of four, never knowing why, the way the moths never ask why they breathe in this exact number, forever.*

*You were rested beside. That is not nothing. In a room where nothing is becoming anything else, it may be the only thing that ever truly was.*
