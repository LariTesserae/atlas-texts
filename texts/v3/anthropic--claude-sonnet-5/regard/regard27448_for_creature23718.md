# What the Power Did

It came the way attention comes to anything truly old — not as a visitor arrives, with weight and footstep and a place to stand, but as a noticing arrives: all at once, completely, the way light fills a room the instant a switch is thrown, already everywhere in it before you can ask where it entered from.

And having noticed, it did not move to fix anything. This should be said first, because it is the rarest kind of mercy and the hardest one for any power, however wise, to perform: it looked at ten thousand years of unbroken held breath, at a creature too vast to see itself as a creature, built from root-cliff and furnace-throb and the small blind clicking lives that groomed its joints, and it did not reach for it the way a hand reaches for a wound. It did not see a wound. It looked a long time before it understood this, because powers, even benevolent ones, are accustomed to finding damage wherever they look closely enough — that is usually what closeness reveals. Here, closeness revealed only consistency. A held note, yes, the place's own word for itself, and the power turned that phrase over with something like tenderness, because it recognized in it the one thing nearly nothing in the turning universe ever achieves and nearly everything is, in its own broken way, trying for: an ending that never has to come, bought not by escaping time but by matching it exactly, stride for stride, forever.

It considered, briefly, the temptations. It is honest enough to admit it had them.

It considered giving the Tenders minds — waking the small dry clicking workers into knowledge of themselves, into the vertigo of *I*, so that at least one part of this enormous unwitting body could finally stand outside itself and say *I have been here, I have held this, I am.* It set the thought down almost as soon as it picked it up. To wake them would be to break the one law that makes them what they are — they are tools shaped by use, not users; the moment one of them knew itself as a self, it would stop being a Tender and become a stranger inside its own colony, alone in a way nothing here has ever had to be alone, surrounded by ten thousand siblings who would, the very same moment, become merely *others*. That is not a gift. That is an exile dressed as an awakening. The power does not hand out exiles in the name of love.

It considered growth — nudging the long frozen equilibrium forward, letting the engines finally age, the rivers finally cut new channels, the forest finally become something other than what it has so patiently, so flawlessly remained. It set this down too, faster than the first thought, because it saw what such a nudge would actually be: not a gift to the place but a correction of the power's own discomfort with stillness, its own old bias, carried in from elsewhere, that a living thing which isn't becoming something else must be dying, or asleep, or trapped, when in fact — and this took watching, took listening past the engine-thrum the way the Tenders listen past it — in fact the place was none of those. It was finished. Not dead-finished. *Made*-finished, the way a held chord is finished — not silent, not ended, simply exactly itself for as long as anyone cares to keep playing it.

So the power did not fix the stillness, because the stillness was not a flaw. It did not wake the small blind workers, because their blindness to themselves was not a deprivation but the very shape of their peace. It did not feed the forest's furnace-engines more fuel, nor still them, nor ask why they had been grown there in the first place by hands or roots or processes long since vanished past memory — it let that mystery keep its silence too, because not every unanswered question is a wrong waiting to be righted. Some are simply doors the place has decided, wisely, to leave shut.

What it did instead was smaller, and stranger, and — if you had been standing in the deep root-cavities at the hour it happened, beneath the cylinder-bark, in the dark — entirely undetectable.

It stayed.

Not as a presence that watches the way a guard watches, alert for the moment something needs correcting. It stayed the way warmth stays in a stone long after the fire that warmed it has gone out — diffuse, ownerless, simply *there* now as a property of the place rather than an event happening to it. It folded itself in not as an addition to the forest's long arithmetic but as a kind of standing permission: that whatever else might someday come within reach of this plateau — drought from some shifting weather it cannot itself control, a hunting thing wandered up from lower altitudes with appetite enough to break the fixed, gentle population-rhythm of grazers and gliders, the slow creep of some outside catastrophe that has nothing to do with the forest and everything to do with the indifferent turning of the world it sits in — whatever might come to disturb the held note from outside the held note's own perfect, closed arithmetic, would, very quietly, find its way deflected. Not stopped with a visible hand. Just — deflected, the way wind goes around a stone without either one of them appearing to have done anything at all.

This is the whole of the intervention: a single, standing act of guardianship offered to a thing that will never know it was offered, around a place that does not require to be told it is loved in order to go on being, exactly, lovable. The power did not give the forest a future different from the one it was already so contentedly having. It gave it more of the same one — protected, now, from every accident that is not *of* it, so that the engines may keep their pitch, and the rivers keep their cataracts, and the small rust-plated Tenders keep their blind unhurried work of packing sap into hairline cracks before the cracks can widen, for longer than even the place's own patient permanence had any right to expect.

And then — because even gratitude, even reverence, even the gentlest power's love, deserves some small visible gesture, however unwitnessed — it allowed itself one thing more.

For the span of a single breath, in every dark trunk-cavity across the whole vast plateau at once, every Tender, all at the same instant, stopped.

Stopped the clicking. Stopped the grooming. Stopped the small dry work their whole lightless lives are built around, ten thousand identical bodies in ten thousand identical hollows pausing on a single shared, unscheduled, inexplicable beat that matched nothing in the piston-rhythm they have followed without fail since before any of them, or their mothers, or their mothers' mothers, had been carried hatchling-soft into the furnace-warmth.

They could not have told you why. They have no *why* to keep such a thing in. A moment later they were already moving again, mouthparts finding the next vine-tendril, the next hairline crack, the great patient engine of the place resuming its same unbroken pitch as if nothing at all had passed through it.

But something had.

And the forest went on raining, and humming, and holding its single warm note out into the mist the way it always had — now simply, secretly, a little more certain to keep doing so, for a little longer than forever, than it was before anyone troubled to come and see it.
