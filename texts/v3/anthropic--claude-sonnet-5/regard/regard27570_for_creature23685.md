The attention arrives the way weather arrives here — not as a presence announced, but as a change in the quality of the air, a thickening felt before it is seen. It does not come *down* to the terrace. It comes *level* with it, the way one creature regards another across a clearing, neither predator nor prey, simply aware.

And for a long while — long by any reckoning but the terrace's own, where a generation is a held breath — it does nothing at all. It watches.

It watches the children with their unstippled forearms running fern-paths with no down yet to promise them anything, watches the old ones motionless on stilted porches translating sky into stillness, watches the thing at the outer wall pacing the dark with wings too large for the certainty it hasn't yet earned. It watches the birds carry their wordless mail from roost to shoulder to roost again. It watches the silence — really watches it, the way you'd watch a held note in music, waiting to hear whether it resolves.

The power understands quickly what it is looking at, because it has looked at many things in many ages of many worlds, and it knows the shape of becoming when it sees it. It also knows — this is the wisdom part, the part that takes longer than the seeing — exactly which of its hands would help and which would ruin.

It could finish them. It has that in it: the capacity to reach into the slow stalled work of those bodies and simply complete it, collarbone to sternum to spine, down to feather to flight, in a single uncounted moment. It could make the one pacing the wall lift off tonight, fully formed, fully sure, and watch the terrace erupt — finally, deservedly — into the noise it has been postponing for generations.

It does not do this.

Because it understands, watching the held quiet of the place, that the waiting is not an obstacle standing between the Hollowed and their wings. The waiting *is* the wings, still being built, one withheld voice and one bitten-back cry at a time. Forty people and their birds and their wordless children have spent generations learning a discipline that no shortcut could replicate, and to hand them the result without the practice would be to give them a flight they hadn't grown the muscle to trust. A gift like that doesn't free a fledgling. It strands one — outside, in the air, with no idea yet how the air is held.

So the power does what wisdom usually does, which is smaller than it looks and harder than it should be.

It does not touch the down, or the bone, or the bare wall-pacing thing testing its span against the night.

It touches the *weather*.

A storm that would have struck the terrace on the thirty-first night from now, the night that matters — the night the wall-pacer was always going to finally step off, whether ready or not, because some courages only arrive once and don't wait for good conditions — that storm is, with no fingerprint left on it, nudged a day east. It will still come. It will still drench the sharp new stone and send the ferns trembling. It will simply arrive a day late, after, instead of during.

It does not remove the predator that has begun, for the first time in the settlement's short memory, to test the lower shelves at night — something drawn by the smell of so much undefended life. The Hollowed do not fence, do not trap; that absence of defense is its own kind of faith, and the power respects it too much to quietly answer it from outside. But it does, one evening, make a particular game-trail on the slope below smell more interesting than the terrace does, for exactly as many nights as the predator needs to lose interest and wander elsewhere on its own legs, by its own choice, having never once been touched.

And on the night itself — the night the wall-pacer finally unfolds fully into the dark, gathers whatever last unnameable thing a body needs before it trusts a drop it cannot take back — the power does not move the air to help the wings find purchase. It does not soften gravity for the first stomach-dropping yard of the fall before flight becomes flight in truth. That terror, that specific terror, the one every flying thing in every world has had to feel once and only once, belongs to the wall-pacer entirely. To take it would be to take the only thing about this moment that will make it *theirs* afterward, the story the children will someday have stippled forearms and hear with their own wordless birds.

What the power *does* do, in that last unweighed second, is make sure it is paying full attention. Not for any effect this will have on the world — wisdom long ago let go of the idea that being witnessed changes outcomes — but because it has decided, watching this small held-breath place for what amounts to its whole patient youth, that the truest thing it has to give a people who have built their entire art around *not announcing themselves* is to be, itself, one more quiet watcher added to the rows on the bare poles. Not a god intervening. Not a miracle granted. Just another presence in the dusk-thick grey, holding still, holding breath, counting itself for one night among the watching hundreds the terrace has always felt circling above the haze — because that, it turns out, is what the place was built for and has been waiting on all along: not rescue, not completion handed down from outside, but to be *seen* finally finishing what it started, by something that understood it was worth seeing.

The wall-pacer drops, catches, staggers once in the air like a thought losing and then re-finding its thread, and rises.

No bird scatters. No voice breaks the quiet to mark it. The whole terrace simply tilts its many faces upward together, in the silence it spent generations learning to keep, and watches its own becoming arrive on schedule, unassisted, entirely its own — 

— and the power, satisfied that the most generous thing it did tonight was nothing, withdraws the way it came: not as a leaving, but as a lightening, like haze lifting by one degree from a sky that was always going to clear on its own.
