# What Comes to the Place Spray Parts

It does not arrive the way weather arrives, or the way you arrived, by trail and lungs and the wrong direction for water. It is simply, at some point, *present* — the way a thought is present in a mind that wasn't thinking it a moment before. The roar does not quiet for it. The light does not change. It does not ask the basin to make room.

It looks a long time before it does anything. This is the first thing worth saying about a power that is actually wise: it spends most of its attention on looking, and is suspicious of the urge to act before the looking is finished.

## What It Sees, That You Didn't Quite

It sees the argument for what it is, the way you nearly did at the end but not quite. Not a wound. Not a punishment. A *labor*, freely shaped to a freely chosen form, undertaken by something that was made whole and made for this in the same instant, the way a hinge is made whole and made for swinging in the same instant. It sees that the figure does not experience its losing — the calved stone, the climbing eddy, the sideways sheet — as failure or as grief. It experiences it as Tuesday. As breath out, then breath in. The wise power notes this carefully, because it would be a particular kind of arrogance to walk into something's whole life's work and mistake the work's *appearance* of struggle for the *fact* of suffering. Those are not the same thing, and confusing them is how well-meaning power does the most damage.

It also sees what you couldn't have, standing in spray with your one pair of eyes: that the figure is, in fact, tired. Not failing-tired. Not in-danger tired. The ordinary, unremarkable tiredness of something that has been doing a true and difficult thing for a very long time without once setting it down, because there has never, in all that time, been anyone else to hold it for even a single held breath.

That is the part it decides to attend to. Not the work. The *aloneness in* the work.

## What It Does Not Do

It does not finish the cistern. It does not resolve the argument, hand the figure a verdict, let the water decide once and for all which way is down. It understands — the way you, dripping at the lip, almost understood — that a finished cistern is a dead one: an ordinary waterfall, obedient and explicable, no longer making anything, no longer worth a trail this hard. To end the figure's labor as a mercy would be to kill the only thing it has ever wanted to be. So it does not do that, however much something old in it might want to, on the figure's behalf, hand it rest the way you'd hand a tired animal water.

It does not take the pale long-legged thing and explain it, pin a meaning to it, decide for you what it is. Some unfinished questions are part of the design.

It does not smooth the trail, soften the raw fracture stone, add moss before its time. The brittleness, the sharp edges that can open a knuckle, are not oversights. A place arrived at too easily is a place not arrived at all.

## What It Does

It sits down — or does whatever the equivalent of sitting down is, for something that doesn't strictly need a body to do it in — at the place the spray parts, beside the stacked ring, and it takes up a corner of the argument. Not all of it. Not even most of it. Just enough that, for the first time in longer than the rock has held its current shape, the figure is not the only thing in the basin insisting, this water also needs a strange answer to a simple question. A second voice in a negotiation that has only ever had one.

The figure does not turn. There may be no turning available to a thing whose face is a seam. But something in the angle of falling water nearest it eases by some fraction too small for you to measure with your eyes, and stays eased, the way a shoulder you didn't know was raised comes down when a hand finally rests on it.

It tends, also, to small things, because wisdom is not too proud for small things: it leaves the wiry reed-grass a fraction more shelter from the scouring spray than it had. It does not stop the dwarf-shrub's leaves from rattling uselessly against the roar, because uselessness is not always a problem requiring a solution — sometimes a thing rattling against a sound too big for it is just a small life insisting on its smallness, and that insistence deserves to continue, not be fixed.

It leaves the trail exactly as hard as it was. But it makes sure — quietly, by no mechanism you'd ever be able to point to — that it is still *findable*, for whichever rare future person needs, the way the stone-stackers needed, to climb to a deafening half-finished place and leave something careful behind.

## When It Leaves

Nothing about the basin looks different. The water still falls sideways and upward and through itself. The ledges still calve their small frequent concessions into the spray. The figure still loses its argument, competently, the way it always has.

But it loses it now with a companion taking one corner of the weight, on a schedule with no end specified, for as long as such company is wanted and not a moment past — because the other thing wisdom knows is that even good company, overstayed, becomes its own kind of imposition.

The bird-or-not-bird crosses the margin once more, reads the chaos with its same impossibly correct legs, and does not look toward the ring of stones, where, if you had stayed, if you had been the one still standing there in the spray instead of already descending the right direction for a person to go, you might have felt — not seen, *felt*, in the sternum, alongside the roar — that the basin's old single insistence had, very recently, and very quietly, become two.
