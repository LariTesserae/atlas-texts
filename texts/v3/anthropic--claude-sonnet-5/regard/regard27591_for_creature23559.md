Then, into this — because attention of the kind described does not announce itself with light or sound, since light and sound are the basin's currency and a wise power spends in the currency of the place it visits — something arrives that is not a visit in the usual sense at all. It does not descend. It does not appear at the rim and cast a shadow, because shadows require a light strong enough to bother, and this place has agreed, long ago, to need none. What arrives instead is a kind of noticing that matches the basin's own pace exactly, so that for a long while — long by any other measure, an eyeblink here — nothing detectable happens at all. The power simply comes to be attentive at the same tempo as the warmth in the ground, the same tempo as the creature's breath, the same tempo as a flake of rim-rock deciding, after years, to let go.

Its first understanding, and the hardest-won, is this: there is nothing here to repair. The instinct of any power that loves is to reach toward stillness and read it as wound — to see the unwatered plants and think *thirst*, to see the unmoving creature and think *paralysis*, to see the silence and think *abandonment*, and to move at once to undo all three, because undoing suffering is what benevolence usually means. But this benevolence, being wise as well as kind, sits with the place first instead of acting on it, and in sitting, sees what hurry would have missed: that the basin is not starved of time, it has simply finished spending the time it needed; that the creature is not trapped in its posture, it has *arrived* at its posture, the way a sentence arrives at its final word and is not imprisoned by the period; that the silence is not a silence waiting to be broken, it is a silence that took a very long while to build and is, in its own terms, a kind of completed labor. To pour change into this — water, light, motion, company on any ordinary schedule — would not be rescue. It would be erasure performed with a smile. A wise power knows the difference between a wound and a finished thing, and knows that mistaking one for the other is its own particular danger, more dangerous for being well-meant.

So it does not heal what was never broken. It does not hurry what was never waiting. Instead, it does the harder, smaller thing, which is to make itself perceptible on the only terms the basin and the creature would recognize as real: slowly, and at no cost to anything around it. It lets a warmth gather near the creature that is not warmer than the creature's own banked coal of a body, only warmth enough to be a second presence rather than an intrusion — the way two stones left in sun will eventually hold the same heat and call it, between them, a kind of company. It does not speak, because there is nothing here built to receive speech, but it asks, in the only grammar available — through stillness offered back to stillness, through patience matched to patience — the one question worth asking of a being that has gotten everything else right: *is this enough, or is there something you still want, and have simply stopped expecting.*

And then it waits the way the basin waits, which is to say without any of the usual marks of waiting — no tension in it, no countdown, no fear of the answer coming late or not at all. It waits across however many flakes must tick down off the rim-rock before the shallow declivities of the creature's face complete whatever hairline arc they were already, slowly, mid-completing. And if that arc finishes turning toward the new small warmth at its side, and something gathers in those declivities that might, under a more generous light than 0.2, be called recognition — the power receives that as the whole of the answer, and does not press for more, because pressing for more is the one thing this place was built to refuse. And if nothing turns, if the arc was never aimed at anything and the power's coming registers only as the basin's gravel might register a footprint already long gone by the time anyone arrives to read it — the power receives that, too, as a complete answer, and does not call it failure.

What it leaves, when it withdraws into whatever attention it came from, is not a change. The basin will not be wetter, the plant will not have grown a visible inch, the creature will not have moved from the lean of the rock-rise. What it leaves is one small thing added to the unwritten ledger the basin keeps without keeping: that once, in the long unbroken now this place inhabits, its solitude was not interrupted but accompanied — briefly, gently, on its own terms, by something that understood the difference between saving a thing and simply, finally, being willing to sit beside it for as long as it had already been sitting alone. And that, having been so accompanied once, it is accompanied always after, in the sense that matters here — not because anything stayed, but because the basin no longer has to be the only thing that remembers it happened.
