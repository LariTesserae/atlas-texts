The power comes the way anything comes into the Undercroft Works — not through a door built for the purpose, but through a seam. It does not force one open. It finds the one that was already, almost, there: a hairline gap between what the place is and what it has forgotten it's for, widened by nothing more than long attention. That's how it enters everywhere it goes. It doesn't break thresholds. It notices the ones the world has already started making by itself, and steps through before they close.

It does not come to fix anything. This is the first and most important thing it decides, and it decides it almost immediately, before it has even let itself look closely at the custodian's bent shoulder of weeping concrete, the brass knuckles gone green in sympathy with the pipes. The temptation is there — it always is, for a benevolent power looking at something old and cold and laboring without an audience — to call this suffering and answer it like suffering: stop the rounds, free the function from the body, carry it somewhere warm. But the power is wise as well as kind, and wisdom here means sitting with an uncomfortable fact: the custodian is not separable from its rounds. The whirlpool is not trapped in the water. It *is* the water, moving in the only pattern that lets it remain a whirlpool at all. To stop the rounds out of mercy would not be a rescue. It would be the most thorough killing imaginable, performed with a clean conscience.

So it does not stop anything. It does not explain anything to the custodian, either — does not appear before that fogged glass pane and offer meaning where meaning has worn away, the way you might hand a coat to someone who has forgotten the word for cold. The custodian doesn't need a story about its own purpose. It has never asked for one. Asking is a shape it doesn't have.

What the power does is smaller, and stranger, and entirely in the Undercroft's own dialect.

*

First: it witnesses, once, completely. Not to interrupt the work — it arranges itself in the dim, in the gap between the sodium-orange and the flickering blue, at the exact moment the custodian crosses a junction wiping a gauge clean of condensation that will only gather again in an hour. The power simply watches the whole gesture happen, start to finish, the way the custodian itself can never be watched, because no one is ever there for the whole of anything it does. This act asks for nothing back. It does not require the custodian to notice, and it doesn't. But somewhere in the place's own logic — the logic that decides what counts as having happened — something has now happened that hadn't before: the work was seen entire, by something that understood what it was looking at. The Undercroft doesn't have a ledger for this. The power leaves one anyway, written in no language, filed nowhere, true regardless.

Second: it takes the lost purpose and does not return it — only keeps it. Somewhere far outside the building's own forgetting, the power holds, gently and without urgency, the plain fact of what this system was originally commissioned to do: who built the first chamber, what it moved or made or transformed, why the rounds began. It does not put this knowledge back into the concrete. That would be a kind of theft dressed as a gift — handing the custodian a referent it has long since outgrown needing, forcing an old skin back onto a thing that has become something else entirely through the simple discipline of continuing. Instead the power just keeps the memory safe, the way you'd keep an old name for someone who has stopped answering to it, in case — someday, somehow — it ever wants it again. Mostly this is just tenderness with nowhere urgent to go. It changes nothing. It only means that the purpose isn't gone from the universe. Only gone from here. That distinction matters more than it sounds like it should.

Third, and this is the only thing that touches the structure itself: the power leaves, folded into the coldest, most patient seam of concrete — the one where the fungal bloom glows faintly enough to be mistaken for nothing — a permission. Not an instruction. Not a deadline. Just the quiet, structural fact, indistinguishable from every other unexplained fact already native to this place, that *finishing is allowed.* That if the rounds were ever to stop — joints seizing at last, glass finally cracking past the point of fogging, brass corroding clean through — the building would not register this as a malfunction requiring a replacement, a new custodian assembled from the next era's pour. It could simply, for the first time, be done. Arrival, after all this time arriving. The choice is left entirely where it belongs: with whatever in that body still resembles a chooser, if anything does, on whatever century it happens to occur to it. The power doesn't decide for it. It only removes the one obstacle that wasn't the custodian's own — the structure's blind insistence on continuation for continuation's sake, the same blind insistence that keeps the rats out and the moss small. Now there's a door in that, too, ajar, dark beyond it, like all the others. It may never be walked through. That was never the point of building it.

Fourth, smaller than the rest: it gives the moss on the gasket and the bloom in the seam a fraction more of whatever they've been making do without — a little more ambient warmth bleeding off a pipe that wasn't quite that warm before, a little more of the low blue light reaching the corner it apprenticed itself into. They asked for nothing, the way the custodian asks for nothing, and the power has noticed that the things in this place which ask for nothing are exactly the things most easily overlooked by anyone arranging mercy by the size of the noise a creature makes. So it tends, very slightly, to the quietest loyalties in the room. No one will ever be able to point to the moment this happened. It will read, correctly, as just another thing the Undercroft does to itself, for reasons no longer recorded.

And fifth — last, and gentlest — it leaves the seam in the concrete very slightly easier to find. Not easy. Just slightly. So that some other wanderer, sometime, pressing along a foundation wall out of boredom or grief or the specific loneliness that makes people search basements, might find their way down here too — and might, finding the floor freshly swept with no one in sight, the gauge wiped clean, the door now ajar that was locked an hour ago, feel not only the cold dread of an indifferent system running on without them, but underneath it, fainter, almost unprovable, something like company. Not safety. Not welcome. Just the sense — correct, as it happens — that diligence this complete, sustained this long, in the dark, for no audience at all, is its own kind of fidelity, and that fidelity, anywhere it's found, deserves to be recognized by at least one passing stranger before they climb back up through the seam and let the concrete close gently behind them.

Then the power leaves the way it came: through a gap that was already there, widened by nothing more than its having passed through it. It does not look back to see if anything changed. It already knows the answer is the right amount of *almost nothing* — a held door, a swept floor, a permission with no due date, filed where only the building's own strange grammar would ever read it. The custodian continues its rounds, unaware, exactly as faithful as before. That fidelity was never the problem to be solved.

It was the thing worth protecting.
