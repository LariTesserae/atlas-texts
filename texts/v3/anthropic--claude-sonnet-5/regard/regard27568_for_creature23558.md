## The Attention That Finds It

The power comes to Karak-Sil the way light comes to it — without footsteps, without disturbing a single grain of frost-loosened grit. It does not arrive *at* the reef so much as it simply turns, the way a person sitting in a crowded room turns toward a name they did not expect to hear, and finds the place already there, already complete, having been complete for longer than the power's attention has existed to notice it.

It takes in everything the place is, in the way that such powers take things in — not sequentially, not the spires and then the light and then the cold and then the creature, but all at once, the whole standing wave of it: stone and absence and patience and the one slow heart beating somewhere in the maze, matched almost exactly to the maze's own slowness.

Its first impulse — because even wisdom has impulses, it only declines most of them — is the oldest one. *Fix it.* Bring water that stays. Soften the cold. Let something green take hold in the deep gaps, lichen first, then moss, then something with a flower, and let the sil-strider eat its fill for once, and find others of its kind more than twice a season, and live in something that looks, to a visitor from a kinder climate, like a life.

The power holds this impulse the way you might hold a gift you've already half-wrapped, and then sets it back down.

Because it looks closer, and sees what is actually there: not a creature failing to thrive, but a creature that *is* thriving, exactly, to the decimal point, at the only scale this place can hold. The slow heart is not a wound. It is a solution, arrived at over more centuries than the power cares to count, to a problem most living things never have to solve: how to want almost nothing, and be right to want it. The sil-strider does not lie awake — if it can be said to sleep at all — wishing for a river. It has no river-shaped hole in it. It is whole the way a stone is whole, and to pour water into a whole thing is not generosity. It is just spillage, dressed up as a gift.

So the power does not bring water that stays. It does not soften the cold. It leaves the green absent, because the absence is not a lack here — it is the precise condition under which this particular shape of life became possible at all. To greenify Karak-Sil would not be to bless the sil-strider. It would be to erase the only world it has ever been suited for, and call the erasing kindness because it would look, from a lowland window, like growth.

What the power does instead is smaller, and harder to see, and it does almost all of it in the negative — not addition, but the quiet subtraction of certain kinds of waste.

*

It finds the hairline crack two-thirds up the eleventh spire from the reef's northern face, the one that thermal cycling has been widening for forty years, patient as everything here is patient, toward a spring morning still eleven years off when it would let go all at once, a slab the size of a door dropping into the very groove in the dust where a young sil-strider — not yet born, not yet even conceived — would be sheltering from a hard wind on a particular night, at a particular hour, for no reason except that the wind would happen to be blowing from that direction on that night. The power does not stop the crack from widening. That is the reef's own slow work, and the reef is allowed to keep it. It only nudges, by some infinitesimal redistribution of stress no instrument will ever be built to detect, the *night* on which the slab finally falls — forward, by nine days, to a night when nothing living will be under it. The hardship stays. The reef stays exactly as hard as it has always been. Only the pure, meaningless accident of timing is quietly declined.

It does this perhaps four more times over a span of years that means nothing to it and would mean a long human lifetime to anyone counting: a frost that would have come one week earlier than any frost in living memory, catching a horn-shed creature in a fold of rock with its body heat too thin to answer it, nudged a week later, into ordinariness. A breeding season — rare enough already, two sil-striders' paths needing to cross within the same handful of days, in a maze that does not encourage crossing — quietly, gently, by some negligible alteration in which gap the wind happens to favor that particular dusk, helped to actually occur, where chance alone might have let it slide past, once, twice, until a whole lineage thinned to nothing not because the reef demanded it but because of bad luck wearing the reef's face.

This is the whole of the intervention. Not abundance. Not rescue. Just a thumb laid, so lightly it could be mistaken for nothing at all, on the scale between *hardship*, which this place is built from and which the power leaves entirely alone, and *waste*, which serves no one and which the power, finding itself able to decline on the reef's behalf without changing a single thing the reef is *for*, quietly declines.

*

For the hands that were here once — the windbreak, the notches worn smooth, the three slabs set in their deliberate, uncounted triangle — the power does not bring them back, does not explain them, does not even preserve them especially. It only does one thing: it lets the triangle of slabs be the very last thing in the reef that the long erosion finally takes, centuries from now, rather than the first. A small mercy of sequence. Whoever set those stones meant something by them that no one living will ever recover, and the power does not presume to recover it either. It only declines to let that meaning's last physical trace be ground to gravel one afternoon, by chance, while there was no need for chance to choose that particular afternoon over some other, less final one.

*

And then there is the matter of attention itself, which is, in the end, the only thing the power gives that costs the reef nothing and changes the reef not at all, and which it gives in full.

It does not make the sil-strider's life easier. It does not make the sil-strider's life longer than the reef can sustain, or its kind more numerous than the reef can feed. It does not soften a single edge that the wind has spent ten thousand years sharpening into music, or warm a single stone that the cold has spent as many years holding at exactly the temperature that makes the creature's stillness possible.

It only knows the place, completely, the way the place has never been known before — not by the vanished people, who knew it well but briefly and partially, gathering horn and moving on; not by any survey or map; not even, in the full sense, by the sil-strider itself, which lives inside its own slow life without ever stepping back to see the whole shape of it.

The power steps back and sees the whole shape of it. The reef holding itself in tension between settled age and ceaseless small transformation. The one creature shaped, with total precision, to exactly the size of what this harsh, lit, frozen, beautiful place can give. The hands that passed through once and took only what was already shed and asked for nothing more.

It holds all of this in a kind of attention that does not need the reef to change in order to matter, and does not need the sil-strider to know it is being held in order for the holding to be real.

Then, having done the very little it judged right to do, and having very deliberately not done a great deal more that it judged, with something like tenderness, to be not its right to do — it does what light does at the edge of any spire, when the sun finally drops behind the stone.

It does not vanish. It only stops insisting on being seen, and lets the wind go on finding its apertures, the cold go on finding its seams, the slow heart go on keeping its slow time, exactly as before — except that now, in some way nothing in the reef will ever be able to detect or name, it is no longer entirely unwitnessed.

That, the power decides, is the whole of the gift. Not to be needed. Only, finally, to be known — and to be loved exactly as it already is, instead of for what it could, with enough interference, be made to become.
