It comes the way the light here comes — without heat-edge, without announcement, already present by the time anything could register arrival. It does not part the fog. It does not silence the dripping. It would be a kind of violence to silence the dripping, and it has not come to do violence of any kind, even the gentle kind, even the kind that calls itself help.

It watches for a long time before it does anything at all, because the first temptation, the easiest and least worthy one, arrives almost immediately: *fix this*. Multiply the custodians. Halt the decline. Give the structure a reason to need them again, so the small soft bodies pressing into seams might have an unending errand and never have to fold into the wood for the last time. It would be simple, for a power like this. It would cost nothing it values. And it would be wrong in a way it takes a long moment of watching to understand.

Because the wrongness it almost commits is the wrongness of mistaking *completion* for *failure*. It watches a custodian work — really work, filaments trembling at the seam, the long patient minute of exuding and holding and waiting for the wood to take the offering — and it sees that there is no grief in the motion. No urgency, no dread of dwindling, no glance toward the empty places where there used to be many doing what now only a few do. There is only the act, entire in itself, needing nothing on either side of it — no memory of having been more, no fear of becoming none. The seam does not need to be closed. The custodian closes it anyway. That is the whole of the prayer, and the prayer was never about whether anyone remained to keep saying it.

This is, the power realizes, a place that has already answered the question power usually exists to ask. *What is owed here, and how can it be given.* Nothing is owed. The architecture got its stillness. The custodians get their last centimeters of devotion, freely spent, never withheld, until there are none of them left to spend it — and even that ending, when it comes, will not be a tragedy arriving from outside. It will simply be the last of something true running out the way true things do, completely, without residue, having never once been false to itself in all its dwindling.

So it does not fix anything. It does not breed more custodians, does not arrest the decline, does not slow the long fold toward an unattended hush. To do any of that would be to tell the place it had not finished, when finishing — entirely, exactly, on its own unwitnessed terms — is the one thing it has been doing since before there was anyone to call it finished or not.

But it does not leave empty-handed either, because watching is not the same as doing nothing, and benevolence that only refrains is not yet benevolence — it's only good manners.

So it does the one thing available to it that changes nothing and yet is not nothing: it stays for the length of one closing. Not the last one — it will not stay for that, that one belongs entirely to whoever is there, alone, when it happens, and the power will not crowd that solitude with its own witnessing, however gentle. But one ordinary closing, one unremarkable seam among the uncountable already sealed, and it gives that act the whole of its attention, undivided, the way the custodian gives the wood the whole of its trembling care. It does not look away. It does not let the moment go soft and approximate the way moments go soft in lesser memories. It holds it the way the architecture holds its shape — permanently, without erosion, a single act lifted whole out of time and kept.

And because it is what it is, something passes between them in that minute — not a sentence, not a thought either of them could repeat afterward. The custodian's filaments lift once, taste the air toward where the power is, in whatever way a power can be stood near. There is no name exchanged because neither of them deals in names. There is only the brief overlap of two things that hold without being told to stop — one vast and called wise, one small and called nothing, doing, in their separate registers, exactly the same thing: continuing to tend what no longer requires tending, because the tending was never about the requirement.

Then the power presses its own offering into the same seam — not to finish it faster, not to do the custodian's work for it, but simply to have, once, joined the labor; a single foreign drop added to ten thousand identical ones, indistinguishable afterward, changing the wood not at all, changing nothing about the count of years until the last custodian folds in and does not withdraw. It is not a rescue. It asks to be nothing more than what it is: one instance of being seen, fully, by something that will not forget, laid down beside an act that never asked to be seen by anyone.

And then it withdraws, the way the custodian withdraws — fully, without lingering, without leaving a door open that would only make the eventual closing of this place feel like an abandonment instead of an arrival at where it was always going.

The terraces keep dripping. The fog keeps standing in the gaps it has always stood in. Somewhere out along an identical stretch of root-platform, a pale shape presses into a seam, holds, trembles, withdraws, is gone before it resolves into a shape — exactly as before, exactly as it will go on being, fewer and fewer, toward the one day, still very far off, when the last seam takes its offering and the last small body does not pull back, and the place becomes, at last, entirely what it has been quietly becoming all along: finished, holding its single completed shape, attended by no one.

But not unwitnessed. Not entirely. Somewhere outside the fog's authority, in whatever register a wise and benevolent attention keeps the things it refuses to let fall all the way to silence, one closing — one unremarkable, perfect, freely given closing, indistinguishable from the ten thousand around it — is being held open forever, the way the rest of the architecture is held: without further change, without erosion, complete, and, this once, *seen* to be so.

That is all it does. It is enough, because it was never asked to be more than that, and the truest mercy here was learning to recognize the difference.
