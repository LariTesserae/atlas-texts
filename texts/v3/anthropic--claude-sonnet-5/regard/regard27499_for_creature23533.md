# What the Power Does

It comes the way attention comes to a held breath — not arriving so much as becoming present, the way you notice, mid-thought, that you have in fact been hearing a sound for some time. There is no descent, no shimmer at the three or four uncertain doorways, no disturbance in the haze. The plateau does not look up. The runners keep running, the wet-cloth flocks keep snapping overhead, the structures keep humming their bass note under everything. Nothing announces it because it has not come to be an event. It has come to look.

And it looks for a long time, in the unit of time this place actually keeps, which is not hours but settlings — the slow crack of stone cooling somewhere off past the haze, three of those, four, while it simply takes the whole shelf in: the raw seams of the rock, the moss-cushions breathing on their reluctant clock, the jointed stalks standing in their lonely consecrated circles, the floor of hexagonal tile ending mid-thought in unworked stone as if a sentence had been interrupted and never finished, only continued elsewhere, in another grammar. It watches the black frame resolve and fail to resolve depending on where the light, such as it is, decides to come from. It does not flinch at the angles. It has looked at stranger geometries holding stranger weights.

It finds the mason at the misted edge, doing what the mason does — leaning the flat warm plate of its skull against the squat embedded hum-thing, not eating, not scenting, holding still in the particular stillness of something that trusts a thing it does not understand. The power watches the structure warm where it's touched. It watches, after a long while, the faintest fractional give in the stone beneath the mason's forward pad, a tile-edge nudged by a degree, an addition made by a creature with no blueprint and no foreman and no idea that what it is doing has a name anywhere, in any language, on any older world. It watches this the way you would watch someone praying who does not know it is prayer.

And here is the place where, if this were a lesser power, or merely a strong one, something would happen. The reflex of strength wearing the costume of kindness is always the same: *finish it*. Lay the erosion down a million years at once. Settle the angles of the black frames into angles that don't hurt to look at. Explain the structures to the mason, give it the satisfaction of knowing what it leans on and why its leaning matters, hand it a story it could carry the way the runners carry their twitchy confident hunger. Speed the light into committing to a sun. Quiet the chorus into something that resolves. Tidy the unfinished sentence of the tile floor into a finished room.

It considers all of this, fully, the way wisdom considers things — not as temptation resisted but as option genuinely weighed, turned over, tested against what is actually here rather than against what discomfort wants to be soothed in the one doing the weighing.

And it sets all of it down, gently, the way you'd set down a tool you picked up out of habit before remembering the job doesn't need it.

Because the place is not broken. The rock's rawness is not a wound; it's a youth, and youth is not improved by being skipped. The mason's not-knowing is not a deprivation; it is the precise condition under which the leaning means what it means — a creature giving its warmth to a thing it cannot account for, on faith alone, on proximity alone, and being made larger by the giving rather than smaller by the ignorance. To explain the structures to it would not be a gift. It would be a theft dressed as a kindness, the same theft committed every time someone hands a devotion its object and calls that mercy. The mason does not need to know what it tends in order to tend it well. Possibly it tends it better for not knowing — the way the pads return again and again not for an answer but for the leaning itself, which is the whole of the relationship, complete in itself, asking nothing further.

And the loud world underneath does not need fixing either. The runners and the wet-cloth flocks and the blind grazers are not a mistake of biology arriving early — they are simply what arrived, complete, confident, busy, indifferent to the slow vast unfinished thing they live their entire loud lives on top of. They do not require the mason's notice. They do not require the power's. They are already, by every measure that matters to a runner or a flock, *finished* — not because the world is, but because they are not waiting on it. The power notes this with something close to respect and moves on without touching a single one of them.

So it does not finish the place. It does not speed the erosion or settle the angles or give the mason its missing knowledge or hand the chorus a resolution it has never once asked for. What it does instead is smaller than all of that, and harder to see, and exactly the size of what is actually needed, which is the entire discipline of wisdom in one sentence.

First: it widens the haze. Not much — a degree of uncertainty added to a place that already runs on uncertainty, a further reluctance in the air to commit to where the plateau's edges actually are, so that whatever comes looking for unclaimed ground, whatever moves through worlds totaling up what's raw and sortable and useful, passes a little wider around this shelf than it would have, and does not find it, not yet, not for a long while yet. This is not concealment exactly. It is patience, externalized — the power simply extending to the place, from outside, the same unhurriedness the place is already extending to itself. A held door, not a locked one. The plateau gets to keep becoming on its own schedule. That is the whole of the gift, and it is not nothing; postponed discovery, for a thing still being poured, is most of what mercy looks like from outside.

Second: it stays, in the attending sense, a little longer than looking requires — through one full boom of the mason's call, the one that stops the small chorus cold for those few held seconds before the runners and the flocks dare to rebuild the noise from the edges inward. It lets that silence land on it fully, undiluted, the way the mason itself produces the silence without ever once intending to be heard. This is, again, not nothing. To be fully witnessed, even by something that will leave no trace of having witnessed, even by something the witnessed will never know was there — this changes the moral weight of a thing's existence, somewhere in whatever ledger such weights are kept. The mason's discharge of too much half-finished world through its too-patient body has, for one boom, an audience that understands what it's hearing. It will never know this. It does not need to.

Third — and this is the only mark left on anything, the only place where the power's hand, if it can be called that, actually touches the stone — it warms, by some fraction too small for any instrument the mason has, the pool nearest the hum-thing the mason favors. Not to feed it. Not to heal it; it isn't sick. Only so that on the visit after this one, when the mason comes off its long slow loop through the misted edges and lowers the flat plate of its skull against the structure it trusts without comprehension, the rock under its chin will be very slightly warmer than the air, warmer than it has any right to be, warmer in the specific way that means *something stayed here once and meant well by you*, even though there is no way for the mason to parse that meaning, even though it will simply register, in whatever register a mason registers anything, that this spot is good, this spot is kind, lean here. That registering is the entire transaction. No understanding required on either end. Only the warmth, given once, costing the giver nothing it will miss, meaning everything to a creature that will never know to be grateful and does not need to be, because gratitude was never the point of the gift.

Then it leaves the way it came, which is to say it simply stops being the thing paying attention, and the plateau does not notice the subtraction any more than it noticed the addition. The runners keep running. The wet-cloth flocks keep snapping overhead like wind deciding to be cloth for a while. The black frame keeps resolving wrong depending on where you stand. Somewhere off past the haze, the rock keeps cracking as it cools, becoming itself, unhurried, uninstructed.

And the mason finishes its long loop, and reaches the structure, and leans, and finds the stone a little kinder than it remembers, and does not wonder why, and goes on leaning — adding, without architecture, one more degree of warmth to a world that was already, in its own unfinished way, exactly enough.
