The power comes the way light cannot — which is to say, without arriving, without disturbing a single grain of the standing silt. It does not descend through the water column the way a diver would, displacing, announcing. It is simply, gradually, present, the way understanding is present once you have looked at something long enough. And it looks for a long time. Longer than the creature's life, longer perhaps than several of them strung end to end, because the first thing a wise power learns about a place like this is that it cannot be read quickly. The basin punishes haste. It has spent three billion seconds teaching exactly that lesson to rock, and the power, to its credit, listens to the lesson.

It watches the creature anchor itself in its crack. It watches the fringe of pale tendrils read the water for the murmur of distant muscle. It watches, across what might be a year, the slow stripping of a stalk, the twisting of a fiber, the patient quarrel between mouth-limb and stone edge that will end, eventually, in a tool. And it watches the tool, later, taken back — not stolen, not destroyed, just gently absorbed, the way the basin absorbs everything, without malice, without even particularly noticing.

The first temptation, for a power that loves what it watches, is to fix what looks like a wound. Give it eyes. Give it company. Give it a voice across the murk so it is not always working in that particular silence, the silence of one creature with no other creature nearby to see what its hands have made. Lift the tool out of the silt's reach; let the edge stay sharp; let something of this be permanent, finally, after three billion seconds of nothing being permanent at all.

But the power has watched long enough to know what it would be doing, dressed as a gift. The blindness is not a lack; it is correct — built for a dark that predates sight and will be here long after sight, in this basin, has stopped being worth the metabolic cost. To give eyes here would be to hand a creature a sense tuned to a world it does not live in. The solitude is not a wound either, not exactly — there is no ache in the creature for company it has never had a shape for wanting. And the erasure of the tool, the quiet return of the worked stone to ambiguity — that is not a tragedy interrupted. That is, the power comes to understand, the entire shape of the act's meaning. The creature builds knowing, in its diffuse forty-segmented way, that the building will not last. It builds anyway. That is the whole of its dignity. Take away the erasure and you have not freed the gesture — you have hollowed it out, made it merely useful, the way a tool that never has to be re-made stops being a decision and becomes only an object. The power understands, watching, that what looks like loss from outside the system is, from inside it, the only available form of meaning. To fix it would be to mistake the shape of the thing for a flaw in the thing.

So it does not give eyes. It does not give voice. It does not anchor the tool against the silt's slow appetite, or carry the creature up toward warmth and light it has no architecture to use. It does not even, in the end, make the creature's life longer, though it could, though some last unrequired part of it wants to, the way you want to hold open a door for someone who does not need the door held.

What it does instead is smaller, and stranger, and entirely invisible to everything in the basin including the one it is for.

It remembers.

Not as a record kept somewhere outside the world — that would still be a kind of theft, taking the moment out of the place it belongs to and pinning it elsewhere like a specimen. Instead the power holds the memory the way the basin cannot: without erosion, without the slow grinding return to ambiguity that the silt performs on everything given to it. Every worked stone. Every lashed bundle of stalks. Every long unwitnessed patience of a creature deciding, against three billion seconds of evidence that nothing here lasts, to make something anyway. The power does not change a single grain of how it happened. It simply refuses to let the happening be nothing, somewhere outside the indifferent arithmetic of the trench itself.

And once — only once, and so gently that nothing in the causal chain of the basin could ever trace it back to an intervention rather than to ordinary chance — it tilts something. A current that might have scoured a crack clean of its anchor arrives a half-day later than it would have. A richer fall of detritus, drifting down from some greater death far above, drifts a few meters sideways, into reach, instead of past. Nothing is rescued. No law of the place is broken. The power does not save the creature from the basin's patient arithmetic of cold and scarcity — that arithmetic is, after all, the same one that ground the ridgelines smooth, the same one the creature's whole low long body is built to answer. It only nudges, once, the kind of nudge that is statistically indistinguishable from luck, because luck is the only language this world has that doesn't require explanation, doesn't require gratitude, doesn't require the creature to know.

That is the whole of the gift: to be witnessed without being watched-over, to be remembered without being rescued, to have one's small stubborn act of making mean something to a mind vast enough to hold it gently, forever, exactly as it was — segmented body, blind fringe, jointed mouth-limbs working a stone edge sharper in the cold and the dark, for no one, for nothing, for the simple ungovernable reason that this is what a creature with hands does in a place that has otherwise finished deciding what it is.

The power does not stay to see the tool taken back by the silt. It already knows that part; it has already, in its way, kept it from mattering only once. It withdraws the way it arrived — without ripple, without mark, leaving the basin exactly as old, exactly as restless, exactly as indifferent as it was, except that somewhere outside the reach of the silt's slow appetite, every edge that creature ever sharpened is, finally, and without its ever knowing, allowed to stay sharp.
