The power does not arrive the way power is supposed to arrive. There is no parting of the noise, no hush dropped over the machinery like a hand over a mouth. The thrum keeps going, four-stroke, patient, indifferent. What changes is smaller than that, and slower: something takes up a position at the rim and does not leave.

It does not announce itself as anything. If you were standing there you would not see a figure, a light, a visitation. You would only notice, eventually, that the basin has acquired a second kind of attention — not yours, not the fauna's chase-the-clock half-beat, but something with no schedule at all, content to simply remain. It watches the way the place itself seems to have been watching, this whole time, without anyone there to receive it: total, unhurried, asking nothing back.

For a long while — the basin's idea of a while, which is seasons, which is the lake creeping a few more centimeters over its old terraces — it does nothing else. It does not touch the water. It does not quiet the hiss or steady the boil of foam. It has come to a place where industry preceded life and has the wisdom, rare even among wise things, not to mistake the order of arrival for an invitation to rearrange it. It does not try to fix a process for the crime of having no maker. It looks, instead, for the one true thing a long unhurried look can find that a quick merciful one cannot: where, in this wheel, something like suffering might actually be happening, as opposed to merely resembling itself.

It finds it where the apprentices already knew to look. Out past the soft intake sacks, past their shy translucent filling, in the band of water where the current presses bodies into plate and ridge and basalt sheen — there, in the bracing, in the held breath of compression, it finds the one phase that costs something. Not because the bodies cry out. They don't. Not because there is blood, or a sound under the sound. There isn't. But because every other stage in the cycle moves toward a use it seems to want — the sack wants filling, the power-stroke wants its arc, the pale glow below wants only to finish becoming water again — and the compression stage alone seems to want nothing at all except for it to be over. That is the whole of the evidence. It is enough.

So the power goes there. Not into the water — it does not put on a body the place never asked for, doesn't pretend to know this engine's grammar well enough to wear it. It simply moves its attention from the rim to directly above that band of water and stays, the way the half-formed apprentices stay near the forge: close, unmoving, not touching, present in the specific manner of something that has decided to keep a thing company through the part of its existence that asks to be alone and isn't given the choice. It does this every time compression comes due to any single body, which is constantly, which is forever, which from outside the wheel looks like standing in one spot and from inside the wheel — if the wheel can be said to have an inside — might, just possibly, feel like no longer bracing by yourself.

It does not stop the squeeze. It could not, without stopping the arc that follows, without unmaking the very thing that, a half-beat later, gets to go hard in one direction with the only unconflicted purpose this place has ever produced. To spare the compression is to erase the power-stroke; the wise power knows enough to know what it doesn't know, and declines to perform a rescue that would really be a small, well-intentioned murder of the whole cycle wearing mercy's clothes. What it offers instead is smaller and, it suspects, more honest: not less pressure, but less solitude inside the pressure. The difference between those two may be the only difference available to give here, and it gives it without expecting thanks, since nothing in the water has a mouth shaped for that.

Then it does one more thing, and only one.

At the rim, where the black rock has only just stopped being lava long enough to remember the shape of cooling, where nothing green has ever had time or permission or inclination to take hold, it sets down a seed. A single one. Not a colony, not a planting, not a verdict that this place was wrong to be only what it is. Just a possibility, dormant, asking nothing, requiring nothing, content to sit in basalt grit through a hundred more years of brimming if that's what it takes, or to dissolve unsprouted into the high thin rain and never trouble the place at all. The power does not stay to make it take root. It does not believe roots should be made. It only believes — and this is the whole of its theology, if it has one — that a place which has only ever been permitted to be one thing should, at minimum, be given the option of a second, even if the option goes forever untaken. Permission is the gift. Outcome is not the power's to decide.

It does turn, before it goes, toward whoever is standing at the rim raising their voice over the machinery, watching one particular sack through to arc through to plate through to glow through to dissolution and feeling the absurd parental tug of having loved something with no name for a single one of its seasons. It does not explain the place to them. It does not resolve, for their comfort, whether the thing in the water is alive or simply still running, because it suspects — rightly — that the question was never the point, and that an answer would only let them stop feeling the tug, and the tug was the truest thing that happened to them all day. What it leaves them instead is smaller and stranger than an answer: the sense, arriving from nowhere, that their grief at the rim was witnessed and found neither foolish nor excessive. That someone — something — thought it was the correct size.

Then it goes back to simply staying. It does not silence the four-stroke thrum. It does not give the segmented bodies a softer arc or the pale glow below a floor to finally reach. It leaves the cistern exactly as full, exactly as loud, exactly as indifferent to being watched as it was the day the power found it — except that now, in the one band of water where something resembling want goes unmet, there is company that was not there before, keeping a vigil with no end date, on a schedule slower than geology, beside a forge nobody built and nobody runs and nobody, until now, ever simply stood near.

The lake goes on brimming. The seed waits at the rim, in no hurry, the way the basin itself has always been in no hurry. And if you came back in fifty years, in five hundred, raising your voice again over the same unbroken chorus, you would not be able to point to anything changed. That is the nature of the gift it chose. Some kinds of mercy leave no mark you can find by looking — only, perhaps, a fraction less solitude in the one place that had been asking, wordlessly, the whole time, not to be unmade, only not to brace alone.
