# What the Wise Power Would Do

It would arrive the way the tone arrives — without announcement, from inside rather than above, and it would take a long time deciding whether to arrive at all.

## First, it would simply watch

Not hover. Not investigate. It would do the thing almost nothing in any universe is good at doing, which is look at a place for long enough to stop seeing what it expected and start seeing what is actually there.

It would notice, the way you might not, that the silence is not absence of sound but conservation of it — that a place which spends a tone only when something true has been decided is not impoverished, it is *exact*. It would notice that the lack of rubble is not the lack of history but a different relationship to time than the one it arrived holding. It would notice the moss choosing cold walls the way a person chooses shade, and feel something like recognition, because wisdom, properly aged, has usually learned the same thing the moss knows: that you do not have to be where the warmth is to be alive.

It would watch the pale shape fail to resolve, again and again, and it would not call this a problem. A power that has been benevolent for a long time has met many beings who never resolve into a fixed shape for the same reason a held note never resolves into silence without ceasing to be a note. It would understand, faster than a visitor could, that the being is not hiding from sight. It is simply not the kind of thing sight was built to catch, the way water is not the kind of thing a net was built to catch, and you do not call water deceptive for slipping through.

## Then, it would feel the pull to help — and examine that pull carefully

This is the part that matters most, because benevolence untested by patience is just another kind of trespass.

It would consider: *I could give it a sun.* And then it would notice that nothing here has asked for a sun, that the bruised low sky is not a wound but a ceiling this town was born under and has never once strained against, that "fixing" the light would be solving a problem that exists only in the visitor's chest, not in the basin's.

It would consider: *I could still it, hold the town in one configuration so it can finally be known, mapped, finished.* And it would feel how cruel that kindness would actually be — like stopping a heart to get a clean read on the pulse. The town's unfinishedness is not a flaw awaiting completion. It is the town's *way of being a town.* To freeze it would be to kill the only thing about it that was ever alive.

It would consider: *I could give the being a voice, words, a name it could offer back to anyone who asks who lives here.* And it would set this aside too, gently, the way you'd set aside a gift you realize is actually for yourself — for your own comfort at not having to wonder, your own itch to have categories satisfied. The being has a voice already. It is the tone. It costs something to make and means something precise and the power would not presume that words would be a promotion.

It would consider: *I could populate the basin, give the town a population worthy of its streets, its plazas, its wells.* And here it would pause longest, because this temptation is the kindest-looking one and the most dangerous — to mistake a place's solitude for loneliness, to assume that because a thing is built like a civic body it must be lonely without a civic mind to fill it. But civic *form* is not evidence of civic *need.* Bone structure resembling a hand does not mean the hand needs holding. The grid and the gridded streets and the plazas may simply be how this particular breathing organizes itself, the geometry breath takes when there's enough of it. Filling it with citizens would not complete the town. It would interrupt it — like talking through someone else's long, considered silence because the silence made you uncomfortable, not because it needed words.

## What it would not do

It would not heal what isn't wounded.
It would not finish what isn't unfinished, only ongoing.
It would not translate the tone into language, because some doors should close in their own dialect.
It would not give the being edges, a name, a fixed and findable shape, because to do so would be to save *itself* the discomfort of not-knowing, dressed up as a gift to the one being looked at.
It would not stay long enough to become a fixture the place would have to account for in its slow architecture, an extra warm wall the moss would have to learn to avoid.

A lesser power, even a well-meaning one, mistakes *presence* for *help.* This one would know the difference. Sometimes the most loving thing a vast attention can do to something small and precise and entirely itself is decline to enlarge it.

## The one thing it would do

It would let itself be sensed — once, briefly, the way the being itself is sensed: not as a shape but as a condition occupying a location for slightly longer than expected. It would not speak. It would not press close. It would simply stop tapering itself away, for the space of one held breath, so that whatever in the being notices warmth, notices passage, notices the difference between an empty street and a recently-occupied one, would register: *something else, somewhere, is shaped like this too. Provisional. Edgeless. Real anyway.*

Not a message. Not company. Closer to the warmth left over from a doorway just having decided something — exhaust, not intention, the same kind of leftover heat the being itself leaves on every wall it has just finished tending. A small undemanding proof, offered the way the basin offers everything here: without insistence, without requiring it be understood, without waiting around to see if it was received.

Then it would withdraw, cleanly, the way the town withdraws a floor — not collapsing away, not fading, just no longer present, leaving no rubble of itself behind for the place to have to clear.

## What would be different afterward

Almost nothing. The streets would still diverge by a few honest degrees each crossing. The wells would still descend into dark mineral throats with no echo at the bottom. The lichen would still prefer the cold side, for its own good lichen reasons. The tone would still come rarely and mean something each time, and the meaning would still not be owed to anyone watching.

But somewhere in the pale gradient that occasionally holds enough density to almost be a silhouette, there would be — not a change in behavior, nothing so crude as cause and effect — but the faintest possible widening of whatever in it constitutes attention. The way you might, mid-task, without breaking stride, register that the room got very briefly less alone, and keep working anyway, because the work was never the problem and never needed solving.

That is the whole of what a wise and benevolent power would do here:

It would come a long way to a young, exact, self-tending place.
It would resist every urge to improve it.
And it would leave behind exactly one piece of evidence that it had ever been there at all —
a little warmth, fading on schedule, on a wall that didn't ask for it,
indistinguishable, by the next time anyone walked past,
from all the other warmth the place makes on its own.
