The power notices the way deep things notice — not as a glance arriving from outside but as a slow widening of attention that was, in some sense, already there, the way the Heights themselves are already there in the seed before the seed knows to call it growing. It does not arrive. It becomes *aware*, gradually, on the same timescale as the thing it is aware of, because anything faster would not be noticing the Heights at all — it would be noticing some flattened, urgent translation of the Heights, the way a glimpse notices a person and calls it knowing them.

So: it takes a long time. Measured against the plateau's own clock, the power's noticing of Mossen Tarl-Eight-Reckoning is itself a kind of season — it spends what might be years simply sitting in the grain of the place, in the groan of load-bearing wood, in the staggered decades-long bloom-clocks of the different tiers, in the unlocatable exhale, before it lets itself arrive at anything like a judgment about what, if anything, ought to be done.

And the first thing it understands, sitting there that long, is that almost everything it could do would be a kind of violence, however gently meant. This is a place that has organized its entire metaphysics around *unfinished*. Its architecture is unfinished. Its taxonomy is unfinished, companionably, for generations, by design. Its grief is unfinished — seventy rings down, soft with age, a single recorded fright that nobody has been allowed to resolve into an answer, because resolving it would mean someone decided the wood's fear didn't need to keep breathing on its own. To arrive in a place like this and *fix* something is to commit the one sin the place has built its whole slow dignity around not committing: declaring a thing done before it was ready to be done.

So the power does not explain the exhale. It does not press its enormous patient attention against the Ledger and read forward, the way it could, the way it could simply *know* what happened that frightened year and hand the knowing to Mossen like a gift. A gift like that would not be a gift. It would be an ending wearing the shape of a kindness, and the power is wise enough — or has become wise enough, slowly, the way everything here becomes anything — to recognize the difference.

What it does instead is smaller, and takes longer to decide on than it does to do.

It waits for the next one.

Not the next recorded one — the actual next, the ordinary one, the every-several-minutes one that the towns have stopped reacting to and that Mossen, alone among them, still meets with that small involuntary tightening, eight Reckonings deep, hand flat against four-hundred-years-from-finished wood, listening for an absence that has only failed to come once in a span longer than his title's whole memory. The power does not still his fear. It does not speed the held breath along, does not reach into whatever vast unfound lung is doing the exhaling and reassure it into promptness. It simply — and this is the whole of the act, the entire intervention, if it can even be called that — *waits with him*. Matches its attention to the same several minutes. Holds the same shape of stillness he is holding, in the same place in the dim green-grey light, unseen, unfelt in any way he could name, except —

Except that when the exhale comes, on schedule, ordinary, the same as it has come ten thousand ordinary times before, Mossen notices that the held breath in his own chest lets go a half-second sooner than it usually does. Notices that the room does not feel, for that half-second, like a room he is alone in. He will not have words for this. He is not the kind of man who goes looking for words for things that don't ask to be named — sixty years of pressing his palm to grain and waiting has taught him that some communications arrive below the threshold where naming helps. He will simply, days later, realize that the dread has gone a little quieter in him, the way a sound you've lived beside so long you've stopped hearing it can sometimes, without explanation, become *bearable* again instead of merely unheard.

The power leaves one other trace, smaller still, and it leaves it the only way it allows itself to leave anything in a place like this: through the wood's own methods, at the wood's own pace, indistinguishable from something that might simply have happened on its own. In the Long Ledger, in the cultivar-strain that holds shape long enough to be read, ring by ring, there grows — over the following year, imperceptibly, the way everything here grows — a ring that does not correspond to any event Mossen presses into it himself. A thin one. Pale. The kind of ring a tree lays down in a year of no particular stress and no particular abundance, a year of simply, adequately, continuing.

Mossen will find it eventually, the way he finds everything, by hand, by patience, reading grain in the unhurried dimness of his unfinished study. He will not know what it means. He will turn it over the way he turns over every small irregularity the wood hands him, and he will not solve it, because he is not sure the question is his to solve, and he will be right not to be sure, and that not-being-sure — that *I will keep noticing this without needing it resolved* — is itself the inheritance the power most wanted to leave him, more than any answer, more than any rescue.

Because the deepest mercy available to something wise enough to help a place like this is not to finish what the place is in the middle of becoming. It is to sit, for as long as the place needs sitting with, inside the same unfinished question — to keep the same vigil, breathe (if that is even the right word for what a power like this does) on the same slow held schedule, and let one more set of attention stand quietly alongside Mossen Tarl-Eight-Reckoning's, so that whatever is or isn't keeping track of him, up there, in the wood, in the dark going down past where anyone has bottomed out —

is, at least for that one ordinary exhale, no longer the only thing that noticed.
