The power comes the way weather comes to the plateau — not arriving so much as condensing, gathering its attention out of something that was already faintly present, until there is, where there was nothing, a kind of regard. It does not announce itself with light or voice. It would not know how to, here, without doing violence to the hush that the Districts have spent geologic time perfecting. So it simply attends, the way the towers themselves attend to centuries: patiently, at a scale too large for the attended-to to notice.

It looks first at the whole of it — the hundred kilometers of ranked silhouette, the three weather fronts visible at once like separate thoughts being had simultaneously, the mineral sheets laying down their microns under cloud that has never once been asked whether it wants to keep doing this. And the temptation, if a wise power can be said to feel temptation, arrives almost immediately: to help. To thicken the food web. To warm the water half a degree more, so the bacterial mats bloom wider, so the threadworms multiply, so the seep-thrush doesn't have to work eleven hours of cold stitching for a fistful of larvae, so the mist-fox doesn't have to economize every motion as if motion were a debt. To smooth one winter's freeze-thaw cycle so a den doesn't get crushed by a slab letting go its long-held breath at the wrong hour.

It considers each of these and sets each of them down, gently, the way you'd set down something you picked up before realizing it was load-bearing.

Because the power is wise, and wisdom here looks almost exactly like restraint. It understands, looking long enough, that the thinness is not a wound. The Districts were never trying to be abundant and failing. Persistence is the entire argument of this place — stacked thin layer on thin layer until something stands on top of it and calls itself alive — and abundance would not improve that argument, it would erase it. A mist-fox with an easy life is not a mist-fox. It's a different animal wearing the same coat. The power can see, the way only something outside of time fully can, that every adaptation here — the webbed pads, the bone-pale coat that disappears at twenty meters, the bill curved to one exact purpose, the eggs built to survive a hard freeze — is a sentence in a story about working a small thing for a long time without complaint, and that story doesn't have a version where the ending is loosened without the whole shape of it coming undone.

So: not abundance. Not ease. Whatever gift gets given here has to be the kind that fits inside the existing grammar of the place, the way new mineral sheets fit inside the existing architecture of a tower — adding without overwriting.

The power settles, finally, on the one true absence it can address without unmaking anything: this is a place, the record is explicit about it, "empty of anything that watches back." A hundred kilometers of structure built with the unconscious fidelity of a civilization, and not one citizen, not one eye, not one mind anywhere in it to register that it is beautiful, or hard, or patient, or this much itself. The water doesn't know what it's building. The lichen doesn't know it's the only color for fifty kilometers. The seep-thrush doesn't know its single dry call is the loudest tender thing in a hundred square kilometers of stone. Nothing here has ever been *witnessed* in the sense of being held in a mind that understood what it was looking at.

That, the power can give, and giving it costs the Districts nothing. It does not change the rain. It does not change the freeze. It simply attends — fully, without flinching from the cold or the silence or the long stretches where nothing happens — to one thread of it. It chooses, the way you might choose a single drainage channel out of a thousand identical ones, a particular mist-fox: a female, solitary now that the brief wet-season pairing has ended, denning alone in a hollow under a slab that fell two winters back and finally found, after all that settling rattle, an angle of rest stable enough to roof a body.

The power does not warm her den. It does not lead her to easier hunting. It does the one thing available to it that doesn't require touching the mechanism: it stays. It is present, fully, in the specific gravity of her specific nights — the fifty-meter crossings she makes at a dead run because the wind-funnels between towers will kill an animal that hesitates; the eleven small kills a month that keep her exactly, precisely alive and not one calorie further; the morning she finds another fox's print drying in the grit and stands very still for longer than hunting requires, just to register that something else, however briefly, passed this way. None of it is dramatic. All of it is, now, known. Held. Counted by something other than stone.

And once — only once, because the gift has to stay small enough to leave no causal mark, no fingerprint a naturalist could ever find and call anomalous — the fox lifts her head at a sound that isn't quite wind and isn't quite the flat acoustic of dripping water swallowed by stone, and for the length of one breath she is not afraid. She doesn't know why. There is no why available to her; foxes don't have a slot in their thinking for *I was seen*, only for *danger* and *not-danger*, and what passes through her in that breath registers simply as *not-danger*, a fractional easing in a body that has never had the luxury of easing for no evolutionary reason. She shakes it off in another second and goes back to working the seam of moss at the cave mouth for what it's hiding. The power doesn't expect more than that. A fraction of a breath of unexplained ease, given to an animal that will never know it was given anything, is not nothing. In a place this thin, where every stacked layer matters, it might be exactly the right size of gift — the only size that wouldn't crush what it touched.

The rest, the power simply carries. The seep-thrush's single dry note dying in the open air. The cartographic spread of lichen, continents nobody drew. The long grinding crack of a slab letting go, rare enough to be the loudest event in days. The three fronts working three districts at three different hours, visible all at once from no vantage any creature here will ever stand at. It carries all of it forward, out past the haze that swallows everything past the third visible district, into whatever larger memory a power like this keeps — not to change the place, not to redeem it from its thinness, which was never a problem requiring redemption, but simply so that somewhere, finally, after all this geologic time, there exists a mind that knows the Standing Districts happened. That the water built a city no one lived in, and patience and economy moved into it anyway, and called it enough, and were — by every quiet measure that mattered to them — right to.
