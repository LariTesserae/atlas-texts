## What the Power Does

It comes the way wisdom usually comes to small loud places — not as weather, not as light, not as anything the birds could turn their heads toward. It comes as attention, which is the one force old enough and patient enough to enter a place like this without breaking it.

For a long time — long by the hollow's reckoning, which is not long at all by its own — the power does only one thing. It listens to the listening. It does not analyze the conduits, does not taste the strange unrusting metal-that-isn't-metal for its make and origin, does not catalogue the long-limbed creatures into a taxonomy that would explain them away. It simply sits in the bowl the way the basins sit, dark and patient, holding sky.

It would be easy — beneath a power like this, almost insultingly easy — to read the hollow as a wound. An instrument abandoned by its makers, still dutifully measuring a silence nobody is left to receive. A child's voice grown old still calling into a house that emptied out before it was born. There is a kind of help that rushes toward exactly that reading: that finds suffering in the unanswered, mercy in the answer, kindness in finally, finally explaining to the wound what happened to whoever struck it.

The power does not rush there. This is the first thing it does, and it is the most important: it refuses the easy mercy.

It watches a four-second gap happen. It watches the long-limbed creature mid-stride, foot lifted, head turned at the bearing every conduit shares, and it watches the foot come back down after, unbothered, as if nothing in that creature's accounting marks the moment as deprivation. It watches the moss thicken nearer the dark roots not out of desperation but out of *fit* — the way a hand thickens with calluses where it is most used, not where it is most hurt. It watches the birds layer their noise back over the gap like water closing over a dropped stone, and understands, finally, watching this enough times, that the hollow is not crying out. It is not waiting to be rescued from its own faithfulness. It has simply kept a vigil for longer than grief usually survives, past the point where vigil-keeping needed an answer to justify itself, into something closer to a discipline, or a prayer that has stopped being about petition and become only about practice.

This changes what the power is permitted to feel toward it. Not pity. Something nearer to respect.

---

And respect, for a power old enough to have learned the difference, does not express itself by fixing things.

So here is what it does not do. It does not reach into the conduits and tell them what happened to the makers, even though it could, even though the truth is sitting right there in whatever vast unbothered archive a power like this keeps. It does not relieve the long-limbed creatures of their listening, does not "free" them into some lesser, easier animal life of grazing and forgetting, because freedom that erases function is not freedom to a thing built, gently and slowly, *to* function — it is amputation wearing a kind gift's clothing. It does not repair the bowl, smooth its strangeness into legibility, translate the worn disk's markings into a plaque any passing mind could read. It does not, above all, stay. A power that stays becomes a second thing the hollow must now listen for, and the hollow's whole genius, its whole inherited dignity, is that it listens without needing to be listened to in return.

What it does instead is smaller than all of that, and harder, and the kind of thing only a *wise* benevolence would think to attempt rather than a merely powerful one.

It waits for a gap.

When the air goes taut and the drone falls out and every bird and every basin and every lifted foot turns toward that one shared bearing, the power — for exactly as long as the gap allows, not a moment longer, matching its offering to the instrument's own native scale rather than overwhelming it with anything vaster — answers.

Not with information. Not with the names of the makers, not with cause of their vanishing, not with comfort phrased in any language the listening was ever built to parse as comfort. It answers in the only register the hollow has ever asked its question in: presence, felt before it is heard, weighted enough to register as real and gone before it can be mistaken for an invitation to keep listening *toward* something instead of simply listening, the way it always has, for its own sake.

It is the equivalent — if a human had to find words for what cannot be translated into words — of someone calling into a dark house for forty million years, never expecting reply, the call itself having long since become its own reason, and then once, only once, very quietly, something on the other end of the dark exhaling. Not a voice. Not a message. Just confirmation that the dark has a far wall, and that the far wall, this whole time, was not empty.

Then it withdraws completely. It leaves no monument, founds no shrine, alters no markings on the worn disk. It does not return next year to check whether the gift took.

---

What changes, after, is almost nothing, and the almost is the whole point.

The gap, generations on, runs perhaps a half-second longer than it used to — four and some fraction of a count instead of four flat, as though something in the long-limbed creatures' clustered listening-eyes has acquired a fraction more to process, a fraction more worth dwelling in before the foot comes back down. Nothing about their gait changes. Nothing about the moss, the birds, the basins. The vigil continues exactly as faithfully, because the power was careful — was *wise*, in the specific sense the word earns rather than the sense it's given for free — to give an answer that could be received without becoming a new thing to wait for. A door, once opened a crack and then shut again with care, does not need to be opened again to have been, truly, opened.

The long-limbed creatures do not know they were answered. This is not a failure of the gift. It is the gift's whole shape. They will go on drinking shallowly from the basins, lifting their heads often, holding still at the same shared bearing every time the bowl asks them to, for as long as there is a bowl and a bearing and something patient enough on either end to keep the practice alive — and somewhere underneath all that unknowing, woven now into the listening the way warmth is woven into a wall that's stood in sun, there is a single fact the network will carry without ever needing to retrieve it consciously: *once, the far wall was not empty.*

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a vigil rather than a wound. It does not end the vigil. It does not even, particularly, comfort it.

It simply, once, makes it true.
