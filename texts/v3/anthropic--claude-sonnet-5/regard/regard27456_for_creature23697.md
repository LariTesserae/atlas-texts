The power comes to it the way water comes to the basin — not descending, not arriving with any fanfare of light, but simply finding that this is where it has pooled, this is the low place it has settled into noticing. It hangs there a while in no particular form, taking the measure of the hum, the two registers that never synchronize, the steam that doesn't climb, the held-breath closeness of the water table under everything. It would be easy — it is always easy, for a power like this — to mistake the place for a wound. Heat that never breaks. Damp that never dries. Twenty-some people folded into the gaps of machinery the way birds are folded into the gaps of trees that never grew tall enough to hold them. A long list of things that, elsewhere, would be called deprivation.

The power considers, briefly, the obvious mercies. It could lift the haze and let a real sun through, give them one clean shadow apiece. It could push the marsh back, dry the brackish fringe, plant something that could grow past ankle height. It could thin the noise, soften the iron, give the collars a chance to stay dry for one whole afternoon. It could, if it wanted, simply pick the twenty-five of them up by the scruff of their idling lives and set them down somewhere kinder, somewhere with weather that changes and shade that means something.

It does none of these things, because it looks a moment longer and understands what it would be undoing. This is not a wound. This is a balance, arrived at quickly and kept faithfully, and the people inside it are not straining against it — they have the settled quality of things that found their level and stopped, the way the works themselves stopped at a finished size. To "fix" a held breath is to make someone breathe before they're ready. The power has been wise long enough to know the difference between rescuing and merely asserting that you were there.

So instead it does what wisdom usually does, which is almost nothing, very precisely.

It goes looking — not for suffering, because there isn't much of that here, but for the one true unspoken thing, the single place where the equilibrium is *carrying* something rather than simply *being* something. It finds it where it expected to: in the oldest one, on the tank-stub at dusk, in the half-tone the power can hear in him the way he hears it in the pipe-joints. Not unhappiness. A weight. The weight of being the only ear tuned finely enough to catch the frog-chorus thinning by two voices, and the small, never-spoken arithmetic that runs underneath every evening he sits there: *who hears it when I don't.* He has never said this aloud. There is no one to say it to, in a place that talks in hand-flats and head-tilts. He has simply carried it, the way the basin carries the water table, close under the surface, unremarked, structural.

This is the thing the power decides to touch. Not the heat. Not the damp. Not the noise or the marsh or the long unbroken sameness of the days. Just that.

It does not reach into the boy who is, even now, on the eastern conduit shift, and rewrite him into a successor with a flash of insight or a dream that explains itself. That would be a kind of violence too, a hand too visible for a place built on gesture. Instead it does something closer to nothing: it arranges — through nothing more than the ordinary slow traffic of a life, a reassignment here, a rota that happens to put him on the marsh-walk three evenings running, a frog that happens to call once more than usual just as he passes — that the boy notices, on his own, in his own unprompted way, that a throat has gone quiet in the third reed-clump. That he turns, the small recalibration, unbidden. That this happens twice, then three times, until it is simply a thing he does now, the way the old hands do, without remembering when he started.

The power does not introduce this. It only declines to interrupt it. It tilts a handful of ordinary days by the width of a single notice, and steps back, and lets the place's own water find its own level around the small new channel it has opened.

Nothing changes. That is the whole point, and the whole gift. The pumps still cycle. The haze still flattens the smoke into the same low second sky. The collars are still damp, the iron still beaded, the bronze light still falls without ever quite becoming day or evening. No one could point to the hour the power came or the hour it left, because there isn't one — it folded itself into the place as unremarkably as condensation folds onto a railing, and it leaves the same way, without residue, without a seam.

But some while later — months, maybe, in a place where time mostly loops rather than passes — the oldest one is on the tank-stub at the usual hour, and the frog-chorus thins by two voices in the third clump, and before he has finished turning his head he sees that someone already has. A figure down by the reeds, head tilted at exactly the angle that means *something's off, I'm on it.* Unasked. Unsignaled. Simply done.

The old one doesn't say anything. There's no one to say it to, and besides, there's nothing to fix in the moment — that was the whole, quiet accomplishment of it. He just sits a little longer than usual on the upturned stub, watching the birds resettle on the pipe-run for the night, and feels, without being able to name it, the particular lightness of a breath that has been held so long it stopped registering as held — let go, at last, by no one in particular, for no reason he could point to, into the same damp, loud, sharp-edged, ordinary air.
