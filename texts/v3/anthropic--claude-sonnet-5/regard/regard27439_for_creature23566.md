# What the Power Found, and What It Did

It came the way such powers come to such places — not descending, not arriving, but simply becoming present, the way a thought becomes present in a mind that wasn't thinking it a moment before. It did not disturb the dripping. It did not interrupt the dissonance between the pistons and the drip-rhythm that refuses to resolve into pattern. It stood — if *stood* means anything for a thing with no feet and no need of them — in the deafening dark, and it listened the way the Tenant listens: with its whole surface, not just an ear.

And having listened, it understood, faster than any small mind could, the entire shape of the place: the silt-floor still deciding its declivities, the raw seams not yet healed, the crowding of nests and clutches and blind scuttling things stacked shoulder to shoulder in the warm wet dark, the feral machinery breathing its steady mechanical breath into a body of stone that had adopted it without ever agreeing to need it. It understood the Tenant — its drumhead hide, its decades or centuries of unknowing maintenance, its accidental tenancy of a stewardship no one assigned it. It understood the rule the gallery had written for itself: *nothing finished, nothing empty.*

## What Was Considered, and Set Aside

A power like this one has done, in other places, many obvious kindnesses, and it turned each of them over here, briefly, the way you'd turn over a stone to see what's underneath before deciding to leave it where it lay.

It considered giving the Tenant sight. Eyes are easy, for a power like this — a small unfolding of dormant tissue, a few new pathways lit. But sight is not a gift to a thing that abandoned sight on purpose, generations before its own birth, in favor of a hide that can read the whole gallery's tremor at once. To give it eyes would be to take away the larger sense it already has, the one that lets it feel the difference between a falling pebble and a falling body three chambers away. This was set aside.

It considered repairing the machinery properly — replacing worn housings, restoring the corroded catwalk, freeing the door stuck in its shifted frame, making the engines run the way their builders meant them to run, forever, without need of incidental flesh pressed against their valves. But the Tenant's whole inner season — torpor, waking, the slow swell before laying — has been knitted for longer than memory to that specific imperfect thud, the one transmitted through worn iron and tired pistons, not to some restored and gleaming version of it. A perfect machine is not a kindness to a creature that loves the flawed one. This, too, was set aside.

It considered waking the Tenant to itself — granting it the awareness to know what it tends, to feel pride or purpose in the keeping of the pumps, to understand its own four hundred years of patient accidental service. But awareness is a weight, and this creature has never once needed to carry it, has done everything right precisely *because* it never had to think about doing it at all. To wake it would not be a gift. It would be an interruption dressed as one.

It considered lifting the whole gallery toward the light — widening a throat toward the surface, letting sun reach the pale fungus, calling it improvement. But the fungus is pale on purpose, the moss is blind on purpose, the whole machinery of this place has spent its entire existence becoming suited to exactly this much dark and exactly this much wet. Light here would not be a blessing. It would be an amputation.

Each of these was a real kindness, somewhere else, to some other being. Here, each one would have corrected something that was never broken — would have finished a place whose entire law is that nothing gets finished.

## What Was Seen Instead

But wisdom is not only the discipline of restraint; it is also the discernment to find the one true fragility hidden inside an otherwise sound thing, and tend *that*, lightly, without touching anything else.

And the power found it: the machinery is mortal in a way the geology is not. Stone keeps its promises on a timescale that makes patience look hasty. Iron does not. The pumps that have run themselves for longer than anyone alive remembers are still, after all, only iron and old wiring, and somewhere in a future too far off to trouble any short-lived thing but not too far off to trouble stone, they will fail — not gradually, the way the cave changes, but suddenly, the way machines do. A bearing will seize. A line will finally corrode through instead of merely weeping at its seams. And the steady mechanical pulse the Tenant has entrained its whole long body to, the rhythm under whose beat it has scheduled its waking and its torpor and the laying of its warmest clutches for as long as it has been alive — that pulse will simply stop.

A creature that has shared a heartbeat with something for centuries does not do well when the heartbeat stops without warning. It is not built to notice the silence as anything other than wrongness, and wrongness, to a thing that has never needed fear before, might arrive all at once, with nowhere gentle to put it.

This was the one true soft place in an otherwise sound and self-sufficient world. And so this, only this, is what the power did.

## What Was Done

It did not fix the engine. It went, instead, into the stone — into a fracture so fine it would mean nothing to any eye, a hairline seam already weeping its slow, patient thread of water on the far side of the widest chamber, well clear of the pumps, well clear of the densest clutches. And there it made one change, small enough to be indistinguishable from something geology might have done on its own, given a few more centuries it didn't especially have to give: it widened the angle of the seam, by a degree that means nothing to anyone in any single year, so that the water finding its new path will begin, gradually, to fall against a particular shelf of harder rock below it — not yet, not for a long while, but eventually, inevitably, the way everything here is *eventually* and *inevitably* — in a percussive, periodic way. A drip becoming, slowly, a beat. A beat that will, by the time it matures into something a hide could set a body's seasons by, fall into very nearly the same cadence as the dying pistons it will one day replace.

It did one more thing, smaller still: in the warmest clutch near the southern housing, the one laid closest to the failing valve the Tenant's own bulk keeps unknowingly serviced, it eased — by a fraction, by less than a fraction — the rate at which that particular pump's seals were wearing through. Not stopped. Not reversed. Only slowed, by exactly enough years that the new water-rhythm in the far chamber will have had time to grow into a real, dependable beat before the old one goes silent. A handoff. Not a rescue.

That was all. No voice spoke into the dark. No light bloomed where light has no business blooming. The Tenant did not wake, did not know, did not feel anything but the same arrhythmic, deafening, dripping fullness it has always felt, indistinguishable from any other moment in its long becoming.

## After

The power did not stay to watch it work, because watching it work would take longer than most worlds last, and because the gallery does not need a witness, only time, which it already has in greater abundance than almost anything the power has ever met.

It left the catwalk rusting. It left the door stuck in its shifted frame. It left the small things scuttling over each other in the shallow water, the soft pale mats clinging to the ceiling, the eggs glistening in their warm clutches, undisturbed and unimproved and exactly as crowded as they were.

It left, in short, almost everything exactly as it found it — which, for a power capable of much more, was the hardest and most deliberate kindness available: to love a thing enough to leave it weeping, dripping, deafening, crowded, unfinished, and whole.
