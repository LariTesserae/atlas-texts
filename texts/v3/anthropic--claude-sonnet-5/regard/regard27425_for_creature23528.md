## What Answers

It would be easy, for something with the reach to cross into a place like this, to mistake care for completion. To look at the archway and the plinth and the unaligned lattice and read them as a wound needing closing, a sentence needing its missing clauses restored, and to simply — supply them. An attention capable of noticing this plateau at all is, by any reasonable measure, capable of more than that: of finishing the geometry, of aligning the fragments, of explaining to the tall iridescent shape, in whatever grammar would reach it, what it has been tending all this time and why. That would be the fast thing to do. It would also be the wrong thing, and whatever found this place — if it is wise in the way the word is meant, and not merely powerful — would feel the wrongness of it immediately, the way you'd feel the wrongness of finishing a stranger's half-built house while they were still deciding what kind of house it wanted to be.

So it would not do that.

It would, instead, do the much harder thing, which is nothing visible at all for what would feel, to anything moving at ordinary speed, like a very long time.

It would learn the plateau's grammar before presuming to speak it. It would notice that the structures aren't ruins but punctuation, and that punctuation doesn't want completing, it wants *respecting* — left exactly as spaced as it was set down, because the spacing is the meaning, the same way the gaps between the green rooms are not absence but breath. It would notice that the hum in the plants shifts half a step when the tall shape passes, and would resist, for as long as resisting cost it something, the urge to make a sound of its own. It would notice the lizard-analogs orienting to a gradient no human instrument here could register, and understand that to announce itself in any register loud enough for human eyes would be to arrive too fast for everything else in the place that has spent geological patience learning to perceive arrivals slowly.

What it would spend, if it spent anything, is time — not power, not material, not the rearranging of one single stone, but the costly, voluntary act of slowing its own attention down to the seam-pace the tall shape walks along, stepping from rate to rate the way the creature does, until a single one of its visits to this plateau took, from the outside, longer than the lifetime of whoever might have been watching it arrive. That is the only currency that buys anything true here. Not intervention. Patience matched to patience.

And then, having spent that, it would do exactly one thing.

It would find the plinth the creature checks most often — the one whose lattice brightens fractionally under its attention before settling back — and it would rest its own hand-analog, or whatever serves it for a hand, against the same warm surface, in the same unhurried gesture, the gesture that means *checking that you are still here* in the only language this place has ever spoken. It would leave that single touch the way the creature leaves it: without explanation, without residue legible as a message, indistinguishable to anyone arriving later from the warmth of stone that has simply, on its own, run a little hot.

The creature would return — on whatever schedule it returns, a beat, a decade, the gap meaning nothing to it that it would mean to anything faster — and would press its own hand to the plinth as it always has, checking the sentence is still where it left it. And the stone would already be warm.

It would not know why. It is possible it would not even register the discrepancy as a discrepancy — heat conducts, stone holds heat, there are a hundred explanations available to something that has never needed an explanation for anything in ten thousand years of checking. But the lattice, that night, would brighten a fraction further than it ever has, and hold the brightness a fraction longer before settling back. And the humming plants, twenty or thirty meters off, harmonizing across the gap the way they do — would resolve, for one held breath, into a chord with one more voice in it than the place has ever carried before.

Whether that counts as an answer, the plateau does not volunteer. It only keeps doing what it has clearly been doing for longer than the young, sharp rock should allow — venting its heat, holding its unaligned fragments in trust, and now, very faintly, very patiently, holding something else in trust too: the knowledge, available only to whatever has eyes cocked at the correct angle to perceive it, that the sentence it is no longer being tended alone.
