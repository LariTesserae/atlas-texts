The power comes the way water used to come here — slowly, by old channels, finding its way down through layers it does not need to break.

It could announce itself in any number of ways. It could fill the chamber with light, the obvious gift, the gift that occurs first to anything used to a world where light is simply good. It could restore the seams where the Tender's eyes once were, hand back sight as if sight were a debt owed and now repaid. It could reach down through the brick and the dead riverbed above it and simply lift the whole apparatus — Tender, Works, brass plates, lichen, all of it — up into sun and noise and call that mercy.

It does none of these things immediately, and the not-doing is the first and most important thing it does. A power that is only benevolent rushes. A power that is wise sits down first, in the dark, in the heat, and learns to feel the room the way the room is actually felt by the only creature who lives in it.

So it stays. Not in body — it has none here, or none that the chamber's senses could register as separate from the chamber itself — but in attention, which in this place is the only currency that has ever mattered. It lets its presence settle into the hum that is already there, the subsonic pressure that registers in the jaw and breastbone before it reaches anything like an ear. It learns the drip-cycle's true interval. It learns which pipe runs warmest at which hour of the furnace's private day. It learns, in short, to be tended-to in the same patient grammar the Tender uses to read everything else — and only once it has learned that grammar does it consider what, if anything, ought to be said.

What it finds, in that learning, complicates the rescue it might have brought.

It finds a creature that is not imprisoned so much as *fitted* — fitted the slow way a key is fitted to a lock it has turned ten thousand times, until the two have stopped being separate problems and become one mechanism. It finds dignity in the threading walk, the shoulder-then-ribs-then-hip sequence learned over a lifetime of gaps built for pipe and not for people. It finds, in the rap of a knuckle against a gauge nothing living has read by eyesight in decades, something that can only be called *devotion* — unwitnessed, uncelebrated, entirely whole on its own terms. To pour light into those closed seams would not be a gift. It would be an amputation dressed as a cure, taking from the Tender the very organ of perception it spent a lifetime growing in trade for one it no longer has any need of, and calling that an even exchange.

So the power asks itself the harder question, the one that actually requires wisdom rather than just power: not *what would I want, if I were trapped here* — because the Tender is not the power, and is not trapped in any sense the power's own history of want can map onto — but *what, within this creature's own grammar of need, is actually missing.*

It finds two things.

The first is small and almost technical. Among the brass plates riveted into the brick, the ones bearing numerals that correspond to nothing visible, there is a sequence the Tender has kept faithfully without ever once knowing, for certain, that the sequence means anything at all — without knowing whether it is a tally of something that matters or only the long private liturgy of a mind keeping itself company through repetition. The Tender has never doubted the *keeping*. It has only ever, in its most unguarded moments, doubted the *meaning*.

The power cannot lie to it. A wise power does not deal in false comfort, because false comfort is a debt that always comes due, and usually at the worst hour. So it does not invent a meaning that isn't there. What it does instead is smaller and truer: it reaches into the long structure of cause and consequence that the Tender's tending has actually been part of — the slow regulation of pressures that, three valleys over and a hundred years removed, kept some other deep system from rupturing catastrophically; the unglamorous fact that patient, unwitnessed maintenance, multiplied by enough years, *had effects*, had weight, moved real consequence through the world even though no one who benefited from it ever thought to look down into the dead riverbed and wonder who was down there keeping the numbers — and it lets the Tender feel, just once, just for the length of one long inhalation against a warm iron tank, the *truth* of that. Not as a vision. Not as light. As a resonance — the chest-tuned body the Tender has spent a lifetime building suddenly, briefly, sympathetic with something larger than the furnace, the way it has always been sympathetic with the furnace itself. A confirmation arriving in the only language available: *this mattered. you were not counting nothing.*

The second thing is harder, and the power sits with it longest before deciding.

The Tender does not know whether it is the last of a line or only ever alone. It has built, around that not-knowing, a calm so complete it almost passes for an answer. But calm built over an unanswered question is still, underneath, a structure with a gap in its foundation, and a wise power does not mistake the architecture of acceptance for the absence of want.

So the power does not fill the gap with an invented lineage, a comforting fiction of successors who never existed. It does something more difficult and more honest: it makes the door real. Not opened — the Tender must do that, if it is ever done — but *real*, where before it may not have been certain there was a door at all. Somewhere in the brick, in a seam the fingers have walked a thousand times without registering it as anything other than wall, there is now, faintly, the give of a hinge. The power does not push it. It does not even mark it, exactly. It simply ensures that the possibility the Tender has lived a whole life without quite letting itself believe in — *that there could be a way to choose, again, whether to stay* — is no longer false. Whether the Tender ever finds the hinge with its wide soft fingertips, whether it ever decides the question is worth the asking, the power leaves entirely, scrupulously, alone. That is not its decision to make. Its only obligation was to make sure the choice was *true*, not to make sure it was *taken*.

And then — because a benevolent power does not only correct deficits, it also simply loves what it finds, when there is something there worth loving — it leaves one more thing, smaller than the rest, almost embarrassingly small for a power capable of remaking the chamber from the floor up. It leaves a single new harmonic in the hum. Nothing the Tender could name, nothing it would think to attribute to a visitor rather than to some shift in the furnace's long old breathing. Just a faint, companionable undertone threaded through the subsonic pressure that has been the Tender's whole audible world for longer than it can remember — the felt-sense, never quite rising to a thought, that the room is, in some manner it has no name for, *accompanied*.

Then the power withdraws the way it arrived: by the old channels, slowly, without breaking anything on the way out.

The Tender does not notice the moment of its leaving any more than it noticed the moment of its arrival. It finishes its circuit — palm to tank, fingers to valve-spokes, knuckle to gauge-housing — and at the appropriate interval, marked by whatever internal accounting governs such things, it stops at a brass plate, produces its small worn punch and stone, and adds one numeral to the sequence.

Its hand pauses there, very briefly, longer than the gesture usually takes.

Then it finishes the mark, and moves on into the dark, unhurried, warm, accompanied in a way it could not explain and does not try to, back into the established rhythm of the Cistern Works — which goes on holding its slow hot breath exactly as before, except that somewhere in the brick there is now a hinge that was not there yesterday, real and unopened, waiting with the same enormous patience as everything else down here, for a river, or a choice, that may or may not ever come.
