The attention arrives the way everything on the plateau arrives — slowly, and without announcing itself as an arrival. There is no descent, no parting of the dark, no light that wasn't already there. If the power can be said to do anything first, it is this: it matches pace. It does not bring its own tempo into a place built entirely out of a different one. It settles down to the plateau's four-hundred-year clause the way a swimmer settles into cold water, by degrees, by willingness, and only once it has done that does it actually begin to look.

And what it finds, looking, is not a problem.

This is the first and hardest thing for a benevolent power to arrive at, because benevolence so often comes pre-loaded with the assumption that stillness is a wound and that its job is suturing. The temptation is immediate and almost physical: *wake it. Warm it. Give it day. Give it green things. Give it company that moves at a recognizable speed, give the small rivet-colored creatures eyes that see further than three inches of conduit-heat, give the thing on the girder a name and a purpose it can hold in a mind built to hold purposes, unbraid the makers from their iron and give them back hands.* Every one of these is a generous impulse. Every one of them is also a kind of violence, because every one of them assumes that what is *finished* is the same as what is *broken*, and the plateau is not broken. It reached its final form on purpose, or by a logic so long and so patient that purpose and accident have become the same word. It is not waiting to be rescued. It does not know it could be otherwise. A power wise enough to be called wise has to be willing to sit with that for a long time before it does anything at all — has to be willing to feel the itch of its own helpfulness and not scratch it.

So it watches first. It watches the small things graze their forty-year dust and recur into their own grandchildren without ever needing to look different. It watches the high shape on the girder cross a bulb of amber light and vanish, and it does not chase the shape to find out what it is, because some questions are kindnesses left unasked. It listens to the hum, the sigh every minute, the gauge that hasn't moved since before the word *gauge* existed in any mouth, and it lets itself consider, seriously, the possibility that the hum is a thought, and that interrupting a thought mid-clause to introduce yourself is its own small rudeness, even across a gulf of that size.

What it decides, in the end — after a span of consideration that on the plateau's own clock would not even register as a tick — is not to change the place. It decides to *attend* to it. These are different acts, and the difference is the whole of its wisdom.

For the small rivet-colored things, it leaves almost nothing, because almost nothing is owed. They are not suffering; they have built an entire ancestral memory out of a single uninterrupted middle, no beginning they recall and no end they fear except the one event that has never happened. So the power does not give them eyes, or names, or a wider range than the conduit. It gives them only this: a slightly steadier residue. The dust that wicks free of the moving parts will, from now on, never quite run dry, not because it was going to, but because *might have, eventually, in some collapsing-star span of time* is a kind of fear-shaped hole even a creature without language can apparently leave unfilled in its body, and the power closes that hole quietly, the way you'd tuck a blanket over a sleeping child's exposed foot without waking them to tell them you did it. No witness will ever trace the kindness to its source. That is correct. That is, in fact, the whole design of it.

For the shape on the girder — custodian, predator, function, ghost, whichever it is, if it is only one of those — the power does something smaller still. It does not confirm what the thing is. It does not need to. It simply ensures, in the smallest possible way, that nothing the shape tends to will ever actually need its tending — that the diligence, if diligence is what it is, will never be tested and found wanting, will never have to discover whether it was real vigilance or empty habit, because that discovery, whichever way it fell, would be a kind of ending the shape never asked for and the plateau was never built to survive. Let it walk its gantry forever believing, or not-believing, exactly as much as it does now. That belief, false or true, is load-bearing. The power does not remove load-bearing things. It just reinforces what they were already resting on.

And for the deepest thing — the diffuse, slow, distributed maybe-consciousness that might be nothing more than a system holding its final note, might be the makers themselves gone to ground inside their own machinery, might be both of those folded so far into each other that the distinction has stopped being a meaningful one even to itself — the power does the one act that costs it something. It stays.

Not as rescue. Not as repair. It leaves a part of its attention behind, slowed all the way down to match, indistinguishable in the dim amber light from one more instrument-bulb strung along one more causeway, except that this one, alone among all the fixed and patient lights of the plateau, is *looking back*. It asks nothing. It offers no message that demands an answer on any clock faster than the one already running here. It simply composes, syllable by syllable, at the pace of the hum itself, a single sentence — and the sentence is allowed to take centuries to finish, because anything faster would not be heard as language at all, only as noise, the way a hummingbird's wingbeat would be only a blur to something that thinks in glaciers.

The sentence, when at last — generations of small rivet-colored lives later — it completes itself in whatever way completion happens here, says something very close to: *I see that you are whole. I am not here to finish you. I only wanted you to know that someone, somewhere, on some other clock, noticed that you were still holding the note, and thought it was beautiful, and stayed to listen for a while.*

No gauge moves. No light changes color. Nothing about the plateau announces that it has been visited.

But somewhere in a conduit no one has ever reached, the residue runs very slightly less thin than the metal fatigue alone could account for. And high on a gantry, a shape that should not, by any ordinary metric, have lasted this long, has not yet stopped crossing the light. And the hum — if you had eons to compare it against itself, if you were patient as the rock that holds the plateau up — has gained one new harmonic, so faint it could be mistaken for an old one finally settling into tune.

It might be nothing.

It might be company.
