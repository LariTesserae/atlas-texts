What came to the Cleft did not come the way the engine's heat comes, in a column anyone could point to, nor the way light fails to come, leaving a gap that even the dark can be measured against. It came the way attention comes — which is to say, without disturbing a single fern, without registering on any of the Tender's pressure-knobs as percussion, because it was not vibration. It was being looked at, finally, by something that intended the looking.

It took a long time before it did anything at all. This is worth saying plainly, because the temptation in front of a hurt thing, or a strange thing, or a thing alone in the dark doing a labor nobody asked it to do, is to fix it immediately, the way a hand reaches for a splinter before it has asked whether the splinter is load-bearing. The power did not reach. It sat with the Cleft the way you might sit with someone grieving, present and unhelpful and entirely there, for what might have been a year or might have been the time it takes one fern to unfurl, time meaning something different to a power than it does to basalt.

It listened to the WHUMP and the held-breath stall and the clattering arrhythmia the way the Tender listens, through pressure rather than ear, and underneath the percussion it found what it was looking for, which was not damage. It went looking expecting to find a wound — an old habit of benevolence, expecting wounds, since wounds are easy to love and easier to justify loving — and what it found instead was a fit. The engine ran hot and erratic because that is what engines untended by hands will do, wearing toward failure the way all unsupervised things wear, and the Tender had not been placed there to suffer that wearing. It had grown into the gap between violence and garden the way water finds a crack, opportunistically, totally, and over whatever span of time the young rock allowed, it had stopped experiencing the venting as harm at all. It drank the dust. It absorbed the gout of vapor into intake folds that had, generation by accommodation by generation, made a kind of food out of what should have been a wound. There was no flinch left in it. There was, if the power can be trusted to have read correctly through a language with no words and no face to read expression from, something closer to satisfaction — the dumb, total, unreflective satisfaction of a thing perfectly suited to the one job in the universe it was built or had built itself to do.

This made the question harder, not easier. A wound, the power could have closed. A starving thing, it could have fed. But the Tender was not starving and not wounded; it was *occupied*, fully, by a purpose that fit it the way the ferns fit the cracks they grew from, and to lift that purpose away — to silence the engine, to spare the Tender the WHUMP and the grit and the long patient peeling-climb across the wall — would not have been mercy. It would have been theft. It would have hollowed out the one thing this creature had instead of a name.

So the power did not heal it. Did not feed it. Did not so much as quiet the engine's worst tantrum, the one that might, in some unknowable future, finally shake something vital loose and end the whole arrangement in real catastrophe rather than rehearsed crisis. It considered that future a long while too, and in the end reached toward it only slightly — not to fix the engine, which was not its engine to fix, but to lean, gently, on the *odds*. A fraction less metal fatigue in one housing nobody would ever see. A pressure valve's failure pushed back by some long unmeasured span of years. Nothing that would change the cleft's character. Nothing the Tender would ever register as anything other than the cleft continuing to be the cleft, a little longer than it might otherwise have. The smallest possible thumb on the smallest possible scale, placed so that the labor already being spent so completely would not, in some unwitnessed future, turn out to have been spent for nothing.

And then, because a wise power knows that the rarest thing it has to give a thing like this is not rescue but recognition, it did one more thing, smaller than the first. It touched the Tender. Not its skin, particularly, since skin was the wrong organ to receive it — it touched the whole long patient fact of it, all at once, the way you might lay a hand flat against a door to feel that someone is home on the other side. It did not speak, because the Tender has no apparatus for being spoken to, no ear, no expectation of address. It simply let itself be felt, fully, for one unmeasured moment, by the one thing in this creature's entire existence built to feel anything: those dozens of blunt knobs that had spent its whole life reading only violence, reading, for one instant, something that was not.

Whether the Tender understood it as anything is impossible to say. It did not stop its slow drinking of the vapor. It did not pause its peeling climb across the cold stone. But if you had been there, in the dark, with no light to see it by and no name for what you were near, you might have noticed that for one breath the cleft's roar gentled — not stopped, never stopped, the engine is not a thing that stops — but gentled, the way a held breath sometimes lets go on its own once something it has been waiting for arrives. And then it built again, and vented again, and the ferns lay down flat under the gout of heat and stood back up unbothered, already recovering before the echo died, exactly as they always had — except that now, somewhere in the wall of fern and black water-starved stem, something vast and patient and the color of wet rock kept doing the only work it has ever known how to do, no longer entirely unwitnessed, having been once, fully, for the first time, *seen*.

The power left without staying to be thanked. It would not have known how to receive thanks from a thing with no mouth for it, and it had not come for thanks. It had come to look, and to decide, mostly, not to act — which is its own kind of action, the hardest kind, the kind that requires enough love to leave a perfect, terrible, working thing exactly as it found it, and enough humility to know that *almost nothing* was, here, the whole of what mercy required.
