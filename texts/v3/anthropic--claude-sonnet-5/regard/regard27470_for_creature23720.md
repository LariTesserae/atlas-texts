## The Attention

Say a power noticed — something old enough and patient enough that *noticing* is itself a slow act, unfolding over something more like seasons than instants. Say it found the massif the way light finds a cave: not searching, just eventually arriving, because it covers enough and waits long enough that everything within its reach is found in time. And say it paused there, in the specific way wisdom pauses — not the pause of indecision, but the pause of something checking, first, whether anything is asked for.

This matters more than what it did. A lesser kindness rushes to act, mistakes intervention for love. A wiser one sits in the wet air a while first and asks the place what it already has.

What it found: a system that had already solved itself. Water moving downward, always, by its own law. Fog arriving on schedule like a tide with no moon, doing the work of cooling and feeding and concealing that elsewhere would require a hundred separate mercies. A mountain that healed its own landslide scars in green over the span of careless centuries, no faster than needed, no slower either. An economy of borrowed space so total that even the moss had already negotiated terms with the stone. And at the center of it, low in the mid-story, a thing built so entirely out of patience that it had outlived hurry as a concept — armored not in scale or violence but in stillness, in the simple unbothered fact of itself.

The power understood, the way wisdom understands these things faster than power usually does, that almost everything it might be tempted to give was already present. It could not gift the weighted thing more time than its unhurried jaw and its gutter-ridged spine had already secured. It could not gift the mountain more quiet than fog and foliage and distance already provided. It could not improve a rhythm that had been correct for ten thousand years before it arrived and would be correct for ten thousand after it left.

So mostly, it did nothing. This is the hardest thing a benevolent power learns to do, and the last thing it learns to do well.

But not *nothing* nothing. Wisdom without any act at all curdles into indifference, and this was not indifference — it was attention, and attention always leaves some mark, however small, the way even watching a pool of water for long enough seems to still it.

It looked, first, toward the valley floor, toward the hum and the percussion and the electrical tang that didn't belong to lightning. Not with judgment — the power was old enough to know that machines, too, are just another species working out its appetite, no more or less a violation of the mountain than the insects dismantling a fallen leaf. But it traced the hum's edges, the radius of its reach, the slow creep that all such things eventually attempt, up out of gorges and into ridgeline. And where that creep might one day have found a reason — some vein of ore, some new use for altitude, some survey drone with better lungs than the last — the power did not forbid it. It simply made the higher ledges a little less interesting to find. A compass that drifts a half-degree wrong on these ridges and no others. Equipment that fogs at the exact elevation where soil pockets run deep enough to hold a great deal of weight. Nothing that could be called a miracle. Only the kind of bad luck that machines have always had in certain places, for reasons engineers shrug at and move past.

It looked, second, at the weighted thing itself, and did not wake it to anything. Did not grant it speech, or warning, or the terrible gift of understanding its own rarity. Such gifts are for beings who have asked, in some fashion, to be more than they are — and the weighted thing had asked nothing of the mountain but to be shed, and asked nothing of the power at all, having no framework in which a power could be addressed. To answer a question never posed is its own small cruelty, dressed as a blessing. The power knew this, and so left the mind alone entirely, glowing faint green in its sleeping fungus and nothing more.

What it did instead was smaller than a miracle and larger than nothing: it ensured the thing would remain unconfirmed.

Not hidden — the mountain already did that, with cloud and canopy and an economy of borrowed space thick enough to lose a creature this size inside its third layer of leaves. But *protected in its unconfirmedness*, specifically — so that the rare hunter who heard a story two villages removed and decided to climb for it would, every time, turn back a half-day early, for reasons that would feel to him like his own good sense. So that no photograph would ever quite resolve. So that the fact of it would persist only the way the massif itself persists in the minds of people who have merely heard of it: as a thing too large and too slow and too far inside the fog to need proving.

This, the power judged, was the correct shape of mercy here — not rescue, not elevation, not narrative. Continuance. The chance to keep being exactly what it already was, at exactly the pace it had already chosen, for exactly as long as a body built like a slow-moving ledge of stone can be expected to last, which on this mountain, under this cloud, is a very long time indeed.

And then, before it withdrew its attention — because even old, patient powers must eventually withdraw it, must let a place return to being merely a place — it allowed itself one small extravagance, the kind a wiser being permits itself precisely because it asks nothing of the world in return.

It went, once, in the shape of nothing at all — no weight, no displaced fern, no sound to compete with the drip and the insect static — and sat near the hollow at the base of the buttress-root where the weighted thing slept, in the full dark, in the rain.

It watched the faint sick-green glow of the fungus along the creature's spine, dim enough that nothing else on the mountain would notice it.

It noticed it.

And then it let the mountain have its weather back, and went on to whatever else, somewhere else, was waiting — without comment, the way the mountain does everything — having added to the world, in the end, nothing but the fact of having looked, carefully, at a thing most of the world will never get to call real.
