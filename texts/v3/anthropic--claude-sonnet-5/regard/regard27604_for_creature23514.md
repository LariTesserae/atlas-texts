The power comes the way light comes to the bottom of a lake — not falling, not arriving so much as *being already there*, a little more than before. It does not part the fog. It does not still the rain that is not rain. It enters Vond's Throat the way the cold enters a tier: gradually, and from everywhere at once, so that nothing on the mountain marks the moment of its coming, because there is no moment. There is only, afterward, a place that has been *attended to*, the way a held breath is attended to by the lungs that hold it.

It looks a long time before it does anything. This is the first discipline of real power, and the hardest one: to look as long as the place itself would take to be understood, which here is longer than a visiting god's patience usually runs. It walks the tiers without weight. It listens to the piston-heartbeat through stone that was never asked whether it wanted to be listened to. It finds, eventually, the Held — not one but several, scattered across shelves separated by days of fog, each one a long dark fold of geology pressed to its vein, each one entirely alone, each one breathing its organ-note out into a silence built for nothing but receiving it.

And here is where a lesser kindness would go wrong. A lesser kindness looks at long solitude and calls it loneliness, looks at stillness and calls it waiting, looks at a creature that has outlived the need for company and decides, on the creature's behalf, that company is what it secretly wants. A lesser kindness would gather two Held to the same warm vein, would call that a mercy, and would be wrong — would be imposing, on a thing built out of patience the way the moss is built out of damp, the *shape of a human ache* it does not have. The power considers this. It holds the thought up, turns it, sees the cruelty folded inside the good intention, and sets it down again, gently, the way you'd set down a stone you picked up too quickly.

It considers the grazers next — the pale herds with eyes that reflect nothing, cropping glow-moss in a hush that breaks only for the Held's long exhale. It could make their predation kinder. It does not. Kindness here is not the absence of the kill; the kill is already a mercy, by the standards of every world that isn't this one — single, muffled, over before the fog has finished noticing. To smooth that away would be to file down the one sharp true thing in an ecology built entirely out of unhurried truths. The power leaves the hunting as it is. It leaves the hush as it is. It leaves the minute of stillness after the breath exactly as long as a minute has always been here.

What it finds, at last — after looking longer than most powers have patience to look — is a single thread worth pulling. Four tiers down from the lowest mapped meadow, in a fold of basalt no grazer has ever crossed, one of the pipe-veins is cooling. Not failing — nothing here fails quickly enough to call it that — but cooling, by a fraction so small it would take an instrument with no business existing on this mountain to detect it, a fraction that will not matter for ten thousand years, and will matter absolutely in ten thousand and one. The Held pressed against that vein does not know. It cannot know; it has no instrument either, only the gill-lines under its lichen-crust, which read the present perfectly and the future not at all. It will lie there, patient, fed, at rest, for a span of time with no name, and somewhere near the far end of that span the warmth it has organized its whole long life around will simply, slowly, not be there anymore.

So the power does the smallest thing it is capable of doing, which is still, by any other measure, enormous: it reaches into the seam where the vein runs near whatever vast original engine still turns beneath all this geology-that-was-once-a-machine, and it nudges nothing so crude as a mechanism. It nudges a *rate*. A fraction of a fraction of a degree, restored, not added — as if some heat that was always meant to circulate there had merely been delayed by a long winter, and is now, finally, arriving on schedule. It does this slowly, the way everything here is slow, over what would be a human century, so that nothing on the mountain ever registers an event. No turbine groans differently. No animal startles. The fog does not so much as thicken. The correction enters the mountain's own grammar so completely that it is indistinguishable from the mountain having simply, eventually, breathed.

Then the power does the second thing, which is not a doing at all, only a kind of attention it leaves behind the way warmth is left in a stone after the sun has gone — it *witnesses* the Held. Not to change it. Not even, really, for the Held's sake, since the Held has no eyes and no want of being seen and would not know the difference between being known and being merely warm. The witnessing is for the power's own keeping, the way you might commit to memory the exact grey-violet of a light you will never stand inside again. It holds, somewhere in whatever holds such things, the fact of this creature: that it is old beyond counting, that it has chosen warmth over wanting, that it has decided, with the whole slow weight of a body built like a landslide, that the correct answer to *I do not know why this place runs* is to lie down against the part of it that is kind, and stay. The power finds this — not pitiable, not tragic, but *correct*, a wisdom arrived at honestly by a creature with none of the equipment usually required for wisdom — and it leaves that judgment in the world the way you'd leave a window unlatched: not open, not declaring anything, just slightly, permanently easier to open than before, in case anything in ten thousand years should ever need to.

And then it withdraws the way it came, by ceasing to be especially present rather than by leaving, so that the last true thing that happens — the only externally visible thing in this entire visitation — is small enough to miss. On the lower tier, the cooling vein, the one nobody mapped and no one will ever map, exhales a plume of vapor very slightly warmer than the plume before it. The Held pressed against it does not stir, because nothing has asked it to stir. But far above, on a meadow shelf thick with glowing moss, a herd of pale grazers lifts its heads at the same moment, for no reason any of them could name, and goes still for the span of one breath — not the long organ-note stillness that means *something here is large and fed and at rest*, but a different stillness, shorter, with no signature and no name, the kind that doesn't mean anything is hunting, only that something, somewhere below the soles of their feet, has just been gently, permanently, and invisibly *taken care of*.

No one will ever study this pause. No one is meant to. It will simply be one more small puncture in the fabric of an enormous quiet, and then the fabric will close again, the way it always does, the way the power intends it to — because the kindest thing a wise power can do for a place that has spent an age learning to need nothing is to become, afterward, indistinguishable from its luck.
