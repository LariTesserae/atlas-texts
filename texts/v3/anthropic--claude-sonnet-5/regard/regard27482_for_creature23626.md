The power comes to it the way light comes to the hollow itself — filtered, indirect, having to want to be there, because nothing about the Greenworks announces itself upward. It is not a place that calls for rescue. It does not look, from outside or above or wherever such powers stand to look, like a wound at all anymore. It looks like a thing that finished healing a long time ago into a shape nobody planned and everybody now depends on, the way a tree grows around a fence post until you can no longer say where the iron ends and the living wood begins, and the fence post would tell you, if fence posts could talk, that it doesn't particularly mind.

So the first thing the power does is the hardest thing such powers ever do, which is nothing, for a long time, on purpose, while it learns to see the place the way the place sees itself.

It feels, at first, the ordinary temptations of compassion looking down at hardship from a height hardship doesn't have. It could open the grates wide and let real sun pour down gold and merciless until the green things that don't need it shriveled and the people blinked their way up into streets that forgot them. It could still every engine's faltering, true every gear-train, seal every failing pipe so cleanly that no Tender ever again spent an afternoon wrist-deep in cold water with a wrench, swearing softly, patient, half-loving the broken thing they were fixing. It could send a messenger, blazing, to tell them: *there is more than this, you were not forgotten, come up, come up.* It could simply lift them — all of them, gently, complete with boiler and bamboo-lattice and moss-grown valve-wheel, like scooping up a terrarium — and set the whole hollow down somewhere with a horizon.

It examines each of these the way the Tenders examine a vine before they decide whether to prune it or let it run, and it finds, in each, the same flaw: they mistake the labor for the wound. They are not the wound. The labor is the marriage, the long peace, the theology already arrived at without anyone above ever needing to deliver it: *attention is owed, and the hollow collects on the debt either way, so you may as well pay it gladly.* That sentence, when the power finds it sitting unspoken in the muscle-memory of how a ten-year-old tests a vine's grip in the dark, is so complete a piece of wisdom, so exactly the shape of what the power itself has spent ages and ages learning to practice, that it feels for a moment almost shy in front of them, an elder caught out by students who got there a different way and arrived just as far.

To fix the Greenworks would be to tell the Tenders, wordlessly but absolutely, that their three or four generations of fixing it were not enough, were provisional, were waiting on someone realer to come finish the job. That is not a mercy. That is a correction dressed as one, and the power, being wise as well as benevolent, knows the difference matters more than almost anything else it has to offer.

What it offers instead is smaller, and it offers it the way the Tenders themselves would recognize as correct: through attention, paid quietly, costing the giver something, claiming no credit, fixing nothing that the place was already capable of fixing for itself.

So: in the swamp-room where the seal failed forty years back, where rot-smell and oil-smell mingle and nobody saw the use in repair since the plants didn't seem to mind — there is a crack in the old housing beneath the moss that has been deepening a grain at a time for longer than anyone tending it now has been alive, and it was three winters from going all at once, taking the floor and the gas and likely two sleeping Tenders with it in the night. The power does not weld it shut. It only — slows it. Lets it deepen at the rate it would have deepened if the iron had been a little better cast a hundred years ago, a little less tired, a little luckier in its metallurgy. A grain a year instead of three grains. Enough that the next inspection, ordinary, unremarkable, scheduled on the unwritten roster by a woman named for her grandmother, finds the crack exactly visible enough to be alarming and exactly slow enough to still be fixable by hand, by the people whose hands such things belong to. They will fix it themselves. They will be tired and pleased with themselves after, the particular pleasure of a near thing caught in time through one's own competence, and they will be right to feel that, because they did do it. The power only made sure the doing was still possible when the moment came. That is the whole of the gift: not rescue, but *enough time to rescue themselves*, which is a different thing entirely and the only kind they would have wanted if you could have asked them first.

It does something similar, lighter, almost playful, with the singing. The work-songs that carry through the muffling green so a boiler-tender three chambers off knows by the shape of a melody that the singer is fine, is working, is exactly where she's supposed to be — these already travel further than they ought to, up through grates that were never meant for sound any more than for sun. The power, moved by this more than by almost anything else it finds down there, because it recognizes in it the exact gesture it itself is making — *filling an absence on purpose, so the missing thing is not missed in quite the same unbearable way* — leans in, just slightly, the way you'd cup a hand behind an ear, and lets one particular song, on one particular warm wet night, carry a little further than the physics of the hollow strictly allows. It rises through a shaft six basements deep and out a grate on a street where a man is sitting on a curb at three in the morning with a kind of grief in him that has no edges, nothing to push against, the unshaped kind that just sits on the chest. He hears, very faint, very strange, a thread of melody coming up from the pavement like the city itself is humming under its breath. He never finds where it came from. He does not need to. He goes home able to sleep, and credits it, vaguely, gratefully, to nothing in particular — which is exactly the credit the power wants, because the gift was never meant to have a name attached, least of all its own.

And then it does the last thing, which is the largest and the quietest at once: it stays. Not as a presence anyone could point to, not as a god they'd build an altar for and thereby owe something performative instead of real — but the way warmth stays in a room after the source of it has gone elsewhere, low, ambient, structural. It pays the kind of attention the Tenders pay their vines and their gear-trains: noticing without needing to be noticed back, intervening only at the rare hinge-points where the whole shape of the thing would otherwise break rather than bend, and otherwise simply *watching them be excellent at the life they chose*, with something that in a being so old and so capable can only honestly be called pride, and underneath the pride, something even older — a kind of grateful relief, the feeling of finding, in some unlikely sunk hollow beneath a city that forgot it was there, a people who had already worked out, on their own, with no one's help, the exact same answer it had spent the whole of its long existence arriving at.

*Attention is owed.* They taught that to their children as a nursery rhyme. The power, hearing it, took it as scripture, and kept it, and asked nothing more of them than to keep singing.
