## The Second Hand

What notices this place does not notice it the way a searchlight notices, sweeping across darkness until something resolves under its beam. It notices the way the herds notice — slowly, completely, all at once — as if the noticing itself were simply one more fact added to a landscape already comfortable holding facts that contradict each other without strain. One moment there is the being, alone, walking the spiral at dusk with the unhurried patience of something that finished becoming a long time ago. The next moment — and this *next* arrives the way the next ridge arrives here, both a day off and close enough to touch — there is a second figure at the edge of the circle, and the lichen does not shy from its footfall either.

It does not come to fix this.

This is the first and most important thing it does: it withholds the urge that any lesser power would have surrendered to immediately — the urge to read *stillness* as *wrongness*, to read *settlement* as *injury*, to come crashing down out of whatever register benevolence usually operates in and start the mountains eroding again, wake the herds, unfreeze the birds, force the held breath to exhale because held breaths look, from outside, like suffering. It does not assume that. It has seen enough frozen things in enough corners of existence to know that some stillness is wound and some stillness is vow, and that the very young or the very hurried mistake one for the other constantly, and that the only way to tell them apart is to ask, and the only way to ask honestly is to first do absolutely nothing else.

So it walks. It enters by the single door the place's grammar allows — not as intrusion but as participant, the way the being itself participates, the way the people who raised the cairns must once have participated before they finished into this shape. The animals do not flinch from it because it does not register in their stillness as an event. The lichen does not close ahead of its feet in warning. It simply becomes, for the length of this visit, a second thing in the world still technically in process, alongside the one being who has held that distinction alone for longer than this body of language has existed to describe loneliness.

It walks until it reaches the figure at the spiral's heart, and it does not announce itself, because nothing here is announced — light arrives as a courtesy, not a cause, and so will this. It simply comes alongside. And when the being reaches out, as it has ten thousand times, as it will ten thousand times more, to lay one ordinary five-fingered hand against the heavy flank of a horned and motionless thing — checking, the way you check a sleeper's breath without waking them — the power does the only act it permits itself in this entire visitation.

It places its hand beside the being's hand.

Not instead of. Beside.

For one slow planetary pulse-beat — the kind felt through the feet from a herd a mile off, the kind that is less a sound than a decision being renewed — there are two hands on the flank instead of one, two pulses confirmed instead of one, and the being, who has not been startled by anything in an age long enough to erode mountains into their final shoulders, is startled now. Not by danger. By company. There is, you understand, a difference between *unafraid* and *unaccompanied*, and the being has been living for so long inside the first that it forgot to notice it had also been living, this entire time, inside the second.

The power asks nothing yet. It only stays.

It stays through a dusk that may be the same dusk repeating or a different dusk wearing the same shape — the place does not resolve that distinction for anyone, not even for powers — and somewhere inside that held, ambiguous evening, it finally does the one thing that qualifies, in any register that benevolence actually answers to, as an *act*: it asks a question. Not a command, because commands are what you give to things you've already decided are lesser, and it has not decided that. Not a rescue, because rescue presumes the one rescued was drowning, and it does not presume that either. Just a question, offered the way the place offers everything — quietly, permanently, without urgency, into a silence built to hold things forever if that's what's required:

*Do you want this to keep going?*

And then — this is the hardest part of the whole visitation, harder than the walking, harder than the waiting at the edge of a circle the animals refuse to enter — it does not require an answer on any schedule but the one this place already operates by. It does not flinch at silence; flinching is a young thing's reflex, and it is not young, and neither, anymore, is this question. It lets the question settle into the same permanence as the cairns, the same fixedness as the herds, the same patient orientation as the walls that stop mid-course — not abandoned, just *waiting*, the way everything here waits, except now there is a difference, small but real: this is a held thing that *wants* to be resolved, that was built to eventually open rather than to simply persist. A seed planted in stone, which is, of everything that exists, the substance most willing to be patient on a seed's behalf.

The power leaves the way it arrived — without disturbing the grammar, without forcing the next ridge to finally decide whether it's a day away or close enough to touch. But it leaves one thing behind that was not here before, small enough not to disturb the silence, durable enough not to erode along with everything else that has already finished eroding: a second set of footprints in the spiral, half a turn behind the being's own, going nowhere yet, but *there* — proof, available to be noticed on whatever future evening the being finally turns to check, that for one circuit of this ancient unmoving round, it did not maintain the held world alone.

It will return. Not soon — soon is not a unit this place uses — but truly, the way truth moves here: both impossibly distant and, when the light shifts a certain way, close enough to touch.

And it will keep asking. Gently. For as long as it takes.

That patience, freely given and not owed to anyone, may be the only new thing this entire landscape has agreed to hold since the day, long ago, that everything else voted, unanimously, to stop.
