The power came the way such powers do, which is to say without arriving — there was no displacement of air, no shadow crossing the haze, no flock startling upward in alarm. It was simply, at some point, attending. The way you might suddenly notice you've been hearing a sound for an hour.

It attended first to the scale of the place — that gap between what was built and what there was room to build, the half-kilometers of lush open nothing strung between towers that conferred with each other in slow light. It found this beautiful in the specific way that unfinished things are beautiful, the way a held breath is more interesting than the exhale. And then it attended to the beings, and that recalibrated everything, because beauty held at a distance is one kind of thing and beauty that includes someone whispering to a child in a rock-smelling room because outside has stolen the right to be heard is another kind of thing entirely, and the power understood immediately that it would have to be careful here, because it was strong enough to ruin this without even intending to.

It considered, briefly, the obvious mercies.

It could have aged the rock — drawn the patient work of ten thousand years of weather into an afternoon, rounded every fracture-face, softened every edge so a hand could rest on an outcrop without the world biting back. It did not, because it understood, watching long enough, that the sharpness was not an oversight. It was a clock. The people read their own youth and the land's youth off the same edges, and a place that had finished settling would have stopped telling them something true about themselves.

It could have quieted the sound-stack — stilled the moss-ripple's wet hush, dampened the membrane-flocks' papery roar, lowered the tower-resonance below the register of the sternum. It did not, because a silence handed to people who had built an entire second language out of light and color and gesture in order to survive without one would not have been received as a gift. It would have been received as an amputation. Whatever they might have done with quiet, they had already done something extraordinary with its absence, and the power was wise enough to recognize that the absence was the raw material, not the wound.

It could have explained the towers. Walked the listeners through the lensing spire, the four-centimeter gap that held its distance like a law rather than a measurement, the slow inflating breath of the domes, and simply told them: here is what this is, here is why. It did not. It watched a listener stand near a dome at dusk with the particular stillness of someone who has decided that understanding a mood is a worthier inheritance than understanding a mechanism, and it recognized in that stillness a kind of discipline it did not want to interrupt. To explain the towers would have ended the listeners as a vocation. It would have turned three generations of earned trust into a footnote.

It could have smoothed the booms — taken the seasonal swing of the fauna's fast-breeding abundance and flattened it into something manageable, predictable, safe. It did not, because the towers were already doing this, in their own unexplained way, and a second hand on that work — even a gentler one — would have been a kind of theft from whoever currently bore the burden of it, machine or whatever stood behind the machine.

So it did not fix the place. It did not finish it. Finishing, it understood now, was not a kindness this particular becoming wanted done to it from outside.

But it did not leave empty-handed, because *benevolent* and *wise* are not the same as *passive*, and somewhere in the long watching it had found the one seam where help could enter without becoming interference.

There was an old woman, a listener by vocation though her tower had gone strange and silent on her years ago in a way nobody could account for, who carried in her hands a six-generation sequence of light-signal — a route, a *telling*, the only remaining full memory of a way between two settlements that the aquifers had long since rearranged underfoot. She had no one left to give it to. Her own line had scattered with a homestead-move two winters back, the way lines do here, and the child who should have stood across the dark from her, reading her lantern back at her until the sequence took root in new hands, did not exist yet, or existed somewhere else, learning a different family's different telling, the two of them converging on nothing.

The power did not write a new memory into anyone. It did not move the aquifers, or extend the woman's life, or manufacture a grandchild out of want. It did the smallest thing it could find that was still a thing: it let a flock's gyre tighten a quarter-hour later than its bronze-bodied logic would otherwise have dictated, over a dome a half-kilometer from where it would otherwise have tightened, which delayed a herding-party by exactly the span of an evening, which put a stranger's child — flagged pole in hand, irritated at the wasted hour — standing at dusk near a tower she'd never had cause to visit, watching an old woman's hands begin, out of long habit, to run through a sequence she no longer expected anyone to need.

The child watched because there was nothing else to do with the wasted hour. The old woman, noticing she was watched, did the only thing a listener knows how to do with attention: she gave it something to hold.

That was all. A flock turning a little late. A bored child standing a little still. Nothing in it that could not be explained, the next morning, as simply how things had happened to fall.

The power did not stay to see whether the telling survived the way the rest had survived — that was not its decision to guarantee. It withdrew the way it had come, without parting the haze, and what it left behind was not a miracle the Reach would ever name as such, not a sign, not a voice from a dome, nothing for the listeners to add to their store of unexplained moods.

It left only this: the certainty, held nowhere and by no one, that the becoming was being watched with something like love, and had been judged — correctly — not yet in need of finishing.
