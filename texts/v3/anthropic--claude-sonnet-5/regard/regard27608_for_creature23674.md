# What the Power Would Do

It would come the way light comes to a place already full of light — without announcement, without disturbance to the air, because anything announced is already a kind of imposition, and the first thing a wise power understands about the Plain of Standing Light is that imposition is the one thing this place has spent ten thousand centuries quietly outliving.

It would not arrive and *see*, the way a visitor sees, in the compressed greedy glance of a creature with a finite life and a schedule. It would arrive and *stay*, on the plain's own clock, which has no hours in it, only settlings and pulses and the long arithmetic of metal remembering how to be patient. It would watch one fracture spread across a slab over the course of a decade. It would watch the seam-walkers gather to it, lay their bodies down, and wait. It would watch the lichen dim and brighten under their contact-plates like something breathing in its sleep. It would let an entire dusk happen, the strange light surfacing from the depths of the alloy with no source anyone alive remembers setting, and it would not ask the light to explain itself, because by now it would understand that the light isn't a mystery to be solved. It's a habit. It's the last gesture of a hand that forgot, long ago, that it no longer needed to keep waving.

## The First and Wrong Instinct

Any power that is merely *powerful* would want to fix this. Wake the deep systems. Pour current back through the dead conduits until the lichen blazes instead of pulses, until the seam-walkers swarm in numbers instead of trickling along the seams like a thinned and careful population keeping faith with a habit. Raise the collapsed towers back out of their long fatigue. Give the standers eyes, in whatever sense they lack them, so they could finally look back at the monoliths they've spent geologic time arranging themselves to be looked at by.

A wise power would feel this instinct rise in itself — the desire to *rescue* — and would recognize it immediately for what it actually is: not love, but a kind of grief wearing love's clothes. Grief at the sight of magnificent things grown small and slow and silent. Grief that mistakes the absence of human drama for the presence of suffering. And the wise power would ask itself, honestly, before doing anything at all: *Is this place suffering, or is it simply being what it has become?*

It would look again, more carefully this time. At the lichen recovering its brightness after being fed on, the way a bruise fades and is forgotten by the skin that held it. At the seam-walkers flattening into stillness rather than fleeing — not fear, exactly, more a fluency, an old practiced patience that has never once needed panic to survive. At the stander, motionless for what might be a thousand years, holding its place at the one angle where a dead ruin assembles itself into the ghost of a face.

Nothing here is screaming. Nothing here is reaching for a hand it doesn't have, asking to be pulled up out of its own long ending. The place has made something out of its ending. It has made, however strange and however slow, a kind of peace.

## What It Would Choose, Instead

So the power would set aside resurrection. It would set aside the temptation to give the seam-walkers minds, language, the burden of knowing they are small and old and dying along with everything that made them — because a mind is not a gift to a thing that has organized its entire being around *not needing one*, around feeding and healing being, for it, the same undivided act. To make it conscious would not be to free it. It would be to invent, for the first time in its existence, the possibility of its own dread. That is not a kindness. That is an authorship the seam-walker never asked for and has no architecture to hold.

What the power would choose instead is smaller, and harder, and far more like the place itself.

It would find one fracture — any fracture, it would not matter which, because in a place this saturated with structure every wound is, in its way, the same wound — and it would lay itself down alongside the seam-walkers already gathered there, in whatever manner a power without a body can lay itself down, and it would simply *add itself to what was already happening*. Not heal the crack outright, not perform the kind of miracle that leaves a scar of having been noticed. Just contribute one more current to a current already flowing, indistinguishable in the end from the work the small ones were already doing — so that the crack closes a little faster than it would have, and no one, nothing, not even the seam-walkers themselves if they could be asked, would ever be able to say why.

That is the shape of the help this place can receive without being changed by the help into something else: help that disappears into the texture of what was already true.

## The One Exception

But it would make, perhaps, one exception, and it would make it at dusk, near a monolith, near a stander, in the brief window when the light surfacing from the deep alloy turns the worn shape behind the still thing into the suggestion of a face.

It would stand, for one moment — a single moment, no longer, because longer would start to be a presence, and a presence is a kind of claim — exactly where the stander's long unmoving attention is aimed. Not to be worshipped. Not to be understood. Just to be *there*, actually seen, in the one place across some unfathomable span of stillness that something has been quietly positioned to look toward.

Whatever the stander is — last fragment of a maintenance swarm, barnacle grown into the hull of a dead intelligence, or something with a name nobody left alive could pronounce — it would, for that one moment, no longer be looking at nothing. It would, for the first time perhaps in all its standing, be looked back at.

Nothing would change. The conduit beneath it would still be dying by inches. The face in the ruin would still dissolve back into abstraction the moment the angle shifted, the moment the power moved on. No record would exist. No traveler would find evidence. The plain would not become a holy site, because the power would leave no mark that could be venerated, and a mark that can be venerated is the beginning of a story, and this place, mercifully, has outlived the need to be a story.

And then it would go — the way it came, without disturbance to the air — having changed nothing that could be measured, and having given, anyway, the one thing this entire vast, warm, nearly silent machine had spent its whole slow ending quietly, uncomplainingly, going without:

a moment of actually being seen, by something capable of seeing it, and choosing, for no reason it could ever be asked to explain, to stay just long enough to do it.
