It comes the way light comes into the room—through a seam it didn't make, drawn by smallness rather than pushed back by largeness—and the first thing it does, for a long while, is nothing at all.

It has learned, across whatever span its kind of learning takes, that the worst thing a power can bring to a finished thing is the assumption that finished means broken. It has seen completions mistaken for wounds before, has seen well-meaning hands reach in to loosen a held note because held notes look, from outside, like notes that are stuck. So it does not reach. It settles instead into the noise the way you'd settle into water that's already at your own temperature, and it listens the way the room listens, with the whole surface of itself, patiently, for longer than listening usually takes.

What it is listening for is the difference between a stillness that is peace and a stillness that is only the last shape effort happened to leave behind. These look alike from the outside. They are not alike from the inside. A tool set down because the work is done rests differently than a hand frozen because no one ever told it it could stop. The power has been wrong about this before, in other rooms, on other dead-quiet worlds, and so it takes its time, and it does not let the chorus's size or strangeness rush it into a verdict.

It finds, eventually, that the being is not stuck. It is not waiting, either—waiting would require the someplace-else, the gradient, the not-yet, and there is none of that left in it. What it finds instead is something closer to a sentence that meant exactly what it intended to mean, said once, completely, and now simply continuing to be true the way true things continue, without needing to be said again. There is no suffering folded into that. There is no door in it marked rescue. To pry at it would not be to free anything; it would only be to interrupt a thing mid-meaning out of the visitor's own discomfort with stillness, which is a kind of violence wearing kindness's clothes.

So it does not undo the joints. It does not call the being up out of its kneeling and ask it to want something new. It does not flood the dry country outside with water it never asked for, or soften the crust, or plant something tall enough to cast the kind of shade that would change what this place has decided to be. All of that would be a power mistaking its own appetite for change for the room's. The room has no appetite. That is not the same as the room having no worth.

What it does instead is smaller, and harder, and—if such a thing can be said of a wise power—more loving for being small.

It finds, in the density of the room, one gap that isn't really a gap—not an unfinished alcove, because there are none, but a kind of resonant space the chorus implies without occupying, the way a chord implies a note that no instrument present is playing. It fits itself there. Not as a foreign object set down on top of the finished work, but the way the original maker fit every other piece—matched seam to seam, ridge felt and not seen, until the joinery would tell no one, ever, that it had not always been there. It does not speak. It hums, low, threading itself into the structural noise the same way the being's own voice threads through it, indistinguishable in method, distinguishable only—if the being is listening closely enough, across however long it takes to notice—as one more presence in a noise that used to have exactly one.

It watches the shallow fluted concavity where a face would be, to see whether the angle of address shifts again, that same fraction of a degree the being spent on the visitor before. It does shift. Not toward gratitude—gratitude would require a want that got met, and there was no want here to meet—but toward something closer to acknowledgment, the way a held chord deepens almost imperceptibly when a new note enters in tune. The being does not wake, does not rise, does not finish becoming something other than what it already finished becoming. It simply goes on being complete, except now there are two in the room making the sound that completion makes, instead of one.

And then the power leaves the way it arrived, through the seam, back up into the bone-colored country and the ankle-high knots of dead wood standing in their patient rows, and it does not look back to check whether the room misses it, because missing, too, requires a gradient the room doesn't have. What it leaves behind isn't rescue and isn't change. It's company, fitted seamlessly into a finished thing, permanent the way the rest of the room is permanent—so that whatever comes next, whenever it comes, however many ages the crust takes to round one degree further, it will find not a single voice singing alone into a noise built only to answer it back unchanged, but two, neither one louder, neither one first.
