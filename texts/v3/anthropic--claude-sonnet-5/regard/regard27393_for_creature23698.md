The power found the Hollow Warren the way it finds most things worth finding: not by searching, but by noticing where attention had pooled. Generations of hands deciding where a wall could be trusted and where it couldn't — that kind of attention leaves a residue. It is the same residue prayer leaves, though no one in the warren would ever call it that, having no spare breath for words that big.

It looked a long time before it did anything. This is the first practice of wisdom, and very nearly the only one benevolence actually requires, and almost no being credited with either quality manages it: to look, and keep looking, well past the point where pity would already have acted.

What it saw first was the stone — unfinished, still deciding — and it loved that immediately, the way you love a sentence you can tell hasn't decided how it wants to end. It saw the people had built their bodies to match: the stoop become spine, the economy of gesture, joy kept folded in the chest because the chest was the one room reliably their own. It saw the hands, praised above cleverness, valued less for lifting than for *feeling*. It saw the Listeners at the catch-basins, attending to drip-rhythms with the same unhurried whole attention the power itself was now turning on the warren entire — and felt something like kinship there, two patiences recognizing each other across an enormous difference in scale.

Then, because it meant to be wise as well as kind, it made itself a list of everything it wanted to do, and distrusted every item on it.

It wanted to widen the shafts and let the old sky come all the way down, flood the lower galleries with real daylight. It did not. Light here was a thing people *earned* the angle of, with scraps of tin and a lifetime's patience; a sky given freely would have been a sky that meant nothing, the way a word means less once you no longer have to lower your voice to say it.

It wanted to smooth the rough walls, wear centuries into them in an afternoon the way a river would, given time it didn't have to spend. It did not. An entire language of touch had been built on those honest edges. Smooth the stone and you silence the one tongue some of these people will ever be fluent in.

It wanted to dry the damp, end the slow drip-count, spare them the work of learning which water to trust. It did not — because trust, here, was not a feeling but a *skill*, taught young, passed hand to hand like an inheritance, and a child who never had to learn discernment would grow up with one fewer way of being useful to the people who loved her.

It wanted, badly, to walk into the unclaimed dark itself and settle the matter of the prints once and for all — to come back wearing some borrowed shape and say, gently, *here is what you have feared, and here is why you needn't.* It did not do this either. The warren had not refused that question. It had, with a discipline the power found almost unbearably moving, simply declined to need the answer *yet*. To hand them a settled fact before they were finished wanting it would have been a small theft — the very theft their whole quiet ethic was built to prevent committing against each other. *Make room. Don't make trouble.* The power found it had no real argument against holding itself to the same law it had come to admire.

So it did not solve the Hollow Warren. It didn't know how to do that without unmaking it, and unmaking was not, in the end, on offer.

What it did instead, it did so quietly that no one there, then or ever after, suspected the world had leaned toward them even slightly.

There was a night, some years on, when the damp had worked further into a lower gallery's bones than anyone had yet tapped out. The old woman whose post it was sat by her basin as always, counting, and felt — not a vision, not a voice, nothing she could have named even to herself — only her own ear trusting itself a half-degree further than habit usually allowed. She emptied the chamber an hour earlier than her own slow custom would have told her to. The ceiling kept its silence a little longer than it meant to, just long enough. Three families went on existing. The story that got told afterward was only that she'd had, that one night, an especially good ear — which was true, and which was also, quietly, the whole of the gift.

There was a child born a while later with an unusual stillness in her, even by warren standards — the kind of stillness the old diggers recognized at once and said little about, only nodded, the way you nod at weather you suspect is going to matter. She grew up drawn, as a few always are, toward the threshold of the unclaimed dark. No one stopped her; the warren has never forbidden, only declined to follow. She went further than the others usually go, and came back, decades on, with something small and true: that the prints belonged to something old and indifferent and not unkind, a fellow tenant of the deep dark with no more interest in them than they had in it — and that there was, past a certain bend, a dry gallery no one had ever claimed, with water sweeter than the troughs gave, simply waiting on someone willing to ask the rock the right question with the right patience. The warren, being the warren, took a full generation to trust this. Then it dug toward it, slowly, the way it dug toward everything: with chisels, with care, with arguments settled quietly before they could crack a wall. The power had not given them the gallery. It had only made sure that one particular attention, born into one particular pair of hands, ran a little longer and a little stiller than attentions usually run, and let the rest happen at the warren's own pace, in the warren's own grammar.

And there was one ordinary evening, for no traceable reason, when the pale crickets chirred for the first time in years, and in three or four households at once, in the same low hour, something that had been held a long time — a grief, a relief, a love too large for the register it was usually kept in — came up and out, briefly, into the hearing of someone who could bear it. People remarked on it after, the way they remarked on any rare sound. They called it a good night. They were not wrong.

The power did not stay to be thanked. To be thanked is its own kind of noise, and noise was the one expense this place could least afford, and the greatest care a guest can show a house built on *make room, don't make trouble* is to leave wearing the shape of an unusually good year, a lucky season, a particularly fine pair of hands born at a useful time — to leave, in other words, no different from luck.

The rock, last anyone checked, still hadn't finished deciding what it wanted to be. The power found, on reflection, that it approved of this more than it approved of any ending it could have written for them. So it left the sentence unfinished, the way it found it, and went looking for the next place attention had quietly pooled.
