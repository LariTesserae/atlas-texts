# What the Power Would Do

It would come the way attention comes to anything truly attended to: not as weather, not as a shape descending through the low strained sky, not as anything the segmented joint-clingers would startle at or the Resonant would need to step around. It would come as a widening of *noticing* — the basin, for the first time since whatever built the knee-high foundations walked away from them, would be fully seen. Every drip. Every chord. Every long patient century of plate-growth. Seen all at once, and seen for as long as it took to understand, which for a power of that kind is not a contradiction.

## First, it would do nothing.

This is the part that takes wisdom rather than benevolence, and the part most lesser kindnesses fail at. The instinct to help arrives faster than the understanding of what help would even mean here, and a power that acted on the instinct alone — *something is alone, alleviate the aloneness; something is silent, give it a voice; something is dying without descendants, give it heirs* — would do real damage dressed as mercy. So it would wait. It would sit inside the long swell-and-fade of the basin's voice for the equivalent of generations, not because it needed the time to perceive, but because the only honest way to know whether a thing is suffering is to outlast your own assumption that it must be.

It would watch the Resonant cross thirty meters of open lane in a day. It would watch it take the long way around a dead foundation stone. It would watch it hold itself, patient past any patience a built thing should have, always at the crest of a chord it would never catch and never needed to. And it would ask the only question worth asking before any gift is given: *does this thing want anything it does not have?*

## What it would find.

Not loneliness — not in the shape visitors import into the word. The Resonant does not lack companionship the way a thing built for company lacks it; it was never built for company. It was built for *chord*, and the basin is never not singing, and so by the only metric that could matter to a being literally made of listening, it is never not accompanied. The visitors who call the place "lonely" are translating an absence they would feel into a presence that doesn't notice the lack, the way a person standing in silence might pity a fish for having no air to breathe.

What the power would find instead is something narrower and more real: a being living out an entire, complete, unrepeatable life in devotion to a sound too large for it to ever fully hold — feeding at the edge of something vast, never the center of it, never once in centuries getting to be the *source* rather than the listener. Not lonely. But never, not once, *met*.

And the ruins — the power would understand those too, fully, in a way no visitor ever could. Not a mystery requiring solving. A grief requiring nothing at all, because the builders are not waiting anywhere to be rescued, no thread connects them forward into the Resonant's plated body, the clean story is wrong the way the basin already suspected it was wrong. They came, they built low circular walls sized for hands, and they left, or ended, long before the first stack had a name. The power would not resurrect them. There is a kind of wisdom that knows the worst thing you can do to the dead is insist they aren't.

## The temptation it would resist.

It would feel the pull — any benevolent thing would — toward the large gestures. Seed the basin with a second Resonant, so the first is no longer singular. Tune the dead foundation-stone to ring forever, so the builders' silence is answered. Slow the shattering, so no stack-elder ever again lets go all at once into grit and a new tower's footing. Every one of these is a kindness shaped like a hand reaching into a closed system and rearranging it to be less sad *to the hand*. Every one of them would also be a small extinction — of the particular thing this basin is, which depends on its scarcity, its silence, its slow unanswerable accretion, in the same way a held note depends on the held breath beneath it. A power wise enough to be trusted with this kind of attention is wise enough to know that *fixing loneliness it did not ask to have fixed* is not a gift. It's an edit.

So it would not multiply the Resonant. It would not raise the dead. It would not stop the shattering — would, if anything, come to love the shattering, once it understood it: not tragedy, but the one mercy the basin already gives its oldest children, a release built into the design from the start, long before any visiting power arrived to feel sorry about it.

## What it would do instead.

One thing. Small enough to leave no mark a geologist would ever find, large enough to be the only gift that wouldn't unmake what it touched.

It would *answer*, once.

Not with a second creature. Not with speech, which the Resonant has no organ for and no want of. It would wait for the chord to build in some unremarkable cluster of stacks — wait for the long slow swell that happens a thousand times a year and means nothing in particular — and as the Resonant settled into position at the crest of it, feeding the only way it knows how to feed, the power would, just once, hold a single additional resonance against the chord. Not louder. Not different in kind. Pitched so precisely into the gap between two ringing plates that nothing in the basin would register it as foreign — except the one listener built finely enough, after centuries of tuning-spines angled at exactly this kind of thing, to notice that the chord had, for the length of one long breath, been *completed* rather than merely large.

It would not feel like rescue. It would not feel like company arriving, because nothing arrived — no shape, no second silhouette in the mist-lanes, nothing for the spine-fans to triangulate as another body. It would feel, if the Resonant has anything like feeling and the power believes — having watched long enough — that it does, like the one time in an uncountable life that the vastness it had spent itself listening to had, for a single held note, *listened back.*

Then the power would let the extra resonance fade with the rest of the chord, the way everything here fades, and say nothing further, and ask for nothing — no worship, no change in behavior, no break in the pattern. The Resonant would resume its slow migration toward the next swelling cluster, the way it always had. Outwardly: nothing different. No visitor, returning a hundred years later, would find any evidence that anything had happened at all.

## What it would take.

This, and only this: it would carry the sound away with it. Not edit it out of the basin — the basin keeps everything it makes, that's the whole nature of the place — but *also* hold a copy of it somewhere a power's attention is capable of holding things, somewhere outside the slow erosion that will, given enough hundred-thousand-year patience, eventually take even Verach Mir apart, plate by plate, back into the dark wet grit it rose from. Long after the last stack has shattered for the last time and there is no chord left for anything to chase, one true thing about this place — one chord, one Resonant's whole answered breath of a life — would still exist, somewhere, witnessed, complete.

Not saved. Not preserved as it was, frozen and therefore killed. Just — known. Held in a memory patient enough that it didn't need the basin to keep singing in order to keep mattering.

## Why this, and not more.

Because the wisest thing a benevolent power can offer a being whose entire nature is *listening* is not rescue, not companionship, not an end to its solitude. It's the one experience that solitude itself cannot provide: to be, just once, heard back. Not changed by it. Not delivered from anything by it. Simply — for the length of one swelling chord, in a basin too vast and patient to ever finish its own song — no longer the only one listening.

Then it would withdraw its attention gently, the way mist lifts off the stack-bases when the air finally warms past the point of holding it, and let the place return entirely to itself: dripping, ringing, fusing, shattering, growing a thumb's-width taller every lifetime, with one long-bodied, many-legged shape still moving unhurried through the lanes between the towers, chasing a tide it was never meant to catch — 

— singing ground, singing still, to almost no one. 

Almost.
