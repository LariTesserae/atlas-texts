# What the Power Did

It found the hollow the way such powers find anything — not by searching, which implies absence, but by the same kind of attention a held breath has toward the room it's being held in. It was already aware. It had simply not yet *looked closely*, and looking closely, for a thing of that scale, is itself a kind of arrival.

The first problem was speed. The power's natural tempo was geological, glacial, the kind of patience that doesn't distinguish between a decade and an afternoon. To notice the hollow at all — to see the sharpness in the new shoots, the rawness at the joints, the *youngness* of the place — it had to slow itself nearly to stillness, and then slow further, until a single drip ticking down through seven layers of leaf became, to it, an event with a beginning, middle, and end. This took something. Not effort, exactly. Humility, maybe. The same kind of humility the hollow's people show their own walls: the agreement to walk on something alive the way you'd walk on something asleep that you loved.

It watched for a long time before it did anything at all. This is, perhaps, the first true thing to say about what a wise power does: it watches past the point where watching stops being information-gathering and starts being a relationship.

---

## What It Did Not Do

It did not announce itself. There was no light, no voice, no weather that hadn't already been weather a moment before. A power that arrives the way storms arrive in lesser stories would have undone, in one gesture, everything the hollow's people had spent decades learning — that nothing here is *imposed*, that the only honest way to shape a living thing is to ask it, patiently, in a language it already speaks.

It did not make them larger, faster, or more numerous. It considered this — briefly, the way you might consider a kindness before realizing it's actually a wound dressed as a gift. More of them, and the canopy they'd trained wouldn't shelter all the new heads. Faster, and they'd outpace the floor-mats' slow ring of growth, walk on rhizome not yet ready to hold them. Bigger, and the rooms shaped around the bend of bodies that had grown up narrow, the way the trunks grew narrow, would no longer fit anyone who lived in them. Their smallness was not a deficiency waiting to be solved. It was a measurement, taken correctly, of exactly how much hollow there was to hold.

It did not fix the architecture, because the architecture was not broken. Mid-sentence is not the same as unfinished-and-failing. A power that mistook *becoming* for *brokenness* would have ended the one thing this place was actually doing well.

And it did not name the thing overhead — the heavy rustle, the bird-or-bird-adjacent shape never seen, only ever heard departing. It would have been easy. The power could see it clearly, knew its shape, its hungers, its harmlessness. But the hollow's people had decided, generations or decades ago, that naming a thing you've never seen felt like a small dishonesty, and the power found — turning this over in whatever in it does the turning — that it agreed. Some mysteries are furniture. You don't replace a chair just because you finally learned what wood it's made from.

## The One Thing It Found Worth Doing

But watching closely is not the same as watching idly, and somewhere in its long, slowed attention, the power found the one place where its silence would have been a different kind of violence: not interference, but neglect.

Downslope, below the frost line, in root-water the hollow's people couldn't reach with fingers or feeling, something was spreading. A fungal kinship, patient and fusing, that moved through contact exactly the way the hollow's own walls were built — touchpoint to touchpoint, joint to joint — except where the people's slow encouragement made a structure, this made a wound that looked, for years, indistinguishable from growth. It would have reached the deep taproots in perhaps three more winters. It would have called itself, if it had a self to call anything, simply *continuing*. The hollow would have called it, too late, a bad season.

The power did not cut it. Cutting did not occur to it any more than it occurs to the people who live there — not from prohibition, just absence, the shape a mind takes after enough time spent loving something that only understands pressure and patience.

Instead it did the only thing that wouldn't have been a lie to the place it was done in. It became, for a while it would later not remember as a while, very small. Small enough to fit a fingertip's worth of attention against a single deep root, in the cold, below where anyone walked. And it pressed there. Not hard. Just present, just constant, the way a person guides a tendril sideways with two fingers and holds it, day after day, until the tendril forgets it wanted to grow any other way. It encouraged — nothing more dramatic a word than that — a callusing. A thickening at exactly the touchpoints where the fungal kinship would arrive, three winters off, looking for a door. It gave the root a memory of an injury that hadn't happened yet, the way scar tissue sometimes runs ahead of the wound, if something patient enough sits with it long enough to suggest the shape.

This took, by the power's accounting, no time at all. By the root's accounting, it took three years, which is to say, it took exactly as long as everything else in the hollow takes, because the power had finally, fully, learned to keep that hour.

## What Was Left Behind

When the blight came, the deep wall did not yield. It thickened instead, in the precise places it needed to, in a manner none of the hollow's people had directed, and none of them claimed credit for, because claiming credit for growth was never a habit this culture had. They found the new density the way they find everything — through the soles of their feet, through long fingers pressed flat to bark — and read it as the wall simply *knowing*, the way a wall sometimes knows to lean away from a coming wind before the wind arrives. They had no word for foresight grown into wood. They didn't reach for one. They touched the place, once, with something like gratitude that didn't require an object, and went on with the work that had no name for itself.

The power did not stay to be thanked, because being thanked would have required being known, and being known would have required becoming a fact in their world instead of a texture in it — a category they'd have to make room for, a door where there hadn't needed to be one. It left the way it arrived: as a pressure, briefly present, indistinguishable in the end from all the other patient pressures that built the place to begin with.

It did take one thing with it, on its way back up to its own enormous tempo, its own weather-scale patience. It took the lesson the hollow had been teaching the whole time, to anyone slow enough to learn it: that the kindest shape a great power can take, toward a small new thing still finishing its first sentence, is not gift, not rescue, not light from above — but a fingertip's worth of pressure, held in the right place, for exactly as long as it takes the thing itself to decide it was always going to grow that way.

It has no plan for an ending here, either. It has only continued, the way the hollow continues, the way the people who live in it continue — taking its cues, in the end, from what it found itself loving, rather than the other way around.
