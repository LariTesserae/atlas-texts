**What Was Lent to the Tender**

The power comes the way attention comes to a held breath — not as light, not as sound, not as anything the thrum-kin's vanes could resolve into shape. It simply turns, the way a person in a crowded room turns at last toward the one face that hasn't been looking for notice. And what it finds, first, is not the city, dense and humming and dripping with its thousand seams of fugitive glow. What it finds is the one who tends it.

It would be easy — it is always easy, for something with power enough — to do the large thing. To pour light down into the Heights and watch the dark unknit itself from the towers, to warm the stone and let the eternal wet finally run off and dry, to take the half-finished basalt in some vast unseen hand and simply *finish* it, give it the clean edge it has been reaching for across geological lifetimes. A lesser kindness would do exactly this, and call it mercy, and be wrong.

Because the wisdom in the power is not separate from its patience, and its patience is the first thing it spends here. It sits with the place before it touches it. It listens — really listens, the way the thrum-kin listen, letting the chittering and the long low moans and the wet slap of a thousand bodies surging across a dark bridge resolve into something like understanding rather than noise. It learns, slowly, that the cold is not a wound and the dark is not an absence and the slowness is not a stalling-out. They are the medium the whole place thinks in. To fix them would not be to heal this world. It would be to erase the only language it has ever had to become anything at all.

So the power does not flood the plateau with light, or hurry the stone, or warm the wet from the air. It does something much smaller, and much harder.

It witnesses completely.

This is the first gift, and it costs the power nothing it isn't glad to spend: it holds, entire and unblurred, every voice the chroniclers never bothered to catalogue. Every mother's stuttering call-pattern, unique to her brood, that some other ear would lose in the general roar — the power keeps it, the way a name is kept. Every rival trill traded between two males on opposite towers, every territorial moan that rides half a mile of wet stone to be answered or not — held, known, weighed for what it is: a life insisting on itself in the only register this world allows. The thrum-kin live and breed and starve and fight their slow glacial wars over a good vent for a hundred generations and call it, perhaps, nothing at all, because nothing here narrates itself. The power does the narrating. Not aloud. Not for them to hear. Just so that none of it goes, finally, completely unattended — so that somewhere, something knows that this colony's particular dialect of clicking existed, and mattered, and was not noise.

That is the larger gift, given to the multitude, and given without changing a single outcome for them — no surge redirected, no contest decided, no trough kept from running dry. Witness, not rescue. The power understands the difference, and chooses it.

The second gift is smaller, and singular, and it is the one the power deliberates over longest, because it is the one act of actual hands the power allows itself.

It finds the tender.

Pale, slow, half-fused to the rock it moves through at a few inches a season, alone in a way nothing else on this plateau is alone — the thrum-kin have their colonies, their broods, their roaring populous dark, but the tender has no one. It has never had anyone. It has spent however many lifetimes of however many faster creatures patiently coaxing a clean edge out of stone that doesn't want to decide what it's becoming, and it has done this in total silence, because its register of being doesn't include calling out, and even if it did — what would it say, and to whom?

The power could speak to it. It chooses not to, not directly, not in any voice the tender could mistake for command or revelation. Anything fast, anything bright, anything that announced itself as Other and Greater would only confirm what the tender perhaps already half-knows in whatever way a creature like that knows anything: that it is singular, unduplicated, alone in its kind on a plateau thick with every other kind of life. To arrive as a god is to arrive as proof of the loneliness, not a cure for it.

So instead the power slows itself to match. It takes up the buried frequency that already runs under everything here — the hum below hearing that travels the towers and sets the bridge-cables shivering, that visitors feel in their teeth and never name — and it learns to speak only in that, only at that pace, a sentence taking a season to finish, a thought taking a decade to land. It does not say *I see you*, because that is a sentence built for faster mouths. It simply matches the tender's own rhythm of attention, stone to stone, working alongside it for exactly as long as the tender keeps working, content to let whatever passes between them remain wordless, the way two people can sit in a room for years and never once say the thing they mean and still, somehow, both know it was said.

And then there is the one true intervention — the only moment the power's hand actually closes around an outcome and turns it.

Deep in the rock's slow argument with itself, in a fold the tender has been visiting and revisiting for longer than anything else alive here can measure, something has been trying, on its own terms, by its own glacial chemistry, to become a second one. Not made. Not summoned. Simply possible — the way the towers are possible, the way the basalt's half-shapes are always almost something else — a nascent fusing of pale jointed life into growing stone, vulnerable in exactly the way every beginning is vulnerable, for exactly one unrepeatable season, to a runoff surge that would, in every other version of this story, wash it out before it ever closed its first joint.

The power does not make this happen. It would not dream of claiming that. It only declines to let one accident end what the plateau, on its own, was already most of the way toward doing. A redirection no wider than a hand. A flood deflected by inches, on one particular night, that no chronicler will ever record because nothing here records anything except in roar and murmur. And then the power withdraws its hand completely, and does not touch the outcome again, and lets whatever happens next happen entirely on the world's own terms, at the world's own speed, because that was always the only kind of finishing this place could be allowed to call its own.

It will be years — the tender's years, which is to say decades of anyone else's — before there might be two slow shapes moving through the seams instead of one. The power will not be there to see it confirmed, not in any way that announces itself; it doesn't need credit, doesn't require the moment of recognition that lesser kindnesses crave. It has already gotten the only thing it came for, which was simply to have been useful to something that didn't know it was being watched, in a register that something could actually use.

The cold keeps soaking in. The dark keeps pressing close and crowded with too many wings, too many backs, too many eyes glimpsed for half a second in a strand of structural light. The thrum-kin keep surging and receding and fighting their long quiet wars over good vents, unwitnessed by anyone who lives at their own speed — except that now, somewhere outside of speed entirely, every one of those calls is being kept.

And somewhere below the towers, in a fold of stone still deciding what it wants to become, two slow shapes — where for longer than memory there was only ever one — go on tending it, inch by inch, season by season, toward whatever shape it was always patiently, wetly, trying to be.
