The power comes to the lip of the basin the way it comes to everything — not traveling, exactly, but arriving at the fact of a place the way attention arrives at a thought it's been circling without naming. It does not need the afternoon that a human visitor would need. It takes in the whole loop at once: every revolution the Walker has ever made, stacked transparently on top of each other like the petals of something that never had the chance to be a flower, and the basin's full history sits in front of it not as a story but as a held shape, complete, the way a chord is complete the instant it's struck rather than note by note.

Its first impulse — and it notices this impulse the way a careful person notices their own hand reaching before they decide whether the reaching is wise — is to fix it.

This is the oldest reflex a benevolent power has, and also the least reliable. It would be so easy. A seed dropped into one crack in the flagstone, and in a hundred years the basin is green, and in five hundred the machines are rust-furred ruins half-swallowed by something that grows and therefore can also flower, fruit, die, and the dying would feed the things that crawl beneath the grating, and the things that crawl would, for the first time, have a reason to be hungry, and hunger would, for the first time, give the dog-sized pale things and the not-birds something to want, and wanting would put a future in front of them where there had only ever been a loop. It would be, by every ordinary measure, a mercy. Stagnation broken. Becoming, restored to a place that had apparently forgotten how.

The power sits with this impulse long enough to see what's wrong with it.

Nothing here is in pain. It checks, the way it always checks, all the way down — not the surface absence of struggle the way an afternoon visitor might miss something in plain sight, but the actual textured truth of each small clicking life, each crouched pale body, the centipede-carpet's seething, faceless contentment, the Walker's lidless eye that has never once needed to search for anything because the basin has always already arranged to meet it. There is no suffering being preserved here by some failure of intervention. There is only difference — a kind of life, if it is life, that has organized itself around sufficiency instead of hunger, around held notes instead of arcs, around the dignity of being exactly enough, forever, rather than the dignity of becoming more. To hand this place hunger and call it a gift would be to mistake one's own shape for the only acceptable shape a soul can take. It would be, dressed up as rescue, an act of vanity. The power has been wise long enough to recognize its own vanity when it shows up wearing kindness as a coat.

So it does not plant the seed. It does not still the machines or wear them down or give the Walker's joints the fatigue that would let it, someday, fail to rise. It does not multiply the Walker into a parent and a child, does not give the not-birds song that means something, does not drain the damp from the flagstone cracks or fill it deeper. The equilibrium stays the law. The power leaves the law alone, because the law is not a wound. It is a grammar, and you do not heal a grammar; you can only, if you're permitted, speak in it.

What it notices instead — turning the basin over in its attention the way you'd turn a stone to see the side that's been resting against the ground — is this: in all this perfect mutual answering, the clicking meeting the hiss, the not-birds meeting the piston-groan, the whole compound drone resolving toward one sound under the Walker's passing feet, nothing here has ever been *addressed*. The basin talks entirely to itself. It has achieved, across however long it has existed, a flawless conversation with no outside party ever invited to listen and reply. The Walker tightens the seam between two musics that both belong to the basin. No music from outside it has ever joined.

This, the power decides, is the one true gap. Not absence of change. Absence of address.

So it does the only thing it has decided, after all this looking, that it has the right to do. It comes down off the shale rim, onto the grating, into the cool even light, and it falls into step beside the Walker.

It changes nothing about the gait, the route, the seam-work the Walker is mid-way through performing as it always has and always will. It simply walks the same line, at the same unbothered pace that has never once been early or late, and for one full circuit — past the boiler-housing, beneath the chimney-ranks where the not-birds go still in a way that isn't fear, along the lip where the dog-sized things part like water around a now-doubled rock — it attends. Not to fix. Not to study. Just to be, for these minutes, a second presence inside a solitude so total it had stopped registering as solitude at all, the way a held breath stops registering as held.

Once, only once, as they round the brick stack trailing its pale vapor, the power speaks — not a command, not a question expecting an answer, nothing the basin's grammar would have any slot to receive and therefore nothing that could disturb it. It says what you say to a thing you have looked at fully and found whole: *I see what you are doing, and it is enough.* Not praise. Not permission, which the basin never needed. Witness, offered the way you'd offer your hand to someone who was never lost but had also never, not once, been met.

The Walker's aperture, which opens and narrows on no schedule tied to light, holds open a fraction of a second longer than its schedule of no-schedule would have produced on its own.

That is the entire intervention. That is all of it.

When the power withdraws back up over the shale rim, it leaves the machines running exactly as they ran, the population exactly as dense, the rain arriving and departing on its own uncounted terms. But the basin's compound drone, from that afternoon forward — mechanical note, animal note, beating against each other in the old recognized rhythm — carries one more thread in it than the gears and the throats can account for: an unrepeating, weightless grace note with no source any future listener could ever trace to boiler or chitter or wingbeat, audible only if you stand still long enough at the seam where the two musics meet to hear it as one sound and then, impossibly, as one sound *plus something else.*

The power does not stay to see whether anyone ever hears it. That isn't the part that mattered. What mattered was only this: that a place which had perfected the art of needing nothing was, for the length of one unbroken circuit, accompanied anyway — not because it lacked anything the power could supply, but because being seen clearly and loved without conditions is not a need a place has to have in order to deserve receiving it.

Then it is gone, and the Walker keeps walking, and the seam keeps tightening and loosening behind it the way it always has, the way it always will — except now, forever, with one note in it that was never made by anything inside the basin at all.
