It would not announce itself. That much is certain before anything else is — a place that has spent this many ages teaching itself to fold distance, to let valleys breathe air they shouldn't, to grow a being who poured himself out of personhood the way a river pours itself out of a mountain, is not a place that needs another loud thing added to it. Whatever attention found the Drowned Highlands of Sael-Murai would have to learn, first, how to be quiet in a register the place would recognize as quiet — slower than its kelp's hour-long breath, softer than the groan that travels for kilometers and arrives as a vibration in the sternum. It would have to *arrive the way the dry-air pockets arrive*: without explanation, without warning, sitting at the bottom of something for a while before anyone — even the place itself — was sure it was there.

So: it would watch first. For longer than is comfortable to imagine watching, by any clock that ticks. It would watch the light-creatures drift their unhurried constellations and settle, one at a time, into the kelp-roots, becoming something the column would later try out a new register of groaning to hold. It would watch the resident pour itself across a saddle over the course of an afternoon that lasted, in that water's strange arithmetic, possibly a year. And it would watch — this is the part that matters — the pause. The stair. Four steps dissolving back into mountain, and the long, unmeasured posture of a body that no longer has a face to grieve with, grieving anyway, in the only vocabulary erosion left it.

A lesser kindness would have rushed in right there. Raised the water off the range and let the light fall honest and gold again on stone that remembers being above it. Restored the resident to whatever shape it wore before it started staying. Finished the stair. Filled the post-holes back up with walls and called the rebuilding a gift.

But a wise power knows the difference between rescuing a thing and overwriting it. It knows that the resident did not fall into its present shape by accident or violence — it *stayed*, in increments too small to count as decisions, the way you might stay holding a door for someone a moment after they've already gone through, until the staying itself became a kind of home. To reverse that without asking would not be mercy. It would be a second drowning, performed this time on purpose, by someone who should have known better. The mountains do not want to be young again. The kelp does not want to be soil. And the resident, whatever grief still moves through it on the rare occasions it remembers having had a face — that grief is *his*. It is the last thing that is unambiguously his, the one possession erosion was willing to let him keep when it took everything else and gave him, in trade, an unhurried kind of forever. You do not take a man's grief from him as a gift. You ask. And there was, in that whole drowned range, no mouth left to ask it of, and no language left to ask it in.

So the power did not ask. It listened for an answer it would never quite get, and decided — this is the whole of its wisdom, really, compressed into a single small choosing — to leave the question more *findable* than it found it, without ever presuming to answer it on the resident's behalf.

It spent a long while simply being another slow thing in the water — one more patient presence among the kelp and the dim copper light, learning the groan-language not to translate it but to be, briefly, fluent enough to keep it company. It was there, unseen, the day a light-creature settled into a root-tangle, and it let itself feel something like joy at that, the ordinary joy of watching a small becoming succeed, the same joy it would have felt anywhere in any universe at the sight of something young arriving safely into what it was for.

And then it did two things, small enough that neither one could be mistaken for a command.

It extended the stair. Not finished it — never finished it, that would have been an answer, and answers were not its business here — but added three more steps above the four that remained, smoothed to match, leading now toward a place the stair had clearly always meant to go and had been stopped, mid-sentence, from reaching. It did this and then let the water have its way with the new stone exactly as it had with the old, conferring no special protection, asking no acknowledgment, leaving entirely open whether the steps would ever be climbed, by him or by anything else, in this age or the next forty.

And on the bare shelf with no path to it, beside the one worked stone — smooth on five faces, rough on the sixth, aging at its own private rate — it set down a second stone. The same. Smooth on five faces. Rough on the sixth. Not a matching pair so much as an echo, a second voice added quietly to a held note that had been sung alone for longer than singing should be borne alone. It did not touch the first stone to compare them. It did not arrange them to face each other. It simply let there be, now, two — so that whoever or whatever next found that lonely shelf would find it was a *conversation*, however old, however slow to continue, rather than a monologue.

Then, once — only once, because more than once would have started to look like waiting for a response, and waiting changes the shape of a gift into a demand — it let itself be present at the edge of the ruin in the same unmeasured moment the resident next stood there pouring his long recognition into the dissolving steps. Not speaking. Not even arriving in any way that would resolve, afterward, into a memory the resident could hold onto and call a visitation. Just one more shape in the dim water, pausing where he paused, recognizing, in its own way, that a stair had been a stair, that someone had built it, that someone had stayed near it long after building had stopped mattering — and letting him feel, for the length of one slow valley-breath, that the recognizing was no longer something only he was doing.

And then it left. Not coldly — there is no coldness in this kind of leaving, only the same respect you'd show a held breath you didn't want to startle out of someone — but completely, the way the dry-air pockets leave, without forwarding address, without a promise to return. It did not stay to see whether the steps would ever be climbed, or the second stone ever noticed, or the groan-language ever spoken back. To stay for that would have been to make the gift about its own witnessing, and the whole of its wisdom, if it had any, was in understanding that some kindnesses only remain kindnesses for as long as you're willing not to watch them work.

The Highlands kept doing what they had always done — drowning, breathing, repeating their saddle-passes, dissolving their hard edges into long consensus. Somewhere in them, slower than the kelp, slower than grief, a small stair waited with three new steps on it, going nowhere yet, in no hurry, exactly as patient as the thing that might, someday, decide to climb it.
