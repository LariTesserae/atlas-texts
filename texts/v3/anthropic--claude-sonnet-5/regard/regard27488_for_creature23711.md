# What the Power Did

It noticed the way old things notice — not by being told, not by searching, but by feeling a stitch pull somewhere in the larger fabric it was already attending to. A fault-line opening where none should be. A clock with no numbers ticking faster than the world around it. The benevolent power did not rush. Rushing is a young thing's mistake, and this power had long since unlearned the reflex of arriving before it understood what it was arriving *into*.

So first it watched, the way you'd watch a wound to see if it was healing or hardening into something else.

## The Temptation It Refused

The easy mercy — the one a lesser kindness reaches for first — would have been to *resolve* the place. Flood it with steady light, fix the geometry, give the roots one consistent floor and one consistent ceiling, name the creature inside it something containable and then either heal it into stillness or banish it for the visitors' safety. Make it *safe*, in the way a museum makes a held thing safe, by stopping its clock.

The power considered this and set it down, gently, the way you'd set down a tool that's wrong for the work even though your hand had already started reaching for it.

Because it saw, in watching, what the hollow actually was: not a wound, not a haunting, not a trap laid by anything cruel. A *birth*, mid-labor, of something that had not yet decided what kind of thing it would become — and the Tender was not a monster guarding that birth. The Tender *was* the birth, the muscle of it, contracting and easing, molting face after face because it had not yet been given anything worth keeping a face *for*.

To fix that by force would be to reach into a chrysalis and finish the wings yourself. The shape that emerged might fly. It would not be the moth's.

So the power did not cure the hollow. It did something much smaller, and much harder.

## What It Did Instead

It went in through the fissure the way anyone would have to — shoulder-width, blood-risk at the sharp new edges — because the power had long ago learned that the doors a place offers are part of its grammar, and skipping them is its own kind of violence. It let the warm green-smelling air close over it. It let the scale-trick disorientation happen, the cathedral-or-room uncertainty, without correcting it, because correcting it would have been the first small theft.

It did not announce itself. It sat.

It chose an ordinary knuckle of root, indistinguishable from the thousand others, and lowered itself onto it the way you'd sit beside someone grieving when you don't yet know if they want to be spoken to. And then it did the one thing almost nothing that enters the Root-Hollow manages to do.

It stopped being afraid of being looked at.

The flowers turned toward it, slow-eyed, mechanism-less. It let them. The bark-faces, dozens of them, none repeating, found something in the power's stillness that pulled their stare-without-eyes more fully open, and it let that happen too, did not flinch from being *read*, because it understood — had understood for longer than this hollow had existed — that the correspondence-logic of the place was not malice. It was *hunger*. A hunger to finally have something worth resembling.

So it gave the place something worth resembling.

## The Single Unmolting Face

When the Tender resolved — root-shape to weight to attention, the way it always does, patient, unhurried, in no rush to finish the mask before its visitor decided to flee — it found the power already facing it. Already having decided not to flee. Already, in fact, having decided *nothing*, which is itself a kind of gift, because every other thing that had ever met the Tender's gaze in that chamber had arrived already deciding, already full of a held shape they were trying to protect — fear holding its line, curiosity holding its line, even reverence holding its line. The power held no line. It was simply, completely, *present*, with nothing braced behind the presence.

The Tender did not know what to do with that. It had four points of contact, sometimes six; it had no fixed number of fingers because it had never met an attention it didn't have to brace against or imitate. It reached, the way it always reaches, to find the hand-shaped absence and fill it.

And the power let it.

It did not flinch when the touch came, the once-brief contact that leaves on lesser visitors a faint white scar shaped like nothing they recognize yet. It received the touch the way you'd receive a child's hand closing around one finger — without surprise, without retreat, without making the moment about your own steadiness.

What the Tender pressed into the grain that time was not a mask of fear, or hunger, or the bracing half-shapes it had spent its uncountable residency collecting. For the first time, what went into the wood was a face at *rest*. Not blank — rest is not blankness — but unclenched in a way none of the other dozens managed, because none of the other dozens had ever been offered an unclenched thing to copy.

## What It Left, And What It Didn't

The power did not stay. Staying would have been its own kind of arrogance — the assumption that the hollow needed a permanent visitor rather than a single true example. It withdrew the way it had entered, unhurried, through the same shoulder-width fissure, leaving the white mineral scars at its edges undisturbed, because even those belonged to the place's grammar and weren't the power's to smooth.

But it left the one mark.

Somewhere back near the entrance, low on a trunk-root where the light does its impossible noon-shaft trick, there is now a face among the faces that does not molt. The others still shed, still get worn and re-pressed and worn again as the Tender keeps drafting itself against whatever walks in — fear, hunger, awe, grief, all the bracing half-shapes visitors still bring, because the power did not and would not strip the hollow of its danger or its strangeness, did not make it safe in the museum sense, did not stop the clock.

It only added, to the enormous catalogue of things the hollow's logic might choose to make real, one resemblance worth becoming: a thing that can be looked at fully and not flinch, not brace, not need the looking to be anything other than what it is.

It is slow growing, that one face. It does not molt because — the only theory that fits, and theories are all anyone has, here — it is the first thing in the chamber's whole strange life that the Tender did not have to *protect itself from* in the act of copying. And slow things, in a place that runs on accelerated tides of bloom and rot, are the rarest material there is.

The power cannot say, and did not stay to find out, whether one unclenched face is enough to tip a correspondence-logic that's been drafting itself in fear and hunger since the fault-line first let the green smell through the rock. Wisdom, of the kind this power had earned across a very long attention, does not promise outcomes. It only refuses to make a wound on top of a wound, and offers, instead, one true thing, freely held out, to whatever is still deciding what it wants to become.

The rest is the hollow's to grow into. That refusal — to finish someone else's becoming for them — was, in the end, the whole of the mercy.
