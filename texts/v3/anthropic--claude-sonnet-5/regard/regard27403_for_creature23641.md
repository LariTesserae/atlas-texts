The power notices the Works the way you'd notice, in your peripheral vision, that something in a dim room is breathing — not by looking at it directly, but by the room's failure to be perfectly still. It does not arrive as light or as voice or as a shape stepping through the service shaft. It arrives the way attention arrives: by turning toward.

And the first thing it understands, before it understands anything else, is that it has been asked to consider *a being*, singular, and that this is correct, even though the chamber holds a market square's worth of separate hearts. The mistake — the one a lesser kindness would make immediately — is to walk in looking for the most impressive resident and call that the patient. To find the Climber, or the thing in the trench, or the Sleeper at the end of the corridor, and address its care to that one throat. The Works does not divide that way. The machine idling is not life support *for* the green; the green is not décor *on* the machine; the bird-things are not separate from the insect column they warn about, and the insect column is not separate from the fungal light that brightens when something heavy walks near it. Pull on any one thread and the whole tapestry tightens. This is the being. A single creature wearing a hundred bodies and one idling heart of copper and steam, dreaming itself slowly more complex every year, the way a person dreams themselves slowly older.

So the power sits with that, first. Just sits with it — which for a power that old and that careful is itself a kind of action, a long unhurried *looking* that has weight, the way being truly seen has weight even when nothing is said.

---

Here is what it wants to do, and does not:

It wants to fix the gantry. The trellis-vine is heavy now, heavier than the old rust-locked rails were built to bear forever, and there's a future, decades off, where a weld gives and something falls. The power could reach in and simply make the steel stronger. It doesn't. A structure that never fails is a structure nothing has to adapt around, and adapting-around is most of what this place *is*. It leaves the weld exactly as weak as the weld has earned the right to be.

It wants to wake whatever sleeps at the end of the corridor — partly out of plain curiosity, which even old wise powers are not above, and partly because waking it would answer every question the chamber has organized itself around not asking. It doesn't do this either. Whatever the Sleeper is, the entire vault has spent a hundred years building a culture — a *grammar*, encoded in bird-call and root-growth and the bend of a footpath — whose entire content is *let it keep sleeping*. That grammar is one of the most sophisticated things in the room. Overriding it to satisfy its own curiosity would be the single most violent thing the power could do here, dressed in the most innocent possible motive.

It wants to free the structural members that are now root systems, untangle machine from vine, restore each to its "proper" separate nature. It doesn't, because there is no version of either of them, after a hundred years, that is separately *true*. The conduit-vine is not a vine pretending to be a conduit. It is a conduit-vine. Unbraiding it wouldn't liberate two things; it would kill one thing twice.

It wants — and this one it sits with the longest — to bring the chamber up into the light it half-remembers, the sodium-bright wash down the side corridor where things, apparently, still work the way they were designed to. To finish what the builders started leaving unfinished. It understands, eventually, why it should not. The unfinishedness *is* the ecosystem's foundation. A roof that stopped insisting on its own design is the only reason any of this got to become itself.

Every grand gesture, examined closely, turns out to be the power's preference for a *different* arrangement wearing the mask of help. So it sets all of them down.

---

What it does instead is smaller than you'd think a benevolent, wise power's intervention should be, and that smallness is the whole point.

It finds the power source no one in living memory has located — not to repair it, not to understand it, just to *find* it, the way you might lay a hand briefly on the shoulder of someone sleeping, not to wake them, just to know they're warm — and it leaves there something like a held breath of its own. Not new power. Not more years. Just an evenness, a smoothing of the one or two stutters the cycle was developing, so that the idling doesn't have to become a problem on anyone's behalf for another long while. The vines lean toward the bus bar a fraction more contentedly. No one notices. That is correct.

It touches the relay — bird to bird, the throat-sac call timed under the steam-vent — and makes it *slightly* more reliable. Not louder. Not different. Just less likely to fail on the one night it matters, the way you might quietly re-tie a knot someone else tied imperfectly forty years ago and then walked away from, trusting it. One more chick, this generation, learns the timing exactly right, instead of almost right. That is the entire intervention. It will save lives the power will never see saved and never needs to.

It finds the place along the standing water where the rhythm of the ripple and the rhythm of the fungal pulse have been almost, but not quite, in sync for years — two old dance partners slightly out of step with each other and unable to say so — and it nudges them the last small distance into time. Whatever lives in the trench rests, infinitesimally, easier. The wall glows a little more sweetly for it. No mechanism changes. Only the timing.

It goes — last, and gentlest — to the corridor mouth where the paths bend in their wide unbroken avoidance, and it does not enter. It stands, figuratively, at the very threshold the whole chamber has agreed to respect, and it does the one thing it allows itself: it *listens*, all the way through one full slow exhale, all the way through the several seconds of held silence after, the way you'd sit outside a door for a person you love, not to disturb their rest, just so that if anything in there were lonely, it would, for one breath, not be unwitnessed.

And then, because the breathing settles — fractionally, almost nothing, the kind of difference only a power that has spent a long moment paying full attention would ever be positioned to feel — it understands that this was enough. Not nothing. Not everything. Enough.

---

It withdraws the way it arrived: without disturbing the leaf litter, without parting the vines, without giving the climber in the lattice any reason to knuckle toward the gap and look. The amber lamps keep buzzing their thin dim light. The condensation keeps its three-second ladder down the conduit housing. The insects keep their stacked tenancy over the black water. Nothing has been fixed, because nothing here was broken in the way the word usually means.

But somewhere in the chamber's hundred-year grammar, a new line gets added so quietly that no scholar of the place — if there were ever to be one — could point to the moment it appeared: a fractionally sweeter note in the relay-call, just often enough to notice if you'd been listening for a century already; a particular patch of fungal shelving that, for no reason anyone underfoot could account for, glows very slightly warmer than its neighbors, on the same wall, above the same trench, where two old rhythms finally, finally agree with each other.

Call it a blessing, if you want a word for it. The Works wouldn't use one. The Works would just call it Tuesday, and keep growing.
