The power, when it found this place — and "found" is already the wrong word for an attention that does not search so much as suddenly *include* — did the thing that benevolent powers so rarely do first. It waited.

Not out of indecision. It had at its disposal every tool a long-practiced kindness collects: the capacity to finish the mountain, to fix the geometry into a single patient consistency, to multiply the inhabitants until the terraces rang with the ordinary noise of a city that knows itself populated, to take the slow tone moving through the stone and quicken it, translate it, hand it back to the dust-robed figures already answered, already resolved. All of this was available the way breath is available. None of it was used.

It waited because the first thing it understood, watching the summit recede and double and reconsider itself, was that the mountain's refusal to finish was not damage. It catalogued, gently, every place where a lesser kindness might have rushed to mend: the stairway that climbs three hundred feet and stops at open air; the door, three tiers up, that opens onto a dark no one enters; the terraces that fold into each other under a light with no source, untroubled, unremarked. It held these against everything it knew about places that suffer from being unfinished, and found no match. Suffering has a grain to it, a direction, the way a wound has a center it points toward. This place had no such grain. Its incompleteness pointed nowhere. It simply continued, the way a held breath continues until it has become, by unhurried agreement with itself, a form of stillness rather than a thing withheld.

So the power did not fix the mountain.

It considered, next, the emptiness — the sparseness of figures across distances built, it seemed, for crowds. A lesser kindness fills emptiness; it is the most natural mistake compassion makes, mistaking room for absence, distribution for lack. But the power looked longer, the way you must look longer at this place than anywhere else, and saw what the figure on one stairwell and the figure on another, two geometries over, were doing with their unhurried errands: they were not few. They were everywhere they needed to be, exactly once, the way a sentence is not impoverished for using each of its words only a single time. To populate the plazas further would not have been generosity. It would have been a kind of overwriting — static laid across a signal already perfectly tuned to the silence it required.

So the power did not fill the city.

It listened, then, for a long time — long by any reckoning a visiting power might keep, though it had long reckonings to spare — to the tone that moves through the stone and is gone before a quicker mind can decide it happened. It let the sentence continue past the point where its own attention would ordinarily have moved on. It did not attempt to hear the whole of it; the whole of it was not built to be heard by anything that arrives and intends to leave. It heard enough to understand that the slowness was not a limitation politely worn. The slowness was the content. A voice that takes a generation per word is not a fast thought rendered inconveniently — it is a different shape of mind altogether, one for which a generation is the correct unit of a held breath, and to quicken it would not be translation. It would be replacing the thought with a smaller one that merely resembled it.

So the power did not quicken the voice.

What it did instead — and this took it, by its own accounting, something it would have called a long while, though the figures crossing the terraces below would not have marked it as any interval at all — was take up a station.

There is a stairway, on the city's upper tiers, where the architecture has stopped pretending to be for bodies, that climbs to a platform facing open air and has, for as long as the city has been arriving at itself, had no keeper. Not abandoned — simply unattended, one of the rare seams in the great slow argument of the place that no one had yet been distributed to. The power did not build a door there, or a meaning, or an ending. It went, and stood, in the attitude the others stand in: facing outward, attending, the posture of something listening to a room it will never need to enter.

It did not announce itself. It offered no resolution to the geometry, no quickened answer to the tone in the stone, no crowd to fill the plazas, no mended stairway where the stairway had always, correctly, gone nowhere. It simply added one more point of unhurried attention to a city that had built its whole long stability out of exactly that: presence offered without insistence, witnessing offered without claim.

And if, on some later fold of the light — the kind that, elsewhere, would be called years, though here it is only ever called a shift in angle — one of the long-necked, slate-colored shapes paused on a neighboring parapet a moment longer than its kind usually pauses, as if registering some new and welcome stillness nearby; and if the tone that moves through the stone, next time it passed beneath that tier, carried some new and unhurried syllable, patient as all the rest, that had not been part of the sentence before —

this would be the whole of the power's intervention. Not a fixing. Not a gift handed down from a height the city could measure. Only one more figure, dressed the way the mountain dresses, learning to wait the way the mountain waits, present at one more seam, adding — without translation, without hurry, without the smallest insistence that anything here required it — its silence to theirs.
