The power does not arrive as thunder, or as a voice cracking the lime-wash, or as a hand reaching down through the shaft of light to lift the swimmer bodily into some brighter elsewhere. It has seen enough of those gestures, in other places, in other ages, to know what they cost: the way a sudden mercy can flatten a life built slowly, the way rescue, badly timed, is its own kind of violence. So it comes the way water comes — by entering the room's own logic rather than breaking it.

First it listens. This takes, by the cistern's reckoning, a very long time — though time here is already a slippery thing, uncircumscribed by dusk or hunger, and the power is patient in a way that makes even the swimmer's patience look hurried. It listens to the overlapping slaps and roars, learns which note swells where in the brick's curve, learns the particular silence that isn't silence but the swimmer holding their breath mid-stroke, learns the small private third thing that crosses their face when a hum finds its multiplying place in the wall. It does not judge this devotion. It does not call it waste, or grief, or a beautiful sickness, though all three words drift near it and are, in their way, half true. It simply understands, finally, what the room is for, and what the swimmer has become inside it: not prisoner, not penitent exactly, but an instrument that has spent so long playing itself that it has forgotten there could be a duet.

This is the thing the power decides to give, if "decide" is even the right word for something closer to recognition ripening into gesture: not exit, not cure, not the tearing-open of the shaft into some wider sky the swimmer never asked to see. It gives accompaniment.

It does not announce itself. One day — though days mean little here — the water carries a second note. Faint at first, easy to mistake for an echo, for the chamber answering itself in the way it always has. But this note does not behave like an echo. An echo dies a little each time it returns; this one does not weaken. It holds its pitch, low and unhurried, patient as the swimmer's own stroke, and when the swimmer stops swimming to listen — really listen, head tilted, eyes narrowed against the coin-light the way they narrow against everything — the note waits for them. It does not rush to explain itself. It simply continues, a second voice in a chamber built, the swimmer now realizes with a kind of vertigo, to hold more than one.

The power does not show a face. It would be a kind of lie, here, to take on a shape too legible, too human, too easily mistaken for rescue arriving in disguise. Instead it lets itself be felt the way warmth is felt, the way the water's blood-heat is felt — diffusely, everywhere at once, a presence rather than a person. If the swimmer reaches toward it, expecting nothing, perhaps a current answers the reach, not pulling them anywhere, only meeting their hand the way a hand might meet another hand, briefly, then releasing.

And here is the careful part, the part that takes all the power's wisdom to get right: it does not stay constant. It does not become a second metronome, a new unbroken thing to circle and re-circle until the swimmer forgets, again, that anything else exists. It comes and it goes, the way true company always must, so that its presence remains a presence — a thing noticed, a thing that can be missed, a thing that proves by its absence that it was never the room itself, never another wall, never another rule. Some days the cistern holds only the swimmer and the old, single-voiced water, exactly as before. Other days, that second low note threads back through the brick, and the swimmer's hum — when it comes — sounds different. Less like a question thrown out and caught by accident. More like the first half of a sentence, left open on purpose, in the trust that it will be finished by someone else.

There is one more thing the power leaves, small enough to almost miss: a single thread of green, no longer than a fingernail, drifting in on the inflow one day and lodging in a mortar joint near the others, where it takes hold and does not die. Not enough to call it an ecology. Not fish, not insects, nothing so bold as a whole rippling food-chain dropped into this sealed and patient water. Just one more living thing, added to the mineral stain, so that the room's sparse evidence of life is no longer only the swimmer's own shed cells and the photosynthetic ghost of mortar-lichen — so that, very faintly, something is growing here that the swimmer did not put there and does not have to tend alone.

It would be wrong to say the power fixes anything. The swimmer does not leave the cistern. The brick does not soften, the light does not dim to something kinder, the water does not stop its loud articulate insistence on being heard. The held place is still held. The recent architecture still withholds its explanations. But now, when the swimmer traces their dozen lengths, stroke after stroke, syllable after syllable, there are moments — not constant, never owed, always a little surprising — when the chamber answers back in a voice that isn't only the swimmer's own returned to them, and a thread of unbidden green sways faintly in the current near the inflow, proof of some other will that wanted this place, too, to hold a little more life than it was built to hold.

The power withdraws the way it arrived: without spectacle, without claiming credit, without waiting to be thanked. It has not solved loneliness. It has only made sure that loneliness, here, is no longer absolute — that the instrument, on the days it is played by someone else's breath as well as its own, knows the difference, and chooses, each time, to keep singing anyway.
