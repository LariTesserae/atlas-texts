The power comes to it the way light comes to it — flat, even, without announcement, without the drama of arrival that might disturb what it has come to look at. It does not descend like weather or break the horizon like a built thing. It simply attends, the way you might finally turn your full attention to a sound you'd been hearing for years without listening to, and in that attending, the basin and the creature in it become, for the first time, *seen* — not merely present, but witnessed, all the way down to the fissures, all the way down, even, to whatever turns over in the dark beneath the pan.

Its first impulse — and it would be dishonest to pretend a benevolent power doesn't have first impulses, doesn't feel the old reflexive pull toward repair — is to fix something. This is what benevolence usually means, where the power comes from: find the lack, and fill it. Here is a place with no green, no future, no choice that isn't already written into the gradient of warmth and damp. Here is a being with no eyes, no nest, no story it tells about itself across generations, nothing it wants that it doesn't already have within arm's reach of the next salt-stained pocket. The power's hands — if hands is the word for what a power has — itch toward correction. It could give the creature sight. It could give it hunger sharp enough to drive it toward ambition, give it claws, give it a mate it chooses rather than one indistinguishable from any other paused shape on any other stain, give it young that need tending so that tending might become love, give it a tomorrow different from today so that today might become precious by comparison.

It holds its hands still. This is the first wisdom, and it comes slow, the way real wisdom does, by setting the impulse down rather than acting on it and only understanding afterward why that was right.

Because to give this creature a future would not be a gift to *this* creature. It would be the erasure of it and the installation of something else wearing its shape — something that suffers now where it did not suffer before, that wants now where it only took before, that can lose now where loss was never a category available to it. The power looks, really looks, at the low wide body wedged in its fissure through the held warmth of the unvarying day, and finds no grief in it, no fear, no thwarted reaching. It is not waiting for anything. It is not enduring anything. It has been mistaken, by the power's first glance, for a deprived thing, the way a creature from a forest might look at a stone and assume the stone is lonely for not being a tree. But the stone was never reaching toward tree-shape. The basin-thing was never reaching toward more.

The power considers, next, whether there is suffering here too subtle for a first glance — whether the wedging into cool fissures during the warmth, the standing in seep-stains waiting for droplets to gather, the long patient crossing between stains, might carry some thin thread of want unfulfilled, something the creature itself has no organ to register as longing but that a wiser eye could find anyway. It looks for a long time. It finds the bristled palps tuned to the drone, finds the body's whole architecture built not around lack but around *sufficiency* — every need answered by what already bleeds up through the cracks, every rhythm of behavior matched exactly to a rhythm already provided. It is not a creature straining at the limits of a too-small allotment. It is a creature that fits its allotment exactly, the way water fits a held cup, the way the drone fits the basin floor. There is no excess of want anywhere in it to relieve.

So the power asks itself the harder question, the one underneath all the others: is mere sufficiency, mere equilibrium, mere unchanging continuance, something to be pitied at all — or has it mistaken stillness for emptiness the way it nearly mistook the creature's blindness for blindness to something worth seeing? It turns the question over the way the creature turns its palps toward a stronger thrum. And it finds, eventually, that the basin is not a poverty. It is a completeness of a kind so total it doesn't know how to ask for anything, the way a circle doesn't ask to become more circular. The drone beneath the pan has gone on, working at whatever it works at, since before there was anything above it shaped like a witness; the creature has gathered itself out of that leaking warmth the way mist gathers on the underside of a stone, indifferent to whether anyone ever called it beautiful or sufficient or alive. To hand it ambition would not be charity. It would be a kind of violence dressed as a gift — the imposition of a future on something that was never broken for lacking one.

And so what the power does, in the end, is very small, and very large, and almost impossible to point to afterward as a deed.

It does not change the drone, or the temperature, or the light. It does not give the creature eyes, or a story, or a tomorrow unlike its today. It does not reach into the sealed dark beneath the pan to ask what works down there or why, because the asking itself would be a kind of intrusion on a thing that has never needed to explain itself to be real.

What it does is stay. Not as weather, not as event, not as anything the basin's instruments — bristled palp, oily wing-membrane, the slow patient crossing of open ground — could register as a disturbance in the rhythm they've tuned themselves to. It simply holds the place and the being in its attention the way you might hold a held note, for a duration that means nothing to the power and means nothing to the basin either, since the basin has no use for duration as anything other than itself. It is not watching *over* the creature, the way a guardian watches over a charge it expects to outgrow its danger. It is watching *with* it, in the only register the creature would recognize as care if care were a thing it had a sense for: presence without demand, attention that asks nothing back, the way the seep-stain asks nothing of the creature that drinks from it and gives nothing extra either, just exactly enough, exactly always.

If the power does anything that could be called an act, it is this: it takes the basin's own logic and turns it, gently, toward continuance. Whatever vast indifferent thing might someday come from outside this equilibrium — some collapse from above, some failure from below, some hand less patient than its own reaching down to break the lid for what's underneath — the power sets itself, lightly, against that. Not as a wall. As a kind of held breath. A bias toward the basin's right to keep being exactly what it has always been, for exactly as long as it would have gone on being it anyway, unmolested by any other power's idea of what it ought to become. This is the only intervention a wisdom like this allows itself: not the granting of a future, but the protection of the absence of one, where that absence was never a wound to begin with.

And when, in some unimaginable span the basin itself has no instrument to measure, the drone beneath the pan finally does run down — when whatever turns over in the dark stops turning, and the warmth stops bleeding up, and the seep-stains dry for the first time into something other than themselves — the power will still be there, attending, the way it attended the first low scuttling shape it ever found wedged into a fissure, mistaking nothing this time for emptiness, asking nothing of the ending except to be present for it, the way the creature itself was always present for its own held, unchanging, complete and ordinary day.

That, in the end, is what a benevolent power does for a thing that was never broken: it does not fix it. It keeps it company, in a silence exactly the shape of the silence it already knew, for exactly as long as that silence was always going to last — and calls that, rightly, love.
