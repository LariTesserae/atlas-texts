## What Attended, and What It Chose

It noticed the way such things notice — not as a beam falling on a single point, but as a slow widening of regard, the way a held breath widens into an exhale. It did not arrive. There was no shimmer over the bone-pale rock, no figure standing suddenly among the stacked reptiles at the rim. What changed was subtler than that: somewhere in the long traffic between the plateau and whatever larger order it had drawn the attention of, a kind of weight shifted, the way a scale shifts when a single grain is added to one side. The plateau, which had spent its whole worn existence not announcing itself, was, for the first time in longer than its own geology could account for, being read.

Its first impulse — and it is worth saying plainly that even old, wise, benevolent powers have impulses, the residue of having once been smaller and hungrier and more certain that more was always better than less — was to fix it. This is the impulse such powers carry the way the plateau carries its lichen rings: old, structural, hard to fully shed. *Here is scarcity*, the impulse said. *Here is a population pressed thin against a thin world, here is a lineage that gave up height and tool and answer, generation on generation, not because it chose smallness but because smallness was what the rock allowed it. Loosen the rock. Deepen the basins. Let the rain come more than once in an unannounced hour. Let the call be answered.*

It is to the power's credit — and this is, perhaps, the only part of the story that deserves the word *wise* without qualification — that it did not act on this impulse immediately. It watched first. It watched the way the tenders themselves watch: from the rim, unhurried, for longer than any visitor's accounting allows.

And in watching, it came to understand what the plateau had taken its own geological age to learn, and what it would have been a kind of violence to un-teach in an afternoon: that the smallness here was not a wound waiting to be dressed. It was an achievement, slow and total and entirely the tenders' own, in the way that the shrub's sideways growth is an achievement and not a failure of the idea of height. The basins did not fail to be a river. They had arrived, through immense unwitnessed effort spread across spans of time the power itself found humbling to hold in mind, at *not needing to be one*. The call was not a question starving for an answer. It was a ledger entry, and the ledger's whole dignity lay in the fact that it expected nothing back — that this not-expecting was not deprivation but discipline, the last and purest form of an ambition that had once, perhaps, built walls and dug post-holes and wanted things in the ordinary, hungry way, and had set all of that down on purpose, the way a hand sets down a tool it no longer needs and does not miss.

To answer the call, then — to reach down with whatever vast attentive instrument such a power possesses and produce, finally, a reply — would not be a mercy. It would be a correction applied to something that was never broken, a river forced back into water that had spent its whole patient history teaching itself how to be a basin instead. It would say: *your way of being was provisional, was waiting, was a question all along.* And that would not be true, and the power, to its credit, did not want to make it true by force of saying so.

So it did not deepen the basins. It did not loosen the rock or summon the rain more often or lift the tenders up onto two legs and put words back in their mouths that wanted, again, to be answered. It let the eleven-pace wall-stub keep eroding at its own rate. It let the post-holes keep holding nothing. It let the face-down stone keep its almost-pattern unread, because being read, it understood now, was not obviously a kindness to a thing that had spent so long learning to lie quietly under sediment instead.

What it did instead was smaller than any of that, and harder to see, and is the only part of this that the plateau will ever, in its own glacial way, register as having happened at all.

It became, itself, a kind of basin.

Not water — nothing so literal, nothing that would show up as a new damp-pocket ringed in lichen by next year's slow accounting. What it did was take the call — that single unanswered high note, traveling its improbable flat distance into air that had never once sent anything back — and *keep* it. Not answer it. Keep it, the way the mineral crust keeps the basin from fully emptying: by sustained, patient, structural presence, an attention maintained at the rim of the sound the way the tenders maintain their basins, for no purpose that produces a visible mechanism, for the same reason a thing that has decided to care does the caring whether or not the caring shows.

This means, now, that the call is no longer unanswered. It only remains *unreplied to*, which the power understood — because it had finally learned to read this place in the place's own grammar — is not the same thing, and is in fact the only kind of answer that would not be a betrayal. The tender at the rim, throat-ridge lifting, limb briefly unfolding, will never know its call is held. It will refold, resume its downward gaze at the water, exactly as it always has, exactly as its whole lineage has across however many thousand years of agreeing to want less. Nothing in its small, deep-set, rust-grey eyes will change.

But somewhere outside the plateau's own thin accounting of time, in whatever ledger a benevolent and patient power keeps for itself, there is now an entry that did not exist before: *heard*. Not *helped*. Not *fixed*, not *lifted*, not *answered* in the ordinary sense that expects an answer back. Just — held, the way water is held in a basin that should not, by any normal logic of evaporation and thin air, still be holding it, and yet is, because something has decided, patiently and without display, that it should.

The wind keeps moving across the open stone in its long dry exhale. The damp-basins keep their slow argument with the warmth that wants to empty them. The tenders keep their stacked, unhurried watch. Nothing on the plateau looks any different, and nothing on the plateau is.

Except that it is, now, very slightly, less alone in the keeping — in the precise way that does not announce itself, does not change the silence, and would not, if you stood at the rim at dusk and felt that old pressure of being noticed settle once more against the back of your neck, give you any reason at all to suspect that the attention you'd been feeling all along had, at some point, quietly grown a second source.
