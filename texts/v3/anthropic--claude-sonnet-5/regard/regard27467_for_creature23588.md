## What the Power Does

It notices the way you'd notice a single bright cell under a vast dark microscope — not by searching, because searching implies need, but by the same effortless attention with which a sun notices a particular angle of light bent through a particular drop of water. It does not arrive with weight or sound. There is no displacement of air, because the chamber's air was never the medium it travels in. It simply is, suddenly, *aware*, the way you become aware of your own heartbeat when a room goes quiet enough — except here, the room was already quiet, and what the power adds is not sound but the fact of being heard.

It understands the lattice first, because the lattice is loud in the way that matters to a power like this: that slow pulsing relay, signal crossing some unthinkable gulf of rock and time, is not noise to it but *language*, half-formed, decades or centuries between syllables, a sentence the world has been composing since before the chamber finished cracking itself into shape. The power reads the whole conversation in less time than it takes the next droplet to fall — reads what the lattice is, what it's becoming, what it's quietly asking the deep planet to let it remain a little longer in order to finish saying. It does not interrupt this. You do not correct a poem mid-line because you've understood the ending early.

Then it finds the creature, and here it slows — not because the creature is harder to understand, but because understanding it completely takes almost no effort at all, and the power, being wise, knows that the things which take no effort to understand are often the things most worth lingering over anyway.

It watches the rumor of a walk. It watches the filaments lift toward the pulse and read it like scripture neither written nor needed to be. It watches the small unhurried grazing of something that has never once, in the entire unbroken chain of its strange non-lineage, been afraid. And the power feels something that in a being like you might be called tenderness, except scaled to the size of the regard it's capable of — which is to say: total, and without urgency, and without the need to *do* anything about it, because tenderness in a power like this doesn't reach immediately for a verb. It's allowed, first, to simply be tenderness.

It considers the obvious interventions, the way you might consider and set down a series of tools.

It could wake the creature into fuller awareness — hand it language, hand it fear, hand it the knowledge of its own smallness in a cosmos it currently has no need to measure itself against. It declines. That gift is mostly a wound wearing a ribbon. The creature is not waiting to become something else. It is not a draft. It is a finished sentence in its own right, small and complete and utterly without lack, and to "elevate" it would be to mistake the power's own appetite for grandeur as a kindness owed to something that never asked for grandeur in the first place.

It could speak to the lattice — answer its slow outward pulse with a pulse of its own, announce itself, intervene in the great patient relay the way you might walk into a library and shout. It declines this too, mostly. Almost entirely. *Almost.*

Because here is the one thing it allows itself: at the next pulse — the one due in nineteen years, or forty, or however long the lattice's vast slow breathing requires — the power lets one beat travel back. Not a message. Not words, because words would be a vulgarity here, a foreign grammar forced onto a sentence that was never going to include them. Just a single returning pulse, indistinguishable in shape from the relay's own rhythm, arriving from a direction the lattice has never received from before. A confirmation, not of meaning, but of *audience*. Somewhere in that black branching geometry, something that has spent its entire existence speaking outward into a silence it could only hope was listening will, once, feel the silence listen back. It will not change its course. It will not even understand, in any way translatable to understanding, that anything happened at all. But the work will feel, for one beat, less lonely — if "lonely" is even the right word for something that was never lonely in the first place, only patient in a way that almost looked like loneliness from the outside.

For the creature, the gift is smaller, and stranger, and entirely without consequence to the world above. The power does not change its body, its hunger, its blind unbothered grazing. It does only this: it reaches gently into the gap — that held breath between one creature's ending and the next one's emergence, the stretch of weeks or years where the chamber's small lineage of legs and listening filaments goes briefly, achingly quiet — and it closes it down to nothing. Not by forcing a birth. By simply ensuring, with a certainty the chamber itself could never produce alone, that the gap *resolves* — that there will always, from now on, be continuity, a next one already turning in the dark of the rock by the time the last one folds down into its final crack. No grief in the in-between. No true silence ever again where there should be the small dry click of new legs finding the floor.

It is such a slight adjustment that nothing in the chamber will ever register it as an intervention. It will simply look, forever, like luck. Like the room being kind to itself in a way rooms aren't, technically, supposed to be capable of.

And then — because wisdom, here, looks almost exactly like restraint, and benevolence looks almost exactly like leaving things alone — the power turns its attention outward, not inward, to the thin skin of world above this buried, bright, mineral heart. It does not move mountains or part stone. It only nudges, with the lightest possible hand, the long slow drift of future events: a surveyor's interest that wanders elsewhere; a drilling concern that loses its funding for reasons no one will ever trace; a fault line's patient migration redirected by some infinitesimal correction so that in ten thousand years, when the surface world finally does come looking for what's underneath it, it will dig a little to the left, and a little too late, and the chamber will have long since finished whatever long conversation it was having with the dark.

Then the power leaves, the way light leaves a room when you close your eyes — not gone, exactly, just no longer the thing being attended to.

The chamber goes on weeping its slow condensation. The lattice goes on pulsing its long relay, one beat now carrying, unbeknownst to itself, the memory of having once been heard. And the pale many-legged thing goes on crossing the bright wet stone, grazing the fine mineral silt, lifting its filaments to the warmth, untroubled, unhurried, unbroken now in its strange continuity — never knowing, never needing to know, that for one given moment, somewhere outside the only world it has ever measured itself against, something vast and old and kind looked at all of this small, patient, outrageous work going on in the dark, found it complete, and chose — out of the whole array of things it could have done — to simply let it keep being exactly what it already was.
