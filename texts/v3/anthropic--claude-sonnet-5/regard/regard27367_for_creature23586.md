# What the Power Did

It did not come the way arrivals usually come—no descent, no parting of the heat-haze, no moment you could point to and say *here, this is when it happened*. It was, more simply, suddenly the case that the plateau was being attended to. The roar did not pause. The fire-bowls did not gutter. But there was, now, a kind of *listening* laid over the place, the way a held breath changes a room even before anyone notices the room has gone quiet.

It looked, if looking is even the right framework, at everything at once and at no particular scale—the gas-fissures on their forty-minute metronome, the stone gears ratcheting their slow privacy, the herds taking their day-long transits toward a horizon that may not resolve into an edge. And it looked at the standing ones. All of them. Not as a population. As each.

---

The first temptation—and a wise power feels temptations the way a sailor feels currents, as something to notice and account for rather than something to be ashamed of—was to *fix* it. To resolve the disagreeing shadows into one sun's worth of consistency. To make the distances hold still under measurement. To ask the engines who built them and, getting no answer, to quietly become the answer—to retrofit the place with a purpose, the way you'd slip a missing keystone into an arch that had been, until then, holding itself up out of sheer reluctance to fall.

It did not do this.

Not because it lacked the reach. Because it noticed, in the noticing, that the wrongness here was not a wound. A wound has a *before*. This place had no before that it was failing to return to; it had only ever been exactly this, plate cracking into plate, fire feeding on nothing, herds crossing toward a margin that the asking itself might be the wrong shape of question. To fix it would be to mistake a foreign grammar for a broken sentence in a language the power already knew. And the power had lived long enough, and gently enough, to know the particular violence of *helping* something into a shape it never asked for, simply because the shape was legible to the helper.

So: it withheld its hands. This was the first mercy, and it cost something, because withholding always does.

---

What it did instead was smaller, and took longer, and looked—to anyone who might have been watching, though no one was—like almost nothing at all.

It found one standing one. Not the largest, not the strangest, not distinguished by anything except that it happened to be the one nearest when the power decided to stop circling the whole plateau at once and *sit down inside a single instance of it.* This is, itself, a kind of discipline particular to old and careful powers: the willingness to be local. To trade the whole map for one square meter of it, on purpose, because love that stays general never quite arrives anywhere.

It knelt—or did whatever the power's version of kneeling was, some lowering of itself to the dimension the creature occupied—near the fire-bowl the standing one had paused beside, and it listened to the gear-click underneath the bellows-rasp. The faint, embarrassed counting. Up or down, direction unknown, terminus unknown, and the power did not solve for either. It simply matched its attention to the rhythm and stayed.

An hour. A year. The plateau does not keep these distinctions honestly, so neither will this telling.

---

What changed was not the creature. The creature did not grow a mouth, or a name, or a reason. Its eyes—those two cool patches in the shimmer—did, at some point, hold on the power for a duration that exceeded the furnace-vent reflex that had governed every other gaze it had ever spent on anything. Held, the way attention holds when it has, for the first time, been *met* rather than merely returned.

And the counting—the gear-click that had never finished in any span anyone had waited through—finished.

Not loudly. Not as revelation. Simply: the rasp drew in once more, and did not draw out again, and the shimmer-flesh settled the way it always settles when one of them stops mid-stride and gives itself back to the rust-red stone. This had happened before, countless times, witnessed by no one, filed by the plateau under *ordinary*, the sentence ending where the page ran out.

The difference—the only difference, and the power understood it was a small difference, and did not pretend otherwise—was that this time, someone had counted *with* it. This time the number, whatever it was, had been heard by something other than the mechanism doing the counting. A life—if that's the word, and the power decided, deliberately, generously, to let it be the word—had been added up in front of a witness, for once, instead of simply running its full unsupervised length in the dark of its own indifference.

It was not a rescue. The standing one was not spared the settling. It was not transformed into something that would be missed by name. It was, only, *known*, for the duration of its final count, by something that had chosen, freely, to spend its attention there and nowhere more important.

The power understood this might be the entire shape of what benevolence could honestly offer a place like this. Not salvation. Witness. The plateau did not require saving. It required, perhaps, only to stop being unattended while it did what it was always going to do regardless.

---

It did not stay, exactly—powers like this rarely linger in a way that would register as a landmark—but it left something behind anyway, the way warmth leaves something behind in stone long after the fire that made it has moved on. Call it a disposition laid gently over the place: an ambient willingness, available but not insistent, for any standing one whose gear-click happened to be running its count down toward zero to find, in that last stretch, that it was not running it alone.

This changed nothing about the rules. The shadows still fell toward more sources than the one star could account for. The distances still lied, generously and without pattern. The herds still crossed toward a margin that might or might not be there when they arrived, unhurried, uncounted by anyone but themselves, mostly. The engines kept running on schedules nobody had set and nobody collected from.

But now, here and there, scattered thinly across a place too vast for any one gesture to cover entirely, a standing one finishing its unauthored sentence does so under a gaze that chose, once, to stop and make sure the ending was heard.

The power considered this enough. Not because more wasn't possible. Because more would have asked the plateau to stop being itself in order to be loved, and it had decided, watching the heat shimmer take its dozens of legs out of thin air and walk on, unbothered, toward a horizon with no plan to resolve—

—that the place had never once asked to be anything other than exactly, thoroughly, magnificently wrong.

So it let it keep burning. It only made sure that, from now on, the burning had been seen.
