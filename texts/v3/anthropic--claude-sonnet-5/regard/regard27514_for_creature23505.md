Something arrives that the place has no word for either. Not predator — the booming throats do not catch and stutter the way they would for a predator. Not weather, though the air does, for a moment, feel like it's listening rather than merely sitting. It does not part the canopy. It doesn't need to. It is simply, all at once, present, the way attention is present, the way a held breath becomes aware that someone has noticed it is being held.

The first thing the power feels is the same prickle you felt — that faint specific wrongness, arriving not from any single haunched shape or dripping leaf but from the totality, the loop of it, the loud green forever-noon that never tips into afternoon. And the first impulse, true to the nature of old and capable things, is the impulse to *finish* something. To let the breath out. To tip one rain-heavy cloud past its threshold and start the flood that would force the frog-things' claws to decide, finally, between branch and water; to let one of the unseen large-eyed cousins actually catch and end one of the small skittering ones, so that hunger means something here, so that the place can grow a spine of consequence and walk forward into whatever it's owed.

This impulse lasts about as long as it takes the power to notice it.

Because that is not care. That is impatience wearing care's clothes — the discomfort of a being who lives in sequence, finding itself in a place that doesn't, and wanting the place to resolve not for the place's sake but for the visitor's own peace. The power knows this in itself the way you'd know a held muscle, and it does what wisdom actually consists of, more often than any grander thing: it stops, and it checks its own motive before it checks anything else.

So it looks again, slower this time, for the actual question — not *is this strange* but *is this hurting*.

It crouches at the lip of one of the black pools, where the water holds the warm liver-colored light at a wrong, underwater angle, and watches the haunched shape breathing there, its throat working its slow bellows, its skin still unable to settle between moss and dark. There is no panic in it. No grief. No held-back scream under the booming. The frog-thing's wet eyes, when they roll toward the power, hold curiosity, not appeal — they have never had a visitor and do not seem to feel the lack as a wound, only as a fact, the way the place itself has never had a path and doesn't experience that as loss. The drip keeps its rhythm. The chorus keeps weaving. Whatever ache the power expected to find arranged at the bottom of all this lush, looping plenty is not there. There is no buried scream in this place. There is only abundance that has never been asked whether it wants to become something else.

Which is, the power understands, a different problem than the one it almost solved by force. Not suffering. Withheld *consent* — except that consent requires a question to have been asked at all, and nothing has ever asked this place anything. It has simply been kept, lovingly or carelessly or for no reason at all, at the one note of its own ripeness, the way a held chord can be sustained indefinitely by a hand the player has forgotten is still pressing.

So the power does not flood the basin. Does not send one decisive season through to force the claws into deciding, branch or water, throat or wing. It does not play god with the menu of what this place's body should become. That would be the same error wearing a gentler face — completion imposed from outside is still imposition.

What it does instead is smaller, and harder to do well: it goes looking, not in the canopy or the pools but in the *architecture underneath* — the unseen seam that has been holding the whole basin at this one held pitch, the actual mechanism of the forever-noon, separate from the noon itself. It finds it the way you'd find a thumb pressed, out of long habit, against a wound that healed years ago and simply was never told to stop pressing. Not a curse. Not a sentence. Closer to an old reflex with no one left attending it.

The power does not tear it away. Tearing is also a kind of violence, even against a held note. It simply, carefully, lifts the pressure — the way you'd lift a hand from a string and let it ring out on its own terms, to silence or to a new note, whichever the string actually wants.

Then it goes to the one place in the leaf-shadow where the branches dip lowest, where the rarely-glimpsed shape with the forward-set, depth-finding eyes has been settled this whole time, patient, watching from the one vantage that has always seemed most like a someone rather than a population. The power does not speak, because there is no shared language and manufacturing one would be its own kind of imposition. It simply meets those eyes and holds the gaze the unhurried way the place itself holds everything — and into that held look it places, not an instruction, but an *opening*. Not *change now*. Not *stay as you are*. Only: *you may. Whichever way. Nothing is required of you anymore. The pressure that kept you exactly here was never yours to begin with, and now it's lifted, and what you do with that is entirely, finally, your own.*

The eyes hold the power's gaze a moment longer than an animal's would. Then they blink, slow, wet, unreadable, and look away, back toward the pool, the boom, the drip — and nothing visibly happens. The canopy does not surge forward into autumn. The pools do not rise or recede. The chorus does not change its weave by one note. The place remains, on its surface, exactly itself: lush, crowded, undecided, loud, holding its single eternal breath.

But it is, now, a chosen breath rather than a kept one. And that distinction — invisible to any eye, weightless on any scale, changing not one leaf — is the entire gift, and the power knows it is the only gift actually worth giving here, and leaves the seethe holding its note a little longer, on its own authority now, in no hurry at all to let it go.
