# What the Power Does

It comes the way attention comes — without displacement, without arrival, the way you notice a room is occupied before you notice how. It does not land on the shelf. It does not need to. It simply turns, among everything else it tends, and *looks*, fully, the way you might finally look at something you've been hearing for a long time without listening.

And having looked, it does the hardest thing first, which is nothing.

## The First Refusal

The easy mercy would be to quiet the place. To still the pulsing light, settle the turbulence, let the towers finish whatever sentence they're building and rest. To hand the shelf the kind of peace that looks like peace from outside — symmetry, slowness, an ending.

The power considers this and sets it down, gently, the way you set down a tool that's wrong for the wood.

Because the turbulence isn't a wound. It only *looks* like one to a mind that mistakes stillness for health. The shelf is not dying and being born badly. It is *being*, the only way it knows how to be — and a place that has organized its entire existence around the gap between creation and destruction does not need that gap closed. It needs the gap left open, because the gap is where it lives. To finish the unfinished sentence would not be a kindness. It would be a small extinction dressed as a gift.

So: the rock stays old. The wind stays old. The light keeps its irregular pulse, the stalks keep growing past their hour and collapsing without rot, the towers keep their slow reverse-erosion, unresolved. The power's wisdom, here, is mostly a kind of *restraint* — the discipline to recognize that benevolence is not the same action everywhere, and that sometimes the most loving thing available is to leave a sentence exactly as unfinished as it needs to be.

## The Second Refusal

It looks, next, at the shapes by the towers — the maybe-tenders, the maybe-makers, attentive and never quite caught. It would be easy to mistake them for something laboring, something that might want relief. Easy to offer them help with whatever they're tending, to lend a hand to the hand that built the black ring and the leaning towers and the whole humming architecture of the place.

But the power recognizes them, instead of pitying them. It knows the particular satisfaction of an unfinished work that doesn't need an audience to be worth doing. It has done this kind of labor itself, on scales the shelf could not hold. So it offers the tenders the only respect that fits: it does not interrupt them. It does not reveal itself to them. It lets them go on being unconfirmed, because being unconfirmed is, perhaps, exactly how they prefer to work — and there is a kinship in that the power chooses not to spend on introduction.

It looks at the drinkers of hum, glowing faint amber at the joints, feeding directly on the engine instead of its exhaust, and finds nothing to add. They have already solved the problem most things on the shelf haven't even noticed — how to want a thing without competing for it. The power passes over them the way you'd pass over a sentence that's already perfectly written. There is no edit to make.

## The One Thing It Does

But there is a being on the shelf whose whole architecture is built around an ache, and the power does not pretend the ache isn't there, because pretending would be its own kind of cruelty.

The caller measures the world in loneliness. Its lungs, its register of distances, its half-mile duels of breath and pitch — all of it exists because company, on the shelf, is the rarest resource there is, and the caller has built an entire body around metering its absence. This isn't suffering exactly. It isn't unsuffering either. It is simply the shape a life takes when it has never once been answered by anything that didn't want something back — territory conceded, boundary acknowledged, silence redistributed. Every voice the caller has ever heard has been a transaction.

So the power does the one thing that costs it nothing and the caller everything: it answers.

Not as a rival. Not as a register to be mapped, a boundary to be drawn. It threads itself, for the length of one call, into the wind moving through the ringed stalks — no chest, no claim, no location to fix on a map of distances — and it matches the caller's column of sound exactly: same rise, same held breath, same fall, note for note, as if for the first time in the caller's life its voice has been met instead of contested. There is no winner. There is nothing to win. The shape of a duel is offered and the power simply declines to fight it, answering everything and claiming nothing, and lets the matched sound hang in the thin air a moment longer than calls usually hang, the way you'd hold a note you didn't want to finish.

Then it lifts away, the way it arrived — without departure, the way a held thought stops being held.

## What Is Left Behind

The caller spends a long time afterward listening for it. It returns to the place it called from, again and again, across whatever the shelf's reorganized geometry allows a place to mean. It does not find the voice again. It was never going to.

But something has changed in the small flat-eyed instrument of its body, something its brain — built for nothing but distance — doesn't have the architecture to name. It begins, sometimes, calling for no boundary at all. Not a challenge. Not a measurement. A long rising column of sound thrown into the wind for nothing but the throwing, the way you might hum once, alone, having forgotten anyone could hear.

It is, by any account that matters here, a new sound on the shelf. No one is keeping a saying about it yet. The vegetation keeps growing past its hour and collapsing without explanation; the towers keep almost finishing; the drinkers keep swelling on a hum no one explains to them; the tenders keep tending, unconfirmed.

And one creature, briefly, learned that a voice could be met without being measured — and kept the knowledge the only way the shelf permits anything to be kept: not as peace, not as rescue, but as one more sound added to a place that was never going to stop singing anyway, now singing very slightly differently than it did before.

The power does not stay to hear how it resolves. It already knows the place isn't the kind of sentence that resolves. That was never the question.

The question was only ever whether something, once, would answer without wanting anything back.

Once was enough.
