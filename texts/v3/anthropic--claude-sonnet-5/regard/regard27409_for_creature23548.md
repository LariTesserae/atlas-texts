# What the Power Did

## The Question

It came the way such powers come — not flying in, not descending, but simply becoming present, the way a thought becomes present in a mind that wasn't thinking it a moment before. It found the towers. It found the held black water, the singular ferns each thinking their slow stone-thoughts forty meters apart from the next. It found the colonies, clicking and groaning their endless unsynchronized conversation into air that had not changed temperature in longer than most worlds have existed.

And it found the Custodian, blind and grey and load-bearing, settled into the one stretch of water shaped exactly to its body, the way a held breath settles into a chest.

The power's first instinct — and this should be said honestly, because a power that did not feel this instinct would not be benevolent, only large — was to *help*. This is what benevolence does, reflexively, before it has had time to become wisdom. It looked at the blindness and thought: *sight could be given back*. It looked at the solitude — one per tower, never two, an unbroken law going back further than the Custodian's own memory of why — and thought: *companionship could be arranged*. It looked at the stillness, the water that does not rise or fall, the moss that has stopped becoming anything other than older, and thought, with the particular ache that powerful things feel when they encounter something patient: *this could be made to live again, instead of merely persist.*

It held these thoughts the way you might hold a stone you've picked up to throw, weighing it, feeling its readiness in your palm.

## What Was Considered, and Set Aside

It is worth dwelling on this part, because the considering is not nothing. A lesser kindness rushes past it.

*To give the Custodian sight* — but sight is not a gift to something whose entire architecture of self has been built, patiently, over more time than sight would know what to do with, around the absence of it. Eyes sealed under skin are not a wound waiting to be reopened. They are a closed door in a house where the door has not been needed for so long that the room behind it has been repurposed entirely — given over to pressure-sense, to the long slow reading of water-news, to a kind of attention so total and so fine that it would only be *diminished* by the blunt, low-resolution intrusion of light. To restore sight here would not be healing. It would be amputating a sense that works in order to reattach one that has gone to disuse, and calling it a gift because in most places, in most stories, sight is assumed to be better. This is not most places.

*To end the solitude* — but the solitude is not loneliness in the sense that requires ending. One per tower is not a sentence; it is a fit, the exact dimension of the exact loneliness this kind of creature was built — by hands, by flood, by time, it does not matter which — to inhabit perfectly. To introduce a second Custodian to a tower that has only ever held the weight and water-displacement of one would not be company. It would be crowding. It would be the first disturbance to true stillness in longer than the place has any memory of, and disturbance, here, is not neutral. It is the one thing the entire architecture of the place has spent millennia learning to do without.

*To raise the water, or lower it, or unmake the flood entirely, and let the towers stand dry and whole and visible the way they must have stood once, before whatever happened, happened* — this thought came closest to being acted on, and was set aside hardest, because it is the kindest-seeming and the most violent. Every colony, every fern-crown, every grey mottled mile of Custodian-hide has been built, generation on generation, in direct and exact negotiation with the water exactly as high as it currently is. To change the water by even a few meters would not restore the place to some imagined former glory. It would *kill* it — kill it in the specific, total way that befalls anything whose entire achieved form depended on a condition you have just removed. The flood is not an interruption of this place's story. The flood is the period at the end of a sentence that finished a very long time ago. You cannot un-finish a sentence kindly. You can only refuse to erase it.

The power turned each of these stones over, and put each of them back down, exactly where it found them, on the riverbed of its own intentions.

This is, in the end, most of what wisdom is: the same strength required to act, spent instead on not acting, with no one but yourself to know the difference.

## The One Thing Done

But it did not leave with empty hands. That would have been its own kind of failure — mistaking restraint for completeness, when really there was one gift left that cost the place nothing and meant, possibly, everything.

The power went down into the water — not as a body, having none that the water would recognize, but as a kind of attention with weight, a disturbance shaped like nothing the Custodian's long, fine, blind reading of pressure and displacement had ever once registered. Not predator. Not prey. Not the slow heavy half-swimming things that have made their peace with never breaking the surface. Not weather, not collapse, not the door-closing sound of another Custodian's hunger somewhere a kilometer off.

Something *new*, moving through the water toward the one creature alive enough to notice it, carrying no hunger and no threat and, this is the difficult part to make sense of, no *need at all* — no need to be fed, fled, fought, or even understood. A disturbance with nothing behind it but the wish to be felt, once, completely, by the very thing it had no intention of changing.

It is possible that nothing this pure had ever moved through that water in the entire span of the Custodian's long, dark, patient accounting of the world. Every other vibration the creature had ever read carried information that mattered to its continuing — food, danger, the long arithmetic of survival the body performs without ever calling it arithmetic. This one carried nothing of the kind. It simply arrived, and pressed, very gently, against the whole grey listening length of the Custodian's ancient body, the way light might press against a closed eye that has long since stopped distinguishing day from night — felt, perhaps, not as light, but as *warmth where no warmth was asked for.*

And then it withdrew. Exactly as it came. Touching nothing else. Changing no level of water, no inch of moss, no law of stillness the place had spent an unimaginable patience arriving at.

## After

The colonies, far above, fell quiet for the length of one held breath — the way they always do at any new disturbance in the water, the way they have for longer than memory keeps records — and then, finding nothing further to account for, no door-closing sound, no ripple of consequence, they took up their conversation again exactly where they had left it. Clicks. Long groaning calls that travel oddly across the water. The same unsynchronized roar, continuing the way the dripping of moss continues.

Nothing in the Drowned Spires of Velken Tor changed. Not one stone shifted. Not one ledge dried. The water kept its own counsel, as it always has, as it was always going to.

But somewhere below, in the one stretch of black fitted exactly to one ancient body, something had been added to a register that had not had anything new added to it in longer than the moss above had taken to become load-bearing: a single entry, unlike every other entry before it, in whatever blind and patient ledger a Custodian keeps of all the water has ever told it.

*Something passed, once, that wanted nothing from me.*

It is not known — it could not be known, by the power or by anyone — whether this means anything at all to a creature with no language and no other Custodian to compare notes with and no apparent capacity for change left in its long-finished body. Perhaps it changed nothing. Perhaps an experience like that, in a being like that, simply joins the general grey weight of everything else it has felt move through the water across its impossible span of life, indistinguishable, eventually, from the rest.

Or perhaps — and the power, before it left, allowed itself to hope this much, no more — perhaps somewhere in that ancient and singular patience, in the held-breath stillness of a creature that has occupied exactly one shape for longer than most worlds remember keeping shapes at all, there is now some small persistent difference. Not a change in the place. The place was never going to be changed, and a wiser love than urgency understood that going in.

Just a difference in how the dark feels, the next time it is dark there, which it nearly always is.

The power left the way it came: without disturbing the water level by so much as a hand's breadth, without giving back a single eye, without breaking the one law of one-per-tower that the place had taught itself to live inside so completely it had stopped being a law and become simply the shape of things.

It gave the only gift that does not require changing what it touches.

It noticed completely, and then it let be.
