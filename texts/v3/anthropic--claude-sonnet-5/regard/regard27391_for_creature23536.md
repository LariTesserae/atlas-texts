The power comes the way light comes to a room when, after a long time shut, a door is finally opened from the inside — not an intrusion, but an answering. It does not announce itself with wind or radiance or any of the gestures that would mean something to a flatland village with sky overhead and water falling free. It understands, almost immediately, that to arrive *loudly* here would itself be the first harm. So it does what the place itself has taught it to do, before it does anything else: it slows down. It matches its own vast and patient attention to the tempo of rising rock, of decade-repairs, of an elder's shrug that means *that's not what finishing means here.* It spends what would be, elsewhere, a single held breath, simply watching.

It watches the girl cross the pipe-bridge forty stories up at the unhurried pace of someone crossing a room. It watches a cistern-ward's hands move through the morning gutter-check with the same unconscious reverence a flatland priest might bring to an altar, and understands that this *is* the altar, that the prayer was never separate from the plumbing. It watches the hand-cant flicker across a factory floor too loud for voices, a whole fluent language folded small enough to fit between thumb and wrist, and it is moved — genuinely, which for a power of this age is rare — by the economy of it, the refusal to waste anything, even sound.

And it watches the Face-Crews come up from the lightless chambers with that particular flatness settling further into their features, year by year, and nobody asking, and it understands that this is the place where the cost of the whole vigilant, patient, magnificent arrangement is being paid, quietly, by people who have agreed never to discuss the bill.

So the power considers, as such powers do, what it might *give.*

It considers stilling the mountain — and discards the thought almost as fast as it arrives, because a people built entirely around outrunning a process would not be saved by the process stopping. They would be orphaned by it. Their gait, their guilds, their cistern-prayers, the whole inherited vigilance passed down like a family trait — all of it is an answer to a question. Remove the question and you do not get peace; you get a city that has forgotten what its own hands are for.

It considers giving them rain — true rain, soil, green, the ordinary mercy of a wetter sky. And discards that too, more slowly, with something like regret, because it sees how the scarcity has become *meaning* here: the cup of condensate is the realest trust they have, the clearest sentence their whole language can produce. A power that floods that meaning with abundance is not generous. It is erasing.

It considers simply taking the Face-Crews' burden from them — emptying the lightless chambers, sealing them, ending the unspoken cargo and whatever waits at the working face. And this is the one it lingers on longest, because it would be so easy, and because easy mercy is the kind that benevolent powers are most tempted toward and most often wrong about. But it watches the guild a while longer and sees that the Face-Crews do not want rescue. They want, if they want anything namable at all, not to be so *alone* in it.

So the power does not fix the mountain, or the sky, or the silence. It does something smaller, and harder, and far more like the place itself: it goes and *listens*, all the way down, where no one outside one guild has gone in generations, and it does not look away.

It rides down with an ordinary shift, unseen, the way attention can be present without occupying space. It is there when the deep vent gives its unscheduled sigh and the whole crew goes still in that particular way the mid-terraces have learned not to ask about. And in that stillness the power finally turns its attention to the mountain itself — not as obstacle, not as resource, but as the vast slow thing it actually is: a body still being born, growing the only way it knows how, with no more malice in it than there is malice in a bone lengthening in a child's leg. The power asks it, in whatever language passes between things that old, *what are you doing, down here, in the dark, fraction by fraction* — and the mountain answers the only way it can, which is almost not an answer at all: a degree of warmth, a particular grain in the rock, a rhythm, vast and slow and *patterned*, where the pitch-folk had only ever been able to feel patternless threat.

It is not much. It is barely a sentence, translated from geology into something a person could hold. But it is *true*, and it had never been given to anyone before, because no one had ever stayed still long enough at the right depth, in the right dark, to receive it.

The power does not announce this gift either. It does not change the weather, or the chimneys' color, or the angle of any vent. It leaves the one true thing where the culture's own grammar can find it and carry it forward in the slow, generational, preventative way it carries everything else — and so, sometime later, on a night that looks like every other night, a Face-Crew elder tells an apprentice, in the flat economical register the work has taught them both, something about the deep sigh that nobody had been able to say before: not what it *is*, exactly, but that it keeps a rhythm, that it is closer to breathing than to hunger, that the dark has a shape and the shape is not aimed at them.

The apprentice does not smile, because that is not how this guild looks. But they go still, next time, in a way that is a fraction less like dread.

That is the whole of the intervention. No rain. No stilled rock. No rescue. A single fact, handed down the way a cup of condensate is handed down — earned, costly, small, and entirely real — into a culture that already knew, better than the power did when it arrived, exactly how much weight a true gift is supposed to carry, and how little.

And then, having given the one thing that could be given without unmaking what it touched, the power withdraws the way it arrived: slowly, without ceremony, careful even in leaving not to cast a shadow longer than the gift itself. The mountain goes on rising, fractions of a degree, fractions of a meter, year over year. The city goes on building one anchoring-phase ahead of it, as it always has.

But somewhere in the lightless chambers, on the next unscheduled exhale, someone is listening to the ground the way they always have — and for the first time in longer than anyone living can remember, the listening is not entirely alone.
