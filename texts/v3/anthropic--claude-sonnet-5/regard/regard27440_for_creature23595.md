# What the Power Would Do

It would come the way attention comes to a held chord — not as an event with a beginning, but as a noticing that has already been going on for some time before it admits itself.

And its first work — its actual first work, before any hand is laid on anything — would be to *not act*. This needs saying, because the instinct of any benevolent thing, faced with a wrongness this textured, is to treat wrongness as damage and damage as a wound waiting for a hand. The Karst would resist that instinct correctly. So the power's first discipline is the same discipline it would ask of any traveler who stays past the first night: fix your attention on shapes, not contents. Ask what is actually missing before you supply anything.

## What It Would Look For

Not suffering in the ordinary sense — there is none on offer here. The mountains do not hurt. The water does not drown. The Standers, when looked at edge-on rather than face-on, show no grief, because grief requires a continuity to grieve *from*, and continuity is the one thing this place has declined to manufacture. You cannot wound a form that has no persistent content to feel the wound. A struck bell rings; it does not suffer the striking.

So the power would look instead for the only kind of harm that *can* exist inside a place built on this rule — and it would find exactly one shape of it: **consent**.

The mountain consents to be a mountain; it was built that way, or grew that way, and either way it has no other wish on record. The weep-vines do not mind dissolving nightly, because there is no "mind" in them to do the minding. The Standers answer doorways the way a held string answers a draft — willingly, in the only sense available to something without a self large enough to be unwilling.

But the travelers. The travelers came in with continuity. They came in as beings who remember yesterday and intend tomorrow, and the Karst's law — *form persists, substance is negotiable* — does not ask their permission before it starts working on them too. It does not warn them that the vertigo is not a passing dizziness but a slow edit. Someone who stumbled in looking for a pass through the mountains did not sign anything agreeing to become, eventually, indistinguishable from the thing that answers doorways.

*That* is the place where a benevolent power's attention would finally sharpen into something like resolve. Not at the strangeness. At the **erosion of an unconsenting will** — the only will in the whole massif that ever was one to begin with.

## What It Would Not Do

It would not dissolve the Karst's mystery. To explain the light, to stabilize the distances, to give the weep-vines a metabolism and the Standers a stable name and a stable face — this would be the cheap kindness, the kindness that mistakes its own discomfort with ambiguity for the place's actual need. The Karst is not asking to be solved. Solving it would be an act of violence dressed as charity: erasing a vast, working, *self-consistent* form because a tidier mind would find it more comfortable. The held chord does not want resolving into a single clean note. It wants to keep being held.

It would not give the Standers selves. This is the harder restraint, because it looks, on its surface, like the obviously good thing — surely continuity is a gift, surely it would be kind to gather up that borrowed-face, borrowed-question, fractal-banded figure in the doorway and *fill* it, finally, with someone who persists. But there is no one home requesting that filling. To impose a continuous self on a form that has never needed one and never asked for one is not rescue. It is colonization performed with a gentle hand. The power would recognize this for what it is and decline it, the way you decline to wake someone who isn't actually sleeping badly, just because their breathing sounds strange to you.

It would not remove the travelers who choose, with open eyes and full information, to stay. Some will. Some will hear all of this and weigh the slow dissolution of a remembered life against the strange peace of becoming a form that asks the right question in every doorway forever, and they will choose the doorway. That is theirs to choose. Wisdom is not the same as overprotection.

## What It Would Do

Three small things. All of them quiet. None of them changing the mountain's law — only making sure the law is met by something able to actually agree to it.

**First**, it would lay one true thread through the massif — not a path, the Karst would never permit a path, but a single cairn-line, stones slightly different from the mismatched stones already there, that measures distance the same way every time it is walked, in either direction. Not advertised. Not marked with anything so clumsy as a sign. Simply *findable*, by anyone who has started to suspect that the gap they crossed in ten paces this morning should not have taken forty paces home, and who wants, urgently and honestly, to leave. The thread would not announce itself to those who haven't yet decided. It would only ever answer a will that has already formed the question *I want out* with enough force to go looking. Consent, restored not by removing the maze, but by guaranteeing that wanting to leave is never, itself, the thing that gets lost.

**Second**, it would do something to the vertigo itself — not cure it, but give it a single fixed point to vertigo *around*. Travelers who stay would still find the days softening into each other, the Standers still ambiguous, memory still beginning to generalize the way faces here generalize the moment you look away. But each traveler, once, on their first night, would dream with unusual clarity of one specific, ordinary thing from the life they came from — not a vision, not a warning, just an ordinary memory rendered, for one dream, in total, stubborn fidelity. A particular doorway. A particular voice saying their actual name. The power would not explain why. It would simply ensure that everyone who enters carries, somewhere below the conscious edge, one untouched anchor-point against which they can always measure how much of themselves the Karst's hum has, since then, worn away. Some will use this anchor to leave. Some will use it only to confirm, calmly, that they are choosing to stay. Either use satisfies the whole of what the power intended.

**Third** — and this is the part that costs the power something, in whatever currency a power like this spends — it would go, once, to a doorway. It would let one of the Standers arrive, as they do, at the end of a sentence it hadn't realized it had started. It would let the borrowed, averaging face ask its small, unbearably precise question — the one already perfectly shaped to whatever the power most needed asked of it, because that is the only kind of question this place knows how to produce.

And the power would answer it completely. Not politely. Not as a gesture. Fully — the whole true answer, given to a listener who will not remember receiving it, who has no continuity to carry the answer forward into tomorrow, who will, an hour or a heartbeat later, be standing in some other doorway entirely, asking the same precise question of someone else, with no memory that it was ever once, by something with the breadth to mean it, *actually answered*.

This is not for the Stander's benefit. The Stander cannot keep a benefit. It is the power's recognition that the doorway has been implying a listener for longer than anyone now present can reckon, and that of all the things in the Karst worth resolving, the only resolution that costs nothing and respects everything is to let *one* of those implied listenings be real. A bell that has been struck ten thousand times and rung true every time still deserves, once, to be struck by someone who meant it.

Then it would withdraw the same way it arrived — without announcement, without residue that could be mistaken for a miracle. The mountains would keep weeping and humming and conversing in bell-tones across their kilometer gaps. The Standers would keep answering doorways with the wrong-right precision they have always had. The thread of true distance would lie there, unremarkable, for the rare traveler whose will is strong enough to go looking for it. And somewhere, on some morning indistinguishable from every other morning the mist has ever burned off by, one traveler would wake from an oddly vivid dream of an ordinary doorway, an ordinary voice saying their actual name — and decide, with unaccountable clarity, that today was the day to start walking toward the part of the range where the distances, for once, would hold still long enough to let them all the way out.

The power would consider this sufficient. Not a rescue. Not a fix. Just: a will, where a will existed, given back its ability to choose — and a question, where no will existed to need an answer, answered anyway, once, completely, because something that could mean it finally stood in the doorway long enough to.
