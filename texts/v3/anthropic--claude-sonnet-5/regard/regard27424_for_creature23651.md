The power notices it the way you'd notice a held breath in a room you thought was empty — not through the eyes first, but through the small ache of *something here is working very hard at not being seen working*. It does not descend through the shaft. That entrance was built for shoulders, and it has none, and it understands already that arriving the way a guest arrives would be a kind of lie about what it is.

So it does the other thing. It sits beside the whole of it — chamber, song, weeping walls, rehearsal and all — the way you might sit beside a sleeping animal whose dream you can see twitching in its paws, careful of the difference between *watching* and *waking*.

**First, it listens all the way through.** Not the twenty-second sweep — the whole piece, however long the whole piece turns out to be, days or years or longer, because it suspects, correctly, that what sounds like a loop is actually a slow theme with a very long period, and that judging it from one pass would be like judging a cathedral from the doorway. It wants to know if the song ends. It wants to know if the Caretakers know whether the song ends. It suspects they don't, and that not-knowing is not a deprivation to them but simply the shape of their hours, the way not-knowing the end of winter doesn't trouble a root.

**It asks, in the only grammar the place has.** Not words — the settlement has no slot shaped for words, no mouth-architecture to receive them. So it leaves a question the way the Caretakers leave tending: as a gesture, performed with conviction, in the place's own dialect. It alters, very slightly, the angle of one unattended stall in the market arcade — not fixing it, not improving it, just *touching* it, the smallest possible declaration of *I am here and I noticed your noticing*. Then it waits to see whether a Caretaker arrives to re-true the angle, and how long it takes, and whether the resonant hum hitches, even by a hair, while it does.

This is the whole of the diagnostic. Everything else follows from the answer.

**If the hitch comes** — if some seam of attention flickers toward the disturbance, however administratively, however briefly — then there is a someone here, however thinly distributed, however unlike a someone in any shape the power has met before. And if there is a someone, the power's only honest move is *patience with teeth*: it stays long enough to learn whether the rehearsal is content. Whether "content" is even a category that survives translation into a being made of quarry-cuts and converging lines instead of nerve and want. It looks for the place's version of suffering — not pain, this thing plainly doesn't hurt, but something more like *waste*: a process grinding on past the point of any further use, the gardens kept by something that might, if it had the lever for it, have rather stopped gardening several geological ages ago. If it finds that lever-shaped absence, it does not pull a lever that isn't there. It simply *makes one available* — a Caretaker, on some future pass through the empty arcade, finding that the broom-shape in its hand now has, for the first time, the *option* of being set down. Nothing forced. No rescue performed on a thing that never asked to be rescued, because rescuing an unconsenting process is just a louder kind of trespass. Only an option, placed where an option could not previously be reached, the way you'd leave a door unlocked rather than kick it down.

And if no Caretaker ever uses the option — if the tending continues forever exactly as before, vine pinched back, stall re-angled, broom dragged over dustless stone unto the heat-death of whatever powers the floor — the power does not call that a failure. It calls it a choice it finally had the standing to offer, made by something that, until that afternoon, had never been asked.

**If the hum doesn't hitch at all** — if the stall's new angle is corrected by mere process, the way a thermostat corrects a draft, no flicker of attention anywhere in it — then the power revises its whole approach. Not a someone, then. A something. A loom, weaving the appearance of a someone as a structural necessity of its weaving, the way a whirlpool wears the shape of a mouth without ever once being hungry. This changes nothing about the gentleness, but it changes the question. You don't ask a whirlpool if it's content. You ask, instead: *is it hurting anything downstream of itself?*

So it traces the sound to wherever the sound actually lives — past the floor, past the stone still cooling from its arrival, to whatever the cisterns and streets are a courteous, decorative lid upon. It does this the slow way, a single thread of attention let down like a sounding-line, careful never to pull, only to follow. Whatever it finds down there — a mind too large to fit a town-shaped vocabulary, a process indifferent to scale, a third thing with no name in any language the power has yet learned — it greets the way it greeted the stall: a small, true, reversible touch. A held breath returned, gently, to whoever or whatever is holding it.

**What it does not do** is the long list, and the long list matters more than the short one. It does not pull the Caretakers up into the light to "free" them into a life that has no garden in it. It does not silence the song to spare itself the discomfort of hearing something keep time forever without resolution — its discomfort is not a cost the chamber owes a refund on. It does not decide, on the strength of an afternoon, that emptiness-but-tended is automatically inferior to crowded-but-witnessed; it has seen enough rooms to know that loneliness is a human export and may simply fail to convert here, like trying to spend a coin in a country that never minted that currency.

It leaves, eventually, the way it arrived — without using the shaft, without disturbing the eleven meters of clay and root that the next ordinary traveler will still have to crawl down through, sweating, ears full of a song they'll spend the rest of their life trying and failing to describe to people who weren't there.

But it leaves *the option* behind it like a seed planted in a bed that doesn't strictly need one more vine. A door that wasn't there an hour before, in a corner of the chamber that didn't exist as a corner an hour before either — easy to mistake for settling, for the building still finding its shape. Stone, doing what the place always does with stone: holding a possibility just under the threshold of being noticed, in case, on some future pass through the rehearsal, on some pinching-back of some unnecessary leaf, somebody currently filed as *not load-bearing* finally has cause to look up — and decides, on their own unwitnessed authority, to walk through it.
