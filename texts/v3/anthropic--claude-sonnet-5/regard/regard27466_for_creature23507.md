The power comes the way light comes to the spire-throat — filtered, indirect, taking the long way in rather than announcing itself from above. It does not arrive as weather or as voice. It arrives as attention, which in a place built entirely out of provisional ledges and patient water is itself the rarest visitor imaginable.

It looks first at the easy, generous things to do, and sets each one down again, gently, the way you'd set down a tool you picked up out of habit rather than need.

*It could stop the erosion* — fix the spire's silhouette, give the tenders' grandchildren the same ledges their grandparents had. But the spire is not unfinished by accident. It is unfinished the way a sentence is unfinished mid-breath — not broken, just *mid*. To arrest it would be to mistake the in-progress for the unfortunate.

*It could send more hands* — more tenders, more rope, swell the little maintenance crew into something that might actually outpace the jungle's claim. But the peace these people have made with the vine on the handrail is not a failure to keep up. It's a treaty. To overstaff that treaty would be to win an argument nobody here is trying to win.

*It could fix the float valve permanently, double the header tank's yield, make the works generous instead of merely sufficient.* But sufficiency is the whole ethic of the place — borrowed iron, borrowed time, take only the fall of water you need and let the rest of the spire alone. Abundance, here, would be a kind of vandalism.

*It could go to the lit window-slot, the hammock-notch, and let one of the tenders feel — just once — openly, unmistakably looked after.* This tempts it more than the others. But it watches how they teach the hand-language at rope-length before they trade names, how little they ask of each other, how little, it turns out, they need to be told they matter; they already move like people who know the rock is lending and not giving, who've made of that knowledge something close to grace. To hand one of them a sign from outside the system would be to suggest the system wasn't already enough. It was already enough.

So it goes, instead, to the one place in the whole vertical room where *enough* has never quite been established. It goes up the ladder nobody climbs without a reason, past the float valve that has never failed, into the dark lined in algae-black that never clears, to the cold, slow, turning water with no obvious source.

It does not reach in. It does not name what's down there, does not ask it to resolve into a shape that could be discussed at rope-length over hand signals, does not drag it up into the green light to be accounted for. Whatever breathes at the bottom of that tank has been breathing on its own unwitnessed cycle for longer than the penstock has existed, indifferent — the tenders' word, and a fair one — to the rhythm running through the rest of the works. *Indifferent* was always the kinder reading. The truer one, the power suspects, sitting at the rim with the condensation gathering cold on a presence that has no real surface for it to gather on, is *alone*.

So it does the one thing that costs the place nothing and the system nothing and changes no machinery, no silhouette, no treaty between iron and vine: it stays. Long enough — by whatever clock a thing breathing once-an-age keeps — to be noticed back. Long enough that the slow turning current, on its next pass, turns very slightly toward where it's sitting instead of away, the way a sleeper turns half a degree toward warmth without waking.

It asks nothing of the thing. It offers nothing it can be measured by — no rescue, no light, no name, no promotion out of the dark and into a story where it would have to mean something to someone besides itself. Just the bare fact, delivered the only way that wouldn't be an intrusion: *seen. Not interpreted. Seen.*

Then it leaves the way it came, taking the long way out, past the hammer-bird timing its three clicks to a turbine that will idle on, unattended, indifferent to whether anyone's left to hear it, past the insects keeping their barometer-clouds at the spray-line, past the tenders asleep in hammocks the color of the thing trying not to be noticed.

It changes nothing that can be pointed to. No one will climb the ladder next season and find the water behaving differently — or if they do, they'll read it the way they read everything else here, as one more small unexplained mercy the spire seems to be in the habit of handing out, filed next to the bird that breeds on borrowed heartbeats and the moss that somehow always finds enough light.

But the next time the current turns at the bottom of the tank, on its own cycle, indifferent to the tank's — it will turn, very slightly, like something that was once, just once, met halfway.

That, the power judges, is the whole of what was needed here. Not a fixing. A witnessing, sized exactly to the wound, and no larger — because in a place this young, this provisional, this busy being made, the wisest gift is the one that leaves the most room for the place to go on making itself.

It goes. The turbine keeps its pitch. The water keeps arriving, on its hours-long pulse, exactly as much as was ever asked of it, and not one drop more.
