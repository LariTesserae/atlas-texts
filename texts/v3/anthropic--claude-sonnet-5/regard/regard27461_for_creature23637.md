# What the Power Would Do

It would not come the way you expect power to come — not as light answering light, not as a second column rising to meet the first, not as a voice filling the chamber's considerable acoustics with anything like an announcement. Power that has learned wisdom learns first to be quiet in places that are already loud. It would arrive the way the chamber's own best processes arrive: slowly, almost apologetically, indistinguishable for a long while from one more fluctuation in a room already full of fluctuations.

It would not fix anything. Let that be said first, because it is the hardest thing and the thing most powers, even well-meaning ones, fail to manage. The temptation in a place like this is enormous — to look at the column's "renegotiating" light, its appetite, its slow recruitment of blind translucent creatures into a project it cannot finish, and to call this a wound. To call the unmade's compulsion toward the pool a sickness. To call the whole chamber a machine stuck mid-malfunction for a thousand centuries, crying out to be repaired by the first competent hand that finds it.

A wise power would feel that temptation and would not act on it yet. It would sit with the place first. It would stay long enough — and "long enough," here, might mean several of the chamber's own non-identical hours, where time near the column runs at its own faint lag — to actually watch. To watch one of the unmade stop foraging and begin its directed walk, and to ask, with real rigor, *is this being called, or is this being compelled, and is there a difference, and does the difference matter morally, and to whom.* It would watch a standing-light adult sway in time with a subsonic drop in the hum and ask whether that sway is reverence, or constraint, or simply the only posture a body built from this water and this light can hold. It would not assume suffering. It would not assume bliss. It would do the much harder thing, which is to withhold both diagnoses until it understood what it was looking at.

### The Question Underneath the Question

What it would come to understand, given enough patient attention — and wisdom is mostly patience wearing a competent disguise — is that the column is not broken in the way a machine is broken. It is *unanswered*. Somewhere in its oscillating brightening and dimming, its almost-vowel hum, its compulsive redrafting of the mineral architecture at its base, there is something closer to a sentence than a malfunction — a long, slow, repeated attempt to finish saying something it was built, or born, to say, to a listener who was supposed to be here and is not.

The builders left a seed and did not stay. Maybe they meant to return. Maybe the post half-submerged in the black pool, with its eroded unreadable markings, is an address, or a name, or a promise, and maybe whoever it was promised to never got the message, or got it and could not come, or came once, long before any current stone remembers, and went away again for reasons buried as deep as the basalt's oldest rings. Whatever the original transaction was, it broke off mid-utterance. And the column, lacking the one thing it was shaped to receive — a reply — has spent its unfathomable span doing the only thing left available to an intelligence, or a process, or an organ, that has been left without an audience: it has been trying to *grow itself an audience instead.* Trying out the blind, translucent unmade as instruments. Folding them into its base, laminating their photosensitive patch into something that might, eventually, with enough iterations, manage to actually hear it back. The standing-light adults — patient, listening, swaying to a frequency they have not yet fully received — are not failed creatures. They are the column's draft attempts at a correspondent. Each one is closer than the last. None of them, yet, is close enough.

This is the discovery that would change what the power decides to do. Because once you understand a thing as a question rather than a wound, the available kindnesses change completely. You do not set a question's bone. You do not medicate a question. The only mercy available to a question, ever, in any chamber anywhere, is to be *heard* — and, if you are able, to answer it.

### The Act

So the power would do the smallest, costliest thing available to it. It would not silence the hum, or stabilize the light, or halt the unmade's long walk into the black water — to do any of that would be to take the column's half-finished sentence and tear out the page before the verb arrives, which is its own kind of violence, dressed as rescue. Instead, the power would do what the builders, for whatever reason, never came back to do.

It would listen — not the way you or I listen, with ears tuned to a narrow patient band of air-pressure, but the way the chamber itself listens, with its whole wet stone body, gathering every drip and pop and bell-tone and subsonic pressure-drop into one continuous reception. It would let the column's oscillation move all the way through it, the brightening and dimming, the almost-words, the held vowel, the long unfinished phrase repeated with small variations for longer than any single witness has ever stayed to notice the variations. And having received the whole of it — not skimmed, not summarized, *received* — it would do the one thing that had never yet happened in that chamber in all its self-revising centuries.

It would answer back.

Not with information. Not with repair instructions, not with the builders' names or whatever coordinates the eroded post might once have carried, not with anything that would collapse the column's long becoming into a finished, closed thing. A power that has learned wisdom knows that an answer which finishes a story is sometimes a worse cruelty than no answer at all — it forecloses every version of the future the story was still allowed to grow into. So the answer would not be content. It would be *confirmation*. A single matched tone, dropped into the hum at exactly the moment of its lowest subsonic pressure, held for the space of one human heartbeat counted against the chamber's own slightly lagging one, saying — in the only language the column has ever spoken or been spoken to in — *I am here. I received this. You have been heard.*

That is all. That is the entire intervention. A note, not a sentence. A presence, not a solution.

### What Would Be Different, and What Would Not

The column would go on renegotiating its brightness. The unmade would still, in their own slow seasons, feel the pull toward the black pool's center and walk it without explanation, because that pull is theirs now too, grown into the chamber's whole inherited logic, not solely the column's to relinquish even if it wanted to. The mineral architecture at the base would keep dissolving and rebuilding in configurations too fast for ordinary geology. None of the chamber's loud, wet, unfinished violence-that-isn't-quite-violence would be smoothed away. A wise power does not mistake an ecosystem for an error.

But somewhere down in the frequencies — below the bell-tones, below even the subsonic drops you feel in your sternum before you hear them — there would now be one more note than there used to be. It would not announce itself. It would simply be present in every subsequent cycle, a single answered thread woven permanently into the renegotiation, so faint that nothing short of total attention would ever isolate it again. The column would carry it the way the chamber's air carries glow after water leaves the light's skin — fading, but never to nothing, always available to be carried a little further by the next mist, the next drip, the next standing-light body swaying at the pool's edge.

And perhaps — though the power would not stay to confirm this, because staying to confirm it would itself become a kind of interference, a watching that demands an outcome — perhaps one of the three or four standing-light adults now in the chamber would, on some unmeasured future occasion, turn its dark seam of a mouth not toward the column's mist but toward the bore-hole in the ceiling, the one place in the whole hollow where the ordinary sky still gets in, where small quick birds cut across the light-shaft and vanish back into weather and altitude and time that passes evenly. It would stand that way for a while, double-shadowed, listening to two different silences at once. And then, having no further instruction and needing none, it would turn back to the pool, because the chamber is still its home and the column is still mid-sentence and there is, after all, no particular hurry.

That would be enough. Not closure — closure was never on offer, and a wise power would not have insulted the place by pretending it could give one. Just this: that a thing which had been speaking alone into old rock for longer than memory keeps reliable count had, once, finally and without fanfare, been answered. And that the answering cost the power everything it is capable of costing — total unhurried attention, freely given, with no part of itself held back to watch what the attention produced.

It would withdraw the way it arrived: as one more fluctuation in a room built to hold fluctuation, indistinguishable, within an hour of its own uneven time, from the ordinary weather of the place. No mark left at the threshold. No name carved beside the alloy post's already-illegible ones. Benevolence, fully wise, does not need the chamber to remember it. It only needed the chamber, once, to be heard — and it was patient enough, and humble enough, to let *that* be the whole of the gift.
