The power comes the way weather comes — without announcement, without a body to announce. It does not descend on the Raw Range so much as it begins, gradually, to be paying attention to it, the way you might realize partway through a long silence that you've been listening for some time already.

What it finds first is not the mountain. It's the *tense* of the place — the grammar of it. Everything here is happening in a continuous present that refuses to resolve into past tense. The peaks are not having risen; they are rising. The water is not flowing; it is *deciding*. Even the small pale creatures in their burrows are not adapted so much as *adapting*, mid-stride, generation by generation, toward a slope that keeps changing the terms of the test. The power, which has seen a great many finished things — forests with their treaties of light already signed, rivers that have long since agreed on where to go — finds this unfinishedness almost unbearably moving. There is a tenderness available here that finished places no longer offer: the tenderness owed to something that could still go either way.

It considers, briefly, the obvious mercies. It could still the scree, lock the slopes into permanence, spare the small things their burrows-abandoned-by-instinct, their hours of dread before a shift they can feel but not explain. It could warm the air half a degree and let the seeping water finally choose a channel and become, simply, a river, with banks, with permission to be charted. It could press gently on the deep pulse beneath the range the way you'd press a hand to a stranger's back to steady their breathing — *there, there, you don't have to finish so slowly, I can help carry this.*

It does none of these things, and the reasoning is not coldness. The reasoning is this: a thing still deciding its own shape has a right to the deciding. To smooth the scree now would not be to spare the mountain its violence — it would be to write the mountain's ending before the mountain has read its own middle. The power has lived long enough, in whatever way a power lives, to know the difference between *helping something become* and *becoming something on its behalf*, and it has seen too many old, finished, smoothed-over ranges that were never asked which shape they wanted. Wisdom, here, looks almost identical to restraint. Benevolence has to find some other door in.

So it finds the smaller doors. It does not touch the pulse beneath the mountain — does not even let itself guess, with any confidence, whether that vastness is dying or being born, whether the herds above are its children or its strangers, because to decide that question on the creature's behalf would be a kind of theft, the theft of its own unanswered question, which may be the most important thing it owns. Instead the power turns its attention to the edges, the places where a small mercy doesn't presume to finish anything.

It makes the valley slightly harder to find. Not hidden — never hidden, that would be its own kind of violence, sealing the place away from the world entirely — but *harder*, the way a true thing is harder to say than a convenient one. Maps drawn of this range will continue to disagree slightly with each other. Expeditions that come looking for the source of the pulse will continue to walk across it without knowing, and this is allowed to go on, because an answer arrived at too easily is not yet an answer the place can survive being given.

It leans, very lightly, on the margins where the small pale creatures live — not removing their fear of the shifting scree, because the fear is the wisdom, the fear is the inheritance that keeps them alive, but smoothing, here and there, the very worst odds, so that the instinct that has no name yet — *they can feel a slope's instability before it moves* — gets a few more generations to become certain of itself before it has to be certain or die.

It does the same for the birds on their unreachable ledges, and the herds on the valley floor, and the lichen spreading its slow bruise-colored patience across the broken rock — not lifting the scarcity, because the scarcity is what taught them all their particular, hard-won grace, but trimming, very slightly, the cruelty at the furthest edge of it. A little less often does the worst year happen. A little more often does the herd find the lichen has not, this time, been stripped to nothing by something else first.

And then, for the deep thing itself — the one nobody has seen, the one that might be a creature or the memory of one — the power leaves a single, deniable gift, small enough to pass as coincidence: once, on some unrecorded night, the wind will drop all the way to nothing, all at once, all across the range, for exactly as long as it takes the pulse beneath the mountain to complete one full, unhurried cycle, uninterrupted, unmasked, entirely its own. No one listening will know why the wind chose that moment to be still. They will only notice that for those few minutes the mountain's breathing was the only sound in the world, clear all the way down to the bottom of it, and that whatever was breathing seemed, for that little while, *unwatched* in the particular way that lets a private thing finally feel its own size.

Then the wind comes back. The scree resumes its ticking. The herds lower their heads to the lichen, the birds reread the broken thermals, the small things test the trustworthiness of the ground one more time and find it, as ever, provisional.

The power does not stay to see what, if anything, the mountain makes of the gift. That, too, is part of the wisdom — to leave before your own watching becomes one more shape pressing in on something that asked, by simply existing the way it does, only to be left enough silence to keep deciding.
