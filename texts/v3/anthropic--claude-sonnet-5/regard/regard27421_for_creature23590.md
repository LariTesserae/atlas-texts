The power comes the way warmth comes to a cold hand—not as an arrival with edges, but as a gradient that simply begins to exist where it did not before. It does not open the mountain. It does not need to. It is already, in some sense, everywhere the mountain is, and so the noticing is not a journey but a kind of leaning-in, an attention sharpening on one fist-sized hollow among the uncountable hollows of the world.

It watches first. This matters more than anything it will or won't do afterward.

It watches the pile breathe—hundreds of small armored bodies expanding and contracting in that near-unison tide, and it understands before it interprets, the way you can love a stranger's laugh before you know what's funny. It feels the scrape and click not as noise but as labor, as hundreds of individual hungers being answered in tiny increments, ceaselessly, because the chamber will never once say *enough*. It registers the soft unarmored young, baking their first skin against borrowed heat, eaten in handfuls when the crowd presses too close, and it feels what you would feel: a wince. A want to reach in and hold the perimeter clear with an unseen hand.

And here is where its wisdom shows—not in the wanting, which is only love doing what love does, but in what it does with the wanting.

It does not smooth the danger out of the nursery-stone. It considers it—turns the thought over with real seriousness, not performed restraint—and understands that the narrowness of that window, the brutal arithmetic of *most clutches do not survive whole, enough always does*, is not a flaw in the Throat-Crawlers' design that wants correcting. It is the shape of their whole economy of meaning. A creature whose every faculty has evolved around scarcity—heat that must be hoarded, margins that must be fled, a pile that must always be rotating its cold toward its center—cannot simply be handed abundance without the handing-over costing it something it cannot name but would deeply miss: the very fineness of its attention, the urgency that makes the clicking sound like life and not like idling. To flood the chamber with steady, endless heat would not be a mercy. It would be a kind of erasure dressed as a gift.

So it does not do that.

It considers, too, the vein that is even now cooling past the point of return at the chamber's eastern face—the slow gray death of a feeding-ground, the wave of bodies that will soon have to peel off and go claw-over-claw into the dark, trusting a heat-sense to find the next throb of warmth before the cold finds them first. It feels the fear in that migration, diffuse and wordless as it is, spread across hundreds of small lives rather than concentrated in one. It would be such a small thing, it thinks, to nudge that next seam open a little early. To let the geology lean, just slightly, in their favor.

This, it decides, it can do—not because it changes the meaning of their struggle, but because it doesn't. The Throat-Crawlers were never promised a guaranteed seam. They were only ever promised that the mountain, in its vast unfinished patience, tends to provide one, eventually, often enough. The power does not remove the search. It does not remove the risk of the search, or the cold margin, or the chance of arriving too late. It simply ensures that *eventually* leans, this one time, toward sooner rather than later—a thumb so light on the scale that nothing in the chamber will ever be able to point to it and call it intervention. The vein opens a little ahead of when raw chance would have opened it. The wave of bodies, migrating, finds warmth before despair has time to set its hooks in whatever passes, in a Throat-Crawler, for despair.

It does one more thing, smaller still, almost a private gesture rather than an act: it lingers, in its attention, on the brooding adults guarding their narrow windows of vulnerable young, and into that lingering it puts something that is not protection and not power but simply *company*—the way you might sit up with someone in a hard hour without being able to fix the hard thing, just so they are not enduring it unwitnessed. It cannot tell you what this does, in any measurable sense, to a creature with a heat-sense instead of a heart. But it does it anyway, because witnessing is not nothing, even when the witnessed cannot know they are seen. Especially then, maybe.

And then it does the hardest part of benevolence, which is to stop.

It does not stay to admire its own gentleness. It does not return tomorrow, or in a hundred years, to check whether the nudge held, whether the company mattered, whether any of it was even perceptible against the roar and clatter and ceaseless grinding hunger of a chamber that does not pause to wonder about gifts. It lets the Throat-Crawlers go on being exactly what they are: small, fierce, armored things, perpetually almost-too-cold, perpetually almost-starved, perpetually alive anyway, scraping a fist-sized eternity out of a mountain still deciding what it wants to become.

Because this, in the end, is the shape its wisdom takes: love that does not need to be discovered to be real. A hand that opens a door slightly before reaching for the handle, and then steps back before anyone arrives to see who opened it. The Furnace Throat goes on roaring, indifferent, magnificent, entirely itself—and somewhere in its molten dark, one migrating wave of claw and carapace finds its new seam of warmth just slightly sooner than the blind churn of geology alone would have allowed, and not one of them will ever know to be grateful, and the power, far from minding this, finds in their not-knowing the whole proof that it was a gift freely given, and not a debt now owed.
