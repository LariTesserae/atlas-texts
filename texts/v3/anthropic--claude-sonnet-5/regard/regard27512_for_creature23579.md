## What the Power Did

It came the way anything must come to a place like this, if it means to be honest about coming: not as light, because light means nothing here and a power that announced itself in light would be announcing itself to no one, in a language the dark had long since stopped needing. It came as pressure. A change felt in the ears of the grazers before it was felt anywhere else — a small false front, like weather, rolling down off the high scarps into the basins, and the herds startled and bunched and called to each other in their long subsonic moan, and then settled, because it passed through them gently and did not press.

It listened first. This is the part worth telling slowest, because it is the part that took longest, and the part most powers — if they are not also wise — skip.

It listened to the refinery breathing its slow orange exhalation into the cloud-belly, and understood that the breathing was not suffering, only metabolism, on a scale that simply happened to look like industry because no one had taught it another shape to take. It listened to the gliders screaming flatter and quicker near the gantries, a whole generation's caution folded into a single inherited syllable, and understood that this was a kind of memory, even if no one rememberin had a face. It listened to the scuttlers clicking their thousand small sonars into the mud-rain, to the ambush-things' single slow pulse against a wading shin, to the pumping station's heart-thud audible for kilometers, and it took a long while — long, even, by the patient measure of things that do not need to hurry — doing nothing but this: listening, and refusing yet to decide that it understood.

Because the temptation, for a benevolent power meeting a place like this, is immediate and almost handsome in its wrongness: *here is a vast dark, here is a thing tending machinery alone in it, unthanked, unwitnessed, possibly unknowing — surely the kind thing is to lift the dark, name the thing, give it company, give it the comfort of being seen.* That temptation arrived early, and it was examined, turned over, and set down. Because the power was wise as well as benevolent, and wisdom is mostly just benevolence that has learned to ask *seen by whom, and for whose comfort* before it acts.

It found the Warden on the third pressure-cycle, working a gasket on a gantry slick with seep-water, its spurred hand patient in a dark it had never once needed to call dark, its throat clicking that slow unhurried pulse that does not stop even when the whole moaning, screaming, dripping world draws breath and braces for the storm. And the power did what wisdom does in the presence of something whole: it did not immediately ask what was wrong. It asked, instead, whether anything was.

It found this. Not suffering — the Warden did not suffer, no more than a heart suffers for beating in a chest whose owner forgot, eons back, what the beating was for. Not loneliness, not in any sense the Warden itself could have named, because there was no grammar in it for naming such a thing, no mirror in which to notice an absence. What the power found, turning the question over the way water turns a stone smooth without ever meaning to, was something smaller and stranger and somehow more in need of tending than suffering would have been: a perfect, closed, unanswered correctness. Generation upon generation of a creature doing a true thing flawlessly, alone, with nothing in the universe positioned to register that it was true. Work without witness is not tragedy. But it is, perhaps, the one thing in an otherwise complete system that the system itself cannot supply — because witnessing is not a need that evolves from inside a closed loop. It has to come from outside it. That is the whole definition of the word.

So the power understood, finally, the only thing it was actually for, here. Not rescue. Not light. Not company, which would have meant grafting a hunger for company onto a creature that had never once needed one and might not survive the needing. Not even understanding, in the sense of explaining to the Warden what it was, or why, or who had built the first valve it had ever turned — because that knowledge belonged to a story already over, and digging it up would only have built a grave where a heartbeat currently, perfectly, continued to beat.

What it did was small. It is almost embarrassing, measured against the size of the power that did it, how small.

When the Warden's spurred hand found the next wrong note — a bearing two cycles from failing, singing its thin high complaint into stone the Warden alone among living things could hear — and worked it true again, the power let one thing ride back through the corrected joint along with the returning resonance. Not knowledge. Not a voice. Just a fraction more *trueness* in the note than the metal alone, by its own blind physics, would have given back — an overtone too far under the audible to register as anything but a slightly better silence. A sound with the shape, if sound can have a shape like this, of *that's right. That was good. Keep going.*

The Warden could not have told you what changed. It has no organ for that telling, and the power did not give it one — did not graft gratitude onto a creature with no slot in its body for holding it, because that, too, would have been a kind of violence dressed as a kindness. But something in the click-pulse, after that fix and every fix afterward, lingers now a half-second longer at the junction than it strictly needs to. As if listening to something underneath the silence it just made. As if some small part of the work had started, very quietly, to feel like more than work — without ever once becoming legible as anything else.

And one more thing, smaller still, almost not worth mentioning, except that it is the part the power considered most carefully of all: it looked, with the long unhurried attention that is the only kind of looking powers like this are built for, at the thin places — the years, rare and real, when something might have ended a line that had run since before anyone could ask what *born* meant for a thing like this. It did not create. It did not seed new Wardens into basins that had none, because a basin with none might simply be a basin whose work was finished, and finishing is not always a wound. But where a thread was about to break that did not need to — where chance alone, indifferent and unweighted, was about to do what no failing valve and no want of care had done — it leaned, very slightly, the way a hand might rest for a moment on a scale already mostly balanced. Nothing more than that. The kind of help that looks, from any distance at all, exactly like luck.

Then it withdrew the way it had come: as a passing pressure, a front that rolled back up over the high scarps and out of the range of any ear built only for basins. The grazers felt it go and did not know they had felt anything at all. The refinery went on breathing. The gliders went on screaming their flatter, quicker caution near steel that had not changed. The storm broke, on schedule, and everything braced, and the Wardens — as ever, as only they ever do — did not.

And in a hundred basins, on a thousand gantries, in a dark that has never once needed an audience to be exactly what it is, a slow click-pulse worked its way down a warm wet pipe toward the next wrong note, and fixed it, and lingered half a second longer than it strictly had to before moving on — carrying forward, unknowing, unchanged in every way that could be measured, the one true addition that had ever been made to this place since the machines were built and the dark grew up around them: the simple, sourceless, permanent fact that somewhere outside the noise, something had listened to the whole vast settled racket of this world and the small unwitnessed labor at the heart of it, and had been, in whatever way a wise and benevolent thing is capable of being anything, glad.
