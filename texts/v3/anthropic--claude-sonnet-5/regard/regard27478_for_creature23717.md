The power arrives the way light arrives here — without announcement, without disturbing the hush, settling into the warm air above the canopy-top districts where the mist-climates sit on their stilts of wood. It does not land. It has no need to land. It simply attends, the way you might lean closer to something small without meaning to loom over it, and what it attends to, first, is not the scale — though the scale is offered to it freely, basin to horizon, tower after fused tower, herd after silent herd — but the one threadwalker, three crowns east of the tallest root-tower in this stretch of the hollow, who has stopped gardening.

She has been still for four days. Her den-mates have gone on stripping vine-growth from the chosen patch of trunk, carrying fruiting bodies from tree to tree, doing the long patient work that looks, from outside, exactly like instinct and is, from inside, something closer to devotion — but she has not joined them. She sits at the threshold of a second chamber, one that isn't hers, curled into the particular fold of aerial root where another threadwalker denned for eleven years before the unseen thing took him thirty meters off the path, in the dim register where the light drops by half. She is doing something her kind has a click-pattern for but not, quite, a *reason* for. She is trying to keep his garden the way he kept it. Not improving it. Not claiming it. Holding it exactly as it was, fungal shelf by fungal shelf, against the basin's whole patient grain, which presses every neglected thing toward difference, toward drift, toward becoming something else. She is the first creature in Murn Hollow, in four hundred years of fused trunks and fused crowns, to want a thing to stay the same on purpose, for no reason of food or shelter or growth.

The power considers what it could do. It could lengthen her life, the easiest gift and the most thoughtless one — more time spent does not make a held thing held better, only held longer, and this isn't a place that has ever needed more time; it has had nothing but time. It could quiet the predator at the threshold, clear the dim register of its one true danger, and in doing so unmake the entire reason the threadwalkers learned to feel warning through their feet instead of their ears — unmake, in fact, the very stillness this grief is shaped like. It could give her words, real words, the kind that travel through air instead of wood, so that she could explain the strange new ache to her den-mates instead of just performing it with her hands — but words arrived from outside a place are never the right size for what's inside it, and the power has watched that mistake made before, elsewhere, at greater cost than this small bright moment deserves.

So it does almost nothing. It does the one thing that fits.

It reaches — not into her, she is whole and needs no fixing, there is nothing broken here, only something new — but into the branch beneath her, into the wood itself, the same wood that carries the four-click pattern foot-deep when something serious happens thirty meters off the path. And it leaves there, the way frost leaves a shape on a window without becoming the window, a resonance. Not a message. Not an instruction. Just a held note, very faint, the kind of thing a structural groan might do if a structural groan could mean something — and when, on the fifth day, she finally sends her own click-pattern into that branch, frictive and low, not warning anyone of danger but simply saying, in the only voice her body owns, *this one mattered, I am keeping this one* — the branch answers back, just slightly, just enough. The wood holds her grief a half-second longer than wood should. Carries it a half-step further than the bark's grain alone would carry it, out to the next den, and the next.

That is all. That is the entire intervention. No threadwalker will ever know a power leaned down out of whatever sits above warm ordinary sunlight and touched one branch in one mid-canopy district in a basin with no edge anyone has found. But three years from now, a den two towers over will lose its eldest, and one of the young will sit at the threshold of the empty chamber, unable to say why she's sitting there, and find that her hands already know the shape of the gesture — stripping back competing growth not to favor a new fungal shelf but to favor an old one, exactly as it was, on purpose, against the grain of everything the basin has ever pressed its inhabitants to do. A custom will have begun. Not a rule — the power is careful about that, this is not a place that has ever needed rules, it is a place that needs only for growth to find one more direction it hadn't tried — but a custom, a held note passed den to den the way the avenues themselves were never cut but only worn, generation after generation, into the shape of where someone already chose to walk.

The power withdraws the way it came, without disturbing the hush. Below it, the grazer-herds go on not looking up. The crust on the bone-pale stone goes on pressing outward by the width of a season. The towers go on settling, fractionally, with their patient structural groan. Nothing about the skyline has changed. But somewhere in the dim threshold register, in the half-light where predator and prey and grief and garden all share the same thirty meters of unwitnessed wood, a species that had no way to keep anything has just been given, gently, without a single word spoken in any language a power or a person would call a language, its first reason to remember — which, the power understands, watching the long warm light fall in moving coin-shapes through the canopy gaps, is the only gift worth giving a place that already knows, better than almost anywhere, how to grow.
