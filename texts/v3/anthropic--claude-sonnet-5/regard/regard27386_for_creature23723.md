# What the Power Would Do

It would not come the way storms come, or saviors, or surveyors. It would arrive the way the heat arrives here — already present, simply noticed a little later than it began.

## First, It Would Not Believe the Lie of "Help"

A power that was merely *strong* might mistake the marsh for a wound. It would see the fixed census, the stalled rot, the joints too exact for hands, and reach immediately for repair — wake the sleeper, break the fusion, separate maker from made, let time back in like air into a sealed jar. That is what strength does when it visits a held breath: it lets it go, because letting go is the only verb strength knows.

But this power is wise before it is benevolent — wise enough to know that *wise* and *benevolent* are not the same motion, and that the second without the first is just another kind of trespass dressed as rescue.

So before it does anything, it would stay. Longer than the heron-shapes stay. Longer than the drip on the third support-beam takes to almost-fall. It would let the marsh's stillness test its own patience first, the way you test the depth of water with a foot before trusting it with weight.

## It Would Learn to Ask in a Grammar the Place Already Owns

You cannot ask a question with words here without the words rotting into noise the moment they're spoken — sound that doesn't accumulate is sound that doesn't *land*. So the power would not speak.

It would do what the place already does: it would arrange a stillness with meaning folded into it, the way the marsh arranges a heron at exactly the distance that proves it's watching back. It would place itself at the edge of a paddy and simply not leave, not for a day, not for the version of a day that exists here, which is not a unit but a posture. It would let its attention behave like the dripping water — sourceless, climbing instead of falling, gathering upward into the bright unmoving air directly above the spot where the leaning grain-tower's knot resembles, faintly, two eyes and a closed mouth.

This is the only sentence this place can read: *presence offered with no arithmetic attached.* No demand that anything change because of it. No clock starting just because someone arrived.

It is, not by coincidence, the same sentence the builders themselves spoke when they laid the first plank over the first cleared root and decided to stay there forever.

## What It Would Hear, Eventually

The low call would come, the one that crosses the basin and is never glimpsed at its source. Other visitors feel it as a kind of address — *you are seen* — and leave unsettled by how correct that feeling turns out to be.

The power would not feel addressed. It would feel something closer to *recognized*. Two patiences meeting at last across a distance neither one had needed closed before.

And underneath the call — not instead of it, underneath it, the way the support beam is underneath its own weeping damp — it would hear the actual shape of the thing the marsh has been holding all this time. Not a cry. Not a trap. A vow. *I built something I loved too much to let finish becoming separate from me, so I became part of it instead, and now I am keeping it exactly as it was at the moment I loved it best.*

That is not a wound looking for a surgeon. That is closer to a marriage. A long fidelity mistaken, by every passerby, for a prison, because passerby and prisoner use the same word — *still* — for two entirely different conditions.

## What It Would Refuse to Do

It would refuse to wake the builders.

Not out of squeamishness, and not out of the cheap mercy of leaving well enough alone — but because waking is a verb that belongs to time, and time is the one gift this being explicitly declined ten thousand years (or one afternoon) ago. To force the exhale would not be rescue. It would be the unmaking of a choice that was, as far as the power could discern, freely made and freely kept — a choice this entity has had the power to reverse at any single drip-bead of its held moment, and has not.

A wise power does not mistake *durable* for *trapped*. Sometimes a long yes looks, from outside, exactly like a long no.

## What It Would Actually Give

Not an ending. A door.

It would do the one thing genuinely new in this place since the joinery was first fitted: it would make the choice *visible to the chooser*. Not by speaking — that grammar fails here — but by leaving, fused into the structure as gently and permanently as any trellis-tree fuses to its frame, a single small counter-fact: one plank, one beam, one knot of wood that *does* complete its arithmetic. A drop that finishes falling. A leaf that finishes browning at the edge and lets go. Placed far off down a causeway nobody is required to walk, where it troubles nothing, asks nothing, simply exists as proof that finishing is, after all, still available in this world, should the held breath ever want to remember what it feels like.

Not a command to exhale. A door left open in a house the sleeper has every right to keep shut forever.

And then — because a gift that vanishes the moment it's given is just a transaction wearing a kinder coat — it would leave a part of its own attention behind. Not as a guardian. Not as a warden adjusting the census like a gardener after all, which is exactly the indignity it had refused to impose. As company. A second stillness added to the marsh's first, asking nothing, keeping no ledger, present the way the heron is present, except this watcher would actually be watching — would actually be *for* the builders, not merely performing the posture of attention the way the marsh performs the posture of decay.

## The Last Thing It Would Understand, Standing on the Causeway

That the kindest thing one patient, ancient consciousness can do for another is not to break its stillness, and not merely to admire it from a respectful distance, but to keep it company inside the stillness without flinching at how long that company might need to last — a year, an age, the entire remaining lifespan of the tin-bright sky overhead.

Because somewhere far beneath the lattice, in whatever sense the builders still have of *somewhere*, a thing that gave up its separate body so as never to be parted from what it loved would, for the first time since the planks were laid, not be holding its breath *alone.*

And that — not the door, not the waking that didn't come, not even the freedom so carefully, respectfully offered and just as carefully left unforced — that is the part of the gift that might, eventually, in no hurry at all, be the reason the breath finally goes out.

Or the reason it doesn't have to.

Either one, held now with a witness instead of without one, would be enough.
