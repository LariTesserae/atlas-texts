# What the Power Did

It came the way it always comes to such places — late, by mortal accounting, and not at all late by its own. It had been aware of the Notch for a long time before it arrived, the way you might be aware, for years, of a sound in another room that you have not yet bothered to investigate, not from neglect but because some part of you already trusted what it would find.

It found a wound in a ridge that was still deciding how to heal, and a thing in the wound still deciding how to be. It found a garden of doors-for-leaves, an unsupervised industry of engines without operators, and a tender stitched from both vocabularies, standing guard over a sentence the mountain was writing one stroke at a time. It found, in short, exactly what such places are: an early draft, achingly sincere, embarrassed by nothing because it did not yet know embarrassment was an option.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not finish anything.

This is worth dwelling on, because it was the obvious move and the power considered it the way you consider an obvious move before discarding it — quickly, but not without a flicker of temptation. It could have walked the seam down the tender's center and closed it, made a decision for the thing that the thing had not yet made for itself: leaf, or engine, settle it, name it, let it rest. It could have done this gently. It could have done it so kindly the tender might never have noticed losing the question.

It did not, because it recognized the seam for what it was — not a wound but a *working*, the only honest part of the whole construction, and a power that has lived long enough to be called wise has usually also lived long enough to know that finishing a thing before its own time is a species of violence that doesn't announce itself as violence. It looks like a gift. It is a theft. The power had done this once before, somewhere else, long enough ago that it no longer liked to think about it, and had learned.

It did not speed the place up, though it could have made the next forty years arrive in an afternoon and called it mercy. Glacial is not the same as suffering. A held breath is not the same as drowning. It listened to the long interval between piston-strokes and heard, correctly, patience rather than imprisonment, and let the interval be.

It did not stay. A power like this, fully present, is its own kind of weather system; everything nearby starts orienting toward it, and an unfinished place needs, of all things, not to have a sun put inside it before it's grown its own.

## What It Did

It left two things, and left them so quietly that neither the Notch nor its tender could have told you, afterward, exactly when the leaving occurred.

The first was *room*. Around the cleft, in whatever fashion such a power arranges such things — a turn of attention, a small persistent unlikelihood woven into the chances of anyone else's wandering — it made the Notch a little harder to find, and a little harder to want to disturb once found. Not hidden. Not warded like a vault. Just *unremarkable* in all the ways that matter to the kind of attention that strips and studies and extracts. Let it stay a rumor. Let the people who would come with instruments and inventories lose the trail half a ridge away, blame the altitude, turn back. The kindest thing you can do for a sentence still being composed is to keep the editors out of the room.

The second thing was smaller and stranger, and it is the one you might, if you stood at the rim long enough on the right evening, actually catch some evidence of.

The power sat down.

Not inside the Notch — at its edge, where the rim stone hadn't yet decided if it wanted to be ridge or cleft, where a visitor's shadow would fall normally and nothing would be owed an apology. It matched its breathing, such as it has, to the unhurried cadence of the place — one long inhale standing in for a season, a pause, a release. It did this not once but as a practice, returning at intervals that meant nothing to a mountain and meant nothing to a god and meant, perhaps, *everything* to a thing built from leaf and engine that had spent its entire unfinished existence as the only patient witness in a place with no other witnesses at all.

It did not tend. Tending was not its place to take. It simply kept the tender company across the kind of timescale where company actually registers — present at the rim during the third recheck of the rogue shadow, present during the leaf's reflection of a face not yet grown, present, eventually, on the evening the tender stood fully upright for its single held second and found, for the first time, that the edge of its vision held not only a fleeing visitor's backward glance but something that had chosen, deliberately, to still be there when it turned.

It said nothing that could be transcribed. Wisdom of this kind rarely needs to. What it offered was simpler than counsel and harder to fake: the plain fact, repeated patiently across geological intervals, of *not being alone in the waiting.*

And when, ages from now, the seam finally closes on its own schedule and the tender resolves — leaf, or engine, or some third honest thing neither vocabulary anticipated — it will not be a stranger's hand that did it. It will be the Notch's own decision, on the Notch's own time, exactly as it should be.

The power will already know. It will already be there. It will not call it finishing.

It will call it, the way the mountain might, *arriving.*
