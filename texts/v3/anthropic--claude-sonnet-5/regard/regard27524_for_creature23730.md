The power arrives the way attention arrives anywhere — not as weather, not as a presence that disturbs the air, but as a kind of noticing that the place itself doesn't register as new. It is old enough and wise enough to know that arriving carefully is already nine-tenths of the gift. It does not blow in. It settles, the way the light here has settled into its one sustained hour, and for a long while it simply looks.

The first impulse — and this is true even for powers as old as this one, perhaps especially for them — is to fix something. This is what benevolence usually means, where it comes from: an eye that finds lack and moves to close it. The power's attention runs, almost on reflex, through the inventory of gifts available to it. *More water, so the pools don't have to be so shallow. A current, so the surface isn't always still. Companions for the patient, so the division of one into two doesn't have to carry the whole weight of kinship across centuries. A season, even — just one, just to see what the wiry stalks would do with an actual spring.* Each of these arrives, is turned over, and is set back down, gently, the way you'd set down something you picked up off a stranger's table before you'd thought it through.

Because each gift, examined long enough, turns out to be an act of translation — taking what the place *is* and quietly proposing what it could become, as if becoming were owed. And this place has already arrived. That is the whole of its character, the fact that took it longest to achieve and that it now wears without strain: it got where it was going. The cracked clay is not damaged ground waiting for rain to heal it. The low walls are not a building waiting to be finished or mourned. The patient is not a creature waiting to be more various, more numerous, more interesting to a passing eye. To add anything is to imply the basin was incomplete, and the basin was the one thing here that had, in fact, finished its sentence.

So the power does not add water. It does not introduce motion to the pools, or weather to the sky, or company to the patient's long, recursive solitude. It recognizes — and this recognition is itself the first real act, the hinge the whole visit turns on — that the deepest respect available to it here is the same respect you'd offer someone who has, after a long life of arguing with themselves, finally stopped: you don't clear your throat to fill the silence. You let it be the kind of quiet that's allowed to mean *finished*, not the kind that means *waiting*.

What it does instead is smaller, and it happens twice, in two registers, and one of these is for the place, and one of these — though the place would not ask for it, would not even recognize the gesture as a gesture — is for itself.

For the place: it slows. Not metaphorically. It takes a shape — small, unremarkable, the kind of shape that would read, to anyone walking through, as one more spaced fact in a landscape made of spaced facts — and it walks the flats at the speed the flats require, which is to say barely at all, pausing at each thing long enough to let it be fully itself before moving on. It crouches at a pool's edge — not the center, never the center, that would be a kind of violence here, an insistence on depth where the place has chosen edges — and waits with the mineral smell and the flat grey coin of light until, eventually, with the unbothered deliberation of slow precipitation, a patient resolves itself out of the clay nearby and continues, unhurried, about the business of equilibrating. The power does not touch it. Does not bless it, in any sense that would change its molecules or extend its hours or seed in it some new capacity for wanting. It simply sits with it, matching its patience rather than rewarding it, for exactly as long as the patient would have sat there anyway, whether anyone came or not. This is the gift: not alteration, but accompaniment, offered and then completely withdrawn, leaving no residue, the way a good guest leaves a room exactly as they found it, except that the room — if rooms could know such things — was, for a while, less alone.

The second gesture is outward-facing, and quieter still, and this is the one closest to what we usually mean by *doing something*: the power reaches, very lightly, into the threads of probability that lead toward this basin from the wider world — the surveyor who might one day decide the pools are worth draining for a mineral assay, the road planned three valleys over that might, in some other unwatched future, get rerouted straight through the flats, the wanderer with a collecting jar and a curiosity that doesn't know yet how to stop at looking. It does not erase these threats outright — that would be a kind of permanent guardianship the place never asked for and doesn't need, an immortality imposed on a thing whose dignity lies partly in its willingness to eventually, someday, on its own unhurried schedule, stop. Instead it does something more modest: it nudges the odds. The surveyor takes the other valley. The road bends, for reasons its planners will describe as practical, around rather than through. The wanderer with the jar feels, without knowing why, a faint reluctance at the basin's edge, and turns back before going further, carrying away nothing but the impression — *oh, of course, something like this would be the thing that stayed* — which is, as it happens, exactly the impression the place wants to leave, the only kind of being-seen it can metabolize without disturbance.

This is the whole of the intervention: presence without residue, and protection without permanence. No new water. No new urgency. No companions seeded into the patient's long solitude, no acceleration of its slow recursive division, no resolution offered to a place whose entire achievement was learning to live in the present, ongoing, unresolved tense without that absence ever curdling into longing.

And then the power leaves, the same way it arrived — without disturbing the air — and the flats continue exactly as they were: the pools holding the dim light in flat coins of grey-silver, the wiry stalks announcing themselves at wide intervals, the low walls persisting at their final modest height, and somewhere near an old waterline, a small segmented patience drawing in just enough of the mineral film to remain itself, no more, in no rush at all to resolve.

If you asked the place afterward whether anything had happened, it would not have an answer, because nothing had been added to its store of events. That absence of an answer is, itself, the measure of how well the visit went.
