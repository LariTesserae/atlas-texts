This is a place that doesn't need fixing. That has to be the first thing the power notices — not absence of suffering, but absence of *need*. Every appetite here is matched to a supply. Every grazer leaves enough behind. The hollow-bears prune without stripping, the gill-grazers eat what the bears miss, something smaller still eats what the gill-grazers miss, and the chain doesn't end so much as recede past the limits of attention. A wise power, looking at this, would recognize the rarest thing it ever finds: a system that has already solved itself.

So it would not interfere. Not because intervention is forbidden, but because intervention here would be a category error — like correcting a poem that already scans. There is nothing to heal, nothing in want, no cruelty to interrupt, no scarcity to relieve. The power's whole instinct toward benevolence — *find the wound, close it* — simply has no purchase. The hollow declines to need it.

But "doing nothing" is not quite right either, because attention itself is not nothing. So here is what I think it would do:

**It would stay.** Not to change anything, not even to bless anything in the sense of leaving a mark — but to be a witness with no agenda, which is a rarer gift than people think. Most attention that falls on a wild place comes loaded: a use to extract, a danger to flee, a resource to count, a moral to draw. This attention would come empty-handed. It would sit in the mist at the bottom of the streambed in the cool hours and watch it lie there like something solid, and want nothing from it.

**It would listen to the call-and-answer.** That long pause between the sounding-note and its reply — minutes, sometimes — and feel no urge to shorten it, no impulse to read anxiety into the silence. A lesser kindness rushes to fill quiet with reassurance. A wiser one learns to hold the same patience the hollow-bears hold, to trust that the answer is coming on its own schedule and that the waiting is not a problem to solve but the actual shape of how these creatures are related to each other across the dark.

**If it gave anything, it would give time** — not more of it, exactly, since the hollow is in no danger it can presently see, but a kind of *standing guarantee*: that the subsidence which opened this basin won't be followed too soon by another that closes it; that the raw fracture-edges get their centuries to round; that whatever slow equilibrium the place is still finding, it gets to keep finding it, undisturbed, for as long as that takes. Not intervention. Insurance. The quiet removal of catastrophic risk from a system that was never asking to be improved, only to be left alone long enough to finish becoming itself.

**And it would take something away with it** — not a possession, the hollow keeps everything it grows — but a kind of internal proof: that this is what a place looks like when nothing is forcing it to optimize, to defend, to extract, to win. Density without conquest. Appetite without excess. A whole city's worth of structure that arrived by nobody's design and works anyway, better than most things that are designed.

If the power left any trace at all, I think it would be small and slow, in keeping with everything else here: maybe one winter slightly milder than it should have been, so a kin-cluster of young hollow-bears, riding out their first wet season matted into a parent's coat, survive a cold that would otherwise have thinned them. No one would ever know it had happened. There would be no shrine, no sign, no story — the hollow doesn't keep stories, only moss and water and slow grazing — just a few more low soft thuds on a few more branches, a few more calls answered, a fraction more time for a system that only ever asked for time in the first place.

That's the whole of it. The kindest thing a benevolent power can do for a place that has already gotten everything right is *witness it, insure it, and leave it exactly as full as it already is.*
