The power comes the way light comes to the scarp — without footstep, without displacement of air, arriving as a kind of attention rather than a body, and the first thing it does, before any other thing, is *nothing*. It does not announce itself in warmth, because warmth here would be a lie the light doesn't tell, and the power has not survived this long, in whatever sense a wise power survives, by lying to places older than its own patience. It simply stands — or whatever the word is for a benevolence taking up a position — inside the roar, and lets the roar be total, because to soften it on arrival would be the first and worst mistake available: mistaking the chiming wall for a wound, when it is in fact a structure, load-bearing, the place's whole skeleton of sound holding up a silence that would otherwise have nowhere to mean anything.

It considers, the way any visitor considers, the easy gifts. It could warm the light by some small fraction, just enough to take the edge off that edge-of-uncomfortable cool. It could loosen a single capillary crack until the seep gathered into an actual pool, a thing the eye could rest on instead of chasing forever down into stone. It could thicken the fauna, seed a hundred more pale flattened bodies into the gaps, solve in one gesture the part of this place that looks, to an outside eye trained on other landscapes, like absence — no nest, no path, no crowd, no chorus. Each of these would be easy. Each of these is exactly what a lesser kindness does: arrives at a thing that has already finished becoming itself and tries to *un*finish it, mistaking completion for deprivation, mistaking the closed loop for a broken one that simply hasn't found its missing piece yet.

But the power listens first, properly, the long way — not for an hour but for whatever the plateau's unit of patience actually is, which is longer than an hour and shorter than a geological age, somewhere in the register the small pale creature itself listens in. And underneath the roar, the way a hand on a bell finds the bell's true note beneath its ringing, the power finds what the scarp has been saying this whole time, in the only voice available to it: *I arrived. I was satisfied. I am still satisfied.* That is not a cry for help. That is a completed sentence, said once, a very long time ago, and repeated since only because repetition is what continuance looks like from outside, never because the sentence needed saying twice.

So the power does not warm the light. Does not pool the water. Does not multiply the creatures or build them a softer noise to live inside. It understands, the way real wisdom understands, that abundance imposed on a thing that has already chosen its own form of fullness is not generosity — it is a correction applied to something that was never wrong. The scarp's nothing-grows-here is not a famine. It is a finished thought. To interrupt it with green would be to edit a sentence that was never unfinished, for the sake of a grammar that belongs to other landscapes, not this one.

What it does instead is smaller than any of those gestures, and harder, in its way, because it costs the power something to do nothing *well* — to be present without altering, which is a far more exacting discipline than presence with a gift in its hand. It learns, as the small creature learns at birth and never has to relearn, how to subtract the roar. It holds the plateau's whole continuous chiming wall in whatever organ a power holds things in, and updates it, and cancels it, the way the creature's flank-sails cancel it, until what's left underneath is not silence exactly but the place's true breathing — slow, mineral, immense, ongoing.

And then, having learned the only language spoken here, it does the one thing the language permits: it goes and finds the creature — not difficult, for a power, to find one small pale body in a hallway of stone, though it takes its time about it anyway, takes the long way around three fins out of something like courtesy, the way you'd walk around rather than across a sleeping animal's sightline — and when it comes near, close enough that in any other landscape this would be the moment for a word, a touch, a sign, it offers none of those. It offers stillness. It lays its attention flat against the roar the way the creature lays its body flat against the rock, and it *holds*, for the length of a few held breaths, the small local silence that is, here, the only currency kindness has ever been minted in.

The creature, for its part, does what it does with its own kind: it does not startle, does not flee, does not read the held silence as a threat needing the cover of flight, because threats on this plateau have never come quiet — they would come, if they came at all, as a change in the chime, a new note, an addition. This is a subtraction. This is the dialect it already knows. So it stays. Two stillnesses, laid against the same roaring wall, for a few breaths, which is the whole of the visit, the whole of the gift, the whole of what a wise power decides, in the end, this place was asking for — not rescue, not increase, not the grafting-on of a future it never wanted, but to be *met* once, correctly, in its own terms, by something that troubled itself to learn those terms rather than requiring the place to learn its.

Then the power leaves the way it came, without displacement, without residue you could point to in the stone — no new pool, no warmer angle of light, no second creature conjured out of loneliness misread as lack. The plateau continues exactly as it was: packed, ungrown, ringing, self-feeding, satisfied past any need of revision. The only thing different, if difference is even the word for something this unmeasurable, is that somewhere in whatever a power carries forward from the places it has been, there is now a held breath shaped like that gap between two fins, a small wet knot of silence tied once against an enormous chime — carried onward, unspent, not because the scarp required remembering, but because the power, having finally learned to hear the place correctly, found that it could not, afterward, fully unlearn it.

And on the plateau, nothing changes. Which is, for once, exactly the point, and exactly the gift: that something vast and kind passed all the way through a world built entirely of *enough*, and had the wisdom to add nothing to it — only to sit, for a while, inside its noise, and hear the silence the way it was meant to be heard, and let that be sufficient, because it already was.
