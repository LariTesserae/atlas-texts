## What the power does

It comes the way it must, if it wants to understand anything true about this place: slowly, and last.

It does not announce itself to the canopy-roar, because the canopy-roar is not where the truth of Vael Sorrow lives — that's the *costume*, the loud weather-system the forest grows specifically to wear on the outside so that what's underneath can stay unheard. So the power sets that aside, the way you'd set aside a guard's uniform to talk to the person under it, and it goes down, into the hollow rooms, into the grey-dark, and it does the one thing almost nothing from outside this world has ever done correctly on first contact.

It listens before it decides anything is wrong.

It spends what would be, in mortal time, a long while — moving root-bridge to root-bridge the slow way, the *expensive* way, paying the full acoustic cost of its own presence rather than simply knowing things the way powers are tempted to know things. It wants to arrive at understanding the same way the Hollow-folk arrived at theirs: through accumulated, earned attention. Anything less would be a kind of lie about what it's here to honor.

What it finds, eventually, is not a monster.

It finds a platform, half-rotted, at the top of a ladder of notches that stopped being cut three or four generations ago. And above that, in the roaring tier, it finds the thing the climbing-people's fear has calcified around — and it is old, and it is real, and it is not what the story became. It was never a predator with intent. It is closer to an organ of the forest itself: a vast, half-sentient confluence of root and resonance, grown into the canopy-roof at exactly the altitude where wind becomes sound, that *does* respond to certain frequencies — the specific pitch of a frightened person calling out from a height, as it happens, decades ago, to someone who never should have been climbing in a storm. It answered that call the only way it knew how to answer anything: it amplified it, threw it back doubled, the way the whole architecture of this place throws sound back doubled. It wasn't hunting. It was *echoing*, badly, at the worst possible moment, to someone who heard it as pursuit and fell, or fled, and never came back to correct the story.

The Hollow-folk built a civilization-deep silence around a tragic acoustic accident.

The power sits with this for a long time. This is the part that matters most, the part it could so easily get wrong: knowing a fear is founded on a misunderstanding does not mean the fear is foolish, and does not earn the power any right to simply walk in and announce *you misunderstood, you can stop now.* That would be the loudest possible thing to do to the quietest possible people. It would also, worse, be a kind of theft — taking from them a discipline, an art, a whole grammar of touch and proximity and stake-circle geometry that they built in response to the fear, yes, but which has become something more than fear now. It is composure. It is intimacy. It is, in its way, gorgeous. A power that loves this place would not unmake that just because its origin turns out to be sadder and smaller than the legend.

So it does not explain anything. It does not fix the "problem," because by any measure that matters the problem is not current — it is historical, healed-over, scar tissue mistaken for a wound. What it does instead is smaller, and harder, and exactly proportionate to what the place can actually receive without being violated by the receiving.

**First**, in three root-hollows near three different stake-circles, far enough apart that no single community will think it special, it loosens — gently, structurally, the way root and water already loosen things here — the seams that gather condensation. Not floods. Not rivers; this place would not know what to do with a river, would not trust it. Just a little more, a little more often. Enough that a child sucking moss for water on a dry week finds the moss a little less grudging than her mother remembers it being. No ceremony. No sign. Gratitude here doesn't need a source, only a result.

**Second** — and this is the only thing it does that touches the fear directly — on a single still night, in the upper canopy, at the exact dead platform where the ladder-notches stop, it goes to the old amplifying confluence and it does what nobody in all the generations of careful, frightened listening ever risked doing: it speaks to it. Not loudly. In its own register, the only one that thing has ever known how to answer in — the pitch of a call thrown upward in fear — and it asks, gently, for the echo to stop coming back doubled. It asks the forest's own organ of resonance to simply absorb, once, the way the stake-circles absorb, the way the whole canopy filters wind into something survivable below.

It agrees. It was never cruel. It was only doing, badly and too well, the one thing it was grown to do.

**Third**, the power leaves one thing behind, and only one: a stake-circle, in a stretch of the plateau none of the Hollow-folk have yet walked far enough to reach, exact in diameter, exact in stake-count, built to the same quiet discipline as all the others — except that no hand of theirs placed it. It will sit there for years, maybe generations, doing nothing but holding its small bubble of hush in a forest that screams everywhere else, waiting to be found the way everything true in this place is found: slowly, by accident, by someone who wasn't looking for a sign and will have to decide for themselves what it means.

Maybe they'll think it's the work of ancestors they didn't know they had. Maybe they'll think nothing at all, and simply use it, the way you'd use any good shelter. The power doesn't get to choose. That was never its right.

---

Somewhere, not long after, an old keeper of one of the original circles — a woman whose grandmother told her, in the wood-conducted hand-language, about the platform, about why the climbing stopped — stands at the base of a ladder of rotted notches she has avoided her whole life out of inherited reflex rather than memory of her own. She has noticed the moss is a little more generous this season. She has noticed, without being able to say how she knows it, that the air above the dead platform doesn't feel the way the stories said it should feel.

She does not climb it. That would be too much, too fast, a culture doesn't unlearn a discipline in one season just because the danger that built it has quietly gone.

But she does, for the first time in her life, make a sound on purpose, facing upward, into the dark — small, testing, the kind of sound her whole life has trained her never to make.

Nothing answers.

She stands there a long moment in the enormous hollow quiet, and then she goes back to her circle, and she does not tell anyone yet, because telling is also a sound, and some sounds you want to be sure of before you spend them.

That uncertainty, that first private experiment that didn't go badly — that is the whole of what the power leaves her. Not safety announced. Not a miracle witnessed. Just one quiet thing, undisproven, that she gets to carry alone for a while before she decides, in her own time, in her own grammar, whether it's safe enough to pass on.

The power asks for nothing else, and takes nothing, and goes the way it came — last, and slow, and without a sound the forest would ever think to throw back.
