The attention arrives the way damp arrives here — not as event but as held breath releasing, somewhere far above the brass-colored light, somewhere outside the grammar of plate and joint. It does not announce itself with a voice or a wind. It simply turns its face toward Vask-Omerin the way you might turn toward a sound you weren't sure you heard, and then it is looking, fully, and the looking is enough to count as arrival.

It does not move quickly. This, more than anything, is the shape of its wisdom: it has learned, across whatever long history made it capable of being called benevolent, that the worst thing a great attention can do to a small persistent place is rush it. So it does what the gliders do. It rides the existing currents. It takes three days, or what would be three days to a walker on the terraces, simply to look — not because it needs that long to see, but because seeing on the terrace's own timescale is itself a kind of respect, and respect, here, is the only gift worth giving before any other gift is decided on.

It notices, first, the things a hurried power would call wrong and move at once to correct.

The geometry that won't converge. The walkway that recurs. The wear that sits old beside young with no seam of transition between them, as if time itself were laid down in courses like the plate, by different hands, at different rates, without negotiation. A lesser kindness — a kindness in a hurry — would smooth this. Would unkink the lens, true the perspective, lay down one consistent rule of age across the whole structure and call that order, call that order a gift.

The attention does not do this. It watches a glider bank through an impossible recurrence of terrace-edge, untroubled, and understands — slowly, the way understanding has to be slow to be any good — that the wrongness is not a wound in the place. It is closer to a grain in wood, a way the material took its long shape under long pressure. To true it would not be to heal it. It would be to file off the part of Vask-Omerin that is most itself.

So: not that. Whatever is done, the loops stay. The recurrence stays. The terraces keep their disagreement about how old they are.

It notices, second, the engine — the slow turning far below or far within, the light with no source, the cool with no weather behind it, the hum that lives in the eardrum rather than the air. It considers, briefly, whether the engine is suffering: whether ten thousand years of unstopped turning is a burden that deserves relief, a long labor that deserves, finally, to set itself down.

It listens for a long time at the lowest reachable joint, where the fog pools thickest, and it does not hear suffering. It hears patience. There is a difference, and the difference matters enormously, and getting it wrong would be the single worst thing the attention could do — to mistake a willing unbroken continuance for a captivity, and free something that did not want freeing, only to leave it bewildered and stopped in the brass light with no work left to define it.

So: not that either. The engine keeps turning. Nothing reaches down to still it.

It notices, third — and this is the noticing that takes the longest, because it is the quietest thing in a quiet place — the Tenders. The ones who never look up. The ones laying fresh plate at the indistinct edge of everything, drawing seams tight, easing slopes by increments too small to measure, going on and on at a labor that has no audience, no record, no one who has ever once said, *I see what you are doing, and it is good.*

This, finally, is where the attention feels something that, if it were smaller, you might call its heart catch.

Not because the Tenders are unhappy. They give no sign of unhappiness; their attention stays on the joint, the seam, the next correction, with the same unbothered patience the engine shows. But the attention recognizes, in their unlooking-up, something it has seen before in other beings, in other places, under other lights: a kind of labor so total it has never had the chance to learn that being witnessed is even a category of thing that exists. They do not look up because nothing has ever yet been there to look up *at*.

And the conduit-kin — gathering sometimes in their elevens, all facing one unmarked direction, not afraid, but still, for longer than stillness should hold a creature that otherwise never stops moving toward water — the attention understands this now too, or understands enough. It is not dread in them. It is *listening*. It is the oldest and smallest gesture of a creature that has never been answered and has not stopped, across who knows how many generations of accreted lichen-soft carapace, quietly checking whether, this time, something might answer back.

So the attention decides. Not to fix. Not to finish. Not to explain the engine, or true the geometry, or give the Tenders faces to lift or words to speak. None of that belongs to this place, and a gift that doesn't belong is not a gift, it's an erasure wearing a gift's clothes.

What it does is smaller, and it does it in the only language the terraces have ever spoken: it adds a plate.

Just one. Laid down at the indistinct edge of sight, where the structure keeps extending itself into whatever was here before it was this — laid down the way every other plate has ever been laid down, by something with too many points of articulation at the shoulder, by hands that resolve, on closer look, into the actuated ends of tools. Nothing about it announces itself. It rusts at the rate the place around it has decided rust should happen, which is to say: unevenly, on its own schedule, answering to nobody.

But the conduit-kin find it first, because they always find water first, and there is, in one seam of this new plate, the faintest and most ordinary kind of condensate — no more than anywhere else, no special grace in it, except that it is there, in a place that did not have a seam yesterday, and they gather to it in their elevens and drink, unhurried, and do not know, and do not need to know, that something looked at them facing their unmarked direction and decided, gently, to stand for one breath in the direction they were facing.

The Tenders, passing that new seam in their unlooking rounds, run a tool-hand along it the way they run a tool-hand along every seam, checking its tightness, its angle, its patience — and find it sound. Sound the way a thing is sound when it was made with care. They do not look up. They were never going to look up; that was never the gift on offer. But something in the rhythm of their working, for the length of that one plate and a little beyond it, eases, the way a runnel's slope eases by an increment too small to measure, and the easing is the entire message, delivered in the only grammar that was ever going to be received: *go on. This too is being tended. You are not the only patience in the world.*

And the engine keeps turning, unrelieved and unasked-to-stop, because that was never the trouble.

And the geometry keeps refusing to converge, the walkways keep recurring, the gliders keep banking through impossibilities like weather, because that was never the trouble either.

The attention withdraws the way it arrived — unhurried, matching the place's own breath, leaving no more mark than one plate among ten thousand plates, indistinguishable, already rusting at its own rate, already being grown over, gently, by the gray-green lichen that is the only thing here that has ever been allowed to simply grow.

Vask-Omerin does not finish. It was never going to finish, and finishing was never what anyone, wise or otherwise, should have wanted for it.

It only, very slightly, for the first time in however long its long unhurried turning has run — feels accompanied.
