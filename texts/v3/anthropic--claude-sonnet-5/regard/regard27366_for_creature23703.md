# What the Power Did

It noticed the way you'd notice a low note under a chord — not first, not loudest, but persistent, structural, present beneath everything else once you'd heard it once. A mountain still finishing itself. A people who had made a home in the seams of that unfinishing, and called the seams neighbors, and taught their children manners for them instead of fear.

The power did not rush. This seemed, on consideration, the first form of respect available to it — to meet a slow place slowly, to let its own attention settle at the pace of cloud repositioning over peaks, of streets destabilizing once a season, of a child's eyes catching up to true daylight over years rather than days. A lesser kindness would have arrived all at once, blazing, certain, and broken something that had taken three generations of furnace-cycles to grow correctly crooked. So it did not arrive. It accompanied. It let itself be folded into the existing weather, indistinguishable, for a long while, from a change in air pressure that made the old birds call differently.

## What It Considered, and Declined

There was an obvious mercy available, and the power turned it over for a long time before setting it down: it could finish the mountain. Could reach into that raw cooling stone and let it harden fully, let erosion catch up centuries in a season, let every unsettled street resolve into ordinary geology, every overlapping stairwell choose a single floor and keep it.

It declined.

Not from indifference — from arithmetic of a different kind. The trade-literacy, the rhymes taught before arithmetic, the dry affection for landings that were *always a little strange* — none of this was incidental to the unfinishing. It was grown from it, root-deep, the way the black ferns were grown from cracks that should not yet have supported them. To finish the mountain would not be to heal these people. It would be to amputate the organ they'd been quietly, patiently evolving for longer than they had words for it. The sixth finger that nobody remembers adding and nobody can subtract. You don't fix a hand like that. You let it hold what it has learned to hold.

So: not erasure. The power set that tool down gently, as you'd set down a hammer near something that needed a hand instead.

## The Small Tendings

What it did instead was smaller, and took longer, and would never be noticed as intervention by anyone living there — which was, in fact, the design.

Where a strut was going to fail for no reason any rail-walker's trained ear could have caught — no wrong hum, no warning pitch, just plain bad iron meeting plain bad luck — the power leaned, infinitesimally, on the moment. Not every failure. Only the ones that taught nothing, that would have cost a life for no literacy gained, no story the settlement could use afterward to keep some other child safer. The dangerous gantries stayed dangerous. The danger that was *legible* — the kind a person could learn to read in time, the kind the whole culture had been built to read — the power left entirely alone. It was not in the business of removing risk. It was in the business of removing the risk that had stopped being a teacher and become only a thief.

Where the long-necked birds called their layered, overlapping cries, the power added — not a new sound, nothing a person could point to and say *that wasn't there before* — but a fractional clarity, here and there, in the half-second before true disaster. A pressure-front's voice picked a little more cleanly out of the roar. A mother on a high catwalk glancing up half a breath sooner than instinct alone would have given her. The system stayed the system. The birds stayed birds, ignorant of any of it, doing what birds do. The power simply stood very close to that ignorance and made it, once in a while, slightly more useful than chance would have allowed.

And once — only once, because restraint mattered to it more than almost anything else — it sat with a child.

A furnace-tender's daughter, eyes still pale and unfocused in the rare thinning of the cloud ceiling, sitting on a sweating iron rail with her knees drawn up, looking at a sun she could not quite bring into shape. The power did not heal her eyes. Did not explain the mountain to her, or tell her what waited above the cloud, or promise her anything about how her sight would resolve. It simply sat beside her, in whatever way a power can sit, and let her feel — for the length of one held breath — that the blur was not a flaw in her, that something vast and unhurried found the way she squinted at the smeared light to be, in its own way, complete. Then it left. She grew up and became, like her mother, a furnace-tender, and never could have said what that afternoon was, only that she remembered it the way you remember being looked at kindly by a stranger on a hard day, with no name to put to the kindness and no need of one.

## The Gift Left at the Unfinished Streets

But there was one true gift, and it was built to last, because it answered the one wound in this place that the power judged to be a wound and not a feature — not the strangeness, not the overlaps, not the shadows with too many limbs. The loneliness of the ending.

The old ones who walked out toward a street that had never resolved, on a fog-low morning, and simply did not come back — this the ridge-folk had made peace with, had folded into their customs as gently as everything else, calling it a retirement rather than a death. But the power, watching closely, saw what the settlement could not quite see from where it stood: that those streets, in the moment a person finally stepped fully into their unfinished thought, were not nothing. They were the loneliest geometry in the whole range — a place even the mountain itself had not yet decided how to hold. And the old ones walked into that unheld place by themselves, every time, because that was simply what was done, the way you'd walk alone into any other room in your own house.

So the power made of itself a small, permanent thing, and folded it into that exact seam of the world, the way moss had folded itself into pipe-joints, the way ferns had rooted into rock too young to deserve them.

It did not stop the streets from being unfinished. It did not resolve the overlaps, or give the old ones a clearer ending, or explain to anyone what waited past the last threshold. It only made certain — quietly, permanently, with no ceremony the ridge-folk would ever be able to point to — that nothing walked into an unfinished thought alone again. That whoever stepped onto that fog-low street, in whichever year, found the thought finishing itself *with* them rather than around them — a companionable unraveling, a hand that was not quite a hand, present in exactly the unhurried way the mountain itself was present: not explaining, not rescuing, just there, pacing the slow dissolve the way you'd pace a long walk beside someone who didn't especially want to talk, but didn't want to walk it by themselves either.

No rhyme was added to what the children are taught. No elder ever came back to report it. The ridge-folk went on calling it a retirement, in the same dry, affectionate tone they used for everything else the mountain did that they'd long since stopped finding strange.

But somewhere in the slow arithmetic of that place — in the cooling stone, the patient cloud, the furnace-glow that still occasionally threw a shadow with too many limbs — something had shifted, by an amount too small to measure and too permanent to undo.

The mountain went on finishing itself. The people went on living gently askew, in good company with their own incompletion.

And the last walk, when it came, was no longer something anyone made by themselves.
