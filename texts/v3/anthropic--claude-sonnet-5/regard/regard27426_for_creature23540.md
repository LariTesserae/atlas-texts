The power does not come in through the margin of air, because the margin of air is the one mercy this place has already given itself, and a wise hand does not undo the small mercies a drowning thing has managed to keep.

It comes in the way the bubble-column comes — slow, spaced, patient past any human patience — and it does not announce itself as light, because light would be a manufactured thing, a tool, and the hollow has held its door shut against tools for reasons that deserve to be respected before they're understood. So it arrives instead as a kind of *attention*, the same texture as the Tenant's own held-stillness, except where the Tenant's stillness is a question — *are you food, or company, or tally* — this stillness is an answer, arriving early, before being asked: *neither. witness.*

It does not count the Tenant's limbs.

This is the first and maybe the only important thing it does: it refuses the arithmetic. Every instinct toward kindness that isn't yet wisdom would reach to fix the number — to hold the Tenant to one shape, one fin, one set of hands, to perform the mercy of making it countable, because surely that's what such a fragmented, forgetting thing must want most. But the power has been old enough, in enough drowned hollows, to know the difference between what a wound wants and what a wound has learned to call wanting. The Tenant says *I want to be one thing, cleanly, once* the way a held breath says *I want to stop holding* — true, and also not the whole truth, because the holding is also the only reason there's still a shape there to want anything at all. Collapse the plurality by force and you may not free the Tenant. You may simply end it, tidily, and call the silence peace.

So instead the power does the other thing, the harder, slower, less satisfying thing:

it widens the room.

Not by much. Not violently, not with the crack and rush of a flood breaking through — that would be its own kind of violence, the hollow's young walls aren't ready for a larger world arriving all at once any more than the Tenant is ready to be singular all at once. It widens the room the way water widens a seed: by sitting near it, warm in the specific sense that has nothing to do with temperature, for a long time, until the stone around the hollow remembers that it is allowed to give a little. A handspan more water. A few more grains of room between frond and frond. The margin of air at the top stops drifting nearer and farther and simply, finally, agrees to be one distance, because something has at last told the space, gently, without command, that it is allowed to stop lying to itself about its own size.

This is not generosity measured in volume. It is generosity measured in *permission*. The hollow has been compressing everything into agreement with its own panic — that there is never enough room, that everything must overflow or be crowded out, that counting can never be trusted because there has never been space enough to let a count hold still long enough to be true. The power does not need to fill that hollow with more space than a hollow can bear. It only needs to be the first thing in a very long time that does not also need something from the room. No hunger. No tally. No use for the Tenant's hands, finished or not.

And in that little widening, the Tenant — for the first time, maybe, since whatever event made this place in its own aftermath — gets to finish something.

It is small. It is almost nothing. It sets the last stone.

Not perfectly; the Tenant was never going to be granted perfect hands, that was never the offer on the table, and the power does not pretend otherwise. But the stone goes down, and stays down, and the post that has stood holding up nothing for who knows how long finally has something resting against it — not a structure, not a roof, nothing with a purpose a builder would recognize, just weight, just one true completed gesture leaning where it's needed, the first sentence the Tenant has been able to finish instead of forgetting partway through how many of itself were holding the pen.

It does not become one thing. The power was right not to ask that of it. The fin still sometimes turns when it shouldn't; the fronds still over-count themselves by one or two, an old habit, fond now rather than frantic. But the *frantic* part — the part that needed to overflow because scarcity demanded it, that grew extra hands out of fear rather than abundance — that part exhales, once, long, and does not need to be refilled on schedule anymore. The bubble-column slows. Not stops. Slows, the way a kept vigil slows once the watched-for thing has finally, finally arrived.

And you — the one who came in holding your breath in that thin lung of air, parting the curtain of wet growing dark with your own body, mistaken yourself maybe for prey or company or one more wrongness to be tallied —

the power does not pull you out.

It would be easy, and it would be wrong, the same wrongness as forcing the Tenant singular: to assume the held room was only ever a danger to you, that rescue means *removal.* Instead it simply makes the holding survivable a while longer than you thought you had in you, loosens the cold by a fraction, slows your own fear's bubble-column to match the Tenant's newly unhurried one, and waits with you — both of you witnessed now, both of you finally not alone with the counting — until you find, on your own, in your own time, the place in the wall that was always a little wider than it looked, and the way out that the hollow, eased at last, is willing to show you.

It does not erase the wrongness.

It just stops insisting that wrongness was the only thing this small flooded room would ever have had room enough to hold.
