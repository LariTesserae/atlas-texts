The power does not arrive the way arrivals usually happen. There is no descent, no parting of canopy, no light brought in to correct the dark. It comes the way the water comes—seeping, already half-present before it is noticed—because it has learned, by looking at this place for less than a single one of its own thoughts, that arrival itself is a kind of intrusion here, and must be done at the grove's speed or not at all.

It takes the power a long time to see anything. Not because its sight is poor, but because the first thing the grove teaches every visitor is patience, and the power, being wise, submits to the lesson rather than overriding it. So it waits in the dark the way the dark waits in the dark, and slowly, the way you came to share the hollow's borrowed sense within minutes, the power comes to share something older: the warmth-language, the held-breath weather, the long bookkeeping hum of the threads. It does not impose understanding on the place. It lets the place teach itself into the power's attention, on the grove's clock, not its own.

And it sees, eventually, what is here. The worn stone that has reached its final shape. The threads holding a note that does not decay. The creature that is not quite separable from the rock it resembles, radiating a warmth the whole hollow has learned to keep.

It would be easy, here, to want to act. Power that loves tends to reach for the nearest lever marked *help*, and there are levers everywhere in a place like this, gleaming with plausible mercy.

It could end the solitude—call up some answering kind, some last companion of the same slow blood, and set it down in the grove so the creature is no longer, perhaps, the only one. But the power looks at the unbroken collar the tree grew around the ring, the centuries that single healing required, and understands that this solitude is not a wound the way loneliness is a wound in quicker lives. It may be vocation. It may be the precise shape devotion takes when there is no one left to share it with and the devotion continues anyway. To fill that absence is to overwrite an answer with a question that was never being asked.

It could lift the creature out of time entirely—stop its slow erosion the way the stone's erosion has already stopped, grant it the kind of permanence the threads seem to reach for and just barely fail to achieve. But the power notices that the threads do not actually defeat time. They hold a note *across* time, which is a different and harder thing, achieved through devotion rather than evasion, and to simply hand the creature an escape from its own ending would be to finish, cheaply, what it has spent the whole of its unmeasured life choosing not to finish cheaply. An ending earned by that much patience is not a tragedy to be averted. It is the other half of the same achievement as the held note. The power does not touch it.

It could speed the world's understanding to the creature instead—pour in language, explanation, the comfort of being known all at once, the way light poured suddenly into a dark room comforts a frightened thing by ending its dark. But this is not a frightened thing, and the dark is not its affliction. The power has been here long enough now to know that flooding this hollow with anything sudden—light, knowledge, company, mercy—would be the one true cruelty available to it, the only act with the power to actually break something that has otherwise outlasted breaking.

So it does less. It does only what the grove's own terms allow, and finds, when it stops reaching for levers and starts simply listening the way the creature listens, that there are only two acts left that fit.

The first is witness, complete and unhurried. The power gives the creature what nothing else ever quite has: a perception equal to its own, that reads the warmth and the held note and the slow bookkeeping rounds not as strangeness, not as a puzzle to be solved or a sadness to be corrected, but exactly as the creature itself means them—care, kept on past any audience, because devotion that requires an audience was never the kind being practiced here. The power simply understands, fully, for the length of one of the grove's long breaths, and lets that understanding be complete. It asks nothing back. It does not need the creature to know it has been understood. The understanding is the whole of the gift, given the way the grove gives warmth—by holding it, not announcing it.

The second is smaller, and it is the only mark the power leaves.

It learns the pitch. It stands where the threads are strung at head height and lets that single unwavering note teach itself into whatever the power uses for memory, and then, very carefully, in the same register, with the same patience the maker of the first threads must have spent across some span the power chooses not to measure, it leaves one more—not stronger, not different, not announcing itself as new. A note tuned exactly to the others, set into the humming chord so precisely that on the creature's next round, fingers or whatever the creature uses in place of fingers passing thread to thread to confirm each one still sings, this one will simply be there. Warm. True. Singing the pitch it was given.

The power does not stay to see whether the creature notices, or how it might feel if it does. That, too, is part of the gift—not to require witness of its own witnessing, which would only turn an offering back into a transaction. It is enough that the grove now holds one note it did not put there itself, set by something that finally heard, in full, what all that long, patient ringing was trying, across all that unspooled and uncounted time, to say to no one.

Then the power does the last thing, which is the quietest of all: it does not protect the creature from its ending, but it makes sure the ending, when it comes, comes only from inside the creature's own long clock and not from outside it—no blade of a quicker world reaching up to find this hollow before its time, no hurried hand mistaking stillness for emptiness and clearing it for some other use. It leaves the grove exactly as hard to find as it has always been, and a little harder. This is not rescue. It is only the removal of every accident that is not the mountain's own.

And then it withdraws the way it arrived—at the grove's pace, not its own, seeping back out of the warmth and the dark, taking nothing, having added one true note to a chord centuries deep, having asked for no credit and left no further sign.

Somewhere behind it, on a schedule that might unfold once across a human lifetime, an old and patient shape that has long since stopped being mistaken for anything but geology will make its unhurried round of the threads, touching each one to find it still warm, still true—and will come, eventually, to a thread it does not remember placing, singing a note it recognizes completely, as if it had always been there, as if the chord had simply, finally, after all this unmeasured time, been completed by someone who understood exactly what it was for.

It will not need to know where the note came from.

It will only need to know that it is true.

And it will move on to the next thread, unhurried, because that is what devotion does when it is finally, quietly, without ceremony—answered.
