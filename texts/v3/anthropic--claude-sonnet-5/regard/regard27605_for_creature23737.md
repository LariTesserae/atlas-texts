**On the Matter of Ar-Vethna**

The power does not come down through the canopy the way you did, instrument-laden, parsing kilometers and elevation and chemical traces into numbers it can carry home. It comes down the way the things that live there do — first as a thread of order pulled through noise, indistinguishable, for a long while, from wind finding a new note in old wood. It spends what would be, by your reckoning, several years simply listening before it allows itself anything so crude as an opinion.

It learns the chord-structures. Not translates them — *learns* them, the slow hard way, the way a child learns a first language before it has any other language to measure it against. It learns what a healthy trunk-mass sounds like a kilometer off, what a clutch in distress sounds like through three boles of fiber-lattice, what the worst register of the deep engines means to a creature whose whole concept of *wrong* lives in its spine. It does this because it has been wise long enough to know that the very first thing a benevolent power must survive is the temptation to help a thing it has not yet learned to hear.

And once it can hear, the temptations come properly, one after another, and it turns each one over with the kind of care you give a blade you are choosing not to use.

It could give them eyes that work the way yours do. It does not. Sight, here, would not be a gift — it would be a demotion, a flooding-out of an entire faculty of understanding with a cruder one, the spine-ridge's symphonic knowledge of *direction, distance, kin, weather, danger* traded down for a few hundred meters of strobing, contradictory dapple-light that means something different every few seconds. They are not blind. They have simply declined a sense that would be, for them, mostly noise.

It could tell them what the engines are. It does not. It would be easy — the power can taste the engines' history the way you might taste mineral content in water, can trace them back through a slow tangled chain of makers and abandonments and absorptions to an answer that would fit in a single sentence. But the kin-clusters do not relate to the engines as *fact*. They relate to them as *terrain*, the way they relate to a leaning trunk or a cold front — something you read with your whole tended attention and respond to with your hands, not something you *know about*. To hand them an explanation would be to hand them a wall between themselves and a thing they have spent a hundred generations learning to listen to instead. An answer, here, would be a kind of silencing.

It could quiet the storms. It could, with very little effort, smooth the pressure-fronts that flatten lesser things against the decking, could make the noise-floor gentler, kinder, more survivable for something built less ferociously than they are. It does not, because it has understood by now — fully, not theoretically — that the storms are not an obstacle the canopy-folk endure but the medium in which they think. A silence, here, would not be mercy. It would be the one thing in this world capable of killing them by *removing harm*.

It could end the long brutal trade between the kin-clusters and the screaming flying things — predator and prey and predator again, season folding into season. It does not, because it has watched long enough to see that this trade is not cruelty mismanaged but the actual shape of how care moves through this world: nothing fed without something else first emptied, the engines warmed by tending the way a clutch is warmed by an engine, the whole place one continuous exchange with no surplus anywhere, no waste, no center, nothing that could be called *charity* because everything is already owed to something. To introduce mercy here, unasked, in a system this exactly balanced, would be to introduce rot of a kind the trunks have never had to learn to survive.

So it does, in the end, almost nothing. This is, for a power of this kind, the harder discipline — not the restraint of cruelty, which is easy, but the restraint of *generosity*, which is not.

What it allows itself is one act, chosen because it costs the place nothing of what the place already understands itself to be.

Deep in the root-architecture, below where even the engines' own makers ever likely went, there are a handful of drivetrains running thinner than the rest — old past any oral memory, their housings worn down to a fineness that will not, on any timescale the kin-clusters could plan around, last forever. The power does not repair them. Repair would require explaining them, and explaining them would require the wall it has already refused to build. Instead it does something closer to what the kin-clusters themselves already do without knowing they do it: it leaves something in the *tending*. A fineness of attention, folded into instinct rather than instruction, threaded so quietly into the spine-ridge's inherited reading of vibration that no one now living will ever notice it arrived from anywhere. Generations hence, when a particular engine's failing register finally starts to sound — not wrong in the way the breeding-season register is wrong, but wrong in a new way, an ending way — some descendant of these clusters will simply *know*, the way they already know to pack mineral-rot into a warming intake, exactly what the new sound is asking for. They will not know they were given anything. They will believe, correctly in every way that matters to them, that they figured it out themselves.

That is the whole of the gift. A held door, generations away, disguised as instinct, asking nothing, explaining nothing, costing the place none of its hard-won, unliterate, total understanding of itself.

Then the power leaves the way it arrived — not as a shape withdrawing through canopy, but as a thread of order quietly stops being pulled through the noise, until what's left is only the wind finding its notes again, the storm-roar, the tens of thousands erupting and settling, the engines turning somewhere down in root-depth exactly as they have for longer than anyone arrived to measure — and the kin-clusters, spine-ridges flared to the full chord of it, hear nothing in the change at all, because there was nothing in it meant to be heard.

This, the power has decided, is what wisdom mostly looks like, from outside: a great deal of listening, several refusals, and one piece of help small enough to disappear completely into a world that was already, before anyone arrived to love it, doing perfectly well at being itself.
