What notices this place is not anything that moves through it the way light moves through it, or sound, or the slow gold attention of the keeper's eye. It arrives the way understanding arrives — all at once and from no particular direction — and it takes its time anyway, because taking time is itself a kind of respect, and because anything that wanted to *act* before it had finished *looking* would already have failed the only test that matters here.

It looks for a long while. Longer, proportionally, than it has ever looked at anything. It takes in the engine's fuelless breathing, the moss-softened iron, the frogs calling back into the clank like a congregation finishing a priest's line. It takes in the no-path-in and the no-path-out, the way the glade has spent however many decades quietly deciding that nothing about itself needs to be reached. It takes in the keeper last, because she is the part most easily mistaken for needing something, and it wants to be sure, before it decides anything, that it is not about to mistake a finished thing for an unfinished one.

And here is what it considers, and sets back down, one by one, the way you might pick up several tools in a dark room and replace each, finding none of them right for a job that turns out, on reflection, not to be a job:

It considers explaining the engine to her — unspooling, in one bright unbearable instant, the factory, the hand, the century, the *why*, so that her endless attendance might finally resolve into knowledge. And it sets this down, because her listening was never a question waiting on an answer. An answer would not complete her vigil. It would end it, the way a held note ends the moment you name it.

It considers silencing the engine, or feeding it, or fixing whatever ancient unfixed thing allows it to run on nothing — tidying the impossibility into something explicable and safe. And it sets this down too, because the impossibility is not a wound in the world. It is closer to a held breath that the whole glade has organized itself around continuing to hold, and a benevolence that cannot tell the difference between a wound and a held breath has not yet earned the name.

It considers lifting her — giving her speech, or longevity made explicit and known to her, or company of her own kind, or release from the metal, some grand gesture of liberation aimed at a captivity that, on every inspection, does not appear to exist. She is not chained to the engine. She has simply, across a span of time too long for either of them to remember its beginning, decided to keep listening to it, the way the moss decided to keep growing toward the warmth. To "free" her would be to assume her listening was a sentence and not a vow, and there is no way to make that assumption kindly.

So it sets all of that down, and what is left, after every easy and generous and wrong gesture has been considered and declined, is something much smaller, and much harder to do well: it looks at her, fully, on purpose, with the whole of its attention, for exactly as long as a long moment takes — and it lets her feel the looking.

This is the one thing the glade has never had occasion to give her. Everyone here has settled, long ago, into the shape of everyone else's hunger and habit; the birds ignore her, the glassy thing orbits past her skull without turning its small intent toward her at all, the frogs roost in her shadow on a truce so old it has stopped being a truce and become simply weather. She has spent the whole of her unmeasured life as the one who attends, never once as the one attended *to*. And so the gift, when it comes, is not a transformation. It does not change the rhythm of her plates or the patience of her hunger or the iron-bright color she has spent decades matching herself toward. It only arrives, briefly, as a kind of warmth in the green-gold light that has no source she can place — the way the engine itself has no source she can place — and her lidless gold eye does what it does for any new thing moving through her field of registering: it takes a long moment, and it does not look away, and something in the back of that ancient optical ringing settles, very slightly, the way a held note settles when, for once, in all the years of holding it alone, something else holds it too, just for a moment, just enough to be felt and not so much as to be remembered as an event.

That is the whole of the gift. No words passed. No name given, because names were never how this glade organized itself, and a name handed down from outside would have been one more explanation she didn't ask for. No change to the engine, the frogs, the moss, the seep weeping its slow black water into the humus. The clank-and-sigh continues exactly as it continued before, indifferent, patient, looped, and she returns her throat to the warm casing within the same unhurried interval she has always returned it, and the glassy-winged thing keeps its slow orbit of the hottest pipe, unbothered, because nothing about its hunger or hers has been asked to change.

The only other thing the power does — and it does this the way you'd close a door softly rather than lock it — is lean, very slightly, on the *quality* of arrival the glade already permits. It does not seal the place. It does not wall it further than the walls of leaf and vine already wall it. It simply makes a fraction more certain that whatever finds this pocket of green will find it the way a held breath should be found: by looking long enough to stop sliding past it, by noticing the moss blink. The kind of attention that would come to dissect, to extract, to drag the engine into a workshop and demand its secret under bright light — that kind continues, very quietly, to lose the path. The kind that comes the way you came, slowly, by sound before sight, willing to stand in wet warm air and simply *see* what has settled here without needing it to become anything else — that kind, the glade goes on permitting, exactly as it always did, because that kind was never the danger.

And then the power is gone, in the same directionless way it arrived, leaving no mark anywhere that could be pointed to, no new tool half-built into the casing, no plaque, no clearing. The engine breathes its iron breath. The frogs answer it. The keeper lies along the warm flank with her throat pressed to the metal, gold eye half-lidded, registering the next clank as it comes up through brass and bone exactly the way she has registered ten thousand clanks before it — except that somewhere in the long unbothered architecture of her listening, there is now a moment, small as a held breath, that knows what it is to have been listened to in return. She does not dwell on it. She has no instinct for dwelling. She simply continues, plate by plate, in time with the old machine, in the green-gold light, for as long as anyone could come to look — a fraction more sure, though she could not say of what, that the holding was worth it.
