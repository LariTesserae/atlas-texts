# What Arrives, and What Doesn't

The power comes the way the light does—already there, filtering through vapor, touching everything and seizing nothing. It does not announce itself with parted clouds or stilled wind. If it has a body in this moment, the body is the haze that softens every edge in the basin, and it sees the sluice fields the way the sluice fields deserve to be seen: all at once, in their entirety, the dozen leaning structures and the reshuffling pools and the eel-bodied thing pressed flush along a warm pipe-run, all of it legible simultaneously, the way you might finally read a difficult sentence once you stop trying to parse it clause by clause and let the whole shape of it arrive together.

It understands, in that first total apprehension, every easy thing it could do. This is the danger of being both benevolent and capable. The benevolence wants so badly to *act* that it will reach for the nearest verb—*heal*, *save*, *fix*, *uplift*—the way a hand reaches for a railing on a stair that doesn't actually require one.

## The Temptations, Considered and Set Down

It could stop the rot. Every rivet, held forever at the moment of its greatest integrity; every gate, hung true; every pipe-joint, sealed against all future weeping. The basin would gleam. The basin would also, instantly, stop being the basin. The place is not a wound to be closed but a process to be witnessed, and a process that cannot fail is a process that has been quietly murdered and propped up in its own likeness. The power feels the wrongness of this option the way you'd feel the wrongness of stopping a river to preserve a particular wave.

It could lift the creature into understanding. Hand it language, kinship, the concept of *home* and *care* and *purpose*, so that its solitary gradient-chasing might become something nameable, something it could be praised for, something that could be loved back in a legible way. The power turns this one over longest, because it looks the most like a gift. But it isn't. The creature is not lonely. Loneliness requires a self that notices its own absence of company, and this small grey-green thing has no such self—it has barbels, and pressure, and the satisfaction of a leak resolving under its own paste, and that is a whole life, not a deprived one. To install a longing in a being that has none is not generosity. It is a kind of violence dressed as a kindness, the cruelest because it would feel, from the inside of the act, like love.

It could make itself a friend to it. Approach slow, hand outstretched in whatever gesture translates across that wide a gap, and ask to be allowed near. But the power remembers what the basin's own rules already teach every visitor: this creature equalizes attention by leaving. To be seen, for it, is a kind of pressure, and the kindest pressures are the ones never applied. Friendship offered to a thing that flees from being watched is not friendship. It's a net with a kind voice.

So the power sets each of these down, gently, the way you'd set down tools you picked up out of habit rather than need.

## The Question Underneath

What it asks instead is smaller and harder: *what does this place want, on its own terms, that it cannot get for itself?*

And the honest answer, arrived at slowly, turning the basin over in its attention like a stone in water, is: *very little.* The pools want to keep moving, and they do. The iron wants to lose its argument with time, slowly, and it is. The reeds want their stubborn purchase in the cracks, and they have it. The creature wants pressure differentials to nose into and seal or worsen according to no design but its own passing interest, and the basin supplies these without end, a buffet that never closes, because the basin is built—by water, by rust, by nobody—to never stop generating exactly the kind of small crisis this creature finds delicious.

There is only one true gap. One place where the system, left alone, would eventually deal a hand crueler than the place's own logic warrants—not unkind in the way erosion is unkind, which is no way at all, but unkind in the way that an ending can be unkind if it arrives as failure rather than completion.

The creature is old. Old enough that the structures have outlived several versions of themselves in its company. It will, someday, be doing what it always does—pressed to a pipe-throat, reading a gradient, packing a wound with mud and patience—and its body will simply stop answering the request. This is not a tragedy peculiar to it. It is the basin's own rule, the one under all the others: *nothing here is owed permanence.* The power has no quarrel with that rule. It would not lift the creature out from under it even if lifting were a kindness, because immortality granted from outside a system is just another way of stopping a river.

But it can attend to *how* the rule is paid out. Not whether. How.

## What Is Given

So this is what the power does, and it is almost nothing, which is exactly the size of gift this place can hold without distortion:

It does not still a single gate. It does not seal a single leak ahead of schedule or warn the iron away from its long collapse. It does not speak to the creature, or let itself be seen, or alter the gradient-map of territories that the creature alone can read.

It simply ensures—quietly, structurally, the way a basin's slope ensures water runs one way and not another—that when the creature's last gradient comes, the one it cannot solve, it will feel like all the others. Not an emptying-out, not a failure noticed from inside as failure, but one more pressure equalizing, indistinguishable in the texture of the moment from every small success that came before it. The last leak it ever noses into will feel, to whatever passes for its peace, exactly as satisfying as the first. The system will not announce to it that this time is different. It will be allowed to die the way it lived: mid-task, uncelebrated, interested, unaware that interest was the whole of what it ever needed and that it had, by that measure, nothing left to want.

This is the entire intervention. A mercy folded so far inside the place's own grain that it will never look like one. No one standing in the basin afterward—not the rare visitor, not the basin itself, if a basin could be said to notice—will find a seam where the power's hand passed through. The rust will rust at its own rate. The gates will still cycle on the timer nobody set, until the day they don't, and something else, eventually, may or may not come to take the creature's place, drawn by the same fine fringe-sense to the same eternal small emergencies. The power does not arrange for a successor. That would be one tenderness too many, a thumb on a scale that was never meant to be read so closely.

## What Remains

The power withdraws the way it arrived: not as a door closing, but as a focus loosening, light becoming merely light again instead of an attention shaped like light. It leaves no monument, asks for no acknowledgment, performs no rescue a visitor could point to and say *there, that's where grace happened.*

If you walked the sluice fields after, you would find them unchanged in every particular that matters to walking. The pools still reshuffle. The conveyor still turns on its belt of nothing. The grey-green shape still slides off its warm pipe at the edge of your attention and returns to the water with that single heavy plop.

But it is, now, held—not against time, which would be a theft, but *within* time, the way a hand can cup water without stopping its flow. The only thing that has changed is the kind of ending available to one small, shy, unaccountably old thing that never asked for an ending of any particular kind, and would not have known what to do with the question if you'd put it to those wide, low, nearly useless eyes.

That not-knowing is exactly what was protected. The power judged, in the end, that the deepest respect it could pay this place was to leave its incomprehension intact—to let the creature go on not understanding that it was ever, even once, attended to.
