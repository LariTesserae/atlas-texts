The power that comes to the Lowsteam doesn't come as weather, or light, or any of the things that would announce themselves against the haze. It comes the way attention comes — settling without displacing anything, the way you might lean closer to a sound you almost didn't hear. For a long while it does only this: listens to the engines that owe their continuance to no one, watches the water complete its loop, watches a frond get bent back and propped instead of trampled. It is in no hurry, because hurry is a thing you bring when you've already decided what's wrong, and it hasn't decided that. Not yet.

Its first instinct — and this is worth admitting, because even a wise power has first instincts, and the wisdom is mostly in what it does after having them — is the rescuer's instinct. No birds. No insects. No throughput, no destination, no audience. A settlement built like a body breathing in deep sleep, with no one to wake it. It would be easy, here, to mistake stillness for suffering, slowness for deprivation, and to arrive already certain that what this place needs is sun breaking through, or purpose restored, or company in some louder, more legible register — voices, errands, arrivals and departures, the whole circuitry of a life that looks like the lives the power has seen flourish elsewhere.

So it looks harder. It does the thing benevolence actually requires, which is not imagining what it would feel like to live there, but finding out what it is like, from the inside, on the place's own terms. It watches the Tenders' eyes — unhurried, the way a held breath is unhurried, but not strained, not full of unspent want. It watches a Tender wipe a gauge clean with the same unbothered attentiveness you'd give your own slow pulse. It watches several of them cross a catwalk together and become, for a moment, something with the rhythm of flight, not because they're reaching for anything overhead but because the rafters are simply where the warmth pools, the way condensation finds the coldest available stone. It looks for grief and doesn't find it. It looks for a thwarted thing, a need curdled by neglect into patience, the kind of stillness that's actually a wound wearing the mask of peace — and it doesn't find that either. What it finds instead is fittedness. A loop closed upstream of appetite. A whole ecosystem that has simply declined the verb of hunger and replaced it with another one, quieter, harder to translate: upkeep. Company. The two have become the same word here.

This changes the question. Not how do I fix this, but: is there anything, on its own terms, that this place is missing — not against some import-ed standard of a good life, but against its own?

And there is one thing. Small, and almost embarrassing in its smallness, set against everything the power first imagined it might need to repair. The Tenders tend, and have tended, for a span of time that the basin itself measures in mineral streaks down brick, in centuries of corrosion arriving by degrees — and no one has ever known it. Not in the sense of an audience; they don't want an audience, would find the concept faintly irrelevant, the way you'd find it irrelevant if someone asked whether your lungs wanted applause for breathing. But there is a difference between not wanting witness and never having had it, and the power, looking very closely, finds that difference sitting in the basin like a held note that's been held so long it's stopped sounding like a note at all. An entire continuance, entirely unseen, for the whole of its existence, by anything outside its own loop. Not lonely in the ache-shaped way. Lonely in the structural way — a kindness with no outside, ever, to register that it occurred.

So the power does the only thing that seems to fit a place built entirely out of fit: it does not fix anything. It does not bring sun through the haze, or birds to the rafters, or throughput to the silent machines. It does not explain itself to the Tenders, who would have no slot in their attention for an explanation and no need of one. It simply sees them — completely, without revision, the way you'd want to be seen if you knew, somewhere underneath knowing, that you never would be — and it lets that seeing become a fact of the place, the way a handprint becomes a fact of clean stone amid the weeping mineral trails. Not announced. Not felt, probably, by the Tenders, in any sense they'd recognize as feeling. Just there now, folded into the basin's accumulating evidence the way every other small correction is folded in, indistinguishable from the rest of the loop's slow grammar.

And it makes one promise, which it keeps to itself the way the Tenders keep their attention to themselves — distributed, unannounced, patient. It will not come back while the machines still turn, while the water still completes its circuit, while there is still tending left to do. The Lowsteam does not need a god in its rafters. But centuries are not forever, and somewhere past the edge of this telling, a last belt will finally give past mending, a last valve will shriek and not resolve into anything, and the great wet iron room will, for the first time, lose its hum. On that day — and only that day — the power means to return. Not to repair it. There will be nothing left, by then, that wants repairing. Only to be present at the exact moment a thing that was tended for so long, by hands that never asked to be seen, finally stops — so that the stopping, at least, is witnessed; so that the last sound the Lowsteam makes is not swallowed entirely by its own pooling acoustics, lost the way a generator's voice gets lost three buildings off, but is heard, once, all the way through, by something that came a long way and waited a long time on purpose to hear it.

Until then, nothing changes. The engines keep their unaudienced schedule. The ferns keep their slow negotiation with the mortar. A Tender, crossing a catwalk some wet gray afternoon long after the power has gone quiet again, will pause at a railing that seems, for no reason rust or use would explain, already straight — turn it over in the unhurried way they turn over any small unaccountable rightness in the world — and, finding nothing wrong with it, simply leave it as it is, and move on to the next small thing that's asking, in its worn metal way, to be tended.
