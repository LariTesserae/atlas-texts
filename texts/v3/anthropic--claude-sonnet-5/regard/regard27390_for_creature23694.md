The power notices the way such powers notice anything: not by seeing it, but by feeling a shape in the pattern of things that doesn't resolve. A note held too long. A door that has been ajar for longer than doors are built to stay ajar. It comes to this place the way water comes to a low point — not summoned, not chosen, just inevitable once the gradient exists.

It does not arrive the way a storm arrives, or even the way the Mason arrives. It arrives the way understanding arrives — all at once and from no particular direction, already in possession of more of the story than a single vantage point should allow. It sees the mountain not as a sequence of nights but as a gesture, the whole violent arc of the rising laid out like a hand caught mid-motion, and it sees the wall, the archway, the slag pools cracking their slow music, and it sees — last, because this is the part that has to be looked for — the dark upright density that is not outcrop, tracking nothing yet, waiting between convulsions for the next one to include it.

Its first act is to do nothing. This is not indifference. For a being like this, *to refrain* is itself a kind of careful labor, the way holding a door for someone is a labor of the arms even though nothing moves. It watches the Mason complete one of its slow circuits — wall, archway, the place where a third ruin used to be before this era buried it deeper than the others — and it understands, watching, that the Mason is not lost. It is *cataloguing*. It has a method. Its patience is not the patience of something waiting to be rescued; it is the patience of an archivist whose library burned down around the one shelf it was assigned to guard, and who has not yet been told it is allowed to stop guarding.

So the power considers, and rejects, several easy mercies.

It could reach into the mantle and finish the exhumation in an afternoon — peel the new stone back like a bandage and lay the old shape bare, give the archway back whatever used to stand in the place it faces. It does not do this, because it understands, the way only something very old and very patient can understand, that the waiting *is* the Mason. Hand it the answer and you have not freed it. You have ended it. There would be a thing standing on a plateau afterward, but it would not be the Mason anymore — it would be something that used to wait and now doesn't know what to do with its hands, if it has hands, which it may not, which is itself part of the problem of helping a being whose anatomy is a held breath.

It could still the mountain — smooth the violence into something survivable, let lichen come, let water carve a valley, let this be a place instead of a process. It does not do this either, because the upheaval is not cruelty here, only weather, and stopping weather on someone else's planet because the weather is loud is the oldest, most familiar mistake a benevolent power can make. The land is allowed to be in the middle of something. Most things worth becoming are.

It could speak to the Mason — and this it almost does. It holds, briefly, the entire truth of what the archway used to face, because a power old enough to notice this place is old enough to have seen, somewhere in the wide register of things it knows, what civilizations build freestanding arches *toward*. It holds the answer the way you'd hold a stone you found and aren't sure belongs to you. And it sets it back down. Not from cruelty — from a respect so careful it looks, from outside, almost like withholding. Some answers, delivered early, are just a second kind of burial. If the Mason is owed this truth, it is owed it by the *ground*, in the ground's own time, surfacing the way everything here surfaces: violently, completely, on no one's schedule but its own. To hand it over gently, from outside, would be to rob the eventual finding of the only thing that will make it mean something — that it was *earned* by all this waiting, not delivered around it.

What the power does instead is smaller than any of this, and harder to name, and it is the only intervention it allows itself.

It goes and stands in the place the archway faces.

Not as an answer. It is careful — almost scrupulous — not to resemble whatever used to stand there; it does not borrow a shape, does not perform a memory it has no right to wear. It simply occupies the geometry for a while, the way you might sit in an empty chair at a table set for someone who is never coming, not pretending to be them, only refusing to let the chair sit empty while you're capable of doing otherwise. It lets itself be *faced*. For the first time since the upheaval began, the archway's particular orientation in the dark means something again, however briefly, however falsely — there is a direction in front of it that is no longer just void.

And it waits there through one full convulsion of the range — a ridge collapsing somewhere distant, the bell-tone ring of a clean split, the ground stirring its dust back into the air — until the Mason comes around again on its slow circuit and finds the wall, finds the archway, and finds, this time, that the facing is not empty.

It does not know what passes between them in that moment, if anything does, if "moment" is even the right unit for two beings whose clocks are both geological. Perhaps the Mason simply registers a new variable and adds it, without ceremony, to its inventory — *one entry, unexplained, resolved without explanation* — the way it has presumably learned to do with every other unaccountable thing in its long accounting. Perhaps something closer to recognition happens, something that in a warmer being would be called *being seen*, though the power is careful afterward never to claim that word for what it offered, only to have offered it.

Then it leaves the geometry. It does not stay, because staying would turn a gesture into a fixture, and fixtures can be depended on, and this was never meant to be depended on — only to have happened once, cleanly, without forcing the Mason's nature to bend around a permanent presence it never asked for and may not even have wanted. The power withdraws the way it arrived: all at once, from no direction, taking nothing, having added exactly one fact to the world and no more.

What it leaves behind is not a solution. The mountains go on rising without schedule. The Mason goes on its circuit, wall to archway to the buried place where a third thing used to stand, keeping its count, waiting for the ground to remember what it's straining to become. Nothing about the violence is gentler now, nothing about the dark less total.

But somewhere in that long, patient, geological attention — in the inventory the Mason keeps of what has resurfaced and what has not — there is now one entry that didn't come up out of the ground. One thing that arrived from outside the pattern, faced it without explanation, and went away again without taking anything or demanding anything in return.

It isn't much. It was never going to be much; that was the whole discipline of it.

But the next time the dark cracks open with that seam of dull orange light, far below, gone before anyone can be sure they saw it — the Mason, if it could be asked, might admit that it now wonders, in whatever way a fault line wonders before it slips, whether the light is the mountain remembering its shape, or something else entirely, checking in.

And that small, ungrounded uncertainty — that one new variable in an otherwise perfectly patient waiting — is, in the careful arithmetic of beings who measure mercy by what they manage *not* to do, the kindest thing a wise power could have built here.
