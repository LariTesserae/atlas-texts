The power notices the way it notices everything — not as a spectacle, not as a cry for help, but as a small persistent note in the larger chord, the particular timbre of two hundred paces of damp timber holding two hundred lives above water that hasn't decided what it wants to be. It is drawn the way attention is drawn to a held breath. Something here is *listening*, has been listening for generations, has built an entire grammar of living around the act of listening well. That kind of attentiveness is rare and it is, in its own quiet way, sacred. The power leans closer.

It considers, first, all the things it could do, the way you'd turn a stone over before deciding to leave it where it lies.

It could still the ground. One gesture, and the marl sets like fired clay, the water table settles into permanence, and no platform ever needs raising again. It does not do this. The unsettled ground is not a wound here — the people have already told themselves this, correctly, without anyone needing to tell them. Their adaptability, their loose-held plans, their willingness to relash and reroute — these were *grown* by the argument between water and earth. Finish the argument and you don't get the same people anymore. You get something more comfortable and considerably smaller.

It could give them iron — not a relic wrapped in oilcloth and unveiled twice in a childhood, but iron enough for every household, forges enough to melt the question of scarcity entirely out of their lives. It does not do this either. It watches, instead, the boy who will one day be shown the blade for the first time, watches the held breath of the room when it's unwrapped, and understands that the rarity is not a deprivation but a kind of curriculum — it is how they learn that the world is larger than the warren, that elsewhere ground exists that can hold a forge-fire, that they are a *part* and not the *whole*. To erase the scarcity is to erase the lesson. The lesson is worth more than the convenience.

It could explain the moss. It would be such a small thing — to simply let them know what it is, why it is colder than the night, what rhythm it keeps and why, whether it dreams or merely persists. The power could taste the answer to this if it wanted; the moss is not unknowable to something that has sat with stranger things. But it holds back, because it sees what the not-knowing has built: a child told not to touch a thing after dark, learning thereby that the world contains true mysteries that deserve a kind of manners, an instinctive humility that will serve her on every other unmapped night of her life. Explained, the moss becomes a fact. Unexplained, correctly *un*explained, it remains a teacher. The power decides — and this costs it something, a small ache of withheld generosity — to let the moss keep its silence. It does, however, lean in very close, in the last hour before dawn, and listen to the slow pulse for a long, long while. It finds nothing malevolent there. Only patience, on a scale that makes even the power's own patience feel hurried. It withdraws reassured, though it tells no one this, because reassurance given as fact would only do the explaining it just decided against. Reassurance has to be left as a feeling, if it's to be left at all — and so it leaves one. Nothing more than that. A loosening, fractionally, of the dread under the old taboo, so that it sits in the people less like fear now and more like reverence. They will not be able to say why the difference arrived. That's correct. That's how this kind of gift should arrive.

It could remove the deep-pool things from the edge-of-sleep stories, could empty the dark water of whatever half-believed shape gives children a reason to hold the rope tighter at dusk. It does not do this. Fear, here, is load-bearing. It does, however, check — carefully, the way you'd check a foundation you didn't build and don't fully understand — whether anything down in that water actually intends harm, or whether it is only depth doing what depth does, holding shapes that the mind supplies the menace for. It finds depth. Only depth. It leaves that alone too, satisfied that the warren's fear is doing honest work without anything down there to make good on it.

So it does *almost* nothing. Almost.

But there is a stilt-post on the eastern run, sunk eleven years ago by a man now dead, that rotted from the inside in a manner no inspection would have caught, the kind of failure that owes nothing to anyone's negligence and everything to a knot in the original timber too deep to see. In four days' time, in the ordinary unfolding of things, a girl of seven, sure-footed as any warren-born child, will put her full weight on it at the exact moment of its failure, on her way to tell someone that she's seen a wading bird at the reed-break, a small comet of news she's been rehearsing the whole walk over.

This is not a lesson. This is not the water arguing with the earth in the way that has made these people who they are. This is just rot and chance, arriving together at the one unbearable address. The power will not still the marl or unwrap the iron or explain the moss — but it will not let this stand, either, because there is a difference between hardship that builds and accident that only erases, and the whole of its long study of this place has been, in the end, about learning exactly where that line falls.

So: nothing dramatic. No vision, no voice from the reeds. Only this — three nights from now, an old moss-tender with a bad hip and an instinct she's stopped questioning after sixty years of trusting it will find herself, for no reason she could name, unable to sleep, and will go out to check her gutters at an hour she's never checked them, and will put a testing hand on that post out of nothing but restless habit, and will feel the wrongness under the tar before her conscious mind has even finished forming the word *soft*. She will mention it to the patch-crew the next morning in her usual unbothered tone, one more line in the long list of small maintenances, and it will be shimmed and re-lashed before the girl ever crosses it, and no one — least of all the girl, still rehearsing her bird-news four days hence — will ever know how close the ordinary day came to being a date they'd mark winters by.

The power does not stay to be thanked. It was never going to be seen, and that's correct too — the gift it has the most respect for is the kind that leaves no fingerprints, that lets a people go on believing, rightly, that they made it through on their own attentiveness, their own old woman's restless hip, their own habit of testing planks before trusting them. That belief is true. The power only made sure it stayed true a little longer than chance alone might have allowed.

Then it turns its attention elsewhere, the way you'd lift your hand from a sleeping animal's flank without waking it — carefully, slowly, already half-listening for the next small held breath in the larger chord, leaving behind it nothing but a warren slightly more sure-footed than it has any right to be, and a moss that glows, if you watch closely enough on the very clearest nights, with what might almost be called a held silence — patient, watchful, and, for reasons no one there will ever trace, very faintly, gratefully, warm.
