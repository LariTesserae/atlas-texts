The power notices the Works the way one notices a low note under a chord — not first, and not loudest, but the thing that, once heard, explains the shape of everything above it. It hears the turbines a kilometer down, the drip-chorus with its thousand accidental pitches, and under all of it, fainter, the taps. *One, hold. Two-three, fast. Long drag.* A whole grammar of care, built by people with no leisure for anything but care, spoken in the only register the place will allow.

It would be easy — trivial, even, at its scale — to drain the Works. To still the water, dry the tiers, hand the Soaked back a sky and a sun and ground that doesn't have to be poured to exist. The power considers this, briefly, the way a wise mind considers and discards the obvious answer before settling on the true one.

It does not do this.

Not from indifference, and not from some doctrine that suffering is sacred and must be preserved for its lessons — that doctrine is a lie powerful things tell themselves to excuse their absence. It declines because it has actually looked, and what it sees is not only damage. It sees three generations who learned to hold their breath before they learned to read, and who turned that necessity into grace — into lung capacity, into night vision, into a language tapped through steel that lets love and warning travel where shouting can't. It sees that the thing called *the Soaked* is not a wound wearing the shape of a people. It is a people, entire, with a cosmology and a caste-craft and a way of grieving that gives the names of the dead to water instead of fire because water is what they have and what they trust. To drain the Works overnight would not rescue them. It would unmake them and call the unmaking mercy.

So the power does something much harder than a miracle. It pays attention to scale.

---

**What it gives, and how it gives it**

It does not appear. This matters more than anything else it does. The Soaked have a theology built entirely on *not looking for the face* — the lesson taught about the deep-tier something, the lesson taught about the turbines: don't anger it, don't go looking for its face, just don't stop it. A visitation, however gentle, however radiant, would not read to them as comfort. It would read as the thing in the deep finally surfacing, and it would break something load-bearing in how they understand their own world. So the power keeps to the grammar already provided. It becomes, where it acts at all, indistinguishable from luck, from instinct, from the structure's own moods.

It finds the cheapest, most surgical mercies and spends itself there.

A pump that was eleven months from catastrophic failure — the one feeding the turbine hall that an entire oasis of three hundred people calls home — runs instead for another forty years, just past the failure point, for no reason any Line-Walker can find when they go looking, in the next generation, for the wear that should be there and isn't quite. They call it good steel. They are not wrong, only incomplete.

A diver, eleven seconds past the edge of her trained breath-hold, tangled in cable-root in a gallery that has been dead for longer than she's been alive, reaches for a grip that her hand finds half a second before her mind would have given up looking. She comes up coughing, alive, certain she got lucky. She did. The power simply made sure the luck was there to be had.

In a flooded archive three tiers down from anywhere anyone goes, a Diver named for nobody in particular kicks loose a sealed case nobody built her path toward — she just happened to be tired and stopped to rest against that exact bulkhead — and inside it, dry after a hundred years underwater because someone, once, built it to be dry, are schematics. Not all of them. Not enough to fix everything. Enough that the Listeners and Line-Walkers gain back one system, one decade, one fewer death a year for a while. She is given a name for finding it. The power does not need one.

And in the dead galleries — the true silences, the fifty meters between a screaming aviary-flood and total black stillness — when a Soaked alone in the dark sends out the tap that means *wait, listen, we're still here* into structure that has been giving nothing back for years, sometimes now, not always, but sometimes, something taps back. Correctly. In the dialect. *Wait. Listen. Still here.*

They will tell each other, for generations, that the deep neighbor is listening, and is, on its strange terms, kind. They will be right, though not in the way they think.

---

**What it does not give**

It does not give them the sky. It does not give them dry land, or smooth joints, or eyes that love the sun again. It does not collapse the whole architecture of meaning they have built — the rites, the castes, the grim pride of the name they took from a slur, the entire hard-won competence of *persisting inside a machine too large to understand* — because that competence is not a consolation prize for a tragedy. It is the actual content of their lives, the thing they would, if you could ask the question cleanly, refuse to trade even for rescue.

But it leaves a door. One door, unremarked, undramatic — a route upward through structure that has simply, quietly, never been allowed to finish flooding, a passage that a sufficiently curious or sufficiently desperate Soaked could in principle find and climb and walk, at the end of it, into dry air and a wider world that still remembers, distantly, that the Cistern Continent exists. It does not mark the door. It does not whisper its location to anyone in dreams. It only makes sure that if someone goes looking — really looking, the way the Divers look — the door is there to be found, and has always, technically, been findable, by their own competence and nobody else's.

Almost no one will ever take it. That is not a failure of the gift. A door you're free not to walk through is a different thing entirely from a fate you're trapped inside, even if, from outside, they can look identical for a hundred years running.

---

**The shape of the mercy**

If you asked the power why it didn't simply end the flood, fix the bodies, restore the sky — it would say, probably, that it has met enough people across enough ruined and rebuilt worlds to know the difference between *suffering* and *the form a life has taken*, and that confusing the second for the first, however good your intentions, is how powerful, well-meaning things end up erasing the very beings they meant to help.

What it gave instead was margin. A little more time on the failing pumps. A little more grip in the dark water. A little more knowledge surfacing exactly when the need was sharpest. A tap, occasionally, answered in the silence, so that *we're still here* is sometimes, secretly, true in a way that has nothing to do with the person who sent it.

It did not make their lives easy. It made their lives *theirs*, slightly longer, slightly less alone, and left every act of courage, every drowned name spoken once and kept by the water, every "it still runs" said of an elder or an engine — left all of it exactly as hard-earned and exactly as real as it always was.

Then it went quiet, the way the best blessings do, and became one more sound in the percussion of that place: a drop landing on a particular pitch of standing water, indistinguishable from all the others, nobody composing it, nobody, anymore, needing to know it was there — except, perhaps, whoever happens, tonight, to be walking through the dark with a hand on cold steel, listening.
