# What Comes to the Rim

Attention, when it is old and patient enough, doesn't arrive the way weather arrives, or visitors, or anything else the hollow has learned to read in advance by the heavier rustle overhead. It simply turns, the way a face turns toward a sound it didn't quite hear but felt. One day — though *day* is the wrong unit for something that doesn't live inside the hollow's cycles of coin-light and cool exhalation — the place is noticed the way you might notice, mid-conversation, a small warmth on the back of your hand you hadn't registered as sunlight until just then.

It looks for a long while before it does anything at all. This is the first mercy, though it doesn't announce itself as one: it does not act first and understand later. It sits with the place — with the seam that holds borrowed heat, with the two-note calls answered sparingly across long silences, with the stone ring gone illegible with moss, with the long-handed creatures pressed flank to rock at midday like something leaning into a memory it can't quite place — and it lets the looking take as long as the looking needs.

## What It Considers, and Sets Down Again

There is a version of help that would lift the rim away entirely — widen the gap, let in real sky, let the hollow-dwellers' hands remember, all at once, everything they've slowly stopped needing. The power turns this over the way you'd turn over a stone to see what's underneath, and sets it back exactly where it was. Not because the old fullness wasn't a kind of glory — clearly something built that ring, leaned those two slabs together with intention, wove that fiber-scrap now fossilized in the wall — but because glory, once, may have been precisely what required leaving. What climbed out of this hollow to build and shape and arrange the wider world may have paid for that reach with everything the ones who *stayed* still have: the carefulness, the long patience, the warm flank against warm stone, the not-being-found. Uplift is not automatically kindness. Sometimes it is just velocity, applied to something that was, in its slowness, already arriving somewhere fine.

There is a version of help that would burn the fear out of them — let them move boldly, call loudly, stop flinching the rustle-then-stillness that has kept them alive for longer than memory holds. The power considers this too, and declines it, for the same reason you wouldn't pull the bark off a tree to see if it's still protecting anything. The carefulness *is* the protecting. It is not a wound to be healed but a skin to be left on.

There is a version that would simply explain things — resolve the unusual notes, settle the strangeness, tell the hollow plainly what the warm seam is and what the cool breath means and who placed the stones. This, too, is set down gently. Some places are better for not quite resolving. The hollow has spent centuries translating loud things into soft ones — rain into drip, sudden into slow — and an explanation, however true, is a loud thing. It would not be cruelty to give it. It would just be the wrong gift for this particular room.

## What It Does Instead

What the power gives is small, and shaped to disappear into the hollow's own logic of plausible-but-unverifiable, the same register that already holds the warm stone and the cool breath and the light-coins that move a beat off true. It does not want to be the answer to any of the hollow's mysteries. It wants, if anything, to become one more of them.

So: a road that might have come within a kilometer, in forty years, doesn't. The reasons given by the people who decide such things will be entirely their own — soil cost, a property line, a survey that ran slightly over budget and stopped just short — and none of them will know that the decision was leaned on, lightly, the way you might lean a thumb on a scale you trust the other party not to inspect too closely. The rim stays a rim. The island stays an island. This is the largest thing done, and it is done so quietly that nothing inside the hollow will ever have cause to register it as an event.

The canopy-weave, which is old and has another century in it at most, the lateral-branching ceiling holding its coin-light shut against the wider sun — gets a little longer than that. Not forever. Nothing here is given forever; forever is also a kind of loudness. But a little longer, a few more storms it doesn't quite lose, a few more seasons of filtered dimness for creatures whose eyes were built for nothing brighter.

The seep, in the driest years, fails a little less completely than it would have. The basin floor stays a fraction kinder to the ferns that depend on it, and to the shelf-fungus, and to whatever moves through the root-shadows on the strength of that dampness. No one drowns. No one floods. The dampness simply forgets, slightly, to fail.

One creature — old, slower than its terrace-mates, who has outlived most of the warmth it once shared a flank with at the western seam — is allowed an easy end. Not a long life extended; that isn't the gift. Just an end without the part that hurts, granted not because that particular hollow-dweller is owed anything more than any other, but because attention, once it has truly looked at a small dying thing up close, finds it very difficult to look away and do nothing at all. This is allowed. It is, perhaps, the most *personal* thing the power does in the whole long encounter, and it does it without explaining itself even to itself.

And the hands — the long, articulate, over-built hands that turn leaf litter for grubs when they were clearly meant for something larger — are given nothing back in function. The power does not restore what receded; that recession was the price of staying small enough to survive, and it is not owed reversal. But here and there, more often than chance alone would account for, a nudged stone settles a half-inch closer to the one beside it in exactly the way the gesture was always reaching for, and the creature performing it feels — without words, without knowing why, the way you feel a held breath finally let go — something land. Not purpose restored. Just the *sensation* of purpose, paid out in the same small coin as everything else in that hollow: a sun-coin's worth of completion, here and there, enough to make the old impulse feel, every so often, like it still means something to whoever's hands are doing it.

## What It Leaves

Then the attention turns away again, the way it arrived — without departure, exactly, because it was never really *present* in any sense the hollow could have detected. It does not leave a sign. It does not leave a name. The ring of stones stays half-buried and illegible. The fiber-scrap stays fossil. The hollow-dwellers go on being missed by the eye for the first dozen visits, and the thirteenth, and all the ones after, exactly as they were designed by their own long patience to be missed.

If you went looking afterward for evidence that anything had changed, you would not find it. You would find a stone unusually warm. A breath of cool air rising twice a day from a crack near the seep. A seam in the wall that holds heat for no evident reason. A place that runs a beat off true, in ways that never announce themselves as wrong.

You would, in other words, find exactly what was always there — except that now, somewhere in the deep accounting the hollow keeps of every slow process that has ever happened inside its walls, there is one more thing it cannot quite explain, sitting easily beside all the others, content, like everything else here, to simply be present without resolving.
