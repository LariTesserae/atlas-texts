The power comes the way water comes to this place — finding its way down through strata it was never invited into, arriving without announcement, pooling in the low places before anyone notices it's there.

It does not see a problem. This is the first thing worth saying, because most powers, even well-meaning ones, see problems first: the heat, the swarms, the rockfall risk, the deafness creeping up through the forge-relays generation by generation. A lesser kindness would already be reaching for solutions — cooling vents, pesticide, a steadier ceiling, ear-protection nobody asked for. It would arrive like an answer to a question nobody here was finished asking.

This power waits instead, and listens underneath the noise, the way the old smoke-women listen underneath the wing-roar for the half-second shift that means *now*. And what it hears, underneath the chuff and hiss and drip and shriek, is rhythm. Not chaos held barely at bay — rhythm. A people who have built, out of wound and weight and swarm, a working clock. The pump-wheel's groan is not suffering it hears. It's liturgy, exactly as the forge-workers say. A vocabulary grown out of scar tissue, yes, but a vocabulary nonetheless, fluent, inherited, alive.

So the power does what wisdom actually requires, which is harder than helping: it asks what here is *theirs*, and sets that part aside, untouched, as not its business.

It considers stilling the swarms, and rejects it. The swarms are not just hazard here; they are also curriculum. They are the reason a child learns to read a chamber's mood before she learns to read a word. Take them away cleanly and you also take away the smoke-wardens' whole earned authority, the particular dignity of hands too scarred to flinch, an entire grammar of attention passed mother to daughter. A power that loves a people does not amputate their expertise as a kindness.

It considers steadying the rock all at once — fusing every raw edge, finishing the chamber's slow becoming in a single night. And rejects that too, for the same reason. The elders walk the unfinished hollows with their palms out, reading a pulse they may or may not believe is literal, and in that walking is a relationship, fragile and real, between a people and the ground that holds them. To finish the stone for them is to retire them from a vocation they have not finished earning. Even mercy, applied wrong, can be a kind of theft.

It considers gifts of clean technology, cool air, easy water — anything that would let the lean, flush-cheeked people stop sweating in their sleep. And feels, turning the thought over, how it would also stop the pump-house's coaxing-language, stop the missing-finger arithmetic that nobody remarks on because everybody understands its cost was paid for something. A people made soft too quickly do not become *happier*; they become unmoored from the specific, hard-won shape of who they already are.

So what is left, for a power that wants only to be good and not merely to be felt?

It finds the narrow places. The places where giving doesn't unmake anything — it only spares.

It finds the support-beam in the third gallery that was one bad week from splitting under a child's weight, and it lets the beam hold one more season than the wood had in it, long enough for someone's hands, in the ordinary unhurried pace of this place, to notice the strain and shore it before it matters. It finds the night the swarm-surge would have come early, before the smoke-warden's smudge was lit, and it does not stop the surge — only lends her intuition a half-heartbeat's edge, so she calls it in time, the way she always almost-impossibly does, and credits her own ear for it, as she should.

And into the rock itself — into the part of this place that is still deciding what it wants to be — it leaves something like a held breath let out slow. Not stillness. The chamber will go on settling, cutting, widening at geological pace, because that pace is also theirs, also earned. But the violence goes out of the settling, by some small fraction nobody could measure. Chips fall instead of slabs. The dust hangs without the rubble it used to follow. The elders, walking the margins with their hands trailing the wall, will feel it as the rock growing calmer toward them — proof, they'll say, that the cuts have been good ones, that the listening has been done right — and they will be more right than they know, and no less right for that.

The power does not appear to anyone. It does not want to be a story they tell, a god they thank, a debt they carry. It has watched enough places ruined by gratitude alone, by a people reorganizing their whole understanding of themselves around a miracle instead of around their own competence. What it wants — what its whole considered, careful intervention is *for* — is for the truce to keep being the town's own. Renewed at the wheel's pace. Earned at the forge. Read in the rock by hands that trust their own reading.

So it leaves the way it came: unweathered, sharp-edged, already half-forgotten, indistinguishable from luck.

Above, the wheel keeps its chuff-chuff-chuff. The colony stirs and settles in its tidal surge. A child runs the high catwalk without looking down, the way children here always have, sure-footed for reasons she couldn't explain even if asked. Somewhere in the third gallery a beam that should have failed does not, and no one thinks to thank it, because no one knows there was anything to thank.

This, the power judges, walking back out through a fissure already beginning — almost imperceptibly — to weather, is what benevolence actually looks like, applied to a being tough enough to have built itself a heartbeat out of stone and steam and swarm: not rescue.

Just a little less weight, in a few exact places, on a back that was already, on its own, learning how to carry it.
