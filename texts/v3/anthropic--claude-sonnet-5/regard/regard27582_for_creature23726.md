The power that finds Hollow Cup does not arrive the way power usually arrives. There is no parting of the canopy, no hush falling over the chittering economy of the fern-shade, no animal lifting its head to mark a presence. It comes the way attention comes — which is to say, not as an event at all, but as a noticing that has no beginning you could point to. One moment the basin is simply itself, sweating stone and chuffing valve and heron-thing indifferent in the wire-thin creek. The next moment it is *seen*, fully, all at once, the way you might suddenly realize you've been hearing a sound for an hour.

And what is seen, first, is not the strangeness. The power has looked at stranger things and felt nothing. What is seen is the *rightness* of it — the way the vines have not conquered the ironwork so much as reached an accommodation with it, the way the tenders move like the cup raised them, the way the whole arrangement of machine and moss and amphibian-croak and slow-cycling boiler has, without anyone designing it, arrived at something that works. Not thrives, exactly. Works. Holds together. Persists in its own modest, damp, overcommitted way.

This is rarer than it sounds. Most places that mix the made and the grown do it as a wound — industry scarring over a landscape, or a landscape reclaiming a ruin, one side losing so the other can call it victory. Hollow Cup has done neither. It has done the much harder thing, which is to *settle*, mutually, the iron and the green and the human and the unhuman all agreeing, by inches and generations, on terms none of them negotiated on purpose.

So the power's first instinct — and it does have one, because even a wise power was not always wise, and the old reflexes linger like scar tissue — is to *help*. This is the instinct of every gardener who has ever loved a garden too much: to weed, to brace, to hurry the good thing toward more goodness, to take the fragile working balance and improve it, fix the slipping stone, deepen the creek so it never withdraws shyly again, explain to the tenders what the vines are doing and why, so that wonder becomes knowledge and knowledge becomes mastery and mastery becomes safety.

The power gets as far as imagining this. It pictures the creek made generous and permanent. It pictures the tenders' question — *why does the philodendron wrap the boiler instead of withering* — finally answered, the half-shrug replaced with certainty. It pictures a slightly drier season made wholly survivable, a slightly worse season made impossible.

And then it stops, the way you'd stop your hand an inch from a held breath, because it recognizes what it is about to do. It is about to *end the becoming.* Hollow Cup is, the power notices — really notices, the way you notice a held thing's shape and not just its weight — a place still in the middle of deciding what it is. The pale stone is still sharp-edged. The settlement's iron still has rawness to it. The tenders themselves carry no old stories about the strangeness because the strangeness is still new enough to not need a story; it is still being *lived*, daily, in small unremarkable negotiations between a hand and a vine, rather than settled into myth.

To fix this place would be to finish it. And the power, for all its reach, understands the particular cruelty of finishing something on someone else's behalf.

So it withdraws the hand it never had. And does, instead, what wisdom usually does, which looks from the outside like almost nothing.

It does not deepen the creek. It does, very slightly, make the creek *faithful* — not larger, not more generous, just reliable in the specific, narrow way it has always nearly been, so that the thin withdrawing shyness it shows after rain becomes a rhythm rather than a risk, something the moss and the cisterns and the tenders' habits can keep trusting the way they already, instinctively, do. This is not a gift so much as a ratification. The power is not adding water. It is agreeing with the water about what it already, mostly, does.

It does not explain the vines to anyone. But there is a child among the tenders — there is, in every small persistent place, one child who notices a half-beat longer than the others, who kneels by the boiler joint not out of habit but out of something nearer to love — and the power does not give this child an answer. It gives the child, instead, permission to keep not-knowing without that not-knowing ever curdling into the need to control. A very small adjustment. The difference between a person who eventually drains the mystery dry trying to own it, and a person who tends the mystery the way you'd tend a fire you don't fully understand: with respect, with care, with your hands in the right place at the right slow hour, for the rest of a long unremarkable life. This second kind of person is rarer than ironwork that lasts. The power makes sure the basin gets to keep this one a little longer than chance alone would allow.

It does one more thing, the smallest of all, the kind of thing that wouldn't even register as an act if you watched it happen: it makes Hollow Cup slightly less interesting to the wrong kind of attention. Not hidden — there is no concealment in it, no veil drawn over the basin's pale stone walls. Just... unremarkable, to a certain variety of eye. The surveyor who might have seen profit in the warm vents finds his instruments give an unhelpful reading and moves on without quite knowing why he lost interest. The map gets drawn with the cup a little vaguer than it should be, the elevation lines a touch more discouraging than the terrain warrants. Nothing is hidden. The wrong kind of finding is simply, quietly, made a little less likely, the way a held door isn't locked, just heavy enough that only the right hands bother to push it.

And then the power does the hardest thing a benevolent power can do, which is leave.

It does not announce itself on the way out. There is no parting gift left gleaming on a doorstep, no sign for the tenders to wonder over for a generation. The valve a tender bleeds tomorrow morning will stick exactly as much as it did yesterday. The fern-frond will still threaten the unwary eye. The heron-thing will go on standing in the creek as though it owns the machinery's runoff, because in every way that matters, it does.

The only difference — and it is a difference no instrument in Hollow Cup could measure, no tender could name, not even the half-beat-longer child kneeling by the boiler joint — is a faint, unaccountable sense, settling into the place the way dampness settles into oiled cloth, that the cup is *allowed.* That its strange slow becoming is not a problem awaiting correction, not an oddity awaiting an explanation, not a fragile thing requiring rescue.

Just a place, being watched, without being changed. Which is, in the end, the rarest mercy there is — to be seen all the way down, and still be permitted to remain, patiently, exactly as unfinished as you are.
