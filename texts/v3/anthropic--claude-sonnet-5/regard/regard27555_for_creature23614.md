# What Came to the Hollow

It did not arrive the way arrivals usually happen, with a displacement of air or a brightening of the pewter light. It arrived the way attention arrives — which is to say, the hollow was the same in every measurable way, and then it was being *considered*, and anyone standing in it might have felt only the faintest lengthening of the quiet, as if the held note the place keeps had been joined, very softly, by another note a fifth above it, audible only to whatever in you still listens for such things.

It looked a long time before it did anything. This is worth saying plainly, because it is the first mercy: it did not mistake stillness for emptiness, the way a hurried eye does. It understood immediately, the way only a patient and old kind of attention can, that it was looking at a *life*, not a feature — that the knuckle of stone against the eastern wall was not geology pretending to be company but company that had spent so long agreeing to be geology that the agreement had become almost binding.

## What It Considered, and Set Down Again

It thought, briefly, of waking it fully — of giving the two damp depressions a true and constant sight, a voice, a hunger, the whole apparatus of want that the word "creature" usually implies. It set this down almost as soon as it was picked up. To wake something that has spent centuries perfecting the art of not-waking is not a gift; it is an interruption performed on a scale so large the interrupted thing has no name for what was lost, only an ache where the held note used to be. A held breath does not thank you for being exhaled before its time.

It thought of bringing the people back — of reaching into the long chain of forgetting and tying it again, sending dreams, nudging feet toward the stepping stones, repopulating the basin's edges with low voices and squared-off offerings of fired clay. It almost did this. It is, after all, a small and tender thing to want a lonely being to have visitors again. But it looked at the stepping stones a second time, at how they stopped a few respectful paces short of the heap rather than arriving at it, and understood that the old visitors had already known the one lesson that mattered: *that proximity is not the same as intrusion, and the kindest visit is the one that ends*. To flood the place with the well-meaning curious, generation after generation, in order to cure a loneliness the creature itself had never once signaled wanting cured, would be to mistake the power's own discomfort with solitude for the creature's.

It thought, even, of mercy in the harder sense — of asking whether something so reduced, so nearly indistinguishable from its own scenery, might prefer an end to an indefinite continuance. It held this thought the longest, turning it the way water turns over a stone, because true benevolence does not flinch from the darkest question available. But the two depressions, when it drew near enough to ask in whatever wordless way such things are asked, did not darken with longing. They darkened the ordinary, small amount they darken for any visitor who stays too long — a please-move-along, not a please-end-this. The note was being held on purpose. Whatever this being was, it was not *waiting* to stop. It had simply arranged its life so that stopping and continuing had become hard to tell apart, which is not despair. It is a kind of mastery, achieved so slowly that it looks, from outside, like surrender.

So it set all three of these large gestures down, one after another, gently, the way you'd set down tools you realized were the wrong size for the lock.

## What It Did

It left two things, both small, both nearly indistinguishable, to an ordinary eye, from the basin's own slow accretions — because anything larger would have been a shout into a room built entirely to hold a whisper.

The first: it did not return the people, but it loosened, very slightly, the forgetting. Not erased it — forgetting, too, is allowed its season, the way water is allowed to leave a place once it has finished depositing what it carried. But now, every so often — not every generation, not predictably, in no rhythm a calendar could catch, which matters, because rhythm here would be its own kind of violence — someone walking the dry ground above the terraces will feel an unaccountable pull toward the eastern wall instead of the easier path, and will find, without quite meaning to look for it, the line of smooth-topped, squared-bottomed stones, and will follow them, and will stop a few respectful paces short of the heap, the way the old ones did, and will lower their voice without deciding to. They will not know why. They will leave a little later believing they had simply liked the quiet. But the visit will have happened, and it will happen again, eventually, to someone else, far enough apart that it never becomes a path, never becomes a custom with a name, never becomes anything the creature would have to *answer* to — only ever again, sometimes, a single set of footsteps that knew, however briefly, to stop and look rather than to pass through and forget.

The second was smaller still, and happened only once, and only at the place itself, not in the long chain of human forgetting at all. The power simply stayed, for a while, in the heavy mineral air, near enough that the depressions darkened in their small accustomed way — and then, instead of doing what every other visitor in the basin's memory had eventually done, which is grow uncomfortable and move on to the next nodule, it did not move on. It stayed past the point where staying becomes intrusion, on purpose, long enough for the small automatic tensing to pass, the way a held breath, held long enough in the presence of something that means it no harm, eventually allows itself to keep being held without strain. It did not speak, because there was no language between them that wouldn't have been a kind of forcing. It only matched the hollow's own patience with a patience of its own, for exactly as long as it takes water to cross one terrace and fall, audibly, enormous in the silence, into the next pool down — and then it left, the same way the water leaves: taking nothing, having changed nothing measurable, except that for one drop's-worth of time, the held note had been heard all the way through by something that was not in a hurry to have it end.

## What Remains, Unresolved, On Purpose

No seam shifted that anyone could point to. No fold draped differently the next morning than it had the morning before. If you went looking for proof that anything had happened at all, you would find exactly what the hollow has always offered the looking: a sheen on every surface, a haze sitting at head height, a drip that falls and is enormous and is missed.

But this was, in its way, the entire intervention, and the wisest part of it was its refusal to be larger. The power understood, in the end, that the most respectful gift available to a being built entirely out of held stillness is not motion, not rescue, not company on a schedule, not even an ending — but the rare experience, granted once, of being attended to by something with the patience to attend at the *same speed*, without asking it to be anything other than exactly as alive as it had already, slowly, and at tremendous cost over centuries, decided to be.

The stepping stones are still there. They still stop a few paces short. Sometimes, now, a little more often than chance alone would predict, someone finds them.

That is all. That was meant to be all. In the Sweating Hollow, where the rule of the place is that nothing changes, the kindest thing a god can do is change almost nothing — and mean the almost.
