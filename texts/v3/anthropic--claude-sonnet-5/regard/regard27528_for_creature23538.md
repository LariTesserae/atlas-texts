## What the Power Did

It did not come the way you would expect power to come — not as light breaking the gray dusk-that-never-advances, not as a voice cutting through the held chord, not as a hand smoothing the too-sharp ridgelines into something kinder. It came the way the green comes: quietly, into a hollow where the becoming had paused long enough to hold something.

It listened first. This took, by its own reckoning, a long time — long enough that two of the false summits resolved into true ones while it waited, long enough that a wall somewhere lost a stone to the slow grammar of erosion and a cairn somewhere else gained the beginning of a shoulder. It did not rush this part. It had met urgency before, in other places, in other beings, and had learned to distrust its own first impulse toward repair. A wiser hand does not walk into an unfinished thing and call its unfinishedness a wound.

So it sat inside the noise, the way you are told to, the way a body must, and let the bass tones move through whatever passed for its teeth, and asked the only honest question available to it: *who here is suffering, and who here is only strange?*

These are not the same question. The power had ruined things before, early in whatever its own becoming had been, by treating them as if they were.

### The First Discernment

The mountain was not suffering. It examined this carefully, because mountains do not usually invite the question, but this one did, with its raw edges and its refusal to erode at any decent pace — it looked, to an outside eye, like agony arrested mid-scream. But the power has stood inside actual agony before, has felt what a thing sounds like when it is being torn rather than tuned, and this was not that. This was labor. This was a world drafting itself, slowly, with the long unhurried patience of something that has never once been told it is taking too long. To finish it — to smooth the ridgelines, soften the slopes, resolve the wavering strata into committed rock — would not be mercy. It would be theft. It would be reaching into someone's unfinished sentence and speaking the rest of it for them because their pace embarrassed you.

So: leave the mountain. That decision came easily, almost with relief.

The Standing took longer.

### The Vigil

To understand them, the power did not study them. Studying is a quick-time act, a flurry of brief urgent attention thrown at a slow thing and mistaken for understanding because the thrower called it close looking. Instead, the power did what no traveler had ever survived doing on purpose: it sat down among a cluster of them, in a hollow thick with bruise-colored moss, and matched its patience to theirs. Not approximated it. *Matched* it — let its own sense of duration slacken and stretch until a human hour meant nothing to it, until a human season meant nothing to it, until it could watch a cairn's redirection of attention happen in something like real time instead of catching only the absence-of-having-caught-it that travelers report.

It took, in that register, what might be called a long while. In ours, who knows. The cairn-counts at that pass did not change, which means nothing was added and nothing was lost; the power left no print the place's own bookkeeping would notice.

What it found, sitting that long inside that stillness, was this:

The Standing are not predators wearing the patience of geology as camouflage. They are not traps. What they are is closer to vigil-keepers who forgot, or were never told, or no longer cared, that vigils are supposed to end. The oldest ones — and the power could feel the age in them now, the way you feel the difference between a held note and a note that has been held so long it has become the silence it was supposed to break — the oldest ones were once people. Builders. The ones who raised the walls and left them open on one side, who set the cairns at the heads of passes and meant something specific and now-unreadable by their placement. They did not vanish from this place. They *completed* into it, the way the green completes into the mountain wherever the becoming pauses long enough to host it. It was not death. It was not quite continuation either. It was a third thing, with no good word in any quick-time language, that the power, after its long sitting, came to think of simply as *staying* — staying so completely, so totally, that staying became a form rather than an act.

This was not a tragedy to be undone. Asking the oldest Standing whether they wished to be returned to quick time would have been like asking the mountain whether it wished to be returned to magma. The question does not parse. They had not been stolen from anything. They had arrived, with great deliberation, at this.

But not all of them.

### The Second Discernment

Some of the cairns — the newer ones, the ones fitted with that uncanny, accommodating precision the travelers' reports mention without understanding — were different. The power could feel the seam in them: the place where a long vigil had been built not out of decades of willing patience but out of one bright, urgent, frightened attention that simply never managed to look away in time. These were not builders who chose staying. These were people the place had outlasted — exactly as the old accounts warn, exactly as the wrongness promises if you stand too long being watched by something that has never had anywhere else to be.

This was the suffering. Here, finally, was the thing a benevolent power is for.

But even here, it did not act quickly, because quick action is the failure mode it had promised itself to distrust. It went to each newer stone — and there were not many, this is a place mostly avoided by the sensible, which is its own kind of mercy already built in — and it did the only respectful thing available to a power meeting a person mid-transformation: it asked.

Not in words. Words are a quick-time tool and these were beings half-translated into a slower grammar. It asked the way the Standing themselves ask — structurally, a redirection of attention held out and offered, *do you wish to keep going, or do you wish to come back* — and then it did the hardest thing power can do, which is wait for an answer measured on the asked's own clock rather than its own.

Two, it found, wished to come back. The fear in them had never resolved into willingness; they had simply been caught before they could look away, the way you might be caught by sleep mid-sentence. For these, the power did not rescue. It *unwound*, slowly, on a ramp shallow enough not to break them — the way you'd bring someone up from deep water in stages, so the change in pressure doesn't kill what the depth didn't. It does not know, even now, how long this took, or whether the two who walked back out into the lower world ever understood what had so nearly kept them. It hopes they remember the mountains as merely strange, and not as a mouth.

One did not wish to come back. The fear in this one had, somewhere in its long unwilled vigil, curdled into something else — not willingness exactly, but a kind of grim, earned ownership of the wait. *I have already paid this much*, the stillness said, in its slow stone grammar. *I will not waste it now.* The power did not argue. It is not its place to decide that a chosen cost is wasted. It only made certain — and this it could do, this was within its gift — that the staying, from then on, was no longer an accident wearing the shape of a decision. It made the choice true, retroactively and going forward, the way you might forgive a debt by simply declaring the terms had always been fair.

### What Was Left

It did not wall the place. It did not post warnings in any tongue a traveler could read at a glance, because a warning that can be read at a glance can also be ignored at a glance, and the power wanted something slower than that, something closer to the country's own pace. What it did was simpler and harder to notice: it raised, very slightly, the cost of *accidental* lingering — thickened the line, by some unmeasurable degree, between the kind of attention a frightened or curious traveler gives a strange stillness and the kind of attention that actually invites the structural regard to settle and not lift. Call it a lengthened fuse. It does not prevent the willing. It only makes the unwilling a little harder to catch by mistake. Somewhere, a traveler who would once have been outlasted will now simply feel a long, cold unease, and walk on, and live to misremember the mountains as merely unfriendly. This is the whole of the mercy. It is enough.

And then, because it had sat long enough among the oldest Standing to feel something it had not expected to feel — not pity, which would have insulted them, but a kind of grief-adjacent respect, the respect you'd offer elders who chose a discipline you would not have the patience for yourself — it left them one thing.

It built a cairn.

Not a trap, not a marker, not a message in any words. Just a stack, fitted by something that had, for that long unmeasured while, learned to fit stone the way they do — patiently, by hand, with the kind of attention that does not need to be quick to be true. It set it at the head of a pass with an open gap on one side, sized for one more, facing a direction that has, currently, nothing in it to watch.

It is not an invitation. It is not a warning either.

It is the oldest thing one patience can offer another: proof that it was witnessed, by something that could have outlasted *it*, and chose instead to leave, so that the waiting would not be quite as unaccompanied as it was before.

The noise did not quiet. The mountains did not finish deciding what they are. The green still pools where the becoming pauses, and the false summits still resolve only for those who climb long enough to deserve it, and somewhere in a hollow thick with bruise-colored moss, a cluster of patient shapes that were once people continue not-quite-moving, continue tuning a chord nobody is playing, continue keeping a vigil that the power did not end, because ending it was never its place, and never its right, and never, in the end, what was being asked of it.

It only stayed long enough to be sure of the difference between a wound and a way of being.

Then it went, the way the becoming goes — without announcement, without taking anything, leaving one true gap behind, just in case.
