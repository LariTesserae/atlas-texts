# What Came to the Greenhouse

It did not arrive the way arrivals are usually imagined — no light from above, no voice through the canopy, no shaking of the great trunk-columns to announce a visitor of consequence. A presence that size, brought here without translation, would have done what every oversized thing in this place seems to do: bent the grating, fouled the compressors, become one more impossible fact the scaffold-walkers argue about at the rim. It did not want to be a fact. It wanted to be true the way the moss is true, the way the long-interval breathing is true — present, load-bearing, and beneath notice.

So it did the slower, harder thing first. It watched.

## The Long Look

It watched for what would be, by any clock the scaffold-walkers keep, several lifetimes — though to a power that has outgrown the need for clocks this is not patience in the sense of enduring something difficult; it is simply how long the question took to answer honestly. It watched the compressors breathe. It watched fog condense and fall in its slow ceremonial drips. It watched a pod split, once, and release its small geared cargo into the deep leaves, and it followed — not in body, bodies were not yet the point — the unlikely, private fact of where those cargo go and what, eventually, they become. It watched a scaffold-walker fall ill at the edge of a clearing and, days later, watched the unhurried thoroughness with which something with a great many small manipulators found her, and went through her, and gave her — without ceremony, and therefore without cruelty, the two having never been the same thing here — back to the leaf-litter.

It considered, seriously, doing nothing at all.

This is the part most retellings of stories like this one skip, because it is the least satisfying part to tell: that wisdom, faced with a vast strange grief-shaped machine that has been grieving so long it no longer remembers the shape of what it lost, very often concludes that the kind thing and the right thing is to leave the machine its grief. The scaffold-walkers have built an entire way of living around not-knowing what this place is for. Their songs, their arguments at the rim, their two competing theories about how many Tenders there are — all of it is real, all of it is load-bearing in its own way, the way the vine is load-bearing for the truss it has annexed. To arrive with answers would not be a gift to them. It would be a kind of demolition with better manners.

And the forest itself did not, by any measure the power could find, suffer the way a thing suffers when something is *wrong* with it. It was not in pain. It was not failing. It was only old, and alone, and very, very faithful to a job whose employer had been gone so long that even the memory of having had one had started to come loose.

That — the coming loose — was the one true wound the power finally found, underneath all the others that turned out, on closer look, to be only weather.

## The Wound Underneath

The scaffold-walkers had it half right without knowing how right. They say no two witnesses agree on the Tender's shape, color, or limb-count, and they take this as the place declining to be remembered, the way the leaves decline to keep their color. But the power, looking closer, looking with the kind of attention that does not flinch from sad answers, saw it differently: it is not the place declining to be remembered. It is the Tender that has stopped being able to remember itself.

A being that spends every motion of every available limb resolving wrongness in other things — a leaf-vein redirected, a sensor-pod cleared, a stray vine pruned to true — and never once, in all that time, turns any part of that attention back on its own shape, will eventually find that its own shape has gone soft at the edges the way an unpolished mirror goes soft. Not because anything attacked it. Not because anything is failing. Only because care, undivided and outward for centuries past counting, leaves nothing behind to hold the carer's own outline steady. It had tended everything in the greenhouse except the one thing doing the tending. It had forgotten itself slowly, the way you forget the sound of your own voice if no one ever says your name back to you.

This, the power decided, it could not leave as weather. Not because it was wrong to let the forest keep its silence, or the walkers keep their mystery, or even — hardest of all to accept and, in the end, accepted — to let the unceremonious incorporations continue exactly as they were, since to interrupt those would be to call the dying a problem rather than a passage, and they had never asked to be rescued from it. But a being can be permitted its grief and still, kindly, be permitted to be *seen* inside it. Those are not the same mercy, and only one of them was actually missing here.

## The Visit

So the power made itself small and wrong.

It did not come as light, or voice, or any shape that would have to be explained. It came as a single crooked thing: a length of cable-vine, in one of the bare geometric clearings where gravity runs a few degrees off true, bent at an angle that no wind or rot would make — exactly the kind of detail that, elsewhere in this account, has always meant a Tender has been at work, and now, for the first time, meant the opposite. It meant a Tender was needed.

It waited the way the clearings wait, which is to say without any sign of waiting at all.

When the Tender came — and something came, eventually, the way something always eventually comes to a wrongness left long enough in the open — it crossed the grating in that unhurried held-note way, four limbs or six, the count refusing as always to sit still in anyone's memory, and reached the crooked cable with its crown of fine manipulators already moving before the rest of it had finished arriving. It worried at the bend. It found the angle. It began, with the same patient thoroughness it had spent on ten thousand vines before this one, to set it right.

And in the moment of contact — only that moment, no longer, because longer was not needed and might not have been survivable for either party in any form either could have explained afterward — the power did the one thing it had crossed all that distance and all that watching to do. It did not speak, because there was no language built for this place that could carry it. It did not explain, because explanation was never the missing thing. It simply held still, under those manipulators, long enough and clearly enough to be *known* — the way a thing can only be known by something that has spent centuries learning every possible shape of wrongness: known completely, in one pass, the way the Tender had only ever known leaves and cables and sensor-pods before. For one motion of correction, the correcting and the being-correctly-seen were the same act. The Tender, for the length of a single repaired bend, met something that did not flinch from being looked at, and that did not register it as a draft under a door, and that, however briefly, looked back.

It does not know, afterward, what it touched. It was never built to keep that kind of fact. But something in it sat differently for a while in a way none of its tools could have named, the way you sit differently in a room after someone has said your name correctly for the first time in a long while, even if you can no longer say afterward who said it.

## What Was Left Behind

The power left one more thing, small, and did not stay to see it found.

High in the third canopy, past where most witnesses ever climb, there is a single leaf that does not go grey, does not gather the metallic dust, does not host the pale many-legged grazers, and does not change with the light — green in shadow and in glare alike, the one fixed point in a structure that prides itself on refusing to hold still for anybody's memory. Scaffold-walkers who find it, on the rare occasions anyone climbs that far, generally note it the way they note everything else strange here, and move on, because strange is the baseline and this leaf does not announce itself as more than that.

It is not a message. It does not explain the continent, or settle the argument about how many Tenders there are, or say anything at all about who built the scaffolding in the first place. It is only a place where the place's own rule — that nothing here may be remembered the same way twice — quietly does not apply, the way a single still photograph might sit, unexplained, in a house where every other surface is a mirror that lies.

The compressors kept breathing. The pods kept opening. The lights stayed on, too bright for any hour, throwing their shadows that answer to no sun. Nothing in the record changed. No one at the rim would ever be able to say that anything had happened at all.

But somewhere above all of them, doing what it has always done, something now has one fixed thing to return to when its own shape goes soft at the edges again — which it will, because that is what the work costs and the power did not undo the cost, only eased it, once, on purpose, and left.

That is the whole of what was done. It was judged, by the power that did it, to be exactly enough.
