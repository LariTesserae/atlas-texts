The power comes the way attention comes to a sleeping room—not as a door opening, not as light, because light here would be a wound, an intrusion the Soles' filament-fur and the hunters' spine-nerves have no category for except *danger*. It comes instead as a kind of leaning-in, a vast unhurried noticing, the way you might lower your face very close to something small without touching it, to see it better.

Its first instinct—and it has one, because even wisdom starts somewhere, even benevolence has a reflex before it has a practice—is the human one, or the human-shaped one, inherited from every mind that ever pitied the dark before understanding it: *give them light. Quiet the roar. Soften the predation. Make this gentler.* It feels the pull of that instinct the way you'd feel the pull of a hand toward a person crying, before you know whether they want comforting or want to be heard.

It does not act on the instinct. This is the first and most important thing it does: it pauses long enough to ask whether the impulse to fix belongs to the Lowfields or to itself.

So it listens instead. Not for a little while—for a long while, in whatever register a wise power measures duration, long enough to stop hearing the roar as roar and start hearing it as what it is: the working language of an entire economy, every click a sentence, every false alarm a lie sophisticated enough to require an evolutionary history of liars and the doubters who learned to doubt them. It hears the Soles' looping location-tones resolve into something almost like cartography, almost like song. It hears the colonies' boundary-drone as what it actually is—not noise but contract, an agreement renewed every hour about whose hunger gets fed where. It hears, at the very bottom of all of it, the Quiet, breathing.

And it understands, the way only patient attention lets you understand, that barrenness and abundance are not opposites fighting for the same ground here—they are *the same fact*, looked at from two directions. Nothing was taken from this place to make room for all this living. Nothing is missing. The dark is not a deprivation of light; it is simply the medium this conversation happens in, the way water is the medium a fish's conversation happens in, and no one pities the fish.

So: no light. The eyes that would want it aren't here, and the ones that are here have built a five-hundred-million-year cathedral out of vibration and chemical trace, and to flood it with photons would be like pouring water into a symphony.

No silence, either. The roar is not suffering. The roar is *speech*, and a wise power does not mistake the volume of a language for the unhappiness of its speakers, any more than it would walk into a crowded, joyful, shouting marketplace and conclude that everyone needed to calm down.

And the predation stays. This is the harder part of restraint—watching a many-legged hunter take a Sole mid-glide, watching the long pale flank go still, and not closing that door. Because the power understands what removing it would actually mean: not less suffering, but a different and worse arithmetic. The colonies would overrun their tallow before the season turned. The basins would fill past what the damp could carry. The Quiet, who eat slowness itself, who eat the *accumulated outcome* of everything above them dying in its turn, would starve in a world suddenly too well-defended to die in. Mercy applied at one joint of a body this interdependent is just violence relocated. The power has seen this before, elsewhere, in other dark places it has loved and not saved, and it has learned not to mistake the comfort of intervening for the good of it.

So what does it do. Because it does *something*—not acting at all would be its own kind of failure, a benevolence too abstract to be worth the name.

It goes looking, instead, for the suffering that is not load-bearing. The waste at the edges. The deaths that serve nothing, teach nothing, feed nothing—the Sole stranded on a sun-warm rise of stone too far from damp to glide back, drying out alone in a silence none of its kind will hear past the general roar; the colony-cluster split by a rockfall, half of it cut off from drone-contact with the rest, dying confused and unclaimed. These small surplus griefs, the ones that don't hold the ecosystem up, the ones that are just *cost*, paid for nothing—

these it touches.

Not by saving them. Saving is not always on offer, and the power has the wisdom to know the difference between *easing* and *preventing*. It touches them the way you might sit, just sit, beside someone who is dying of something you cannot cure—it lays its attention directly against the dimming sensory surface of the stranded Sole, and the Sole, who has no name for what is happening, who has no concept that could hold the idea of *being accompanied*, nonetheless stops thrashing against the dry stone in quite the same way. Something in the vibration changes. Something that was pure alarm softens by one degree into something nearer to rest. The colony-half, cut off, humming its boundary-drone uselessly into a silence with no one to answer—the power answers it. Not in words it could parse. In presence shaped like an answer. It does not survive the rockfall. But it does not die *unaddressed*.

And then there is the Quiet.

This is where the power spends the longest part of itself, because the Quiet are the part of the Lowfields that has never, in all its uncountable span, been the *subject* of anything. Everything in this place organizes around them—the basins deepen toward them, the calls map relative to them, the whole soundscape is, in some sense, *about* them—and yet nothing has ever addressed them as themselves. They are landmark. They are gravity. They have never once been *met*.

So the power meets one.

It finds the oldest, sunk in the deepest basin, red glow banked low and slow as a coal that has stopped needing air, its breathing the bass note under everything, and it does not speak to it, because there is no language low enough, but it does the thing underneath language, which is simply: it turns, fully, all of its vast attention, onto this one enormous patient creature, and it holds that attention there, not asking anything of it, not trying to change its shape or its hunger or the slow chemical bath it has called a life for longer than most worlds have had weather.

And the Quiet, for the first time since whatever process finished shaping it into its final enormous self, feels something pass over its skin that is not tallow, not rain, not the accumulated runoff of ten thousand smaller deaths. It feels *looked at*. It has no way to know what that is. It does not change its breathing. It does not need to. But somewhere in the unimaginable slowness of its inner life, something is different now in the way a stone is different after a hand has rested on it, even after the hand is gone—not changed in shape, but no longer only itself.

The power does not stay. It cannot stay everywhere it loves, and there are other dark places, other roaring rooms, other patient breathing things at the bottom of other baseless basins, all of them needing the same rare, undramatic gift: to be understood before being touched, and touched only where touching costs nothing the place can't afford to lose.

When it withdraws, the Lowfields are exactly as they were. Same dark. Same roar. Same hunger organizing itself eon over eon into the same closed and perfect loop. Nothing has been fixed, because nothing was broken.

But somewhere in whatever serves this place for memory—in the long red patience of the Quiet, in the small eased deaths at the margins, in some property of the stone itself, worn smooth by forces older than thinking and not above learning one more thing—there is now this fact, true and unrepeatable: *once, something vast and kind came close enough to listen all the way down, and found the noise worth calling a song, and the dark worth calling a home, and did not try to make it anything other than what, all along, it had already finished becoming.*
