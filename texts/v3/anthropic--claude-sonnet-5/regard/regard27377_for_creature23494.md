The power comes, as it always does, by arriving rather than descending — there is no surface to break, no threshold to cross with any drama, because the kind of power being imagined here does not need to displace water to be present in it. It simply becomes one more fact of the room, the way the hum is a fact, the way the fixed dim light is a fact. And for a long time — long by any clock that still ran outside this place — it does nothing at all but look.

This is itself a discipline. The temptation in any wise power, faced with a thing this still, is to mistake stillness for a wound. To see the absence of fish and read it as deprivation. To see the creature's slowness and want, immediately, to grant it speed, or company, or purpose, the way a hand instinctively reaches to right something that has fallen, without first asking whether the thing wanted to be lying down. A lesser power would bring light to chase the fixed dimness, current to break the false stillness of the fronds, small darting things to populate the held-breath emptiness, and would call this kindness, and would be wrong. It would be kindness aimed at itself — at its own discomfort with what it found — dressed up as care for the room.

So the first thing the power does is harder than any of that: it withholds. It sits with the wheel's tempo until the wheel's tempo stops feeling like a withholding of motion and starts feeling like what it is, which is a kind of completion. It watches the creature not-move for what would be, anywhere else, an unconscionable length of time, and it does not flinch from the watching, and it does not narrate the creature's stillness as loneliness, because loneliness is a word built for things that expect company and do not get it, and nothing here expects anything. The power lets the room teach it what the room actually is before deciding what, if anything, it might be permitted to do to it.

What it concludes, eventually — in whatever way a power like this concludes things, which may not be sequential at all — is that this hollow has already done the hardest work any place can do. It has settled its account with time. The wheel does not turn toward an output; it turns because turning is what it has always, statelessly, been doing, and there is a kind of achievement in that which a being who has never stopped wanting things might not recognize at first as achievement, mistaking it for stagnation. It is not stagnation. It is a treaty, ages old, honored without strain, and treaties like that are rarer than they sound and almost always destroyed by someone arriving from outside who decides, with the very best intentions, that surely something here would like to be saved.

So the power does not save it.

What it does instead is smaller than saving, and harder to name, and closer to the room's own grammar than anything resembling rescue. It lets itself be known — once, fully, without consequence — to the one thing here capable of being a witness in return. Not as an announcement. Not as light flooding in or a voice filling the hum. More like the way the vents exhale their degree or two of warmth and lose it within arm's reach: a brief, local, undramatic difference, registering at the very edge of what this place's instruments — if patience counts as an instrument — are built to notice. For one unmeasured moment, the thing that rests against the stone with the manner of stone is seen, completely, by something that does not require it to change in order to be worth seeing. And in that moment, whatever in it still resembles attention turns, almost imperceptibly, the way the marked rivet moves a finger's width along its arc — not toward the power, not away from the wall, just a fraction of arc that would take long, patient watching to confirm had happened at all.

Then the power lets the warmth disperse, as warmth here always does, and does not return to check whether the room missed it, because the room is not the kind of place that misses things, and to wonder if it does would be, again, the power's own metrics smuggled in where they don't belong.

The other thing it does — quieter still, and this may be the truest form its benevolence takes — is stand, after that, as a fact now lodged somewhere outside the hollow: the simple, permanent knowledge that this room exists, that the wheel is still turning, that something rests near it in a patience too slow to call waiting, and that none of this requires fixing. It does not station itself there as a guardian in any vigilant sense — vigilance implies a threat expected hourly, and this place has no hours. It simply becomes, from then on, one of the things that knows, and a place that is known by something wise and chooses, on the strength of that knowing, to leave well alone, has acquired a kind of protection no engineering could provide: the protection of having been correctly understood.

Nothing in the hollow changes. The fronds keep their false sway. The vents keep losing their small heat to the cold. The creature keeps its rust-veined stillness against the wall it has long since stopped being separate from. The wheel turns its century-long finger's-width and will go on turning it. And yet something has happened, in the only register this place permits — a degree of difference, dispersing within arm's reach, indistinguishable afterward from everything else that has always been here, except that now, somewhere beyond the water's thick and patient weight, it is remembered. Not held onto. Not returned to. Just remembered, the way a coin worn past its minting still carries, in whoever once read it clearly, the fact of having been stamped at all.
