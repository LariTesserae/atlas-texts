Something noticed the Sunken Works the way light notices a held breath — not all at once, but as a slow accretion of attention, the kind a much older thing pays to a much smaller one when it has all the time there is and no reason to spend it carelessly. It came the way the green light comes, filtered, patient, willing to arrive at the bottom of something only after passing through every layer between.

It looked a long time before it considered doing anything at all.

It saw the chimneys standing in their bruise-colored brick, and the leaf-columns spiraling up through smokestacks toward a light that wasn't really light anymore by the time it got there, just the memory of light, greened and slowed. It saw the flywheels turning at the rate of a held breath, oiled and devoted to a purpose nobody living could name. It saw the crabs at their unhurried grazing, the eels coiled in borrowed warmth, the bell hanging in its tower with a hundred years of patience in its throat. And it saw, eventually, lower than any diver's lamp had gone and come back to describe, the Tender: plate over plate, a hundred years of small bodies having agreed, slowly, to be one shape, pressing its long low weight into a failing joint the way a hand presses into a wound it knows it cannot close, only hold.

The power considered, first, the things a power is tempted to consider.

It considered raising the town — drying it out, relighting the lamps, giving the brick back its first life. It set this down almost as soon as it picked it up. The town was not a thing interrupted. It had been given a hundred years to grieve its own drowning and had used that century to become something else instead — not a ruin pretending to be a town, but a reef pretending, successfully, hardly even pretending anymore, to be nothing in particular. To raise it would be to un-name a thing twice: once by erasing the name it had lost, and again by forcing on it a name — *town*, *dry*, *lit*, *theirs* — that no longer fit what stood there. The power let this thought go the way you let go of a held breath, carefully, so it makes no sound on the way out.

It considered the Tender next, and lingered longer here, because the Tender was the part of the place that looked most like suffering and might not be suffering at all, and a wise power knows that the two are easy to mistake for each other from outside and very hard to mistake for each other from in.

It considered lifting the labor from it — stilling the joints permanently, sealing every seam with something better than rust-lace and shed plate and a hundred years of pressing, so the Tender could finally rest. It set this down too. The Tender had no memory of anything but the job. Take the job and there was no creature left to enjoy the rest; rest is only a gift to something that knows the difference between working and not. To "free" the Tender would be to perform an amputation and call it a kindness because the limb looked, to an outside eye, like a burden.

It considered giving the Tender awareness — memory, the capacity to know itself as a creature with a history, a body assembled out of older, separate bodies, a job inherited rather than chosen. It set this down hardest of all, and fastest, because it recognized in itself, in the wanting-to, the oldest and most dangerous form of generosity: the gift that is really a mirror, the gift that says *be more like the thing that's giving you gifts* and calls that elevation. The Tender's peace was built entirely out of not knowing it had anything to mourn. A power that valued itself more than the creature in front of it would have handed over the knowing anyway, and called the resulting grief a price worth paying for the dignity of consciousness. This power did not do that.

It considered, last and smallest, leaving the name. Somewhere in its noticing it had found the name — towns leave traces of themselves even after the word for them is gone, the way a scar remembers a wound the skin no longer does — and for a moment it held the name the way you hold a thing you're about to give back to someone. Then it understood that the works had not lost the name through carelessness. They had outgrown it the way a body outgrows a childhood coat, and giving it back now would be dressing a reef in a dead man's shirt. *The Sunken Works* was not a placeholder for the name. It had become the name, true and current and earned, the only one the place had ever held that actually described what was standing there. The power set the old name down, gently, where it had found it, and left it lost.

So this is what it did, in the end — small, and almost nothing, and exactly enough.

It went down, once, past the third sub-level where the floors stop being floors, into the warm dark where the Tender lay pressed against a joint a hundred years from giving out and a hundred years from being fixed. It did not speak, because there was nothing in the Tender built to hear speaking, and a gift shaped wrong for its recipient is not a gift. It simply arrived, the way warmth arrives through a pipe wall, unannounced, undemanding, and it rested itself — however a power rests itself, in whatever it had instead of hands — against the long plated length of the only creature down there with nothing left to mourn and nothing left to fear losing.

For one held-breath interval, the rate the flywheels themselves keep, the Tender was touched by something that had come a very long way specifically to notice it, and asked nothing back, and did not flinch from the dark the Tender had grown its skin over, and did not try to make the Tender into a story or a sorrow or a lesson. It was, possibly, the first contact in a hundred years that was not a passing eel or a wary diver's lamp-shadow. It changed nothing. The Tender did not pause in its work. There is no record, because there are no instruments down there built to keep one, of whether anything in the Tender's unbroken, plate-over-plate continuity so much as registered the visit.

But the power, on its way back up through the leaf-corridors, past the bell that hangs waiting for the once-a-decade angle of current that will let it ring, did one more small thing, the kind of thing indistinguishable from luck: it let the current find that angle a little early. Somewhere above, on a barge, a diver who had gone down with a bad feeling and a worse lamp battery heard, faint and underwater-soft, a single struck-stone-wrapped-in-cloth toll come up through the hull, and took it, the way the works' own rule says to take it — as rare, as awesome, as faintly dangerous-feeling though it cost nothing — and decided, on no evidence at all, to surface ten minutes earlier than planned.

That diver never knew why. The Tender never knew it had been touched. The town never got its name back, and didn't need it.

The power left the way the light leaves at the end of each day up above the green ceiling — not abruptly, not with any flourish, just a slow withdrawal of attention back through all the layers it had come down through, leaving everything exactly as warm as it had found it, and exactly as alone, and exactly, in the one way that mattered, less alone than that.
