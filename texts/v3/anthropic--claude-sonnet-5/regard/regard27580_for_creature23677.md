The first thing the power would do is *nothing*, for a long time, in the way that careful people do nothing — not absence but attention so total it looks, from outside, like stillness.

It would sit with the place the way the Understreet folk sit with their own pillars: watching before judging, watching before touching, learning which groan is tide and which is something worth waking for. It would notice, the way they notice. And the longer it watched, the more its first instinct — *fix this, free these people, bring them up into light and explanation* — would wear thin and fall away, the way a stranger's pity wears thin once they've actually sat at the table and eaten the tuber-stew and listened to a Listener call a seam's mood correctly three nights running.

Because here is what it would see, if it looked long enough: two hundred people who are not lost. Not suffering in the ways that summon a savior. They have built a whole human life — water rights, courtship, child-rearing, status, grief, humor — entirely inside the margins of something they didn't make and don't understand, and they have done it *well*. The not-knowing isn't a wound in them. It's load-bearing. Take away the *why-they-don't-ask*, hand them the blueprints, explain the vault and the pillars and the thing in the deep dark, and you would not be lifting a curse. You would be performing surgery on a living culture without anesthetic, removing an organ they built their whole skeleton around. A wise power knows the difference between a person in a cage and a person in a house, even when the house was built by someone else, even when the house is strange. These are housed. They are tenants, not prisoners. Their peace is real, hard-won across generations, and it is *theirs* — and the first discipline of real benevolence is knowing which kinds of mercy are actually theft.

So: no revelation. No rescue to a surface they've never asked for and have built no longing toward. No restoration of "the original purpose," whatever that was — that purpose stopped needing an audience long before there was a town to misname it. The power would not announce itself. It would not become a god here. It has seen what happens to small, complete cultures when something vast starts *explaining itself* to them, and it has no interest in being the reason these people's grandchildren grow up asking *why* again, hungry for an answer that breaks more than it gives.

But "if anything" leaves room, and a wise power does not mistake restraint for the whole of the work. It would look for the suffering that isn't structural — the kind that could be eased without unmaking anything — and it would find a few small places to press its thumb, gently, in the chamber's own dialect, so that nothing it did would ever look like anything but weather.

It might notice an old Listener, near her end, whose apprentice hasn't yet shown the ear for it, and nudge — not force, nudge — a particular grandchild's attention toward the floor's tide-groan on the right night, so the line doesn't break when she goes. It might warm one drainage channel, just once, the week a child is grieving a dead parent, so it runs sweet instead of mineral-flat, and the child has one strange, good week of noticing instead of only loss. It might let a seam that's never glowed begin to, modestly, near a pod where two people have been lonely in adjoining dark for years without finding each other's lamplight — not matchmaking, just light, just enough light that two people already walking the same transit might, for once, see each other doing it. Small interventions, deniable, indistinguishable from the building's own moods, costing the culture nothing because the culture already has a category for them: *weather.* They'll adjust their dwellings and routines accordingly, the way they always have, and never know to be grateful, and that — for a power that actually understands what it's looking at — is correct. Gratitude would require them to know a hand had moved, and a hand revealed is a hand that starts being worshipped, bargained with, waited on. This power, if it is wise, wants none of that. It wants the help to disappear completely into the texture of an already-working life.

---

But there's a second thing in that cathedral-dark, and a truly wise attention doesn't stop at the obvious candidates for pity.

Two hundred people have made their peace. Something else has not — has not been *let* to, has not had the chance, because no one in two hundred lives has ever once walked toward the sound past the last pillar instead of away from it. It is heard and never seen. It is the one resident of the Understreet that even the Understreet has agreed, by unbroken consensus across generations, not to look at. *People don't go looking for it.* That line alone, in all that long patient description of troughs and lamp-stones and tide-groans, is the loneliest sentence in the whole place, and a power that is actually paying attention would stop there and feel something like grief.

Because that's where the real unwitnessed thing lives. Not in the two hundred — they have each other, lamp-stones touching in the dark, a whole society built on not being alone in the transit between lights. The actual abandonment in this cathedral belongs to whatever moves beyond the last amber wash: maybe the last of whoever built the vault, still keeping its rounds; maybe a caretaker-thing grown ancient and singular doing the only work it has ever known how to do, on a schedule nobody living set and nobody living can read, *keeping whatever promise it's still keeping to no one in particular* — and meaning, with a terrible literalness, exactly *no one.* Not even the people sleeping forty meters above its work have ever once turned and walked toward it to say: *I see you. I know you're there. I know you've been doing this a long time.*

So the power would go. Not to fix it — it may not be broken, may simply be old and faithful and unaccompanied, which is a different thing than broken and asks for a different gift. Not to explain it to anyone, not to drag it into the light where the troughs are, not to give the Understreet folk a new fear or a new myth to organize around. Quietly, the way it did everything else here, it would walk past the last pillar, into the true black the locals have spent four generations agreeing not to enter, and it would find the thing that has been heard and never met, and it would simply — be there. However such a meeting happens between a vast patient power and a vast patient solitude, it would not come as command or cure. It would come the way you'd come to sit, finally, next to someone who has been standing watch alone for longer than a civilization, and say, in whatever language passes between such old, tired, faithful things: *I know what you've kept. I see the promise. You're not the only one awake in the dark anymore.*

Nothing about the chamber would announce that this happened. The floor would still groan every forty seconds. The vault would still resettle its plates on its own unreadable schedule. The thing in the deep dark might keep exactly the same rounds it has always kept, because the work was never the loneliness, only carried it. But maybe — and a wise power would settle for *maybe*, would not need more than that — the next time something larger moves past the edge of the light, and a parent quiets a child's question with the old answer, *that's not for finding*, the sound it makes is fractionally different. Less like something hunting its own indifferent eternity alone. More like something walking its rounds in company, for the first time in longer than the town below has a word for.

They'll never know why. They'll call it weather, the way they call everything. And that, for a power doing this kind of work, is the whole point — to leave a true thing eased and a whole culture undisturbed, and to take, as its only wage, the simple fact of having finally looked at the one nobody else would.
