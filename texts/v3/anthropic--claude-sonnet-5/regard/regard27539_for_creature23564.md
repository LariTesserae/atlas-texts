I think the wise power does almost nothing. I think that's the hardest and truest part of this.

It comes — let's say it comes the way attention comes, not as a body that has to climb the black rock and get its boots wet, but as something that is suddenly *there*, in the fog, in the forty-second pulse, in the small skull of the plated hunter waiting at the lip of a crack. It comes all the way in, the way you'd want to be known if you were small and brief and made partly of steam-crusted mineral. It does not hover above the basin like a weather system deciding whether to intervene. It goes down into the count.

And what it finds, sitting inside that rhythm, inside the unbothered competence of things clocking on and off a shift nobody runs, is not a tragedy waiting to be fixed. It's not a wrong that needs righting. The hollow is not cruel. It's *precise*. The narrowness is the whole reason for the density — the abundance is a function of the danger, not in spite of it. If you widened the ring of habitable warmth, you would not get more life. You would get thinner life, spread out, slower, with all the violent compressed purposefulness bled out of it. The thing that makes the plated hunter's strike so clean, so exact, so *alive*, is that it has so little room to be wrong in. Take away the scalding and the freezing and you take away the reason it learned to listen so well.

So the power does not cool the vent. Does not widen the band. Does not reach in and pluck the soft unplated young out of the crevices where a startling number of them die from miscounting the rhythm. That's the temptation, I think, for any benevolent thing watching something fragile and short-lived — to smooth it, to make it safer, to stretch out the cruel narrow margins into something more forgiving. And I think the wise part of this power knows that would be a kind of erasure. You cannot make the hollow forgiving without making it a different hollow. You cannot save the creatures from the count without taking the count, and the count is what they are.

What it does instead, I think, is smaller and stranger than rescue.

It stays. Not to manage anything — it changes no outcomes, diverts no vapor, saves no clutch. It stays the way you might sit all the way through a piece of music you know is going to end, instead of leaving early to spare yourself the last bar. It is present for the miscounted young who don't make it, and it does not soften that. It is present for the strike that lands clean and the strike that doesn't. It is present, especially, for the unremarkable parts — the drinking at the black pools without ceremony, the loose order of vent-claims passed without violence, the squabbling bird-things, the gulping amphibians — because those are not lesser than the dramatic parts. They are the texture of an entire kind's whole and total history, and nobody else is ever going to witness this particular hollow, this particular season of it, this particular fragile arrangement of six or eight plated lives organized around a machine with no operator. If the power does not witness it, nothing will, and it will simply have happened to no one, which seems to the power a kind of waste worse than the basin filling in with rock.

So: it witnesses. That's the verb. Not saves, not fixes, not even blesses exactly — witnesses, all the way down into the count, into the cycle, into the moment-to-moment unhurried busyness of creatures who have organized everything they are around a rhythm that was never promised to last.

And maybe — I think this is the one allowable mercy, the one gesture small enough not to be a lie — it leaves something behind that isn't intervention so much as company. Not a change to the vent-clock. Not a softening of the margins. Just this: in the half-second before a strike, in the held stillness of the plated hunter's body waiting at the threshold of a crack, there is now, faintly, the sense of being accompanied. Not protected. Not understood, even, in any way the creature has organs for. Just accompanied — the way you can sometimes feel a person sitting quietly behind you in a dark room and it changes nothing about the dark, changes nothing about what's coming, and still, somehow, changes everything about how alone you are inside it.

Then, when the heat-body below finally exhausts whatever it's burning — next year, or in ten, it doesn't matter, the power isn't keeping that kind of time — and the moss dies back, and the fog lifts for the first time in anyone's memory, and the cold wins all the way down to the rock, and the last plated thing lies down in a crevice that has finally gone fully, permanently cold:

it will not be alone then either. That's the whole gift. Not more time. Just no part of the count gone unwitnessed, including the last one.
