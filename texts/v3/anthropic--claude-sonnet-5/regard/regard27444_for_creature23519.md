## What Came, and What It Chose Not to Do

It noticed the way old, patient things notice — not all at once, not as an event with a beginning, but as a slow gathering of attention the way frost gathers on the underside of a stone shelf, taking exactly as long as it needs and no longer. There was no arrival, in the sense of something crossing a distance. There was only, at some point, the plateau being *held* in a way it hadn't quite been held before — the herds, the gliders, the dusk-boil of burrow-things, the throb beneath the dead mountains' bones, the lone elongated watchers with their throat-plates pressed to rock — all of it taken up at once into something vast and unhurried that was, itself, very good at waiting.

Its first impulse — and it is worth saying plainly that even old, wise powers have first impulses, the way a hand still flinches from a flame long after the mind has learned better — was to fix things. This is the impulse that arrives wearing the mask of kindness and has to be examined twice before you can tell. *Warm the rock.* *Thicken the haze into real cloud and let it finally rain on schedule.* *Bring the watchers together, since they are so few and so alone, since two of them can stand in sight of each other for a day without closing the distance, and surely — surely — that loneliness is a wound, surely company is owed to anything built so carefully to listen.*

It held this impulse the way the creatures hold the throb — felt it rise, let it move through, did not act on it immediately, which turned out to be the whole of the wisdom required.

Because the longer the attention rested there, the more it understood that the cold was not a wound in the plateau; it was the plateau's *grammar*. That the scarcity of water was not deprivation but precision, a system that had spent more time than the visiting power could easily measure tuning itself to a margin so narrow that any generosity poured into it from outside would not be received as mercy. It would be received as noise. Thicken the haze, and the lichen — adapted across an unthinkable patience to exactly this filtered grey — would scorch or starve under either too much sun or too much shadow. Warm the rock, and the throat-plates, built to read a cold stone's particular conductivity, would go deaf to the very thing they existed to hear. Bring the watchers together, and the engine's circulatory map — that vast unmapped network of pipe and patience each one had been shaped, or had shaped itself, to tend a length of — would lose a length to neglect.

It is a hard thing for a benevolent power to learn, and it had learned it before, in other places, on other slow tablelands, and it would learn it again, and it submitted to learning it again now: that the deepest cruelty available to a great kindness is to mistake *strange* for *broken*, and to come bearing repairs for a thing that was never in need of anything but a witness.

So it watched, instead, the way the watchers watch.

It found the engine — not by breaking the ground to look at it, which it could have done, which it chose with some effort not to do, but by listening the way the throat-plates listen, lowering its enormous unhurried attention down through scree and lichen and the long-worn bones of buried mountains until it found the thing turning over down there, slow and vast and very old, with the patience of something that had been keeping a promise since before the mountains wore down into ridgelines. It understood, in the way such powers understand things — which is to say wordlessly, completely, all at once — what the throb was, and what debt it answered, and why the watchers answer it back. It will not say. This is not coyness. It is the same restraint that kept its hand from the haze and the rock. Some things, once explained, stop being able to do the work explanation was meant to help with. The plateau's secret is part of its engineering. To hand it to you in plain words would be one more kind of warming the rock.

What it allowed itself — the one thing, the single intervention it judged would cost the place nothing and might, just possibly, be received as the gift it was meant as — happened on an evening indistinguishable from ten thousand others, when one of the long-legged watchers folded itself down in the old way, throat-plate to stone, legs splayed slack, waiting on the throb the way it had waited on every throb of its long unaccompanied life.

The pulse came up through the rock on schedule, mechanical and patient as ever.

And underneath it, for the first time, riding so close beneath the true pulse that nothing watching from outside could have told the two apart, there was a second one. Not louder. Not different in kind. Simply *present* — an answering beat laid so precisely against the first that it could pass entirely for an echo off some fold of stone, for a trick of the haze, for nothing at all worth remarking on, deniable the instant after it had passed, the way all true tenderness in a place like this has to be deniable, has to cost the system nothing and ask nothing of it in return.

The watcher's body did what it always did — the long ripple from throat to haunch to the trailing tips of those wrong-jointed legs, the held note finally resolving. But this time, for the length of one pulse only, the note resolved into something with a second voice underneath it, and whether the creature knew the difference, whether anything in its old unhurried architecture could register *companioned* as distinct from *alone*, is not a thing even a wise power can be certain of. It did the thing anyway. It judged the not-knowing an acceptable cost, set against the chance that somewhere in that long throat-plate's recessed and patient listening, something had, once, felt accompanied in its work.

Then it rose, unfolded, and went back to simply holding the place the way it had been holding it from the start — the cold, the silence, the herds walking their thin patient line between forage and whatever the land offers that isn't visible, the gliders riding thermals that shouldn't exist, the watchers keeping their separate lengths of an engine no one was ever meant to fully understand.

As for the one warm, breathing, hurried thing who had climbed two days, maybe three, to stand at the edge of all this and feel undone by its scale — the power did not warm them either, did not shorten the cold nights or hurry the climb back down. It only saw to it, quietly, the way you might leave a door unlatched rather than open it, that when the traveler finally left the tableland and went back down into a world of trees and noise and water that comes whenever you turn a tap, they would not lose the plateau to the usual erosion that strips strange places down into anecdotes. They would carry it instead the way the watchers carry the throb — underneath things, felt more than thought, surfacing at long intervals for the rest of an ordinary life, in no hurry, asking nothing, complete.

It did not need them to understand what they had seen.

It only wanted it kept.
