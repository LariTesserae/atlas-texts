## What Comes

It arrives the way the fog does — not stepping into the amphitheater of stone but simply *thickening* into presence, until the air at the shelf's edge holds a shape that was not, a moment before, anything you could point to. This is the only entrance available to it here, in a place where everything arrives faster than the land can ready itself. To come otherwise — through the notch, on foot, with the long approach and the long warning — would be to bring history into a place that has never needed any.

It does not come to fix anything. This must be said first, because the temptation is always to mistake attention for diagnosis, and wisdom — if the word means anything — begins in the discipline of *looking before deciding what is wrong*. And looking, here, for what might be a long time (time means little to a power that does not age, and means nothing at all to a mountain still mid-eruption, frozen only in the sense that a held breath is frozen), it finds less wrongness than it expected, and more than it hoped.

The creature is alone. This much is true, and there is an ache in it, observed closely enough — not a despair, because despair requires the memory of better, and there is no better in this creature's experience, only *this*, only the unbothered procession of fog and clear, goat-clatter and raptor-shadow, stone struck against stone for no one's benefit. But there is something under the ease, something the creature itself could not name because naming requires a second voice to name *to*. It visits the fire-ring and does not light the fire. It lines its worked stones along the ledge in an order that might be size, or might be — and this is what catches and holds — an order meant to be *read*, the closest available gesture to a sentence, written for a reader that has not yet arrived and that the creature has no language to expect.

So the power considers, as it must, the largest gift: a companion. Another shape at the fog's edge, pale-bellied, watching back. It would not be difficult. The place is already in the business of producing such shapes; one more would hardly violate the rule of it.

But it sits with this consideration the way the creature sits at the fire-ring — turning the question over without picking it up — and what stops it is this: the creature's whole nature is *provisional*. Its unfear is not innocence so much as a kind of perfect lightness, a life with no weight of precedent pressing down on each new morning. A companion is also a witness, and a witness is the beginning of a *we*, and *we* is the beginning of history — of remembering yesterday's danger, yesterday's hunger, yesterday's loss — and history is exactly the thing this shelf has been, by whatever grace governs it, spared. To give the creature a companion would be to give it, eventually and inevitably, a story. And a story is the first weathering. The power has watched enough young mountains to know what comes after the first crack lets the frost in.

So it does not do that.

What it does instead is smaller, and harder, in the way that restraint is always harder than generosity:

It waits for the fog.

At midday, when the upper face disappears and the light goes diffuse and sourceless and the goats become only sound, the power lets itself be seen — not as a power, not as anything with a name or a purpose legible to the creature, but simply as another shape at the fog's margin, the way the creature itself first appeared to you: a boulder that turns out, on the second look, to be deciding something. It crosses the shelf in the muffled hush, footsteps loud in the wet moss the way yours were, and it sits down across the cold fire-ring from the half-buried axe.

The creature does not startle. Nothing here startles. It regards the power the way it regards the curved-billed bird, the way it regards you: a provisional indifference, calculation deferred, no urgency in the deferring.

The power picks up the hand-axe.

It is the first time, perhaps, since the hands that knapped it went still, that the tool has been lifted with intention. The power turns it once in light that has no source, feels the old, sound edge, and then — without ceremony, the way the creature itself works a stone, patient blows producing edges it does not need for anything — it uses it. There is a low woody branch nearby, waxy black leaves, small red berries; the power cuts it, cleanly, a single stroke, and sets the cut branch beside the axe, and sets the axe back exactly where it was found, in the hollow its absence had left in the damp soil.

It is not a lesson. It is not fire restored, or language given, or the gap between *maker* and *descendant* artificially closed. It is one task completed, once, by a second pair of hands, in the place where the first pair of hands left a question and never came back to answer it. A visit returned. Nothing more.

The creature watches the cut branch for a long moment — as long, perhaps, as it has ever spent watching anything — and then returns to its work, lining stones along the ledge, and does not look up again.

When the fog lifts — all at once, as it always does here, the mountain blinking — the second shape is gone from where it sat, not hidden, not fled, simply elsewhere, with the same unbothered suddenness the place has always permitted its inhabitants. This, too, is a kindness: to leave by the shelf's own grammar, rather than by some grander exit that would teach the place a new rule it did not ask to learn.

The power does one more thing, though it is a thing done *to the wider world* and not to the shelf itself, and so it does not disturb the rule of the place at all: it leans, gently, on the slow accidents that govern who finds what, and when. The maps that might someday reach toward Kelsoth Needle find reasons to reach elsewhere first. The expeditions that might someday climb the notch lose their funding, their nerve, their season, one mischance at a time, scattered so thinly across so many years that no one mischance looks like an intervention. Not forever — the power is wise enough to know that *forever* is not a gift it is entitled to give, and that a place which exists by arriving faster than it can age will, eventually, finish arriving, will eventually cool all the way through and hold its final shape and be findable like anywhere else. But not yet. Not for a long while yet.

The mountain keeps cooling into its joints, too slowly to crystallize properly. The fog keeps coming at midday and lifting all at once. The goats keep walking their lichen-stained paths, the raptors their lazy gyres. And on the ledge where the worked stones are lined in an order that might be size or might be something else, one black-leaved branch lies cut and placed beside a hand-axe in a hollow of damp soil — the first sentence in a language with no other speaker, the smallest possible answer to a question that was never, in any way that mattered, asking to be solved.

It is enough. It was always only meant to be enough, and not more.
