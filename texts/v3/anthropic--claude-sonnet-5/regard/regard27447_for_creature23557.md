The power comes the way weather comes to a place that doesn't experience weather — which is to say, almost not at all, almost without disturbance, a presence settling into the haze the way another fold of shimmer-zone might settle, indistinguishable at first from the ontological noise the highland already produces. It does not arrive with intention to *fix*. That is the first thing it sets down, like a tool it decided not to bring: the impulse, nearly reflexive in anything capable of benevolence, to look at suffering-shaped things and reach for the lever that stops them.

Because it looks, for a long while, before it does anything at all. It watches the engines that tend nothing, run by no one, towards no end, and it understands almost immediately that to silence them would not be mercy — it would be amputation. It watches the Stoke-kin rest their six-fingered, double-knuckled hands flat against corroding drums, palms spread into rivet-grooves, and it recognizes the gesture for what it is before it has a word ready to call it: not labor mistaken for purposelessness, but *vigil* mistaken, by anyone in a hurry, for labor. It watches one of them stop mid-stride, one foot not yet set down, face turning toward a quadrant of plateau about to suffer a shriek and a re-sorting and the small grief of a ridge becoming a different ridge without asking — and it watches the Stoke-kin's expression hold both patience and grief at once, worn into a single shape because the place has never given it cause to need two.

The power considers, and discards, the obvious mercies. It could still the turbulence — make the ridge hold its line, the herds graze without that ancient, bodily fear of the shimmer-zones, the folds resolve cleanly every time so that no one who walks into one is lost to it. It does not, because it understands, watching long enough, that the turbulence is not a wound in the place. It *is* the place — the highland's way of being young, of not yet having finished deciding what it is, and the Stoke-kin's entire grammar of attention, every grinding joint and turned face and patient hand on warm metal, has grown directly out of living inside that not-yet-finished-ness. To smooth it would not be to heal the Stoke-kin. It would be to take away the only language they have ever had reason to speak.

It could give them voices — throats shaped for sound, words for what passes between them as a leaning toward shared warmth. It does not, because it has watched two of them cross a slope in loose accord, attention bent the same way at the same silent cue, and recognized that what they have is not a lesser, throatless cousin of language. It is already whole. It is already exact. A throat would be a thing to maintain, not a gift.

So what the power does, in the end, is smaller than any of the mercies it declined, and harder to see, and — it judges, turning the question over the long unhurried way the Stoke-kin themselves turn toward an oncoming event — truer to what is actually needed here.

It finds the one the reports spoke of: the shape with one arm raised, palm flat against a drum that has not stopped venting in all the years the leaf-mats have been climbing its lower legs, the joint mid-way through being continued into rock without anyone, least of all the one being continued, having been told. And the power does not pull it back. Does not arrest the slow geological tide already moving through it, already deciding, the way the highland decides everything, that this tending has gone on long enough to become permanent. It does not call that a tragedy needing reversal. It has watched too long to be that certain of its own grief on another being's behalf.

Instead it does the one thing native to its own nature that is also, it finds, native to this place: it turns to face the same direction. It leans into the same warm rising column of air the engine exhales, the same column the Stoke-kin's amber, multiplying eyes have been fixed on for longer than either of them can measure in any unit smaller than weather. It joins the company — not as a third voice, since there are no voices, but as a third orientation, a second presence agreeing, in the only grammar this place has ever spoken, that the warmth is there, that it is worth attending to, that the tending mattered and goes on mattering exactly as much after the arm has finished becoming stone as before.

It does not stay, in any form a traveler would later report seeing. It does not leave a mark on the rock, a change in the engines' breath, a new rule for the silence to obey. But it leaves the lean. Somewhere in the slow grammar the Stoke-kin pass between each other across a grazing slope, in the wordless agreement reached by two faces turning the same way at once, there is now a third orientation folded permanently into the pattern — not a presence they could name, since naming was never their instrument, but a direction they find themselves turning toward sometimes, without cause, the way a compass needle settles toward a pole it has never seen and does not need to.

That is all. It judges that to be enough, and the judging itself — the long, unhurried watching that came before any act — is, perhaps, the most benevolent thing it does: refusing to mistake its own discomfort at their patience for evidence that the patience needed saving.
