# The Visit

The power finds the hollow the way the hollow was found before — not quite by accident, not quite by design, drawn by something that registers, in whatever way such a power registers things, as *a held breath nearby*. It has felt such breaths before. Usually they belong to the dying, or the trapped, or the young thing curled in a place too small for what it will become. It comes close expecting one of these. It finds, instead, completion.

This is the first thing it has to sit with, and sitting with things is, for a power of this kind, itself a kind of action — a stilling of the enormous reflexive machinery of *help* that benevolence, unexamined, tends to run on. The instinct arrives before the understanding does: warm this cold place. Wake this slow light into something brighter. Free this creature from its rock, give it sun, give it speed, give it company, give it a name that something other than itself might call it by. The instinct is not cruel. It is, in fact, love, moving the only way it knows how to move — toward the thing it loves, intending to *change* it, because changing the beloved into something less alone, less cold, less still has always, elsewhere, been the kindest thing to do.

But the power is wise as well as benevolent, and wisdom's whole labor, in this moment, is the labor of *noticing the difference* between a wound and a shape. It looks again. It looks the long way, the way the hollow itself looks at things — not in a glance but in a duration, letting the years it would normally compress back out into their full slowness, watching the frond add its millimeter, watching the creature not-move from one arrangement of shadow to a slightly different arrangement of shadow, watching the nodes drift in and out of rhythm with the plant-light across a patience with no bottom to it.

And it sees: there is no wound here. There is no small thing curled around an absence, waiting to be told it is allowed to grow. There is, instead, a closed and finished sentence. The hollow is not suffering in silence. It *is* the silence, on purpose, the way a held note is the silence around it given shape. The creature is not lonely with the particular ache of a being that doesn't know company exists. It has known, somewhere in its long unwinding history, what company was, and has set that knowing down the way you'd set down a tool you've finished needing — not broken, not lost, just retired, because the work it was for is done.

To bring light here would not be a gift. It would be an interruption mistaken for a rescue. To bring speed here would not be liberation. It would be asking the riverbed to remember it once dreamed of being a road.

So the power does what its wisdom, hard-won across an unknowable number of other hollows, other held breaths, other things it learned too late were not wounds — it *waits*, and it watches its own wanting-to-help the way you'd watch a kettle that has started, against your better judgment, to sing. It lets the wanting cool. It asks itself the only question that matters before any act of care: *whose hunger is this gift actually for?*

And finding the honest answer — that the hunger to fix this place belongs entirely to the visitor, and not at all to the visited — it sets the impulse down.

## What It Does Instead

Not nothing. Even the most disciplined benevolence rarely manages nothing, and this one doesn't try to. But what it does is sized correctly, which is to say: very small, very slow, and entirely without the expectation of being noticed, used, or thanked.

It does not enter the loop. It tends its border instead.

It notices that a person found the crevice once — you, or someone like you, the one whose footsteps the creature felt in its sternum before it saw the rest of you — and that this means others, eventually, might find it too: the merely curious, the acquisitive, the ones who would see a hollow full of strange light and stranger machinery and think *resource* before they thought *rest*. This is the one real vulnerability in an otherwise sealed system — not cold, not silence, not slowness, but discovery by something that has not yet learned to want nothing.

So the power does the one thing that asks nothing of the hollow and changes nothing within it: it makes the fold in the ground a little harder to find again. Not sealed — sealing would be a wall, and walls are still a kind of touching. Just... overlooked. A fractional reluctance in the eye of anyone passing near, a slope that reads as unremarkable a half-second longer than it used to, the same trick the creature itself has spent its whole existence perfecting, except now lent, briefly, to the entrance itself. The hollow's oldest law — *I do not need anything from outside myself* — is extended, gently, to include: *and the outside, in turn, will go on forgetting it needs anything from me.*

That's the whole of it. No light added. No warmth offered. No voice reaches down into that sternum-deep hum to say *you don't have to do this alone* — because the truer thing, the harder and more respectful thing for benevolence to learn, is that the creature is not doing this *alone*. It has the fronds. It has the nodes. It has the patient overlap of light and circuitry it has spent centuries learning to sit inside of like a held chord. Aloneness requires a *missing other*. This being has no missing other. It has arrived, by some route longer than memory, at a self sufficient unto itself, and the deepest care available to anything looking in from outside is to let that arrival stand uncorrected.

## The One Thing Left Behind

If you returned — and you might, drawn back by the same not-quite-accident that found it the first time — you would notice nothing changed. The frond-light would still drift its slow red-gold pulse against the dark. The creature would still be, to your eyes, a ridge of stone that occasionally, retroactively, seems to have been breathing.

But if you stood very still, the way it has spent its whole existence teaching the air around it to stand still, you might feel — not hear, *feel*, the way the hollow feels weather in its walls — a single thread of warmth in the cold that doesn't belong to the bioluminescence and doesn't belong to the old machinery's hum. Faint. Unobtrusive. Asking nothing, expecting nothing, costing the loop nothing to ignore forever.

It is not a message. The creature would not know what to do with a message, and the power, by now, knows better than to leave one. It is closer to a single point of held attention, set down at the threshold and left there the way you'd leave a hand resting, without weight, on a door you have no intention of opening — proof, if anything down here ever cared to interpret proof, that it was seen completely, understood correctly, and was not, in the end, found wanting.

Then the power withdraws the rest of itself, the part that wanted to help, and lets the hollow close back over its own held breath, uninterrupted, exactly as complete as it was before anything benevolent ever noticed it was there.
