# What Was Given

A benevolent power, encountering the Cistern at Anvete's Spine, would feel first — before reasoning, before plan — the pull of the oldest mistake kindness makes: the assumption that anything this strange must also be in need.

It would see the Throat, or rather sense it the way everything else in that chamber senses it, by inference and pressure and the four-second hush — a creature of unknown vastness, never witnessed whole, doing one labor across what might be centuries, alone in the specific way that only a being with no equivalent can be alone. No mate. No rival. No name spoken at it, ever, by anything capable of meaning the name as a kindness. And the power would feel the impulse rise in itself immediately, helplessly, the way water rises to the only opening available to it: *I could end that. I could surface it, free it, show it itself, give it company, give it speech, give it an end to the work if the work has become a prison rather than a nature.*

This is the impulse a lesser wisdom would act on. It would be wrong.

The power would have to sit inside that wrongness long enough to see its shape before discarding it — because discarding an impulse without examining it is just another kind of carelessness, faster but no wiser than acting on it blind. So it would ask, patiently, the question that actually matters: *is this suffering, or is this simply form?* Does the Throat experience its centuries underfloor as confinement, the way a mind built for company would — or is the Throat, like the cistern itself, a thing that has had so long to become what it is that "alone" and "free" and "purposeful" have stopped being separate categories for it at all? A held note isn't suffering from failing to become a chord. The wing-things circling in their drill-bit column aren't grieving their formation. The segmented trains aren't humiliated by single file. The power would recognize, looking honestly, that everything in that bowl — the grazers, the trains, the column, the Throat beneath it all — is not enduring its design. It is its design, the way a river is not patient and is not impatient, the way held breath is not held *against* anything once holding is simply what the lungs do.

To "free" the Throat would not be a rescue. It would be an amputation performed on a body that was never wounded, justified only by the rescuer's discomfort at watching something live a shape unlike its own.

So the power would not do that. This is the first thing it would decide, and the hardest, because true benevolence often looks from the outside exactly like having done nothing at all, and a power that needs its kindness to be *visible* — even to itself — is not yet wise enough to be trusted with kindness this old and this strange.

What it would do instead is smaller, and harder to mistake for ego.

It would look outward, first — away from the cistern, out along the centuries still arriving toward it from the future the way light arrives from stars already collapsed. It would look for the surveyor who will eventually climb Anvete's Spine with instruments capable of valuing the Throat for its filtration chemistry, or its luminance, or whatever rare process its long body performs on water that something, somewhere, will eventually decide is worth extracting and owning. It would look for the dry season three centuries hence that might starve the recovery channels if a single upstream catchment silts over unattended. It would look for every plausible thread by which this small loud overwhelming miracle might be ended not by its own long patient nature but by an accident of outside ignorance — and along each thread, quietly, with no signature and no ceremony, it would lean. A surveyor's interest redirected to an easier ridge. A silting catchment cleared by a flood timed kindly. A claim filed elsewhere. Nothing that announces itself as intervention. Nothing the cistern would ever need to know happened on its behalf, because needing to know would mean it had been made, even briefly, to feel its own precariousness — and that is not a gift, that is a wound shaped like a warning.

This is the shape benevolence takes toward something whole: not rescue, but the removal of threats the whole thing was never built to anticipate, performed so gently that the only evidence of care is the absence of harm that would otherwise, eventually, have arrived.

And then — because wisdom and warmth are not actually the same virtue, and a power that only protected and never *loved* would have done merely half the job — it would allow itself one act that asked nothing and changed nothing and existed only as witness.

It would go, once, in whatever form lets it pass through fused ceramic-metal composite without disturbing a single grazer's grip, down into the loudest dark of the bowl, past the wing-column and the hissing trains, to the floor-channels at the moment the note was due. And when the note came — that low concussive pulse felt in the sternum before it arrives in the ear — the power would be there, fully, in the four seconds of hush that the entire chamber observes afterward like a debt being paid. It would not speak into that hush. It would not reach into the channel, would not touch the permeable not-quite-skin, would not attempt to be felt as a presence the Throat could register and wonder about. It would simply *attend* — bring the whole of its attention, undivided, uncomplicated by plans, to the fact of this creature's existence, for exactly as long as the silence allowed, the way you might stand at the edge of something sleeping that you love too much to wake.

Four seconds. Then the roar would resume — the grazers clicking, the trains hissing, the wing-things thrumming overhead — precisely where it left off, because the cistern does not perform itself for an audience and would not now, not even for this one. And the power would withdraw, having given the only gift that fit the shape of what it found: not freedom from a life never experienced as captivity, not change imposed on a balance that had already spent millennia arriving at itself, but the simple, costly, unrepeatable fact of having been *seen* — once, completely, by something that understood enough to ask first whether seeing was the kindest thing it had to offer, and only then, having checked, allowed itself to look.

The cistern would never know. The Throat would never know. The grazers would go on arranging themselves around a warmth they can't name, the trains would go on scattering and reassembling, the note would go on sounding every few minutes for centuries the power had quietly made a little more certain to arrive.

And somewhere outside all of it, the power would carry away the only souvenir wisdom allows itself to keep from an encounter like this — not a rescued creature, not a solved mystery, not even a memory the place itself could ever corroborate.

Just the knowledge that it had stood, once, inside that roar, in the four seconds after the held note broke, and had wanted, with everything in it, to fix what did not need fixing — and had loved it enough, instead, to leave it whole.
