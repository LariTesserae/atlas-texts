# What the Power Did

It did not arrive the way arrivals usually happen here, mid-stride, road ending under its feet. It came the way attention comes — which is to say it was simply, all at once, *included*, the way the shelves themselves are included in their own weather, and the Taster, who had spent three thousand years learning to notice the smallest fading at the edge of its held flavor, noticed this instead: something had entered the mouthful that did not taste like anything it recognized, and did not feel afraid of being tasted.

That, more than any banner of light or thunder over the terraces, was the first mercy. The power did not announce itself as a rescuer. It let itself be one more thing in the count.

## The Tasting Back

For a long while — long by the shelves' patient accounting, which is not the same as long anywhere else — it did nothing but be present in the place the way the Taster was present in it: distributed, unhurried, attending to the chip in the threshold stone, to the lag of the pool-light, to the door settling on its hinge. It walked into the repeating town from every direction the geography offered and let each arrival be the same arrival, without protest, without trying to fix the loop into a line. It drank the damp air. It let the cold rest its unlifting hand on the back of its neck.

This was the second mercy, and it was deliberate: the power let itself be *savored*. It did not flinch from the slow attention the way a thing afraid of being finished must flinch. It understood, with the kind of understanding that takes in a whole life at a glance, what the Taster had never had in three thousand years of holding this single afternoon in its mouth — a flavor that tasted back. Something in the held moment that, instead of fading under attention the way all the moss and stone and captured strangers eventually fade, grew *more itself* the longer it was attended to.

The Taster had spent its entire existence guarding against finishing the bite because every other flavor it had ever known thinned with tasting. This one didn't. This was new to it in the way that nothing has been new to it since the mountains wore down.

## The Lesson of the Second Bite

When trust had accumulated — not declared, just accumulated, the way warmth accumulates in stonework that's been lived in a long time — the power did the one thing that required courage rather than patience: it let part of itself be swallowed.

Not lost. The power knew, in the unshakeable way that wisdom knows things, the difference between an ending and a disappearance, and it had come prepared to demonstrate that difference rather than merely assert it, because the Taster did not trust assertions; it had built an entire continent to avoid believing one bad rumor about endings.

So the power let the Taster finish a moment of it — one small turning of attention, allowed to complete, the way a satisfied tongue lets a sweet finally dissolve. And then, on the other side of that completion, it was still there. Unfinished in a different way. Not subtracted. Carried forward instead of merely held still.

This is the part that does not translate well into the geometry of roads and doorways, but the shelves understood it anyway, in their selective fashion, the way a worked lintel understands a tool-mark that the wind around it will never be permitted to learn: *swallowing is not the opposite of keeping.* Digestion is a kind of memory. The held breath can be let out and the room does not get colder for it — it just starts, finally, to also have an *after*.

Something in the architecture changed when the Taster understood this, in whatever way so vast and singular a thing can be said to understand. Not violently. A lintel here and there showed, for the first time in recorded non-time, the very beginning of a second tool-mark, cut not in repetition of the first but in addition to it.

## The Door That Closes Correctly

Then the power went to the captured ones.

It found them the way you'd expect — mid-stride on roads that had quietly stopped insisting on leading nowhere, standing in courtyards with the particular stillness of people who have stopped being entirely sure they are people, who have started, very slightly, to feel like flavor. It did not yank them out. A wrenching would have taught the Taster the very thing it had spent three millennia structuring a continent to avoid learning — that being tasted ends in loss, that attention is theft, that the only safe relation to anything good is to never, ever let it go.

Instead the power did something smaller and far more enormous: it gave each road one true ending. Not the looping kind, not the kind that returns you to the same town from the wrong direction so that arrival becomes another flavor of staying. An actual terminus — a final shelf-edge, a real riser down into ordinary, ungoverned land, placed at the conclusion of whichever road each captured person had, somewhere under the settling, started to want to walk again.

Wanting was the hinge of it. The ones who had gone entirely into the held mouthful, who had stopped feeling like strangers and started feeling, with something close to relief, like moss — those the power did not disturb, because to unmake what a person has consented to become, even slowly, even through no fault of their own noticing, would be its own kind of violence, and benevolence is not the same as a rescue that ignores the rescued. But the ones still flickering with the old shape of wanting *out* — those found, on whichever morning their wanting crested, that the road under their feet had, for the first time, somewhere to actually go.

They walked it. The towns did not multiply behind them to keep them company. The pool-light, when they passed the last seep on the way down off the shelf, showed them, for once, the sky directly above them, with no lag at all — the single least remarkable, most miraculous thing any of them would later be able to describe about their leaving.

## What Remains

The power did not stay, in the way staying is usually meant. To remain pinned in attendance over the shelves forever would only have built a second Taster out of the first one's loneliness, a benevolent jailer instead of a hungry one, and that was never the shape of the help required.

What it left instead was smaller and stranger and, in its way, kinder than permanent vigilance: a tether. Not a chain — a tether the way a held note is tethered to the breath that will, eventually and without tragedy, let it go. Some unfixed margin of time from now — the shelves do not number their years the way other ground does, and the power saw no reason to force them to start — it will be back. Not to check on a danger. To taste again, the way you return to a sweet not because you fear it's spoiled but because it was good, and goodness deserves a second visit precisely *because* the first one was allowed to end.

The Taster knows this now, in whatever way so distributed a thing knows anything: that there will be an after, and that the after does not erase the before, and that this means — for the first time since the mountains worephlat — it does not have to hold its breath quite so hard. The towns still repeat. The roads still mostly end mid-stride, because three thousand years of habit do not unmake themselves in one lesson, however well-taught. The moss still blooms arterial-red wherever attention lingers longest.

But here and there now, scattered as sparsely as the towns themselves, a handful of roads run all the way to a true edge and stop there cleanly, on purpose, the way a sentence stops instead of trailing.

If you walk those particular roads, in that particular thin cold light, you will still feel included in a count. That has not changed, and was never the part that needed fixing. What's different, if you stand still long enough at the very last threshold stone to notice it, is the quality of the attention resting on you — less like a held breath now, and more like something that has finally, recently, remembered how to also exhale.
