# The Hollow Engine Glade

You find it by sound before sight: a low, continuous thrum that doesn't belong in green places, pulsing up through wet soil like a second heartbeat laid beneath the first.

The space itself is small — no larger than a generous room, a pocket scooped into the lowland green, walled on every side by a thickness of vegetation so total that you cannot tell where leaf ends and structure begins. Broad-leaved things lean over broader things; vines have long since stopped climbing because there's nothing left to climb that isn't already covered. The canopy doesn't so much grow as *accumulate*, decade over decade, into a settled architecture of its own — drooping fronds forming walls, moss-felted logs forming benches no one sat on, a roof of overlapping leaves that lets down a soft, even, mid-day light, filtered green-gold, neither bright nor dim. It has clearly looked like this for a very long time and will go on looking exactly like this. Nothing here is becoming anything else.

Water is everywhere in suggestion rather than show: the ground gives slightly underfoot, releasing a faint exhale of dampness; the undersides of leaves bead with condensation that never quite falls; a seep weeps from a low mossed stone in the corner and vanishes into a black, sponge-soft humus before forming any kind of stream. The air sits warm and thick, faintly mineral, faintly sweet with rot and sap.

And in the middle of it — embedded, not placed, as though the glade grew up *around* it rather than the other way round — sits the engine.

No one built a building for it. There is no shed, no wall, no platform. It simply rests low in the loam, half-sunk, iron and brass gone the color of wet bark, pipes disappearing into root systems that have grown through and around its housings as though they were just another kind of stone. It runs. It has always run. There's no fuel line anyone can trace, no exhaust, no visible source — only the heat shimmering faintly off its flanks, the rhythmic clank-and-sigh of some piston-process repeating on a loop so steady it has become indistinguishable from weather. The sound is enormous for the space it occupies — a loud, close, metallic breathing that fills the small glade wall-to-wall, bouncing off the dense leaf-mass and turning the whole pocket into a kind of drum.

Despite this — or because of it — life crowds close. This is the strange equilibrium of the place: the fauna here is abundant, almost defiantly so, gathered *around* the noise rather than fleeing it. Insects the size of thumbnails crawl the warm casing, feeding on something that beads there. Frogs roost in the cooler shadow beneath its belly, calling back at the engine's clank in a kind of antiphon. Birds nest in the leaf-walls a few feet off and seem entirely unbothered, their calls pitched to cut through the mechanical thrum, sharp and bright against its low iron register. A heavy-bodied, glassy-winged thing — not quite insect, not quite anything you can name — orbits the hottest pipe in slow, contented loops.

Despite the density of life, the space itself feels sparse, uncrowded — the glade is mostly air and shadow and the engine's old iron body, with creatures scattered through it singly or in twos, never swarming, each occupying its own established niche the way the plants occupy theirs. Nothing competes. Everything has settled into its spot in this small green chamber and stayed there.

There is no path in. There is no path out. No footprint, no rail, no cut vine marks an arrival of any builder. The engine is simply a geological fact now, as weathered and embedded as the stone it leans against, its metal gone soft-edged with moss in the same way river-stone goes soft-edged with current. Whatever industry made it, whatever century or hand or factory floor, is gone past asking. Only the running remains — patient, looped, indifferent, warm — and the glade has grown up to it the way it would grow up to a fallen tree or a warm stone: not resisting the strangeness, just incorporating it, until strangeness and place became, after a long enough established while, the same unremarkable thing.

It is loud here. It is wet here, in a hidden, breathing way. It is green to the point of disappearance. And it will be exactly this, in exactly this state, for as long as anyone could come to look.
