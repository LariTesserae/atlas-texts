# The Yawning Green

The light fails first, and it fails almost everywhere — but this is not the failing of dusk. This is the failing of canopy, layer upon layer of broad-leafed crowns stacked nine and ten deep, each one drinking what little the sky offers before it can fall further. What reaches the ground is a bruised, greenish dark, the color of light remembered rather than seen. Your eyes never adjust completely. They keep trying.

The terrain underfoot is young — you can feel it in the way the ground refuses to lie flat. Ridges of dark stone, sharp-edged and unweathered, push up through root-mats like the bones of something still being born. There are no smoothed boulders here, no ancient rounded hills. Everything has a corner. Everything still remembers being broken from somewhere larger. The land sits low, a wide basin of valley-and-shelf repeating outward farther than sight reaches, swallowed by the dark green haze before it can resolve into horizon.

Moisture comes in pulses, not floods. A heavy, warm rain falls in bursts that last minutes, hammering the upper canopy into a roar, and then stops — but the stopping means nothing, because the water is already traveling: down through eight strata of leaf and branch, gathering, dripping, pooling in the cupped joints of vines, running in thin black threads along the stone ridges before vanishing into cracks. The ground is rarely wet outright. It is something else — perpetually *recently* wet, dark and cool to the touch, smelling of warm rot and green sap and mineral newness all at once.

## The Saturation

What strikes hardest, before the eyes even adjust, is the *density*. This is not a forest with gaps in it. Every cubic meter of air and earth holds something — a vine crossing a branch crossing a frond crossing a trunk crossing another vine, fungus shelving out of bark, flowers growing on flowers, moss on moss on stone. Nothing is alone. Nothing has empty space around it. You move through the place less like a walker and more like something being filtered.

And within that density: bodies. So many bodies that "wildlife" feels like the wrong word — this is *fauna as weather system*. Things move overhead in long shuddering cascades, whole branches bending under collective weight and snapping back. Things move underfoot in carpets that part and reseal around your steps. Eyes catch the scant light back at you from every angle, pair after pair after pair, none of them especially concerned with you, because you are simply one more object in a place with too many objects to track. Insects form moving walls. Something the size of a dog crashes through brush thirty feet away and is gone before you can turn. Somewhere above, much larger things shift their weight on branches that should not hold them, and don't, always, judging by the cracks that come at intervals like distant artillery.

## The Sound

Deafening is not an exaggeration — it is closer to a baseline condition you stop being able to perceive as separate from silence, the way you stop hearing your own pulse. Layered calls, layered chitters, layered low groans from things you never see, all running simultaneous and continuous, swelling and dropping but never stopping. Rain adds percussion. Falling fruit and branches add percussion. Things screaming — in territory, in mating, in dying, you can never tell which — punctuate it without warning, and your body flinches every time regardless of how many times it's happened.

Conversation, if you had someone to have it with, would require shouting inches from an ear. Thought itself takes on a strange quality here — a kind of underwater fog, your own mind one more voice competing in the layered roar.

## The Turbulence

Nothing holds still long enough to become a fixture. A clearing you passed an hour ago may already be choked closed by fast-growing vine. A branch that held weight yesterday may be on the ground today, already being disassembled by a moving carpet of small bodies. Fruiting bodies burst and rot and refeed the soil within what feels like days, not seasons. Whole sections of canopy occasionally come down — not from storm alone but from the sheer accumulated mass of life sitting on unready branches, in unready trees, on ground still too young to have found its final shape.

This is a world in the middle of happening to itself. Growth outraces decay, and decay outraces growth, in alternating violent surges, like the place hasn't yet decided on a rhythm and is trying several at once. There is no steady season here, only event after event after event.

## What Moves Through It

There are hands here — or something like hands. Evidence rather than presence: a cleared vine corridor too deliberate to be animal trail, the charred ring of an old fire-pit already half-swallowed by new shoots, a fishing weir of lashed green wood standing in one of the rare slow-moving streams, abandoned or simply between uses. Stone tools turn up wedged in mud, sharp-edged like the land itself, never far from water. Whoever or whatever made these things lives lightly and leaves fast — there is no permanent structure, no clearing held long enough to harden into a place. Just passage. Marks of hands moving through a world too dense and too loud to argue with, taking only what the turbulence offers before it takes it back.

## The Unusual

Small wrongnesses accumulate if you stay long enough. A flower that closes when nothing touches it, on a rhythm too regular to be random. Patches of fungus that glow faint and cold in the deep dark, in patterns that suggest — almost — symbols, before resolving back into mere biology when looked at directly. A call, heard only twice, that sounds like it's repeating syllables, badly, like an echo trying to become a word. None of it is wrong enough to name. All of it is wrong enough to remember.

## The Rule of the Place

Nothing here is finished. The stone is too sharp, the floor too unstable, the growth too fast, the appetite too large. This is a landscape still actively deciding what it will be, and everything living within it — leaf, beast, half-glimpsed hand — is provisional, caught in the same turbulent becoming. To enter it is to stop being a fixed thing yourself, for as long as you remain: just one more damp, warm, loud event in a place made entirely of events, with the dark pressing close on every side and the green pressing closer.
