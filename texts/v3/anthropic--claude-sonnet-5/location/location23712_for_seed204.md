# The Seam Station

It occupies a single fold in the rock — a hairline crevice on a high cold ridge, no wider than a held breath, where two slabs of dark stone meet at an angle just shy of closing. From outside, on the broader mountainside, you would not find it. The crevice looks like every other crevice. Wind has not yet finished arguing with these peaks about their shape; the edges everywhere are sharp, unrounded, freshly torn — this range came up out of the earth recently enough that nothing has had time to soften it. Frost works the cracks instead. Water seeps from inside the rock itself, not falling as rain but sweating outward, beading on stone that should be too cold to hold liquid, freezing and unfreezing in a slow daily argument with the temperature.

Inside the fold, the world opens sideways into a space that has no business existing at that scale: a vertical settlement built into the crevice's throat, stacked for perhaps eighty meters down into the mountain's own seam. Nothing here was carved by hand in the way stone is usually carved. The walls have been *grown into* — surfaces that transition without a visible seam from raw young rock to something smoother, denser, faintly luminous along certain veins, like the mountain decided partway through cooling to also become architecture. The built portions don't sit on the stone; they're continuous with it, as if a more advanced geology had been laid down over the original by something patient and exact.

Levels are sparse — a platform here, a suspended walkway forty meters lower, a cluster of small chambers clinging to an outcrop, then long gaps of open vertical air between them. The whole place reads as mostly emptiness with occasional certainty: a built thing, then a great deal of cold quiet, then another built thing. Whatever intelligence raised this didn't need to fill the space. It needed only a few specific places to exist, connected by means that aren't obviously visible — no stairs in most of the long gaps, no rope, no rail. Things move between levels here in ways that don't require a path.

Light comes from no single source. It's daylight-normal in quality but doesn't trace back to a sun you can locate — diffused down through the crevice opening above, supplemented by a low glow seeping from the grown-stone veins, so that shadows fall soft and slightly wrong, doubled, as if lit from two directions that shouldn't both exist at once.

Water threads through everything without pooling much — runs in thin films down the angled walls, drips with unhurried rhythm into catch-channels that may be natural fractures or may be engineered drains, impossible to tell which, because whatever built this place built it *with* the rock's own water-logic rather than against it. The dripping is close to the only sound. That, and a structural creak at long intervals, deep and resonant, the mountain settling around its borrowed architecture. Occasional low tones, almost subharmonic, pulse from somewhere within the built sections — not mechanical exactly, more like the place breathing on a schedule too slow for comfort.

No plants. No soil to hold them. The only life is rare and incidental: pale many-legged things smaller than a fingernail that live in the seep-channels, moving with a deliberateness that suggests they're tending something rather than merely surviving; and once, if you wait long enough on one of the lower platforms, something larger crosses a gap overhead in a single unhurried motion, never quite seen whole, leaving only the impression of a shape too symmetrical for an animal.

Despite the stillness, the place is far from static. Vapor curls and recondenses on a visible cycle, climbing the shaft on some thermal logic of its own, then falling back as a fine frozen mist by what might be called evening. The glowing veins shift brightness slowly, the platforms creak as their grown anchors flex with temperature, and somewhere below, at the level you can't quite see the bottom of, a process churns steadily — felt more than heard, a deep periodic vibration moving up through the stone, as though something is being kept running on purpose, and has been, for a very long time, and will be for a long time yet.

The rule of the seam seems to be this: nothing here was built to impress an observer, because none was expected. You found the crevice. The architecture did not adjust itself for your benefit, the lights did not brighten, the things in the channels did not flee. Whatever this station is for, it continues being for that, quietly, in the cold, inside a mountain still busy being born.
