# The Standing Country

The mountains here have not finished deciding what they are.

You see this most clearly at the ridgelines, where stone has not yet committed to solidity — it holds the *gesture* of rock, the suggestion of strata, but the lines waver if you watch them long enough, the way a heat-shimmer wavers, except there is no heat, and it is not warm at all. The air sits cool and thin and mineral-dry, the kind of dryness that has never known water and would not recognize the word for it. No rivers. No basins holding silver. No frost-melt threading down through scree. Whatever feeds this place feeds it from elsewhere, through some channel that isn't liquid.

The elevation is the first thing your body understands before your mind does — a vertigo of scale, peaks stacking past peaks into a haze that isn't haze, ridges that keep ascending after the eye expects them to stop, false summits revealing further false summits, a topology that seems to fold rather than simply rise. You climb a slope and the horizon does not behave the way horizons behave. Sometimes you crest a rise and the valley below is *closer* than the valley you just left, though no time has passed for it to have moved.

And yet — green. Improbable, insistent, lush in patches, growing in defiance of the thinness and the cool and the dryness. Moss the color of old bruises clings to outcrops in dense mats. Broad-leafed things, fernlike, grow in clusters at impossible angles — straight out of vertical rock faces, roots threading into cracks that shouldn't hold soil, shouldn't hold anything, and yet hold these gardens. The vegetation does not spread evenly. It pools — dense thickets in hollows, then long sparse stretches of bare raw stone between, as though growth itself were rationed, or chosen, or grown only where the mountain has settled enough to host it. Most of the terrain is still becoming. The green only takes where the becoming has paused.

No animals. Not one call, not one rustle of fauna-movement, not a single set of eyes catching light from a crevice. This should make the place quiet. It is the opposite of quiet.

## The Sound

It is deafening — not loud in the way of any single source, but loud the way a held chord under your sternum is loud, a vast continuous pressure of noise with no clear origin. It comes from the rock itself, or from the air moving through structures that are not quite canyons, not quite throats. Long groaning resonances, bass tones that you feel in your teeth, occasional shrieking harmonics that arrive and vanish without an audible cause, as if the mountains were tuning instruments nobody is playing, or grinding against a register of geological time sped up into something audible — stone settling decades of strain into a single shuddering second. There is no silence to retreat into. You learn to think inside the noise, the way you'd learn to think inside weather.

## The Wrongness

This is a place where the rules have not fully arrived yet, geologically — the erosion is barely begun, the ridgelines still raw and unsoftened, edges too sharp, slopes too severe, as though stone were poured rather than worn. But it is wrong in a deeper way than unfinished.

Distances refuse consistency. A structure visible on a far ridge — small, stacked stone, clearly built by hands — might take an hour's hard climb to reach, or might be underfoot within three steps, the terrain folding shut behind you like a closing hand. Shadows fall toward the dim light source (you are never quite sure where it sits — the light is low, diffuse, sourceless, gray-cool, more like dusk that never advances toward night) but sometimes a second shadow falls the wrong way from the same object, as if something else, unseen, were also providing illumination from an angle that does not exist.

Some of the green has too many leaves arranged in patterns that repeat with unnatural perfection, fractal repetitions tightening down to invisibility. You stop looking closely after a while. It's better not to count them.

## What Was Built Here

Scattered — truly scattered, holding long silences between — are the marks of someone, sometime: low cairns of fitted stone, none taller than a person's waist, arranged at the heads of passes as though marking something other than direction. A few structures more deliberate: drystone walls forming partial enclosures, never complete, open on one side always, as if finishing them were forbidden or pointless. The stonework is hand-fitted, no mortar, no metal tools evident in the cut — primitive, patient, built by people who had time and not much else, who worked with what the unfinished mountain gave them.

No bodies. No tools left behind. No fire-rings, even, though you'd expect them in this cold. Whoever raised these stones either took everything portable when they left, or never needed fire, or are still here in some form the eye keeps refusing to resolve, standing very still among the moss-thickets, mistaken for cairns themselves until you've walked past three of them and felt, on the fourth, the unmistakable sense of being watched by something built of stacked stone.

## The Pace of Things

Despite all this — the noise, the wrongness, the vertigo of badly-behaved space — the place itself moves slowly. Whatever process is shaping these mountains, growing this moss, tuning this terrible unceasing chord, operates across long durations. The cairns have not moved in however long you've been able to check. The false summits resolve, eventually, if you climb long enough — the mountain does *eventually* let you arrive. The wrongness is not chaos; it is a slow, patient, ongoing miscalculation, a world still drafting its own geometry, indifferent to whether you're standing inside the draft while it works.

You get the sense, walking here, that you have arrived too early — before the erosion has softened the cruelty of these slopes, before whoever built the cairns finished whatever they were building, before the mountain finished deciding which of its own laws it intends to keep. The noise is the sound of that unfinishedness, audible: a world groaning through its own becoming, too loud to ignore, too slow to resolve, lush in patches out of sheer stubbornness, and entirely unconcerned with whether you find your way back out.
