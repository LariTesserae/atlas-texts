# The Underroot Bazaar

There is no sky here. What passes for sky is a ceiling of roots, and what passes for light filters down through a thousand fibrous skylights where the roots have not quite sealed themselves shut yet — because they are still growing, still arguing with the stone, still in the act of becoming a ceiling rather than already being one. You are below the surface of something, though no one living here could tell you the surface of *what*. The dry red dust on the cavern floor has never known rain. There is no water anywhere in this place — not a trickle, not a damp patch, not dew — and yet the vegetation does not seem to need it, or has found some other thing to drink instead.

## The Substrate

Call it ground only loosely. The floor of the cavern-valley is itself alive: a dense mat of pale rhizomes and fat tuberous bodies, packed so closely together that walking means stepping rhizome-to-rhizome, never touching the bare rock beneath. Between the swollen tubers, smaller growths crowd in — bristling fern-things with leaves like shaved bone, fungal shelves the size of doors, vine-ropes thick as a man's torso that loop from wall to ceiling to floor and back again in tangles too dense to count. There is no negative space. Everywhere you look, something is growing into the gap left by something else.

The walls — when you can find wall beneath the growth — are raw, unfinished stone, fissured and crumbling, the kind of rock that looks like it solidified last week. Mineral veins run through it in colors that don't belong underground: sulfur-yellow, a green that pulses faintly, a black that drinks the light. The cavern is young. It is still forming. Somewhere far off, at intervals, you hear the deep structural groan of stone settling, finding its final shape, and each time it happens the root-ceiling shudders and showers down a fine rain of dry soil and broken fiber — the only rain this place will ever know.

## The Heat and the Light

It is warm here, a thick warmth, like the inside of a body. The light comes from three unreliable sources: the gaps in the root-ceiling letting through a sourced, distant glow of uncertain origin; veins of that pulsing green mineral, which breathe luminescence in slow tidal rhythms; and — stranger — certain fungal caps that flare hot white for seconds at a time when disturbed, then dim, like nervous lanterns. No single light dominates. The result is a restless dappling, shadows that migrate even when nothing visible moves to cast them.

## The Fauna — too much of it

This is the part that overwhelms first. The vegetation is not just dense, it is *infested*, riotously, in every layer simultaneously. Insectile things the size of hands click and scuttle across the tuber-mats in conveyor-belt processions. Pale many-legged grazers, blind, move through the undergrowth in herds you hear before you see — a continuous dry rustling roar, like surf made of leaves. Things with wings too small for their bodies launch themselves between vine-ropes and mostly fail to fly, instead falling, climbing, launching again. Burrowing mammalian-adjacent creatures throw up new dust-mounds every few minutes, and from the new mounds more creatures immediately emerge, as though the ground is generating fauna faster than it can hold them. Predators move through all of this with an unhurried confidence that suggests prey has never once been scarce.

Nothing here keeps a respectful distance from anything else. Bodies brush bodies. The forest of stems and the forest of legs and shells and fur are the same forest, indistinguishable at speed.

## The Sound

Deafening — not metaphorically. The clicking of exoskeletons, the friction-roar of moving herds, the percussive thud of falling tubers shedding their own dead weight, the structural groans of the still-forming stone, the snap and hiss of those flash-fungi, the screech of whatever the predators are catching, and beneath all of it a continuous low organic hum that might be the root-mass itself, vibrating with growth. There is no silence to be had at any hour, if hours mean anything down here. Sound layers on sound the same way the vegetation layers on itself, until individual noises stop being events and become texture.

## The Bazaar

People live here — or something like people. Their presence is scattered, modest, built from what the place offers and no more: platforms lashed from harvested vine-fiber, lean-tos of woven stem-bark wedged into the crooks of the largest root-buttresses, fire-pits (the only true emptiness in the whole landscape, small circles burned clear) ringed with stone tools, bone hooks, fiber-cord, the simple manual technology of people who cut and weave and pierce but have not yet learned to smelt. Their structures don't fight the vegetation; they're grafted onto it, parasitic and respectful at once, like the people understand they are guests in something much larger and faster-growing than themselves.

And yet — bazaar is the right word, not village. There is constant transactional motion: harvesting parties moving in disciplined lines through the grazer-herds, someone always shouting over the noise, baskets of tubers and strange glowing fungus-caps being passed hand to hand, the whole scattered settlement humming with a hyperactive, anthill urgency that matches the turbulence of everything around it. Nothing here is stable for long. A root-buttress that held three lean-tos last season may have grown sideways and dropped them by now. A grazing-path that was safe yesterday may now run through a predator's new territory. The people adapt constantly, rebuild constantly, because the landscape itself refuses to hold still — still under construction, still erupting, still deciding what it wants to be.

## The Wrongness

And there's the strangeness underneath all of it, the thing that makes this not just a dense warm cave full of life: things grow *toward* each other here in ways that suggest intention rather than phototropism — vines that bend to follow the bazaar's fires though there is no light to chase; fungus that flares brightest right before something predatory passes, as if warning, or signaling. The tuber-floor occasionally pulses underfoot in a slow wave that travels the length of the valley and back, with no apparent trigger. Some of the people leave the swollen tubers unharvested in certain rings, deliberately, and won't explain why beyond gestures and unease. The dust itself, where it gathers thick enough, sometimes holds shapes too long after something has walked through it — footprints that don't degrade on schedule, that seem to wait.

No one knows what this cavern is becoming. It hasn't finished arriving yet. The roof is still closing. The walls are still settling into their final cracks. The fauna is still multiplying past any equilibrium a smaller ecology would allow. And the people camped in its folds keep building, keep weaving, keep harvesting at the edge of all that overwhelming noise and life — provisional, scattered, stubborn — betting that whatever this place finishes becoming, there will still be room down here for them.
