# The Warren-Vaults of Kesh-Anneth

The cavern does not announce itself with darkness, as caverns are supposed to. It announces itself with light — warm, ochre-gold, pouring from veins of something luminous threaded through the stone ceiling like fat through meat, twenty meters up, sometimes forty, the ceiling itself uncertain of its own height because it keeps becoming. This is young rock. You can feel it in your molars before you see it: a low continuous percussion of settling stone, the architecture of the world still deciding its final shape, walls weeping fresh mineral sweat that hasn't yet learned to be stalactite or flowstone or anything with a name.

## Ground

The floor is not floor so much as *accumulation* — raw, unweathered rubble fused at the edges by heat and time too short to call geology, more like very slow cooking. No soil. No grit worn smooth. Every stone is still wearing its corners. Where moisture gathers — and it gathers often, in shallow concave pockets, dripping down from the glowing seams above — the water sits black and still and faintly mineral-sweet, untouched by silt because there's been no time to make silt. You drink it carefully. It tastes of birth.

There is no plant life here, not even the simple kind. What grows, if anything grows, are pale colonies of something between mineral and organism — crystalline lattices that may be alive, that pulse faintly in time with the light-veins above, that nobody has decided whether to call fungus or geode. They do not feed the world. They are not eaten. They are simply present, like punctuation no one has parsed yet.

## What Lives Here

The fauna arrived before the world finished arriving — that is the unspoken rule everyone obeys without saying it aloud. The warrens teem. Not metaphor: *teem*. Creatures move through the open galleries in numbers that should not be sustainable, packed flank to flank in the wider chambers, a heaving fur-and-chitin tide that parts around standing stone and re-merges on the other side like water around a piling. Some of them are recognizable — burrowers, six-limbed grazers of the mineral lattice, things with too many eyes adapted to a light that's always the same intensity, so there was never selective pressure toward day-and-night vision, only toward *more* eyes for *more* directions. Others are not recognizable at all, and you learn fast not to stare too long at the ones with no apparent front or back.

The noise is constant and enormous — chittering, low groaning calls that travel through stone as much as air, the percussive thunder of herd-movement through narrow channels, occasional shrieks that silence whole galleries for ten heartbeats before the din resumes. Loud is the wrong word almost. It is closer to *weather*.

## The People, If That's the Word

Small settlements cling to the calmer alcoves — not villages, more like *encampments that have lasted long enough to acquire furniture*. Lean-to structures of fused rubble and bone-strut, a firepit, racks of tools made from chipped stone and the harder local metal-ores beaten cold because no one here has discovered how to get a forge hot enough, not yet, not in rock this young. Hand-axes. Bone needles. Rope of sinew and hair. They live the way people live when the world keeps rearranging the furniture: portable, alert, ready to relocate when a chamber floods or a new fissure opens or the herd-tide changes course and threatens to simply walk through camp.

They have a relationship with the fauna that is neither domestication nor predation exactly — more like *traffic management*. You do not fight the herd. You read it, you predict its channels, you harvest at the margins, you build your fires where the tide cannot reach. Children are taught the herd-roads before they're taught to walk straight.

## The Turbulence

This is the part that never settles. Once a day, roughly — though "day" means nothing here, there's only the light-veins' patient burn — the rock groans somewhere distant and a new gallery opens, or an old one collapses, or a fissure exhales superheated air that smells of struck flint and sends the nearest herd-segment into a stampede that takes minutes to outrun and hours to forgive. The world is *under construction*, and construction does not pause for residents. Pools appear overnight where none existed. Passages that led somewhere yesterday lead into solid rock today. The luminous veins migrate, slowly, so that a chamber bright as noon last season dims to twilight and somewhere else, blind stone wakes up glowing.

People here speak of the cavern the way others speak of weather or tide — not as backdrop but as agent. *The warren decided.* *The warren opened a door.* There's a fatalism to it, but not despair; more the practiced calm of people who've learned that turbulence, here, is simply the texture of being alive in a place still being made.

## Smell, Touch, the Underneath of It

Hot stone and wet mineral and the ammoniac musk of ten thousand packed bodies moving in close formation. Condensation beads on every cold surface, evaporates within the hour as the rock's residual heat reclaims it — the whole place breathing in tiny humid exhalations. Touch anything load-bearing and you feel the tremor underneath, low-frequency, continuous: not danger exactly, just *process*. The sound of a world laying down its own bones in real time, and several thousand restless animals, and a few dozen humans with stone tools and good instincts, all living inside the metabolism of something not yet finished becoming a place.
