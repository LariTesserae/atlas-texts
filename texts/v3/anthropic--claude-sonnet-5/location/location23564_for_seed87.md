# The Sunken Hollow

You find it by accident, or you don't find it at all — a fold in the seabed below a shelf of cold water, no wider than a held breath, where the rock has not finished deciding what it wants to be. This is a city that grew rather than was built, though it was built nonetheless. Below the surface, below the elevation where light still tries, there is a settlement of soft stone and slower things.

## The Architecture of the Becoming

The walls here are not finished. They drip — not water, the water is everywhere already, ambient, total — they drip *mineral*, pale ropes of calcite and something else, something organic-adjacent, descending from a ceiling that hasn't decided its final height. Towers exist, but only the bottom thirds of towers; structures truncate into raw rock and then, oddly, resume a few feet on, as if the place is being assembled out of order, or disassembled, or both, depending on the century you ask.

Streets — and they are streets, you can tell by the way smaller stone tunnels organize themselves into a grid-adjacent logic — run between dwellings that are more grown than carved. Spiraled, ribbed, asymmetrical, like the homes of things that don't have hands. No tools have touched this place. No tech, not even fire. Whatever built this city built it with the patience of bone accretion, of coral logic extended into something that wanted streets and plazas and didn't know it was wanting them.

The scale is wrong in a way you only notice slowly: this entire settlement could fit inside a cupped palm of the larger dark around it. A pocket. A held breath inside a held breath. The ocean outside is vast and you understand that vastness in the abstract, but here, in the hollow, everything compresses — towers a forearm tall, plazas you could cover with a dinner plate, and yet every scale-cue inside the place insists it is a true city, fully inhabited, fully real, simply small. Or you are large. The hollow does not clarify which.

## What Moves

The water is thick with presence. Not crowded exactly — there's room to drift between them — but dense in the way a held breath is dense with unspent air. Pale bodies thread the spire-gaps: eyeless, mostly, or eyed with something that isn't quite an eye, soft-bodied things that pulse along the canyon streets in loose processions that might be migration, might be commute, might be neither. Schools turn together in the larger plazas like flocks of weather. Something larger moves at the edge of things, glimpsed only as displacement, a shouldering-aside of smaller bodies, gone before it resolves into shape.

They are abundant, all of them, but nearly silent. The sound here is the sound of a sealed room — water moving against itself, the occasional tick of stone settling, the soft concussive nothing of a large body turning in deep water two streets over. No calls. No bells (there is nothing to ring them, no one with hands). It is a quiet that has weight, that you feel against the eardrums like pressure rather than absence.

Sparse threads of vegetation cling to the high lintels of the unfinished towers — not plants exactly, paler than plants, swaying with a rhythm independent of the current, as if they are listening to something deeper in the rock and answering with their bodies.

## The Logic of the Place

Nothing here is finished and nothing here is new. This is the strangeness underneath the strangeness: the city reads as ancient — its accretions, its worn archways, its sediment-logic — and also as embryonic, mid-formation, a thing still arriving. Walk one street and the stone is old, fossil-pale, a thousand years settled. Turn the corner and the stone is raw, wet-edged, still extruding itself centimeter by centimeter into the cold water, the way a wound continues to seal long after the cut.

The cold here isn't the cold of ice. It's the cold of depth, of water that has never been warm, water old enough to have forgotten the idea of the sun. Light arrives dim and indirect, a bioluminescent rumor more than an illumination — pale and blue, sourced from nothing visible, perhaps the walls themselves, perhaps the slow bodies drifting through them, perhaps the city remembering light it once had access to and refusing to fully let it go.

There is no record of who or what raised the framework of these streets. The fauna that fill them now may be inheritors, may be builders, may be something in between — tenants who became architects by sheer persistence, finishing the unfinished work one secreted millimeter at a time, one slow generation after another, while something above forgets this hollow exists and something below it keeps building toward a ceiling that isn't there yet.

You leave — if you leave — uncertain whether you witnessed a ruin being born or a city quietly, patiently, dying into completion.
