# The Lattice-Marsh of Anseth Ka

There is a place where the ground was never ground at all.

What stretches before you — and it does stretch, basin after basin of it, low and shining under a sun that seems to occupy half the sky — is a wetland built from wood and woven fiber and lashed cane, total in its construction down to the last visible surface. No mud here is true mud. No reed grows from soil; it grows from the cracks between deliberately placed things. The builders, whoever or whatever they were, have not been seen working in living memory, yet the work continues to look fresh, sharp at the joints, as if finished yesterday and also as if finished ten thousand years ago. This is the first wrongness, and it does not announce itself loudly — it simply persists.

## Terrain and Material

The basin floor is a vast lattice of raised plank-walks, woven causeways, and pit-pools, all constructed at a scale that dwarfs any single tool that could have made them. The planks are dark and swollen with damp, fitted with a joinery too exact for the hand-adzes and stone awls that lie scattered along the walkways — primitive instruments, the only tech visible anywhere, abandoned mid-task as though their users simply stepped sideways out of the world. The wood never quite finishes rotting. Some boards seem to weep continuously, beading water along grain that should have dried out centuries ago, the moisture sourceless, climbing rather than falling in places, gathering upward into the bright air to hang as low, stationary mist over the pools.

Between causeways, the land — if it can be called land — dips into shallow constructed paddies, each lined with fitted clay tile or fired brick gone soft green with growth. Water sits in these tiles at a near-constant depth, neither rising with what should be frequent rain nor falling with evaporation, despite a heat that presses down like a held breath. It is hot here, swelter-hot, the kind of heat that should empty such basins by midday. It never does. The water table sits just under every surface, seeping up through joints, fogging the undersides of woven canopies, condensing in fat unmoving drops on support beams that were clearly cut, clearly placed, clearly young in their cutting — sharp edges, unworn corners, joinery still showing tool-marks despite having stood, by every other sign, since before memory.

## Vegetation

Lushness pours from every gap the architecture leaves open. Vines the color of unripe limes spiral up lashed pylons that hold up nothing, support roofs over no rooms. Broad-leafed cane grows in the paddies in dense stands, leaves glossed with permanent wet, never wilting in the heat, never browning at the edge. Flowering trellis-trees — clearly cultivated once, clearly trained along frames built for them — have long outgrown their frames in some places, frame and trunk grown together into a single fused material, wood-of-tree indistinguishable from wood-of-construction. This is the second wrongness: nothing here can be sorted cleanly into made and grown. The plank is also root. The trellis is also branch.

Despite this lushness, the place reads as sparse rather than crowded — wide gaps of open water and open platform between the green stands, distances that make the eye travel before finding the next feature: a leaning grain-tower here, a stand of fused trellis-trees there, a long causeway vanishing toward a horizon hazed bright white with heat-light and standing damp.

## Light and Atmosphere

The light is total, shadowless in the way only certain dreams are shadowless — bright from everywhere, sourced from a sky the color of hot tin, no sun pinpointed, no cloud doing the work of softening it. Things still cast shadows, technically, pooled dark beneath causeways and inside paddy-tile, but the shadows do not move, do not lengthen or shorten, because nothing here moves in that sense. Time does not pass through this place so much as sit beside it.

## Sound and Stillness

It is mostly quiet — the kind of quiet that has texture, made from the small persistent noises of water finding its unmoving level: a drip that never finishes dripping, a settle of cane leaf against cane leaf with no wind to cause it. No human voice. No tool-strike, despite the abandoned tools. Occasionally a long low call moves across the basin from something unseen, abundant but reclusive — fauna heard far more often than glimpsed.

## Ecology

Life here is abundant in a scattered, generous way: fish-shapes turning in the clear standing paddy water, never quite caught by the eye in full; long-legged wading things stalking the shallows with a stillness that competes with the stillness of the architecture itself, so that sometimes a "bird" is noticed only after minutes of being mistaken for a carved finial; soft drumming insects in the cane that never seem to multiply or diminish in number no matter how long one watches. Every population here seems held at a fixed census, as though the whole basin were a single sealed terrarium, balanced once and never adjusted since.

And this is the place's deepest law, the one the weirdness keeps returning to: **nothing here changes.** The water never finishes rising or falling. The wood never finishes aging. The tools are forever freshly dropped. A fruit hangs in permanent unripeness on a fused trellis-branch nearby; a fish is forever turning, never surfacing, never caught. It is not frozen, exactly — there is motion everywhere, drips and turns and the slow drift of mist — but it is motion without arithmetic, without sum. Walk the causeways for a day and you will find the drop of water on the third support-beam from the eastern grain-tower in precisely the position you left it. Nothing accumulates. Nothing depletes.

## Rules of the Place

- **Everything is built, but building has stopped.** No hand is ever seen at work, yet decay and growth proceed at exactly the rate needed to look permanently mid-process — never finished, never lost.
- **Wood and root are the same substance.** Anything fused to architecture long enough stops being distinguishable as separate, structurally or, it seems, in the eyes of whatever maintains this place.
- **Water seeks no level.** It pools, beads, and mists according to old intention rather than gravity, gathering where the original builders wanted moisture, regardless of what physics would now prefer.
- **Census is fixed.** The living things here exist in a held number. Something dying elsewhere in the basin seems to correspond, eventually and without visible mechanism, to something being born — though no one has stayed long enough to confirm this, only long enough to suspect it.
- **Tools remain at first-use sharpness.** The adzes and awls scattered on the planks will cut as well today as the day they were dropped, and will not be picked up by anything you can see doing the picking.

To walk here is to feel the heat genuinely, to feel the damp genuinely, to hear the quiet and see the abundant green and the abundant unseen animal life genuinely — and beneath all of it, a low persistent sense that you have wandered into the held breath of something that finished a thought long ago and has simply not yet exhaled.
