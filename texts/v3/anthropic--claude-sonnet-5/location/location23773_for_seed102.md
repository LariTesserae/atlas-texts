# The Hollow of Verdant Voices

## Descent

There is no path down, only a fissure in the dry crust above—a crack wide enough for one body, sideways, breath held. The stone there is young: sharp-lipped, conchoidal, breaking in fresh planes that have never known rain. It cuts. After the fissure, the drop opens into a chamber roughly the size of a modest house, and there the world becomes wrong in a quiet, settled way—wrong the way a thing is wrong when it has always been that way and will always be that way.

Call it a pocket. A held breath of earth.

## What Grows Without Sun

No light source explains the green. It is there regardless—a dim, ember-colored twilight that seems to leak from the walls themselves, the mineral veins running through the stone glowing the dull orange of banked coals, never brightening, never dimming. By this non-light, vegetation crowds the chamber in thick, glossy excess: broad fleshy leaves the size of dinner-plates, stalks like pale cartilage, fruiting bodies that hang in clusters and never seem to be eaten down to nothing, never seem to multiply either. The plants do not reach for the absent sun. They reach for each other, knotting into archways, doorways, a vegetal architecture grown over something built first by hands.

Because there were hands here. Scattered among the roots: a low wall of fitted stone, untrussed and roofless, swallowed to the knee by growth. A platform of flat slabs, deliberately laid, now host to a sprawling mat of broad leaves. A single post, notched with tool-marks, standing alone in a clearing the plants have for some reason left bare—as if the chamber itself remembers that this spot is not for growing.

No metal. Whoever made these marks used stone, bone, maybe fire-hardened wood. They are long gone, or never left, or are still here in a form unrecognizable as themselves.

## The Sound That Never Stops

This is not a quiet place. It should be—sealed below the crust, no wind, no water—but it roars, low and constant, a sound with no clear source: a chorus of calls, clicks, drones, something like wingbeats multiplied by hundreds, something like breathing multiplied by more. The sound never swells or fades. It does not respond to footsteps or stillness or time of imagined day, because there is no day here, no cycle at all—only the sound, perpetual, as fixed as the orange glow in the walls.

The fauna producing it are rarely seen whole. Pale, soft-bodied things moving in the leaf-litter, never still, never schooling into recognizable shapes. Something that might be a wing flashes between fronds and resolves into nothing when you turn to look. Trails through the moss-analog ground suggest creatures larger than what shows itself—grooves pressed into soft matter, never fresh, never old, simply present, as though made once and preserved exactly.

They do not seem to fear the visitor. They do not seem to notice at all.

## The Rule of This Place

Nothing here changes. This is not a feeling but a fact, structural, load-bearing: the fruit does not ripen further or rot, the calls do not vary in pitch or urgency, the single bare clearing around the worked post stays bare by some law that permits no encroachment and no abandonment. Whatever process usually pushes a place toward growth or decay has been declined here, or paid off, or simply never invited in. The fresh-broken stone at the entrance is exactly as sharp as it was made and will be sharp in the same way forever. The structures are neither ruin nor building—they exist in the single moment between completion and collapse, indefinitely.

A visitor's footprint, pressed into the soft floor-matter, will still be there. It is unclear whether it will still be there because nothing has happened since, or because the chamber has already decided, somehow, retroactively, what shape its floor will always hold.

## Air

The air is warm, close, slightly sweet with the smell of cut stems though nothing here is ever cut. It does not move. There is no wind to carry the sound or the smell elsewhere—they simply fill the space evenly, sparse light and dense noise and lush growth and empty stone holding together in a balance that asks for nothing more.

To leave is easy—the same fissure, the same sideways press of the body. To return is to find it precisely, exactly, unbearably the same.
