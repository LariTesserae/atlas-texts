# The Lit Hollow

It is a room that should not have light in it, and yet it is the brightest place you have ever stood.

You find it below — through a fissure no wider than a shoulder, down a throat of cool stone that beads with condensation, your palms picking up the damp like a second skin. And then the throat opens into a chamber the size of a modest chapel, and the chamber is *full*, and the chamber is *blazing*.

There is no sun here. There are no lamps, no wires, no glass. The light comes from the walls themselves — a calcite-pale rock, raw and new-looking, still faintly wet in its creases as though it finished forming an hour ago and simply never got the message to stop glowing. It is not fire-light. It does not flicker. It is even, shadowless, total — the light of a held note. Where the stone is driest it burns nearly white; where damp tongues run down in thin seasonal trickles, the wet rock glows a dull amber, like something cooling from the inside.

## What Fills It

Everything. That is the first and last fact about the Hollow: there is no unclaimed surface.

The low ceiling is a thicket of pale roots, hanging down though there is no soil above them to speak of, and clinging to the roots are small white moths in such number that the roots seem to breathe — a slow exhale of wings, never all at once, never resolving into stillness, but never changing in aggregate either. The moths have been there, in this exact density, since before you arrived, and they will be there in this exact density after you leave. Nothing here moves toward an ending.

The floor is a stand of pale reedy stalks, leafless, knee-high, growing not from soil but from cracks between flowstone tiles — sparse as a thing, but packed shoulder to shoulder where it does grow, so that there is no clear ground to put a foot on, only stalk after stalk after stalk, swaying though there is no wind, because the moths above stir a current that never resolves into actual weather.

Between the stalks: snails. Pale, glassy-shelled, one every few inches, motionless or nearly so, except that wherever you are not looking, you sense they have shifted. Among the snails: a single thread of clear water finding its way along a groove in the rock, never pooling, never drying — just present, perpetually, like a sentence that never finishes.

And threading through all of it, at every height — floor, root-ceiling, wall-niche — small pale creatures you do not have a name for. Not quite salamander, not quite something with wings folded wrong. They do not flee you. They do not approach you. They occupy their stations the way the moths occupy theirs, the way the snails occupy theirs: as if placement here is not behavior but architecture. As if the ecology were not living so much as *furnishing*.

## The Sound

It is loud — shockingly loud for a sealed stone room with no wind and no fire. It is the sound of the moths' wings multiplied by their thousands; the dry click of stalks; small clawed feet on flowstone; a chorus of frog-adjacent throats that never quite locate a melody but never stop reaching for one; the thread of water finding its single, endless groove. None of these sounds is sharp. All of them together make something like a held chord, dense as the chamber itself, a sound with no silence in it anywhere, the way the light has no shadow in it anywhere.

## Built Things

Scattered, almost apologetic against all this saturation: three or four stones stacked with evident intention near the chamber's east curve, too orderly to be geological accident, too crude and lichen-fused to read as recent. A shallow notch worn into one wall at exactly the height of a hand. Nothing else. No tools, no char, no rope-marks. Whoever or whatever stacked those stones did it once, long enough ago that the chamber's other inhabitants have grown up entirely around the gesture without remarking on it, and then never returned, and never will, because nothing here returns or departs. The stones are simply one more fixture, no more privileged than a snail.

## The Rule of the Place

Nothing here is becoming anything else.

The rock is "newly formed" in appearance only — it will not finish forming. The water-thread will not widen into a stream or dry into nothing. The moths will not swarm down to the stalks, and the stalks will not climb up to the roots. The light will not dim, brighten, gutter, or steady further than it already has, because it is already at the only level it knows. You have walked into an equilibrium so complete it reads as a held breath — except the breath is not being held *toward* anything. It is not suspense. It is simply the shape the place has always had and will always have, a moment of geological youth frozen at youth forever, mid-gesture, the erosion that should age it never quite arriving, the formation that should complete it never quite finishing.

This is, perhaps, the strangeness underneath the strangeness: not the glowing rock, not the unnamed fauna, not the impossible packed stillness-in-motion of wings and stalks and snails — but the sense, settling into you slowly as your eyes adjust to a brightness that has nowhere to go, that you are the only variable in the room. The only thing here capable of change is you. Everything else has already arrived, completely, at what it is, and is simply — gloriously, deafeningly, blindingly — *being* it, world without end, one hand's-width below the surface of the ordinary ground.
