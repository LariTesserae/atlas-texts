# The Hollow Concourse

The ceiling is close enough to feel rather than see — a low gray vault of stone that never lifts higher than thirty meters and often dips to ten, running unbroken for what must be kilometers in every direction. This is not a cave in the way that word suggests intimacy or enclosure. It is a *plain* that happens to have a lid. The scale of the place reveals itself slowly, in the way distant sound arrives before distant shape does: you understand the size of this hollow through your ears long before your eyes adjust enough to confirm it.

## Ground and Stone

The floor is broken rock, young rock — sharp-edged slabs and rubble that haven't yet had the centuries needed to round themselves smooth. Fracture lines run across it in long pale seams, evidence of a ceiling that settled into its current shape not so very long ago, geologically speaking, and then stopped. Everything here gives the impression of having arrived recently and then frozen. No slow drip has carved a basin. No flowstone drapes the walls in the patient sheets that take ten thousand years to form. The stone is honest about its youth: angular, undecorated, raw at the edges.

Underfoot the rock varies between dry dust-grit and patches of damp where moisture has crept up from somewhere below or seeped in from some unseen fissure above. These wet patches don't pool — there's no standing water, no streams — but they darken the stone in irregular blots, and where they persist, a faint mineral smell rises, cool and metallic, mixing with the drier dust-smell of the open stretches between.

## Light

What light exists comes from nowhere obvious. Perhaps thin seams in the ceiling stone let through a diffuse grayness from a sun this place barely remembers. Perhaps something in the rock itself holds a faint luminescence, the chemical afterglow of minerals reacting slow and steady. Whatever the source, it is dim and even, casting no real shadows, making every distant shape into a suggestion rather than a certainty. You see motion before you see form. You see silhouette before you see detail. The dimness doesn't frighten so much as flatten — colors are muted to grays and browns, and only up close does anything resolve into texture.

## The Sparse Green

Vegetation survives here against expectation — not lush, never lush, but present in a scattered, stubborn way. Pale fungal shelves cling to outcrops. Wiry colorless lichen spreads across the underside of low ceiling-shelves where the dim light pools thickest. Here and there a stand of blanched, leafless stalks pushes up from a damp patch, rootless-looking, swaying faintly though there should be no wind this far below. These aren't forests. They're punctuation — single sparse marks in long stretches of bare stone, marking the only places where moisture and light overlap enough to permit anything to hold on.

## The Sound Before the Sight

What dominates this place, what makes it impossible to describe without describing first, is the noise.

The space is loud — not with any one source but with accumulation. Clicks. Skitters. Long low calls that roll across the open ceiling-space and return as echo, layered over fresher versions of themselves. Wingbeats, though you rarely see what's flying. Click-trains of communication or echolocation bouncing in chains across unseen distances. Underfoot, constant small disturbances — rock-scrape, claw-tick, the rustle of many bodies moving over loose stone, never quite synchronized.

This is a hollow absolutely thick with animal life, vastly out of proportion to the sparse plant cover and thin water. The fauna doesn't make ecological sense by surface logic — there's not enough green here, not enough wet, to support what the ears insist is an enormous standing population of creatures. But the rules here are not the surface's rules. Perhaps the food web roots somewhere unseen, in fungal mats that don't register as "vegetation" by ordinary measure, or in nutrients carried down through that intermittent damp from above. Perhaps it doesn't need to make sense — the ecology of this place is simply *given*, stable, balanced at a level that has nothing to do with what an outsider would predict from the available resources.

The creatures themselves keep their distance from clear sight even as they crowd the dim space with sound. Pale shapes flickering at the edge of vision. Many-legged things that move in quick scuttling bursts across open ground and vanish into shadow-creases between rock slabs. Things that fly low and erratic between ceiling and floor, never landing where you can watch. Occasionally something larger lumbers across a distant stretch, visible only as a slow gray mass before it merges back into the general dimness. Eyes — when you catch them — are pale or absent, adapted long ago to a place where seeing matters less than hearing.

Despite the density of sound, the density of *presence* — of solid, countable things you could point to — stays sparse. Long open stretches of bare stone separate one cluster of activity from the next. You can walk for a long while through near-silence relative to the rest, then crest a low rise of rubble and walk straight into a wall of noise where a hundred unseen things have congregated around some damp seam or fungal shelf. The hollow doesn't distribute its life evenly. It clusters it, then leaves long gaps, then clusters again — a rhythm that repeats across the whole visible-by-sound extent of the place.

## Hand-Tool Traces

There is almost nothing built here — no walls, no shelters, no worked stone structures. But not nothing. If you search the rubble near the damp patches you might find a few worked flakes, a stone shaped crudely to an edge, the dropped tool of something that passes through with hands and purpose but builds no home. Whoever or whatever shapes these tools doesn't stay. They pass, they discard, they move on through the dim and the noise, leaving only these small deliberate marks among the much larger quantity of natural rubble — proof that something here thinks ahead, however simply, however briefly.

## Equilibrium

Nothing here is becoming anything else. The temperature holds cool and constant, the dim light neither brightens nor fades, the damp patches neither spread nor dry. The fracture-young stone, despite its sharp edges suggesting recent violence, has settled into a configuration that will not visibly change across any span of time a visitor could witness. The overwhelming animal population is not migrating, not booming, not crashing — it simply *is*, at this density, making this much noise, forever, as far as the place is concerned. 

This is a hollow at rest in its own strangeness: too loud for its visible life, too sparse in green and water to explain its abundance, too young in its stone to have settled — and yet settled anyway, fixed at this exact unlikely balance, stretching out past sight in every direction under its low close ceiling, world without edge, without change, full of unseen things calling to each other across the dark.
