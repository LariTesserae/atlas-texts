# The Standing Greens

There is no ground here, only the agreement that something must be stood upon, and the architecture has resolved this agreement entirely in vegetal terms.

What you walk on is built — every surface, every grade, every terrace cut at the elevation that geology would have taken a hundred thousand years to arrange. But geology never touched it. Instead: trunks grown to load-bearing thickness in patterns too regular for any forest, branches woven and fused at the joints into something between lattice and rib cage, holding up platforms of packed root-mat wide enough to lose a town in. The whole structure reads as *recently arrived at its final form* — bark still pale at the seams, fresh growth-scars where one limb was guided into another and made to stay, the slight greenness-of-newness on surfaces that haven't yet weathered into anything. It is finished, but only just. As if it grew yesterday and will never grow again.

Because it won't. This place does not change. Whatever forced the growth into these load-bearing forms — whatever intelligence or pressure or grammar — stopped the moment the structure achieved equilibrium, and now it simply holds. No further erosion. No seasonal drop of leaf, no slow lean toward collapse. The wet keeps everything supple rather than rotting it; the warmth keeps everything green rather than dormant. It exists in a held breath.

The scale opens outward — this isn't a glade, it's a *region*, terrace after terrace of grown architecture receding toward a horizon hazed with condensation, climbing in elevation the way a hill town climbs, except every wall, stair, balustrade, and roof is a cultivar, alive, breathing slightly, glistening. Between these built-green masses there is real distance — wide aired-out gaps of mist and standing water, walkways of root that go a long way before reaching the next grown structure. Sparse, despite the totality of the vegetation: the green doesn't crowd, it *occupies* — declaring territory and then leaving the rest open, the way a sparse arrangement of monuments declares a plaza.

Water sits everywhere at the surface without ever quite becoming a feature of its own — it's not a river or marsh, it's an ambient seep. Platforms shine wet. Stairs of braided root weep at the joints. Fog stands in the open gaps between terraces and never burns off, because the light here, while clear and normal in its falling, doesn't carry enough heat-edge to dry anything. Everything sounds like dripping even when nothing is.

And the sound otherwise is loud — not chaotic, but *full*: a steady, layered acoustic pressure that seems to be the architecture itself working. Creaks too slow and resonant to be wind. Long groans like ships' hulls, except nothing here floats and nothing here moves. Wet ticking. A continuous low underexhale that might be air finding its way through the structure's countless living vents. There is hardly any fauna to make these sounds — what little moves here is glimpsed rarely, small and pale, gone before it's identified — so the loudness belongs to the plant-built mass itself, the sound of something enormous holding its shape.

There is no tool anywhere. No metal, no cut edge, no device. Whatever built this did so by training growth, not by manufacturing — the total built-ness of the place and the total absence of technology sit together without contradiction, the way a hedge maze is built and yet pre-tool. Every wall was *grown into being a wall*. Every stair was a root persuaded downward at intervals.

The strangeness is not in any single object but in the accumulation: the wrongness of total construction with zero artifact, the held-breath stillness of something so clearly mid-act, the way distances keep opening between green monuments that should, by their density of meaning, be close together. You keep expecting builders. There are none. There is no record of them either — no tool marks, no discard, no path of construction debris. Only the seamless presumption that the green simply arrived already arranged, and has been arranged, without alteration, ever since.

Underfoot the root-platforms give very slightly, springily, releasing a smell of warm wet bark and standing water. Overhead the canopy-roofs filter the normal daylight into a green-gold wash that does not flicker, does not move with wind, because the air here, too, has agreed to be still. Walk far enough along any terrace and you will reach another terrace exactly like it in kind but never in detail — the same grown logic, restated, world without end, holding its single completed shape forever.
