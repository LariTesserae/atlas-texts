# The Singing Scarp

The plateau sits at a height where the air thins and brightens, and the light here does not so much fall as *accumulate* — a white-gold density that seems to have nowhere to drain to, pooling against every surface of stone until the whole landscape looks slightly overexposed, slightly too true. There is no haze to soften it. No leaf, no frond, no lichen-crust interrupts the光 with shadow-play, because nothing grows here, nothing has ever grown here, and the rock has the clean, unbitten look of a world still waiting for the first spore that will never come.

What there is, in place of vegetation, is *stone in excess* — an absolutely packed field of mineral forms crowding the highland from edge to edge with no negative space between them. Fins of pale rock stand shoulder to shoulder like a frozen crowd. Boulders nest inside boulders. Wherever the eye looks for a gap, a clearing, a patch of bare ground to rest on, it finds instead more shape: nodules, blades, stacked plates, hollowed domes, all packed so tightly that walking through it means a constant negotiation of shoulders and knees against cold mineral flank. The density is the first thing the body understands about this place — that there is no room here, that everything has already been filled.

And it is wet, despite the height and the chill. Water doesn't pool on the surface so much as *occupy* the stone from within — every fin and dome weeps faintly at its base, a slow seep finding the path of least resistance down through capillary cracks, beading at lips of rock, dropping. The whole plateau is laced with thin trickles you can hear before you see, threading between the packed forms, vanishing into the deep press of stone-on-stone where the eye can't follow. The rock itself looks perpetually damp-dark at its lower third, pale and dry above, like material standing in shallow water it has stood in for a very long time and will stand in for a very long time more.

This is the source of the sound, and the sound is the place's true dimension — not ambient, not background, but total. Because the rock is so tightly packed, so uniformly hard and resonant, every drop that falls rings against a surface that has nowhere to absorb it, and the ring travels, bounces, multiplies against ten thousand neighboring faces, until what reaches the ear is not water-dripping but a vast continuous chiming roar, deafening, structureless, coming from every direction with equal weight. There is no quiet pocket. There is no lee side. Stand anywhere on the scarp and the sound is total immersion, a wall of bright ringing noise built from nothing but gravity and stone and the high thin trickle of melt or seep finding its way down through cracks that have channeled it this same way for longer than anything has been alive to notice.

Because nothing changes here. This is a landscape locked at equilibrium — the erosion settled into its long-final shape generations past any visible recalibration, the water cycling through the same capillaries season after season without measurably reshaping them, the light arriving at the same bright angle, the cold holding at the same edge-of-uncomfortable plateau-cool, day after unmarked day. Nothing is happening *to* this place. It simply continues being exactly this, with the fixed permanence of something that reached its final form long ago and was satisfied.

There is no built thing anywhere in it — no wall, no path, no cairn, not even the accidental architecture of fallen branches, because there are no branches. No tool has touched this stone. It is unworked in the most absolute sense: every fin and dome and plate is exactly the shape that frost and seep and the original cooling or settling of the rock gave it, untouched by hand or hoof.

Fauna is a rumor here, not a population — perhaps once in an hour something pale and small moves between two fins, low to the rock, gone before its shape resolves; a thing that may have wings, may have none, that finds whatever it needs in the packed mineral interstices and asks nothing of the surface. No nest, no track, no call distinguishable from the water-ring. The place is too loud for any creature voice to register as anything but a strange overtone briefly riding the chime before submerging again into the dominant roar.

The unusualness of the place isn't dramatic — there's no impossible geometry, no glow, no violation of physical law — but it accumulates in the body anyway: the wrongness of brightness without warmth, fullness without growth, sound without source you can point to, wetness with no pool, no stream, no destination — water that seems to seep eternally without ever collecting anywhere visible, as though the whole plateau drinks its own runoff and gives it back to itself, a closed loop, self-feeding, going nowhere, changing nothing, ringing forever at the top of the world under a light that has already decided what time it is and will not reconsider.
