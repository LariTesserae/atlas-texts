# The Needle-Throat

There is a spire here, but to call it a mountain would be wrong — it is too sharp, too young, too deliberate. It punches up out of a cracked basin of dark glass-stone, and you do not see its top. Vapor wreathes it at the middle elevations and never higher; what happens above the vapor line is a rumor the rock itself doesn't have access to.

You are not on the spire. You are *inside* a single fissure near its base — a vertical wound in the mineral flesh of the thing, wide enough at its mouth to admit a person sideways, narrowing and widening unpredictably as it climbs. This is the whole of the place. The world, for now, is this throat of stone.

**Material and heat**

The walls are obsidian-black and obsidian-sharp, fractured along conchoidal lines that haven't had time to soften — every edge here could still open skin, because nothing has come along yet to dull it. Run a hand along the surface and you'll find facets like the inside of a geode that never finished growing. The stone is warm to hot, depending on depth — heat bleeds upward from somewhere below the reachable world, a slow geothermal exhale. In places the rock is almost too hot to lean against; in others, just a few meters on, it's merely warm, like skin.

Moisture comes as condensation, not rain. Where the rising heat meets cooler air pockets trapped in wider chambers of the fissure, water beads on the black facets and runs in threads no wider than a hair, vanishing into hairline cracks before they can pool. Occasionally — rarely — a wider basin in the floor holds a held breath of water, mirror-still, hot to the touch, gone within a day.

**What was built here**

The strangeness is this: the fissure is not empty of intention. At irregular intervals — never where you'd predict — the black stone gives way to *structure*. A ring of fused metal, seamless, embedded flush with the rock as though grown rather than installed, circles the passage like a vertebra. Further up, a lattice of lines too fine to be wiring and too patterned to be mineral veining spreads across forty meters of wall, pulsing faintly with a light that has no clear source and serves no clear purpose. There are thresholds — not doors, exactly, but places where the fissure's walls have been *finished*, smoothed to a polish no natural process here would produce, then left to the elements with no further explanation.

This is urban in the way a single nerve ganglion is urban relative to a body: organized, dense with function, but isolated, a settlement-fragment with no settlement visible around it. Whatever built the rings and the lattice either has retracted entirely from this place, or was never here in a way bodies would recognize. The engineering exceeds what the rock should permit — materials phase into the stone without seam, structures hold geometries that argue gently with how shadows should fall. Stand at the right threshold and your own shadow will lag a half-second behind your movement, then catch up, as if apologizing.

**Emptiness, scale, quiet**

Nothing lives here. No lichen, no insect, no breath but your own. The emptiness is not absence so much as held space — the fissure is mostly air and updraft and the occasional drip, with vast unlit verticality above and below any given foothold. Sound doesn't travel far before the convolutions of the rock eat it; a footstep returns to you smaller than you sent it out, and longer silences settle in afterward, broken only by:

- the tick of cooling stone readjusting,
- a low subsonic hum from the lattice-walls that you feel more than hear,
- distant percussive pops, like settling, somewhere far below in the throat's depths,
- and, very rarely, a long exhalation of warm air moving upward through the whole structure at once, smelling faintly of hot metal and nothing organic at all.

**Rules of the place**

The fissure does not erode the way old stone erodes. Things are sharp because they are new — the whole spire may be young in some geological reckoning, thrust up by forces still active beneath it, the rock still settling into its final shape. This activeness is the dynamism here: not weather, not life, but the structure itself still arriving, still finishing. Walls shift by millimeters between visits. A passage that was open last time may have pinched shut, sealed by something extruding from inside the rock — not lava, not flesh, something else, cooling into the same obsidian black so seamlessly you can't find the scar.

The built elements seem to respond to depth, or pressure, or some variable that isn't obvious from inside — the rings glow faintly brighter the further up you climb, the lattice grows denser, until at some unmarked point further progress is refused, not by collapse or wall, but by a soft, total resistance, like trying to walk through deep water that isn't there.

Whoever — or whatever — finished this place is not present, but the place still seems to be *for* something. You are a guest inside an unfinished instrument, hot-veined and lightless and watching you, in whatever way a fissure can watch, with patience that predates the concept of a visitor.
