# The Static Canopy

There is no horizon here, only the place where the dark green becomes the dark black, and even that boundary is a guess. The land — if it can be called land, since nothing solid has been seen or touched by anything resembling a creature in longer than memory allows — is a single continuous mass of vegetation extending beyond any scale a body could comprehend. Not forest. Forest implies gaps, implies floor and canopy and the architecture of separate trunks. This is something else: plant tissue as bedrock, as atmosphere, as the only substance that has ever existed or will exist. The world is not covered in growth. The world *is* growth, all the way down, for a depth no one has sounded.

It is young. The fronds and stalks and the thick fibrous trunks-that-aren't-trunks still carry sharp edges, fresh ruptures where growth split bark-analog only recently, sap-channels glistening wet and unweathered at their lips. Nothing here has had time to soften. And yet it does not change. This is the contradiction the place insists you accept: newly emerged, and frozen that way. As if something grew at tremendous speed to this exact point and then simply — stopped. Forever. The fracture-lines in the great stalks are permanent fresh wounds, healed shut without scarring, held in eternal first-day rawness.

## Scale and Dark

You could fall through this for the length of a season and call it falling, though there is no sky to fall from or floor to land on — only more of the same wet, warm, layered mass in every direction, close enough in places to brush against from all sides at once. Density without crowding-panic; rather the sense of inhabiting a lung, a single organ vast enough to generate its own internal weather. Somewhere far off — kilometers, maybe more — condensation gathers into private rain clouds that exist only within pockets of the growth, raining down through forty meters of leaf-layer before reaching anything below, and dripping for hours after the cloud itself has dissolved back into vapor.

The darkness is total, but not oppressive — it's ambient, the visual equivalent of silence. There is no sun to expect, no day-night cycle to mark absence against. Wetness condenses everywhere: on stalk-skin, on the undersides of vast frond-canopies, beading and falling in slow individual drops that take, by some accounts, whole minutes to complete their fall through the layered mass before landing on the layer below. This is the only sound. A drip. Then, much later, another, from somewhere else entirely. The quiet is not emptiness — it is a held breath at the scale of a continent.

## What Moves Without Moving

No fauna. No animal sound, no rustle-of-something-passing, no eyes anywhere in the warm wet dark. And yet — the place is not still in the way that emptiness is still. There is a hum.

Not literal machinery, or perhaps exactly that: somewhere within the vegetable mass — woven into it, grown through it and around it as if industry itself were just another nutrient the plants metabolized — there are structures that pulse and turn and process. Not built. Never built; nothing built has ever touched this place. But *industrial* nonetheless: chambers within the largest stalks that compress and expel gas in long mechanical sighs, vast slow turbine-shapes formed from cartilage-stiff leaf-spirals that rotate by some pressure-differential the place generates itself, fluid coursing through vein-systems thick as canals, regulated by valve-organs that click shut with a sound like distant doors. The vegetation has, over some unthinkable span before it froze into stillness, grown its own factory. Not a metaphor for one. An actual mechanized circulatory system, photosynthesis-free since there's no light to feed it, running instead on some chemical engine buried in its own root-mass, processing something, producing something, endlessly, in the dark, forever, changing nothing, going nowhere.

## The Rule of the Place

It does not evolve. This is the deepest strangeness, stranger than the dark or the industrial pulse or the absence of any animal witness: this place reached its current form and then simply ceased to develop further. The fresh wounds never scar over into old scars. The turbine-spirals never wear down. The drip that falls today falls at the same rate, from the same gathering-point, as it did in whatever counts as the beginning. There is growth without the passage of growing — the young rawness is not a phase but the permanent address of this place. Cause without consequence. Engine without combustion's end.

Nothing here is waiting to become something else. It already is, entirely, what it will always be: a warm wet dark green machine, planet-sized, breathing slowly to itself in a silence so deep that the drip of condensation off a frond fifty meters away sounds like a held conversation. There is no one to hear it. There never was. There is only the place, doing the one thing it does, forever, without history and without future, a single enormous moment of becoming that arrived and then declined, gently and permanently, to finish.
