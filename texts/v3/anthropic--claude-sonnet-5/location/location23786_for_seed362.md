# The Greenhouse Continent

There is no soil here, and there never was. What you walk on is scaffolding — kilometers of it, riveted and welded into a lattice that runs from horizon to horizon and, by report of those who have climbed far enough, downward into strata nobody has reached. The scaffolding is iron once, copper in places, something unnameable in others, oxidized to colors that don't appear on any chart: a green that is almost a sound, a rust that pulses faintly like a coal that won't quite go out. Onto this frame, something planted a single forest and never stopped tending it. That tending continues. No one knows by what.

## The Mass

The vegetation is total in the way a flood is total. It does not grow *on* the structure so much as it has become co-extensive with it — vines thick as freight cars spiral the load-bearing trusses, root systems have grown through riveted plate the way ice splits rock, given enough patient centuries. Canopy stacks on canopy: a low tier of pale fan-fronds clatters gently against pipe-work at ankle height, a middle tier of broad waxy leaves the size of barn doors forms a second floor of shade some twenty meters up, and above that — far above, lost in the bright haze — a third and possibly fourth ceiling that you only ever glimpse as a deeper color against the white sky. Between these tiers run gantries of bare cable-vine, structural and alive at once, impossible to say if they are growing toward the machinery or the machinery was built to receive them.

Everything is made. There is no dirt, no rock, no exposed ground at any point in the traversable world — only decking, grating, plate, conduit, and the plant-mass that has annexed all of it as a single uninterrupted understory. The line between infrastructure and organism dissolved a long time ago, by erosion's quiet evidence: seams have softened, rivets have grown a bark-like patina, gauges on ancient control panels read in units that match nothing, half-buried under a moss that glows faintly chartreuse in shadow and goes invisible in direct light.

## Engines

Tech runs through all of it, industrial-grade and largely untended, still working. Somewhere below, vast compressors breathe — you feel it more than hear it, a slow pressure-change every several minutes, like the whole structure inhaling. Pipes thick as silo-tanks run alongside trunk-vines so dense in their tangle that you cannot tell, by sight, which one is carrying steam and which is carrying sap; in places they appear to be carrying both, the same fluid, indifferent to source. Lights are strung overhead on cable that may be vine or may be true wire — eternally on, white-gold, far too bright for any time of day, throwing hard clean shadows that don't move with any sun because there isn't one to find. The light source is simply *up*, diffuse and total, like being inside a bulb the size of a sky.

The factories are still running. You find their products everywhere and never see them made: smooth ceramic pods the size of skulls, hanging in clusters from particular branches, faintly warm; spools of cord extruded continuously from a valve in a tree-trunk that is also, unmistakably, a pipe joint; fine metallic dust that drifts down in the stillest hours and coats every upward-facing leaf in a film like frost.

## Weirdness

This is where the place stops behaving. Perspective bends near the largest trunk-columns — walk toward one for an hour and you arrive having somehow also passed it twice. Leaves occasionally hold their shape but lose their color entirely, going to a flat television-grey that nonetheless still photosynthesizes, you assume, because the plant doesn't seem to mind. In certain clearings (and there are clearings — paved squares of bare grating where nothing grows, perfectly geometric, for no findable reason) gravity runs a few degrees off true, just enough that dropped objects roll toward a corner that isn't the lowest point. Echoes arrive before their sound. Some of the larger pods, when they finally split open — rare, maybe once a "season," though there's no real winter to mark one — release not seeds but small mechanical shapes, geared and silent, that crawl off into the deep canopy and are never seen again.

Scale defeats memory. This is not a forest or a building; it is a continent's worth of both, fused, and weather happens inside it as if it were sky. Fog generates in the mid-canopy and rains downward as condensation, never as true rain, beading on broad leaves and falling in fat, occasional drops that strike the decking far below with a sound like a single dripping faucet, amplified across kilometers into something almost ceremonial. Wind moves through the upper tiers in long slow swells, audible as a hiss that takes a full minute to cross your position and is gone for ten before the next.

## Quiet

Fauna is scarce, and what exists keeps its distance and its silence. Pale, many-legged things the size of a hand move along the underside of leaves, grazing on the metallic dust; you see their tracks — faint trails wiped clean through the film — far more often than you see them. Occasionally something larger shifts in the deep canopy overhead, a transfer of weight from one great bough to the next, gone before it resolves into a shape. No birds. No calls. No chorus. The dominant sound is mechanical-organic and very, very quiet: the long-interval breathing of the compressors, the tick of cooling metal, the slow continuous creak of branches taking on new growth, the occasional far-off clatter of a pod releasing its cargo into the leaves below. Silence here is not absence; it's a held breath the size of a world, kept that way on purpose by something patient enough to still be tending it.

## What It's For

No one who lives at its edges claims to know what the continent produces, or who it produces it for. The leading theory, passed mouth to mouth among the scaffold-walkers who map its outer rim, is that it isn't producing anything at all — that this is what's left after the *purpose* finished and only the *process* remained, industrial habit running on past its reason, vegetable growth filling every gap the factories left open, the two locked now in a partnership neither remembers starting and neither knows how to end. The lights stay on. The pods keep opening. The compressors keep breathing. And the forest, vast and bright and built down to its smallest leaf, keeps very, very quietly growing.
