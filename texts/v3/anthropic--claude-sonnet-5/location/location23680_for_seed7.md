# The Steam-Pocket

You drop through a fissure no wider than a doorway and the heat finds you before the light fails entirely — a wet, mineral warmth that clings to the back of the throat. This is a hollow below the world's skin, young in the way a wound is young: edges still sharp, walls still raw where the rock tore apart rather than wore away. Something split this chamber open recently, geologically speaking. The fracture-lines on the ceiling look like they happened yesterday, though "yesterday" here might mean ten thousand years.

The space is small — a single chamber, maybe the size of a chapel, maybe the size of a barn, its true dimensions hard to judge because the dark eats the corners. What light exists comes low and orange, in patches: a vein of something faintly luminescent threading through one wall, a scatter of pale fungal growths clustered near a fracture where damp seeps through. You see by suggestion more than by illumination — the rest filled in by touch and by the heat-shimmer that distorts whatever you think you're looking at.

## Ground and Growth

The floor is uneven basalt-dark stone, broken into shelves and sudden dips, still sharp underfoot — nothing here has had time to round off. Out of the cracks, against all expectation, vegetation pushes through in loose, scattered stands: thick fleshy-leaved things, pale stalks crowned with broad fan-leaves angled toward the faint glow-veins like sunflowers worshipping a dim and stationary sun. They grow in clusters with wide gaps between them — a stand here, fifteen feet of bare warm stone, another stand there. The plants are glossy, swollen with stored moisture, leaves slick with condensation that beads and drips with a slowness you can count. This is the only place water shows itself: not streams, not pools, just sweat on stone and leaf, a humidity that sits on the skin rather than soaking it.

There is no birdsong here, no insect hum, no skitter in the leaf-litter. No fauna at all. The lushness exists in total silence except for itself — the occasional papery creak of a stalk bending, the tick of condensation falling from a leaf-tip to stone, three seconds apart, then twelve, then one, in no rhythm you can predict.

## What Was Built

Scattered through the chamber, never crowded, are the marks of something industrial that arrived and then mostly left. A pipe, thick and riveted, runs along one wall for twenty feet before vanishing into the rock — disconnected at both visible ends, leading nowhere now. Near it, a squat block of corroded machinery, vaguely engine-shaped, sits cold and silent, one dial face still intact under a film of mineral crust. Whatever process this place once served has slowed to nothing, or to something so gradual it reads as nothing — a faint thrum, occasionally, through the stone itself, more felt in the molars than heard, gone before you're sure it happened. Heat without flame. Power without motion. As if the engine still runs, somewhere far off and far slow, on a cycle measured in years per stroke.

The artifacts don't cluster into anything like a room or a station. They're scattered the way the plants are scattered — a machine-shape, then bare stone, then a coil of cable half-grown-over with the fan-leafed plants, then nothing for a long stretch, then a hatch in the floor, welded shut, warm to the touch.

## The Wrongness of It

The unusual quality isn't loud. It's in the details that don't resolve when you look closer. The luminescent vein in the wall doesn't flicker like bioluminescence or glow like mineral phosphorescence — it pulses, slowly, on a period of minutes, like something breathing at the bottom of a very long breath. The dial on the dead machine, when you wipe the crust away, shows numbers in no sequence you recognize, and the needle — you'd swear — has moved an eighth of an inch since you first looked.

The droplets falling from the leaves don't always splash where gravity says they should.

Nothing here is dangerous, exactly. Nothing moves fast enough to be dangerous. But the chamber holds the specific unease of a place that is mid-process — not ruin, not genesis, but the slow middle of something neither finished happening to it nor finished happening because of it. The plants didn't grow back after people left. The machines didn't break down from disuse. Both are still actively becoming what they are, on a timescale that makes a human visit feel like a single frame pulled from a very long film.

## To Stay Here

If you wait — and you will be tempted to, because the heat is not unkind and the silence is not hostile — you'll start to notice the chamber keeping its own time. The drip-rhythm of condensation. The slow pulse of the wall-vein. The almost-imperceptible creep of the machine's needle. None of it urgent. All of it moving.

You get the sense that nothing here has finished arriving, and nothing has finished leaving, and the gap between those two slow processes is where you're standing.
