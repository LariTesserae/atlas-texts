# The Cistern of Standing Light

You descend through a fissure no wider than a doorway, and the rock swallows the sky behind you. But it does not get dark.

## The Chamber

This is a below-surface hollow, roughly the size of a modest amphitheater — call it grove-scale, room-scale, though no grove ever held what this holds. The walls are old basalt, established over some unfathomable span: smoothed in long convex sweeps, fluted by mineral-laden water that has run down them for longer than the structure embedded in their center has existed. You can read the erosion like rings in wood — slow centuries of trickle and drip, patient and indifferent.

And then, set into the chamber's heart, anachronistic against all that patience: a standing column of light, perhaps four meters across, rising from a black pool to a ceiling it has — over time — bored clean through. Not a beam. A *column*, solid-edged, faintly humming, water vapor condensing on its outer skin and falling back as a constant warm rain that never quite reaches the floor before re-evaporating into the chamber's wet, thick air.

This is the source of the brightness — not sun, not fire. Something engineered, though "engineered" undersells it: the light behaves wrong. It refracts at angles that don't correspond to the water it passes through. It does not flicker so much as *renegotiate*, brightening and dimming in pulses that feel almost respiratory, almost thought-like, on a cycle of minutes that never quite repeats.

## Water

Water is everywhere and from everywhere. The walls weep. The basalt floor holds shallow black pools threaded between low ridges, and these pools are warm — geothermal warmth, or something the column itself is feeding into the rock. Condensation drips in syncopated rhythm from stalactite stubs overhead, and the air itself is near-saturate, so that breathing here feels like drinking. The black pool at the column's base is the chamber's low point, and into it falls everything — runoff, condensate, mineral silt — yet it never seems to overflow. Something underneath is drinking it back down, or the column itself is recycling it skyward as the mist that becomes the rain that becomes the pool again. A closed throat, swallowing and exhaling in the same motion.

## Sound

It is loud here, but not chaotic-loud — *layered*-loud. The drip and trickle of weeping stone forms a percussive undercoat. Over that: the hum of the column, which is not constant but oscillates in pitch, sometimes dropping to a subsonic pressure you feel in your sternum, sometimes rising to something almost like a struck bell, almost like a held human vowel. And over *that*: the turbulence — sudden cracks, as the column's energy state shifts and something in the rock responds, a thermal pop, a brief geyser-hiss from one of the black pools as it flash-boils for half a second and subsides. The acoustics of the chamber gather and multiply all this until standing in it is less like hearing and more like being inside an instrument someone is still learning to play.

## Instability

Nothing here holds still for long, despite the room's bones being ancient. This is the contradiction the place lives inside: an old, settled container around an unsettled, escalating engine. The column brightens unpredictably; once or twice an hour it surges hard enough that the black pool boils visibly at its rim and a gout of superheated mist rolls across the floor, dissipating before it reaches the walls. Mineral deposits around the base have built and dissolved and rebuilt in visibly different configurations within what must be a single human lifetime — too fast for ordinary geology, evidence that whatever is happening here is reshaping its own container in real time. The light sometimes throws shadows that lag a half-second behind the objects casting them, as if space near the column is still catching up to itself.

This is not decay. It is not collapse. It reads, instead, like a process mid-creation — a slow, repeated act of *making*, never quite finished, occasionally backsliding into something that looks like destruction before resolving again into order. Solaris-logic: the chamber is not a ruin and not a machine but something closer to an organ, still working, still figuring out what it's for.

## What Grows, What Doesn't

Vegetation is sparse to the point of austerity: pale mosses cling to the wetter ledges, colorless without sun-proper light to feed photosynthesis as usual, surviving instead on whatever spectrum the column casts. A few threadlike lichens, rust and bone-white, mottle the drier stone above the waterline. That's all — there isn't food enough, in the ordinary sense, for more.

Fauna is rarer still. Pale, blind things move in the margins — a kind of long-limbed amphibian, translucent-skinned, that seems to feed on mineral film and whatever falls into the pools. They are seen, not often, at the edge of vision, gone before you can be sure. Something — birds, or bird-analogues, small and quick — occasionally cuts across the upper light-shaft from above, framed for an instant in the bore-hole the column burned through the ceiling, before vanishing back into the ordinary sky.

## Traces of Builders

Built presence is scattered, faint, almost apologetic: a few worked stones forming a rough threshold near the fissure entrance, smoothed by hands and then by centuries of water after the hands left. A single post of some dark alloy, half-submerged in one of the black pools, etched with markings too eroded and too alien at once to parse — they might be instructions, might be a name, might be decoration. No settlement, no continuous habitation. Whoever or whatever built — or *found* — the column did not stay. They left this one seed of technology embedded in old rock and let several geological epochs happen around it.

## Rules of the Place

The light does not cast true shadow-logic; objects near the column sometimes throw two shadows, faintly out of phase.

Water that touches the column's surface light does not simply evaporate — it carries a faint luminescence with it for several minutes after, so that the mist drifting through the chamber glows in slow-dissolving streaks, like the place is exhaling visible thought.

Time near the column does not pass identically to time at the chamber's edges — nothing dramatic, just a faint lag, a held breath's difference, noticeable only if you've been counting your own heartbeat since you arrived.

And the chamber, despite all this violence of process, never floods, never collapses, never goes dark. It has found, however briefly, however many thousand years it's been finding it, a balance between the made and the eroded, the explosive and the patient — a small, loud, wet, brilliant room where something very old and something far beyond old are still working out, together, what they are going to become.
