# The Weeping Throat

There is a fissure on the high shoulder of the mountain — no wider than a held door, no deeper than three men standing on each other's collarbones — and inside it, the world stops behaving.

You would not find it unless you were already lost. The approach is extreme elevation, a blade-ridge of stone so new it still has the fracture-gloss of rock that has never felt a thousand winters — sharp enough to open boots, edges that haven't yet been told by time to round themselves off. The air up here is thin and should be cold, and is not. It is hot the way breath is hot, the way something alive is hot, and this wrongness is your first clue.

The fissure exhales steam in slow pulses, like lungs underwater. Condensation runs down both inner walls in continuous sheets, not dripping but *flowing*, the rock weeping from pores too small to see, and where the steam meets the cooler outer air it forms a curtain across the opening — you have to walk through warm rain to enter a place where it is not raining.

## Inside

The pocket-world inside is small. You could shout from one end and be heard at the other, if shouting were a thing anyone would do here, which it is not. Everyone who enters this place lowers their voice without being told to.

The walls are wet basalt-black stone, young-fractured, their faces still showing the conchoidal scars of whatever violence made them — yet they bend wrong. A ledge you swear ran level along the left wall has, by the time your eye reaches its far end, become the ceiling. There is no single moment where this happens. It simply *is* the ceiling now, and was always going to be, and you cannot locate the seam where the betrayal occurred. Water beads and runs along this impossible surface in directions that argue with each other — three separate streams crossing the same square foot of stone, each following its own private idea of *down*.

The heat is wet-hot, a held-breath heat, sweat that won't evaporate, condensing instead on your own skin to join the general dampness of the place, so that within a few minutes you are simply part of the same moisture cycle as the rock.

## Who Else Is Here

This is the part that should not fit in a space this size, and does.

The fissure is *full* — packed, layered, near-touching — with bodies. Not many kinds. A single kind, repeated past sense: pale, eyeless, segmented things, longer than a forearm and thinner than a wrist, the color of the inside of a mouth. They cling to every wet surface, walls, the wrong-ceiling, each other. They move in slow continuous unrest, never still, a mass with the texture of held breath, thousands where there is room for tens. They do not bite, do not flee, do not seem to register you except as another wet warm surface to redistribute themselves around. Their only sound is a faint collective tick and slide, shell-against-stone, swallowed almost entirely by the steam-curtain at the entrance. This is the quiet that the 0.4 demands — not silence, but a hush built from an overwhelming number of small things being careful.

Once an hour, without any visible trigger, the entire mass shudders in one wave from the back of the fissure to the front, like a held flinch passing through a single nervous system. Then it settles back into its slow boil of motion. This is the ecosystem in motion the place insists on — a tide with no moon, a pulse with no obvious heart.

## The Hands

Despite being lost, despite the wrongness, you are not the first. Wedged into a dry recess above the worst of the dripping — dry by some grace of the bent geometry — there is evidence of someone's tools. Not many. A hafted stone point, lashed with cord gone stiff and dark with the damp. A second tool, a scraper, edge still keen, primitive but deliberate, made by a hand that understood leverage and edge-angle without understanding metal. No structure, no shelter, no sign anyone *stayed* — only that someone came this far up the mountain, into this fissure full of pale moving bodies, and left a tool behind, either as offering, or in flight, or because the geometry of the place took the rest of them and left only what they were holding.

## What the Place Does

The reality-break is not loud. It does not announce itself with light or sound. It is patient and structural: surfaces continue past where they should end, distances inside the fissure do not match distances measured from outside it, and twice — only twice, ever, to anyone who has spoken of this place at all — someone has found a second tool-mark above the first, identical, made by the same hand, dated by the dust to a hundred years apart.

The rule, if there is one: the fissure does not grow, does not erode the way young stone should, does not let its silt build up the way three feet of constant weeping water should silt a floor in any normal span of years. It stays exactly as fresh, exactly as sharp-edged, exactly as recently-made as the day whatever made it finished making it. The pale things may be the reason. They may be the proof. They may simply be tenants, as confused by the bent ceiling as anyone, going about whatever business a thousand blind segmented things conduct in the warm dark, while outside, the ridge goes on being extreme and empty and correctly old, and only this one wet mouth in the mountain's shoulder refuses to grow up.
