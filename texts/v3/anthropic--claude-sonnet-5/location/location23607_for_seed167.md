# The Drowned Warren

The water has no surface here, or rather the surface is so far above that it has become theoretical, a rumor the warm currents tell as they pass through empty doorways. This is a city that the sea took slowly and then kept, the way a mouth keeps something it has decided not to swallow — held, suspended, dissolving by degrees that are too patient to call decay.

Darkness is total except where it isn't: pale threads of bioluminescence following the old roadways like nerve-light, the soft pulsing undersides of things that drift between the buildings. You navigate by these veins. There is no other way.

## The Architecture of What Stayed

The construction here was dense once — stone and something like fired clay, low domes and long terraced structures built close together, narrow channels between them that might have been streets or might have been irrigation, the distinction lost. No tower exceeds three stories; the builders kept low, kept level, kept close to whatever ground this was before the ground became a floor for the whole ocean to stand on.

Walls have softened at their edges. Corners that were once square now curve, sanded by two thousand years — or twenty thousand — of unhurried current. Roofs have mostly given way, opening rooms to the water like flowers that forgot to close. Doorframes stand without doors. The wood is gone; only the stone remembers it was ever divided from the outside.

There is no vegetation to speak of — a scatter of pale anemone-like growths in the building-mouths, fans of something rootless clinging to lintels, but nothing green, nothing that photosynthesizes in any meaningful sense this far from a sun that doesn't reach. What little life that could be called "plant" survives on whatever drifts down, filtering, waiting, sparse as a held breath.

## What Moves Instead

The fauna is where the density lives. The buildings are layered with bodies — not crowded in the way of a reef, but populated in the way of a place that has been adopted, room by room, by things that found the architecture convenient. Eel-bodied things coil through window-gaps. Schools move through the larger halls in slow wheeling masses, scattering the bioluminescent light into momentary constellations before reforming on the other side. Something large moves at the edge of visibility at all times — you hear it more than see it, a displacement, a pressure-change, gone before it resolves.

Predation happens here at the pace biology allows underwater: unhurried, inevitable, almost courteous. A shape will follow another shape through three streets before anything happens, and when it happens it happens without sound, without struggle that travels far, swallowed instantly by the volume of water around it.

## The Quiet

Sound carries strangely and sparsely. Mostly there is a low pressure-hum, the sound of deep water being deep water, occasionally broken by:

- the percussive click and clatter of something testing a wall with its body
- a soft collapse, somewhere distant, as erosion claims another roof, another threshold
- the long groan of settling stone, infrequent, geological in its patience
- nothing else, for long stretches — quiet enough that your own pulse becomes a kind of trespass

## Rules of the Place

There is no light-giving sun, no fire, no device, no tool — and there will be none. Light exists only as biology: bodies that glow because glowing serves them, not because anyone asked for illumination. You go where the glow goes, or you go nowhere safely.

Erosion is the only architect still working. It does not build; it only reveals, slowly, what the original builders didn't intend — new doorways where walls thinned, new rooms where roofs gave way, an ongoing unbuilding that looks, from a certain angle, like a kind of growth.

The warmth is unusual for this depth and this darkness — a thermal generosity that shouldn't coexist with the absence of light, and the locals (the fauna, the only locals there are) have arranged their lives around this contradiction without needing to resolve it. Something below or beyond the visible warren breathes heat upward. No one asks the source. The water simply carries it, the way it carries everything else — patiently, without urgency, certain that time will do the rest.

The streets remember being streets. That is the unsettling part — not the dark, not the depth, not the slow things moving past the windows, but the persistent, structural memory of a place built for feet, for breath, for some inhabitant who is no longer here to be addressed. The water moved in and kept the shape of their absence intact, the way a held silence keeps the shape of the last word spoken before it.

You are a guest in someone else's unfinished sentence. Nothing here will tell you whose.
