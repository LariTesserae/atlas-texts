# The Condensation Works

There is a room — though "room" suggests human proportion, and this exceeds that almost immediately — built into the cold heart of something high. You can feel the altitude in your ears, in the thinness that should be there but isn't, because the air has been replaced by something denser, wetter, engineered. The walls sweat constantly. Not from heat. From the deliberate, endless labor of machines that have only one purpose: to pull water out of nothing and put it everywhere.

## Substrate

There is no ground, not really — only grating, riveted plate, catwalk, scaffold-floor, level upon level of cold iron that was clearly poured, forged, bolted into place by hands or arms or processes that intended this exact configuration and no other. Look down between your feet and you see another floor below, and machinery between, and below that another floor, descending into a dim industrial throat that the eye gives up trying to measure. Look up and the same thing happens, upward, into a ceiling-that-isn't, vanishing into pipework and gantries and the suggestion of more levels above.

Everything that could be called terrain here is actually infrastructure wearing the disguise of terrain: a slope is a drainage ramp; a ridge is a conduit housing; a outcrop is the housing of some component nobody has named in a long time. The few plants that exist — and they do exist, in pockets, sparse and strange — grow in the gasket-seams where condensation pools, pale rootless tufts clinging to bolt-heads, leafless, the color of old solder.

## The Wet

Water is everywhere but never falls as rain — there's no sky to rain from. Instead it sweats from every cold surface, beads on pipe-flanges, drips in syncopated arrhythmic patterns from a thousand points overhead, runs in threads down support struts, pools in the grating and drains away through systems clearly built to catch it, recycle it, return it to whatever process consumes it. The whole place exhales moisture the way a held breath does in cold air made visible. Frost rims certain joints where the chill is sharpest; elsewhere brass fittings have gone green-black with permanent damp. Nothing is dry. Nothing is flooded. It is held, perpetually, at the exact point of wet.

## The Fauna (overwhelming, and this is the heart of it)

They are everywhere. In the pipes. On the catwalks. Roosting in the gantry-joints, clustered along warm conduits, perched in rows on railings that were not designed to hold them but now exist, it seems, primarily to hold them. Pale, many-limbed, vaguely birdlike or batlike or neither — built creatures, in a built place, that seem less evolved than installed, as if they came with the architecture, original equipment never removed. Their numbers press against the available space; every surface holds something breathing, twitching, watching, calling.

And the calling — this is where the deafening lives. A continuous overlapping shriek-click-thrum, thousands of voices at every pitch, ricocheting off iron walls that were never built to absorb sound, only to reflect it, layer it, return it amplified. Underneath the animal noise: the engines, never resting, a bass drone so constant it has stopped being heard as sound and become instead a kind of pressure on the chest. Combined, the effect is total — a wall of noise with no silence anywhere inside it, not even a pocket, not even a held breath's worth.

## Light

Dim, amber-grey, the color of a bulb behind grime — sourced from caged industrial lamps spaced along the gantries, supplemented by a chemical-green glow leaking from vents whose purpose is unclear. No daylight reaches here; there may be no "outside" in any sense that matters. The dimness makes the density worse — you sense more creatures than you see, hear far more than the light reveals, catch the edge-glint of an eye or a wet wing only when it passes near a lamp.

## The Strange

Nothing here resembles a known ecology assembled by known rules. The creatures seem to feed on something the machines produce — condensate, runoff, a mineral residue that crusts certain pipes in crystalline blooms — but the relationship feels inverted, as though the machines were built to sustain the animals rather than the reverse, an industrial ecosystem with its causality backwards. Certain pipes hum at a frequency the creatures answer to, in unison, briefly, before resuming their chaos. There are chutes that lead nowhere visible. There are levels that, climbed, do not connect to the levels seen from below, as if the structure's interior exceeds its exterior. Nothing about this announces itself as wrong, exactly. It only resists being fully understood, the way a working system resists explanation from someone who didn't build it.

## The Stillness Beneath the Roar

Despite all of it — the noise, the press of bodies, the ceaseless drip and hiss — nothing changes. This is not a place becoming something else. The engines have always run at this pitch. The creatures have always roosted in these exact densities. The water has always wept at this exact rate, no more, no less. It is a frozen maximum: total saturation of sound, fauna, and built form, locked into permanent equilibrium, as though some original designer tuned every variable to its ceiling and then welded the dials in place. There is no future tense here. There is only the eternal present of the machine at full, sustained capacity — cold, wet, deafening, inhabited beyond reasonable limit, and entirely, permanently itself.
