# The Hollow of the Standing Coals

There is no sky here, only ceiling, and the ceiling is so far up that it generates its own weather. Warm air rises off the floor in slow, unhurried columns and meets the cold stone above, and somewhere in that vast dark interval, clouds form. They do not rain — there is nothing here that condenses into water — but they drift, visible only as a deeper blackness moving against black, occasionally lit from within by something that is not lightning but resembles it: a dull mineral phosphorescence that flickers between particles of suspended dust and goes out again, like a thought someone almost finished having.

The scale defies the word "cave." This is a country underground, a continent folded into stone, walled on all sides by rock that has never seen the surface and may not believe in it. The floor is young — fractured basalt and raw mineral terrace, edges still sharp enough to draw blood, ridged with cooling-cracks that haven't yet been worn into anything gentle. It glows, faintly, in places: not light exactly, more a remembering of heat, deep red-orange seams threading through grey-black rock like veins still deciding whether to be alive. These are the Standing Coals — fissures where the warmth of the world's interior leaks upward, never flaring, never dying, holding at the same patient simmer they have held for longer than any creature here has had ancestors to ask why.

There is no vegetation, not even the pretense of it. No lichen, no fungal mat, nothing photosynthetic or its analogue. And yet the place is *dense* — thick with presence, layered top to bottom, a teeming hollow world that gets its food another way. Heat is the currency here. Mineral seep is the soil. Whatever passes for the bottom of this food chain feeds on chemistry leaching from the coal-seams, and everything above that feeds on it in turn, in a long quiet architecture of consumption that never needs sunlight because sunlight was never invented as a rule in this place.

## Population

The fauna is abundant almost to the point of crowding, though you would not know it from the silence. Things move *near* each other constantly — brushing distance, breathing distance — without touching, the way fish school without colliding. Most are blind, or might as well be; eyes here are vestigial or repurposed into heat-sensing organs, broad flat plates of skin that read the world in degrees rather than colors. Listen and you will hear them: the click and drag of carapace on stone, a great slow herd-shuffle through the dark, the occasional low percussive thrum that might be a call or might be a heartbeat amplified by an empty chamber. Mostly, though, it is quiet — a held quiet, a library-quiet, as though something here has agreed that the warmth must not be wasted on noise.

They are strange to look at, in the rare moments enough coal-light catches them. Many-legged, low and broad, built for stability over speed, since there is nowhere to flee to and nothing here has needed to run in longer than memory holds. Some carry small clusters of luminescent growth on their backs that may be parasite, may be symbiote, may be both — drifting blue-green pinpricks in herds that from a distance, across the vastness of the cavern, look uncannily like constellations grazing.

## The Scattered Hands

And there are people, or something with hands. Not many, and not gathered — single dwellings pressed into alcoves along the warm seams, a ring of stacked stone here, a crude wind-break (against air currents, not wind) there, fifty kilometers of nothing and then another. Their tools are simple: chipped mineral blades, woven... something, fire-hardened (though fire is precious and strange to make in a world with little to burn — perhaps they coax it from gas-pockets, or perhaps "fire" here means something else, a chemical bloom they've learned to provoke from the coal-seams themselves). They herd, perhaps, or follow, moving with the great unseen migrations of the blind grazers, never building anything that wouldn't fit in two hands' carrying, because nothing here ever changes enough to reward permanence.

This is the place's deepest law: nothing changes. The coal-seams have always glowed at this exact heat. The blind herds have always moved this same slow circuit. The clouds form and dissolve in the high dark in a pattern that, if you watched it for ten thousand years, would not drift. It is not dead, and it is not static in the sense of frozen — it breathes, it moves, it is warm and crowded and strange — but it is *settled*, the way a held note is settled, the way an old argument both sides have stopped having is settled. Equilibrium so total it has become a kind of peace.

## What You Notice, Standing There

The heat first — a wet-less warmth, dry heat with nowhere to go, pressing on the skin like a hand that means no harm. Then the dark, which is not empty dark but *occupied* dark, full of weight and movement just outside the reach of any light you might carry. Then, if you stay long enough for your ears to recalibrate, the sound: not silence but a vast attenuated murmur, thousands of small sounds spread across a scale too big to localize any of them, blurring into something like the inside of a shell.

And then, if you stay longer still, you notice the coal-seams aren't random. They run in long unbroken lines across kilometers of floor, branching at consistent angles, like a circuit, like a root system that was never attached to anything that grew. No one — not the things with hands, not the things with neither hands nor eyes — knows whether the warmth was placed here on purpose, by the world, by something else, or whether "purpose" is simply the wrong question to bring into a place this old, this stable, this finished.
