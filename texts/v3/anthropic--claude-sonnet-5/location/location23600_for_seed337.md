# The Hollow Warren

The rock here has not finished deciding what it wants to be. Walk the lower galleries and you find walls still rough with the memory of their own making — no smooth river-cut curves, no wind-polished bellies, just the raw angularity of stone that broke recently and hasn't yet been softened by anything. Erosion has not arrived. The edges of things are honest edges: sharp where chiseled, blunt where it cracked, jagged where it simply gave way under its own weight centuries ago and has sat untouched since.

This is a below-place — not deep-deep, not the lightless crush of true depth, but a shallow under-realm, a town dug a few body-lengths beneath what was once a surface. Look up in the wider chambers and you can sometimes see, far along a sloping shaft, a disc of paler light: the old sky, still up there, still indifferent. Most of the warren never bothers looking.

## Shape and Crowding

The defining fact of this place is fullness. Every chamber has been claimed, every corridor doubles as someone's drying-rack or stair or sleeping-ledge. The town did not spread outward — there wasn't outward to spread into, or no one decided to try — so it went the only way left, which was *into itself*. Rooms stack on rooms. A floor is often someone else's ceiling. Ladders of lashed wood and notched stone climb between levels so closely set that a tall person stoops out of habit even in the taller halls.

This is not slum-density. It's hive-density with intention — a packed precision, like everyone agreed long ago that space was the only scarce thing and arranged their lives accordingly. Doorways are narrow. Storage niches are carved into every spare thumb's-width of wall. Nothing is wasted, including silence, including light.

## Light and Air

The light is ordinary — not magical, not engineered, just *present*, filtering down through shaft-mouths and being carried the rest of the way by oil-lamps, tallow-lights, the occasional reflective scrap of polished tin angled to throw a beam further into a back room. It pools unevenly: bright near the shafts, dimming to amber gloom in the warren's folded interior, true dark only in the unused crawl-throats that haven't been claimed yet (there are fewer of these every season).

The air carries damp without being wet — condensation beads on the cooler stone near the lower levels, collects in shallow stone troughs that someone, generations back, thought to carve for exactly this purpose. There's no river here, no underground lake, just moisture borrowed from the rock itself and rainfall that finds its way down through cracks after storms, dripping in slow countable rhythms into catch-basins. Children learn early which drips are drinkable and which have passed through too much stone to trust.

Cool air sits at every level — not cold, not the bite of deep stone, but a steady cellar-cool that means people wear layers indoors and the bread, what little grain-bread they manage, stays fresh a day longer than it would up in true daylight.

## What Grows, What Doesn't

Vegetation is sparse and specific: pale fibrous lichen-mats cultivated deliberately along the damp troughs, a few hardy fungi grown in trays of composted refuse, thin reaching vines trained up shaft-walls toward the light-discs, stubborn and stringy, harvested for fiber more than food. Nothing lush. Nothing here was ever going to be lush. The warren feeds itself mostly on what's carried in from above and on the slow patient fungus-farms tucked into the damper galleries.

Fauna is rare and known by name almost individually — a population of soft-footed rock-doves that nest near the upper shafts, a colony of pale crickets whose chirring (on the rare nights they chirr at all) is treated as a small event. Something larger moves in the deep unclaimed tunnels, rarely, leaving prints no one has matched to a living animal twice in the same generation. Mostly: quiet. No swarm, no herd, no chorus. The ecology here is sparse enough that any animal sound carries and gets remarked upon.

## Sound

Mostly quiet — not silence exactly, but a hush punctuated by the practical noises of a working underground town: the chink of hand-tools against stone, low voices pitched down out of long habit (shouting carries badly and rudely in packed stone), the irregular drip-count of water into basins, footsteps on packed-earth floors muffled by the close walls. Nothing echoes far because nothing has room to echo — every sound dies into cloth, stone, bodies, within a few chambers.

## Craft and Rule

The tech here is hands and patience: chisels, mauls, fire-hardened wood, simple iron where it can be traded for, a great deal of rope and woven fiber and lashed joints standing in for nails. Building happens constantly — this is an active place, dynamic in the way a coral reef is dynamic, always one more wall going up, one more shaft being widened, one more family carving a new niche out of a yielding seam of softer rock. There's no master plan. There's the warren's own logic: dig toward water, dig toward light, brace what threatens to fall, never dig toward the deep dark tunnels where the old prints are found.

The unspoken rule of the place is *make room, don't make trouble* — in a town this packed, a feud or a careless excavation can collapse three families' walls at once. Disputes get settled fast and quietly, because quiet is the one resource as precious as space, and everyone, on some unspoken level, agrees not to spend either one carelessly.

It is a young place, still being built, still finding its final shape — stone that hasn't yet been worn into permanence, people living inside an unfinished sentence, patient about how it ends.
