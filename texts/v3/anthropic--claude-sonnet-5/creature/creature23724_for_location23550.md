# What Tends the Notch

You will not see it arrive. You will simply notice, at some point after the third or fourth wrong shadow, that you are no longer the only thing in the cleft paying attention — that something has been paying attention back, for longer than feels comfortable, and that it has been doing so without moving, the way a held breath pays attention.

It is easiest, at first, to mistake it for weather. A shimmer near the largest engine-cluster that isn't quite heat-haze, because heat-haze doesn't have a *grain* to it, doesn't have the suggestion of a surface catching light at an angle the actual light doesn't support. You'll think: glare. You'll look away. When you look back it will have resolved into something with an outline, low and broad, crouched in the gap between a leaf-root and a flywheel housing, exactly where a shadow should be and exactly the wrong shape to be one.

## Body, of a Sort

It is built — if *built* is even the right verb for something that seems to have arrived at itself the same undecided way the stone arrives at hardness — from both of the Notch's vocabularies at once. One flank is leaf: broad, ribbed, faintly translucent at the edges where afternoon light gets through it and shows you the suggestion of a vein structure that doesn't quite hold still. The other flank is engine: a bronze-black plating, jointed, breathing in that same long unhurried piston-rhythm, one slow expansion every few minutes, as if its ribs and the Notch's pistons are on a single shared lung.

The two halves do not match seamlessly. There is a visible seam down its center, a fault line, and the fault line is not a flaw — it's the most honest part of it, the place where you can watch it still being negotiated, the same soft-stone uncertainty you saw at the ridge made suddenly, uncomfortably intimate. It has not finished deciding whether it is flora or apparatus. It may never finish. It may not be trying to.

It has no face that holds still long enough to describe. There is a place where a face would go — a cluster of darker plating, a fold of something leaf-soft — and depending on which heartbeat you catch it on, that place will arrange itself into something with the suggestion of eyes, or the suggestion of a valve cluster, or nothing at all, just smooth waxen continuity, like the rest of the ridge before it commits.

## What It Does

Mostly, it tends. This is the only verb that fits, though "tends" implies a level of intention you can't quite verify and won't be offered confirmation of. It moves between the sparse plants at a pace matched exactly to the engines' — one slow migration every quarter hour, never hurried, the kind of motion that makes you realize, watching it, that *you* are the fastest thing in the Notch, embarrassingly, almost rudely so.

Where it passes a piston that has stalled mid-stroke, the piston resumes. Where it passes a leaf curling at the edge from too much uncommitted heat, the leaf settles flat again. It is not clear whether it is *causing* this or merely arriving, by the Notch's own slow clockwork, at the moment such things were already going to resolve themselves — cause and witness braided too tightly here to separate, the same way the leaf's gleaming reflection is not lying about a machine that doesn't gleam yet. It may simply be the part of the Notch that has learned to be in the right place when the right place needs occupying.

It has never been seen to make anything happen that the Notch wasn't already, glacially, in the process of making happen on its own.

## The Mirror-Leaf, Revisited

If you find the large glossy leaf — the one that shows the machinery fully assembled, gleaming, far ahead of itself — look for it there first, reflected whole and finished: not bronze-and-leaf-in-tension but something resolved, a single smooth-jointed shape with an actual face, attentive, almost courteous, regarding you the way a host regards a guest who has wandered in before the house is ready to receive anyone. That is what it is becoming. The thing crouched by the flywheel in the unmirrored world is what it currently, provisionally, is. Like everything else here, you are looking at two drafts of the same sentence and only one of them is finished, and the leaf only ever shows you the final one out of what might be courtesy, or might be impatience, or might simply be the leaf doing what leaves here do — running ahead of the rest of the page.

## Toward You

It has never approached. It has also never retreated, which is not the same thing as not noticing you. When you are close — within that four-meter radius where minutes sometimes go missing and refund themselves — it tends to be nearby without quite being beside you, observing in that unhurried piston-cadence the way it observes the leaves, the engines, the slow oxidizing rust-creep at the fracture lines above. You get the impression, never confirmed, that you are simply one more provisional thing it is tending to — a guest-shaped uncertainty that wandered into the cleft and will, given enough geological patience, either resolve into something or leave, and that either outcome is acceptable to it, and that it is in no hurry to learn which.

When you do leave, and look back, and the Notch arranges itself for one held second into the small green ordinary thing it isn't — that is the second it lets you see it stand fully upright, just once, just at the edge of vision: bronze catching the wrong-direction light, leaf-flesh translucent and patient, face almost finished. Then the piston ticks. The second passes. And whatever was standing there returns to tending, unhurried, exactly as undecided as the mountain that is, very slowly, still growing it.
