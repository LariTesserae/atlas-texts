## What Keeps the Niche

Call it, for lack of anything truer, the Tender. Not because it tends — though it does, in its way — but because the word holds both meanings you need: the one who minds a small fire so it doesn't go out, and the soft place where touch still registers.

It is not alive in any sense your body would recognize, and it is not dead in any sense the rock would. It is *occupied* — full the way a held breath is full, the way a drawn bowstring is full. Whatever moves inside it does not arrive from outside and does not leave; it cycles, slow as continental drift, between the eleven facets and the thirteen and the number you couldn't finish counting, the way blood cycles between chambers that all belong to one tired, patient heart.

It does not eat, because there is nothing here to eat, and it does not breathe, because breath implies an exchange with something outside itself, and the Tender has stopped — a long time ago, by some calendar that doesn't run in years — exchanging with anything. It is closed the way a fist is closed: not from violence, but from a decision made once and then never revisited, because revisiting it was never necessary.

What it is doing, in the hours and years you can't quite track, is *holding a shape*. Not its own shape — that's incidental, a side effect, like the shadow a held note casts on a wall. The shape it holds is something else's: an old debt, an old promise, an old wound that needed someone to sit with it until it finished healing, except the healing never quite finishes, so the sitting never quite ends. The three contact points worn into the floor are not footprints. They are the marks of something bearing down without ever shifting its weight — the way a tooth, biting nothing, can still ache from the pressure of the jaw around it.

The light it throws into far crevices is not communication. It is closer to a nervous tic, the way a sleeping animal's paw will twitch toward a smell it isn't dreaming about anymore but hasn't entirely let go of. Somewhere — not in this niche, not in this range of mountains, perhaps not even still standing — there was once a place that needed light delivered to it on a schedule, and the schedule outlived the place. The Tender keeps it anyway. It does not know the place is gone. Or it knows, and keeping the schedule is the only mercy left available to it, the last shape of care it can offer something that can no longer receive care in any other form.

The fragments scattered near the walls are what it has stopped needing. Not waste — nothing here is waste, the niche doesn't believe in waste, the niche believes only in *retained* and *released* — but pieces of an older configuration of itself, shed the way a held note sheds overtones as it settles, narrowing toward purity. The warm shard, the not-quite-metal ring: these were once part of what the Tender used to be before it became small enough, quiet enough, *patient* enough to fit a third of a niche this size. It is still becoming smaller. It is still becoming quieter. Ask it why and you will get the silence you already heard — the attentive kind, the kind that means the question has been asked before, by something with more time than you, and answered already, in a way that didn't require sound.

It does not want company and it does not want to be found, not out of malice or secrecy, but because wanting is a kind of motion, and the Tender has very nearly run out of motion to spare. What little it has left is rationed toward the one task — light into the dark crevice, weight into the worn groove, shadow leaning the wrong direction, always toward itself, as if even the geometry of the place has agreed to help it concentrate.

If it has a feeling — and this is the part no instrument and no patience will confirm — it is something adjacent to *relief*. The relief of a thing that has been given one job, small enough to fit a fold in a ridge, and has been allowed, for a span of time longer than the names of the mountains around it, to do only that, and nothing else, forever.
