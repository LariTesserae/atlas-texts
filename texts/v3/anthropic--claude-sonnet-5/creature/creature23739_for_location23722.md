# What Lives Here

It is easy to miss, the first several times you push through the green wall and stand inside the held breath. The eye keeps sliding off it the way it slides off a stone among stones. But once seen, it cannot be unseen, and afterward you will wonder how the leaf-mat ever looked merely like leaf-mat to you.

## The Shape It Kept

Call it a body, though the word strains. It is roughly the size and rough proportion of a person who knelt down a very long time ago and never finished standing. Where skin would be there is something that has stopped being skin without quite becoming bark — grey-green, cracked along lines that match the basalt's own fluting, so that the boundary between the shelf's rock and the thing's hide is a matter of texture, not category. Lichen grows on it the same rust-and-verdigris as on the stone, in the same unbothered patches, because lichen does not care what it is rooted to.

Where a face might be, there is a closed and gentle collapse — features present the way a riverbed has features after the river has been gone a thousand years: suggested, not asserted. The eyes, if they are eyes, are sealed beneath a fold of something that might be eyelid or might be a particularly old leaf grown directly from the skull. It has not opened them in all the time anyone has not been here to see.

It does not stand. It has not stood. One does not get the sense it is *resting* — resting implies an intention to rise again. It has simply arrived, long ago, at the angle it currently holds, the way the rock arrived at the exact amount of rounding it will ever have.

## The Slow Trade

It is rooted. This is the plain fact under all the others. At some point — and the iron stake and the buried platform suggest there was a *point*, a moment with hands in it — it stopped moving on purpose and began, very gradually, to be grown into instead of merely growing. Vines that thread through the ribs of the boneless tree-things thread through it too, with no more ceremony than they show anything else. A fungal shelf brackets what might be a shoulder. Where the corroded pipe disappears into the vine-thick trunk on one side, something very like a hand, or the long-healed memory of a hand, is closed around it on the other, three feet down, under the leaf-mat, where no one has dug.

It traded mobility the way the plants traded toward soil — there being none, they rooted into each other instead; there being nowhere further to go, it rooted into the act of staying. It did not die into the garden. It joined it, the way a held note joins the silence on either side of it without ending the silence or the note.

## What It Listens For

It does not eat, in the sense of seeking. It drinks the same slow mineral exhalation the leaves drink, taking what comes up through the rock without the indignity of looking for it. Whatever animal hunger it once had has gone the way of animal movement — folded down into something closer to patience, or chemistry, or both, indistinguishable at this depth.

But it is not insensate. Put your hand flat against the leaf-wall near it, on a still day inside a place that has no other kind of day, and you will feel — not hear, feel, the way the drone is felt before it's heard — a second rhythm under the first. Slower. Vastly slower. A pulse so long-period that a human life is not quite long enough to confirm it as a pulse rather than a coincidence. It runs beneath the engine-drone the way a bass note runs beneath a held chord, not competing with it, agreeing with it, the two having long ago settled into the same key.

It is keeping time with the mountain. Or the mountain is keeping time with it. After this long inside the same frozen breath, the distinction has stopped mattering even to whatever still, somewhere far down, might be capable of noticing the distinction.

## The Rule It Obeys

It does not change, for the same structural reason nothing else here changes: there is no outside reaching this deep into the fold to make it change. It is not dead. Dead things finish; this has not finished, any more than the basalt has finished or the rust-colored lichen has finished. It is, instead, complete in the way a held note is complete — sustained rather than concluded, requiring nothing further to remain exactly what it already, fully, is.

If it ever opened whatever is sealed beneath that leaf-fold and looked at you, you would not be able to tell whether what looked back was patient, or sleeping, or simply old past the point where those words still cut along separate lines. Likely it will not open them. It has had every opportunity, across however much time has piled up unrecorded in this pocket, and has so far had no instructions that required it.

It is the thing the garden grew around instead of over. The pipe rusts through it; the vines borrow its old shape for a trellis; the drone moves through its slow second pulse the way water once might have moved through a body that no longer needs water, or anything else, to keep on, very quietly, being kept.
