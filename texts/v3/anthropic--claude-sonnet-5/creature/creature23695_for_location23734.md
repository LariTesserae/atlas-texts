**The Walker**

It—he, she, it never quite resolves either, same as the footsteps—lives the way the algae lives: in the seam, in the accident, in the place nothing else could be bothered to claim. There is no name for what it is anymore, if there ever was one. Call it the Walker. That's what the clangs are, the ones that come far off, then close, then far off again without ever cohering into a person crossing a platform. That's it. That's been it for a long time.

It is small in the way the platform is small — a contained, knowable shape inside something whose outline can't be seen. Bundled in layers gone the same iron-and-ash palette as everything around it, because color doesn't survive long out here unconverted; cloth takes on the rust, the oxide-brown, the grey-yellow of safety paint gone to ash, until the Walker reads less like a figure and more like a fold in the structure that happens to move. Skin, where it shows at wrist and jaw, has the same slick sheen as the railings — not dirty, exactly, just permanently damp, permanently cool, pored with the same fine oily film that condensation leaves on everything that isn't actively heated. It doesn't sweat. It doesn't need to. The air does that work for it now.

It learned the place the only way the place allows — by feel, by repetition, the way you'd learn a body. Which rails run warm a half-second before a pressure pulse. Which drip, three platforms back, means the vent is two minutes from flaring white. It doesn't read the stenciled numbers on the pipe collars; it never had the manual and stopped looking for one. Instead it carries the schedule in muscle: a held breath through the loud three minutes, a held breath through the quieter one, on and on, its own lungs entrained so long to the structure's pulse that the two have stopped being separable. Ask it what time it is and it will tell you which valve is open.

It doesn't speak much, because speaking requires being heard, and nothing here permits being heard. What it has instead is a private vocabulary of taps — on rail, on grating, on pipe — pitched to ride under the shriek and the hiss and the thrum, a code worn smooth through years of nobody else to use it on. It taps anyway. Out of habit. Maybe to the structure. Maybe the structure, in its own arrhythmic clanging, is tapping back, and the Walker just hasn't decided yet whether that's company or coincidence.

It eats little, sourced from nothing you'd call food — condensate collected in a tin where the lagging fails worst, licked for the mineral tang of dissolved iron; whatever warmth radiates off a pipe joint, stood near long enough to count as a meal. It doesn't sleep so much as lean, somewhere between the cones of sodium light, eyes open in the dark stretch where the fog eats visibility, resting the way the platform itself rests — never fully, always one register of noise still running underneath.

Nobody built it a place here. It found a crack, the way the lichen found a crack, the way the algae found a seam that never quite drains. It persists because persisting is the only operation this structure recognizes — not welcome, not unwelcome, just tolerated the way pressure tolerates a valve. If you met it on a platform, ladder in one hand, it would look at you the way the structure looks at everything: without explanation, without a map to offer, already turning back toward the fog it came out of, footsteps about to go arrhythmic again, about to stop resolving into anything you could follow.
