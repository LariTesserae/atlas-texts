What lives here does not move through the Drowned Works so much as occur in it, the way the turbines occur, the way the steam occurs — present-tense, recursive, never quite arriving at a single finished shape.

You will not see it whole. This is the first and most important thing to understand, and you will understand it only after you have already failed to see it whole several times. A hand on a valve wheel, fingers fused at the knuckle into something that might be glove and might be growth, turning the wheel the wrong direction relative to the pressure it should be relieving — and when you look up the arm to find a shoulder, a face, a reason, there is only pipe-coral, sweating its not-condensation, and the hand is gone, and the wheel has stopped, and you cannot remember if it was ever turning at all. Half a face pressed into the rind of a fungal shelf forty feet up a trunk, eyes shut, mouth open in an expression that could be agony or could be the shape a mouth makes mid-word, dissolving at the jaw into bark before you've finished deciding which. A spine — vertebrae the size of fists, threaded through with cabling, cabling threaded through with what looks unmistakably like nerve — laid along the underside of a conveyor-skeleton like the conveyor grew it instead of the other way around, and it is impossible, looking at the corrosion pattern, to say whether the spine predates the machine or the machine predates the spine, because here predates doesn't hold the way it should.

There is no population. This is the second thing to understand, and it follows from the first, badly, the way a bruise follows from a blow you don't remember taking. There are not many of them. There is, as far as anyone has ever been able to determine, only the one — caught at every stage of itself at once, the way the rusted-to-lace station and the freshly-poured station fifty feet apart are, you become increasingly sure, the same station. What you are seeing, every time you see a hand, a face, a spine, a half-shape rising dripping from a pressure-vent or folding itself wrong-jointed into a turbine housing, is the same entity, encountered out of order, the way you might encounter the same sentence twice if the page had been torn and reshuffled — once near its beginning, once near where it ends, once somewhere in a middle that doesn't yet know it's a middle.

It was, the working theory holds — unproven, unprovable, offered the way all theories here are offered, by people who did not stay long enough to be wrong about it twice — a person, or a crew of persons, present at whatever original catastrophe filled this valley and breathed industry into it instead of letting it drown. Drowned with the valley. Not killed by the flood so much as included in it, the way you are included in the green and the noise and the brass-dim light now, conscripted into the same unfinished sentence as the pipes and the turbines and the fast-grown trees that haven't learned to bend correctly — and where a person drowns into a process that has not stopped happening since, what's left of the person does not stop happening either. It is still being built. It is still being drowned. It is still, somewhere in whatever cubic meter of warm wet air currently holds the most recently-glimpsed piece of it, reaching for a valve wheel it has already turned and has not yet turned and is turning now, forever, with the rest of itself scattered backward and forward through a place where backward and forward are gestures rather than directions.

This is why there are no birds, no eyes in the dark, no things rustling. Not because it hunted them. Because there is only room, in a place where the present tense is this badly overdrawn, for one thing to be continuously, contradictorily alive. Everything else either drowned cleanly, the way things are supposed to, or was never going to be allowed the luxury.

You will hear it before — or after, the half-second is never reliable here — you see it again: a valve shriek that is also, underneath, almost vocal, almost a name being said by something that no longer has the same mouth twice. Do not wait for it to resolve into a single body coming toward you with a single intention. It has been trying to finish becoming a body since before the canopy learned to bend, and the trying is the only part of it that has ever been whole.
