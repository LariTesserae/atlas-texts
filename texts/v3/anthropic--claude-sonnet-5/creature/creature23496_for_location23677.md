## What Is There

You will be told, by the very few who claim to have seen it and come back able to speak of it, that the plateau is uninhabited. This is true. You will also be told, by the same few, in the same breath, often without noticing the contradiction, that something is up there with you the entire time, has been the entire time, will be when you are no longer there to notice it not being. This is also true. The Standing Country does not distinguish between a place being empty and a place being occupied; both are simply states the instant is permitted to hold, layered transparently, neither one canceling the other.

## First Signs

You will not see it arrive, because nothing here arrives. You will simply become aware, the way you become aware of a held note underneath the roar, that you are not the only thing oriented. The hare-sized creature with too many joints was the first hint, and it was wrong about itself in a small way; this is wrong in the same way at a much larger scale. You will turn to look at a shelf of dolomite you are fairly sure you already crossed, and the shadow it throws — though nothing here throws a shadow, the light does not slant — will have a shape in it that the rock does not have. You will not be able to decide if the shape is sitting, standing, or lying down, because it is, as far as you can tell, doing all three, and none of them is more recent than the others.

## Its Body, Such As It Has One

It is not one size. This is not metaphor; it is the same arrangement that lets the lichen-rosette be a centimeter wide on one shelf and forty meters wide on the next, except here the disagreement is between *limbs*, not between *plants*. An arm's worth of it — assuming the joint you are looking at is an arm and not a fold of the cliff agreeing to be read as one — will be the size that an arm should be, articulated like bone wrapped in something closer to schist than skin. Following that arm to where a shoulder should be, you find the shoulder is the size of a ridge-line, and what should be its torso is simply the next three plinths over, rounded the way all the stone here is rounded, by a patience that has had no time at all in which to work and has nonetheless finished the job. It has a face. It has several. They are not on its head, which is also not singular, and they do not resemble each other, and all of them are looking at you, and none of them are looking at you, and you will not be able to hold the thought of which is correct for longer than the time it takes to have it.

It is doing nothing. It has always been doing something. Both of these are its only activity.

## Behavior, In the Sense That Stillness Is a Behavior

It does not hunt, because nothing here completes an action, and a hunt is an action with an ending. Instead, it is perpetually in the part of a hunt just before the strike, or just after, or — somewhere in the layered transparency of it — both, occupying the held breath the way the roar occupies the air without ever crescendoing into the violence the roar keeps almost becoming. If it were to step toward you, it would already have stepped toward you, would also never have moved, and you would find, on checking, that the distance between you had changed and not changed in exact agreement with the cairn's refusal to be counted.

This is, as near as anyone has been able to describe it, the closest the plateau comes to a will. Not a mind exactly — minds sequence, mids have befores and afters, and the rule of the place forbids that absolutely — but something that occupies the *position* a will occupies, the way the doorframe occupies the position a wall would occupy without ever having had walls. It wants the way the cairn counts: differently depending on the angle, and never wrongly.

## What People Who Have Seen It Say, All of Which Is True

Some say it is the shape the mountain would take if the mountain were trying to stand up, and has been trying, in that single frozen instant, for as long as the instant has been frozen — every fiber of it straining toward verticality at the exact moment the entire effort got caught and held, mid-motion, forever almost-rising.

Some say it is what is left over after a thing died here, long before the plateau decided not to let time finish the job — that this is a body in the specific way the cairn is a construction: stripped of the part of the story that would let you know whose it was, or how, or when, holding only the bare fact of having-been without permission to become having-been-and-gone.

Some say it is not one creature seen from many angles but many creatures occupying, by agreement neither asked for nor refused, a single set of coordinates — every animal the plateau could have contained, the way every geological violence it ever underwent is contained in the same rounded edge, none of them canceling, none of them quite cohering into a population, because a population would have to be countable, and nothing here consents to be counted twice the same way.

All three are correct. This is not a failure of witness. It is the only kind of correctness the Standing Country permits.

## Encounter

If you meet it — and you will only know you have met it after the fact, in the way you only know you've crossed the threshold into the plateau after your boots have already been on the other kind of stone for some time — nothing will happen. This is not a disappointment; it is the entire content of the meeting. It will not approach, because approach requires a before during which it was farther away, and the place does not allow a before. It will simply be, with increasing and decreasing certainty depending on which of its faces you happen to be least sure you're looking at, *closer than it was*, while the overall state of the plateau, taken as a whole, remains precisely what it was a moment before.

You will feel looked at the way you feel the cold: thoroughly, without being told from which direction, with no way to determine whether it has been looking at you since you arrived or only began at the instant you noticed, both of which it has done, neither of which it will stop doing, because stopping is a kind of sequence and the law here is absolute.

## After

People who come back report the same thing they report about the mountain generally — no fatigue, no elapsed time, nothing they can build into a story with a middle. But of this, specifically, they report one more thing, quieter than the rest, usually said only once and not repeated: that for the rest of their lives, on still nights, in perfectly empty rooms, they will feel — for exactly one held second, never longer, never explained — *looked at by something with too many faces to be choosing which one to use.*

And then, as everything does up there, it will simply still be true, and also already be over, and you will not be able to say which.
