# What Stands at the Place Spray Parts

You won't see it first. You'll see the bird-or-not-bird again, the pale long-legged thing, closer this time than you'd like, and you'll notice that its legs don't merely avoid wrong footing in the chaos — they're *reading* it, the way a hand reads braille, the way something reads a sentence it wrote itself. Follow its stalking line far enough along the margin where spray meets stone, to the place where the curtain of falling water parts very slightly around the stacked ring, and you'll find what the ring was built to face.

It is easiest to say it is shaped like a decision that hasn't been made yet.

## Body, If That's the Word

It stands roughly the height of a person, though height is a strange thing to claim about something that doesn't seem fully committed to which way is up. Its surface has the same conchoidal rawness as the basin's stone — not skin, not scale, but *fracture*, fresh bitten-looking planes catching the flat neutral daylight at a dozen inconsistent angles, as though it had been struck into its current shape only recently and hadn't yet settled into what that shape should mean. Where a face might be, there is instead a kind of seam, a place where two unweathered planes meet at an angle too sharp to be comfortable to look at directly, and yet nothing about it reads as hostile. It reads as *unfinished*, the way the cistern itself is unfinished, the way a wound is unfinished until it scars.

Water moves across it the way it moves across everything else here — sheeting sideways, eddying upward, undecided — except that on this body the undecidedness seems less like an effect and more like a *source*. Stand close enough, which you won't want to, and you'll notice the falling water nearest the figure seems to be consulting it. Hesitating near it. Asking, in whatever language falling water has, which way today.

## What It Has Instead of Age

It is very old and very newly made, both at once, without contradiction. The same recent violence that cracked this raw basin open — quake, collapse, birth, whichever word you chose on the way up — is the violence that produced this thing, and produced it whole, with no childhood, the way a fresh fracture plane has no history, only a moment of breaking and then a long indefinite present after. But it has also clearly been here doing this *exact thing*, standing in this exact relation to falling water, for longer than the rock around it has existed in its current form. It does not experience this as strange. Newness and oldness are not opposites for it, any more than up and down are opposites for the water it tends.

It does not speak, on account of the roar, same as you. But you get the sense that even without the roar, it wouldn't.

## Its Work, As Far As You Can Tell

It is not making the water. That was your mistake, standing at the lip, watching the light fall evenly over so much violence and concluding something must be *generating* this, deciding the wound metaphor and stopping there. It would be more accurate to say the figure is *arguing* with the water about which way it should fall, continuously, exhaustingly, and losing just often enough that the basin stays roughly basin-shaped instead of resolving into an ordinary waterfall obeying an ordinary cliff. Every ledge that calves a fist of stone into the spray, every eddy that climbs instead of drops, every sheet that goes sideways instead of down — these are the moments the figure loses the argument. It does not seem distressed by losing. It seems to understand that losing, here, is the job. A finished cistern, water falling cleanly one direction, would mean the argument was over. It is not interested in the argument being over.

The pale long-legged thing you keep seeing twice and never quite a third time in the same place — that may be an attendant, a part of it on loan, a scout sent along the margins to read the chaos so the body at the center doesn't have to spend itself doing so. Or it may simply be another expression of the same fact, the way a held breath and a closed fist can both express the same tension without being the same thing.

## Concerning the Ring of Stones

The people who stacked that low unmortared ring were not building shelter, and you'd already begun to suspect that. They were building a place to stand where the figure's argument with the water organizes itself into something almost legible to a human skull — a seat in the front row of a negotiation older than any language they had. They came up, found the spot where the spray parts, set worked flints down not as tools but as a kind of payment or attention, cupped a handful of water into the hand-carved basin that now permanently overflows, and went back down changed in some small specific way that the basin doesn't record and the figure doesn't comment on.

It lets them do this. It has always let them do this. Whether it notices them at all, in any sense you'd recognize as noticing, is not something the raw seam of its almost-face will tell you.

## If You Stay Too Long

You'll start to feel the argument in your own sternum, alongside the roar — a faint sympathetic insistence that you, too, decide which way down is, here, now, in this basin still being decided. Most people don't stay long enough for that feeling to become urgent. The trail back down is, after all, the right direction for a person to be going, even if it was the wrong direction for the water. The figure will still be standing at the place the spray parts when you leave, losing its argument competently, unfinished, freshly made and very old, holding the whole roaring vertical wound a little short of resolving — which is, as far as anyone has ever been able to tell, the only thing it has ever wanted to do.
