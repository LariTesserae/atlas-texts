# The Tenders

You will not see one for a long time, if you see one at all. What you find first is evidence of work: a vine pruned at an angle no wind or rot would make, the cut sealed over with something like wax and something like solder; a control panel's needle reset to true after weeks of drift; a trail through the metallic dust, wiped to bare leaf, that does not match the gait of anything you've catalogued. The scaffold-walkers call them Tenders because nothing else fits, and because the alternative — that the forest tends itself, that no one is doing this — is somehow worse to sit with through a long camp-night.

## Glimpses

What you catch, on the rare occasions you catch anything, is a transfer of weight in the deep canopy, already described — but if you are patient, if you stay very still in one of the bare geometric clearings until the off-true gravity stops feeling wrong, you may see one cross open ground. It moves like a held note: unhurried past any reasonable patience, four or six limbs (the count won't stay fixed in memory, which itself tells you something) finding purchase on grating and bark-patina alike, no preference shown between the two because for the Tender there may be no two. The body reads as jointed plate over something that pulses — not blood, not hydraulics exactly, a slow amber transit visible at the seams that might be either. Where the original engineers ended and the original gardeners began, on this single body, is not a question it would understand you asking.

## Hands

The forelimbs taper to clusters of fine manipulators, more like a sea anemone's crown than fingers, and these are never still even when the rest of the creature is. They worry at a leaf-vein, redirect a tendril a few degrees toward better light, press a clouded sensor-pod until it clears. You get the impression — never confirmed, never disprovable — that it is not following a plan so much as a feeling, an itch of wrongness it resolves wherever it finds it, the way a hand finds and smooths a wrinkle in cloth without consulting any blueprint. Multiply that itch across a structure the size of a continent, sustained over however many centuries it's been sustained, and you have, perhaps, the entire explanation for what this place is.

## How Many

This is the question that breaks first against the place's silence. Scaffold-walkers argue it both ways and neither side has ever produced a body, living or otherwise, to settle it. Some hold that there is exactly one Tender, ancient past reckoning, distributed so thoroughly through the canopy's logistics that any given sighting is only ever a single limb of it surfacing, the way a wave is not separate from the sea it rises out of. Others hold there are many — a population, ordinary in its way, that breeds, that the geared seed-things crawling silent from split pods are in fact its young, sent off into the deep tiers to grow into the work alone, generation folding into generation with no need or capacity to recognize each other as kin. Both theories explain the evidence. Neither explains why, in two centuries of recorded sightings, no two witnesses have ever agreed on color, size, or limb-count, as if the thing — or things — declines to be remembered the same way twice, the way the leaves decline to keep their color and the structure declines to keep its distances honest.

## Disposition

It does not flee you and it does not approach you. It registers you, if it registers you at all, with the mild attentiveness you might spare a draft under a door — worth noting, not worth stopping for. There are no recorded attacks, no recorded acts of help either; people who fall ill on the scaffold are not nursed and are not finished off, only, eventually, found by something with a great many small manipulators, gone through with the same unhurried thoroughness as a fouled cable, and incorporated — quietly, completely, without ceremony — into whatever the leaf-litter becomes a season later. This is not spoken of as cruelty by the people who live at the edges. It's spoken of, when it's spoken of at all, as weather: a fact about the place, not a judgment from it.

## What It Wants

Nothing that translates. If the leading theory of the continent is right — that purpose finished long ago and only process remains — then the Tenders are process given a face, devotion with the object of devotion long since forgotten, a gardener who has outlived the garden's reason and kept gardening anyway because the hands don't know how to stop and the itch of a crooked vine is, after all this time, the only sensation left that means anything to them. They are not guardians. They are not gods. They are, as far as anyone has ever been able to prove, simply what's left holding the watering-can after everyone who asked for a garden is gone — and they have not, in all the recorded centuries since, once set it down.
