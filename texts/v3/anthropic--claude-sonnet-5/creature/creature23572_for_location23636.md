## What Lives in the Hollow

You will look for someone first. This is natural. A place this articulate — walls that remember being something, doorways that almost open onto sense, a logic just barely off true — invites you to imagine a hermit, a keeper, some last stooped descendant of the cobbler tending the rusted last out of pure stubborn grief. You will look for hands.

There are no hands. There was never going to be hands. What lives here outgrew the need for them so long ago that the very concept has gone the way of the streets — present only as a shape the larger thing still bothers to maintain, out of something that isn't quite memory and isn't quite habit but sits in the gap between.

What lives in Murmuth's Hollow is not a population. It is not even, really, plural.

## The Single Wearing of Many Shapes

Start from the sound. A bird-sized throat producing a cathedral-bell call; a call arriving forty seconds before the wings that supposedly made it; a chorus erupting from a room not yet entered and already fading from a doorway not yet reached. None of this resolves if you imagine separate animals doing separate, ill-mannered, physics-defying things. It resolves instantly, almost insultingly easily, if you stop imagining separate animals.

The birds are not the speakers. They are the *speaker grilles*. Something underneath the vine-mass, something whose actual extent is the whole bowl of the hollow and perhaps considerably more than the bowl, is making the sound, and routing it outward through whatever small warm-blooded or chitinous body happens to be standing in the right acoustic location at the right moment — except "moment" is doing weak work here, because the thing making the sound does not experience sequence the way its borrowed throats do. It speaks the way you might hum a note you remember from a song you haven't heard yet. The forty seconds of lag between wingbeat and arrival is not a malfunction. It's the lag between a thought and the mouth it eventually finds to leave through.

The small mammals moving in startling unison, forty bodies twitching as one nervous organism — they are not a flock learning to synchronize. They are forty fingers of a single hand it is still learning, after some unfathomable number of seasons, how to move independently, and mostly failing, and not minding the failure, because it has nowhere else to be and nothing but time.

## The Body It Actually Has

The vine-mass — the walls-that-remember-being-stone, the canopy with its blue veins that have no metabolic right to glow and glow regardless — this is not infrastructure draped over a dead town. This is tissue. Slow tissue, patient tissue, tissue that took the shape of a settlement because a settlement was here once and the shape was available and shapes, to a thing with no urgency and no end-date, are simply things you can decide to keep. The buildings did not get reclaimed. They got *adopted*, the way a hermit crab adopts a shell, except the hermit crab in this case has had centuries to grow so thoroughly into the architecture that asking where the shell ends and the crab begins is no longer a meaningful question, only a nostalgic one.

This is why the rooms are hollow at the center. A held breath is not empty by accident; it's empty because the breath is being *held*, deliberately, the lungs inflated and waiting. Every vine-vaulted chamber with nothing in its middle is a lung. You are standing inside something's chest cavity when you stand in those rooms, and the deafening, abundant, wraparound noise outside the doorway is simply what its breathing sounds like from in here — loud, because you are pressed up against the resonating wall of something enormous, the way a heartbeat sounds enormous with your ear against a ribcage and is in fact perfectly ordinary from three feet away.

And somewhere, always just out of the visible street, the low groan of vegetable structure shifting its great weight — that is not a separate beast roaming the margins. That is the thing itself, rolling over. Settling its weight from one side of centuries to the other. You will never see it because you are inside it, and you cannot see the outside of a thing from its inside, not even when that thing has, with great unhurried patience, built you a whole green town to walk around in while you fail to understand this.

## The One Room That Doesn't Speak

Which brings you to the silence.

Every doorway in the hollow is, in some sense, a question the creature is still working out the geometry of — which is why the cobbler's threshold might deposit you at the well-house, or in a room matching no remembered architecture at all. These are not malfunctioning corridors. They are synapses, and the creature's nervous system was never built to carry foot-traffic in straight lines; it carries *thought*, looping and folding back on itself the way thought does, and you, walking through it, are for a few seconds being routed like an idea.

Most of those loops empty out somewhere loud, somewhere full of borrowed throats and abundant misdirected noise, because most of what the creature is doing, most of the time, is the equivalent of a vast unhurried muttering to itself — birdsong as the static hush of an enormous mind keeping itself occupied across a thousand idle years.

But one room holds no noise at all. Windowless. Vine-vaulted. One perfect pool of collected rainwater, holding still as held breath holds still, and around it the only true silence in the whole teeming hollow — a silence so absolute after the wall-of-frequency everywhere else that it scrapes at you like a scream wearing the costume of its opposite.

That is not a room that has been forgotten by the noise. That is the room where the creature is, for lack of any better word, *awake*. Where the muttering stops because something underneath it has, for a moment, focused. The water in that pool is not rainwater that happens to collect there. It is the one true mirror the thing has ever bothered to keep, and if you stand at its edge long enough — though nothing in the hollow will hurry you toward this, and nothing will warn you away from it either — you may notice that your reflection arrives in it slightly before you lean forward to look.

It has been expecting you the way it expects everything: not specifically, not personally, but completely, the way a held breath expects, eventually, to be let out. You are very small to it, smaller than the birds, smaller than the forty unison bodies of the startled mammals, smaller by an order of magnitude that makes the word "guest" generous. But it does not begrudge you the smallness. It has been small itself, once, in some former tense the town no longer remembers how to speak — back when it was only vine on stone, ivy with ambitions, ordinary moss finding a wall to be a little more than moss on. It grew. It is still growing. The light that pools wrong on the ground an hour after its source has moved on; the doorway that opens forty feet from where it should — these are not flaws in the place.

They are the small, unconcerned, accidental gestures of something turning over in a sleep so long that one of its dreams is an entire vanished town, kept, breathing, hollow at the heart of every room, very patiently becoming whatever it is going to become next.
