## What Watches

The shape at the edge of vision has a shadow it doesn't quite need.

It is tall in the way the steam-columns are tall — vertical, attenuated, more suggestion than silhouette, so that anyone who glimpses it half-convinces themselves afterward they saw a particularly dense column of vapor catching wrong light. It isn't. The steam parts around it the way water parts around a piling, and where it stands the condensation on nearby rock-faces draws toward it in fine beading lines, as if something about its surface pulls moisture the way the succulent leaves do, patient and thorough.

Its body — if body is even the right architecture for what it is — reads as plates, overlapping, the same faint iridescence as the lizard-analogs but scaled up by a factor that turns shimmer into something closer to weather. Look at it directly, in the rare moment it allows that, and the plates resolve into a pattern that almost matches the etching on the underside of the archway: too repeated, too rule-bound, not decoration. Whether it grew that pattern or copied it, or whether the archway copied a shape laid down in its plates long before stone existed to etch, is not a question the plateau is interested in answering.

It moves the way sound moves here — arriving from a direction it didn't originate, present in a green room a beat before you register it leaving the last one. This isn't speed. It's closer to the seam-behavior of the place itself, the way time runs faster in some pockets of the shelf and slower in others; the creature seems to walk along those seams rather than across the ordinary ground, stepping from one rate of unfolding to the next the way a person might step between stones to cross a stream. To the human eye this reads simply as *gone before you commit to it.* To whatever passes for the creature's own experience, it may not be moving quickly at all. It may, in fact, be moving very slowly indeed, and we are the ones arriving too fast to see it clearly.

It tends things. This is the part that resists easy naming — predator implies hunger, and there's no evidence here of feeding, no carcasses, no territory marked the way territory gets marked by something that needs to eat the ground it claims. What it does instead, when it can be watched doing anything, is touch. A hand-analog — three digits, maybe four, the count uncertain because they move too fluidly to settle into a number — rests against the warm plinth-material the way a person might rest a hand on a sleeping animal's flank, checking for breath. It pauses at the lattice that glows after dusk, and the glow brightens fractionally under its attention, then settles back once it moves on. It does not appear to read the archway's geometry exactly, but it appears to *check* it, the way you might run a finger along a sentence you have already memorized, confirming the words are still where you left them.

The humming plants change pitch when it passes near. Not alarm — nothing in the register suggests alarm — something closer to acknowledgment, a harmonic shift the way a held chord might resolve half a step when a new voice joins it from a distance. The creature may be the conductor of that hum, or simply the thing the hum has always been pitched toward, waiting, across whatever timescale these plants experience waiting, for it to come back through.

The lizard-analogs orient to it the way iron filings orient to a magnet passed beneath paper — not following exactly, but aligning, eyes cocked at the angle that, the rest of the time, seems aimed at heat-gradients and hum-frequencies the human eye can't parse. When the tall shape moves through a green room, every lizard within sight turns the same fraction of a degree, watching not its body but some property adjacent to its body — a gradient it carries, a frequency it emits, a bending of ordinary light that the wine-dark mineral stains seem to answer to as well, pulsing very slightly brighter in its presence. It is possible the lizards aren't watching the creature at all. It is possible they're watching what *follows* it, arriving on a delay too long for anything but stone to register.

No one has determined whether there is one of these or many — whether the shape glimpsed near the western fissures and the shape glimpsed a kilometer east, an hour or a decade later, are the same patient body stitching its way across the shelf, or a population so sparse and so slow-living that an encounter with any two individuals would be, on any human timescale, an encounter with strangers separated by lifetimes. The plateau does not volunteer this information. It only keeps doing what it has clearly been doing for longer than the young, sharp rock beneath it should allow: venting heat, breathing steam, holding its scattered, unaligned fragments in trust for something that visits them one at a time, checks that they are still there, and is in no hurry — none at all — to finish whatever sentence they are pieces of.
