# Those Who Leave Before Being Found

You will see the weir before you see them. You will see the cleared corridor, the fire-ring going green at its edges, the worked stone half-sunk in mud — and you will assume, reasonably, that whatever made these things has the decency to be somewhere, holding still, waiting to be encountered. It does not. It has already gone. It went before you arrived and it will go again before you leave, and the going is not fear, exactly. It is method.

## The Body Built for Passing Through

They are small — smaller than the canopy's scale of things would suggest a hand-user ought to be, and this is not incidental. Lean, long-limbed, the build of something that climbs as readily as it walks, with shoulders that rotate further than yours and feet that grip as much as they step. Skin tends dark and mottled, broken up in patches of deeper pigment along the forearms and collarbone, not quite stripe and not quite blotch, the kind of pattern that does nothing for you standing still and everything for you moving through dapple and shadow at a half-run. Hair, where it grows long, is kept close-bound or shaved to stubble at the scalp's crown — vines catch, and a caught thing is a held thing, and a held thing in the Yawning Green does not stay whole for long.

What marks them most is not size or color but quality of attention. The eyes move constantly, in small disciplined sweeps — up, ahead, down, up again — because stillness of gaze here is how you fail to notice the branch deciding to fall or the carpet of small bodies deciding to reseal around your ankle rather than your footprint. They have learned to listen *through* the baseline roar the way you learned to ignore your own pulse, and so a change in it — a stutter in the layered calls, a held breath in the insect-wall — reaches them as clearly as a shout would reach you in a quiet room. Newcomers do not have this. Newcomers are the first to be caught, and the place is patient about teaching the rest.

## The Camp That Isn't One

They do not build to last because nothing here rewards lasting. A shelter is lashed green wood and broad leaf, raised in under an hour, abandoned within days — not from disaster but from arithmetic. The fruiting tree that fed them this week will be rot and fiber next week, refeeding soil that grew something else by the week after, and a fixed home does not move with that math, so they don't have fixed homes. They have routes. Loops worn faint through the basin's repeating valley-and-shelf, walked and rewalked until the body knows the next forty steps without the eyes confirming them, until the next rockfall or vine-choke forces a new route to be learned all over.

The fire-rings you find half-swallowed by shoots are not abandonment so much as rotation — a hearth used hard for three or four nights, then left to the forest's appetite while its makers move on to the next point in the loop, trusting that by the time they cycle back, something here will have changed enough to be worth returning for. They do not fight the turbulence. They have simply stopped expecting anything to hold still long enough to be owned.

## What the Weir Knows

The fishing weir — lashed green wood, standing in one of the rare slow currents — tells you more than the fire-ring does, if you read it right. It is built to be rebuilt. The lashings are simple, fast, replaceable; whole sections can be torn free and reset downstream in the time it takes the current to silt one channel and open another, because the stream itself does not hold its bed for long in ground this young. The people who built it did not build a weir. They built a *technique* for building weirs, repeatedly, against a river that keeps changing its mind — and that, more than any tool or fire-scar, is the actual artifact. Not the object. The practiced response.

This is true of everything they make. Stone tools are struck quickly, used hard, discarded rather than carried far — sharp-edged like the land itself because the land's own broken stone supplies the pattern, and because a tool worth carrying any distance is a tool you'll eventually be caught holding when both hands are needed for climbing. They travel light because the place punishes accumulation. Whatever they value most, they likely carry in memory and muscle rather than in any bag.

## The Listening Ones

There is a smaller matter — spoken of, if at all, in the way you mention something you've decided not to decide about. The call heard only twice, the one that sounds like it's trying badly to repeat syllables. Some of them have heard it more than twice. You can tell by the way certain individuals go still at a particular pitch in the layered noise, head canted, before resuming whatever they were doing with a deliberateness that suggests the stillness was the thing they didn't want you to notice.

They do not name it. They do not seem to hunt it, or flee it, or leave it offerings — at least nothing you'd recognize as offering. But the cleared corridors, on inspection, sometimes bend around stands of the cold-glowing fungus rather than through them, a few feet of extra walking that costs nothing and explains nothing, except that it is consistent, generation after generation of footprint pressed into exactly that same unnecessary curve. Whatever they've decided about the almost-symbols and the badly-echoed word, they decided it long enough ago that it's stopped being a decision and become a habit of the feet.

## The Rule They've Made With the Place

They have not tamed the Yawning Green, and seem to carry no ambition toward taming it. What they have done instead is consent to its terms with unusual completeness — to be provisional, as everything here is provisional; to take what the turbulence offers in the brief window before it reclaims the offer; to hold nothing, build nothing, name nothing longer than the forest itself seems willing to let it stand.

This is, perhaps, the only way anything endures in such a place: not by resisting the becoming, but by becoming just as quickly, just as lightly — one more damp, warm, fast-moving event, indistinguishable at a glance from all the others, gone again before the green has finished closing the gap behind them.
