# What Answers

You will hear it before you see it, which is the only way anything is ever encountered here. A long, rising column of sound that seems to originate from several directions and resolve, after a confused moment, into one throat.

## The Caller

It stands — when it stands, which is not often, since standing is a vulnerability it allots itself sparingly — on four limbs so disproportionately long that its body, a narrow keel of muscle and hollow bone no bigger than a deer's, seems to hang suspended between them like cargo. The limbs are not built for speed. They are built for *height*, for raising the chest cavity into the thin air and the wind's path, because the chest cavity is the instrument and the instrument needs the wind to play it properly.

Its lungs — there are three, nested, the largest wrapping the spine like a second skeleton — take up more of its mass than any other organ, more than the heart and stomach and brain combined, because the brain does not need to be large here. It needs to do one thing: hold a register of distances. Each caller maintains, somewhere behind its small flat eyes, a map of every other caller's voice within a day's wind — not their location exactly, but their *boundary*, the line past which a call becomes provocation rather than weather. It rarely sees the owners of these voices. It does not need to. The map is enough. The shelf is too sparse, too loud, too widely strung-out across its own emptiness for crowding to mean anything; what matters is who has claimed how much silence, and silence here is the only thing scarcer than company.

When two boundaries drift into contention — and they do, because nothing on the shelf, including the geometry the callers measure their distances against, holds still for long — the resolution is vocal, not physical. A duel of register and breath-length, conducted across a gap of half a mile, each caller throwing its whole hollow body into a sound built to outlast the other's air. The loser is not injured. The loser simply runs out first, and goes quiet, and walks — long-limbed, undignified, fast — toward whatever unclaimed quiet remains, which on a shifting shelf is never very far away and never very permanent either.

They graze on the ringing stalks between calls, snipping the hollow chambers at the base with a beak-like fold of cartilage, chewing slowly, listening always. Some of them, it's said by no one in particular because no one is here long enough to compile a saying, have learned to bite the stalks at specific lengths — to *tune* them before breaking them off, leaving a field of stumps that the wind plays differently afterward. Whether this is intention or accident is not a distinction the caller's small flat brain has any use for.

## The Drinkers of Hum

Lower, smaller, and far less proud are the things that gather at the black ring in the cool hours — joint-heavy, many-limbed, the size of a folded hand, with a translucency at the elbows and knees that means nothing until the ring's hum rises, at which point those joints begin to glow, faint amber, in sympathy.

They don't eat in any sense the callers would recognize. They press themselves to the warm understone near the ring's foot, or simply sit close enough to vibrate with it, and something passes into them — not heat, not sound exactly, but whatever the ring is actually emitting beneath the frequency you can consciously register. They swell almost imperceptibly as they feed. When sated, glowing now at every joint, faint amber chains running down each limb, they detach themselves from the stone with a strange unsticking motion and crawl off to wherever they breed, which no one has found, because they only ever travel at night and only ever in directions away from observation.

They have no call of their own. They are the one quiet thing on the shelf, and the quiet is, in its way, the loudest fact about them — proof that something here has learned to feed directly on the engine rather than on what the engine throws off as side effect: the stalks, the wind, the noise. They are closer to the architecture than to the ecology, if those are even different categories on the shelf. Perhaps they are what an animal becomes when it stops competing with the building and starts attending it instead.

## The Tenders, Maybe

There is a third kind of resident, and it is the one you will be least able to confirm, because it is never quite caught in the act of being anything. Near the towers — the three leaning ones, never taller than a person, surfaces running their slow reverse-erosion — there are sometimes shapes. Upright, still, roughly the proportions of the callers but without their advertised hugeness of lung, without any sound at all. They are seen at the corner of attention, in the pulse-dimmed light, standing close to the towers in postures that read, despite every effort not to anthropomorphize, as *attentive*. Tending. Checking on something mid-repair.

No one has gotten close enough to one to settle whether it is animal, automaton, or some local fusion the shelf doesn't bother distinguishing between. They do not flee, exactly. They simply are not there anymore by the time you've decided to approach, the way a held thought isn't there anymore once you reach to write it down. If they are the makers — if the towers and the black ring and the impossible angles are theirs, still being built, still being rewritten in real time alongside everything else turbulent here — they have not announced it, and the shelf does not require them to.

## What This Adds Up To

None of these — the long-limbed throats measuring their loneliness in decibels, the small glowing joints feeding on a building's exhalation, the maybe-shapes tending a maybe-machine — behaves as though it expects to be the last of anything, or the first. They are not surviving the turbulence. They are *inside* it the way the rock is inside it, old in form, immediate in moment, governed by the same unfinished sentence as the stalks and the towers and the wind.

You happen, briefly, to be standing in the room where it's happening. So is the caller, mapping a boundary that will be wrong again in an hour. So is the joint-glowing thing, swelling on a frequency it didn't ask the meaning of. So, maybe, is the shape by the tower, which may not even know it's been seen.

None of them are waiting for you to understand the room. They were already living in it.
