# Inhabitants of the Hollow

You assumed, at first, that you were looking at fauna. Drifting things, soft and incidental, going about the business of small lives in the lee of architecture too strange to have made on purpose. This was wrong, or wrong enough to matter. The things that move through the Hollow did not move *into* it. They are not visitors to the architecture. They are its authors, its residue, and in some unsettled sense its present tense.

## The Pale Ones

Call them that, for lack of anything better — there is no word for them above the shelf, and likely none below it either. Soft-bodied, finger-length to forearm-length depending on age or caste or some other distinction you can't parse, translucent enough that in the bioluminescent rumor of the place you can sometimes see a paler structure inside them, branching, slow-pulsing, doing something that might be circulation and might be thought.

They have no eyes, mostly. A few — the older-seeming ones, the ones whose bodies have gone slightly opaque with sediment-years — carry a single pale dimple where an eye might once have been useful, vestigial, pointed at nothing in particular. They do not need to see. They *secrete*. This is the first true thing to understand about them: every surface they touch for long enough acquires a film, and the film hardens, and the hardening is the calcite rope, the dripping mineral, the slow blind architecture of the towers. They build the way you sweat. The city is not a thing they made and then moved into. The city is what happens to the water around them if they stay still long enough.

This is why nothing here was made with tools. There were never hands. There was only duration.

## The Logic of Caste, or Something Like It

They do not seem to communicate, not in any way you'd recognize as signal — no flash, no touch-language, no current-pattern repeated with intention. And yet they coordinate. The processions through the canyon-streets bend together at the same moment, turn at the same plaza-mouth, disperse into the spire-gaps in a pattern too clean for chance and too loose for command. You start to suspect the coordination isn't between them at all. You start to suspect it's *underneath* them — that the rock itself, threaded through with old secretions, slow-pulsing chemical memory laid down over centuries, is doing the deciding, and the Pale Ones are simply the part of the organism that can still move.

Some are smaller and faster, threading the high gaps near the unfinished tower-tops, restless, never settling, never adding to the stone. Call these the young, if age is even the axis they vary along. Some are slower, heavier, traveling the lower streets in looser drift, trailing the faint mineral haze that means they are, even now, mid-secretion, mid-becoming-wall. Call these the elders, though *elder* implies an end they may not have. You do not know if they die. You suspect, instead, that they simply slow, and settle, and stop being distinguishable from the architecture they once moved through — which would explain why some of the "raw" stone, the wet-edged unfinished stone, is faintly warm, faintly responsive to touch, in a way mineral has no business being.

## The Listening Threads

The pale growths at the high lintels — you took them, reasonably, for vegetation. They are not separate from the Pale Ones either. Look closely enough, in the dim blue light, and you can see they originate from the same translucent tissue, anchored where some elder-thing settled long enough to stop being mobile and start being structure, still alive in some attenuated way, still doing the one thing it has left to do: sway, and sense, and answer the rock.

They are the Hollow's ears, maybe. Or its memory, externalized — each thread a slow-motion nerve, picking up the settle and tick of the stone two streets over, the turning of something large in deep water, the arrival of a held breath that should not be there, drifting down through a fold in the seabed no wider than its own. You wonder, standing among them, whether they have already reported you. Whether the soft procession reorganizing itself around the plaza ahead is reorganizing itself *around your presence*, politely, the way a held breath reorganizes around an intrusion of cold.

## The Larger Thing

It is glimpsed and not resolved, and this may be deliberate, or may simply be a feature of scale you haven't reconciled. It moves like a shouldering-aside, a displacement of smaller bodies, and then it is gone before geometry catches up to it. Two possibilities sit unresolved in you, refusing to collapse into one:

That it is a predator — something that has learned to live in the gaps of this slow civilization the way a wolf learns the gaps of a flock, taking the young-fast ones when they stray too far from the secreting elders, keeping the population in the shape the architecture can support.

Or that it is the oldest one. The first secretion. The thing the entire Hollow has been quietly, patiently building *around*, generation after blind generation, the way a pearl is built around the grain that started it — and what you are walking through is not a city with an unknown architect. It is a city with a buried heart, still alive, still slowly being entombed by its own children's devotion, mistaking burial for shelter, or shelter for the same thing as burial, because down here, at this pressure, in this cold, there may be no difference left to draw.

You do not get close enough to know which. The Pale Ones don't seem to know either — or if they do, they have no eye to tell you with, no voice to spend on the answer, only the soft concussive nothing of a large body turning, two streets over, patient as sediment, in no hurry at all to be understood.
