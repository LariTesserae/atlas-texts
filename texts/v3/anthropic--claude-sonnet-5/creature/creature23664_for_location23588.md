## Those Who Tend

Call them the Tenders, since you have to call them something, since "people" arrives already wrong in your mouth and you feel it the moment you try to use it.

They are recognizably bipedal, recognizably warm, recognizably alive in whatever sense the word still does any work here — but the resemblance is load-bearing only up to a point. Their skin has the same fused-grain quality as the walls, a bark-pale, faintly luminous undertone visible mostly in the wrist, the throat, the places where vein sits close to surface. You will notice, eventually, that none of them have quite the same number of fingers twice. You will notice this and then stop being able to remember which hand you were looking at.

They do not speak so much as *root* — a low continuous murmur passed mouth to mouth to wall to vine to mouth again, so that conversation, when you overhear it, sounds less like dialogue and more like sap finding the path of least resistance through a structure that wants, eventually, for everything to reach the same place. You will rarely catch a full sentence. You will catch the *shape* of one, the way you catch the shape of a fish under disturbed water.

Their labor is constant and looks, from outside, indistinguishable from worship. A Tender at a kiln does not feed it so much as confide in it. A Tender at the loom sits with the twitching cloth across her knees for an hour after cutting it, palm flat, as if apologizing, or as if listening to make sure the silence that follows is the right kind of silence. They are not unkind to you. They are not anything to you, particularly. Their attention, when it lands, lands the way moss lands — settling, not choosing.

## What They Feed the Engines

The small dense objects burned for fuel are not gathered. They are *grown*, somewhere in the deep wet dark beneath the town's actual floor, in a process no one will show you and several will calmly, almost kindly, lie to you about. You have seen a Tender press one to her sternum before carrying it to the furnace, the way you might press a letter to your chest before you can make yourself send it.

This is the closest thing to grief you will witness here. It does not look like grief. It looks like inventory.

What burns is, as established, someone's first house, decades gone — but whose. This is the part nobody answers, and you've come to suspect the unwillingness is not secrecy but plain ignorance: the memories are not stored *as* anyone's. They have been grown loose from their owners the way fruit grows loose from the idea of the flower that preceded it. The town runs on orphaned memory, vast tracts of it, harvested from some root-system reaching back further and wider than the visible settlement could possibly justify, as if Vell's Crossing's true size is mostly underground, mostly past tense, mostly other people's.

## The Canopy Population

Above the Tenders, indifferent to them, exists the second population — the chittering abundance, the wingbeats, the claw-thock climbers — and these are stranger by an order of magnitude, because they were never trying to resemble you in the first place.

There is the thing the locals call, in their root-murmur, something that translates loosely to **the patient one** — large-bodied, slow, grazing the bioluminescent veins down to total dark and moving on, leaving whole corridors blind in its wake for days until the light, slowly, scars back over. You never see it whole. You see the absence it leaves and infer the shape from the absence's edges, the way you'd infer a body from a chalk outline, except the outline keeps moving.

There are the **joint-things**, sentences that revise themselves mid-step, whose number of limbs seems to be a function of how closely you're paying attention rather than a fixed biological fact. Stop watching and recount: different number. Nobody finds this remarkable enough to mention, which you eventually understand is the most remarkable thing about it.

There is the wet clicking thing that is mostly its own sound, arriving from three directions before — or instead of — arriving from a source. The Tenders leave small offerings near the places it clicks loudest: scraps of the twitching cloth, a bead of the rust-and-chlorophyll drip-water cupped in a leaf. Whether this is feeding it, worshipping it, or simply being polite to a neighbor whose face they've never needed to see is a distinction that doesn't seem to exist in whatever ethics governs this place.

And occasionally, in the vine-fluid running the factory walls, the things with eyes — visible for four meters, blinking once, swept on toward the next valve. The Tenders call them nothing at all. They simply step around the pipe at that point in its run, a small habitual deference, the way you'd step around a person sleeping in a doorway you walk past every day without ever learning their name.

## The One in the Square

If Vell's Crossing has a center, a held breath has a shape, and the shape, when the fog is thickest, resolves — sometimes, not reliably, not for everyone — into a figure that does not so much stand in the square as *occupy* the square's grammatical position, the way a verb occupies a sentence.

It is the singing you heard, or it is what the singing is doing on its way toward you. It is built, the way the cloth is built and the fuel is built and the Tenders themselves may be built, from things that no longer have anywhere else to be: the names of trees extinct everywhere outside this fold of land, hung together into something that functions as a body only because enough syllables agreed, at some point, to stand in proximity to one another.

It does not approach. It does not retreat. Ask a Tender about it and they will give you the closest thing to a direct answer you'll get in this entire town, delivered without weight, without ceremony, the same tone you'd use to mention the weather if the weather here were a thing that happened rather than a thing withheld:

*That's just what's left standing when enough is forgotten in one place at once.*

Then they will go back to the kiln, or the loom, or the long patient work of confiding a small dense house-shaped grief into a fire that runs the whole damp, dark, climbing, listening town — and you will understand that you have been shown, as much as this place ever shows anyone anything, exactly who lives here: everything that was never going to fit anywhere else, growing together because growing apart had stopped, a long time ago, being one of the available directions.
