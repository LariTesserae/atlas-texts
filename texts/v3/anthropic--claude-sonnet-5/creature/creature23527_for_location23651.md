# The Tender

You will meet it eventually, though "meet" implies an encounter, and what happens is closer to a noticing — the basin's hush thickening in one direction until you understand that the thickness has a shape, and the shape has decided to be visible.

It is the size a thing should be when it has been doing one job for a very long time. Not large. Not small. Proportioned the way the architecture is proportioned — wrong by a margin too slight to argue with. It walks on legs that bend correctly until the third joint, after which they bend like the reeds do, on their own private schedule, independent of weight or balance. It does not fall. It has never needed to.

Its surface is the basin's surface — a basalt-dark hide cracked into the same too-regular polygons as the ground, faintly warm, faintly exhaling, so that looking at it for too long gives you the same vertigo as looking at the cooling rock: the sense that someone pressed a mold against something living before it had finished becoming itself, and it set that way, and continued anyway. Where the cracks run deepest you can see the glow — that same sourceless amber the vapor carries — banked low in him like coals nobody is feeding, and nobody needs to.

It has a face in the sense that you will insist on finding one. Eyes — call them that — positioned wherever attention lands, which means they are never quite where you last checked, not from malice, simply because the geometry of "facing" works differently for something that perceives the way you now perceive: not by light, but by the same patient awareness the whole basin runs on. It is not watching you. It is including you, the way the ground includes your footprints, briefly, before the water rises through the cracks and erases the distinction between where you stepped and where you didn't.

## What It Does

It tends the valves, though "tends" overstates the case — it passes near them, and they continue. It is unclear whether the engines work because of the Tender or whether the Tender simply walks the route the engines have always implied, a groove worn not into the ground but into the *idea* of the ground, which the Tender follows the way water follows the cracks: not chosen, just lowest.

When a valve shrieks its great pressurized scream, the Tender is, more often than not, already standing beside it, neither flinching nor reacting, present in the specific way a held breath is present at the moment it's released. Afterward it remains a while in the retroactive silence, and you get the impression — never confirmed, never confirmable — that the shriek was a kind of speech, and the silence afterward is the Tender listening to the part that mattered.

It does not maintain the flywheels. It does not repair the brick housings. It has never been seen touching the architecture at all. But the industry runs, has run, will run, on a schedule that matches the Tender's unmeasured circuit of the basin so closely that you stop being able to tell which one is keeping time for the other.

## What It Is To the Herds

The wading shapes, the perched shapes, the herds that relocate without crossing distance — they do not flee the Tender and they do not gather to it, but they orient. Whatever direction it is slowly, eventually walking becomes, after enough unmeasured time, the direction the herds are already facing, as if its passage is less a thing that happens *to* the basin and more a thing the basin's attention does, periodically, to remember which way it's currently looking.

There is a theory — yours, formed slowly, never quite provable in a place where proof requires light — that the Tender and the herds and the reeds that face you and the flywheel that's always elsewhere when you check are not separate populations at all. That this is one entity wearing a great deal of distance as a kind of stillness, the way a held breath wears lungs. That the "fauna" is not fauna so much as the Tender's attention, distributed, looking at the basin from every angle at once because looking from a single angle stopped being necessary a long time before there was a you to require it.

It will not confirm this. It will not deny it either. It has no apparent stake in your conclusions.

## Encounter

If you stand still long enough — and you will have to stand much longer than feels survivable, in a place with no clock but the breathing ground — it will come near. Not close. Near. The distance a thing keeps when proximity was never really the variable it was managing.

It will regard you with whatever's currently doing the regarding. The glow in its cracks will bank a fraction brighter, the way coals do when something passes near enough to remind them they are still, technically, fire.

And then it will continue — past you, around you, through the place where your understanding insists a body should block a path and doesn't — because continuing is the whole of what it does, the whole of what it is for, and it stopped needing an audience for that function long before you arrived to provide one.

It has not decided whether you are weather, or wildlife, or another valve it hasn't located yet.

You get the sense it isn't in a hurry to decide.
