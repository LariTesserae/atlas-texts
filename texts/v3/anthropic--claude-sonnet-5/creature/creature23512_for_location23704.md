## What Answers the Chorus

Start small, because the Stack does — every colony begins from the bottom and works up, and so will this.

The clicking things are called, by the hand-tool people who have to call them something, *the small mouths*, though most have no mouths you'd recognize, just intake slits or feathered fronds that pull whatever they eat directly from the air. They are pale because nothing here has needed pigment — no sun to burn off, no predator-eyes tuned to color, just the gray omnipresent non-light and the cold. Eyes too large for the heads that hold them, the better to drink in a light that barely arrives. Some have wings that are really just skin stretched between elongated fingers, enough to glide from shelf to shelf but not enough for real flight; some have legs in numbers that seem more like a design still being revised than a finished animal. They cluster in colonies that breathe as a unit — you can watch a shelf of them contract and expand together, some shared rhythm passing through a hundred bodies like a held thought.

They do not eat the lichen, true. What they do is press themselves, periodically, into the basin-pools — not to drink exactly, more the way a hand goes into water to test it. They come up glistening, and for hours afterward they are still, sated, humming at a lower register than the rest of the chorus. The pools are not just water. The people who live at the base do not call them pools. They call them *teats*, and say it without irony, because that is the plainest word for a hollow that keeps something fed.

Above the small mouths, on the broken terraces where the overhangs throw their deepest shadow, are the ones the climbers call *watchers* for lack of anything closer. They are larger — dog-sized at smallest, something closer to door-sized at the upper terraces where no one has stayed long enough to measure properly. They move the way old water moves, slow and certain, no wasted motion, and they do not click or keen with the rest. They make no contribution to the chorus at all. Their stillness is the loudest thing about them. They watch the small mouths feed at the basin-pools the way a farmer watches livestock, proprietary rather than hungry — though hunger comes later, on the smaller and slower of the small mouths, in a culling so quiet it's mistaken for natural attrition.

But the watchers do not explain the wrongness either. They are predators in an ecosystem too rich for the rock that hosts it, which only pushes the question one level down, into the stone itself. And here the hand-tool people stop guessing aloud, because the guess that occurs to all of them eventually is the same guess, and saying it tends to end conversations.

What they suspect — what the warmth gradient on the upper terraces suggests, what the basin-pools that are not pools suggest, what a fauna density with no plant base to support it absolutely demands — is that the Stack did not rise so much as it was *grown*, and that something vast and patient and barely animal still occupies the deep architecture of it, the way a heart occupies a chest. Not a creature *on* the mountain. A creature the mountain is wrapped around, or wrapped *by* — old in a way nothing else here is old, predating the basalt's hard young edges by some measure no one has a tool to take. Its body, if body is the word, may be the warm dark interior the climbers report and never reach. Its circulatory fluid may surface in fist-sized basins as something the small mouths mistake, gratefully, for water. Its breath, slow on a scale of years rather than seconds, may be the reason nothing on the Stack hurries, the reason lichen takes a decade to cross a handspan and pools fill on a clock tuned to something underground.

The small mouths are not separate from it. They are the part of it that clicks and keens and shows itself in daylight-that-isn't, the visible froth on a much larger, much slower tide. The watchers are not separate from it either — they are simply older small mouths, given time enough on the upper terraces to grow into something closer to whatever waits beneath. The chorus that wraps the Stack like weather is not many voices arguing over a thin resource. It is one immense organism's outer skin, murmuring to itself in ten thousand mismatched throats, because it has not yet decided — four hundred meters of new stone into its life, with all the unhurried patience of something that intends to be here for a very long time — what shape it wants its voice to finally take.

The hand-tool people at the base do not climb past the lower terraces. They take what drips down to them — condensation, runoff, the occasional dead small mouth fallen from a shelf — and they do not ask the mountain for more, because they understand, in the unspoken way you understand a held breath beside you in the dark, that the Stack is not finished being born, and you do not interrupt a birth that large to ask it for a favor.
