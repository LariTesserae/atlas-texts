The thing that tends this place — and something does tend it, the valves don't bleed themselves clean, the pressure gauges didn't learn to read themselves — is easy to miss because it has stopped trying to be seen.

Call it the Warden, for lack of anything it has ever told anyone, because it has never told anyone anything. It is vast and slow and you could walk past it twice, mistaking it for the architecture, before some shift in the green — a fold of leaf-tissue contracting where no draft moved it — gives it away.

## Shape

It does not have a shape so much as a *terrain* of its own, draped across forty feet of wall and machine-cluster like something poured and then left to set. Where it begins and the vegetation ends is not a meaningful question; the same waxy, light-drinking leaf-tissue that blankets the bowl thickens, here, into something closer to hide — pale-veined, faintly luminous along old scar-lines, ridged in places into something that might once have been articulated, jointed, *legged*, before it gave up locomotion as a habit it no longer needed.

It has limbs, or had them. Four, maybe six, vestigial — long tapering vine-thick members that have fused at the tips into the brass fittings of the boiler-clusters, grown through and around valve wheels the way a tree grows around a fence post, until you cannot say whether it is holding the machine or the machine is holding it. It feeds pipe-heat into itself the way the ferns feed on the ambient glow: not eating, not exactly, but drawing the warmth through some membrane along its underside and letting it diffuse upward through tissue that pulses, slow as the drips, slow as the vents, on a cycle measured in the same unhurried unit as everything else down here.

## Breath, of a Kind

It does not breathe through lungs. None of this bowl's residents do, and the Warden least of all — what passes for its respiration is a long, soft exhale of warmed vapor released from gill-like slits along its flank, timed to the hiss-clank of the machinery so precisely that for a long while you will believe the *machine* is breathing and the Warden is just additional plumbing. The two sounds married themselves to each other so many cycles ago that neither remembers which one started the rhythm.

This is, perhaps, the most important thing about it: it has stopped being separate from the system it maintains. It does not *operate* the vents in the sense of choosing to. It has grown into the role the way water grows into a channel — by repetition, by least resistance, by sheer accumulated time. Where a valve threatens to seize, a tendril has long since wound itself into the mechanism and learned, by some dumb persistent feedback no engineer designed, to flex at the correct interval. Where a pressure differential builds, a fold of its hide thins and vents the excess as vapor before it can become dangerous. It is gardener and thermostat and ghost of maintenance crew all at once, performing a job it may not remember was ever explained to it, for reasons that died with whoever — if anyone — built it for this.

## Senses

It does not see, not as you'd recognize it. The same photosensitive cells that let the ferns find the warmest light run through its hide in greater density, giving it something more like a slow, full-body squint — an awareness of gradient, of where the heat pools and where it thins, of where a stranger's body disturbs the established pattern of warmth by simply standing in one place too long. This is how it knows you're here. Not sight. Subtraction. You are a cool patch moving through a warm museum, and it has noticed the absence you make.

It will not approach you. It has not approached anything in longer than it could measure even if it wished to — approach implies urgency, and urgency is not a register this place permits anymore. But if you stay long enough, motionless enough, you may feel the air shift fractionally toward you, a slow exploratory exhalation, vapor drawn off some unseen vent and curled, almost curiously, in your direction — the closest thing to a greeting the bowl still remembers how to give.

## What It Wants

Nothing, as far as can be told. Or: the same thing the vents want, the same thing the moss wants, the same thing the drips want when they fall — to continue exactly as they have been continuing, on schedule, unhurried, until the heat finally loses its long argument with the cold mountain above and the whole bowl, Warden included, settles at last into stillness. It is not waiting for that day. It is not dreading it either. It simply has not happened yet, and so the valves get tended, and the vapor gets breathed, and the green keeps closing slowly over the brass, one century-slow inch at a time.
