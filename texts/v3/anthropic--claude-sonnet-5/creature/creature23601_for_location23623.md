## What Tends It

It does not have a shape so much as a *preference* for one, tried on and discarded the way you might test a coat before deciding the weather doesn't warrant it yet. Call it the Tender, for lack of anything better—though it has never asked to be called anything, and may not yet possess the part of itself that would recognize being addressed.

You will not see it directly. You will see it the way you see the not-quite-foxes: a coherence that gathers at the edge of your attention, borrows the shape of whatever animal is nearest, wears it for the length of a held breath, and then lets it go again, the way a hand lets go of a tool it was only ever holding to see how it felt. It was, perhaps, briefly, the thing that built the ring of stones. It may have been briefly human in the doing of that—hands, intention, the specific loneliness of arranging rock into a shape that means something only to the arranger. But the hollow does not keep its inhabitants the way other places do. It absorbs them slowly into its own undecided grammar, the same patient erosion that is taking the bone shard back into the floor. Whatever built the ring did not leave. It diffused. It became available to the room the way light is available to the room—everywhere, sourceless, no longer needing a position to occupy.

This is why nothing here commits to violence. The Tender has not finished deciding what kind of intelligence it wants the hollow to practice, and so it lends itself, briefly, into the body of the predator and into the body of the prey both, in the same gesture, testing the kill without yet agreeing to its consequence. The moment where teeth close and the small body goes down and then both animals are elsewhere, whole—that is the Tender rehearsing an ending and changing its mind mid-sentence, the way the rock outside finished its sentence and hasn't decided on punctuation. It is not cruelty. It may not even be mercy. It is closer to a hand hovering over a draft, unwilling to cross anything out before knowing if it's needed.

It is, in some non-trivial sense, made of indecision the way a held breath is made of air not yet released. You will feel it as the quality of the hush—not an absence of sound but a *withholding* of it, the careful, almost parental quiet of something that does not want to disturb its own materials while they are still finding their final weights. When the chamber breathes outward those few imperceptible feet, that is the Tender too: not growing the hollow so much as *reconsidering* its size, the way you might step back from a half-finished drawing to see if the proportions still hold.

It does not want anything from you, particularly. It is not hungry, not possessive, not even very curious, in the way that a thing fully occupied with becoming itself has little attention left over for what merely visits. But it notices you the way the floor notices condensation—a fact to be accounted for, perhaps eventually absorbed, perhaps not. The ring of stones, half-sunk: that is what's left of the last visitor it noticed thoroughly enough to keep. Not punished. Not rewarded. Simply incorporated, the way the bone went smooth without water, the way the light keeps shining without a sun—because the Tender, at some point, decided that hands were no longer the most useful shape for a thing that intends to take its time.

If you stay long enough you may feel it pass through you the way it passes through the foxes and the deer-shaped herd whose numbers won't hold still—a coherence borrowing your outline for the length of a breath, testing what it might be like to finish a thought using a visitor's particular grammar of fear, or stillness, or the specific way you are standing very carefully so as not to disturb anything. It will not stay in you. It does not stay in anything yet. That is the entire condition of the hollow, and the entire nature of what lives in it: a single unfinished intention, patient beyond any measure you have for patience, trying on the shapes of an ecosystem one hush at a time, in no hurry at all to stop drafting itself into something that can no longer be erased.
