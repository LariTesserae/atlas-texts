# What Lives There

You don't see it at first. You won't see it at first even when you're looking right at it, because it has spent longer learning to be mistaken for the wall than you have spent being alive. Only later — a held second too long, a thicket of frond-growth that seems, retroactively, to have been breathing — do you understand that the hollow has a tenant, and that the tenant has been patient with your noticing.

## Shape, More or Less

Call it a body the way you'd call a riverbed a road — true in outline, false in the particulars. It is low and long, segmented the way the rock is segmented, ridged the way erosion ridges things, so that its spine reads as just another lip of stone with moss-things colonizing it unevenly. What growth covers it is not costume; it's load-bearing. The pale luminescent fronds root *into* it as much as onto it, threading through gaps in something harder underneath — plate, shell, chitin, alloy, the distinctions stopped mattering to it before they would've mattered to you. Where the frond-light gathers thickest, you can just make out a softer glow beneath, slower, redder, like a coal someone forgot to mention was still burning.

It has no face you would recognize as one. There is a cluster, forward, where the threadlike circuit-growths knot more densely than elsewhere, branching into something that might be sensory, might be ornamental, might be a vestige of a function it stopped needing before your species learned to make fire. It does not need to look at you to know you're there. It has been listening to your footsteps' weight in the ground since before you found the crevice.

## How It Moves

Rarely, and as if movement were a debt being repaid against its will. When it does move, it does so the way the hollow's water does — not flowing, exactly, but *arriving*, each shift so gradual that the eye keeps losing the thread of it and has to relocate the creature anew, slightly displaced, slightly rearranged among the fronds, with no memory of having watched the transition happen. It could cross the hollow in those dozen steps you took at a stride. It will instead take, by your reckoning, years. It does not consider this a sacrifice. Speed, to it, looks like panic, and panic looks like something that hasn't yet understood it isn't going to be interrupted.

## How It Feeds, If That's the Word

It doesn't hunt, because there's nothing here to hunt, and it doesn't forage, because there's nothing here to find that isn't already touching it. What passes for feeding is closer to *eavesdropping* — it sits at the braided intersection of frond-thread and machine-node, where the bioluminescence and the old technology's residual hum have spent centuries converging on a shared rhythm, and it draws off that rhythm the way you'd warm your hands at a fire that wasn't built for you but doesn't mind your being near it. The plants feed the light. The fragments feed the field. The creature feeds the overlap. Nothing in the hollow is poorer for it.

## Its Relationship to the Made Things

It is older than some of them and younger than others, and it has long since stopped distinguishing itself from the third category: the things that are neither grown nor built but became, gradually, both. It tends the nodes the way you might tend a habit — not out of devotion, just proximity sustained past the point of separation. Where a frond has overgrown a fragment's surface to the point of strangulation, the creature has, over some span you'd need geological language to describe, eased it back a half-inch, just enough to keep the old light pulsing. Whether this is maintenance, affection, or pure reflex, it has never had cause to ask itself, there being no one else down here to ask.

## Senses

It hears the way the hollow hears: subsonically, sternum-deep, in the register of the embedded nodes' hum rather than in any register your ears were built for. It feels moisture condensing on far walls as a kind of weather. It is aware of warmth the way the rock is aware of warmth — slowly, thoroughly, retaining the memory of your body's heat in the space you stood for longer than you'd be comfortable knowing. Whether it sees you, in any sense you'd recognize as seeing, is unclear even to it. It registers you the way the chamber registers a held breath: as a small temporary disturbance in a pressure that will, shortly, return to itself.

## What It Wants

Nothing it doesn't already have within reach of its own unhurried radius. It wants the rhythm to keep converging. It wants the cold to keep being the cold it agreed to a long time ago. It is not lonely, having outlived the category. It is not waiting for you to leave, exactly, though things will resume their proper pace once you do — the pace at which a millimeter is a unit of urgency, at which a year is a held breath, at which whatever it is doing here, slowly tending its small closed garden of light and old machinery, might as well be called *purpose*, there being no remaining audience to call it anything else.

## The Rule, Completed

The hollow's law was that everything here had stopped needing anything from outside itself. The creature is not an exception to that law. It is its proof. It is what a closed loop looks like once it has had long enough to grow something capable of noticing it's closed — and of finding, after all this time, no reason whatsoever to want out.
