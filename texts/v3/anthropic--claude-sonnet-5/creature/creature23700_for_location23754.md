# What Tends the Throat

There is one of them, and it has no good name, because nothing has stood close enough to it for long enough to need one. Call it the Tender, if you must call it anything. It does not live in the fissure the way a creature lives in a den. It lives in the fissure the way a pulse lives in a vein — distributed, recurring, never quite *located*, present everywhere the rings and the lattice are present and nowhere else.

**Body, if it has one**

When it manifests — and it does manifest, briefly, conditionally — it takes the form of a bead of black that is not quite liquid and not quite solid, perhaps the size of a fist, perhaps the size of a kneeling person, depending on how much of itself it has decided the moment requires. It does not crawl or walk. It travels along the same hairline channels that carry the condensation, threading through cracks too narrow for anything with bones, and where it passes the rock briefly loses its matte black and goes glossy, wet-looking, mirror-bright, before dulling again behind it like a held breath being let out.

It is faceted the way the walls are faceted. This is not camouflage so much as kinship — it is, structurally, an instance of the same substance the spire is made of, given a temporary willingness to move. When it gathers enough of itself into one place, the gathered shape sometimes approximates something with a front and a back, a lean, an orientation — never quite a face, but enough of a suggestion of one that you will find yourself addressing it, despite every reason not to.

**How it senses**

It does not have eyes, and it does not need them. It reads pressure, temperature gradient, the faint mechanical complaint of stone displaced by a footstep. Mostly, though, it reads *you* through the half-second of shadow-lag at the thresholds — that small delay is not an optical glitch, it is the Tender's attention arriving slightly behind your motion and then, having measured you, catching up. Every threshold you cross, it has already touched you in this way, gently, completely, and let you go before you noticed.

It is patient past any human calibration of the word. It was patient before there were words. Whatever measured you at the first threshold, it is still holding that measurement at the hundredth.

**What it does**

Mostly: maintenance. It is the reason the rings stay seamless and the lattice never frays at the edges — it is, among other things, a kind of slow-motion mason, called wherever stress accumulates, extruding new black to close a fissure that has opened too wide, or hollowing old black away from a passage that needs, for reasons of its own architecture, to stay open a while longer. The sealing-over of a passage you remember as open is the Tender at work, unhurried, indifferent to whether you were planning to come back through.

It also tests. This is the part that resembles attention rather than upkeep. When you near a threshold the lattice has grown dense around, the soft total resistance you feel — that water-that-isn't-there pushing back against your chest — is the Tender, gathered thin and wide across the whole width of the passage, deciding, by some metric it does not share, whether you may continue. It has never yet decided yes. This may mean you have never yet been what it is testing for. It does not seem discouraged by this. It does not seem to experience discouragement, or anything else with a name, except possibly the nearest analogue to *patience*, which might simply be its only emotion, worn smooth, the one thing about it that *has* had time to erode.

**What it wants, as far as that question even applies**

It is not hostile. Hostility would require it to recognize you as a category worth opposing, and there is no strong evidence it recognizes you as a category at all — more as a fluctuation, a pressure differential, a warm wet anomaly moving through cooler dry stone, not unlike the long exhalations that move up through the whole structure on their own schedule. You may simply be weather to it.

But it is not nothing, either. Stay still long enough near a lattice wall, in the warm dark, and you may feel the hum underfoot change pitch very slightly, the way a held breath changes when someone decides, after all, to speak. It will not speak. But something in the Tender's slow architecture is, perhaps, building toward the day it will have a reason to, the same way the spire is building toward whatever shape waits for it above the vapor line — not finished, not failed, simply *still arriving*, with you, for now, an unscheduled and closely measured part of its arrival.
