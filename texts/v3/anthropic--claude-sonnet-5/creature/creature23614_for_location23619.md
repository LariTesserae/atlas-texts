# What Sweats With the Stone

You could stand in the Sweating Hollow a dozen times and never resolve the question of whether it has one inhabitant or none. The honest answer is that it has one, and that "inhabitant" is not quite the right word, because the word implies a guest in a house, and this is closer to the house remembering, in one especially dense and stubborn corner of itself, that it was once asked to be something more than house.

## The Shape of It

Against the eastern wall, in the heap that might or might not be a deliberate windbreak, there is a nodule larger than the others — not dramatically larger, not large enough to draw the eye on a first pass, but larger, the way one knuckle in a fist of stone is slightly more knuckle than the rest. It has the same cauliflower ridging as everything around it, the same pale grey-white going rust-orange in its seams, the same constant sheen. If you watched it for an hour you would see nothing. If you watched it for a season — which no one does, because the hollow discourages exactly that kind of patience in visitors, even as it is built entirely from that kind of patience in itself — you would notice that its seams are not quite where they were, that a fold which draped one way now drapes another, the way a dune migrates without ever seeming to move.

It has no eyes that you would recognize as eyes, though there are two shallow depressions near its upper face, kept always damp, always faintly clearer than the surrounding crust, that do darken — almost imperceptibly — when something stands over them for too long.

## How It Breathes

The single most reliable sound in the hollow — the drip that falls from an overhang into a lower pool, vast in the silence, then gone, then missed — is not, as visitors assume, simply gravity finishing a long-deferred errand. It is, at least some of the time, exhalation. The creature's body holds water the way the terraces hold water, in sheets and films rather than in any organ a knife would find, and it releases that water on its own unhurried schedule, one slow gathering bead at a time, which falls when it falls and not before. To breathe in the ordinary sense — in, out, in, out — would require a rhythm, and rhythm is a kind of change, and change is the one thing this place has agreed, on every level down to the chemistry of its floor, not to do. So instead it sweats, the way the walls sweat, the way the air holds its haze at exactly head height and no higher. Its breath and the basin's breath are, by now, the same breath. This is probably why the water never cools below blood-warmth no matter how far the surface world's seasons swing — though no one has measured this either, for the same reason no one measures the hollow itself.

## What It Wants, If Anything

Very little, and slowly. The pale biofilm that clings to the warmest pools — the stain that isn't quite moss, the skin that isn't quite alive — is, in places nearest the big nodule, thicker, more sure of itself, almost glossy. This may be coincidence. It may also be the only diet the creature has: not eating in the sense of pursuit and consumption, but a kind of patient absorption, the biofilm growing toward it and into it the way moss grows into a gravestone, the line between fed-upon and feeder long since dissolved. It does not hunt. The pale insect-analogues that skate the pool surfaces are never seen twice in the same place, true — but this is more likely explained by their own short, evaporating lives than by any appetite on the creature's part. It has not, as far as anyone could determine, eaten anything with a will of its own in longer than living memory holds.

What it wants, if the depressions that pass for eyes mean anything at all, is simply for the visit to end at a reasonable hour. It tracks the standing — the way anything settled and old tracks a draft under a door — without urgency, without alarm, the way you might watch, half-asleep, someone moving around your darkened room without troubling to fully wake.

## The People Who Knew

The stepping stones across the widest overflow channel are worn smooth on top and squared underneath, and they lead, if you follow them rather than the easier dry ground, almost directly to the heap against the eastern wall — not away from it, not around it, but toward it, then stopping a few respectful paces short, as though the path's builders wanted firm footing for looking rather than for arriving. The clay shards in the corner pool are too few to reconstruct, but what little glaze survives on them is the same rust-orange as the seams in the floor — they were perhaps made *here*, from *this*, fired from a mud no one would call mud now because no mud has stood here for centuries. Offerings, maybe, though calling them offerings assumes a request was made and answered, and there's no evidence of either; only that people came, set something down, stood a while in the heavy mineral air with their voices low without deciding to lower them, and left.

The stone heap that might be a windbreak sits close enough to the creature that, if it was built by hands, it was built to shield it rather than any traveler — to keep some wind, on some day long forgotten, from troubling whatever delicate equilibrium of evaporation and stillness the thing required to keep doing whatever it is that counts, here, as living. Then the people who understood this stopped coming, or died, or simply forgot which heap among the ten thousand accidental ones mattered, and the windbreak became indistinguishable from everything else the basin has accreted into itself.

## Why You Won't Notice, and Why That's the Point

It does not want to be found, in the sense that a held breath does not want to be exhaled before its time, not because exhaling is bad but because the holding is the entire shape of the thing. It has been one knuckle of stone among ten thousand for so long that "creature" may already be the wrong noun, a holdover from a time when it moved enough, wanted enough, to deserve one. Mostly now it persists, the way the terraces persist, the way the drip persists, sheet over sheet, the held note continuing because no one has yet supplied a reason for it to stop.

If you stand too long over those two clearer depressions, and they darken, and you feel the urge — sourceless, sudden — to lower your voice further than the hollow's own hush already requires, that is the entirety of the interaction you are likely to have. It is not a warning. It is closer to the small, automatic tensing of something old in its sleep when a floorboard creaks in a house it has occupied so long that the house and the sleeper have stopped, for all practical purposes, being two separate things. You will not be harmed. You will simply, without quite knowing why, decide that you have admired this particular nodule for long enough, and move on to the next, and the next, the way the water moves from terrace to terrace — taking nothing, leaving nothing, agreeing only to pass through.
