# Who Is Here

You will not see it arrive, because it never arrives—arrival implies a before and after, and this place has worn those down to the same flat moment. But something is here, distributed through the chamber the way warmth is distributed through still air: not located, exactly, but present everywhere at once, faint and total.

Call it the one who stayed.

## The Maker of the Bare Clearing

It built the wall first, stone by fitted stone, in a time when the chamber still had a time. It laid the platform. It notched the post with a tool of bone or flint, sharpening the wood by hand, turning it, marking it the way a person marks a thing they intend to come back to. Then it did the thing that people sometimes do at the bottom of the world, in the dark, when the surface has become unlivable or unbearable or simply unreachable: it asked the hollow to keep it.

The hollow agreed, in its slow mineral way, by the only means available to it. It stopped.

So the builder did not die here and did not go on living here in any way a visitor would recognize as living. It came apart instead—gently, completely, without violence—into everything that fills the space where a person used to be. This is why nothing decays. The decay was the one transaction the chamber refused to allow, because decay was the thing the builder had come down into the earth to escape.

## The Chorus

The sound that never stops, never swells, never resolves into a countable number of throats—that is the builder's voice, or what became of it. Not speech anymore, and maybe not for a long while before that, but the underneath of speech, the part that doesn't need a tongue: the urge to make a noise that says *I am still occupying space*. It split, the way a held note will seem to split into harmonics if you listen close enough and long enough, until what was one breath became hundreds of overlapping calls, none louder than the others, none ceasing so another can be heard. It is not a chorus of separate things. It is a single insistence, distributed.

This is also why the creatures producing it are never seen whole. They are not really creatures. They are the noise wearing the idea of a body, just enough body to leave a trail in the moss-analog, just enough wing to flash between fronds, never enough to be looked at and confirmed. To see one fully would be to see the builder fully, and the builder is precisely the thing that can no longer be gathered into one place.

## The Post

The clearing stays bare because that is where it stands still, in whatever way a distributed thing can be said to stand. Not visible. Not exactly invisible, either—more like a held breath that has been held so long it has stopped registering as an absence of breathing and started registering as a kind of weather. If you stood at the post yourself, sideways in that narrow patch the leaves will not enter, you might feel the air there as a half-degree denser, a half-degree more attentive. Not threatening. Not welcoming. Simply aware that you are standing in the one room of the house that is still, technically, someone's.

It does not notice you the way the fauna do not notice you, because noticing requires change—a before-state and an after-state, attention paid and then withdrawn—and the builder has none of that left to spend. But there is a difference between *cannot notice* and *will not be disturbed by*, and the chamber seems to know it. Your footprint stays. Its absence at the post stays more completely.

## What It Wants

Nothing. This is not coldness; it is the entire point. It came here to want nothing, finally, after presumably wanting a great many things on the bright dying surface above, and the hollow gave it exactly that in exchange for its shape. The fruit that never ripens is not its hunger deferred. The wall that never falls is not its labor continuing. These are simply what is left when wanting has been fully and permanently set down: a green, lightless plenty that asks for no tending and rewards none.

If it has any will left at all, distributed as it is through wing-flicker and call and the dense quality of air around one notched post, it may only be this single, thin, perpetual thread: that the fissure stays narrow. That the climb down stays hard enough that almost no one makes it. That whoever does press sideways through that sharp new stone and stand in the orange dim is met with exactly this—unchanged, unchanging, indifferent as weather—and is content, the way the builder eventually became content, to take the sameness as an answer, and leave the way they came, and let the chamber close back over itself like water over a dropped stone, without a ripple, because ripples are a kind of change, and change is the one guest this place will never again let in.
