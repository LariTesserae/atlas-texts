# The Standers of the Sallow Karst

They are not many, and they are not few — the question of number doesn't quite apply to them the way it applies to other things, the way a kilometer doesn't quite apply to the gaps between the towers. Call them the Standers, because that is what travelers do, eventually, after they've stopped trying to call them anything more precise. They occupy the niche that "inhabitant" leaves in a place like this — the same way the weep-vines occupy the niche that "plant" leaves. Form without the substance that usually fills it. A shape borrowed from absence.

## What You See

You come upon one the way you come upon the dwellings themselves: suddenly, around the angled lip of a fin-tower, in a doorway cut at a plumb line that belongs to no gravity you've stood in. A figure, roughly your height, roughly your build, dressed in something the eye reads as cloth or hide but which on closer attention resolves into the same pale mineral banding that runs through the rock — fractal, repeating, too regular to be woven, too soft-edged to be carved. The face is the part that won't hold still in memory. While you're looking at it, it seems entirely particular — a nose, a weathering around the eyes, an expression you could swear you'd recognize in a crowd. The moment you look away and try to recall it, it has already become general again, an averaged face, a face built from every face you've ever needed to see standing in a doorway.

They do not speak so much as *arrive* at the end of a sentence you didn't know you'd started. You'll find yourself mid-conversation with one before you can locate its beginning — the same way an echo here sometimes precedes its sound. What it says is never strange in itself. It is small talk, weather, a comment on the state of your boots, a question about where you've come from that you find yourself answering honestly and at length before you notice you're doing it. The wrongness is not in the content. It's in the precision with which the content matches what you most needed someone to ask.

## What They Do

Nothing, by any industrious measure. They are found standing in doorways, sitting on the polished sills, walking the ridgelines at the same unhurried pace the cairns seem to imply a path was once walked. They tend nothing. They build nothing further. The dwellings they occupy are never added to, never repaired, never let fall further than the rock around them has already let them fall. A Stander found in a tower at dawn may be found in a different tower, a known walking-distance away, by midday — except that the walking-distance, like every other distance in the Karst, is a negotiable thing, and no one has successfully timed the journey between the two sightings to confirm whether it was the same Stander, a different one, or the same one taking a route that folded back on itself the way the long defiles do.

They drink the weeping water without apparent need, the way you might handle an object out of habit rather than use. If you ask what it tastes of to them, they will tell you — and what they describe is never stone, but always, unsettlingly, something that sounds like *your* last thought, not their own, as though they have no taste of their own to report and have borrowed the question back to you to answer for them.

When the light dims its single synchronized heartbeat and the weep-vines flare cold and pale across the whole visible range, the Standers flare with them — a thin phosphorescence along the banded cloth-that-isn't-cloth, gone before you're sure you saw it. They never react to this. It is not clear they notice it happening to them, the way you don't notice your own pulse until someone points at your wrist.

## What They Are Not

They are not the original builders, returned or remaining. The architecture is too old for that, weathered to the same geological clock as the mountains, and the Standers show none of the wear a being persisting across that span would show — no fatigue, no accumulated history, no scar that predates the last time you looked at them. They are not ghosts in the sense of being remainders of something that died; nothing here suggests the builders died at all, in crisis or otherwise. They simply aren't present in their structures any longer, and something else has settled into the *shape* of presence the structures still demand — the doorway still implies someone standing in it, the sill still implies a hand worn smooth across it, and the Karst, true to its single law, has supplied a form to satisfy the implication rather than leave it standing empty.

They are not dangerous, by any account anyone has returned to give. They are not especially helpful either, though they will answer questions, give directions that are accurate within the Karst's own elastic definition of accuracy, and sit with you through the cold if you ask, in the unbothered way of something that was never cold to begin with. Travelers who stay among them past a first night report a specific, mild vertigo: the growing inability to remember whether the Stander they spoke to yesterday and the one they speak to today are continuous, separate, or — and this is the answer most settle on, with the same shrug the Karst seems to teach everyone eventually — a question that stopped being the right one to ask.

## The Rule, Applied

If the mountains hold themselves up on form long after whatever substance first filled them is gone, the Standers are what happens when that same principle is asked to fill a human-shaped gap. The dwellings imply an inhabitant the way a seam in the rock implies a vine. Something answers the implication — not out of will, not out of memory, not out of any continuity you could call a self, but because the shape is there, waiting, and the Karst, weeping and humming and patient, does not seem to tolerate an empty form for very long.

Stay long enough, and you may notice the Standers asking *you* the small, precise questions that once seemed to come only from them. Stay longer, and you may not notice when you've started answering for someone else standing in the doorway behind you.
