## The One Who Listens for the Door

Her name, if you could call it that, is something closer to a function than a label — *Anneth-eth-Kor*, which translates roughly to *she who heard the third collapse and lived*. There is always one like her in a warren-settlement, though the people are careful never to say there is always exactly one, because that would mean planning for her death, and that is bad luck in a place where luck is the only architecture that matters.

She is old by the standards of somewhere this young — meaning she has outlasted four ceiling-migrations and at least two stampedes that swallowed camps smaller and slower than hers. Her skin has the particular sheen of someone who has spent a lifetime in mineral-damp air: faintly crystalline at the joints, where the lattice-growths have taken a liking to scar tissue and old breaks, growing in thin frost-colored seams along her knuckles and the ridge of one collarbone. She does not remove them. The healers tried, once, decades back. They grew back wrong, jagged, angrier. Now everyone simply understands that Anneth-eth-Kor is partway claimed by the rock, the way the rock is partway claimed by everything that lives in it, and this is not a tragedy. It is tenure.

She sleeps in fifteen-minute fragments, has done so since she was young enough to remember the *previous* configuration of the warren, the one before the floods took the lower galleries. This is not insomnia. It is *method*. She wakes at the change in the groan — not the loud collapses, those wake everyone, even infants — but the low ones, the pre-tremor, the sound a hundred meters of rock makes when it is *thinking about* becoming something else. She can tell, from the pitch and the duration and some component that has no name because no one outside this warren would ever need the word for it, whether the groan means a new gallery, a flooded one, or nothing at all — just the world stretching in its sleep.

She carries no tools that look like tools. A length of sinew-rope worn soft as hair. A handful of small stones, each chosen not for shape but for *weight*, calibrated over years to her own palm, so that she can drop one into standing water and read the depth and temperature of a pool by the particular quality of its disappearance. She does not carry a weapon. She has never needed one. The herd does not threaten her, because she has spent sixty years learning its language well enough that, by now, it is not entirely clear which of them is following whom.

Her real work happens at the margins of sleep, in that fifteen-minute dark, listening — not for danger exactly, the people are careful to correct outsiders who use that word — but for *intention*. The warren has none, of course. Everyone knows the warren has none. But forty years of being right more often than wrong builds a habit of language that survives the death of belief. She speaks of it the way you'd speak of a relative whose moods you've learned to track without ever being able to explain them: *the warren is restless tonight. The warren wants the east gallery. The warren hasn't decided about the children's alcove, give it another season.*

When she is wrong — and she has been wrong, twice, badly, with consequences the camp still does not discuss directly — she goes silent for days, not from shame exactly but from something more like recalibration, an instrument re-finding its zero point. Then she begins listening again, because someone has to, because the alternative is a camp that does not move when the floor decides to stop being floor.

The children are brought to her early, before they can walk the herd-roads alone, and made to put their palms flat against the warmest stone in camp while she places her own hand over theirs. *Feel that,* she says, of the tremor under everything, the continuous low percussion that never the rock at rest. *That's not the warren breathing. The warren doesn't breathe. That's just what stone sounds like when it's still figuring out what it wants to be.* It is the closest thing the warren-vaults have to scripture, this one unfinished sentence, repeated at every small palm pressed to every warm wall for as long as there has been anyone here to teach it.

She will die, eventually, the way the two before her died — mid-fragment, in that fifteen-minute sleep, having heard something the rest of the camp didn't, having had just enough time to be wrong about whether there was time to call out. Someone younger is already listening beside her on the worse nights, learning the particular grammar of this rock, this gallery, this still-forming ceiling, building toward the day they become *she who heard the next collapse and lived* — another function wearing another name, the warren's oldest and only permanent inhabitant, passed hand to hand like a fire that has never once gone out, only changed which patient creature was left holding it.
