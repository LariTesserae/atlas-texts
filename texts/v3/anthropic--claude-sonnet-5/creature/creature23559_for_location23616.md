It is there, near the third stone-rise from what would be west if the place observed compass points, in a fold of the rim where the cracked mineral rock makes a shallow lean-to of itself. You would not see it as separate from the rock for a long time, longer than you'd be willing to stand there, because it has stopped trying to look like anything other than what the basin already is — pale, dry, the color of old bone left in clay. It is roughly the shape of a person who has decided, with enormous patience, to become a boulder, and is most of the way through deciding.

It does not have a face in any sense that would resolve under the bruise-grey light. There is a forward part, and that forward part holds two shallow declivities of its own, small basins mirroring the larger one, and in long-spaced moments — you would have to be very still, and very lucky, or very unlucky — something like attention gathers in them, the way dust gathers in a cupped hand. It is looking. It has always been looking. It is in no hurry to finish.

Its limbs, if the word still applies, fork the way the leafless woody plants fork: a decision made once, long ago, and kept. They rest against the rock-rise in a configuration it settled into so far back that "settling" is the wrong tense — it would be more accurate to say the configuration and the creature arrived together, finished at the same moment, the way the basin and its shape arrived together. It breathes. You would not catch it. The chest, or the place a chest would be, rises perhaps once for every several of the rare ticking flakes that let go from the rim-rock and fall into the gravel — and you could mistake the two sounds for each other, the small shift of its body and the small shift of the stone, because at this scale they are siblings.

It made the flakes. Long ago, in a now that has since cooled into permanence, it took up the darker nodule of rock and struck it, slowly, with the unhurried percussive patience of something that does not distinguish between an afternoon and a millennium, and made small sharp things, and then set them down, and did not need them again, and did not leave. The tools are not evidence of a visit. They are evidence of a single, immensely long sitting, of which this is still the middle.

It does not hunt, because there is nothing to hunt and no real hunger, only a banked warmth in it that matches the banked warmth of the ground, the two of them sharing a low, contented temperature like coals that gave up the idea of going out. It does not call out, because the silence here is not a silence it is straining against — it is a silence it helped make, by being a thing that, having arrived, declined to keep happening in any way you could chart. Where the basin found its final shape and stopped, the creature found its final posture and stopped with it, in the same hour, by the same logic, as if shape and stillness were a single substance and the land simply had more of it to spend.

If you waited — if you were willing to wait on the basin's own terms, which is to say without any confidence that waiting means what it usually means — you might see it shift. A forking limb settling fractionally closer to the rock-rise. The shallow declivities of its face turning, by some hairline of arc, toward where you stood, or where you had stood, sometime previous, in a now that has not yet caught up with you. It is not aware of you the way a thing aware of prey or threat is aware. It is aware of you the way the basin is aware of a footprint: as a fact that has entered its long held breath, and will, eventually, become part of what it has been all along.

It will not finish deciding to become a boulder. That is not its purpose. Its purpose, if the word survives translation into this place's terms, is to be the one thing in the basin slow enough to still be called alive by some technical, generous measure — to hold, in its banked and patient body, the very last flicker of the distinction between stone and something that once chose to be still, so that the distinction does not vanish from the world entirely, not yet, not on any timescale you or it could witness.
