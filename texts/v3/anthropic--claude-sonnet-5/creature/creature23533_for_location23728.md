**What Lives Here**

Most of what moves on the shelf is easy to name, if not to classify — the low many-jointed runners, quick and twitchy, their too-many legs each finding purchase a half-second after the last like a thought catching up with itself; the wet-cloth flocks overhead, snapping in and out of solidity as they bank above the haze; small blind grazers that work the moss-cushions with mouths like wrung cloth, pulsing in time with what they eat. These are the loud, easy citizens of the place, abundant past all reason, busy with appetite and territory the way fauna everywhere is busy. They are not what you came to ask about. They are the noise the real thing moves under.

The real thing leaves tracks at the misted margins and almost nowhere else — a wide, dragging print, four-point but uneven, as if its weight keeps deciding where to settle. It booms. The deep notes that seem to rise out of the stone itself, the ones that silence the chorus for a few held seconds before the small voices dare to rebuild — those are not geology. Geology only sounds like that because something has spent a long time learning to sound like geology, on purpose, the way a thing hides by agreeing with its surroundings.

Call it, for lack of anything truer, the **mason** — though it has never been seen to build with intention, only with proximity. Picture a body the size of a draft animal, low and broad, its hide the same raw, seam-sharp grey as the rock it crosses, beaded with the same uncaused moisture, so that at twenty meters in the dim light it reads as outcrop until it shifts its weight and the outcrop turns out to have a spine. Its limbs are thick and stub-jointed, ending not in claws but in flat, warm, faintly humming pads — the same texture, the same temperature, as the seamless installations grown into the rock. It is not clear which came first, the pads or the structures. It is not clear there's a meaningful difference.

It keeps to the edges, where visibility drops to a few meters, because the open plateau is for the small loud things and it has no interest in being counted. It travels slowly, in long loops between the black frames and the floor-fragments and the squat embedded hum-things, and when it reaches one it stops, and presses — flank, or pad, or the broad flat plate of its skull — against the structure for long minutes, not eating, not scenting, just leaning, the way a tired animal leans on a fence it didn't build but has come to trust. The structures warm faintly where it touches them. They have been seen, on the visits after, fractionally displaced — an archway rotated a few degrees, a hexagonal tile-floor extended by one more row into the raw stone, as though something had been quietly, patiently adding to a thing it does not understand any more than a bird understands the aerodynamics of the nest it is building entirely from itch.

It does not appear to eat the moss or hunt the runners. What it takes, if it takes anything, may be the fields themselves — the ambient hum, the bent light, whatever physics the plateau is privately running on, drawn in through those warm flat pads the way a root draws water. Its booming call, the one that arrives from inside the rock and stops every other voice on the shelf, may not be communication at all. It may simply be the sound of it discharging — too much half-finished world passing through too patient a body.

You will likely not see it directly. You will feel its weight in the ground a moment before the booming starts, find its drag-print at the soft misted edge of your path, notice a structure standing very slightly differently than it stood the last time you looked. The small loud ecosystem will go on regardless, abundant and confident and indifferent to the larger thing moving underneath its noise — biology arrived and comfortable, as the place itself seems to be, going about its ordinary business while something older than the noise and younger than the stone keeps making its slow rounds, leaning its warmth against each unfinished structure in turn, adding without architecture, tending without plan, a single patient custodian for a world still being poured.
