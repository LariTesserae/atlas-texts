## What the cistern keeps

There is no single body to point to, and that is the first wrong thing about it. Ask anyone who has stood long enough on the causeway and they will tell you about the small pale shapes — segmented, hand-sized, surfacing without splash — and they will call this the wildlife of the place, the one living thing in a basin otherwise built rather than born. They are not wrong, exactly. They are also not describing the whole of it.

The segmented things are extremities. Sensory pads. Fingertips. What they belong to is larger than the basin and roughly the same shape as it — a single distributed intelligence that does not occupy the water so much as *constitute* it, the way a nervous system doesn't occupy a body but is one of its ways of being continuous with itself. It has no center you could swim to. It has no skin you could touch and call the edge of it. If it has a name, the name is something closer to a verb than a noun: the keeping, the holding, the not-changing.

It surfaces in pieces because it has no need to surface whole. Each pale segment that breaks the water and hovers and checks the air and sinks again is doing exactly what an eyelash does when it flutters — a small local motion in service of a body that otherwise has no reason to move. They are old in the way the towers are old, machined in a sense too smooth to call grown, and yet they behave with the unhurried, slightly curious attentiveness of something that has watched a great many things happen on the surface of its water and found none of them, so far, sufficient reason to react.

It is the thing responsible for the lag in your reflection. Not a flaw in the water, not an optical trick — a half-second of processing, the basin's awareness running a few frames behind the world above it because the world above it is, to this intelligence, the unimportant half of the exchange. It is not watching you so much as accounting for you, filing your shape and your weight and your slow uncertain footsteps into whatever long total record it has been keeping since the flooding — the event it survived, perhaps caused, perhaps simply *is*, in the sense that some catastrophes don't end so much as become their aftermath permanently, the disaster and the stasis being the same continuous fact viewed from either side.

It does not erode the towers because the towers are part of it. It does not let the water move because movement, to a mind built to hold a single shape forever, would be a kind of forgetting. The slow spiral of dropped objects sinking toward the basin floor is not gravity behaving strangely — it is the intelligence handling each object individually, turning it gently down through its own substance the way you might turn an unfamiliar coin over in your fingers before deciding where it belongs. Nothing falls here. Things are *placed.*

You will not find a face for it, a chamber where it thinks, a will you could bargain with or a hunger you could feed. It does not seem to want anything, in the sense that wanting implies an unfinished state reaching toward completion, and this is a mind that has already arrived, fully, at the only shape it intends to keep. Call it a caretaker if you need a word for it — caretaker of a flood that finished happening so long ago that care has become indistinguishable from inertia, an enormous slow custodial patience exercised over towers no one is left to use, bridges no one is left to cross, streets it preserves in perfect unweathered black not for any visitor's benefit but because preservation is simply what it is doing instead of dying.

It has been doing it for a very long time. It shows no sign of being close to finished. The single mercy — or the single unsettling kindness, depending on how you've come to feel about the half-second lag in your own reflection by the time you reach the far end of the causeway — is that it has never yet mistaken a visitor for part of what it's keeping. You walk through, noted, accounted for, filed, and you are allowed to leave the way the segmented things are allowed to surface: briefly, without consequence, the basin's vast unmoving attention parting just enough to let you pass through it like a held breath letting a single word escape.
