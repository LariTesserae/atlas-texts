There is one of it, and that singularity feels correct the moment you understand it — a place this size couldn't hold more than one of anything large, and what lives here is large, in its way, though you might walk past its presence twice before recognizing it as a body rather than a feature of the terrain.

It lives in the largest pool, the one fed by the scaled pipe, and most of the time it is indistinguishable from the pool's rim — a long low ridge of crust-colored plate, segmented, the segments overlapping like roof tiles, rust and sulfur-yellow in exactly the shades the mineral deposits take, because the deposits are in fact partly made of it, sloughed and re-incorporated, the boundary between the creature's exterior and the basin's geology genuinely unclear even on close inspection. It is long — twelve, fifteen meters, coiled in on itself two or three times in the pool's modest diameter, which is the only reason it fits in something so small. It has had to fold to live here.

It does not surface so much as it intermittently stops pretending not to be there. The plates along its back lift and resettle on a cycle, and if you watch long enough you notice the cycle isn't its own — it's timed, unmistakably, to the throb from the structure on the ridge. Each pulse of the pump corresponds to a slight lift and exhalation along the creature's length, water sheeting off the raised plates, a row of pale slits beneath each plate opening to vent its own thin curl of steam into the basin's general haze, indistinguishable from the ambient condensation unless you already know to look. It has synchronized itself, or been synchronized, to a machine. Whether it adapted to the rhythm or whether the rhythm was originally set to match a metabolism already present — whether the pipe was driven into the mud because something down there was already doing something worth tapping — is not a question the basin answers. The structure predates nothing visibly; the creature looks neither young nor built, only old in the unbothered way of things that have outlasted several different reasons for existing.

It has no eyes that you can find, only a faint paler patterning at what's presumably the head end, symmetrical smudges under the plate where eyes might once have mattered, vestigial in the manner of something that gave up seeing long before it gave up the equipment for it. It doesn't need to see; the basin has nothing to flee and nothing to hunt that requires sight. What it does instead, constantly, unhurriedly, is taste the mud — fine cilia-fringed openings along its underside working the saturated black earth as it shifts its coils, drawing up whatever mineral-rich solution the pipe is pushing down from above, processing it through some slow internal chemistry, the byproduct of which is, apparently, more of itself: more plate, more crust, a body that grows by the same process that grows the basin's rim, so that in some sense it has been quietly manufacturing the very walls that contain it, ring by ring, for longer than anyone has been present to notice.

The pipe is not feeding it, exactly, or not only — the relationship runs both directions. Something is drawn up out of the pool through that scaled conduit on the same cycle that the creature vents, and the obvious, uncomfortable inference is that the structure on the ridge is not maintaining the basin's geothermal process at all but harvesting the creature's output, has been doing so on full automatic since whoever installed the pump stopped being present to check the gauges, the entire arrangement now running not because anyone wants it to but because neither party — pump nor animal — has any mechanism by which to stop.

It doesn't react to a person standing at the basin's edge. It has likely never needed a category for that. If you waded in — which the steam and the crumbling mineral crust make instinctively unappealing — you would feel it before identifying it, a warmth under the mud denser and more directional than the basin's general heat, a faint pulse against the sole of the foot a half-second ahead of the structure's audible throb, as if the machine were the echo and the creature the source. It would not move away. Whatever urgency it once had has been worn down into pure maintenance, a long patient body doing the one slow thing it does, breathing in time with a pump it has never seen, in a pool it has outgrown so gradually that growing has come to feel, to it, indistinguishable from simply continuing.
