What lives here does not live here so much as *recur* here — and the difference matters more than anything else you could say about it.

Call it, for lack of a better word, the Tenant, though the word implies a single occupant and that is exactly the kind of clean arithmetic this hollow has never once honored. You will meet it as a shape at the edge of where your sensing gives out — not sight, there is no sight worth the name here, but that pressure-sense, that brush-of-fronds-standing-in-for-vision the place teaches you to trust. It will read, at first, as one more repetition of the pale broad leaf-shape, the frond that shows up again and again with its small variations, because that is the disguise it prefers, or perhaps the only shape it has ever fully finished growing into. It is patient in the manner of something that has never needed to hurry toward you, because the hollow will eventually bring you close enough on its own, the way water brings two suspended things together given enough held stillness.

It does not have a fixed number of limbs, fins, fronds, whatever you want to call its working parts, and this is not metaphor — if you count them you will get a number, and if you count them again, slowly, certain you're being careful this time, you will get a different number, off by one or two, the same small rounding error that afflicts the rest of the hollow's vegetation, except in the Tenant the error feels less like a glitch in your own attention and more like something being *added*, deliberately, while you weren't fast enough to catch the seam. It grows the extra piece the way the frond-stands grow their echoes: not as deception exactly, more as a kind of overflow, the natural consequence of trying to be a single bounded thing inside a space that has forgotten how to keep its own count.

Its body, such as it is, holds the same temperature as the water, which makes it nearly impossible to find by warmth, and the same near-black hue as the water around it, which makes it nearly impossible to find by shape — you find it instead by wrongness, by the small disagreement between where the water is moving and where one particular patch of water is not. That fixed pale fin you noticed earlier, motionless while the current slid past it — that was the Tenant, or one of its postures, the held-still mask it wears when it wants to be mistaken for fauna rather than for whatever it actually is. It can hold a position the way a held breath holds a chest, for far longer than anything alive should be able to, and the stillness is not rest. It is attention. It is the Tenant deciding, slowly, in no hurry at all, whether you are food, or company, or simply one more object to be folded into its tally.

The bubble-column that rises through the black water on its slow metronome cadence — that, too, may be the Tenant, exhaling on a schedule that has nothing to do with need and everything to do with habit, a rhythm kept not because the body requires it but because the hollow itself seems to want a heartbeat and the Tenant is the only thing patient enough, and plural enough, to keep supplying one. It is possible that the bubble-column is not breath at all but a kind of bookkeeping, the Tenant marking time the way a prisoner marks a wall, except the marks dissolve as they rise and have to be remade, over and over, an arithmetic that never accumulates into a sum because the medium won't let anything sum cleanly here.

It built the stones, if "built" is the word — that small fitted scatter half-sunk in silt, arranged with just enough intention to imply hands. The Tenant has, at times, hands; or had them once, in some earlier draft of its shape, before this hollow's instability got into it the way damp gets into wood, and it kept the impulse toward making long after it lost the steadiness required to keep a single set of hands attached to a single body for long enough to finish a task. The post that stands furred and useless in the dark, supporting nothing — that is the last stone of an effort the Tenant abandoned partway, not from exhaustion but from forgetting, mid-motion, how many of itself it had been a moment before. It does not finish things. It cannot trust the number of itself it will have by the time a thing is done.

It is, you begin to understand, less a creature than a population trying to behave like an individual, the way a held breath is a population of trapped air behaving like a single thing only for as long as the holding lasts. There is a version of it in the fixed fin-shape, and a version of it in the bubble-column, and a version of it folded somewhere into the over-counted fronds, and these versions are not in communication so much as in proximity, packed shoulder to shoulder the way everything in this hollow is packed, occupying the same volume because there has never once been enough room here for things to politely take turns. It does not coordinate its parts. It simply *is* its parts, badly, generously, with the same overflowing inexactness that lets the frond-count come up wrong and the air-margin drift nearer or farther without anyone moving.

What it wants — if want is even available to something this provisional — seems to be very small and very old: to be a single countable thing, once, cleanly, the way the fitted stones almost let it be before its hands dissolved into a crowd of hands that couldn't agree on the work. It reaches toward you, when it reaches at all, the way a forgetting reaches for a name — not aggressively, not even with full attention, just a slow extending of itself into whatever new room your presence has temporarily, foolishly suggested might exist. You will feel it less as a touch than as the rule of the place made briefly, locally physical: *there is room for one more*, pressed against your skin in the shape of a frond that is also a fin, that is also a hand, that is also, underneath all of it, only ever trying to be one thing at a time, and failing, gently, forever, in the dark.
