# What Dwells in the Sweating Niche

## The Ticking Thing

Call it that, because no one — no one who has come back to tell of it — has had a name ready when they felt it cross their wrist in the dark.

It is small. Smaller than a thought given weight. Its body is the same temperature as the rock, the same temperature as the air, the same temperature, near enough, as blood — which means it can rest against your skin for a long moment before you register that something has touched you at all. It has no eyes, but this should not be read as deprivation; eyes are for places with edges, and this place stopped having edges to see by when the dark closed over it and never thought to lift again. What it has instead, ringing its blunt head like a second mouth, is a crown of unossified knobs — the held promise of eyes, eyes the way the stalactite-stubs are the held promise of stalactites — sensitive to a kind of heat-shadow, a guess at where the warm water pools and where the warmer flesh of its own kind might be.

It does not grow. This is the thing to understand about it, the thing that makes it native to this place rather than merely tolerant of it: somewhere in the soft architecture of its body, the same arrest that stopped the rock's long patient grinding has stopped its own slow patient aging. It hatched — if hatched is even the word, if there was ever an egg, rather than some quieter mineral parturition in one of the still basins — at the size it is now, and it will tick across the stone at that size for as long as the niche itself declines to finish becoming. It does not mate, so far as anyone has witnessed, though there must be more than one, because more than one geometry of scrabble-print has been felt in the wet dark by the hands that have come here. Perhaps it simply persists, the way the water in the basins persists, neither filling nor draining, a population held at a fixed and modest number the way the heat is held at a fixed and modest fever.

It eats the pale filament-growths, the leafless fungal-adjacent things in the mineral seams, and is in turn — very rarely, very quietly — eaten by nothing, because nothing else lives here large enough to want it. This is an ecology with one rung. It does not need another.

## The One Who Leaves the Stone

But there is, too, the other kind of visitor — rarer by orders the ticking thing cannot count, since it does not count, since nothing here counts except perhaps whatever folded the basalt in the first place.

Call this one the Depositor, for lack of better, since "pilgrim" implies a destination chosen and "hermit" implies a staying, and this visitor does neither. It comes — once, as far as the worked stone and the single ash-ghost suggest, though "once" might mean once per visitor across some long procession of visitors rather than once in all of time — through the crawl of cooling basalt, into the heat, into the wet, carrying a tool shaped by hands much like the hands you'd recognize, and it sets that tool into a basin, or scratches a mark into a nodule, or burns a small fire whose smoke has long since stopped meaning anything, and then it goes.

It does not stay to watch what becomes of the mark. It cannot, because nothing becomes of it; that is the whole reason it was safe to leave a mark here at all. Whatever this visitor is — and the descriptions, such as they exist, are unreliable, more felt-shape than seen-shape, more warmth-against-warmth than testimony — it seems to come for exactly this: a place where a single human gesture, a single human object, can be set down and trusted to remain a single human gesture forever. Not preserved the way a museum preserves, with case and label and the violence of explanation. Preserved the way a held breath preserves the shape of the lungs that held it — by refusing, for now, to exhale.

No one knows if the Depositor is one being returning across some impossible span, or many beings, each finding the same crawl-space through some shared and wordless inheritance, each leaving their single object and turning back toward the light without ever knowing another had done the same in some niche-eternity before them. The worked stones do not accumulate in a pile that would answer the question. They simply sit, each in its own basin, each waiting at the exact moment of having-just-been-placed, the way the rock waits at the exact moment of having-just-cooled.

## What the Two Have in Common

Between the ticking thing that never grows and the Depositor who never returns to see what becomes of what they left, there is a single shared fact, and it may be the only law that governs who or what is permitted to live in the Sweating Niche at all: nothing here is allowed to *become*. The ticking thing is forever newly hatched. The Depositor's mark is forever newly made. The rock is forever newly cooled. 

Whatever this place is for — shrine, womb, wound, threshold — it seems to exist specifically to hold its inhabitants at the single warm wet breathless instant just after arrival, refusing, with the same patience it refuses erosion, to let any of them go on to whatever they were about to become next.
