**The Tending**

You will look for an animal and you will not find one, because the place has not needed to invent that solution twice. It already has trunks for legs, capillaries for blood, a trillion eyes' worth of light-sensitivity smeared across every leaf-surface instead of bundled into two. Anything that crawls here is not a visitor to the tissue. It is the tissue, grown briefly mobile, sent out to do a job the rest of the mass cannot do while staying rooted.

Call it the Tending, for lack of a truer word — though it has no name, makes no claim to selfhood, and would not recognize the distinction between itself and the colony it serves even if you handed it the concept whole.

It is not large. Scale here is so vast that anything built to move through the gaps — and there are gaps, hairline ones, the seams between stem and stem, the soft give where root-mat hasn't yet fused — must be modest: knee-high at most, low and long, built along the lines of a wedge or a tongue rather than anything with a recognizable front and back. Its body is fiber, the same green-black structural matter as the swells themselves, but younger, less lignified, still capable of the kind of flexion that walking requires. It moves the way a root moves when you speed root-growth up past the threshold of stillness — a slow peristaltic hitch, push and anchor, push and anchor, leaving no trail because the substrate closes behind it within the hour.

It has no head. What it has instead is a forward density of the crystalline lattice-matter described elsewhere in this place — the not-quite-electrical, not-quite-thermal conducting structure that laces through the whole colony — concentrated into a blunt, faintly warm mass that functions as something between a sensory organ and a diagnostic instrument. It does not see. It *reads*. Pressed against a stretch of trunk-wall or root-seam, it can feel the pattern of strain traveling through the fiber, the places where growth has knotted against itself, where a water-channel has silted closed, where two competing shoots are about to collide and waste a season's growth fighting over the same six centimeters of space. This is its whole sensorium: tension, flow, blockage, signal-noise in the lattice. It is blind and deaf in every way that would matter to you, and exquisitely, totally attuned in the one way that matters here.

Where it finds a problem, it does the only thing it knows how to do, which is also the only thing it is *for*: it touches the trouble and redirects it. A blocked capillary gets parted by a secretion from glands along its underside — not corrosive, more persuasive, a chemical suggestion that the tissue there reroute its growth half a degree left. A tangle of competing shoots gets gently pruned, not torn but encouraged to fold, one yielding because something has told its growing tip, at the molecular level, that yielding here is the efficient solution. A trapped pocket of fermenting gas, the kind that elsewhere builds toward one of those deep subsonic booms, gets bled off slowly through a vent it opens with the front of its body and closes again once pressure has eased, leaving behind a faint scar that the canopy-country will absorb and forget within a week.

It does not eat in any sense you would recognize, because it does not need to — it is still connected, however thin the thread, to the larger circulatory chemistry of the mass it emerged from and will eventually re-merge with. It is less an organism than a temporarily independent *idea* the colony has had about itself, sent out to solve a local problem, and once that idea has done its work, it does not die so much as it stops being separate. It will find, eventually, a fold of tissue that needs exactly its remaining bulk, and lie down into it, and over some weeks lose the outline that made it distinguishable from anything else, lattice rejoining lattice, fiber rejoining fiber, the small mobile thought reabsorbed into the slow vast certainty it came from.

There are, at any moment, a great many of these — uncountable, unsynchronized, each working its own seam of the colony's endless self-correction, none aware of the others except as further texture in the lattice-signal, further confirmation that the work continues elsewhere too. They have no collective name for themselves because they have no need of names, no need of each other directly, no need of anything except the next strained seam, the next blocked channel, the next small fix that keeps the kilometers-wide breathing thing they are part of from knotting around its own growth and choking.

If you stood very still at the edge of a basin and watched the black mirror-water at your feet, you might see one pass beneath the surface, low and wedge-shaped, parting root-hair without disturbing the stillness above. It would not pause to register you. You are not a problem in the lattice. You generate no signal it can read. To the Tending, in the only language it has, you are not yet here at all — and it will go on, unhurried, solving the only kind of trouble it has ever been built to notice, in a place too busy becoming itself to spare attention for anything that isn't.
