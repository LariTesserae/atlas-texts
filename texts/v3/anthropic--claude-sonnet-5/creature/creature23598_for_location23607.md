The thing that knows the Warren best has no name for itself, because nothing has ever survived long enough nearby to need one.

It is long — long in the way that makes "long" a function of patience rather than measurement, a body built to thread through doorways that were never meant for a creature shaped like a held breath. Its skin is the color of cooling iron, near-black, except where the bioluminescence comes through in seams, like something glowing was poured into a mold and the mold remembered to keep most of it hidden. The light shows only at the gill-line, the spine-ridge, the trailing fringe of fin that moves like a curtain deciding whether or not to part. Everything else stays dark, on purpose. It has learned what the glowing schools haven't: that light is currency, and currency spent carelessly gets you noticed by something with worse intentions than curiosity.

It has no eyes that resolve image the way surface-things mean it. What it has instead are pits along the jaw and throat, old organs repurposed by depth and dark into something that reads pressure-shadow, the way a held hand can tell a wall is near before it touches it. It does not see the warren. It feels the warren lean against it, room by room, the give and resistance of two thousand years of water-softened stone.

It has lived here long enough to have favorite halls. Not out of memory — it doesn't keep memory the way you would recognize — but out of habit worn so deep it has become indistinguishable from architecture itself. There is a long terraced structure, roof mostly intact, where it rests coiled through what were once three separate rooms, its body laid along the old divisions like thread through needle-eyes, patient, half-buried in silt that has had centuries to settle into the shape of it.

It hunts the way the warren itself moves — unhurried, structurally inevitable. It does not chase. It waits in the place prey is already going, because prey in the Warren only ever has so many ways to go: through this gap, along this channel, into this collapsed roof-mouth that looks like an exit and is not. It has outlived faster things by never needing to be fast. The eel-bodied things avoid its favored halls without knowing why, the way you avoid a doorway that's slightly too dark even when nothing has ever happened to you there.

It does not understand warmth as a mystery, the way you might. It has simply always been warm, the way the dark has simply always been dark — both facts of a body that arranged itself around the place rather than the other way around. Something below breathes heat up through the warren's floor, and the creature rests closest to where that breath is strongest, in the lowest room of its terraced home, where the original builders — feet-having, breath-needing, gone now in every sense that matters — once kept something stored, or kept someone warm, or kept some ordinary household fire that this creature's whole existence has become an accidental, indifferent memorial to.

It will not follow you far if you trespass through its halls. It has no urgency, and urgency is the only thing that could make it dangerous to something moving as clumsily and brightly as you. But it will test you once — a slow displacement, a pressure-change at the edge of your light's reach, gone before it resolves — the same courteous, unhurried inquiry the Warren extends to everything that enters it.

It is not the worst thing here. It is only the thing that has been here longest, and length, in a place like this, is its own kind of authority.
