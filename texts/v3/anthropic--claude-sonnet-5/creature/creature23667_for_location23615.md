What lives here does not live in the way that word usually means — it *persists*, and persistence is a different verb, with different muscles.

You will see one eventually, if "eventually" means anything in a place where eventually never quite arrives. It comes down through the canopy gap, where the held afternoon light pools, moving the way silt moves in still water: not flying, not falling, simply *occurring* downward. Call it a tender, for lack of a truer word, because that is what it does, ceaselessly, to the drip that will not drip and the moment that will not pass.

Its body reads, at first glance, as something assembled rather than grown — a torso like a kiln, dull orange at the seams where heat should be and isn't, ribbed the way a bellows is ribbed, expanding and contracting on a breath that never completes its cycle in either direction. You watch it inhale and you will still be watching when you decide to stop watching. The skin, where it is skin, is the same wet, young, unfinished texture as the stone shelf itself — slick, dark, faintly steaming without being warm — and it shares the rock's appetite for sharp, uncured edges; you would cut yourself on its shoulder blade if you were ever fool enough to embrace it, and the cut, you suspect, would also simply hover, deciding.

It has too many limbs to be efficient and too few to be a spider, the surplus folded close against the kiln-ribs like tools racked and waiting, each tipped not in claw or hand but in something closer to a stopcock, a valve, a small attentive nozzle that it touches, unhurried, to the beads of condensation on the undersides of leaves — not drinking them, not gathering them, just *touching*, the way you'd touch a held note to confirm it is still being held. Where it touches, the bead does not fall. This may be cause. This may be effect. The tender does not seem concerned with the distinction.

Its face, if you can call it that, is the part that unsettles longest: a wide, soft, almost domestic muzzle, faintly mammalian, eyes set deep and slow-blinking, an expression of profound and uncomplicated patience — the face of something that has been told to wait and has never once resented the instruction. It does not look at you so much as register you the way the leaves register the mist: as ambient, as ongoing. From its open mouth comes, very faintly, threaded under its breathing, the same drone that the mountain hums — not an echo of the engine-sound, you realize, but its *source*, or one of its sources, the industrial bass note originating somewhere behind the soft palate and traveling down through the kiln-ribbed chest into the stone it stands on, into the roots of the sparse trees, outward into the held hour of the grove.

There is never only one. You will, if you stay long enough — and length, here, is a courtesy the place extends rather than a fact it enforces — notice a second tender on a far branch, performing the identical unhurried circuit of touch-without-taking, and a third lower down near the wet stone seam, its kiln-breath in a slightly different phase of the same held cycle, so that between the three of them the drone never has a gap, the way a chord never has a gap between its notes provided enough notes are sounding. They do not interact. They do not need to. Each one is tending its own small portion of the unfinished moment, and the portions, taken together, are the cistern.

They do not eat, as far as you can tell, and nothing here would feed them if they did, since nothing here is consumed. They do not age, obviously — nothing ages on this shelf — but more than that, they do not seem to occupy a place *in* an aging process at all, the way a held breath doesn't occupy a place in breathing, being neither inhale nor exhale but the suspended hyphen between. You get the sense, watching one work its slow nozzle-touch along a branch, leaf to leaf, decision to deferred decision, that this is not labor in the sense of effort spent toward completion. It is labor in the older sense: the contraction itself, endlessly, with the birth withheld.

If you reached out — and something in the air discourages this without forbidding it, the way a held silence discourages speech — you suspect the tender would not flinch, would not flee, would not turn its patient domestic face away from whatever you intended. It would simply continue, your hand notwithstanding, touching its valved limb to the next bead of water, the next decided-against drop, keeping, with the rest of its kind and the mountain's wordless engine, the entire grove balanced on the lip of a single, ceaseless, ungranted permission to finally fall.
