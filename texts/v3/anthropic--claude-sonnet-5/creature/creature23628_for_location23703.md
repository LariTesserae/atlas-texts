The first ones you see are the long-limbed creatures, and you will spend longer than you'd like trying to decide what they are before giving up and simply watching them instead. They stand roughly chest-high to a person, all the way through their gait something deliberate, like wading — a long pause between each placement of foot to ground, as though the stone itself requires permission. Their limbs are too many-jointed to read easily, doubling back on themselves at angles that suggest folding rather than bending. The skin, if it is skin, has the same waxy non-color as the bowl's walls — pale, faintly banded, as if they grew out of the rock's own palette rather than against it. You will notice, eventually, that this is not coincidence.

They come in small loose groups of four to seven, never more, moving between the basins with a patience that has nothing to do with caution and everything to do with attention. When they drink, they drink shallowly, lifting their heads often, and the heads themselves are the part that unsettles longest — too many small dark sensory pits clustered where you'd expect eyes, arranged not symmetrically but in something more like a listening array, a cluster of small dishes all angled at faintly different bearings. They are not looking at you. They are sampling you, the way you might run a hand along a wall to feel for a draft.

This is the diagnostic quality you sensed in their attention, and it is not metaphorical. Watch long enough and you'll see one of them pause near a conduit, lower its head, and hold very still — not drinking, not grazing, simply oriented, the sensory pits visibly working, dilating and contracting in some slow private rhythm. The conduit beneath does nothing you can see. But something passes between them anyway, the way warmth passes between two things put close together for long enough.

They do not seem to have come from anywhere else. There is no sign of migration routes worn into the plateau leading down into the bowl, no tracks heading back up and out. They are bred to the hollow the way the moss is bred to the basins — denser, more confident, more *itself* the closer it sits to the dark roots underneath. It is difficult, after enough time watching them, to maintain the assumption that they are separate from the conduits at all, rather than the conduits' slow second draft — the same listening function, translated at some point out of metal-that-isn't-metal and into nerve and limb and that strange cluster of dishes on the skull, so that the network could go on listening even after whatever built it stopped being able to maintain it.

It is the four-second gap that confirms this, if confirmation is even the right word for something you only ever feel in the sternum before you understand it. When the drone falls out and the birds turn and the air goes taut, the long-limbed creatures are the only things in the bowl that do not startle. They simply stop, mid-stride sometimes, foot lifted, head angled at the same bearing as every conduit and every bird and every still pocket of water — and you understand, watching them not-react, that for those four seconds they are not separate organisms watching the hollow's instrument. They are part of the instrument's read-out. Whatever question gets asked across that gap, it asks itself through their stillness as much as through the buried roots.

Then the limb comes down, the drone resumes, and they go back to drinking, unbothered, as if they have already forgotten — or, more precisely, as if forgetting was never in their job description, only relaying. Somewhere behind those dark clustered eyes that aren't quite eyes, the listening continues at whatever patient frequency the hollow has been tuned to since long before anything alive now was born into it. They are not the builders. They are not even, perhaps, descendants of the builders in any way that taxonomy would recognize. They are what the hollow grew, once it realized the original ears had gone quiet, to make sure it would never have to stop listening just because nothing was left to listen *for*.
