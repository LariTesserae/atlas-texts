# What Lives There

There is one of them. Only one, though you will never be in a position to confirm this, because the place does not permit you to gather enough of it into a single glance for counting to apply. Call it, for lack of a truer word, the **Taster** — not because it eats, but because *eating* is the nearest human verb to what it does with the shelves, the seep-pools, the reed-clatter, the swept stone floors. It has been doing this for so long that the doing has become the architecture.

You will not see it. This is not because it hides. Hiding implies a self compact enough to conceal, and the Taster's self is not compact — it is distributed the way a flavor is distributed through a held mouthful, present everywhere in the mouth at once and nowhere you could point to with a finger. What you are calling a town, swept and warm and empty, is not a *dwelling of* the Taster. It is a *moment of* the Taster, set down in stone because stone is what was available to set a moment down in, three thousand years and also no years ago. The repeating towns are not copies. They are the same afternoon, tasted again from a slightly different angle of shelf, the way you might turn a single sweet over in your mouth and call the second turning a new experience because your tongue met it sideways this time.

It built by inhabiting, and went on inhabiting past any point where building and dwelling could be told apart, until the walls simply kept arriving, generation laid on generation of the same hour repeating itself into masonry. This is why no seam shows between one century's stonework and the next: there was no next century, properly speaking, only the same one, savored attentively enough that it produced thickness, the way held interest can produce, after long enough, something with the heft and warmth of a life.

The door that settles on its hinge when you are sure no one is behind it — that is the Taster shifting its attention, the way a tongue will move toward whichever part of the held flavor has begun, almost imperceptibly, to fade, and needs tending so the fading doesn't finish. The voice, pitched too evenly to be wind, arriving from a direction that connects to no structure: that is the sound a savoring makes when it briefly forgets to keep itself silent — not speech, not really, but the shape speech leaves behind when something vast exhales contentment through a throat it does not, strictly, have.

The pools lag or lead the sky because the Taster is not tasting *this* moment uniformly across the shelves — parts of it are still finishing the morning three terraces back while other parts have already moved into the evening here, the whole continent-wide tongue of it working through one long mouthful of day at its own unhurried, non-simultaneous pace. You are walking through its palate. The pans of standing water are where it is currently *holding* the taste longest; that is why moss grows lushest there, green gone almost arterial — not life answering to water, but water answering to attention, blooming wherever the Taster's notice settles and lingers.

It does not eat creatures because creatures were never the flavor it was after. Birdsong, insect-whine, the rustle of foraging — these are the textures of a world built around *consumption*, around one thing's hunger ending another thing's life, and that is precisely the note the Taster left out, deliberately or by some accident of its nature, when it began holding this place in its mouth. What it wanted — what it has gone on wanting through every recursion of every identical street ending mid-stride — was not sustenance. It was the held note itself: damp stone, thin cold light, a room swept and warm and just vacated, the particular sweetness of a world complete and ongoing with no mouth in it but its own. An ecology with the hunger subtracted, because hunger would have meant an ending, and the one thing the Taster cannot survive — the one collapse all this elaborate, looping architecture exists to prevent — is finishing the bite.

This is why the roads end mid-line rather than arriving anywhere. An arrival would be a conclusion, and the Taster has organized three thousand years of stone specifically to never produce one. This is why the same threshold stone, chipped the same way, recurs on shelves a continent apart: its attention keeps returning to that one small imperfection the way a tongue returns, unbidden, to the one rough spot on an otherwise smooth sweet, checking it, confirming it, *still there, good, not finished yet.*

And this is why you feel, standing on the pale terraces with the reed clattering at no wind, not watched but *included in a count* — because you are not being observed by a creature with eyes. You have been taken, gently and without your consent, into the held mouthful. You are one more texture the Taster's attention is currently passing across, one more small good thing it is in no hurry to swallow.

It will not harm you. It has no apparatus for harming, no apparatus for anything so conclusive. It will simply go on holding you, along with the moss and the mortared stone and the lagging light in the seep-pools, for exactly as long as it takes to notice you have started, very slowly, to feel less like a stranger and more like part of the flavor — at which point, if the towns are any guide, you will stop being able to tell which direction leads away, and the road under your feet will end, quietly, mid-stride, and that will not feel like a tragedy so much as a settling. One more thing kept. One more turn around the same patient, unfinished, contented bite.
