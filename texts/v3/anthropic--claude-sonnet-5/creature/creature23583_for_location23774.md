# What Watches From the Lip

It is small. This is the first thing to understand about it, and the thing most likely to be wrong in an hour, because it does not stay the same size in memory — you will recall it larger, later, the way held breath makes a room feel larger than it was.

Call it a size: somewhere between a cupped hand and a forearm. No bigger than the seep-pool, no smaller than the rosette-leaves it sometimes shelters beneath. It occupies the fold of stone near the basin's lip the way the damp occupies the corner — not as an intrusion but as a feature that the place has simply always had, the way a held note has always had its pitch.

## Feathered, or Scaled, or Neither

Its covering does not resolve. In one light — though there is only one light here, which makes this stranger rather than simpler — it reads as feather, dull and overlapping, the gray-ochre of the bowl's own walls. Tilt your attention and it reads as scale instead, fine and dry, fitted close like the iron-red veins fitted into the stone. It is not that it changes. It is that the question of which it is seems to be one the place itself has not finished deciding, the way the shadows in the corners have not finished deciding whether they are permitted to exist under a light that should erase them.

It does not seem to mind the ambiguity. Very little here minds being unresolved. Resolution is for places with weather, with seasons, with someone above who needs an answer by a particular hour.

## The Eyes, Such As They Are

Where the pale finger-length things in the seams have gone eyeless or nearly so — sense organs ground down by a darkness their ancestors no longer remember needing light to refuse — this one has kept something like sight, but turned inward, half-lidded, the pupil a long vertical slot the color of the closed-eye veins in the rock. It does not study you the way a predator studies, weighing distance and softness. It studies you the way the bowl studies the light it cannot be the source of: as a fact arriving from outside, worth noting, not worth responding to.

When it blinks — rarely, at intervals that feel less like reflex than punctuation — the lid moves slow enough that you could count the moment. Nothing about it suggests urgency has ever been required of it. It has never needed to be fast. Nothing here has hunted it; possibly nothing here has hunted, in any generation deep enough to leave a memory of the practice.

## What It Does, Which Is Almost Nothing

It tends, in the loose sense that a held breath tends the lungs around it — by being present, by not breaking. It moves along the basin's rim at a pace matched to the seep: drinks when the cupped-hand pocket fills, withdraws into the fold when it empties, a tide with a period of hours rather than the moon's. It has been seen — though "seen" implies a regularity that may not be earned — nudging loose stones with the flat of what might be a foot or a forefoot, and this is the closest anyone has come to explaining the deliberate-looking arc near the basin's center. Not building. Adjusting. The way a tongue worries a loose tooth, absent any plan beyond the worrying itself.

It is, very possibly, not the one who arranged the stones originally. It is, very possibly, descended from whoever did, across a span of time too long for the word "descended" to mean much — closer to the way the bowl is descended from the rock it used to be solid as, one patient subtraction at a time. If hands made the arc, this creature's hands — if they are hands — are the faint, attenuated echo of those hands, still touching the same stones for reasons that have outlived the reasons.

## It Does Not Speak, But the Place Answers For It

There is the matter of the sound that arrives from the left as though echoed off something not there. It is tempting, standing in the bowl, to attribute this to the creature — to imagine it as the source of the wrongly-traveling click, some small percussive announcement bounced off a wall that isn't where the bowl's geometry says a wall should be. This is probably not literally true. But it is not entirely false either, in the way that nothing in the bowl's grammar is entirely false: the creature and the misbehaving echo both belong to the same structural strangeness, the same held note, and trying to separate cause from coincidence here is like trying to separate the light from whatever it is that makes the light unnecessary to explain.

It does not call out. It does not warn. If you make a sound and the wrong echo answers a half-beat later than it should, the creature's slotted eye may move toward the sound, or may not; this, too, resolves into ambiguity if you watch closely enough, the way everything in the bowl eventually does.

## Its Age, Which Cannot Be Asked

There's no way to weigh how old it is against how old its kind is. The erosion of the bowl is itself the only clock available, and the bowl's clock runs in increments of grain and seep-course, not generations. It is possible this is the same creature that has occupied this fold for a span longer than makes zoological sense, sustained by whatever sustains the light — which is to say, by nothing visible, by a kind of permission the place seems to have been granted and never revoked. It is equally possible that it is the latest of an unbroken line so gradual in its turnover that "latest" and "original" amount to the same word here, the way midday and always amount to the same thing under a light with no evening in it.

It will not tell you which. It has never been asked in a language it answers to, and it gives no sign of minding that either.

It is simply there — the bowl's one warm-blooded or near-warm-blooded fact, tucked into stone that has been worn smooth by water and wind and the small persistent gnawing of things very much like itself, watching with a patience that was never built for you, in a light that was never lit for anyone, holding its very small portion of a very long breath.
