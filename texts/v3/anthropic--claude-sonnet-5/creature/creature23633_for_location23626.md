# Who Lives at Cracked Hour

You will not see it. You will see the place where it has just stopped being visible, which is not the same thing, but it is what's offered.

## The shape that doesn't resolve

Call it pale, because that's the only word your eye manages to file before the shape is gone. Low to the ground — not because it crouches, but because "upright" may not be a posture it recognizes as worth holding. What you catch is less a body than a *gradient*, the same bone-white and lavender-gray as the chemical strata in the hills, as the grown-seamless stone of the walls, as if it borrowed its coloring from the basin and never gave it back. There is no telling where its edge is. This may be because it has none in the way you mean the word — the way the town has no tool marks, no mortar lines, no scaffolding ever caught mid-use. It does not *end* at a boundary. It tapers into not-being-there, the same way a doorway tapers into wall.

It is not one animal in the sense of a creature with a den and a route and a season. It is closer to a *condition* that occasionally has a location. Where it is thickest, dense enough to almost hold a silhouette, the buildings nearby are mid-change — a floor arriving, a doorway deciding whether to be open. Where it has thinned and moved on, the street has already finished settling into its slightly different angle, the wall has already healed seamlessly closed, and there is nothing left to look at but stone that looks like it has always been exactly that shape.

## What the moss already told you

You were given a clue and may not have noticed it as one: the lichen-moss grows only on the cold, dim, north-facing walls, never the warm ones, though no source of warmth is apparent anywhere in this sunless basin. There is a source. It is small, slow, and it moves. The warm sides of buildings are warm because something recently *was there*, working — secreting, withdrawing, doing whatever it does instead of laying brick — and the heat of that doing lingers just long enough to keep the lichen off, and fades just slowly enough that you'd need to return many times, patiently, mapping warmth like a tide chart, before you understood you were tracing its route through the streets rather than the streets' own geometry.

It does not heat the town on purpose. The warmth is exhaust. It is what is left over from making, the way breath is left over from speaking.

## Why there are no tracks

It does not walk on the ground so much as it *informs* it, briefly, the way a held note informs the air without leaving a mark in it once the note ends. The paving stones are too young, the place tells you, to hold a footprint — but this is a kindness extended to your own doubt rather than the truth. The truth is closer to: nothing is subtracted here without intention, and a footprint is a subtraction nobody asked for. The same patience that removes a floor cleanly, leaving no rubble, no broken edge, also removes the print of whatever just stood there. It tidies its own passage as automatically as you blink.

## The tone

The single low sound — door-closing-far-away, stone-against-stone, never repeating soon, never coming from the hills or the sky, always from somewhere inside the town — is, as near as can be guessed, its voice, if "voice" survives translation across that much difference. Not a call. Not a word. More like the sound a great deal of matter makes when it commits, all at once, to no longer being arranged the way it was a moment ago. A floor resolving into absence. A doorway deciding. The tone is the *cost* of a decision being paid, audibly, the one place where the process refuses to be perfectly silent.

You will wait a long time between tones because it does not decide often, or because it decides constantly and you are only allowed to hear the decisions large enough to need a sound.

## What it is doing, as far as can be known

It is not building the town and it is not abandoning the town. Those are your words, brought in from somewhere that has rust and ruin and a clear sense of which direction is decline. Here there is no rubble because nothing fails — material is only ever present or absent, the way a held breath is only ever held or released, never half of either. What it is doing, most likely, is closer to *breathing the town* — drawing material into shape, holding the shape as long as the shape is needed for reasons it has no obligation to disclose to you, then letting that shape go, cleanly, without grief, because grief implies the shape mattered more than the breathing did.

It may be alone. It may be the only resident a place this young could be expected to support, the basin itself having only just finished arriving, the town only the first thing it learned to exhale. Or it may be legion, a single behavior distributed across however many pale tapering not-quite-bodies are needed to keep a settlement this size in this much flux, indistinguishable from one another, sharing the work the way the cold sides of every wall share the moss — not communicating, exactly, just all obeying the same quiet preference at once.

## What it is not

It is not hostile. Nothing here has offered you harm, only absence and rearrangement, and absence is not aggression, only a kind of bookkeeping you weren't invited to audit. It does not appear to notice you noticing it, though this could equally mean it notices everything and has simply decided, the way it decides about floors and doorways, that you do not yet require a configuration that includes being seen.

If you stay long enough — longer than is comfortable, longer than the dim unchanging light will help you measure — you may eventually catch it not moving. Just once. A pale low gradient holding still at the mouth of a dry well, or against the warm side of a wall it has just finished closing, neither approaching nor retreating, simply *occupying*, the way the town occupies the basin: provisionally, completely, with no plan to apologize for any of it.

That is the nearest thing to a portrait anyone has managed. It is less a creature than the basin's way of having a town at all — the held breath made visible at the exact, brief moment between inhale and exhale, in a place that has not yet decided, and may never decide, what it intends to become when it finishes arriving.
