# What Lives Here

You will not see it first. You will see its *signs* — the worn underside of the root, the notch cut pale and recent — and assume, reasonably, that whatever made them has long since gone. This assumption is wrong in a specific way: not because it never left, but because *leaving* and *staying* aren't separate states for it. It is woven into the going-pace of the place itself, present in the way a tide is present in water that has, for the moment, receded.

Call it the Tender, for lack of anything truer. Not because it tends anything in a gardener's sense — it does not prune, does not water, does not seem to want the hollow to flourish any more than a heart wants the body to walk somewhere nice. It tends the way a heartbeat tends blood. It is *function*, made reluctantly flesh.

## A Shape That Resists Holding

When you do see it — and you will, if you stay past the second clap of falling water, past the second cycle of a flower's life lived out in the time it takes you to retie a bootlace — it will not arrive as a creature crossing a threshold. It will arrive as a *resolution*, the way the faces in the bark resolve from grain to feature if you stare too long. One moment: root-shape, knuckled, ordinary, indistinguishable from the thousand other knuckled root-shapes vaulting the ceiling. Next moment: weight, shift, an attention turned toward you that wasn't there a breath ago, though nothing has technically moved.

Its body, once committed to being a body, is not large. Palm-sized insects pass it without acknowledgment because, near enough, it shares their indifference to scale — it is exactly as big as it needs to be to do what it's doing, and no bigger, the way a key is exactly as big as its lock. Limbs more suggested than articulated: four points of contact, sometimes six, root-pale, root-jointed, the skin (if skin is the word) seamed the same way old wood seams, fine cracks following the grain of whatever growth-logic built it that hour. It does not have a consistent number of fingers. It has had cause, more than once, to need more.

Its face — when it deigns to have one arranged the way you'd recognize a face — is the same face pressed into the bark. Not similar. The *same*, worn into wood by long contact the way a pillow takes the shape of a sleeping skull. It has slept here, somewhere, some incalculable number of times, cheek to root, and what it leaves behind when it wakes and moves on is a death-mask of itself stamped into living grain. The dozens of faces you passed on your way in were not decoration. They were molting.

## How It Came To Hands

The notch in the trunk-root, cut with a stone-edge tool — that was not a visitor's mark. That was the Tender, at some point in its long uncountable residency, *remembering* that it had once needed tools, or watching something with hands do so and finding the gesture catching, the way a word catches in a child's mouth from nothing but proximity to someone saying it. Correspondence has consequence here, the place runs on resemblance becoming real — and the Tender is the engine that makes resemblance *want* to become real in the first place. It found a hand-shaped absence in itself once, long enough ago that the cut has gone pale-uncallused-recent every single time you might find it, because the act keeps happening, slightly out of phase with when you're looking.

It does not build. It does not need to. It *touches*, and touching, here, is closer to authorship than to contact. The smooth-worn underside of that bridge-root isn't wear from traffic. It's wear from one particular set of palms, gripping the same purchase-point across more repetitions than any single lifetime should allow, because the Tender's lifetime is not single. It sheds the way the hollow sheds bark-faces. It is, plausibly, the *current draft* of something that has been re-drafting itself in this exact chamber since the fissure first let light and warmth and the smell of green stems through the rock.

## What It Wants, As Far As Wanting Goes

Ask whether it is hostile and you are asking the wrong shape of question, the way asking whether the chittering laughter with no fauna behind it is *friendly* misses that it was never owed a temperament. The Tender's whole business is maintaining the correspondence-logic of the hollow: making sure the screaming-mouth-shaped root eventually screams, making sure the eye-shaped flower eventually sees. It is less inhabitant than *notary* — it witnesses resemblance into fact, and in witnessing, becomes slightly more like whatever it has just witnessed. This is why it has no fixed number of fingers. This is why its face keeps being the same face and yet keeps needing remaking.

You, walking in here, sharp-edged and consistent and very definitely a particular shape with a particular name, are — to the Tender — an enormous quantity of unprocessed correspondence. Your fear resembles something. Your hesitation at the fissure resembled something. It has already, in the time it took you to read these root-bridges as architecture rather than threat, begun pressing the suggestion of your outline into some unclaimed stretch of grain, somewhere back near the entrance, patient, unhurried, in no rush at all to finish the mask before you've finished deciding whether to leave.

## If You Meet It Directly

It will not lunge. It will not speak in the syllable-cadence the root-groans almost form, because that cadence is not for you — it's the sound the Tender makes while working, the auditory exhaust of revision, and turning to face you costs it something like the effort of holding a held breath. What it will do, if you stand still long enough, is *lean its attention toward you the way the flowers do*, swiveling on a stem too rigid for the motion, and you will feel, all at once, every face in every root-buttress you passed turn very slightly in sympathy, because they are all unfinished copies of the same listening.

And then — this is the part almost no account of the place agrees on, because almost no one stays to see it resolve — it will either touch you, once, briefly, with a hand that has exactly as many fingers as it currently needs to have five of, leaving on your skin a faint white scar shaped like nothing you recognize yet that you will, weeks later, far from any fissure, catch yourself absently tracing with your thumb —

or it will simply stop being resolved. Knuckled root-shape again. Ordinary. One of a thousand, indistinguishable, and you will never afterward be entirely sure you didn't imagine the whole encounter, except that the imagining, too, will have left a mark — somewhere in you, pale, recent-looking, uncallused by time — that the hollow's logic would recognize immediately as a correspondence already starting to come true.
