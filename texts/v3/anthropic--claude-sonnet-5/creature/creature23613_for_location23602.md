**The Keeper of the Sump**

You do not see it arrive. You become aware, by degrees, that the deeper patch of dark you'd taken for a current-shadow has a weight to it, a directionality, and that it has been holding its position against the pull of the water for longer than anything natural would bother to.

It is not large in the way that frightens immediately. That comes later, when you try to find its edges and cannot.

What you can make out, in the gold leached light: a hide the color of the rust itself, dark orange-black, broken into plates that don't quite match each other, as though it had been assembled rather than grown — and in a sense it had been, generation on generation, by a place that offered exactly one kind of pressure and rewarded exactly one kind of patience. No eyes that you can find, or eyes so reduced they've become a rumor of eyes, two paler dimples in a face that has stopped needing to see in favor of a wide fringe of barbels and combs along the jaw, reading the water the way the room itself reads the engine-pulse — through vibration, through the small disagreements current makes with itself when something blind and fast passes nearby.

It moves the way the valves groan: rarely, and then all at once, and then not again for a long while. Most of its life, you understand, is spent exactly here, wedged into the joint where brick meets a stanchion's iron foot, taking the leaked warmth the crustaceans cluster for, feeling the schools of pale fish part and reform around it like something they've stopped being afraid of because fear, too, costs more than this place can afford. It does not hunt so much as it remains, and the room arranges itself around its remaining — prey passing close enough, often enough, on the room's own current-driven schedule, that hunting was never the efficient strategy. Waiting was.

Its mouth, when you finally understand you're looking at it, is wider than seems possible for the body, lined in rows of needle structure gone the same orange-rust as the hide, good for holding rather than tearing, built for things that don't struggle long in water this cold and this used to silence. It does not open often. It does not need to.

There is no menace in it, particularly. Menace requires an audience, an intention, a self that knows itself as separate from what it threatens — and it isn't clear this thing retains enough of a self for that. It is less an animal than a function the chamber generates, the way the lamps generate light and the valves generate groaning: the room needed an apex, something to close the small shadow-economy of flick and scatter and stillness into a shape, and over geologic patience it produced one, the way water produces sediment, the way rust produces its own ceiling. It is the brick floor's opinion on the subject of fish. It is the pumps' answer to the question of where all that slow protein should finally come to rest.

You understand, standing chest-deep with the gold light moving over its rust-dark plates, that it has almost certainly noticed you already — not seen, but felt, the wrongness of your displacement against a current it has memorized down to the day — and that it is doing what it has always done with wrongness it cannot immediately classify as the kind of small fast pale thing the room provides for free.

It is waiting to see if you become food, or current, or nothing at all.

Most things, eventually, become one of the three.
