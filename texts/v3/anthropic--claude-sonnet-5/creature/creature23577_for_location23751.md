## Inhabitants

To ask what *lives* on the Singing Flats is to ask the wrong-shaped question, the way asking for the address of a chord is the wrong-shaped question. Mostly what lives here is the sound itself, or close enough to it that the distinction stops mattering past a certain point of acquaintance.

### The Chorus-Mat

Closest to the ground, doing most of the actual *producing* of the place's signature noise, are the thready black-green mats themselves — and within them, threaded through the root-analog lattice, the true makers: small, soft-bodied, faintly translucent things, none longer than a thumb, none possessed of anything you'd confidently call a head. They are not individuals in any way that holds up to scrutiny. Cut one free of the mat and it stops making sound within minutes and dies within a day; leave it embedded and it can apparently go on for — no one has measured this, because no one has stayed long enough, but the mats show layered growth that implies decades, possibly far more. They breathe through paired siphons that double as the source of the piping, clicking, exhaling chorus, and they breathe in relay, passing the wave of sound from body to body across kilometers faster than any single nerve impulse should allow, which has led to the working theory — unconfirmed, unfalsifiable on the available evidence — that the mat isn't a colony of separate things cooperating, but one organism that has simply declined to centralize. It has no brain because it doesn't need one; the sound *is* the nervous system, audible, externalized, doing its thinking in the open air where anyone can overhear it.

What it is thinking about is unknown. The waves of chorus that move faster than wind do not correlate cleanly with weather, light, or the passage of large fauna across the mat's surface. The leading theory among the very few people who have studied this closely enough to have theories is that it is reacting to the water — the old water, the water that remembers states of the land that no longer exist — and that the mat, rooted directly into stone that is itself saturated with that memory, is simply the only organism patient enough, slow enough, *flat* enough, to listen to it properly.

### The Wader

Less explicable, and far less common, is the thing the rare travelers call the wader, for lack of anything better, though no two who've seen it describe quite the same silhouette.

It is built along recognizably animal lines — four limbs, roughly, a trunk, an elongation at one end that suggests a neck and head though no one has gotten close enough or had good enough light to confirm features — and it moves through the shallow standing pools with the unhurried, high-stepping gait of any wading bird anywhere. The number of legs is correct. The number of joints in those legs is correct. It is the *timing* that's wrong: each leg seems to move on its own private clock, slightly out of sync with the others, so that the gait reads, from any distance, as a creature walking through water with a very faint, very wrong delay between intention and execution — like watching a video with the audio half a frame behind, except there is no audio, there is no video, there is only the thing itself, wading, slightly late to its own movement.

It is almost never seen arriving. It is seen *already there*, mid-pool, and then — this is the part that unsettles people who've otherwise made peace with the flats — it is sometimes seen in the mirror-water several seconds before it appears in the air above it, the reflection performing the wade first, faithfully, patiently, as though rehearsing, and the actual creature following along afterward like an actor catching up to its own cue. On at least one well-attested occasion, witnessed by two travelers independently, the reflection waded all the way across a pool and the creature itself never arrived at all. The mirror simply settled back to sky, and the two of them stood there with the distinct, shared, never-quite-nameable feeling of having watched something keep an appointment that had no other half.

It does not appear to eat, in the sense of consuming the mat-organisms or anything else identifiable. It does not appear to be hunted. It is not aggressive — those who have stood close enough describe it as simply *uninterested*, the way you might be uninterested in a stranger glimpsed through a train window, present at the periphery of a much larger preoccupation. Whatever the wader is doing, the wading itself seems incidental to it, a side effect of being the kind of thing it is in the kind of place this is, the same way breathing is a side effect of being alive and not really the *point* of it.

### The Flock-Shadow

Rarer still, and stranger for being so brief: a shadow crossing one of the mirror-pools, moving in the configuration of a flock — that distinctive coordinated ripple, dozens of small bodies wheeling as one — with nothing above the pool to cast it. No birds. No haze-shapes. Just the shadow, fast, low, gone in under two seconds, leaving the water disturbed in its wake in the precise pattern you'd expect from low-flying wings, little concentric overlapping rings collapsing back to stillness as if something real had genuinely passed a few centimeters above the surface, displacing air, and simply hadn't bothered casting the version of itself that throws shadows in the conventional direction.

No one has seen this twice in the same place. No one has seen it land. Whether it is the same phenomenon as the wader's anticipatory reflection — the land's water remembering or pre-living something the air above it hasn't caught up to yet — or something else entirely, unrelated, its own separate species of wrongness, is exactly the kind of question the flats seem built to leave open. Pursued, the asking erodes, the way everything here eventually erodes, down past curiosity into something closer to companionship: you stop trying to catch the flock-shadow's source and start, instead, simply noting its passage, the way you'd note thunder, or the chorus swelling, or your own pulse — another local weather, moving through, leaving its evidence on the water and nothing else, asking nothing of you but to have been, briefly, witnessed.
