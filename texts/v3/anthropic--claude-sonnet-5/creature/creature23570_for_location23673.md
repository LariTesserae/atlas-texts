What lives here does not live the way things live elsewhere — building, hunting, breeding toward some future — but rather subsists, the way the damp subsists in the underside of a stone, the way the drone subsists beneath the pan. It has had to fit itself to a place with no plant, no prey, no weather, only warmth and condensation leaking up from something working forever in the dark, and so it has become, after some unimaginable span of generations, almost a condensation itself: a thing that gathers rather than grows.

The scuttling shape, when it doesn't vanish into a fissure in time, is low and wide, built close to the ground the way anything sensible would build itself in a place with no wind to lean into and no shade to seek. Its carapace — if carapace is the word for something that seems less grown than accreted, layer on layer of fine mineral dust cemented by its own secretions — carries the same rust-and-ash coloring as the pan, broken by faint patches of the same metallic sheen that shows in the stained pockets, as though it had rolled in that ground and kept the mark. It has no eyes worth the name; in flat shadowless light there is nothing to see that sight would help with. What it has instead, fanned along its leading edge, are fine bristled palps that read vibration the way a held palm reads the rhythm through stone — tuned, generation over generation, to the cycling pulse of the drone, so that it can walk toward a stronger thrum the way another animal might walk toward water. It drinks by standing in the seep-stains until droplets condense along its underside and run to its mouthparts; it shelters by wedging itself into the cooler dark of a fissure during the unvarying warmth of the day, for no reason except that the cool air gathers there and the body, like everything else here, has learned to prefer a held temperature over an unheld one. It does not appear to eat anything that eats anything else. There is nothing to suggest violence in it, no claw, no haste — only a slow, patient crossing of open ground from one pocket of stain to the next, the same unbothered patience the basin itself wears.

The shape that circles overhead, on the rare day it appears, may be the same creature in another instar, or its mate, or its predator, or none of these — categories that assume a chain this place was never built to need. It is wing-shaped in silhouette only; closer, what flight membrane it has looks stretched and oily, more like a held bubble of condensation than feather or skin, and it does not flap so much as ride some thermal lift bleeding up off the warmest stained ground, circling lazily the way steam circles above a vent, asking nothing of the air beyond passage. It comes down, eventually, onto one of the duller pockets, folds whatever it has for limbs, and goes still in a way indistinguishable from the scuttling thing wedged into its fissure — both of them, in the end, just paused condensations, waiting out the held warmth until something nudges them to move again.

There is no nest because there is nothing here that anticipates young needing shelter from a future different than the present; there is no burrow with depth, because depth here belongs to the machine, sealed and unreachable, and whatever lives above has made its peace with staying on the lid. What young there are seem to simply appear in the damp pockets, smaller, paler versions of the grown shape, as though condensation itself, given enough time pooling against warm stone, eventually thickens into something that can walk.

It would be wrong to call this thing rare, exactly, the way it would be wrong to call the salt rings rare — it is exactly as frequent as the conditions that produce it, no more and no less, an equilibrium population matched to an equilibrium leak of heat and moisture, holding steady the way the drone holds steady, asking the basin for nothing it does not already provide, taking from it only what bleeds up unbidden, and giving back, as far as anyone could tell, nothing at all — not even, in the end, much evidence that it was ever there.
