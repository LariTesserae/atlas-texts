## What Lives Here

You will see them only twice, if you are patient, and never clearly.

The first are the small ones — the pale, translucent fish-shapes already glimpsed drifting between the stalks. Call them fish for lack of better word, though they have no fins to speak of, only a slow ripple along a body like a held breath given shape, organs visible as soft smudges of shadow within near-clear flesh. They do not school. They do not flee. They move as though they have never once needed to outrun anything, because nothing here has ever asked them to. Watch one long enough and you'll see it isn't moving toward or away from a thing at all — it drifts the way the seed-pods drift, caught more in the slow thermal breath of the water than in any will of its own. It eats, presumably. It is eaten by — nothing you will see. It exists at the very threshold of being alive enough to count as fauna, as if even the water had to be talked into allowing it.

The second is the shadow. Singular, or perhaps several wearing the same shape, the same unwillingness to resolve. It appears at the limit of the long, drifting visibility — past the lattice-light, at the soft edge where the gold-green dims toward the unlit blue further out — and it is always *leaving*, never arriving, a darkness the size of something significant moving with the unhurried, gravity-careless grace of a thing that has never once needed to react quickly to anything in its long life. You will not get a silhouette. You will not get a wake, a fin, a limb, an eye. You will get the *impression* of bulk, the suggestion of an outline too large and too slow to be one of the small pale fish-shapes grown old, and then the bend of a stalk-cluster or the slant of refracted light will swallow it, and it will not reappear in that hour, or that day.

What it is, exactly, the place does not say. It may be one creature that has lived in these flooded heights since before the water settled into its patience, growing along with everything else here, scaled to match the slow abundance around it — vast not from hunger or dominance but simply because nothing has ever pressured it toward smallness. It may be many such creatures, solitary by nature or by necessity, each one a continent unto itself, passing each other across the long submerged distances perhaps once in a decade, with no more urgency than two glaciers might acknowledge each other.

Or it may be that the shadow is not a creature in the way the small pale fish are creatures — that it is closer kin to the groan of settling stone, the felt-not-heard pressure that moves through the water when the young mountains shift beneath their drowned weight. A geological event with the outward manner of an animal. Something the place produces the way it produces light-lattices and slow flowering stalks: not a being that lives *in* Verdance Tor, but a expression *of* it, the mountain's own slow dream of having something to hold all this abundance for.

Either way, it does not hunt the small translucent fish, has never been seen to. It does not graze the dense fronds or disturb the coral-moss colonies furring the northern faces. It passes through the green-on-green abundance the way the light does — present, undeniable, utterly without appetite — and this, more than its size or its refusal to be seen clearly, is what marks it as belonging here rather than visiting. Everything in the Hanging Sea is rehearsing for a future or grieving a past that required eating, mating, claiming. The shadow alone seems to have already arrived wherever the place is going. It is patient the way the water is patient. It has nowhere else to be, and an unhurried eternity in which to not be there.

If you wait long enough — an hour, a day, longer than you meant to stay — you may feel its passage before you see it: a soft directionless pressure, the way sound arrives here without origin, a sense that something large has decided, somewhere beyond the lattice-light, that you are not yet a question it has finished asking either.
