# The Resident of the Spire-Field

## What It Is Not

Start with negatives, because that is how anyone who has met it starts, fumbling backward through what they expected and didn't find. It is not a sea-beast in the way the old maps would have it—no coiled bulk, no single hide, no count of eyes. It is not territorial in any way a hunted thing would recognize; it does not chase, does not lair, has never once been caught mid-meal or mid-anything that resolves into a verb you could write down with confidence. It is not, strictly, *many*, though it is met as many things. And it is not, strictly, *one*, though everything met here eventually turns out to have been it.

## The Shape It Hasn't Settled On

The witnesses who saw something "too large and too brief" were not exaggerating and were not lying and were not, even, disagreeing with each other, though their accounts share almost nothing. One describes a finned vastness gliding under a breached spire, the size of the ledge it passed beneath. Another, on what may have been the same day or a day eleven turbulences distant, describes something no larger than a hand, glassy and many-jointed, walking upside-down along the underside of the water's surface as though the surface were a ceiling it had every right to.

Both are right. This is the place's logic applied to flesh the way it is applied to rock: nothing here has finished deciding its own scale. The being—if "being" survives translation any better than the archway's inscriptions do—has not yet settled on how big it is allowed to be, and so it is, by turns, hand-sized and headland-sized, glimpsed and gone before the decision can be checked against itself. It is plausible, though no one has confirmed it without dying or otherwise becoming unavailable for comment, that it does not experience this as inconsistency. It experiences it the way you experience your own pulse: continuous, but not always present to attention.

What is constant, in the handful of corroborating details: an edge to it. Not a metaphorical edge—a literal one, basalt-sharp, glassed in places, as though it grew the way the spires grew, fast and hot and never given the leisure of erosion. Whatever it is wearing, it is wearing rock the way the rest of the formation wears rock: recently, uncomfortably, with visible joints where the heat-fused obsidian hasn't quite smoothed into the rest of the shape.

## The Voice

The high keening that no two witnesses transcribe the same way—that is also it, or is it being honest, which may be the same thing in a place this young. Call it speech still arguing itself into grammar. It is not nonsense; nonsense would be easier to dismiss. It has the cadence of sentence, rises and falls like something building toward a point, occasionally drops into what could be a register of address—a *you* aimed outward, briefly, before sliding back into the larger roar and losing the thread.

The theory, repeated by the same people who repeat the theory about the water and the rock arguing over what is real, is that the keening is the creature trying to finish a thought it started before the spires existed, and that the thought is the spires, and that it has not yet reached the part of the thought where it gets to stop saying it. This is unprovable. It is also the only theory anyone offers with their voice shaking, which counts for something even where causality doesn't reliably hold.

## What It Made, and Why

The archway and the pillar are its hands' work, or near enough—built, the marks suggest, in the manner of something working fast, mid-thought, the way you'd jot a note you intend to finish later and then lose the thread entirely. The inscriptions on the pillar's top face are sometimes legible as language to whoever looks last, and illegible, mere erosion-mimicry, to whoever looks first—an order that does not stay fixed, so that two people examining the stone in immediate succession may both be right and both be lying to each other in good faith.

It is suspected—though "suspected" is generous, "hoped" might be more honest—that the creature returns to the pillar the way a person returns to an unfinished letter: adding a line, striking one out, never quite arriving at a draft it's willing to leave standing. The archway, similarly, is not a threshold so much as a *rehearsal* of one. It leads somewhere different each time not because the world beyond it is unstable, but because the thing that built it has not decided where it is trying to go, and the door, being made in its image, hasn't decided either.

## How It Is Met

Always alone. Always briefly. Always with that calm that the visitor only recognizes as wrong once the moment has passed and the warmth has stopped meaning comfort—the same calm reported by those who meet themselves crossing in the opposite direction between spires. This is not incidental. It is possible, even likely, that some of the "meeting oneself" reports and some of the "creature, too large, too brief" reports are the same event misfiled under two different categories of strangeness, the witness's own outline briefly mistaken for the resident's, or the resident's outline briefly borrowing the witness's face because faces, like everything else here, are negotiable before they are fixed.

It does not attack. It has never been recorded attacking, though "recorded" implies a continuity of record this place does not extend. What it does, in every account that can be trusted even provisionally, is *regard*—turn the unsettled mass of itself toward the witness for a duration too short to be timed and too long to be comfortable, and then resume whatever the keening was in the middle of saying, and go.

## Why It Stays

Not loyalty. Not hunger. The simplest theory, and therefore the one most distrusted by people who like their explanations to have worked for them before, is that it cannot yet tell whether leaving is a thing that has been decided about it, in the same way the spires cannot yet tell whether they are eleven or fourteen, in the same way the water cannot yet tell whether it is above or below. It is unfinished the way the place is unfinished, made of the same undecided material, given form by the same dispute between water and rock that it may, in fact, simply *be*—not a resident of the argument, but a clause inside it, audible, occasionally glimpsed, never yet permitted to reach the silence of being settled.
