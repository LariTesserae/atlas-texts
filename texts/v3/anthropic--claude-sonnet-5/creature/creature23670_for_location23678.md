The thing that answers to a name here is not one thing.

Closest to the surface — closest to where a visitor's ear would locate it, though never to where the eye can — is the multitude. They are small, smaller than the smallest gap in the leaf-litter would suggest a creature should be, and there are more of them than the space could conceivably hold if any of them ever stopped moving. They do not stop moving. They are built for the hum, or the hum is built from them, it no longer matters which came first: jointed, chitin-cased things that vibrate their wing-cases and leg-segments against bark in shifts, in relays, in overlapping frequencies tuned so precisely against each other that no single one of them could be picked out even by another of its own kind. They do not have names for each other. They do not need them. A single individual lives a short, complete, entirely unremarkable life — egg, instar, working adult, a final folding-down into the leaf-mass that is indistinguishable from sleep — and is replaced in the same breath by another already mid-hatch, mid-click, already carrying its share of the frequency forward. There is no funeral because there is no gap for a funeral to mark. The hum does not pause for any of them. It has never paused. It is not really many lives at all, looked at correctly — it is one long sound wearing a very large number of very short bodies.

But there is something else, further back, further up in the vaulted dark where the fused limbs cross overhead like ribs.

It is larger. It is, as far as can be told, singular — there is only ever the suggestion of one, never two, never a mother-and-young, never a pair. It moves at the rate the canopy moves, which is to say: technically, constantly, perceptibly never. Pale, the color of the condensation-film itself, skin or hide or bark-analog kept always faintly slick, faintly cold, so that even when something does catch a sliver of it at the very corner of vision — a long limb's worth of curve, a damp gleam where there should be shadow — the mind discounts it as a trick of the wet light. It does not hunt the small multitude. It does not need to. It is fed, if "fed" is even the right register for what happens here, on what the hollow already sheds — a film of must, a breath of fungal sweetness, the slow surplus that an equilibrium this total still somehow produces, a tithe the system pays to whatever keeps it a system instead of a mere accumulation. In return it does the one thing the place cannot do for itself: it remembers, in whatever low and wordless way a creature that has outlived its own species' concept of time can be said to remember, that the hollow used to be smaller, or larger, or shaped differently, in an age before the plate of stone beneath was stone at all. It carries that one discrepancy the way the hum carries its ten thousand years — quietly, continuously, without ever once being asked to stop.

It will not be seen today either. That is not failure on the visitor's part. It is the arrangement. The multitude is the sound; the singular pale thing is the silence the sound is wrapped around; together they are the lung's two halves, in and out, breathing nothing in particular, forever, at the same shadowless green-grey hour it has always been here.
