The first sign is never a body. It is a gap — a place where the swarm-columns thin without cause, a moment where the heron-thing's hourly strike comes a half-second late, as though it deferred to something passing. You learn to read the basin's small disruptions before you ever see what makes them. By the time you do see it, you've already been watched for longer than you'd like to think about.

It is roughly the shape of a heron crossed with something that was once, a very long time ago, mammalian — long-legged, narrow-bodied, moving with that same unhurried, joint-folding economy as the water-birds, but its skin has the same wet translucence as the amphibians draped on the stones. Under the skin, faintly, you can see structure: not bone exactly, more like the etched pattern on the archway, a circuitry of paler lines that brighten very slightly when it stands in direct light, the way the archway brightens when the reeds hum. It is never quite dry. Condensation beads along its limbs the same way it beads on the broad leaves, and it does not seem to mind, the way nothing here minds the water — it has simply been included in the basin's slow, continuous weeping.

Call it a tender, for lack of anything truer. Not a god of the place — gods imply worship, and nothing here performs worship, not even toward it. It is closer to a gardener who has been gardening so long that the garden has started gardening itself, and the gardener's remaining work is mostly attention: the held thought that passes through the stone underfoot, brief and gone. When the swarms rise in their glittering columns and hang without colliding, that is not the tender directing them. That is the tender having directed them so long ago, and so thoroughly, that direction became instinct, became the swarm's own private mathematics, the way a parent's voice becomes a child's conscience. The tender's actual labor now is smaller, stranger: noticing. Walking the basin's edge at no fixed hour, pausing where a flower has begun tracking its unseen point in the air a half-degree off true, and correcting it — not with hands, but by standing near it for the length of three slow breaths, after which the flower resumes its proper bearing.

It does not eat in any way you'd recognize, though it is sometimes found motionless at the black mirror-pools for hours, water threading up through the pale lines beneath its skin the way water threads between the root systems — as if it drinks not through a mouth but through the same seepage logic as everything else here. It does not speak, though near the archway, when both it and the structure are wet, you can feel a change in the reeds' silent hum, a third frequency entering the chord, and you understand, without being able to prove it, that this is conversation.

Its eyes — when it turns them on you, which is rare, and feels less like being seen than being *included in a count* — are the same near-liquid smoothness as the worn stone underfoot. Old in the same finished way. Not predatory, not kind, simply *calibrated*, the way the whole basin is calibrated, an iris that has stopped needing to widen or narrow because the light here never changes enough to ask it to.

It sleeps, if that is the word, inside the ring of low stones, lying along their curve with a fit so exact you suspect the stones were placed around the shape of its resting rather than the other way around — or perhaps, given how long everything here has had to settle, both are true at once, cause and effect arrived at jointly, the way the basin's construction and erosion shook hands. In that posture it looks less like a creature sleeping and more like a key returned to its lock, a piece of the architecture briefly made motile so it could go inspect the rest of itself.

You will not see it do anything dramatic. It will not strike, like the heron-thing, with hourly precision; violence here is beneath its job description, already delegated to systems that perform it better than any single body could. What it does, all it does, endlessly and without apparent strain, is *keep the basin arriving* — nudging a flower's angle, standing near a reed-stand until its hum settles back into key, passing a hand-shaped not-quite-hand near the archway so the etching remembers what it's for. Patience given a body. Tending given legs. The held breath of the whole hollow, walking around inside itself, making sure the breath stays held exactly as long as it needs to, and not one moment longer — though no one, including it, seems to know what happens, or what is finally allowed to exhale, when that moment finally comes.
