**What Tends the Seam**

Start small, because that is where the evidence starts: the pale many-legged things in the channels are not vermin and they are not wildlife. Watch them long enough and the deliberateness becomes unmistakable — they pause at the same fractures each cycle, work some invisible adjustment with foreclaws too fine to see clearly, and move on. They are not foraging. They are checking. Call them caretakers, though that flatters them with too much will; they may be closer to cells than to workers, components of something larger that has been subdivided down to a scale convenient for crawling into cracks. They have no eyes worth the name. They do not need them. They know the seam the way a hand knows which finger itches.

They were put there, or grown there, by whatever it is that occupies the next tier up in scale — the symmetrical shape that crosses the gaps overhead in that single unhurried motion, never seen whole. It is tempting to call this a creature, and perhaps in some sense it is: it moves with intention, it has favored routes through the empty vertical air that owe nothing to gravity, and on the rare occasions it passes close enough to displace air, that air carries a faint mineral warmth, like breath off sun-struck stone, though there is no sun here. But its symmetry is too exact for an animal, too exact even for most machines — the kind of symmetry that suggests it was assembled to a standard rather than born to a body plan. It may be one individual. It may be several, indistinguishable, performing the same patrol in rotation so that the station always seems to have exactly one watcher and never reveals whether that watcher is singular. It does not flee observers because it has already decided, correctly, that nothing on the ridge above can follow it past the first forty meters of open air.

But neither the small tenders nor the crossing shape are, properly speaking, what *lives* here. They are extremities. The actual resident is lower, where the platforms give out and the glow in the veins runs brightest and slowest, where the deep periodic vibration originates and climbs the throat of the crevice like a pulse climbing a vein it was named after. Something down there is being kept running. Whether it is a process, an organ, an engine, or a mind is a distinction that may not survive contact with whatever built this place — the same way the walls do not distinguish between grown and carved. It is patient in the specific way that only non-biological patience manages: not enduring time, but simply not yet finished with it. It has needs that the architecture serves without explaining — the water-logic, the vapor cycle, the slow brightening and dimming of the veins, all of it metabolism wearing the shape of geology.

If it has attention at all, that attention is not pointed outward. It was not built to be found, and so it was not built with a face, a greeting, a threshold ritual, anything that a visitor's mind could fasten onto and call *welcome* or *warning*. The mountain came up out of the earth recently enough that its edges are still sharp; whatever lives in its one deliberate fold is, in its own way, just as unfinished, still mid-process, still being assembled or maintained or grown by stages too slow to watch directly. You could stand on the lower platform for a year and see only: small things tending channels, one symmetrical shape crossing overhead at intervals, a glow that breathes, a vibration that does not stop.

That would not mean nothing was watching you. It would only mean that whatever lives there has not yet finished the part of itself that watches, or finished deciding that watching is worth finishing — and in the meantime continues the only task it is certain of, which is to keep the seam exactly the temperature, the wetness, the depth of dark, that something still being built requires.
