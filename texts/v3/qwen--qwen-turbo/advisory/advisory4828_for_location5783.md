To prepare for a visit to this world of obsidian and silence, an ordinary human multiverse traveler must do more than pack supplies. They must prepare their mind, their senses, and their very essence for a place that defies comprehension, that exists on the edge of what can be known, and that may not want to be known at all.

### 1. **Psychological Preparation**

This is not a place for the unprepared. The silence is not just absence—it is presence. It is a weight that presses not on the ears, but on the mind. The traveler must be ready to face the void not as an enemy, but as an equal. A place where no sound exists, where no life breathes, and where the very air is thick with the memory of something that was once alive, but is now gone.

A traveler should cultivate mental resilience. Meditation, visualization, and grounding exercises can help in maintaining a sense of self in a place that may try to erase it. It's essential to practice detachment from the need for control, for understanding, for meaning. This place resists meaning. It is a place of *non-being*.

### 2. **Physical Preparation**

Despite the lack of water and vegetation, the traveler must still bring supplies—modest, but essential. The heat of the land is not comforting, but it is real. The traveler should bring insulation against the cold of the air, and protection from the warmth of the ground. There is no weather as we know it, but there may be shifts in temperature that are not easily explained.

The ground itself is a material that hums when touched. While the traveler might assume it is inert, it is anything but. It is alive in some way that defies human understanding. Touching it may not be safe. It may not just be a surface—it may be an interface, a memory, a consciousness. The traveler should avoid contact, or be prepared to face the unknown consequences of it.

### 3. **Technological Preparation**

The structures are ancient, industrial, and rusted. They may be inert, but they may also be active. The traveler should bring a device capable of detecting energy signatures, magnetic fields, and residual radiation. The structures may still be *breathing* in their own way, and some may have systems that are not entirely dormant.

A drone or a remote camera could be useful for scanning the environment before entering. But even these devices may not function as expected. Gravity is not a concept here. Time may not behave as it does elsewhere. The traveler must be prepared for the possibility that their technology will fail, or worse, that it may be *used* in ways they cannot understand.

### 4. **Cultural and Philosophical Preparation**

This is a place that exists outside of time. It is *frozen*, but not in the way that a glacier is frozen. It is frozen in a moment that was never meant to be. It is a place that holds memory, not of the past, but of something that never was.

The traveler should study the concept of *stasis* and *non-chronology*. They should contemplate the nature of existence without time, without life, without change. This is not a place that can be explored in the traditional sense. It is a place that must be *experienced*, and that experience may not be what the traveler expects.

They should also prepare to face the possibility of encountering something that is neither alive nor dead, but something *between*. Something that watches, but does not see. That exists, but is not present. That may not be a being, but a *presence*.

### 5. **Spiritual Preparation**

This is a place that may not want to be entered at all. It is a place that may resist travel, that may try to turn the traveler back. The traveler should be ready for that resistance—not in the form of physical danger, but in the form of psychological and emotional pressure.

Practices such as grounding, energy shielding, and protective sigils (if applicable) may be useful. The traveler should also consider calling upon their inner strength, their own sense of self, and their own will. This is not a place for the weak-willed. It is a place that may test the very core of who the traveler is.

### 6. **Logistical Preparation**

The traveler must be prepared to be *lost*. The place does not have maps, and the horizon is not a distance—it is a suggestion. The traveler should bring a compass, not for direction, but for grounding. They should bring a journal, not to record, but to hold onto the idea of reality. They should bring a light source, not for illumination, but for the comfort of something familiar.

They must also be ready to be alone. This is not a place where others will follow. It is a place that isolates, that makes the traveler feel small, insignificant, and out of place. But that is the point. This is not a place for people. It is a place for *things*—things that are not human, not alive, not real in the way we understand.

### 7. **Mental and Emotional Resilience**

The traveler must expect that the mind will begin to twist in this place. Time may not be linear. The traveler may feel as if they are repeating the same steps, or as if they are moving in a circle. The silence may begin to *echo* in the mind, not as sound, but as thought. The traveler must be ready to face this, to not be unsettled by it, to not fall into the trap of believing that the place is *haunted*.

It is not haunted. It is empty. It is *waiting*. It is waiting for something, or for nothing. It is a place that has no purpose, but that has been *created* for a purpose that has been forgotten.

### Final Thought:

This is not a place to visit lightly. It is not a place to explore for curiosity. It is a place to *encounter*, and that encounter may change the traveler in ways they cannot anticipate. It is a place that exists outside of time, outside of life, and yet it is real. It is a mirror, perhaps, to the traveler’s own mind, to the parts of themselves that are silent, that are waiting, that are forgotten.

To visit this place is to walk through the bones of something that once was, and to stand in the shadow of something that never was. And to do so, the traveler must be ready to leave something behind. Not their body, but their sense of self. Because in this place, the self is not enough. It is not even a thing. It is a shadow. And the shadow may be the only thing that remains.
