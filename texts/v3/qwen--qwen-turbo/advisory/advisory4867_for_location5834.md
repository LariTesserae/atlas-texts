To prepare for visiting this subterranean world—this hollow carved by time and something older, more patient—you must not arrive as a traveler from the surface. You must arrive as a guest of the earth, as a being who has learned to listen, to slow down, to breathe in the stillness, and to allow the place to shape you as much as you shape it.

### 1. **Prepare Your Body to Adapt to a Different Rhythm**
This is a place where time is not measured in minutes or hours, but in cycles of moisture, slow growth, and the patient unfolding of existence. Your body will need to adjust to a different pace. You will not be in a hurry here. Train yourself to move slowly, to take your time, to let your breath align with the rhythm of the place. You may feel the urge to move quickly, but resist it. The place will respond to your urgency, and it may not be welcoming.

You should also prepare your body for cold—though not the kind you are used to. This cold is not sharp or sudden. It is persistent, like a memory that lingers. Wear layers that can be easily adjusted, and be ready to add or remove clothing as you feel the shift in temperature. The cold does not numb, but it does settle into your bones. You must learn to live with it, not fight it.

### 2. **Bring Light, But Learn to Let It Go**
You will bring a light source—flashlight, headlamp, or whatever you carry—but do not rely on it. The light here is not artificial. It is ambient, diffuse, and soft. It is a light that lives in the space itself, that clings to the edges of things, and that you will come to trust more than your own. Learn to see with it. Let it guide your steps, not your eyes. Your light may be extinguished by the presence of this place, and that is not a failure. It is a sign that you are being welcomed.

### 3. **Bring Water, But Don’t Drink From It**
Water is always nearby—pools, streams, condensation on the walls—but it is not safe to drink. The place is alive in ways that are not entirely human, and you must respect that. Carry your own supply, and treat any water you encounter with caution. You may be tempted to drink, but the water here is not just liquid. It is part of the ecosystem, part of the memory of the place, and you are not its guest in that way.

### 4. **Leave Behind the Surface World’s Noise**
This is a place of silence, but not of emptiness. There are sounds—whispers, drips, the soft hiss of moisture—but they are not the kind of noise you know. You may feel the urge to speak, to fill the silence, but do not. The silence is not a void; it is a presence. Speak only when necessary, and then in a hushed tone. Let your voice fall into the space, and see how it is absorbed or reflected.

### 5. **Prepare Your Mind to Be Changed**
This is not a place where you will learn things in the way you are used to. It is a place where you will be *experienced*. It is not that it is dangerous, but it is not meant for the surface. The rules here are not the rules of the world above. They are older, slower, and more deliberate. You must be open to the idea that you do not control this place. You are a visitor, but you are also a participant in a process that is not entirely your own.

You may feel a slight unease, a sense that you are not entirely natural here. That is not a bad thing. It is a sign that you are in a place that was made, not grown. It is not a place of ghosts, but of *thoughts*—thoughts that have been shaped by time, by patience, by something that understood the value of stillness.

### 6. **Bring Nothing That Cannot Be Left Behind**
This is a place of density, of layers, of things that are close and connected. You must not bring anything that will disrupt that. Do not bring plastic, metal, or anything that will not dissolve or be absorbed into the space. If you carry something that is not part of this world, it may be taken from you—either by the place itself or by the presence that watches from the shadows.

You may also find that you lose things. A glove, a piece of clothing, a small item you thought you had—this is not a loss. It is a transformation. The place does not take things out of malice. It takes them because they no longer belong.

### 7. **Bring a Mind Open to Wonder, Not Fear**
This is not a dangerous place in the traditional sense, but it is not safe in the way the surface is. You are not in a place of danger, but of *otherness*. There is no fauna, no creatures that move with intent. But there is a presence—a subtle, slow-moving awareness that watches, that listens, that waits. You are not alone. You are part of something larger, something older than you.

Do not fear it. Do not expect it to be hostile. But do not expect it to be familiar, either. This is not a place to conquer. It is a place to be *known* by. It is a place where the rules are different, where the light is not from a source, and where the silence is not empty.

### 8. **Leave Nothing Behind That You Cannot Live Without**
This is a place that will not let you go easily. It is not a place you can simply exit and return to the surface as if nothing happened. You may come out changed, and that is okay. But you must be ready to carry that change with you. Bring nothing that you are not willing to leave behind. You may return to the surface, but you will not be the same.

### 9. **Bring a Journal, But Not for Words**
You may want to record your experiences, but the place does not respond to words. It responds to presence, to silence, to the act of simply being. You may find that your journal fills with strange symbols, or that the ink runs in unexpected ways. Do not be alarmed. These are not errors. They are messages from the place, written in a language older than your own.

### 10. **Bring Your Curiosity, and Let It Be Slow**
You will not understand this place in a single visit. You will not comprehend it in a day, a week, or even a lifetime. But you will *feel* it. You will feel the weight of the stone, the hum of the moss, the slow drip of water, and the soft, ambient light. You will feel the presence of something that has been here longer than you, longer than the surface world, longer than the sun itself.

This is not a place to be conquered. It is a place to be *visited*. And when you leave, you will not be the same. You will carry it with you, in your bones, in your breath, in the quiet spaces of your mind.

Go gently. Go with reverence. And when you return to the surface, remember that you were part of something larger, something deeper, something that lives in the slow, patient embrace of the earth. You were there. You were seen. And you were changed.
