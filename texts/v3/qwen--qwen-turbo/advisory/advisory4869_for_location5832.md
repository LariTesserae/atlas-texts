To prepare for visiting this vast, waterlogged expanse, the ordinary human multiverse traveler must approach with a mind both grounded and open, aware that the laws of reality here are not simply different—they are *redefined*. This is not a place to be explored in the conventional sense. It is a living, breathing, ancient entity that resists categorization and resists the assumptions of the traveler. To go there is to step into a realm where the familiar fades, and the unknown becomes the only constant.

### **Preparation: Physical and Mental**

#### **1. Embrace the Water:**
Water is not a hazard here—it is the essence of the place, the medium through which all things flow. Your gear must be designed to work with water, not against it. This means:

- **Waterproof, yet breathable clothing**—not just from the rain, but from the perpetual moisture that seeps up from the ground. Think of garments that are light but have the texture of moss or lichen. Avoid synthetic fibers that might trap heat or moisture.
- **Waders or waterproof boots**—but not the kind you’d expect. The ground is a shifting, unstable mosaic. You may find yourself sinking into silt, or slipping on slick stone. Your shoes must be flexible, but also provide grip. Consider weighted soles or boots with soft, rubbery treads that can grip the slippery surfaces.
- **A waterproof shelter**—but not a tent. This place doesn’t have wind, but it has a *presence*. A shelter made of natural materials, something that can be buried, folded, or blended into the landscape. Maybe even a living structure, like a tarp made from the gelatinous fungi, which might be bioluminescent or at least not decay quickly.

#### **2. Understand the Light:**
The sky is always overcast, and the light is dim, muted, and cold. This affects more than just visibility—it affects perception, mood, and even the way you think.

- **Carry a light source**—not just for navigation, but for your own sanity. In this place, darkness is not absence, but a presence. A headlamp, a lantern, or even a small, glowing object (like a bioluminescent fungus) might be vital.
- **Be aware of the light’s psychological effects**: The dimness is not just a lack of brightness—it’s a kind of *suffocation*. It can make your thoughts feel heavy, your movements slow. You may need to carry a way to remind yourself that you are not swallowed by the gloom. Perhaps a small mirror, or a piece of reflective metal, to create a flash of light and break the monotony.

#### **3. Tread Lightly:**
The ground is alive in a way that defies normal understanding. It is not just a surface to walk on—it is a *living thing*, a shifting mosaic of pebbles, silt, and dark stone. You may find yourself sinking into it, or feeling it shift beneath your feet.

- **Test the ground before stepping**—even if it looks solid. Be prepared to move slowly, carefully, and with a sense of awareness.
- **Avoid heavy boots**—they may sink or make a noise that draws attention. Try to move with a light, deliberate step, as if you are walking on a fragile surface that might respond to your weight.
- **Be prepared for the ground to move**—not just in terms of erosion, but in the subtle, almost imperceptible ways that the landscape seems to *breathe*.

#### **4. Navigate the Fungi:**
The vegetation is not plants in the traditional sense—it is a living carpet of gelatinous fungi that pulse and shift. These are not just obstacles; they may be part of the ecosystem, and possibly even part of the *intelligence* of the place.

- **Do not disturb the fungi unnecessarily**—if you step on them, do so gently. They may have a reaction. Some may glow faintly in response to your presence. Others may react in ways that are less predictable.
- **Be aware of the bioluminescence**—if you see a faint glow, it may be a sign of something living, or something watching. Some of these fungi may be symbiotic with creatures or even with the land itself.
- **Avoid eating them**—unless you have a reason to. Their properties are unknown. Some may be medicinal, others may be hallucinogenic, and others may be toxic. Treat them with respect, not curiosity.

#### **5. Prepare for the Creatures:**
The fauna is not large, but it is *everywhere*. These are small, skittering things that move in patterns, as if they have a purpose, but it is not clear what that purpose is.

- **Carry a small, non-lethal deterrent**—not a weapon, but something that can create a sound or light to deter creatures that might be curious or aggressive.
- **Be aware of your surroundings**—these creatures move in and out of the water, under the fungal carpets, and through the sparse openings in the earth. You may not see them, but you will feel them, or hear them, or feel their presence.
- **Do not provoke them**—they may not be hostile, but they are not friendly either. They are part of the ecosystem, and they may be watching you more than you are watching them.

#### **6. Mental and Emotional Preparations:**

This is not a place for the weak-willed. The scale is vast, the silence is deep, and the presence is ancient. You must be prepared to face the weight of time, the weight of stillness, and the weight of being forgotten.

- **Bring a journal or a way to record your thoughts**—not just for documentation, but for your own sanity. The lack of clear boundaries and the slow, deliberate rhythm of this place can make your mind feel unmoored. Writing can anchor you.
- **Be prepared for the strange**—this is not a place where logic holds. The water remembers. The ground breathes. The fungi pulse like a heartbeat. You must be open to the idea that reality here is not as it seems, and that your perceptions may be altered.
- **Carry a sense of humility**—this place is older than anything you’ve known. It is not a place you will conquer. It is a place you will *encounter*. You are not the center of this world, and you must be willing to listen, to watch, and to learn.

#### **7. Technology and Tools:**

The technology here is primitive, but not absent. There are tools, fragments of metal, and structures that suggest a past. But this is not a place where modern technology will work as it does elsewhere.

- **Bring tools that can be used for practical purposes**—shaped stones, sharpened bone, or simple tools. These may not be useful, but they may be necessary. You may find that the land responds to them in unexpected ways.
- **Be careful with metal**—some of the fragments you find may have a strange effect on the environment. They may resonate, or attract creatures, or even disappear. Treat them with caution.
- **Carry a compass**—but be aware that the magnetic field here may be unstable. The place may not have a north, or it may shift. A compass may not help you navigate. You may need to rely on the stars, the fungi, or the movement of the water.

#### **8. The Unspoken Preparations:**

There is something deeper here, something that cannot be measured with tools or logic. The place is not just ancient—it is *aware* in a way that defies explanation. It remembers. It feels.

- **Be ready to face the unknown**—not just the physical unknown, but the metaphysical. This is a place where the boundary between life and death is thin, and where the past lingers like a ghost.
- **Carry a sense of wonder**—this is not a place to be afraid of. It is a place to be curious about. You may not understand it, but you can learn to *feel* it.
- **Be ready to leave**—and to leave behind more than just your tools. This place may change you in ways you cannot predict. You may return different, or not at all.

### **Final Thoughts:**

This is not a place to be visited lightly. It is a place that demands presence, not just in body, but in spirit. It is a place that tests the limits of what you know, and offers no easy answers. But if you are willing to walk its endless, shifting flatness, to listen to its low, rhythmic hum, and to feel the weight of its ancient stillness, you may find something that no other traveler has found—not knowledge, not power, but a *presence*.

You are not the master of this place. You are its guest. And in its presence, you may find yourself transformed.
