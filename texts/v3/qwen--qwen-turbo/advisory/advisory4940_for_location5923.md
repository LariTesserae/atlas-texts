### 1. **Physical Preparation: The Body as a Vessel**

The traveler must first prepare their body for the environment. The city is not a place of comfort. It is a place of resistance, of moisture that clings to the skin, of air that is heavy with mist and life, and of cold that seeps into the very marrow.

- **Layering is Key**: Wear multiple layers of water-resistant yet breathable clothing. Base layers of wool or synthetic fibers to wick away moisture, middle layers for insulation, and outer layers that are water-repellent but not completely waterproof — the city breathes, and so should you.
- **Footwear Must Be Sturdy and Waterproof**: The ground is soft, squelching underfoot, and the water is always near. Boots with deep treads and waterproof membranes are a necessity. If the traveler is to walk through the city, they must be prepared for the constant squelch of mud and the occasional ankle-deep puddle.
- **Protection from the Cold**: The cold is not seasonal, but it is persistent. The traveler should carry warm gear, including a down jacket, thermal socks, and a insulated sleeping bag if staying overnight. The chill is not just in the air, but in the very structure of the place — it is a cold that lingers, that seeps into bones.
- **Sanitation and Hygiene**: The city is alive, and so are the microorganisms that thrive in the moisture. Travelers should carry antimicrobial wipes, hand sanitizer, and a way to purify water if necessary. The air, though not toxic, is thick with spores, insects, and the remnants of life.

This is not a place to visit on a whim. It is a place that demands presence. The traveler must be prepared for the psychological weight of a city that is *still* there, even though the people are gone. It is a place that is not abandoned, but *claimed* by time, water, and growth.

- **Expect the Unexpected**: The city is full of life — not just the flora and fauna, but the *presence* of something more. The traveler should be prepared to encounter strange phenomena: the way light filters through the canopy, the way the water seems to breathe, the way the buildings seem to lean in, as if listening.

This is not a place for the casual explorer. It demands a toolkit that balances survival and curiosity.

- **Emergency Supplies**: A first aid kit, a whistle, a fire starter, and a way to signal for help should be part of the traveler’s pack. This is not a place for reckless exploration.

This is not just a place of environment; it is a place of memory. The traveler should approach it with a sense of reverence for what has come before.

- **Learn the Story of the City**: If possible, research the civilization that built this place. The traveler should understand that the city is not just a ruin, but a home, a home that has been taken over by nature.
- **Respect the Place**: This is not a place to disturb. The traveler should leave no trace, avoid breaking branches, and avoid disturbing the wildlife. The city is alive, but it is also fragile.
- **Be Mindful of the Unknown**: The city may hold secrets — not in the form of artifacts or treasures, but in the way it *remembers*. The traveler should be open to the possibility that this place is more than it appears.
