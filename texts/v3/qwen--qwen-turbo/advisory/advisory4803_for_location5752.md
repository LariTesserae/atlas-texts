To prepare for a visit to the *Labyrinth of Breath and Bone*, one must approach with more than just physical readiness. This is not a journey you take with a map, a compass, or even a sense of direction. It is a pilgrimage into the deep, the unseen, the unspoken. A place that does not welcome you by any measure of human logic—or even of sanity. It is a place that has *been*, and that, in its own way, *knows* you are here.

---

### Physical Preparation

You must be in peak physical condition. This is not a place for the weak, the indecisive, or the easily rattled. The labyrinth is high, and its thin air will test your breath. Every step will be a battle with gravity, with your own body, with the weight of the mountain pressing down from above. You will need a sturdy, warm layering system—layers that can shift with the temperature, which is not just warm but *intimate*, a warmth that sticks to you like memory. It is not the warmth of comfort; it is the warmth of a secret, of something watching and waiting.

Bring a reliable light source. Not just a lantern or a headlamp, but something that will not fail. The light here is not constant—only in slivers. The darkness is not empty, and it will not let you go easily. You will need a light that can cut through the gloom, but even that may not be enough. The shadows here move. They are not just static, but *alive* in their own way—watching, shifting, reacting to your presence.

For navigation, you will not need a map. Not in the traditional sense. The labyrinth is not linear, and it is not static. It is a place that rearranges itself, that folds in on itself, that seems to exist in multiple dimensions at once. You will need to be able to feel the space—not just see it. You must be able to sense the shifts in the architecture of the place, to notice the subtle changes in the air, the weight of the stone, the way the echoes behave. This is a place that is *sensitive* to your movement, your breath, your thoughts.

Bring a pack that is not too heavy, but not too light. You will need to carry water, food, and a means to purify water. The water here is not constant, but it is persistent. Some of it is fresh, some of it is briny, and some of it is... something else. You must find a way to filter or boil it. The mineral-stained water may not be toxic, but it may be *alive*—or at least, *aware*. You do not want to drink something that is still tasting you.

---

### Mental and Emotional Preparation

This place is not just a labyrinth of stone and air—it is a labyrinth of *mind*. It is a place that will test your perception, your sense of self, your ability to distinguish between what is real and what is not. The sounds here are not just echoes; they are *voices*—whispers, murmurs, clicks, rustles. They are not human, but they are not entirely alien, either. They are *there*, and they are watching.

You must be prepared to face the fact that this place may not want you to leave. It is not a place of escape, but of entanglement. It is a place that has been shaped by life, not by will. It is a place that has *its own purpose*, and it is not obligated to serve yours. You must not come expecting to conquer it, to conquer yourself, to find anything that fits within the framework of your own understanding.

You must be ready to accept that your own sense of time, space, and self may be altered. This is not a place of permanence. Everything here is shifting, not in the way of a storm or a flood, but in the way of a slow, patient unfolding. You may find yourself returning to the same place again and again, or you may find your path leading you deeper, further, into something that was never meant to be found.

You must be ready to face the *presence* of the creatures. Not just their physical forms, but their *awareness*. They are not mindless. They are not hostile, but they are not friendly either. They are *there*. They are watching. They are *part* of this place, and they may not wish to be seen. You must move with care, with quiet, with respect—because this is not a place that mistakes silence for absence.

---

### Psychological and Philosophical Preparation

This is not a place of purpose. It is a place of *presence*. It is not designed, not built, not even *created* in the way we understand creation. It is a place that has *been*, and that continues to be, without the need for a creator. It is not a place of meaning, but of *existence*.

You may find yourself questioning the nature of reality. This is not a place of illusions, but of things that *are* in their own way. The boundaries between self and environment, between life and non-life, between time and space, may blur. You may no longer be sure if you are the observer, or if you are the observed.

You must be prepared to leave something behind. Not just your belongings, but your assumptions, your beliefs, your need for control. This place does not tolerate the arrogance of human understanding. It does not need your logic, your rules, your sense of order. It exists outside of those things.

If you enter this place, you must do so with the humility of a child, the curiosity of a scientist, and the caution of a traveler in a foreign land. You must be willing to be changed. Not in a way that is comfortable, but in a way that is necessary.

---

### Final Notes

This is not a place for the faint of heart. It is not a place for the unready. It is not a place for the lost. It is a place for those who are willing to listen. To the stone. To the water. To the breath of the mountain. To the silence between the sounds. To the presence of something that is not quite human, but is not quite not.

If you go, go with an open mind and a steady heart. But know this: you may not come back the same. You may not even come back at all. The Labyrinth of Breath and Bone does not give passage freely. It gives it only to those it chooses. And it may not choose you.

But if it does... be ready. Be ready for what it shows you. And be ready for what it makes of you.
