To prepare for a visit to this labyrinthine, subterranean expanse—a city swallowed by the earth and reimagined by time—one must approach it not as a tourist, nor as a scholar, but as a pilgrim to a world that has long since ceased to exist in the form we know. This is a place that does not welcome the living in the conventional sense. It is not hostile, nor is it welcoming. It is simply *there*, and it has its own rhythm, its own rules, its own kind of awareness.

### Physical Preparation

First, one must prepare the body. This is not a place that tolerates the modern, the fragile, the unaccustomed. The air is thick with moisture, not just in humidity, but in something more visceral. The dampness does not simply wet the skin—it clings, it saturates, it becomes part of you. To walk here, you must be prepared for the slow, unrelenting weight of it. You must pack clothes that breathe, that can absorb and release moisture, that are not rigid or synthetic. Wool, leather, and thick, layered fabrics are your best bet. Avoid anything that traps sweat. You will get wet, and you will stay wet, but you must not let that become a death sentence.

Footwear is critical. The ground is uneven, the streets are not straight, and the surfaces are slick with water and algae. You need boots that can grip stone, metal, and moss. The soles must be deep-treaded, but not too rigid. The city is not going to be kind to your ankles. You should also consider wearing gaiters to prevent water and debris from slipping into your socks.

You must also bring a reliable light source. The light in this place is not like the light you know. It is dim, it is filtered, it is not steady. You cannot rely on the city’s own illumination. Bring a headlamp, a lantern, and a backup. You may find that your light is not enough, that the shadows here are deeper, that the darkness is not just absence of light but something more. You must be prepared to carry your own light, and to keep it burning.

### Mental and Emotional Preparation

This place is not just physically daunting—it is mentally and emotionally draining. The city is not just old; it is *worn*. It is a city that has been shaped by time, by water, by the weight of its own history. It does not just exist; it *remembers*. You will feel it in your bones. The silence is not empty. It is filled with the echoes of what once was. The sound of water is not just a sound; it is a voice, a whisper, a murmur of the city’s breath.

You must prepare for the *weight of it*. This is not a place that you can enter with the mindset of a casual explorer. You must enter with patience, with humility, with the understanding that you are not the center of this world. You are a guest, and the city is not obligated to welcome you. It is a place that may test you, not with danger, but with an overwhelming sense of insignificance. You may feel lost, not just in the physical labyrinth, but in the *psychological* one.

This is a place that may make you question reality. The light is not steady, the sound is not constant, the space is not as it seems. You may find yourself disoriented, lost in a city that does not follow the rules of the surface world. You must be prepared for that. You must be ready to trust your instincts, your senses, and your intuition. You must not be afraid to move slowly, to take your time, to let the city guide you, even if it is not doing so in a way that makes sense.

### Equipment and Supplies

You must carry more than just a lantern. You need a map, but it will not be a map you can trust. The city is constantly changing, not in a violent way, but in a *patient*, *gradual* way. The streets may shift over time, the buildings may rearrange their positions, the water may carve new paths. You must carry a map, but you must also be ready to discard it when necessary. Have a notebook, a pen, and a way to mark your path. Your memory may not be enough.

Bring supplies for survival, not just for your body, but for your mind. You must bring enough food, enough water, enough tools to last you a long time. This is not a place that will provide for you. You must be prepared to be self-sufficient. You may find that the city offers no food, no shelter, no comfort. You must be ready for that.

You may also want to bring a small device for recording your thoughts, your experiences. This is not just a place to observe—it is a place to be *changed* by. You must be ready to document the changes that occur within you, as well as the changes that occur in the city.

### Psychological and Spiritual Preparation

This place is not just a city. It is an *entity*. It is a place that has lived, that has remembered, that has waited. It is not a place that you can simply observe. It will watch you, it will test you, it will shape you. You must prepare for the possibility that you will not return the same as when you entered.

You must be prepared for the possibility that you will not understand this place. You may come expecting answers, but this place is not built to give them. It is built to *hold* them. It is a place that is more than the sum of its parts. It is a place that is not just *there*—it is *alive* in a way that is difficult to define.

You must also be prepared for the possibility that this place may not want to be found. It may not want to be visited. It may not want to be understood. It may even try to *push* you away, not with force, but with silence, with stillness, with the weight of the earth pressing down on you.

But if you are willing to go in with open eyes, open mind, and open heart, then you may find something that is rare: a place that is not just a place, but a *moment* in time, suspended, waiting, remembering.

This is not a place for the faint of heart. It is for the brave, the patient, the curious, and the willing. If you are ready for that, then go. But do not come back the same. The city has already changed you. You just don’t know it yet.
