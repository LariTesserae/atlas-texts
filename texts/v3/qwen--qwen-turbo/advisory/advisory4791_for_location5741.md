To prepare for a visit to this subterranean metropolis — this city of stone and steam, this place of raw potential and unformed purpose — you must approach it not as a tourist, nor even as an explorer. You must approach it as a student of transformation, as a witness to the slow, patient unfolding of something that has not yet decided what it will be.

First, you must prepare your body. The heat is relentless. The air is thick with moisture, not the kind you find in a swamp, but the kind that clings to your skin, heavy and insistent. Your clothing must be breathable, but not too light. You will need layers, not for warmth — this place is not cold — but for protection. The humidity is not just in the air; it is in the very bones of the city. It will seep into your clothes, your gear, your very thoughts. You will need to carry a water source, not for drinking (though that will be necessary), but for staying cool. You will need to manage your body’s heat, for the city is alive with it. The steam, the machinery, the constant motion — all of it adds to the ambient warmth. You will sweat, and you will need to manage that.

Second, your tools. This is not a place that will welcome your technology. The city is built, but not finished. It is a place of half-formed structures, of scaffolds and cranes, of machines that are old but still laboring. You will need to carry a headlamp, not just for light, but for the psychological comfort of having a fixed point of illumination in a place that is dim and shifting. Your boots must be sturdy, for the ground is not solid, but soft — not in the way of mud, but in the way of something that is still settling, still learning how to hold its shape. You might find yourself sinking slightly, or slipping on the damp stone. You will need to move carefully, not because the city is dangerous, but because it is still in the process of becoming.

Your equipment must be water-resistant. The drizzle never stops, but it is not rain. It is a slow, steady, persistent moisture, like a city’s breath. It will condense on your gear, on your lenses, on your skin. You will need to keep your electronics in sealed cases. Your map — if you carry one — will be useless. This is not a place that can be mapped in the traditional sense. The streets are raw, the structures incomplete. You will not navigate by landmarks, but by the rhythm of the city. You will learn to read it by the sound of the machinery, by the patterns of the bioluminescent plants, by the way the steam rises and falls.

Third, your mind. This city is not a place of people, but of labor, of purpose. It is not a place where you will find warmth, comfort, or familiarity. The fauna is present, but it is not friendly. The creatures here are not pets, nor are they threats. They are part of the system, part of the city’s slow, deliberate process. You will need to observe them with respect, not as creatures to be tamed or avoided, but as entities that have their own logic. The insects, the birds, the shadowed figures in the corners — they are not anomalies; they are the city’s inhabitants. You may not understand them, but you must not fear them.

The city is not yet a city in the traditional sense. It is a place in between. It is not a wilderness, nor a civilization. It is a place of transformation, of slow, patient change. You must be prepared to accept that. There is no clear order, no defined rules. You will not find signs, or directions, or even a clear sense of where you are. You must learn to move with the city, not against it. You must be patient. You must be willing to listen — to the sounds, to the rhythms, to the way the city breathes.

Fourth, your spirit. This place will test you. It will not be kind. It will not be welcoming. It will not give you answers. But it will give you experience. You will leave changed, not because you have conquered it, but because you have allowed yourself to be shaped by it. You will not leave with a story of adventure, but with a story of becoming. You will leave with a sense of what it means to be part of something that is not yet finished.

You must approach this place not with the expectation of comfort, but with the understanding that you are entering a space that is still learning how to be. You must be humble. You must be present. You must be willing to walk through the damp, the heat, the noise, the strange creatures, and the slow, relentless labor of a city that is not yet a city.

This is not a place to be rushed. It is a place to be felt. To be seen. To be understood. And perhaps, in the end, to be part of.
