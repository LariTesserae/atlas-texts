To prepare for a visit to this vast, enclosed cavity—this cathedral of stone and steel, this forgotten cathedral of industry and silence—a traveler must approach it not as a tourist, but as a kind of archaeologist of the unknown. This is not a place to be entered lightly, nor with the expectation of comfort or clear direction. It is a site of strange history, of remnants of a civilization (or civilizations) that have long since gone silent, but whose echoes still linger in the air, in the flickering lights, in the whispering hum of machines that once pulsed with life.

### **Preparation: The Mindset**

The first and most important preparation is mental. This is not a place that can be understood in a straightforward way. It defies conventional maps, conventional logic, and conventional experience. It is a place that resists interpretation, and perhaps even resists being observed. To walk into it, one must be ready to let go of expectations, to embrace ambiguity, to accept that some things in this place may never make sense.

You must not enter with the mindset of "discovery" or "exploration" in the usual sense. This is more like a pilgrimage to a relic that is not even certain to be real. You must be ready for the possibility that the place may not want to be found, or that it may not want you to stay. You must be ready to leave it as you found it, to respect the quiet, the weight, the stillness.

### **Physical Preparation**

**1. Equipment:**

- **Lighting:** The ambient glow of the lichen is faint, and the flickering of the machines is unreliable. Carry a good, durable headlamp with multiple settings—bright, dim, and red. The red light may help preserve your night vision and avoid attracting attention from strange insect-like creatures or whatever else might be in the shadows.
  
- **Battery Power:** This place is not connected to any power grid. Your equipment must be fully charged, and you must carry spare batteries, or better yet, a portable power source. The machines here may still have some residual energy, but you cannot rely on that.

- **Communication Devices:** Bring a reliable radio, if possible, and a backup. But be aware that this place may interfere with signals. You may not be able to reach anyone in the outside world. If you are part of a group, consider using hand signals or pre-arranged codes.

- **Navigation Tools:** A compass, a map, a GPS (if it works here), and a way to mark your path are essential. The space may seem small, but the density of the environment and the shifting shadows can disorient you quickly. Consider using a marker or a trail of stones to return.

- **Body Armor or Protective Gear:** The structures are jagged, the metal is rusted and possibly unstable. Wear sturdy boots, long sleeves, and gloves. There may be sharp edges, and the air may contain particulate matter from the dust and rust. A mask could be useful.

**2. Supplies:**

- **Water and Rations:** This place may not have any water, and the air is dry. Bring enough to last for a day, and perhaps more. Do not rely on the lichen for hydration—it is not safe.

- **Medication and First Aid Kit:** You may not be able to get help quickly. The place is isolated, and there is no guarantee of rescue.

- **Spare Clothing:** The temperature is warm, but it can be oppressive. Bring clothing that is breathable but not too light. The heat is not comfortable; it is the heat of something that has been running for a long time, and it will make your body sweat and strain.

**3. Equipment for Observation:**

- **Camera or Recording Device:** You may want to document your visit, but be aware that this place may not want to be recorded. Some believe that certain places resist being observed, or that cameras may pick up things that are not meant to be seen. Use caution and be prepared to leave the device behind if necessary.

- **Notebook and Pen:** Write things down. This place is not made for memory, and the details will slip away quickly. Even if you are not sure what you saw, record it. The act of writing may help you remember.

### **Psychological Preparation**

This place is not just physically strange—it is emotionally and psychologically overwhelming. It is a place of absence, of something that was once full of life and now is not. It is a place that lingers in the mind long after you leave, that may haunt your dreams or your waking thoughts.

You must be prepared to feel the weight of it. You may feel small, out of place, or even afraid. But fear is not the goal. Curiosity is. You must approach this place with humility, with a willingness to be changed by it.

Some people describe an "uncanny" feeling, as if the place is watching them, as if it knows they are there. This is normal. If you feel that, do not react with fear. Acknowledge it, and move forward carefully.

### **Mental and Emotional Readiness**

- **Let Go of Control:** This place is not for those who want to be in control. It will not follow your commands. It will not respond to your expectations. You must be willing to go with the flow, to accept the unknown.

- **Be Open to the Unexplainable:** This is not a logical place. It may not have a clear beginning or end. It may not have a purpose, at least not in the way you expect. Be ready to accept that some things in this place are simply *there*, and that is enough.

- **Prepare for the Possibility of Time Distortion:** It is said that some places distort time—stretch it, compress it, or make it feel like it has stopped. You may feel as if you have been here for hours, or just a few minutes. The passage of time is not to be trusted here.

- **Be Ready for the Silence:** This place is not silent, but it is not loud in the way that you expect. There is a kind of deep, constant vibration that may make your bones feel heavy. It is not noise, but it is not rest either. You must be able to live with that.

### **Final Considerations**

This is a place that may not welcome you. It is not a place you enter with the expectation of returning. It is a place that may change you, that may leave something of itself in you, and that may not want to let you go.

If you go, do not rush. Walk slowly. Look carefully. Listen. And when you are done, leave it as you found it. Do not take anything. Do not disturb anything. This place is not a ruin—it is a tomb. And some tombs are not meant to be disturbed.

But if you must go, be ready. Be prepared. And above all, be ready to be changed.
