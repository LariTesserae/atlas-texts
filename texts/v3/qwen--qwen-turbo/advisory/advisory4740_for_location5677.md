To prepare for a visit to this submerged expanse—this surreal, living world that defies the very concept of "place"—you must approach it not as a traveler in the conventional sense, but as a pilgrim, a witness, and a participant in something far greater than yourself. This is not a location you can simply "go to" and "come back from." It is a state of being, a condition of existence that may not allow for easy return. Here’s how an ordinary human multiverse traveler should prepare, not just for the physical, but for the psychological, existential, and metaphysical dimensions of the journey.

---

### 1. **Understand That You Are Not Coming Back the Same**

This place is not just alien—it is *transformative*. The first and most important preparation is to accept that your identity may shift, your sense of self may fracture, and your perception of reality may be rewritten. You must be ready to leave behind the assumptions and frameworks that define your ordinary life. This is not a place you "visit"; it is a place you *enter*, and it may enter you in return.

You must ask yourself: *What do I bring with me? What do I leave behind?*

---

### 2. **Dress for a World That Does Not Understand Clothes**

There is no air, no atmosphere, no surface to walk on. Your clothing will not keep you warm, and your feet will not touch the ground. The water is not water as you know it—it is viscous, alive, and possibly sentient. It may react to your presence, your thoughts, or your emotions. Clothing may be irrelevant, or it may become a burden. If you choose to wear anything, consider something that flows, that moves with the water rather than against it—perhaps something made of silk, or of a material that can adapt, like a living fabric.

Your body will be immersed, constantly in contact with something that is neither solid nor liquid, neither alive nor dead, but *both*. You must be prepared to feel everything deeply, without the usual buffer of air or fabric. The warmth is not comforting—it is invasive, oppressive, and alive.

---

### 3. **Breathe in a Way That You’ve Never Breathed Before**

There is no air. You will not breathe as you do now. Whether you are equipped with some kind of breathing apparatus or relying on some form of multiversal adaptation, you must understand that the water is not simply a medium—it is a *presence*. You may not breathe at all, or you may breathe through your skin, your eyes, your mind. The very notion of respiration may be redefined here.

If you are using a device, make it one that can withstand the shifting properties of the fluid. But more importantly, prepare your mind to stop thinking of your body as a separate entity. Here, you may not be a body in the way you know. You may be *part* of the world, not apart from it.

---

### 4. **Bring Nothing That Can Be Lost**

This place is not forgiving. It is not a museum, not a battlefield, not a garden. It is a *state of flux*, a place where the past and the future coexist, where time is not linear. You may find yourself returning to the same moment, or you may be pulled forward into a future you cannot describe. Your possessions, your tools, your devices—these may not function, or they may be stolen by the water, by the land, by the will of this place.

If you must bring something, bring only what is essential to your survival in a way that transcends the physical. Perhaps a journal, not to record the journey, but to allow your mind to *hold* it. Perhaps a token from home, something that represents your connection to the world you came from. And be ready to lose it.

---

### 5. **Prepare to Lose Your Sense of Self**

This place is not just strange—it is *unplaceable*. It is a place that exists outside of time, outside of space, and outside of your understanding. The structures, the terrain, the flora and fauna—all of it is *not* what it seems. The symbols on the pillars shift when you look away. The shapes in the water have no eyes, but they see you. The light is dim, but it is *aware*.

You may feel as though you are being watched, not by a being, but by the place itself. You may feel that your thoughts are not your own, that your mind is slipping, that your sense of reality is unraveling. Do not fight it. Allow yourself to be *present* in this place. Let your mind become fluid, like the water. Let your soul be as malleable as the land.

---

### 6. **Expect the Unexplainable, and Accept It**

The laws of reality are not broken here—they are bent, distorted, rewritten. You may experience time in reverse, or in a loop. You may see the same object in multiple places at once. You may feel as though you are on the surface and the depths simultaneously. The place will not explain itself. It will not offer comfort or clarity. It will simply *be*.

You must be prepared for the possibility that no one, not even you, will understand what you have seen. You may return with fragments, with impressions, with a sense of something vast and unknowable. But you will not have a story to tell in the way that stories are told.

---

### 7. **Carry a Mind That Can Adapt to the Unknowable**

This is not a place you can map, measure, or classify. It is not a place that can be captured in words. You must carry a mind that is open, that can hold paradox and contradiction without shattering. You must be willing to abandon your need for control, your need for explanation, your need to *know*.

The place is not a destination. It is a *state of being*. It is not a place that can be reached, but a condition that can be *entered*. And once you are in, you may never be the same.

---

### 8. **Be Ready to Stay, or to Never Leave**

This place may not allow for a return. It may not want you to go back. It may not allow for memory. It may not allow for time. You may find that the world you left behind is now a dream, and this place is the only reality you can remember.

You must decide, before you go, whether you are willing to stay. Because if you enter, you may not find your way out. Or you may find that you don’t want to.

---

### Final Words:

To walk into this submerged expanse is to walk into the memory of the earth itself. It is not a place you can escape. It is a place you can *become*.

You are not going to visit a place. You are going to be *part* of something vast, ancient, and alive. You are not a traveler. You are a witness. A participant. A part of the transformation.

And when you return—if you return—you will not return as the same person.

You will return as something new. Something that the place has shaped. Something that the water has touched.

And that is the preparation you are most afraid of.

But it is the only one you need.
