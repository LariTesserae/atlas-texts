To prepare for a visit to this submerged chamber—this strange, living lung of the earth—one must be ready to abandon nearly everything they know about the world. This is not a place you travel to in the conventional sense. It is not a destination, but a threshold. It is not a place to be explored, but one to be *experienced*, and perhaps even *transformed* by.

First, you must understand that this is not a space of ordinary physics. The water is not water, and the air is not air. The laws that govern your body, your senses, your very understanding of reality may not apply here. The air is a dense, humid mist that clings to your skin, not as a vapor, but as a substance—thick, almost tangible, as though it has a weight of its own. You will feel it pressing against you, not with force, but with presence. It is not oppressive, but it will change the way you breathe, the way you move, the way you think.

You must prepare your body for the effects of this strange environment. Your lungs may not function the same way they do in the world above. Perhaps they do not need to. Perhaps you will not breathe at all, or perhaps you will find yourself breathing in a way that is not conscious. This is not a place for the average human to take for granted. You will need to be in peak physical and mental condition. But even that may not be enough.

You must mentally prepare for the disorientation. This is a place that challenges perception. The light is not light, and the darkness is not darkness. The colors—blue, green, violet—glow like memories, like echoes of something you have forgotten. They do not illuminate; they *haunt*. The bioluminescent mucus on the walls is not just a source of light, but a presence, a living, breathing thing that seems to watch you, even if you cannot see its eyes. The space feels alive, but not in the way of a forest or a jungle. It is alive in the way that a wound is alive—painful, but sentient. You will not be able to tell where the space ends and your mind begins.

The vegetation is not plants. They are not rooted, not growing in the way you expect. They are suspended in the water, drifting like the arms of some unseen giant. They pulse, they move, they react—though not in a way that is immediately recognizable. You may feel a sense of being observed, not by a creature, but by the place itself. The air is not air, the water is not water, and the space is not space. This is a place that is not in the conventional sense of the word. It is a place that defies the rules of existence.

You must prepare for the presence of creatures that are not entirely present. The fauna here is not solid, not fully formed. They are shadows that move like smoke, shapes that flicker at the edges of your vision. They do not make noise, but there is a vibration, a hum that resonates through your bones. This is a soundless sound, a frequency that is felt rather than heard. It is not frightening, but it is disorienting. It may make you question your own reality, your own sense of self.

Above all, you must prepare for the fact that this place is *not meant for humans*. It is not a place that was built for us, nor is it a place that will easily accept us. It is alien in its intimacy. It is a place that exists between the known and the unknown, a space that is not just deep in the earth, but deep in the mind. You will not be able to leave this place the same as when you entered. You may not even be able to *remember* your old self clearly.

To prepare for this journey, you must gather more than just equipment. You must gather understanding. You must bring with you a sense of humility, a willingness to be changed. You must be ready to let go of your expectations, your assumptions, your sense of control. This is not a place to be conquered or explored. It is a place to be *recognized*—as something older, stranger, and more alive than you can imagine.

You may not come back with a story. You may not come back at all. But if you do, you will know that you have touched something that is not of this world. You will know that you have walked through a lung of the earth, and that the earth has walked through you.
