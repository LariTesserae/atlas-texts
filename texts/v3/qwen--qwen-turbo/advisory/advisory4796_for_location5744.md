### **Physical Preparation**

This means that physical stability is an illusion. You should wear lightweight, flexible footwear—something that allows for gentle movement, not brute force. Boots with good traction are a must, but you’ll quickly learn that even the best boots will not prevent you from sinking slightly into the spongy earth. The dampness of the ground and the air will make your clothes feel heavy, so choose breathable, moisture-wicking fabrics that can handle the humidity. A waterproof layer is also advisable, not because of rain, but because the air is thick with moisture that clings to you, and the ground is always slightly damp.

Carry a small, sturdy pack with essentials: water (you may not find it readily available, though the air is heavy with it), a few energy-dense snacks, a map (though it may not be reliable), a compass (which may not function as expected), and a small, reliable light. The light may be more for your psychological comfort than for practical use, as the place seems to have its own ambient glow, but it’s good to have.
