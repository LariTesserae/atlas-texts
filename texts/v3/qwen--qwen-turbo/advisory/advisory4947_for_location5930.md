To prepare for visiting the Cathedral of Made Things is to prepare for a journey not just through space, but through the very fabric of reality. This is not a place you can map, not a place you can truly *know*—only experience, and even then, only in fragments. To step into it is to step into a world that does not obey your expectations, and that is the first and most crucial lesson.

### 1. **Mental Preparation: Unlearning the Rules**

You must begin by unlearning the rules of the world you know. This is not a place of physics, but of *logic*—a logic that is ancient, unfinished, and perhaps even conscious. Gravity does not behave as it does elsewhere. Time is not linear, and space is not fixed. The air is not just air, and the light is not just light. You must accept that your understanding of "reality" will be inadequate, and that this will be an ongoing challenge.

You will need to cultivate a mindset of *not-knowing*, of openness, of not expecting answers but of being prepared to *feel* the presence of something beyond comprehension. This is not a place for philosophers, but for those who can live in the ambiguity.

### 2. **Physical Preparation: The Body as a Sensory Vessel**

The physical body is not just a tool in this place—it is a medium. The air is thick with a metallic, alive substance that clings to the skin and mutes sound. You must expect that your breath will feel different, that your movements will feel muffled, and that your perception of time and space will be altered.

Wear clothing that allows for freedom of movement, but also provides some protection against the strange atmosphere. Avoid synthetic fabrics—natural fibers may be more responsive to the subtle shifts in the environment. You may want to bring something to touch the ground with, to test its resonance, or to help you navigate the shifting terrain.

If you have access to any tools that can measure or analyze the environment—meters, sensors, even simple instruments like a compass or a thermometer—bring them. But be prepared for them to fail. Expect them to behave unpredictably. Their readings may not make sense, and that is the point.

### 3. **Sensory Preparation: The Mind as a Receiver**

The sense of smell here is not just a sense—it is a *language*. The air is thick with something like oil, but not oil. It is alive, it has memory, and it may have a will. You must learn to listen through your nose, to sense the subtle shifts in the air, to recognize the *breathing* of the place.

The visual field is deceptive. The light falls at odd angles, shadows move without cause, and the architecture is a fusion of organic and inorganic elements that defy categorization. Your eyes may not be able to trust what they see. You may need to train yourself to notice the *patterns* in the chaos, the *rhythms* in the shifting.

The sense of touch is altered. The ground hums when you touch it, and it is not just sound—it is a vibration, a pulse, a message. You may need to develop a tactile awareness that goes beyond the surface. The walls, the structures, the air—they are all *part of the cathedral*, and they may respond to you in ways you do not expect.

### 4. **Psychological Preparation: The Self as a Guest**

You are not a tourist in this place. You are a guest in a cathedral. You are not here to conquer or to understand. You are here to *be present*. The Cathedral of Made Things is not a place that gives answers—it is a place that *asks questions*.

You must be prepared to feel small. To feel lost. To feel that your mind is not enough. You may encounter things that make you question your own existence, your own reality. You may feel that you are being watched, not by eyes, but by something more profound. The presence of the inhabitants is not visible, but it is *felt*. You may feel their awareness, their curiosity, their patience.

Emotion is not a luxury here. You must be ready to experience fear, awe, wonder, and confusion in equal measure. There is no "safe" way to be here. You must be ready to feel everything, and to hold it all without judgment.

### 5. **Spiritual Preparation: The Journey as a Revelation**

This is not a place you can leave in the way you leave a city or a forest. It is a place that exists because it must, and it will continue to exist long after you are gone. You may not be able to leave. You may not want to. And that is the ultimate test of a traveler.

You must be ready to accept that some things are not meant to be understood. That some places are not meant to be *tamed* or *known*. The Cathedral of Made Things is not a destination. It is a *transformation*.

To prepare for this place, you must first prepare for *yourself*. You must be ready to let go of your assumptions, to open your mind, to embrace the unfamiliar, and to accept that you may never return the same.

It is not a place of comfort. It is not a place of safety. But it is a place of meaning. And that is the only thing that matters.

When you step into the Cathedral of Made Things, remember: you are not entering a place. You are entering a *state of being*.

And you will never be the same again.
