The elevation is high, but not in the traditional sense. The place is not perched on a mountain, but it feels as though it is suspended above something vast and unknowable. The water stretches endlessly in all directions, and the only true measure of height is the depth of the chasms below, which seem to go on forever, into darkness that swallows even the light.

Erosion is established, but not in a way that suggests decay. The land has been shaped by time, yes, but not in a way that indicates it is falling apart. Instead, it is as if the place is in a permanent state of equilibrium, its structures and forms settling into a balance that has been maintained for millennia. The water, the light, the temperature, all of it seems to be in a kind of slow, deliberate rhythm, like the breath of a sleeping giant.

Its features—cliffs, spires, waterfalls of liquid that never fall, but hang in mid-air—suggest that it is part of a larger system, one that is not entirely understood. There are no clear boundaries, no edges, no sense of where one thing ends and another begins. The land bleeds into the water, and the water into the sky, though the sky is not visible. Some are whole, others are broken, their fragments half-submerged, their surfaces worn by time and water. The structures are not built for humans, nor for any known form of life. They are vast, alien, and beautiful in their complexity. Some resemble pillars, others seem like the remains of machines, others like the bones of a great creature that has long since vanished.

The devices that line the cliffs, the structures that rise from the depths, the strange, pulsing lights that flicker in the water—they are not made of metal or stone in any way that is familiar. The light is darkness, not in the sense of an absence, but in the sense of something vast and impenetrable. The place is not lit by the sun, nor by any artificial source. Instead, it is illuminated by the bioluminescent organisms that float in the water, and by the strange, ambient glow that seems to emanate from the structures themselves. Yet, the water moves, the structures hum, and the light pulses, as if the place is alive in a way that is not visible.
