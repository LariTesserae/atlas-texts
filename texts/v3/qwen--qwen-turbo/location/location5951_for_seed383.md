It is not the water of life, but of memory. Sometimes it falls as rain from the ceiling, misting the caverns with a cold, silver mist that smells of salt and iron.

Some are living, others are echoes of life, remnants of an earlier time when the caverns were not just tunnels but something else entirely—perhaps a sanctuary, a temple, a nest. The air is filled with a faint, shimmering glow, not from the sun, but from the slow, phosphorescent pulse of these structures. It is the kind of cold that seeps into your bones not from the air, but from the very stone itself—stone that has stood for eons, that has held the weight of the world above it. The air here is not thin, but it is alive with a kind of stillness, a silence that is not empty but filled with the weight of time. You can feel it in your lungs, in your breath, as though you are inhaling the past.

This is a place below surface, yet it is not a small one. It is vast, an underground metropolis that extends for miles, its tunnels forming a labyrinth of corridors, chambers, and great halls. It is open in scale, a city that stretches beyond the horizon of the eye, where the walls disappear into the dark and the ceiling is only a distant memory. The density here is sparse—there are no crowds, no throngs of people or creatures. There are only echoes, only the occasional flicker of something moving in the distance, something that might be a being, or a shadow, or the reflection of the light.

Tools were used here, and they were used with care, with purpose. There are things that look like engines, or machines, but they are not powered by fire or electricity. They hum with a quiet energy, a resonance that vibrates through the stone. They are not broken, but they are not working either.

It is the light of the mineral filaments, of the glow that seeps from the walls, of the reflection of the occasional flame or the flicker of a distant fire. It is a light that seems to pulse, like a heartbeat, or like the slow beat of something alive. It is not harsh, but it is constant, unyielding, and it casts long shadows that move when they should not.

There are creatures here—small, delicate things that scuttle along the walls, that glow faintly in the dark, that leave behind trails of phosphorescent dust. They are not insects, not in the way we know them. They are more like living echoes, or like the remnants of a forgotten world. They move in patterns, in rhythms, in ways that suggest intelligence, but not in the way we understand. They are not dangerous, but they are not friendly either. They are part of the place, as much as the stone and the light.
