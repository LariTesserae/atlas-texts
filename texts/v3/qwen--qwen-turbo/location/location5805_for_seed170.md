The land is a vast expanse of obsidian stone, stretched across an endless plateau that rises like a wound in the sky. The air is thin and sharp, filled with a dry, acrid scent that clings to the lungs. There is no water—no rivers, no lakes, no moisture of any kind. The horizon is a flat, unbroken line, and the sky above is a dull red, not from the sun, but from the constant haze of sulfuric particles that drift in the high atmosphere. The temperature is oppressive, not from heat, but from the sheer weight of the air, which clings to the skin like a second layer of sweat, burning and unrelenting. The sun—though it exists, distant and pale—casts no real warmth, only a kind of sterile, unfeeling light that seems to pass through the body rather than touch it.

The terrain is a network of crags and fissures, all of it hardened and weathered into a texture that feels like the bark of a dead tree. The ground is not soft, not even sandy; it is brittle and cracked, each plate of stone splitting and settling into the next, as though the land is slowly breaking itself apart. The elevation is high, but not in the way of mountains. It is a plateau that stretches endlessly, a flat expanse that defies the idea of a horizon. You can see for miles, but nothing moves. Nothing lives. The only things that exist are the stones, the sky, the wind.

And yet, the fauna is overwhelming. Not in the way of animals or creatures, but in the way of presence. The land is crawling with life, but not in the form of anything recognizable. Vast, shapeless things move beneath the surface, their movements slow and deliberate, as if they are not bound by time. They leave no tracks, but the air trembles when they pass. There are no sounds, yet the landscape is loud. Not with the sound of wind or water, but with the low, resonant hum of something ancient, something that vibrates through the ground and into the bones. It is not a sound, but a feeling, a pressure that builds in the skull, like a voice speaking in a language that the mind cannot comprehend.

The weirdness is not subtle. The laws of reality are not broken, but they are bent. Time moves in different ways in different places. Some areas seem to stretch into eternity, while others collapse into a single moment. The sky is not the same everywhere. Sometimes it is red, sometimes black, sometimes a deep blue that flickers with stars that don’t belong to this place. The light is normal, yet it casts no shadows. The sun is there, but it does not cast light—it casts a kind of presence, a weight that presses on the eyes and the mind.

The scale is open, but not in the way of open plains. It is open in the way of a place that exists outside of time, a space that is not meant to be traversed, but merely observed. There are no structures, no signs of human or artificial presence. The land is untouched, but not in the way of wilderness. It is untouched in the way of something that was never meant to be touched. The density is low, but not because there is nothing. It is low because the things that exist are vast and spread out, their presence felt more than seen. There is space between them, but that space is not empty—it is filled with the weight of something that is not there.

The technology is industrial, but it is not visible. There are no machines, no factories, no smoke. The technology is in the land itself. The stones hum with a kind of energy, a pulse that is not mechanical, but alive. It is not built, but it is functioning. The processes of the world are slow, but they are not still. The land is changing, but not in a way that can be perceived quickly. It is in a state of flux, but the changes are so gradual that they are imperceptible to the human eye. The dynamic is slow, but the weight of the world is heavy.

And the weirdness—oh, the weirdness. It is not a disturbance, not a fracture. It is the core of the place. It is the way the world is. It is the way the air feels, the way the stones move, the way the sky shifts and the light bends. It is not a place that can be understood, only endured. It is not a place that can be left, only passed through. It is a landscape that exists beyond the limits of normal perception, a place where the rules of existence are not broken, but redefined. It is not a place of beauty, nor of horror. It is a place of presence. A place that is.
