The elevation is low, almost flat, but there is a sense of depth in the landscape. It is not that there is no height, but that the land has chosen to stay close to the earth, as if it has no reason to rise. It is the kind of place that feels settled, not in the sense of being finished, but in the sense of being *done*. The erosion here is ancient, almost mythical. The land has been worn down, shaped, and reshaped over countless cycles of water, wind, and time. It is not eroded in a way that suggests destruction, but in a way that suggests *resolution*. The scale is small, but the feeling is vast. It is a cathedral of plants, a grove that feels like a room, a space where the boundaries are soft and indistinct. But even though it is small, it is *full*—full of life, full of movement, full of sound. The density is sparse, but the life is thick. There is space between things, but the things themselves are so dense that they seem to fill every possible gap. It is not empty, but it is not crowded. It is balanced.

The sound is loud, but not in a way that is disorienting. The fauna is abundant, but not in the way you would expect. It is a place that is always becoming something new, but in a way that feels inevitable, as if it has always been like this and always will be.
