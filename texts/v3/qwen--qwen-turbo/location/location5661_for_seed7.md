The walls are smooth in some areas, as if polished by some great, unseen force, and in others, they are sharp and jagged, with edges that catch the faintest glimmers of light. There is no erosion, no sign of weathering—this is a place that has not had time to change. It is as if time itself is reluctant to touch it, or as if the place is suspended in a state of arrested development.

The scale is small, but the chamber is vast in its own way. It is not a cathedral, nor a grove, but something between. The ceiling is low, pressing down, and the floor is uneven, making movement difficult. The space is not entirely enclosed—there are fissures and openings that let in faint shafts of light, but they are far above, and the light does not reach the center of the chamber.
