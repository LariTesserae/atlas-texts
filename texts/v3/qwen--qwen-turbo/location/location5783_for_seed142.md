The land is a vast expanse of obsidian stone, polished by time and wind into a surface that reflects nothing. It is high, perched on the edge of a world that has long since forgotten the concept of gravity. The air is dense with the weight of silence, and the only sound is the occasional creak of metal as the structures embedded in the ground shift with the slow, ancient pulse of the earth.

There is no water, no vegetation, no sign of life. The only material present is a dark, glass-like mineral that hums faintly when touched. It is warm, though not in a comforting way; the heat is more like the memory of fire, a lingering warmth in an otherwise lifeless place. The sun—visible only as a distant, dim glow—casts no light, and the darkness is not the absence of light, but a presence of its own, thick and insistent.

Erosion has sculpted the land into an intricate, sprawling pattern of plateaus and valleys. Some areas are smooth and flat, as if the earth had been pressed into shape by an invisible force. Others are jagged and sharp, as though the land had been broken and left to heal itself in the wrong way. The scale is immense—this is not a place for small things. It stretches beyond the horizon in all directions, a place where the mind cannot hold its full size, and where the concept of "distance" is a suggestion rather than a truth.

The density is sparse, but not empty. There are structures, scattered and ancient, rising from the ground like the bones of a long-dead giant. They are urban, in the sense that they were once made by hands, but they are not alive. They are industrial, built with purpose and function, now rusted and silent. Some are towering spires of metal, their surfaces etched with symbols that no one can read. Others are low, sprawling complexes, their interiors hollowed out by time and neglect. The tech is industrial, but it has not been maintained. It is a relic, a reminder of a civilization that once thrived here and then vanished.

The light is almost entirely absent. What little there is comes from the faint glow of the structures themselves, as if they are breathing in the dark. The sound is mostly quiet, but there is a low, constant drone that hums beneath the surface of the world, as though the land is alive in some way that defies explanation. It is a sound that does not come from any single source, but from everywhere at once, a sound that is felt more than heard.

The fauna is rare. There is nothing that moves, nothing that breathes, nothing that sings. But if you listen closely, if you let your mind settle into the stillness, you might feel the presence of something watching. Not an animal, not a creature, but a thing that exists in the space between what is and what was. It is strange, this place, and it does not change. It is frozen in time, caught in a moment that was never meant to be. It is a place that exists outside of time, outside of life, and yet it is real. It is a place that holds its own truth, and it will not let go of it.
