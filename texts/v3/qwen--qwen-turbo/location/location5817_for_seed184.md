The place is a submerged cathedral, a cathedral of water, built by hands that are no longer here. It is not the ocean, nor the deep sea, but something in between—an artificial abyss, a city of glass and stone and metal that lies beneath the waves, long abandoned, slowly drowning.

It is below surface, yes, but not in the simple way of a lake or a trench. It is a place that was once built for people, but now exists in the quiet, slow rhythm of the deep. The water is not liquid in the usual sense. It is thick, dense, almost viscous, as if the air itself has been replaced with something heavier, more sluggish. It moves in slow, deliberate currents, carrying with it the weight of time. The sun never reaches here, and so the light is dim—not dark, but low, like the glow of a dying ember in a forgotten hearth.

Erosion has taken hold, but not in the messy way of nature. The erosion here is deliberate, a slow and patient reckoning. The city was never meant to last, but it has. The stone has softened into something almost organic, the metal has corroded into strange, crystalline formations. The walls, once white and bright, are now a mottled gray, streaked with green and blue from mineral deposits and the slow spread of algae. The water has become a sort of living thing, creeping through the cracks, swallowing the edges of what was once architecture.

The scale is small, but that is deceptive. It is cathedral-scale, yes—a vast, echoing space that once held thousands, now reduced to a few chambers, a few streets, a few corridors. But it feels like a world. The density is packed. The space is full, not with life, but with structure. Every inch has been filled with something—pipes, scaffolding, machinery, remnants of the past. It is as if the city was sealed in a moment, and now it is holding its breath, trapped in a slow, inevitable collapse.

The built environment is urban, but it is not people. The buildings are there, but they are not inhabited. The infrastructure is still functional, in a way, but it is silent. The factories and engines that once roared are now quiet, their great mechanisms frozen in place. The air—if it can be called that—has been replaced by water, and the electricity that once powered this place has long since gone out. The tech is industrial, but now it is a relic, a ghost of progress.

There is little fauna. The place is not empty, but it is not teeming. There are fish, but they are few, and they move with a kind of reverence, as if the place is sacred. There are creatures, too, that have adapted—slimy things that cling to the metal, that slip through the cracks. They are not rare, but they are not common, either. They are strange, almost alien, as if the water has changed them, reshaped them, made them something else.

The sounds are quiet. There is no wind, no birds, no human voice. Only the creak of metal, the occasional drip of water, the slow, deep groan of the building settling. It is a place of silence, but not of emptiness. There is a presence here, a weight, a memory. The sound is not absent, but it is subdued, like a whisper just out of reach.

The weirdness is there, but it is not loud. It is a quiet strangeness. The water is not just water—it is something else, something that has taken on the shape of the city, the shape of the past. It is as if the place has become a kind of living thing, a deep, slow pulse that beats in the dark. The air is not breathable, but the water is not entirely alien, either. It is a place that has been forgotten, but not abandoned. It is waiting.

And the temperature is warm. Not hot, but warm—comfortable, in a way. The water is not cold, and that adds to the strangeness. It is as if the place has its own climate, its own rhythm, its own logic. The warmth is a quiet comfort, a gentle reminder that this place is not dead, not entirely. It is just... slow. Waiting. Watching. Moving, but not rushing.

This is a place that was built to last, and in the end, it has. It is a city of the deep, a cathedral of water, a place where time moves in slow, deliberate steps. It is not a place for the living, but it is not empty. It is full of memory, of structure, of the quiet, lingering hum of something that was once alive.
