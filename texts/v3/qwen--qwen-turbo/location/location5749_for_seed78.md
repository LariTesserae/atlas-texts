It's the cold of the high places, where the sun is weak and the wind cuts through layers of clothing. They are new, as if the land itself has just risen from some deep, primordial slumber. The edges of the terrain are not rounded or softened, but instead cut and precise, like the ridges of a blade. The scale is vast. This is not a place that can be contained in a single moment of observation. The horizon is distant, but not in the way of a flat, open plain. It is distant because the terrain is so immense, so full of towering structures and endless green. The sky seems to press down, but the land stretches up, so that the whole place feels like a single, vast organism, breathing in slow, deep pulses.

It is more like the space between the spires, between the great, living structures, between the roots and the air. There is room to move, but the land is so full that even the vastness feels crowded. There are a few built structures, scattered and faint. Some are broken, others are intact, but all are small and insignificant compared to the rest of the landscape. They are not ruins, but remnants—left behind by something that no longer exists, or perhaps by something that never was.

Technology is primitive. The structures are not made with tools recognizable to human hands, but they are simple in design, as if crafted by a species that has not mastered more complex methods. There is no fire, no smoke, only the quiet hum of the place itself. It is a place that seems to have no need for human industry, or perhaps it has simply never been touched by it.

Light is scarce. The sun is low, and the sky is often obscured by a thin veil of mist. The light that does reach the ground is pale, blue-tinged, and cold.
