The place is a submerged chamber, a vast and sealed hollow deep within the ocean floor. It is not a natural cave, but a constructed space—its walls are smooth, seamless, and cold. The material is a dark, glassy composite that refracts light in strange ways, catching the glow of the surface above and bending it into shifting patterns across the floor and ceiling. The water here is not the same as the sea; it is heavier, denser, and carries a faint electrical charge that makes the air (or what passes for it) hum with static. It is as if the chamber is alive with a low, steady pulse, like a heart beating in the dark.

The terrain is flat. There are no hills, no ridges—just an expanse of smooth, dark stone, stretching out in all directions, interrupted only by occasional pillars and arches, all of which are built, not grown. The entire space is a city, or the shell of one, built by hands long gone. The structures are tall and narrow, like skeletal remains of a civilization that once tried to merge with the sea. Their surfaces are etched with symbols and patterns, some of which seem to shimmer when the light hits them just right. The water moves sluggishly through the streets, but there is a sort of current, a slow, continuous movement that suggests the place is not entirely still. It is a place of quiet, but not of absence.

The temperature is warm, but not in a pleasant way. It is the kind of warmth that clings to the skin, that makes the water feel like a living thing, breathing against your body. The air is thick with it, and though there is no actual air, the pressure is enough to make your bones ache. The density is low—there is a lot of space between the structures, and between the water and the walls. The silence is broken by the occasional creak of the architecture, the sound of water shifting, and the faint, rhythmic hum that seems to come from the walls themselves.

There is no animal life, but there is a kind of presence. The place is not empty, but it is not full either. It is a place of echoes, of memory. The sound is loud in a way that makes your head throb, not because of any living thing, but because of the way the water and the stone amplify every movement, every ripple, every breath. It is a place that listens, that watches, that remembers.

The scale is hidden, a pocket within a pocket. It is a place that exists only in the depths, a fragment of something larger, a room that is not a room, a space that is both inside and outside. It is built in totality—no natural elements, no wildness, no dirt or stone. Everything is constructed, everything has a purpose, even if that purpose is not known. The technology is primitive, but not in the way you might expect. There are no machines, no tools, no signs of industry. The construction is seamless, as if it grew from the ocean floor itself, as if the builders were not human, but something else—something that understood water as a material, as a medium, as a living thing.

The light is bright, not because of the sun, but because of the way the water reflects and carries it. It is a light that is not harsh, but it is constant. It is the kind of light that makes everything seem too clear, too exact, as if the place is watching you just as much as you are watching it. The weirdness is low, almost nonexistent. There is no magic, no mystery. Just a place that is built, that is alive in its own way, that exists in a state of quiet motion. It is not a place of danger, but it is not safe either. It is a place of silence and sound, of warmth and cold, of presence and absence, of memory and construction.

It is a place that is not meant for humans, but perhaps it was once. Or perhaps it was always meant for something else.
