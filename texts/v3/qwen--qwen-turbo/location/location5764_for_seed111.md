The place is a labyrinth of breath and bone, carved into the spine of a mountain that has only just begun to rise. It is not yet a mountain in the human sense — no grandeur, no enduring majesty — but a raw, jagged wound in the earth, still fresh and bleeding in the way that only newly-formed rock can. The air is thick with the scent of wet stone and something else, something metallic and sweet, like rusted iron and the inside of a bird’s beak. The temperature is cool, not cold, a chill that clings to your skin like a second layer, not harsh but persistent, like the memory of a place that was once much warmer, and then forgot.

Water is here, but not in the way you would expect. It comes in whispers. A thin, silvery sheen coats the lower walls of the caverns, trickling down in slow, deliberate rivulets that vanish into cracks and crevices. Pockets of water exist, hidden beneath the roots of trees that do not quite belong here — spindly, black-barked things with veins of gold running through the bark, their leaves like parchment, flaking away in the dry air. The vegetation is sparse, but not absent. It grows in clusters, in tight, knotted bunches, as though trying to hold on to life in a place that resists it. Some of it moves — not by wind, but by something else. There is a pulse in the plants, a faint hum that matches the rhythm of your own heartbeat.

The scale is small, but the scale is deceptive. What begins as a cave, a narrow passage, becomes a cathedral of stone, its ceiling lost in shadow. The walls are close, but you are never truly alone. The density here is high, not in the way of people, but in the way of creatures. The fauna is overwhelming — not in number, but in presence. Beings that seem to be made of shadows and light, their shapes shifting like smoke in the wind. Some have too many legs, some too few. Some have eyes that glow from within, and others have no eyes at all. They do not move quickly, but they are always *there*, watching, listening, breathing in sync with the air.

The sound is loud, but not in the way of thunder or wind. It is a deep, resonant hum that vibrates in your ribs, coming from the stone itself. It is not a noise, but a presence, a living thing that sings in the dark. Sometimes it is a rhythm, a pattern that repeats, as if the mountain is thinking. Other times, it is a chaos, a cacophony of echoes and whispers, as if the place is trying to speak but cannot find the right words.

There is no built structure here. No roads, no walls, no signs of human hand. The place is untouched, but not empty. It is *alive* in a way that defies logic. The tech level is primitive — if there is any, it is not visible. The light is dim, filtered through a thin veil of mist that never really lifts. The weirdness is thick, like a fog that seeps into your thoughts. It is not dangerous, but it is *off*. The rules of the world are not fixed. Time does not move in straight lines here. The air is heavy with the weight of things that have not yet happened, and things that have already faded.

Erosion is low, but not absent. The place is young, and the edges are sharp, the cracks fresh, the formations still forming. It is a place of birth, not decay. The dynamic is slow — everything here moves at the pace of stone, of roots, of breath. You could stand here for hours, and not notice a single change. And yet, there is a sense that the place is watching you, that it is learning you, as you learn it.

It is a place that does not want to be understood. It is a place that wants to be *felt*.
