The place is a submerged urban sprawl, buried deep beneath the surface of a planet that no longer remembers what it was like to be above water. The water level here is not just high—it is *total*. The air is gone, replaced by a thick, endless ocean that presses in from all directions, not as a force of nature, but as an entity, a living, breathing presence that has long since forgotten the surface world. The city, if it can still be called that, is a cathedral of rust and glass, a broken and abandoned metropolis that has been swallowed whole by the sea.

It is not the ocean of Earth, but something older, more alien. It is a liquid that is not quite water, though it behaves like it. It is heavier, denser, and carries a faint, metallic tang that lingers in the throat. The pressure is intense, and yet, the city still stands—built from materials that seem to have been designed not for the surface, but for this abyssal existence. The buildings are not constructed in the traditional sense. They are fused, grown, and fused again, as though the city was not built, but *formed* in the deep.

There is no vegetation—only the slow, relentless creep of bioluminescent bacteria that cover the walls, the ceilings, the floors. They glow in soft blues and greens, pulsing like the heartbeat of the city itself. The light is dim, but it is not absent. It flickers and shifts, as if the city is alive, aware, and watching.

The temperature is warm—warm in a way that is unsettling. It is the warmth of a forgotten engine, the warmth of a place that has been running for centuries without rest. The heat comes from pipes and conduits that run through the city, their surfaces glowing faintly, their contents unseen but felt. The air, if there were any, would be stifling. But there is no air. There is only the ocean, and the constant, low hum of machinery that has not been maintained in decades.

Erosion is present, but it is not random. The city has settled into a kind of equilibrium, a balance between the weight of the water and the strength of its structures. It is a place that has been here for a long time, that has weathered the collapse of its own purpose. The streets are not paved, but carved into the walls, winding through the ruins like veins in a corpse. They are wide and open, but the city is empty—*almost* empty. There are things moving through the shadows, but they are not animals. They are not even life in the way humans understand it. They are echoes, remnants, something between machine and memory.

The scale is hidden. This is not a city that is seen in its entirety. It is a pocket-scale world, contained within the vastness of the ocean, a micro-environment that exists in the mind of the sea. The buildings are tall, but the space between them is vast. The city is not dense. It is sparse, hollow, with room for the water to move through it, to breathe through it, to shape it.

The built environment is still present, but it is not thriving. It is a city that has been abandoned, but not destroyed. The infrastructure remains, though much of it is broken. There are factories, though they are silent. There are engines, though they are not running. The industrial scale is there, but it is a ghost of what once was. The sound is deafening—not the sound of people, but the sound of the city itself, the groan of metal, the hiss of steam, the click of unseen mechanisms that still try to function, even in the dark.

The fauna is rare, but not absent. There are things that move through the water, not in schools, but in clusters, in patterns that suggest a kind of intelligence. They are not fish, not in any human sense. They are shapes, forms, shadows that flicker and then vanish. They are not predators, but they are not prey either. They are something in between, something that has adapted to the city, to the water, to the noise and the heat.

The weirdness is subtle but ever-present. The city does not follow the rules of the surface world. Time moves differently here. The water is not just a medium, but a presence. The structures are not just buildings, but something more—maybe even something sentient. The place has a rhythm, a pulse, a life that is not entirely human. It is not a place that can be understood, only experienced.

And the sound—oh, the sound. It is deafening, not in a way that hurts, but in a way that *fills*. It is the sound of the city breathing, of the ocean moving around it, of things that are not meant to be heard, but are. It is a sound that lingers in the bones, in the mind, in the memory of the place itself. It is not chaos, but it is not order either. It is something in between, something that defies the laws of the world above.

This place is a city lost to the deep, a world that was built for a purpose now forgotten, and now exists in a state of uneasy suspension. It is a place of heat and water, of sound and silence, of life and absence. It is not a place you would go to, but it is a place that would *find* you, if you were to drift too close.
