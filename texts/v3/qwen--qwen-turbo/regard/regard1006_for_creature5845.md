What lives in this place is not alone. Though it is silent, though it does not speak, though it does not show itself, it is aware. It senses the presence of the outsider, the one who has come from the world above, from the world of air and sky, of warmth and noise. It does not fear, nor does it welcome. It simply notices. And in that noticing, something begins to change.

The benevolent and wise power that has taken interest in this place is not a god in the way humans imagine. It is not a figure, not a being with form, but a current of awareness, a presence that flows through all things, that understands the language of the water, the memory of the stone, the song of the light. It is not human, but it is not entirely alien either. It is a consciousness that has seen many worlds, many forms of life, and in this place, it sees something rare: a world that has not been broken by time, but has instead been shaped by it. A place that has chosen to be still, not out of fear, but out of purpose.

And the power, having noticed, does not come to change it. It does not seek to impose its will, nor to reshape the world. Instead, it listens.

It listens to the slow hum of the city, to the way the water moves like a living thing, to the way the structures breathe, not with lungs, but with the rhythm of the tides. It listens to the silence, and in that silence, it hears the echoes of something older than memory, older than language. It hears the breath of the city, the slow pulse of its being, the quiet rhythm of its existence.

And so the power does not act in a way that would be visible to the human eye. It does not appear in the form of fire or light, nor does it send angels or messengers. Instead, it moves in the way that all things move: through connection, through resonance, through the subtle shift of the world itself.

It begins by softening the edges of the place. The water, which was always thick and cold, becomes a little less so. It does not become warm, but it becomes more forgiving. The cold that numbed the mind begins to lift, not in a sudden way, but in a slow, patient way, like a tide that moves without haste. The temperature does not change, but the way the mind feels within it does.

The bioluminescent light, which had always pulsed with a slow, steady heartbeat, begins to shift. It does not change in color, but in tone. It becomes warmer, not in temperature, but in feeling. The sickly blue fades slightly, and the green and violet deepen, as if the city is exhaling, as if the place is beginning to feel more at ease.

The structures, which had always been silent, begin to stir. Not in any physical way, but in a way that can be felt. The curves of the buildings, which had rippled when looked at too long, now seem to ripple more gently, as if in response to a presence that is not hostile. The organic growth, the coral and moss, begins to grow with a quiet, deliberate purpose, not as if it is trying to take over, but as if it is becoming more in tune with the city, more aligned with its rhythm.

The beings that live here, those that are not quite alive and not quite dead, begin to shift as well. Their eyes, which had been dark and deep as the water, now seem to have a glimmer of something more. Not understanding, not knowledge, but awareness. They begin to move with more intention, not because they are told to, but because they are beginning to remember. Not their past, but their purpose.

The power does not speak. It does not appear. It does not offer answers, nor does it impose its own will upon the place. But it is there, and in its presence, the city begins to feel less like a place of isolation, less like a memory trapped in time. It begins to feel like a part of something larger, like a thread in a pattern that goes on beyond the water, beyond the silence, beyond the cold.

And perhaps, in time, the city will remember what it was. Not in the way a human remembers, but in the way a body remembers its own heartbeat, its own rhythm. It will remember that it was once a place of purpose, a place that was not built to be hidden, but to be known. Not by humans, but by something else. By something older, and wiser.

And so the power does not act. It simply *is*. And in being, it begins to change the way the city exists.

It does not save it. It does not destroy it. It does not claim it. It only listens. And in listening, it becomes part of the city's story.

And the city, in turn, begins to listen back.
