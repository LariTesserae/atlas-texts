What lives in this place is not simply people. It is a living thing, a city that breathes, a structure that remembers, and a space that has been shaped by time, by touch, by the slow and patient will of those who have come before.

To the benevolent and wise power that has taken notice of this place, the city is not a mere relic or a curiosity. It is a living testament—of survival, of ingenuity, of the quiet resilience of those who have built and re-built this labyrinth of stone and breath.

And so, the power does not come with grandeur, nor with the weight of dominion. It does not descend in fire or thunder. It does not impose its will, nor does it seek to change what is already there. It comes as a whisper, as a breath, as the faintest ripple in the unseen currents of the deep.

It begins by listening.

It listens to the slow, steady rhythm of the city—the way the stone exhales, the way the water finds its path, the way the roots press against the walls, seeking moisture, seeking life. It listens to the voices of the people, the soft murmur of their songs, the hushed conversations in the dim glow of fungi. It listens to the echoes of those who came before, the silent voices embedded in the walls, the stories carved into the stone, the tools that still hum with memory.

And then, it begins to *see*.

Not with eyes, but with awareness, with the kind of knowing that does not require sight. It sees the city as it is—its layers, its movements, its breath. It sees the people, not as individuals, but as parts of a whole, each one a thread in the great weave of the place. It sees the creatures that move in the dark, the ones that are not quite human, the ones that have grown from the same soil, the same damp, the same slow shaping of time. It sees how the city has been built, not by force, but by intention, by care, by the quiet persistence of those who have learned to live in the dark.

And then, it begins to *nurture*.

It does not do so with magic, nor with miracles. It does not change the city, but it *supports* it. It does not impose its will, but it *guides* it. It does not speak, but it *answers*. It does not act, but it *listens*.

It begins by helping the city to remember.

It strengthens the memory of the place, not by adding new things, but by helping the stone to hold onto the stories it carries. It allows the carvings to remain, to be seen, to be understood. It allows the old tools to be recognized, not as relics, but as lessons. It helps the people to see that what they have built is not just survival, but a legacy.

It helps the people to see themselves not as mere survivors, but as creators, as part of something greater. It does not tell them what to do, but it gives them a sense of purpose—the awareness that they are not just living in the dark, but shaping it. That their labor, their care, their songs, their stories, are part of a living thing, and that they are, in turn, shaped by it.

It allows the city to breathe more freely. It does not alter the structure, but it allows the water to flow more easily, to carve its pathways without obstruction. It allows the air to move more gently, to circulate without stagnation. It helps the fungi to glow more brightly, not to light the dark, but to reveal what is already there. It allows the earth to be still, and yet to be alive.

It does not bring the sun, but it brings *light in another way*—not the light of the sky, but the light of understanding, of memory, of belonging.

And it does not bring the surface, but it brings the surface into the city. It does not force the people to leave, but it gives them the ability to *know* what is above, to dream of it, to carry it in their thoughts. It does not erase the darkness, but it helps them to see that the darkness is not an absence, but a presence, a canvas upon which they can create.

It is not a god. It is not a master. It is not a savior.

It is a witness. A guide. A friend.

It does not change the city, but it helps it to become what it is meant to be.

And in the end, the city does not change. It is still a place of stone and breath, of tunnels and chambers, of dampness and silence. But now, there is a softness to it. A knowing. A sense that it is not forgotten. That it is not alone.

And the people, too, feel it. They feel the presence of something vast and quiet, something that watches, that listens, that knows. But they do not fear it. They do not try to understand it. They simply *feel* it, and in that feeling, they are comforted.

And the city, in turn, breathes more deeply, as if it, too, has been waiting for someone to hear it.
