It is not bound by the laws of space or time, and so it exists here, in this place that resists definition, and yet is defined by its very presence.

It begins, not with a gesture, but with a *stillness*. It does not move through the water as we do, but it is felt in the way the currents shift, in the way the light flickers in a rhythm that matches the pulse of the place. It does not speak, but it *listens*—not to words, but to the silence between them, to the hum of the structures, to the slow, deliberate breathing of the place itself.

It does not seek to know the past, for it already knows it. It does not seek to change the present, for it is not a force of will, but of *presence*. It is not a being, but a *resonance*, a vibration that lives in the spaces between, in the spaces that were never meant to be filled.

But in this place, there is something that needs to be known. Not by the power, but by the place itself. Not by the memory of the lost civilization, but by the thing that remains of it. The structures do not decay, the water does not erode, the light does not fade. They endure, not because they are alive, but because they are *remembered*.

It offers the space for the past to be what it was, and for the present to be what it is, without interference.

It does not bring knowledge, but it brings *clarity*. It does not bring change, but it brings *harmony*. It does not bring answers, but it brings *understanding*.

And in that understanding, the place does not change. It is not saved, not lost, not found. It is simply *seen*, and in being seen, it is *known*.

So the power does not act, but it is *there*. And in its presence, the place is no longer just a memory. It is a *presence*.
