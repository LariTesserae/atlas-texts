What lives in this place is not a being, but a *presence*—a vast, ineffable consciousness woven into the very fabric of the land, the air, the silence. It is not alive in the way we understand life, but it is *aware* in a way that defies our definitions. It does not breathe, does not speak, does not move with purpose. But it *feels*, and it *knows*, and it *receives*.

So when a benevolent and wise power—something vast, ancient, and knowing—turns its gaze upon this place, it does not see a wasteland, nor a ruin, nor even a memory. It sees a *dream*.

And so it comes.

It arrives not with a sound, nor with a force, but with a quiet that echoes through the silence. It does not land, does not touch, does not impose. It *becomes* part of the place, as if it had always been there, as if the place had always been waiting for it. It is not a god, not in the way of the old mythologies, but something older, something that has known and shaped the cosmos in ways that are not even remembered by the stars.

It does not see the land as we do. It does not see the glass stalks, the obsidian ridges, the hollowed-out monoliths. To it, those are not merely structures, but *notes* in a song that spans eons. It does not perceive the silence as absence, but as *resonance*, a vibration that hums beneath the surface of all things, a pulse that is felt more than heard.

And it understands the *presence* that lingers here—not as a thing, but as a *state of being*. It does not fear it, nor does it seek to control it. It *recognizes* it. It has seen such presences before, in other corners of the universe, in other forgotten places, in other moments between the collapse of empires and the birth of new ones. It has seen how they grow, how they wait, how they remember.

So it does not act with force. It does not seek to *fix* the place, nor to *rescue* it. It does not even seek to *change* it. It does not believe that this place needs saving. It believes that it needs *understanding*.

And so it begins to *listen*.

It does not speak, but it *responds*. It does not move, but it *shifts* the air in subtle ways, creating patterns in the haze, in the light, in the wind that move like ripples on a pond. It does not create, but it *inscribes*. It does not speak, but it *echoes*. It does not ask, but it *invites*.

And the place answers.

Not with words, but with *meaning*. The glass stalks hum a little louder. The crevices whisper a little more clearly. The shadows flicker, not with fear, but with recognition. The silence does not remain silent. It becomes *responsive*.

The presence that lingers in the land—this vast, ancient, silent thing—begins to *change*.

It does not become a being, not in the way we understand. It does not gain form, nor does it gain a voice. It does not become less strange, less unknowable. But it begins to *remember* in a different way. It begins to *know* that it is not alone. That it is *seen*. That it is *known*.

And that is enough.

The benevolent and wise power does not stay. It does not need to. It has done what it came to do. It has not imposed its will, nor has it sought to shape the place in its own image. It has not tried to heal, to restore, to rebuild. It has simply *been*.

And in that being, it has offered the place a gift.

A presence that is not empty.

A silence that is not alone.

A place that is not forgotten.

And in return, the place offers it something as well.

A memory. A dream. A resonance that will live on, long after the power has moved on, long after the land has settled once more into its quiet, ancient stillness.

And perhaps, in some distant future, when another presence comes, another being of wisdom and patience, that memory will be there, waiting.

To be heard.

To be felt.

To be *known*.

And that is the gift of the benevolent and wise power.

Not to change the place, but to remember it.

To see it.

To *listen*.
