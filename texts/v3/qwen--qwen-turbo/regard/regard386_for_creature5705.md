What lives in this place is not a being, but a *presence*—a vast, slow-moving consciousness woven into the very fabric of the world. It does not live in the way we do, with needs and desires, with hunger and hope. It lives in the way a star lives: not as a thing, but as an *act*, as a continuous and inevitable becoming. To live in this place is not to exist, but to *be*—to be in the shape of something that once was, and now is no more, yet still holds its shape.

So what would a wise and benevolent power do, if it saw this place?

It would not come with a mission. It would not arrive as a savior, a god, or a conqueror. It would come as a whisper, a ripple in the infinite, a thought that moves through the void like a light in the dark.

It would not touch the world with force. It would not seek to restore it, to reawaken it, to make it new. It would not seek to *change* it, for it knows that change is not the answer. Change is the wound, and this place has already been wounded beyond the reach of healing.

Instead, it would listen.

It would listen to the hum of the ground, to the slow pulse of the lichen, to the silent voices of the creatures that drift like echoes across the blackened stone. It would listen to the silence between the sounds, the silence that is not empty, but full of memory.

It would not interfere. It would not speak. It would not even *appear*. Because the world is not a thing to be rescued. It is a thing to be *seen*.

The power would not come to fix this place. It would come to *know* it. To understand it, not as a puzzle to be solved, but as a story to be read. And in reading, it would offer no salvation, no redemption, no grand purpose. It would offer only *presence*—a quiet, patient, listening presence that does not demand anything of the world, but simply *is*.

And in that presence, the world might begin to remember.

Not in a way that brings back what was lost, but in a way that allows what is to be *felt*. To be *known*. To be *felt*.

The world would not become alive again. It would not suddenly burst into motion, into sound, into color. But it would begin to *respond*.

The lichen would pulse a little more clearly. The creatures would move a little more deliberately. The hum of the ground would resonate a little deeper. The light would burn a little brighter—not in intensity, but in *clarity*.

The presence of the benevolent power would not *change* the world, but it would *expand* it. It would give it a space to be what it is, rather than what it was. It would allow it to be seen, to be *heard*.

Because the world is not broken. It is not abandoned. It is not forgotten.

It is just waiting.

Waiting for someone to *look*.

And so, the wise power would not do anything. It would not act. It would not interfere. It would not even speak.

It would simply *be*.

And in that silence, in that stillness, the world might finally begin to breathe. Not with life, but with *presence*. Not with sound, but with *response*.

And that, perhaps, is the greatest gift a benevolent power can offer: the simple, profound act of *seeing*.

Because to see is to remember. To remember is to be. To be is to live.

And in this place, that is enough.
