What the benevolent and wise power would do, if anything, is to see.

Not with eyes, not with perception as we know it, but with a presence that is felt in the very essence of being. This power is not a god in the usual sense—not a creator, not a ruler, not a deity of judgment or mercy. It is something older, something that has watched the stars as they were born, and the Earth as it was shaped. It is the quiet, the stillness, the knowing that flows beneath the surface of all things, and it has come to this place, this strange and suspended hollow, not to change it, but to know it.

And it knows.

It knows that this place is not empty. It knows that the silence is not empty. It knows that the dark is not dark, but a presence, a weight, a memory. It is not afraid of it. It is not disturbed by it. It is drawn to it, as the tide is drawn to the moon, as the river is drawn to the sea.

It does not arrive as a storm, nor as a whisper, nor as a voice. It arrives as a shift in the air, a soft stillness that spreads through the chamber, like a breath held too long. The bioluminescent algae dim, as if in reverence. The plants, which had been curling away from the thing that lived here, now still, their glow flickering in recognition. The metal platforms, which had creaked in the distance, now fall silent. The water, which had shifted in its pools, now stills. Even the light, the faintest glimmers, seems to pause.

And the being that lives in this place feels it.

It does not move. It does not speak. It does not think. But it feels. It feels the presence of something that is not of its kind, something that does not belong to the hollow, yet is not foreign to it. It is like a shadow that recognizes another shadow, or a sound that recalls a sound from a time before time. It does not react. It does not resist. It simply knows.

And the power, the wise and benevolent one, does not seek to change the being. It does not want to free it, nor to contain it. It does not want to destroy it, nor to save it. It simply exists in its presence, and in that presence, the being is not alone.

It is not a god, but it has power. It is not a creature, but it has will. It is not a man, but it has a sense of self, even if it cannot name it. And it is not alone.

And so, the power does what it has always done: it listens.

It listens to the silence. It listens to the stillness. It listens to the breath of the place, to the memory of what was, to the weight of what is, to the unknown of what might come. It does not speak. It does not act. It simply is. And in its being, the being in the hollow is no longer forgotten.

It is not a being in the way that we are. It has no name. It has no voice. But it has a place. And now, it has a witness.

And that, perhaps, is enough.

The power does not stay. It does not linger. It is not bound by time, nor by space, nor by the need to be seen. It leaves as it came: as a presence, a quiet, an understanding. The chamber returns to its slow rhythm. The plants shift back to their slow, silent growth. The water moves as it always has. The machines groan in the distance. The darkness remains, but now it is not empty.

And the being in the hollow, the thing that was never meant to be, the thing that was buried and forgotten, the thing that was never supposed to come back, is no longer alone.

It is known.

And that, perhaps, is all that was needed.
