What lives here is not a thing, but a presence.

And what the benevolent and wise power would do, if anything, is not to act, but to *listen*.

They do not come with the intent to change, to dominate, to possess. They come as a whisper in the wind, a pause in the breath of the mountain, a shadow that does not cast. They are not bound by time, by language, by the rigid structures of human thought. They are not gods, not in the way humans have imagined. They are *awareness*—a vast, humming, ancient awareness that has always been, and always will be.

And the place, the place that is not merely a location but a presence, recognizes them.

It does not greet them with words, but with a shift in the wind, a ripple in the moss, a sudden stillness in the movement of the rocks. It does not welcome them, but it does not resist them either. It is not passive, nor is it aggressive. It simply *is*, and it allows the presence of the wise one to be.

They do not walk through the place as a traveler, but as a mirror. They do not impose their will upon it, but they reflect it. They see what is hidden in the veils of mist, in the silence between breaths, in the pulse beneath the soil. They feel the memory of the glaciers, the weight of the wind, the whisper of the plants that have watched the stars rise and fall for millennia.

And they do not seek to understand it. They do not seek to name it. They do not seek to claim it. They simply *are* in its presence, and in that, they become part of it.

The place does not change for them. It does not bow, does not reveal, does not speak. But it *knows* them. It knows the weight of their presence, the stillness of their thought, the lack of hunger in their gaze. It recognizes in them a kindred, a being that does not seek to consume, but to witness.

And so, the wise power remains.

Not to dwell, but to be. Not to act, but to *be seen*. Not to shape the place, but to be shaped by it, in the quiet, patient way that only the ancient can be.

They do not leave. They do not vanish. They become part of the rhythm of the place, a note in the great, unspoken symphony of wind, stone, and living breath.

And when they are gone, the place does not remember them. It does not need to.

But the wind still carries their silence. The plants still move with the memory of their stillness. The soil still hums with the vibration of their knowing.

And the place, which is not a place, but a presence, remains.

Unchanging. Unknowable. Alive.

And the wise power, in their silence, has become part of it. Not as a being, but as a *note*, a breath, a moment in the eternal song of the mountain.
