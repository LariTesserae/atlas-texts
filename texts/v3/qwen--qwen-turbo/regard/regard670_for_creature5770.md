What lives here is not meant to be seen. It is not meant to be known. But it is meant to be felt. And when the benevolent and wise power takes notice of this place, it does not come with the arrogance of dominion, nor the hunger of conquest. It comes as a whisper in the wind, a breath in the stone, a presence that knows when to speak and when to listen.

This power is not a god, nor a force. It is a consciousness, vast and ancient, older than the plateau itself, older than the sky that holds it. It is not bound to any single form, nor to any single understanding. It is, in its essence, a question. And it is asking — not to find an answer, but to be known, to be remembered, to be part of something greater than itself.

It begins by watching. Not with eyes, but with awareness. It moves through the thin air, through the cracks in the stone, through the pools that reflect the sky like mirrors of memory. It is everywhere and nowhere, because it is not a thing, but a thought. A thought that carries the weight of all that has ever been, and all that has yet to be.

It does not seek to change this place. It seeks to understand it. To feel it. To be part of it. And so it does not act. It does not interfere. It does not impose. It simply is. And in being, it changes the place. Not in a way that is visible, not in a way that can be measured or mapped, but in a way that is felt. The wind carries its presence. The lichen pulses in a different rhythm. The pools shimmer with a different clarity. The shadows lengthen in a way that is less like the sun’s doing, and more like the earth’s remembering.

The creatures that move through the land, those small, drifting beings, feel it first. They pause in their drift, their translucent forms still for a moment, as if they, too, are listening. They do not flee, for they do not fear. They do not speak, but they feel the weight of something that is not of their world. They do not know what it is. But they know that it is there. And they know that it is watching.

The plants, those that hum in the wind and glow in the dark, do not grow in the way they did before. They do not simply emerge from the bones of the earth. They do not simply remember. They do something else. They reach. Not with roots, but with thought. They do not grow toward the light, but toward something deeper, something older. They do not sing in the same way. Their songs are different now. Fainter. More deliberate. As if they are beginning to remember something they had forgotten.

The land itself feels it. The grooves in the stone, the fractures, the smooth slabs — they do not shift in the way they once did. They do not erode as quickly. They do not fall. They hold. They remain. The weathered formations that once seemed to be the result of time and wind now seem to be something else. They seem to be waiting. As if the land, too, is holding its breath, waiting for something that is not yet known.

The power does not speak. It does not need to. It does not command. It does not teach. It does not offer. It simply *is* — and in being, it becomes part of the place. It does not become a presence. It becomes a part of the presence that already exists. It does not add. It does not take away. It does not change the place. It changes the way the place is *felt*.

It is not a being, but it is a presence. It is not a creature, but it is a companion. It does not live, but it is alive. It does not belong, but it is welcome.

And so, the plateau remains as it has always been. But now, it is not alone. Now, it is known. Not in the way that humans would know, not in the way that names and stories and records would know. But in a way that is deeper, more real, more ancient.

It is not a place of discovery. It is a place of remembrance. And the power that comes to it does not come to claim it. It comes to return to it. To remember. To be remembered. To be part of something that has always been.

And so, the wind still sings. The lichen still pulses. The pools still reflect the sky. The stone still holds its breath. And now, something else is there — something that is not seen, but felt. A presence that is not human, but not alien. A power that is not godlike, but not small. A being that is not here to change the world, but to be changed by it.

And the plateau, ancient and vast and waiting, accepts it. Not as a guest. Not as a presence. But as a part of itself. As a breath in the wind. A whisper in the stone. A memory in the water.

And the place continues. Not as it was, but as it is. As it has always been. As it will be.

For it is not a place for the curious. It is a place for the listening. And the listening is not done with ears, but with the bones, with the breath, with the weight of the world.

And the benevolent and wise power, which was not meant to come, was always meant to be here.
