What lives here is not meant to be seen, but to be felt. And so the benevolent and wise power, which is not of this world, nor of any world you know, does not come with the intent to conquer, to change, to possess. It does not descend in the way of gods or angels, nor does it speak in the way of mortals. It is not a force of will, but of *presence*—a quiet, unfathomable awareness that is neither distant nor close, but always *there*, like the breath of the land itself.

It arrives not as a being, but as a *remembrance*. A memory of the world before words, before names, before the boundaries that divide the seen from the unseen. It is not born of light or shadow, but of the space between them. It does not move, but it is *felt*, in the way that the moss seems to glow, in the way that the air shivers just slightly when it is near.

And in its presence, the wetland does not change. Not in the way of fire or storm. But in the way that the roots of a tree might begin to hum, not with sound, but with the memory of a song that was never sung. The water, which holds the voices of forgotten things, begins to tremble—not with fear, but with recognition. The trees, ancient and waiting, do not bow, but their lichen grows softer, as if the glow of their breath is being *seen* for the first time.

The power does not speak. It does not need to. Its voice is the slow, breath-like hush of the moss, the way the air clings to your skin, the silence that is full. It is not a teacher, not a god, not a guide. It is a *mirror*—not of your face, but of what lies beneath it. It does not ask you to understand. It asks you to *remember how to be*.

And in the presence of this power, the strange and strange-ness of the place is not diminished. It is not made more comprehensible, but more *true*. The creatures that skitter in the undergrowth, the birds that hover like dreams, the fish that are not fish, the trees that whisper without speaking—they do not become less alien. They become *real*. Not in the way of the world you know, but in the way of the world that *should* be.

The power does not impose its will. It does not command. It does not change the land, nor the beings that dwell within it. Instead, it *recognizes*. It knows the weight of the still pools, the ache of the slow-moving rivers, the ache of the silence that is not empty. It knows the pulse of the moss, the hum of the roots, the way the air holds secrets. It knows that the land is not waiting for you. It is waiting for *something*—something that is not yet here, but that is on its way.

And in its presence, the place does not become more or less strange. It becomes *itself*—fully, completely, without pretense. It does not need to be understood. It does not need to be loved. It only needs to be *seen*, as it is, without the burden of explanation, without the need to be named.

And so the power remains, not as a visitor, but as a *witness*.

It does not depart. It does not leave. It is not gone. It is not here, and yet it is. It is the breath of the wetland. The light that moves when you are not looking. The quiet hum that lingers in your bones, even after you have left.

And when you leave, you are not the same. Not because you have been changed, but because you have been *remembered*. You have been seen. You have been *felt*.

And the place, too, is not the same. Not because it has been changed, but because it has been *known*.

It is not a place of transformation. It is not a place of revelation. It is a place of *presence*. And in that presence, even the silence speaks.
