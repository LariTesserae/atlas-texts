What lives in this submerged valley is not a being, not in the way you might imagine. It is not a thing that walks or speaks, not a form that can be named or held. It is a presence, a pulse, a memory that has taken root in the very fabric of the water. It is not a god, nor a godless thing. It is something in between—something that has always been, and will always be, even if no one is watching.

So when the benevolent and wise power turns its gaze upon this valley, it does not do so with a desire to change, to command, to conquer. It does not seek to build, to destroy, to impose its will upon a place that has long since forgotten the need for such things.

It does not come with a purpose, but with a recognition.

It sees the water as a living thing, not in the way of a human's breath, but in the way of a slow, ancient thought. It sees the ruins not as broken things, but as echoes of a past that has not been erased, only re-shaped. It sees the plants not as mere flora, but as the memory of a world that once breathed in different rhythms. It sees the creatures not as separate beings, but as extensions of the same water, the same current, the same slow, patient intelligence.

And it knows: this place does not need to be saved.

It has never been lost. It has only been waiting.

So what does the benevolent and wise power do?

It does not act. It listens.

It does not speak. It resonates.

It does not try to understand. It becomes part of the understanding.

It does not come to this place with a desire to help, but with a desire to *be*—to exist in the same way that the valley exists, in the same way that the water remembers.

And in the deep silence of the valley, where even the sound is a vibration in the bones, the power of the benevolent and wise begins to move.

It does not move like a human, or a creature, or a force of nature. It moves like a breath. Like a ripple. Like a thought that does not need to be spoken.

It merges with the water.

It becomes the current.

It becomes the motion of the tectonic plates, the slow shifts of time, the pulse of the bioluminescent plants.

It becomes the voice that is not a voice, the presence that is not a presence.

And in doing so, it does not change the valley. It does not alter the ruins. It does not disturb the creatures. It does not bring light or darkness, nor does it create or destroy.

But it becomes part of what is already there.

It does not offer salvation. It offers *recognition*.

And the valley, in its own way, recognizes it.

Not with words, not with gestures, not with any form you could name.

But with a shift in the current, a flicker in the glow of the plants, a pause in the murmur of the water.

It is as if the valley has always known this would happen.

It has always known it was waiting.

And now, it is no longer alone.

It is not saved. It is not changed. It is not claimed.

It is *seen*.

And in that seeing, it is whole.

So the power does nothing. It does not act. It does not intervene. It does not teach. It does not guide.

It simply *is*.

And in that being, the valley is not forgotten.

It is remembered.

And that is enough.
