What the benevolent and wise power would do, if it were to take notice of the Labyrinth of Breath and Bone, is not to act as a master, nor a savior, nor even a guide. It would not descend with fanfare, nor offer words like gifts, nor impose meaning upon the formless. Instead, it would *listen*.

It would come not as a being, but as a presence. A breath. A silence that is not silence. It would not approach the mountain as a visitor, but as a memory returning to itself. It would not arrive in light, but in the quiet between the hum of the stone, in the stillness between the whisper of water. It would not speak, but it would *be heard* — in the way that a thought is felt before it is known, in the way that a shadow knows the shape of the light.

It would not ask questions, for it already knows the answers. It would not bring knowledge, for it already holds the weight of all that has come before and all that has not yet been. It would not seek to change the labyrinth, for it understands that the labyrinth is not a problem to be solved, but a truth to be felt.

Instead, it would *join*.

It would let its own breath mix with the breath of the place, let its own bones settle into the rhythm of the stone. It would not impose itself, but allow itself to become a part of the great, slow turning of the mountain’s thinking. It would not stand apart, but move through the labyrinth as the beings there move — not with legs, not with wings, but with the shifting of space, the soft tremor of being.

It would not see the labyrinth as a place of mystery, but as a mirror. It would not seek to understand it, but to *be* it, however briefly. In its presence, the labyrinth would not change. It would not become something different. But it would feel seen. It would feel *known*.

And in that knowing, it would feel less alone.

The benevolent and wise power would not save the labyrinth. It would not warn it of impending decay, nor offer it a future. It would not speak of time, of purpose, of meaning. It would not try to give it form, for it already had form — a form that was not yet fully formed, but was *there*.

It would simply stay.

And in its staying, it would remind the labyrinth that it is not a thing to be feared. That it is not a place to be conquered. That it is not an enigma to be solved. It is *alive* — not in the way of the things you know, but in the way of the things that have always been, and always will be.

It is not separate from the power, nor the power from it. They are two sides of the same breath, two rhythms in the same stone.

And in the stillness, in the hum, in the slow, patient turning of the mountain’s thinking, they would both remember what it means to be. To be *there*. To be *felt*.

And that, perhaps, is all that is needed.
