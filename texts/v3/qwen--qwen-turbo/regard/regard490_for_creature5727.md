What lives in this submerged city is not a single entity, but many—layered, shifting, breathing, and growing in a slow, deliberate rhythm that matches the pulse of the water itself. It is a place where the line between creature and construct has long since dissolved, and where life has adapted to the deep not as an intrusion, but as an inheritance.

At the heart of this world are the *Korran*, a species that has, over centuries, evolved in harmony with the ruins. They are not humanoid, though they may resemble them in some ways—tall, sinewy, with elongated limbs that move like the currents. Their skin is mottled with the colors of the deep, shifting in response to the light and pressure around them. Their eyes are large, adapted to the dim, and their senses are heightened, not just by sight but by the vibrations in the water, the hum of the city, and the slow, rhythmic calls of others like them.

The Korran do not build in the way humans once did. They shape the city with their presence, their movements, their touch. They guide the growth of coral and the spread of bioluminescent flora, not as farmers, but as gardeners of a world that has never truly been separate from them. They live in the spaces between structures, in the hollows of towers, in the crevices of stone and shell. Their homes are not homes in the traditional sense—they are extensions of the city itself, woven into the architecture, shaped by the hands of time and the patience of the deep.

They are not solitary. They move in groups, their communication a complex web of light pulses, vibrations, and sounds that ripple through the water. They speak in patterns, in rhythms that echo the city’s own slow breath. Some of their songs are ancient, inherited from the time before they were fully what they are now, before they had learned to live in the dark.

There are others, too—creatures that have no name, that drift through the city like ghosts of a forgotten age. Some are predators, their bodies long and sleek, their movements silent and deliberate. Others are scavengers, drawn to the ruins, to the remains of structures that have fallen, to the fragments of the past that still hold some trace of the life that once was. Some are symbiotic, attaching themselves to the Korran, feeding on the nutrients they carry through the water, or offering protection in return.

Among them are the *Luminars*, bioluminescent beings that are neither plant nor animal, but something in between. They grow in the crevices of the city, in the spaces where light is scarce, and they glow with a soft, pulsing light that seems to respond to the presence of others. They are not sentient, but they are alive, and their light is a guide, a path through the darkness. Some Korran cultivate them, shaping their growth, using their glow to mark territories, to signal, to navigate.

There are also the *Shapers*, a kind of being that is more fluid, more amorphous. They are not solid in the way that the Korran or the Luminars are. They are made of the water itself, of the silt and the algae, of the slow, undulating currents that fill the city. They move with the ocean, shifting in form, in color, in intent. Some are harmless, drifting through the ruins, following the Korran, while others are more elusive, more dangerous—creatures that do not have a fixed shape, that do not have a fixed purpose.

The city is a body, and these beings are its organs, its nerves, its thoughts. They do not rule it, nor do they control it. They are part of it, as the coral is part of it, as the ruins are part of it, as the water is part of it. They do not see the city as a place to conquer, but as a place to belong to. They do not look up, because there is no up. They do not look for the sky, because it has never existed for them. They are of the deep, and they live in the slow, patient way of the deep.

In this world, life is not defined by the absence of light, but by the presence of something else—something that moves, that grows, that remembers. The city does not end. It does not fade. It is not a relic. It is alive, and it will continue, long after the last human has forgotten what it was.

**What the Power Would Do**

When the benevolent and wise power, that which exists beyond the reach of time and space, turns its gaze upon this submerged city, it does not do so with dominion or destruction. It does not seek to impose order upon the chaos, nor to claim the depths as its own. It sees not a place to be tamed, but a place to be understood, to be honored. It watches not as a god of fire or fury, but as a guardian of the unseen, a keeper of the forgotten.

This power, which exists in the space between creation and dissolution, is not merely a force—it is a presence, a resonance, a song that lingers in the deepest currents of the ocean. It has seen many worlds, many lives, many forms of being. And in this city, it finds something rare, something precious: a world not shaped by will, but by harmony. Not built by hands, but grown by time. Not ruled by a single voice, but sung by many.

So the power does not intervene with grand gestures or miracles. It does not send messengers or prophets. It does not alter the city in any visible way. Instead, it listens.

It listens to the hum of the city, to the slow, steady pulse of the Korran, to the flickering of the Luminars, to the shifting of the Shapers. It listens to the language of the deep, the unspoken, the unspoken truths that live in the water. It hears what the city has always known, even if the city does not yet know it itself.

And it does not speak. Not in words, not in sound. It does not try to explain. It simply *is*. And in its being, it gives the city something it has never known before: recognition.

This is the first gift. Recognition. The knowledge that it is seen. That it is understood. That it is not forgotten, even though time has moved on and the world above has turned away. The city is not a relic. It is not a shadow of something that once was. It is a living, breathing, growing thing. And the power knows this. It does not need to say it. It does not need to prove it. It simply *is*, and through its presence, the city feels it.

Then, the power does not intervene. It does not change the city. It does not offer salvation, nor does it warn of destruction. It does not seek to elevate or to guide. It only *watches*.

But this watching is not passive. It is not mere observation. It is a kind of meditation, a kind of communion. It is the power allowing the city to exist as it is, to continue its slow, patient breath. It leaves the city alone, but it does not turn away. It does not leave. It remains, like the deep itself, like the water that holds the city in its embrace.

And so the city continues, as it always has. The Korran move through the ruins, the Luminars glow in the dark, the Shapers shift and change. The water hums. The light flickers. The silence is full, not empty. And in that silence, the power is present. Not as a master, not as a stranger, but as something older, something deeper, something that has always been.

And in the depths, where no eye can reach, the city remembers that it is not alone. It is not forgotten. It is not lost. It is *known*.

And that is enough.
