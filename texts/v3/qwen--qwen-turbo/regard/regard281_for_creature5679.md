If a benevolent and wise power were to look upon this place, it would not come with the intent to change it. It would not arrive as a conqueror, a savior, or even a guide. It would arrive as a witness, as a being attuned to the slow, deep pulse of the world, and it would see what humans cannot: the land not as a thing to be mapped or known, but as a being in its own right.

It would not speak in human words. It would not seek to explain. It would *feel*. It would feel the heat not as a burden, but as a presence, as a living weight pressing against the skin of the world. It would feel the water not as a resource, but as a memory, a river of time and breath. It would feel the silence not as absence, but as a language, as an echo of what is and what could be.

And so, the benevolent and wise power would do what it knows how to do. It would listen.

It would listen to the wind as it moves through the mist, not as a sound, but as a thought. It would listen to the pulse of the land, the slow, rhythmic rising and falling of the earth as if it were breathing. It would listen to the ruins, not as abandoned structures, but as voices that have not been silenced. It would listen to the creatures, not as animals, but as extensions of the land’s will, as beings that do not live, but *become*.

It would not impose anything. It would not teach. It would not reveal. It would simply be there, in the same way the land is there—waiting, watching, holding space for what is already happening.

Because this land is not waiting for a human. It is waiting for something else. Something that has always been here. Something that is not separate from the land, but *embodied* in it.

And so, the benevolent and wise power would not intervene. It would not save. It would not change. It would not claim. It would simply *rest* in the presence of the world, not as a visitor, but as a companion. As a being that understands that some places are not meant to be understood, but to be *felt*.

And in that presence, the land would breathe. Not in a way that is sudden or obvious, but in a way that is slow, deliberate, and eternal.

In the end, the benevolent and wise power would do nothing. And that would be enough.
