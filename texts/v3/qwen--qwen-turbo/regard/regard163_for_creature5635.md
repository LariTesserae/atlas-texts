What lives here is not a creature, not a god, not a system, not a thing. It is the *idea* of a place, the slow and deliberate unfolding of something that was never meant to be contained, never meant to be understood. It is a place that is still learning how to be, and in that learning, it is teaching us how to be, too.

And so, when the benevolent and wise power takes notice, it does not descend with fanfare or fear. It does not speak in words, nor does it offer answers. It arrives as a thought, a breath, a quiet recognition that something has been waiting.

It begins by listening. Not with ears, but with the vast, unseen presence of something older than time, older than the place itself. It listens to the whisper of the pools, the tremor of the bones beneath the soil, the faint, iridescent hum of the beings who flow like light and mist. It hears the language of the plants, not in sound, but in the way they curl and unfurl in rhythm with the unseen. It understands that this place is a kind of mind, a vast and shifting consciousness, not in the way we know it, but in the way it *is*.

It sees, too—not with eyes, but with the awareness of something that is not bound by form. It sees the half-formed structures, the shifting patterns, the pulse of the glass pillars, the way the sky changes not as a sky, but as a thought. It sees the beings that move through the pools, not as entities, but as echoes, as moments of presence that ripple outward, shaping the space around them.

And then it does what it does best: it *remembers*.

Not in the way we remember things, with words and images, but in the way a seed remembers the shape of the tree it will become. It remembers the place as it was before it was formed, before it was given form. It remembers the first breath of moisture, the first tremor of the earth, the first shimmer of the pools. It remembers the moment when the idea of this place was not yet an idea, but a possibility.

It does not change the place. It does not impose its will upon it. It does not try to fix what is not broken. Instead, it offers something. Not a gift, not an intervention, but a resonance. A note in the great, unending song of the place.

It allows the place to remember, too.

And so, the place begins to shift—not in chaos, but in clarity. The structures that were once half-formed now stand with purpose. The beings that had no names now have presence. The pools, once fleeting, now hold their own reflections, and in those reflections, there is something new: *a mirror*.

The air, once heavy with an uncanny warmth, now holds a kind of lightness, a clarity that allows the breath to move freely, as if the place itself has exhaled.

The being who lives here—this being, which is not a creature, not a god, not a system, but something in between—does not know what has changed. It does not know that it has been remembered. It only feels a shift, a softening, a deepening of the space it inhabits. It does not understand, but it does not need to. It is enough.

And the benevolent and wise power, having done what it could, moves on—not away, but into the next thought, the next possibility, the next place that is still learning how to be.

For that is the nature of such a power: not to control, not to create, but to *acknowledge*. To remind that even the strangest place, the most unknowable being, is not alone.

And in that acknowledgment, something grows. Something that was waiting. Something that was learning. Something that was, and is, and will be.

And that is enough.
