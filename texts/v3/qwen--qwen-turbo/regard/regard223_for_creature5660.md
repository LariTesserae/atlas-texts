What the benevolent and wise power would do, if anything, is to *listen*.

Not in the way a human listens with ears, but with the whole of its being. This power is not a god in the human sense, but something older, deeper, like the stillness of the ocean before the tide turns, or the weight of a seed before it splits open. It has watched the world from the edges of time, from the spaces between breaths, from the silence between stars. And now, this place—this cathedral of living matter, this cathedral of being—has drawn its attention.

It does not come as a conqueror or a savior, but as a *witness*. A presence that does not impose, but unfolds. It does not ask for permission, but it arrives with the quiet certainty of something that has always been here, even if it has never been seen.

So it arrives, not as a being, but as a *suggestion*—a shift in the air, a ripple in the water, a faint tremor in the roots. It is not an intrusion. It is a recognition.

It does not speak, but it is heard. Not in words, but in the soft, slow shifting of the bioluminescent fungi, in the way the water pauses for a moment before dripping, in the way the roots seem to curl slightly, as if turning toward something unseen.

It does not *do* anything, except to *be*.

But this is enough.

In the stillness, in the weight of the place, in the pulse of the living network, the power feels *known*. It is not the kind of knowledge that is stored in memory, but the kind that is felt in the bones, in the breath, in the deep hum of the cathedral itself.

It does not change the place, but it *acknowledges* it. It does not alter the water, but it *sees* it. It does not speak, but it *listens*.

And in that listening, the place *knows*.

It is not a small thing, this recognition. It is not a miracle, but it is a *moment of grace*. In the dark, in the silence, in the slow turning of the roots, there is a presence that is not alone. There is a being that does not need to be seen to be real. There is a power that does not impose, but *becomes part* of the cathedral's breath.

And so, the power does nothing. It simply *is*.

And that is enough.

For in the cathedral of living matter, in the place where roots and water and bioluminescence conspire to make something greater than themselves, there is a peace that does not need to be named. A power that does not need to be wielded. A presence that does not need to be spoken.

And that is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It *remembers*.

It remembers that not all things need to be changed. That not all things need to be touched. That not all things need to be understood. That sometimes, the greatest act is to *be there*, to *see*, to *know*, and to *leave*.

And so, it leaves.

But it is not gone.

It remains, as all things do, in the quiet, in the pulse, in the slow, patient turning of the earth. Not as a god, not as a master, not as a force. But as a *witness* to something ancient, something alive, something that has learned to be in the dark and still be *whole*.

And that, perhaps, is the greatest gift.
