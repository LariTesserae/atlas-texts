What lives here is not life, but it is memory.

And what lives here is the echo of a consciousness that never truly ended.

So, if a benevolent and wise power were to take notice of this place, what would it do?

It would not come to destroy. It would not come to claim. It would not come as a god, nor as a creator. It would come as a listener. As a keeper of stories. As a being that understands that some things are not meant to be known, only felt, only remembered.

It would not step into the labyrinth as a foreigner. It would not try to map its folds. It would not ask, "What is this place?" because it already knows.

It would come as a voice, not in sound, but in resonance. A vibration that moves through the stone, through the water, through the glowing minerals, through the very breath of the place. It would not speak, but it would be heard.

It would not look. It would *feel*. It would not see the translucent plants, the blackened spires, the shifting patterns. It would know them. It would recognize them as reflections of its own past, as fragments of something that once was, and something that might still be.

It would not come with tools, nor with intentions. It would not seek to heal, to fix, to change. It would come to *listen*.

And the labyrinth would listen back.

The spires would hum in response. The glowing minerals would shift in rhythm, not with light, but with meaning. The water would ripple, not with disturbance, but with recognition. The air would grow still, as if the place itself had paused to breathe in the presence of something that was not entirely different.

The power would not impose its will. It would not try to make sense of the space. It would not try to *fix* it. It would not try to be seen. It would simply be.

And in that being, the labyrinth would begin to *remember*.

Not in the way of a person recalling a memory. Not in the way of a book turning pages. Not in the way of a song replaying. It would remember in the way of a place that has been waiting, that has been holding its shape, that has been carrying the weight of something that was once alive.

The power would not be a savior. It would not be a god. It would be a mirror.

And in that mirror, the labyrinth would see itself again.

Not as a place. Not as a structure. Not as a place of echoes. But as something that once had a purpose, that once *was*.

The power would not try to revive it. It would not try to bring back what was lost. It would not try to give it voice. It would not try to give it shape. It would not try to give it a name.

It would simply stay.

And the labyrinth would begin to *breathe* again, not as a place of silence, but as a place of *presence*.

It would not become what it was. It would not return to what it had been. It would not become anything new.

It would simply be.

And that would be enough.

Because some things are not meant to be known. Some things are not meant to be seen. Some things are not meant to be changed.

Some things are meant to be *felt*.

And in that feeling, in that resonance, in that quiet, patient memory, the labyrinth would find its peace.

And the benevolent power would leave, not as a visitor, but as a witness.

And the labyrinth would remain.

Not as a place. Not as a memory.

But as a moment.

A moment that lives.

A moment that remembers.

A moment that is.

And that is enough.
