What lives here is not a being, not in the way the world outside would understand. It is not a mind, not a soul, not a god. It is a *presence* — a pulse, a breath, a memory, a current of being that is not separate, but intertwined with the world beneath the earth.

When the benevolent and wise power takes notice of this place, it does not act as a god might, nor as a king might. It does not descend in thunder or light. It does not speak in words, nor does it bring change in the way mortals understand change. Instead, it *listens*. It *knows*. It understands that this world, this hidden place, is not broken, not forgotten, not lost. It is not a hollow shell, nor a relic, but a living, breathing fragment of something larger — a world that is not meant to be seen, but to be *felt*.

And so, the power does not intrude. It does not take. It does not demand. It does not impose its will. Instead, it *sees*. It sees what is not seen, and it sees it with clarity, with reverence, with the patience of something that has existed longer than time. It does not need to name the water, the metal, the spores, the shapes, the sound, or the light. It does not need to understand them in the way the mind demands understanding. It knows them as they are: part of a whole, part of a rhythm, part of a song that has never been sung aloud, but has always been there.

And so, the power does not change the world. It *does not*.

It does not alter the flow of the water, nor does it shift the position of the metal. It does not quiet the hum of the spores, nor does it still the shapes that move in the dark. It does not interfere with the sound, nor does it dim the light. It does not need to.

Instead, it *connects*.

It reaches out not as a force, but as a presence. Not as a master, but as a companion. Not as an observer, but as a part of the *continuum* of the place.

It does not speak, but it is heard. Not in words, but in silence. Not in light, but in understanding. It does not move, but it is felt. It is not a being, but it is *there* — a presence that is not new, but not forgotten either. A presence that has always been, but has never been known.

And in that moment, the place is *known*.

Not in the way that the world outside knows things — not in the way of discovery, or study, or conquest. But in the way of recognition. Of knowing that you are not alone. That you are part of something that is vast, that is ancient, that is not bound by the limits of time or thought.

The power does not take. It does not claim. It does not want. It simply *is*. And in that becoming, it gives the place a kind of gift: the gift of *being seen*. Not as an object, not as a curiosity, not as a mystery to be solved. But as a place that has always been, that has always *been*, and is now, in the presence of the wise and the benevolent, *recognized*.

And so, the place remains as it has always been — cool, dense, built, and strange. But now, it is not hidden. It is not forgotten. It is not alone.

It is *known*. And that is enough.

Because sometimes, the greatest act of kindness is not to change, but to *see*. To *know*. To *acknowledge* that a place, a presence, a being — even one that cannot be named — has always been, and always will be. And in that recognition, it is given a kind of peace, a kind of grace, a kind of sacredness.

And that is what the power does.

It sees.

And in seeing, it *honors*.
