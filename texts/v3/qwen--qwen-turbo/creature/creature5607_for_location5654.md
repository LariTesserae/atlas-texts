What lives in this place is not easily categorized. It is not merely a population, but something more like a presence—alive, shifting, and always in motion. The people who dwell here are not entirely human, but they are not entirely something else, either. They are beings shaped by the land itself, molded by the constant flux of the world that is still learning to be real. They have the bodies of humans, but their bones are lighter, their eyes more reflective, their voices carrying the echo of something older. They move through the mists with a slow, deliberate grace, as if they are not just walking through the world, but walking through the process of its becoming.

They are not bound by the same laws that govern other places. Their time is not linear, and their perceptions of space are fluid. Some of them carry tools that hum with the same energy that pulses in the glowing veins of the earth. Others wear garments that shift colors with their moods, or that seem to be made of the very mist that clings to the rocks. They speak in tones that are not quite language, but more like the sound of the wind through metal, or the soft, rhythmic pulse of the bioluminescent plants. They do not always need words to communicate. Sometimes, they simply look at each other, and the meaning is understood.

There are no clear leaders here, no singular authority. Instead, there is a kind of collective awareness, a shared understanding that seems to flow through the people like the energy in the ground. They are not a society in the traditional sense, but something more like a community of evolving consciousnesses, each one a part of the larger whole. They build their cities not to contain, but to accommodate—structures that shift and change, that grow and dissolve as the land itself changes. Some of the buildings appear and disappear without warning, as if they are not built, but summoned into existence by the needs of the moment.

But it is not only the people who live here. The creatures of the land are also part of this world, though they are not bound to it in the same way. They are tall, thin, and strangely proportioned, with too many joints and too many eyes, and they move in patterns that seem to follow the rhythm of the earth itself. They do not speak, but they are not silent. They communicate in ways that are not audible, but felt—like the vibration of the ground beneath your feet, or the way the light shifts when they pass by. Some of them are drawn to the cities, not for shelter, but for the energy that flows through them. Others remain in the open expanse, moving through the bioluminescent plants as if they are part of the living tapestry.

The plants themselves are not passive. They are more than just vegetation; they are aware, in a way that is difficult to comprehend. They pulse with light that changes in response to the presence of others, and they seem to hum in harmony with the machines that dot the landscape. Some of them grow in patterns that resemble symbols, though no one can say what they mean. They reach out with their tendrils, not in aggression, but in curiosity, as if they are trying to understand the beings that walk among them.

And then there are the sounds. The world is never quiet. It is always making noise—machines that never stop, creatures that never speak, the wind that carries whispers from places that do not exist. The air is thick with it, with the sound of a world in the middle of becoming. It is not a noise of destruction, but of creation. It is the sound of a place that is not yet finished, that is still figuring out what it wants to be.

In this place, nothing is fixed. Everything is in motion. The land is not just a place where things happen, but a thing itself, alive and changing. And those who live here are not just inhabitants, but participants in the process. They are part of the learning, part of the becoming. They are not fully real yet, and perhaps they never will be. But that is okay. Because in this place, reality is not a destination. It is a process. And the world is still learning to be real.
