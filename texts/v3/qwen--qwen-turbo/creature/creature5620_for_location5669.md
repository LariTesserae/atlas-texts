What lives in this place is not easily named. It is not entirely of this world, nor of any other, but it is here, in the bones of the mountain, in the breath of the wind, in the hush between the stones.

The people are the most obvious, but even they are not entirely human. Their skin is pale, as if the sun has never truly touched them, and their eyes gleam with a light that seems to come from deep within. They move with a careful grace, as if the very air is delicate, as if the world is not meant to be disturbed. They speak in a language that flows like water through the cracks of the city, a tongue that does not always translate into words, but into something more felt than heard. Their voices are low, their gestures slow, as though they are not in a hurry, as though time is not a thing they need to chase.

But the people are not the only ones who live here. There are others, creatures that move between the cracks of the city, between the stones and the shadows. They are not entirely visible, not entirely real, but they are there. They have the shape of animals, but their forms are not fixed. One moment, they appear as a fox, sleek and sharp-eyed, watching from the edge of a terrace. The next, they are a bird, wings folded, perched on a rooftop, watching with a stillness that is not natural. They do not seem to belong to the world, but they are part of it, as much as the city itself.

These beings are not creatures in the way we understand them. They are not animals, nor are they wholly human. They are something in between, something that has not yet decided what it wants to be. They are born of the place, of the mountain, of the cold and the stillness. They exist in the spaces between the buildings, in the hollows of the rock, in the corners where the light does not quite reach. They are not loud, not destructive, but they are present. They watch. They listen. They remember.

There is also something in the water. The pools and springs that dot the city are not just sources of moisture, but places of quiet power. They are still, dark, and deep. Some say they are mirrors, not of the sky, but of something else—of the past, of the future, of what lies beneath the surface of the world. The people do not drink from them, not in the way we do. They touch them, they watch them, they speak to them. And sometimes, something answers.

The wind is not silent, though it is not loud. It moves through the city like a whisper, like a breath held too long. It does not carry scent, nor does it bring sound, but it carries something else. It carries the weight of memory, of things that have been and things that have not yet come. It is the wind that moves the stones, that shifts the shadows, that makes the city feel as though it is moving, even when it is still.

The city itself is alive in a way that is not obvious. It is not breathing, not in the way we understand, but it is aware. The stones remember. The walls hold secrets. The bridges, the stairways, the terraces—they are not just structures, but parts of a whole, parts of something larger than themselves. They are not built, but grown, as if the mountain had decided to shape itself into a home, and the people had only followed.

And then there are the things that are not seen at all. The things that exist in the spaces between the light and the dark, between the silence and the sound. They are not loud, not destructive, not even dangerous. They are just there, existing in the quiet, in the stillness, in the way the city breathes. They are not gods, not spirits, not monsters. They are something else. Something that has not yet been named.

In this place, what lives is not what you expect. It is not what you can easily describe. It is not what you can hold in your hands or in your mind. It is what remains after the world has been carved, after the city has been built, after the people have come and gone. It is the thing that lingers, the thing that waits, the thing that is always there, even when you do not see it. It is the quiet power of the mountain, the silence of the sky, the stillness of the place. And it is not done yet.
