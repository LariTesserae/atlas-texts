What lives here is not easily named, not in the way we name things. It is not merely a collection of creatures, but something more pervasive, more integral to the land itself. This place is alive, not in the way we think of life—breathing, moving, speaking—but in a deeper, older sense, one that hums in the bones of the stone and pulses in the breath of the wind.

There are the small ones, the ones that scuttle and dart, the ones that seem to vanish when not looking. They are not animals in the human sense, but they are not entirely unlike them. Some have wings that flicker like the edge of a shadow, others have legs that move in a way that suggests they are not bound by the laws of gravity. They are silent, but not without sound. Their movement is a whisper, a rustle in the dust, a pulse in the stillness. Their eyes, if they have them, are too large, too dark, too knowing. They do not fear the cold, nor the thin air, because they are part of it, as much as the stone and the wind.

There are others, too, that do not move. They are not statues, but they might as well be. They are shapes in the rock, not carved but formed, as if the land itself has learned to take on the form of something that once lived. Some are long and sinuous, like the remains of a creature that has been eroded into the very earth. Others are more angular, like a skull or a ribcage, but they are not just bones. They are alive in the sense that they remember, that they hold the echo of something that was, and maybe still is. They do not speak, but they sing in the wind, in the way the stones vibrate when the air moves through them.

There are the ones that are not seen, only felt. They are the sound, the deep rumbling that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere. They are the vibrations that move through the ground, that make the bones hum. These are not creatures, but they are not mere forces of nature either. They are something in between. They are the land itself, breathing, shifting, remembering. They are the pulse of the place, the heartbeat that does not beat in the way we understand.

And then there are the ones that are not here, but they are here. They are the shadows that do not follow the light, the shapes that appear when the eye is not looking directly. They are not real, but they are not illusions either. They are the memory of the place, the stories it has kept, the things it has forgotten. They are the ones that come to those who stand too long in the silence, who listen too closely to the wind. They are the ones that do not speak, but they are heard.

This is a place that does not need to be inhabited, but it is inhabited in ways that defy understanding. It is not empty, but it is not full. It is not alive, but it is not dead. It is a space between, a place where the rules of existence are not fixed, where the line between the seen and the unseen is blurred, where the self is not a thing, but a vibration, a shadow, a whisper in the wind.

What lives here is not a being, but a presence. It is the land, the wind, the sound, the silence, the motion, the stillness. It is not separate from the place, but it is the place. And it is watching.
