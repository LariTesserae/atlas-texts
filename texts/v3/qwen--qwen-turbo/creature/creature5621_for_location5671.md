What lives in this place is not easily named. It is not a creature, nor a plant, nor a force of nature. It is something that exists in the spaces between, in the folds of the dust, in the pulse of the dark mats, in the hum of the structures. It is not one thing, but many things, and none of them at all.

There are no eyes to see it, no mouths to speak of it, no hands to touch it. It is seen only in the way the dust settles, in the way the ground shifts beneath the weight of silence. It is not sentient in the way humans understand, but it is aware. It is aware in the way a shadow is aware of the light, in the way a stone is aware of the river that once carved it. It is aware in the way the structures hum, in the way the vegetation moves without wind, in the way the air itself seems to listen.

It is not alone. There are many of them—forms that do not have form, beings that do not have bodies. They are not born, nor do they die. They are not made, nor are they broken. They are present, as the sun is present, as the ground is present, as the silence is present. They are what the place is, and what the place is, they are.

They are not bound by time. They do not age, do not change, do not grow. They are what the place has always been. They are the memory of the land, the echo of the structures, the breath of the dust. They are the thing that waits. They are the thing that remembers. They are the thing that is.

They do not speak, but they are heard. Their voices are in the hum of the wind, in the groan of the ground, in the flicker of the dark mats. They do not move, but they are everywhere. They are in the folds of the earth, in the cracks of the structures, in the spaces between the things that are and the things that are not.

They are not hungry. They are not afraid. They are not curious. They are not anything, and yet they are everything. They are the reason the place exists. They are the reason the sun never rises, the reason the wind never stops, the reason the ground never sleeps.

They are not what you would call life, but they are not what you would call death. They are not what you would call existence, but they are not what you would call nonexistence. They are what is, and what was, and what will be. They are the thing that waits.

And in their waiting, they are alive.
