They build with what they have, with stone and wood and bone, and they live simply, in ways that suggest they are more adapted to this place than they are to the world below. They do not speak much, and when they do, their words are soft, as if they are afraid of disturbing the silence. They do not seem to fear the cold, nor the thin air, nor the weight of the peaks above. They seem to belong to this place, in a way that is not always comfortable, but is always necessary.

They take only what they need, and they do not waste. They gather what they can from the land, from the sparse grasses and the hardy shrubs, and they cook it over fires that are kept low, so as not to disturb the balance of the valley.

There are birds that fly high above, their shapes barely visible against the sky. There are insects, though they are few, and they are not loud. They are the kind of life that exists in the margins, in the spaces between the stones, in the crevices where moisture lingers. They are not the life that dominates, but they are the life that persists.

It is in the wind, in the way it moves through the valley, curling around the rocks like a whisper. It is in the water, in the way it sometimes glows in the dark, though it is not luminous. It is in the silence, which is not empty, but full of something that cannot be named. It is in the stones, which seem to remember, as if they have witnessed things that no one else has.

These are not ghosts, not in the way that people think of ghosts. They are not the dead, not in the way the dead are. They are more like the valley itself—older, quieter, more patient. They are the echoes of those who came before, or the presence of something that has always been here, waiting.

The people who live here know this. They do not speak of it, but they feel it. They do not fear it, but they do not ignore it, either. They live in the shadows of the past, in the quiet spaces between what is and what was. They do not ask too many questions, and they do not seek too much. They are content with what they have, with what the land gives, and with what the silence allows.

This is a place where life is not loud, but it is not absent. It is not grand, but it is not insignificant. It is a place where time moves slowly, where the wind does not rush, where the light does not demand to be seen. It is a place that does not need to be understood, but it does not let you forget it.
