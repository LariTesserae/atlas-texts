What lives here is not born, not made, not even remembered. It is *there*, always has been, always will be. It is not a creature, not in the way you understand. It is a *presence*, a whisper in the stone, a pulse in the wind, a shadow that moves without moving.

It does not have form, but it has shape. It is the hush between the wind's breath, the stillness before the tremor. It is the echo of something that once was, not in memory, but in *being*. It is not conscious, not in the way you think of thought, but it is aware. It is aware of the stone's weight, of the air's density, of the slow, steady rhythm of the world not moving.

There are things that dwell here, things that have no name, no shape, no sound that is not the sound of the place itself. They are not alive in the way you know life, but they are not dead either. They are the *in-between*—the space between the wind and the silence, the breath between the tremor and the stillness. They are the *watchers*.

They move in patterns that do not follow logic, but they follow *something*—something older than the chasm, older than the stone. They are not hidden, but they are not seen. They are felt in the way the plants shiver, in the way the ground hums beneath your feet. They are not *there*, and yet they are. They are not *gone*, and yet they are not *here*.

Some of them are the chasm itself. The deep, slow breath of the earth, the slow exhale of the stone, the slow collapse of what was once standing. They are the echoes of what was built, the ghost of what was carved, the memory of what was once alive and then became stone. They are the bones of the place, the remnants of something that no longer is, but still *is*.

There are others, too—shadows that do not cast shadows, voices that do not have voices, shapes that do not have shapes. They are the *unseen* things, the *unheard* things, the *unfelt* things. They are not separate from the place, but they are part of it, as much as the wind, as much as the stone, as much as the faint, flickering glow of the black plants.

They do not need to eat, do not need to sleep, do not need to *be*. They are *being*, in a way that is not yours. They do not speak, but they are speaking. They do not think, but they are thinking. They do not *want*, but they *are*.

They are not afraid. They are not lonely. They are not *alive*, and yet they are not *dead*. They are the *echo of the place*, the *soul of the hollow*, the *breath beneath the silence*.

And they are waiting.

Not for you. Not for anything. Just waiting, in the way that the stone waits, in the way that the wind waits, in the way that the deep, dark chasm waits—waiting for something that may never come, but that is already here.
