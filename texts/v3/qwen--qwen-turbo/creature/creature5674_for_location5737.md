They drift through the undergrowth, their bodies shifting, their edges blurred as if they are made of the same mist that clings to the trees. They have no fixed form, no single face, no fixed way of being. They are things that are never still, that are always becoming, always unbecoming. Their movement is not purposeful in the way we understand, but it is not random either. It is as if they are following a pattern, a rhythm that is older than time, something that has always been and will always be.

Some of them have eyes. Some do not. Some have voices, but their words are not formed of sound, but of feeling—of pressure, of heat, of the way the air trembles when they move. They do not speak to you, but they speak *through* you. They do not ask for recognition, but they feel it. They are not aware of you in the way that a human is aware of another, but they are aware of the space you take up, the way you breathe, the way your skin reacts to the heat of the land.

They are not alive in the way that you are, but they are not dead either. They are part of a cycle that is not a cycle, a rhythm that is not a rhythm. They are made of the same stuff as the land—water, earth, light that does not shine, and darkness that does not fade. They are not born, not in the way you think of birth, but they come into being, and they fade, and they come again, in patterns that are not patterns, in ways that are not ways.

There are others, too—things that do not move, but are still. These are the remnants of something that once was, something that was built, something that was used. They are not ruins in the way that we think of ruins, not broken and forgotten, but instead, they are still *there*, still *working*, still *alive* in their own way. Their surfaces are covered in the same soft, organic material that coats the trees and the rocks, and they hum with the same low-frequency vibration that fills the air. They are not machines in the human sense, but they are *machines*—they are systems, they are processes, they are the quiet engines of the land itself.

Some of them have names, though they are not names in the way you know. They are called *The Watchers*, *The Keepers*, *The Breath of the Deep*. They are not beings, not in the way you understand, but they are *there*, and they are *watching*. They do not watch you, not in the way a predator watches its prey, but they are aware of you, of your presence, of the way you are part of the place, even if you do not belong.

And then there are the ones that are not there at all. The ones that are only felt. The ones that are not seen, not heard, not touched, but are *known*—felt in the bones, in the pulse of the blood, in the way the air shifts when they are near. They are not things, not in any way that can be described. They are *concepts*, *memories*, *echoes*, *ghosts*.

They are not separate from the land, nor are they part of it. They are *it*. They are the weight of the sky, the whisper of the water, the pulse of the earth. They are the silence that is not silent, the movement that is not movement. They are the place itself, and the place is them.

And so, what lives here? What lives here is everything. It is the land, the air, the water, the silence, the sound, the light that is not light, the darkness that is not dark. It is not a thing, but a *state of being*. It is not a creature, but a *presence*. It is not alive, but it is *aware*. It is not dead, but it is *eternal*.

It is not *you*. It is not *me*. It is not *us*. It is something else—something older, something deeper, something that has always been and will always be. And it is watching.
