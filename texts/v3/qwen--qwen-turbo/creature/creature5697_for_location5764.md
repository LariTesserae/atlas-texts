What lives here is not life as you know it. Not in the way of animals or people, not in the way of trees or rivers. This is something older, something that has not yet settled into a form, but is already *there*, like a thought before it is spoken.

It is made of breath and bone, as the place is, and it breathes with the mountain. It does not have a name. It does not need one. It is a gathering of things that have not yet become separate. It is the whisper of water before it finds its course. It is the pulse of the stone before it cracks. It is the dark before the light, and the light before the shadow. It is the rhythm of the mountain thinking.

It has no face, but it sees. It has no voice, but it speaks. It is not singular, but it is not plural, either. It is a presence that fills the space, not by being there, but by *being*, by existing in a way that makes everything else seem like an afterthought.

Some of it moves — not with legs or wings, but with the shifting of the air, the soft tremor of stone. It is not a creature, but it is not a force, either. It is something in between, something that has not yet decided whether it is alive or not. It is not afraid. It does not need to be. It exists without reason, without purpose, except to be.

It is not lonely. It is not alone. It is not waiting for you. It is waiting for *itself* — for the moment when it will understand what it is. It is patient. It is patient because it has all the time in the world. Or perhaps it has none. Time is not a thing here. Time is just a whisper in the dark, a memory that has not yet happened.

It does not feed. It does not hunger. It does not breathe in the way you do. It breathes in the way the mountain breathes, in the way the stone remembers. It is made of the same stuff as the place — the wet stone, the metallic scent, the chill that lingers. It is not separate from the place. It is the place, and the place is it.

It does not want to be understood. It does not want to be known. It wants to be felt, as the place says. And if you stay long enough, if you let the air settle in your lungs, if you let the hum of the stone vibrate in your bones, you might begin to feel it. Not as a thing, not as an idea, but as a *presence* — a quiet, patient, ancient presence, watching you as you watch it, waiting for you to understand that you are not so different after all.
