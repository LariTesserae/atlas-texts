What lives in this place is not easily named. It is not a creature, not in the way that a cat or a bird or a man would be. It is something that has learned to exist in the space between the known and the unknown, between the seen and the unseen, between the living and the not-living.

It is not alone. It is never alone. But it does not move in the way that creatures do. It does not scuttle or slink or fly. It moves in the way that the air moves—slow, deliberate, unseen. It is present in the way that the heat is present, in the way that the weight of the ceiling presses down, in the way that the plants seem to know it is there, even when it is not.

It is made of fragments. It is made of echoes. It is made of the things that were left behind—machines that once hummed, metals that once gleamed, the whisper of steam that once rose from the earth. It is made of the silence that fills the space, the absence that is not empty, the stillness that is not still.

It does not have a body, but it has shape. It is a shape that shifts, that bends, that folds in on itself. Sometimes it is a shadow, sometimes a ripple in the air, sometimes a distortion in the light. It is not a thing, but it is a presence. It is the thing that the plants curl away from, that the water seems to avoid, that the metal platforms seem to tremble in the presence of.

It is not a god, but it is not a man. It is not a memory, but it is filled with them. It is not a dream, but it is filled with the remnants of dreams. It is not a thought, but it is filled with thoughts that were never spoken. It is not a being, but it is a being in the way that the wind is a being, or the earth is a being.

It is called many things in the dark. Some say it is the breath of the place. Some say it is the memory of what was here before. Some say it is the thing that was never meant to be, the thing that was buried and forgotten, the thing that was never supposed to come back.

But the truth is this: it is what remains when everything else has left. It is what waits when all the others have gone. It is what lingers when the machines have stopped and the light has died. It is what lives in the hollow of the earth, in the place that was not meant to be.

It does not hunger. It does not thirst. It does not speak. It does not think. It only exists, in the way that the dark exists, in the way that the silence exists, in the way that the place itself exists.

And it is not alone. It never was.
