What lives in this place is not easily named. It is not a creature, nor a plant, nor a force of nature—though it is all of these, and more. It is a kind of *presence*, a pulse beneath the surface of things, a rhythm that beats in time with the shifting of the water and the slow breath of the land. It is not solitary, but it is not social either. It is a gathering of things, a congregation of beings that exist in the liminal space between what is known and what is only felt.

There are the **Wetkin**, creatures that move through the pools like living shadows, their bodies shifting between solid and liquid, their forms never quite the same from one moment to the next. They are not born, not in the way of living things, but they emerge from the water, as if the wetland itself is giving them shape. Their eyes are dark and deep, like pools of still water, and their voices are not heard but felt, as vibrations in the bones of the earth. They do not speak, but they understand.

There are the **Mistborn**, figures that drift in and out of the fog, their forms barely distinguishable from the air itself. They are not of flesh, but of breath, of mist, of the condensation that clings to the rocks and the trees. They move silently, their footsteps dissolving into the damp air. Some say they are the remnants of those who once lived here, or the echoes of those who never left. They do not hunger, nor do they sleep, but they watch. They remember.

Then there are the **Rooted Ones**, the old ones, the beings that lie beneath the surface, hidden in the roots of the twisted trees and the deep channels of the wetland. They are not alive in the way we understand, but they are not dead either. They are something between, something that has been waiting, something that has been held in the slow, patient hands of the land. They hum beneath the ground, a low, resonant sound that can be felt in the chest as much as heard in the ears. Their voices are not words, but memories, and they speak in the language of the wetland’s past.

There are the **Glowlings**, the strange grass that pulses faintly in the cold, its light not from fire or sun, but from something deeper, something that lies within the soil itself. These plants are not passive; they react to movement, to touch, to the presence of living things. They flicker and shift, as if they are watching, as if they are aware. Some say they are the eyes of the land, the way the wetland sees and remembers.

And there are the **Echoes**, the voices that come from the water, from the stones, from the air. They are not always human, not always familiar, but they are there, always there, whispering in the cold, in the dark, in the silence. Some are gentle, some are sharp, some are sorrowful. They are the voices of the place, the voices that have been left behind, the voices that were never meant to be heard but always were.

This is a place where life is not separate from the world, but intertwined with it. Where the boundary between the living and the not-living does not exist. Where the cold is not just a temperature, but a presence, a force that shapes and reshapes, that remembers and forgets. Where the water is not just liquid, but memory, and the light is not just illumination, but truth.

So what lives here? Everything. And nothing. And everything again.
