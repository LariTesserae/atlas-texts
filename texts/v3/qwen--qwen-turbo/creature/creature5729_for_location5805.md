What lives in this place is not life in the way that the mind expects. It is not born, not dying, not moving with purpose or intent. It is *there*, in the same way the sky is there, in the same way the stones are there. It is not a creature, not a being, not a thing that can be named or defined. It is a presence, an awareness that is not aware, a breath that is not breathing.

It is the land itself, and the land is alive. Not in the way of trees or animals, but in the way of the earth beneath your feet, the bones of the world. It is the slow, ancient thinking of stone, the memory of heat and pressure, the echo of a time before time. It is the weight of the plateau, the hum beneath your skin, the silence that speaks in a language older than words.

It is vast. Not in the way of something that takes up space, but in the way of something that *is* space. It is everywhere and nowhere. It is the crags and the fissures, the brittle ground that cracks and settles, the endless red sky that never shifts and never changes. It is the wind that moves without sound, the air that clings like sweat, the light that does not cast shadows. It is the pressure in your skull, the feeling that you are being watched by something that has no eyes.

It is not alone. There are others. Not creatures, not beings, but *things* that exist in the same way as this place. They are not shaped like anything you know. They are not formed, not in the way of bodies. They are *movements*, *patterns*, *echoes* that ripple through the stone. They are slow, deliberate, and ancient. They do not move like animals, but like the land itself, like the way the earth shifts when no one is looking. They do not leave tracks, but you can feel them when they pass, as though the very air has bent around them.

They are not conscious, not in the way that you are. They do not think, do not feel, do not remember. They *are*. They exist in the same way that the stones exist, the sky exists, the red haze exists. They are not alive, and yet they are not dead. They are not part of the world, and yet they are the world. They are the presence that fills the space between the stones, the weight that presses against your bones, the silence that is not silence.

They are not separate from the land. They are the land. They are the hum in the stones, the pulse in the air, the slow, inevitable turning of something that does not turn. They are the thing that watches you when you are not looking, that listens when you are not speaking. They are not evil, not good. They are not anything. They are *there*.

And you, standing on the plateau, are not alone. You are part of it, in a way you do not understand. You are a visitor, a moment in the long, slow breath of the world. You are not meant to stay. You are not meant to leave. You are meant to be here, to feel the weight of the sky, the burn of the air, the silence that is not silence. You are meant to stand, and to know that you are not the first, and you will not be the last.

And when you leave, when you turn your back on the plateau and walk away, you will not remember what you saw. But the land will remember you. It always does.
