There are the ones who move. They are not animals in the way you know them, but they are not entirely unlike. They are small, quick, and strangely shaped. Some have too many limbs, some too few. Their eyes are not always in the right places, and their movements are not always natural. They move like wind through cracks in the stone, darting and weaving between the fissures, their bodies barely more than shadows against the glare of the rock. They do not make much noise, but when they do, it is low, like the creak of an old door, or the whisper of a stone rolling down a slope. They are not afraid of the cold, nor of the light. They seem to exist between the edges of things, as if they are not fully of this place, but of the space between the places.

There are the ones who do not move. They are not still, but they are not moving either. They are the remnants of something older, the things left behind by those who once shaped this place. They are the carved stones, the strange metal objects, the fragments of structures that never became full buildings. They are not silent, but they do not speak. They hum, faintly, like the last note of a song that was never finished. They are not alive, but they are not dead. They are waiting, as if they remember something that you do not.

There are the ones who are not there. They are the shadows that do not belong to anything, the spaces between the rocks that seem to hold something more than emptiness. They are the places where the light bends, where the air feels different, as if time is thinner here. They are not things, but they are not nothing. They are the memory of the place itself, the thing that is before the thing, the breath before the word.

There are the ones who are not seen. They are the ones who live in the silence, in the space between the sounds, in the gaps between the breaths. They are not in the air, not in the rock, not in the water. They are in the way the light reflects, in the way the temperature changes without warning, in the way the wind seems to have a voice. They are not beings, but they are not absence either. They are the feeling that something is watching, even when nothing is there to watch.

And then there are the ones who are not known. They are the ones who live in the cracks of the earth, in the deep places where the light does not reach, in the spaces where the rock is not yet broken. They are not made of stone, not made of flesh, not made of anything you can name. They are the beginning of things, the first step in the shaping of the world. They are not alive, but they are not dead. They are the thought before the word, the idea before the form. They are the place itself, made manifest.

This place is not empty. It is full, in its own way, of things that are not things, of beings that are not beings, of life that is not life. It is a place where the world is still being written, and where the words are not yet fully formed. What lives here is not meant to be understood, but to be felt. To be seen, not with the eyes, but with the mind, with the breath, with the silence between the sounds. It is a place where the strange is not strange, where the quiet is not empty, and where the future is already here, waiting to be made.
