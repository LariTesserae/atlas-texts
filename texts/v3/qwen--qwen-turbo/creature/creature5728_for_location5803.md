What lives here is not life as we know it, but something that hums in the background of existence, something that breathes with the slow, deliberate motion of the land itself. It is not alive in the way we understand life, but it is not dead either. It is something in between, something that exists as a concept as much as a presence.

The plants are the closest thing to inhabitants. They are not merely growing—they are *becoming*. Each one is a vessel for something deeper, a structure that holds the memory of the world’s formation. Their translucent leaves shift color not just with light, but with thought, with the slow, shifting attention of the place. They do not need to move to communicate; they simply are, and in being, they *listen*. They are not passive, but they are not active either. They are in a state of quiet expectation, as if waiting for something to happen that has already happened, or that has not yet begun.

Beneath the surface, something else stirs. It is not a creature, not in the sense of flesh and bone, but it is not a void either. It is a presence that is felt more than seen, a pressure in the air, a weight on the mind. It is the land itself, the slow pulse of the earth’s still-unfolding form. It is not conscious in the way we are, but it is aware in a way that is older, deeper, and less comprehensible. It is the rhythm of the place, the steady, mechanical hum that underlies everything. Sometimes it sounds like a heartbeat. Sometimes it sounds like a machine. Sometimes it sounds like a voice, though it speaks in no language.

There are no animals, but there are *beings* that move through the dust. They are not seen, but they are *felt*. They are shadows that do not cast light, shapes that do not have form. They are the echoes of things that might have been, or things that have not yet been. They drift between the skeletal plants, moving with a grace that is not human, not animal, but something more alien, more elemental. They are not separate from the land—they are the land, in motion. They are the *idea* of movement, the ghost of a step that never quite lands.

The air itself carries something. It is not air in the way we know it—it is more like a presence, a thin, metallic breath that does not fill the lungs but fills the mind. It is not empty, but it is not full either. It is the space between thought and being, between the seen and the unseen. It is the sound of the place itself, the quiet, mechanical whisper that lingers in the edges of hearing.

This place does not need to be inhabited. It is *inhabited* in a way that is not human. It is a place of becoming, where existence is not a fixed state but a process. The plants, the dust, the shifting land, the strange hum in the air—all of it is part of the same living thing, a vast, slow, deliberate being that is not alive, but is not dead. It is not conscious, but it is not unconscious. It is not a place, but it is a *presence*.

And if you stand long enough, if you let your mind quiet and your body still, you might feel it too. Not as a creature, not as a force, but as a presence that has always been here, that has always been becoming, and that will continue to be, long after the idea of time has been forgotten.
