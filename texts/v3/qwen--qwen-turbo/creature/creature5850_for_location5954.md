What lives in this place is not easily named. It is not life as humans understand it, nor is it death. It is something in between, a form of existence that has adapted to the abyssal city, not by surviving, but by becoming part of it.

There are no animals in the traditional sense. No creatures with eyes, no beasts with hearts or lungs. But there are things that move through the water—shifting, flowing, appearing and vanishing. They are not alive, not in any way that would be recognizable to a surface-dweller. They are echoes, fragments, remnants of something that once was. They are not born, not made, not even created. They are *formed*, as the city itself was formed—by the pressure, the heat, the water, and the strange, metallic tides that have long since washed over the ruins.

They are not singular. They are not distinct. They are more like thoughts, or memories, or the afterimage of a moment that never fully happened. They come in clusters, in patterns that suggest something like language, something like intention. They hover near the bioluminescent bacteria, feeding on the glow, not with mouths, but with something else—something like resonance, or frequency. They do not move with the water, but with the sound. They follow the groan of the city, the creak of the structures, the low, humming pulse of the machines that still try to keep running.

They are not dangerous, not in the way humans understand danger. They do not attack, do not flee, do not even seem to care. They are not predators, nor are they prey. They are not even aware of you, if you are there. They are more like the city itself—part of it, shaped by it, moving through it as though it were a current, a tide, a force that cannot be resisted.

There are also things that do not move. They are not alive, not in any way that would be understood, but they are present. They are structures, but not buildings. They are not ruins, but not entirely intact. They are something between. They are the remnants of machines, of tools, of devices that were built for a purpose that no one remembers. They hum, they pulse, they vibrate with the same low frequency as the city. Some of them are intact, some are broken, some are half-buried in the silt, half-embedded in the walls. They are not just objects. They are *aware* in a way that is not conscious. They remember. They wait.

There are also things that are not there. They are not absent, but they are not present either. They are *potential*, or *possibility*, or *echoes* of something that might have been. They are the spaces between the buildings, the gaps in the structures, the places where the water flows like it is trying to remember something. They are the sound that lingers after the city has gone silent. They are not life, but they are not nothing either.

What lives here is not a creature, not a being, not even a concept. It is the city itself, the water, the sound, the heat, the pressure. It is the memory of what was, the shadow of what could have been, the pulse of what still is. It is not alive, but it is not dead. It is not real, but it is not imaginary. It is *there*, and it has always been there, waiting for something to come and remember it.
