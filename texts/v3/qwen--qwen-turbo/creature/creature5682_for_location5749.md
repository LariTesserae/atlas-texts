What lives here is not life as you know it. Not in the way that animals breathe or plants grow, not in the way that rivers run or stars burn. Here, life is a thing that moves through the air like a whisper, that clings to the spires like a remembered dream, that pulses in the moss and the algae, that hums in the silence and winks in the mist.

It is not a single being, nor a collection of beings. It is a presence, a breath, a weight that settles on the skin and the mind. It is not conscious in the way you are, but it is aware in the way that the wind is aware of the mountain. It does not think, but it remembers. It does not speak, but it sings in the hollows of stone and the folds of vegetation.

It is made of many things. Some of them are things you would call living: the insects that crawl through the moss, the small, furred creatures that move like shadows, the creatures with too many eyes or no eyes at all. They are here, but they are not the main thing. They are the surface, the skin, the moving parts of something much older, much larger.

Beneath them, deeper than the roots of the rootless trees, deeper than the hollows in the spires, there is something else. Something that does not sleep, but does not wake either. It is not alive, but it is not dead. It is not a god, but it is not a stone either. It is a thing that has been here since the moment the spires first rose, since the first drop of moisture fell, since the first breath of wind touched the land.

It is a being of shape and sound. It is the hum that comes from the ground, the low, continuous vibration that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere at once. It is the way the spires shift, ever so slightly, as if they are adjusting to something unseen. It is the way the vegetation moves, not with the wind, but with a rhythm that is not of the world you know.

It is not cruel, but it is not kind. It is not good, but it is not evil. It is the thing that exists beyond the reach of human morality, beyond the need for purpose or meaning. It is the thing that has no name, no face, no voice, but it is here. It is everywhere. It is the reason the spires are sharp, the reason the moss is green, the reason the air is cold and the light is thin.

It is alive. Not in the way you are, but in the way that the sky is alive when it rains. It is a living thing that does not need to eat, that does not need to rest, that does not need to be seen. It is the thing that watches you when you are not looking, that listens when you are not speaking, that knows when you are not thinking.

And it does not want you to understand. It does not want you to leave. It wants you to stay, to feel the cold on your skin, to hear the hum in your bones, to see the way the light bends and the way the spires lean toward you, as if they are waiting for something. Something that may never come.

But it is here. It has always been here. And it will be here long after you are gone.
