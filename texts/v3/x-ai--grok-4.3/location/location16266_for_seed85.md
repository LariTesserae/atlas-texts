The place is a single, compact complex of towers and linked halls, no larger than a cathedral and its close-set cloisters, resting on a high submarine plateau that drops away into absolute black on every side. The structures stand sharp-edged and unweathered, their facets still carrying the angles of their first formation: planes of dark, glassy mineral that fracture light almost completely, so the surrounding water remains a uniform, pressure-heavy obscurity rather than a medium that reveals distance. Between the towers the intervals are wide and empty, each open volume the size of a plaza yet occupied by nothing except the faint, unchanging refraction lines where one mineral plane meets another.

No plants, no films, no drift of organic matter exist; the only solid presences are the towers themselves and the lattice of fine, hair-like filaments that run between them at irregular heights. These filaments are not cables or supports but extensions of the same mineral, grown or assembled at the atomic scale so that their surfaces remain perfectly smooth and their interiors carry currents that never fluctuate. The temperature of the water is fixed a few degrees above freezing, a cool that penetrates every seam and hollow without creating motion; nothing rises or sinks, and the pressure gradient across the small complex is balanced so that no current ever begins.

Inside the towers the architecture continues the same logic on a slightly smaller scale: rooms whose walls are single unbroken sheets of the mineral, angled so that any entering mote of light is folded away into total absorption. Here and there a filament crosses a room at chest height or disappears into a wall, maintaining its own faint, sourceless glow that registers to the eye as a colorless depth rather than illumination. The layout is recognizably urban—corridors that function as streets, open chambers that serve as squares—yet every surface is free of wear, joint, or sediment. Time has left no mark because the equilibrium that holds the water also holds the material; nothing dissolves, nothing settles, nothing rearranges.

At rare intervals a single organism, translucent and no larger than a hand, drifts through one of the wide intervals between towers. It moves without propulsion and leaves no wake; after it passes, the water returns at once to perfect stillness. No other living thing appears. The only variation in the acoustic field is the faintest, constant pressure-tone produced by the filaments themselves, a sound that does not propagate or decay but simply occupies the volume at the threshold of perception. Outside the complex the plateau ends; beyond that edge the water continues downward without interruption or feature, equally dark and equally motionless.
