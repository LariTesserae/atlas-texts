The valley floor lies low and open, a broad, shallow depression that stretches for many kilometers before the land rises again into sharper ridges still forming themselves out of the crust. Water is never far from any surface. It beads on every leaf and rock, gathers in quick, dark pools between root masses, and seeps constantly from seams in the stone so that thin curtains of droplets trace the black cliffs like slow tears. The cold is pervasive yet not frozen; a damp, penetrating chill that keeps the air heavy and the breath visible in pale wisps even under the perpetual half-light.

Vegetation crowds in dense, layered thickets. Broad, waxy fronds and needle-leaved canopies overlap so closely that movement through them feels like parting heavy curtains. Moisture gleams along every vein and edge. The plants grow rapidly enough to repair storm damage within days, their tissues knitting over torn stems with pale new growth that soon darkens again.

Above and between the growth, the young terrain remains sharp. Jagged shelves of recently exposed basalt and schist jut at steep angles, their fracture faces still clean and unsoftened. Water running across these fresh planes cuts narrow, glittering channels that re-form after every surge, so the rock itself seems to be rearranging at the speed of weather.

The only built things are scattered and half-swallowed. Low domes of a seamless, matte-black material sit half-buried in the marshy soil, their surfaces unmarked by time yet clearly artificial. A few lean, crystalline spars rise among the reeds, each one emitting a steady, sub-audible thrum that keeps the surrounding water in gentle, concentric motion even when the larger storms are absent. These remnants do not dominate; they simply persist, integrated into the churn like any other feature of the valley.

Sound fills every interval between the plants and stones. Thunder rolls almost without pause, layered over the continuous roar of water sheets sliding down the cliffs, the explosive crack of branches under sudden wind gusts, and the dense chorus of unseen creatures calling, scraping, and striking against one another. The noise is constant and overlapping, so that silence never arrives and even the smallest movements produce an immediate, amplified echo within the saturated air.

Fauna moves through this density in abundance. Large, slow-bodied amphibians drift between the pools, their skin slick with condensing mist. Smaller, quick forms skitter along the undersides of leaves and across the rock ledges, their movements blurred by speed and numbers. Larger shapes occasionally cross the open stretches of water, displacing heavy wakes that lap against the bases of the crystalline spars and set them ringing at a slightly higher pitch.

The entire basin exists in a state of relentless, low-level turbulence. Sheets of rain arrive and depart within minutes, altering water levels, knocking loose small rock fragments, and flattening then lifting sections of vegetation. The atmosphere itself appears to be condensing and releasing moisture in visible pulses, so that visibility shifts from a few dozen meters to nearly a kilometer and back again without pattern. Through it all, the scattered remnants of advanced engineering continue their quiet, unfathomable work, holding the valley’s chaotic processes within boundaries that never quite allow total destruction or total order. The landscape feels both newborn and already exhausted, perpetually remaking itself under the weight of water, cold, and continuous sound.
