The plain rises in long, imperceptible steps, each level a single unbroken sheet of mineral that has forgotten it was ever anything higher. At this height the air is warm and perfectly motionless; nothing moves it, and nothing cools it. The surface itself is not rock in the usual sense but a pale, dense amalgam of salts and metallic oxides, polished by eons into a single continuous floor that extends until curvature hides it. No dust lifts; nothing erodes further. The plateau has already reached the only form it will ever hold again.

Across that floor, at distances of many minutes’ walk, stand the works—towers, low sheds, and open frameworks of the same mineral, grown or poured in the same endless moment the plain achieved its final smoothness. Their surfaces show no seams and no wear; the angles are exact because they have always been exact. Thin filaments of darker alloy run between them like frozen lightning, carrying a current that never fluctuates. Inside the sheds, colossal pistons and flywheels turn at a speed so constant they produce only a single, sub-audible pressure in the air, felt rather than heard. Nothing ever stops; nothing ever speeds up.

Light is almost absent. What little illumination exists leaks from narrow seams where two mineral faces meet; it is the color of cooling iron and gives no warmth. Between the structures the darkness is total, a dry absence that makes distances impossible to judge. A single moving point of that same iron-colored glow sometimes crosses the interval between two frameworks—an autonomous carrier, or perhaps only the reflection of a carrier—and then vanishes again into the uniform quiet.

Nothing grows and nothing drinks. Occasional low crystalline lattices, no taller than a hand, stand alone at random intervals; they are not alive, only the latest expression of the same mineral equilibrium that built the plain and the engines. Far rarer are the moving forms: slow, jointed shapes of the same stuff, each the size of a vehicle, that travel in perfectly straight lines for hours before stopping for hours. They leave no marks. When they pause they simply become part of the stillness, indistinguishable from the fixed towers until they resume their measured progress.

Sound, when it occurs, is brief and local: a single metallic click as two carrier facets lock, or the faint, unchanging drone of an engine that has been droning since before any record. These noises do not travel; they are absorbed at once by the warm, dense air and the age-polished floor. After each one the plateau returns to its permanent near-silence.

Nothing arrives and nothing departs. The entire arrangement—plain, towers, filaments, moving lattices, and the rare slow forms—has occupied this exact configuration for so long that change itself has become the impossible quantity. The warm dark holds everything where it is, and the mineral remembers nothing else.
