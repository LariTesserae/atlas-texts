Within a seam of fractured quartz no wider than a closed palm, a vertical city has taken shape. Its towers rise and needle together like splinters forced upward through the same fracture plane, their flanks faceted and unweathered, edges still bright from the moment they cooled. There is no moisture anywhere; the air itself is a sparse drift of metallic sublimate that settles in fine gray films on every horizontal ledge and is immediately scoured away again by the next slow convection current.

The structures are packed so tightly that from most angles only vertical slices remain visible: a knife-edge balcony, a single rail of crystalline track, the dark slot of a shaft dropping straight into shadow. Between them move the inhabitants—small, many-legged forms whose bodies are accretions of the same mineral dust that coats the walls. They flow in continuous threads along the narrowest ledges, sometimes crossing from one tower to the next by extruding temporary bridges of extruded silicate that later retract without trace. Their motion is nearly silent; the only audible evidence is an intermittent soft ticking when a joint of one creature’s exoskeleton shears against another.

Light seeps downward from thread-thin veins of photoluminescent crystal embedded in the ceiling rock, dim enough that shadows hold for minutes before shifting. Temperature stays just above the point where the mineral vapor would condense; the chill keeps every surface dry and slightly tacky to any hypothetical touch. Engines buried inside the towers turn without lubricant or liquid coolant, their moving parts cushioned instead by interlocking tongues of piezoelectric quartz that sing at frequencies too low to register as sound. The city’s continual small rearrangements—sections telescoping upward, entire narrow streets tilting a few degrees and locking into new alignments—register only as a faint, directionless tremor felt through the feet or the shell.

Because everything is still forming, nothing has been smoothed. Stairs end in abrupt overhangs; railings terminate in glassy spikes; loading platforms overhang voids whose depth is measured in finger-widths rather than stories. The fauna exploit these unfinished edges without hesitation, prying loose fresh mineral grains and carrying them into the humming interiors where they are re-fired into new structural members. Occasionally a tower segment will shear free, drop a hand-span, and re-anchor itself at a slightly different angle; the creatures already on it adjust their gait and continue. No water, no vegetation, only this sealed, sharpening, densely occupied verticality that keeps reconfiguring itself inside its own fracture.
