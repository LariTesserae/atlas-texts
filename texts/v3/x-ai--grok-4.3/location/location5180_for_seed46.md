In a sealed fold of rising stone, high within what has not yet been named a mountain, the rock has begun to remember itself as something other than rock. The cavity is no larger than a single held breath held too long—an intimate hollow pressed between two embryonic slabs that are still deciding whether they will become cliffs or plates or something without name. Light arrives as it does anywhere else, straight and uncolored, yet the heat within the seam is thick enough to bend the air into slow, glassy ripples.

Across the raw faces, mineral lattices unfold at the pace of cooling magma. They branch and rebranch in arid exuberance, each facet catching and returning light in dense, salt-bright clusters that read, from a distance, as foliage. No sap moves inside them; they grow by precipitation and ordered fracture, drawing structure from the very dryness that should forbid life. Between these crystal thickets lie expanses of open, heated dust—yards of bare surface where nothing has yet arrived to occupy the silence. The emptiness is deliberate, part of the same slow logic that lets the lattices expand without collision.

Farther in, the walls themselves are performing industry. Veins of denser material have begun to align into repeating geometries: cylinders that extrude and retract by fractions of a millimeter over hours, slotted plates that mesh and release fine plumes of superheated vapor. Nothing was assembled; the mechanisms are the mountain’s first attempt at articulation, still half molten at their cores, still learning the difference between vein and valve. They produce no products, only the measured exhalation of their own becoming. The sound of this labor is almost absent—only an occasional, low crystalline chime when two expanding planes meet and decide, for now, to remain distinct.

At irregular intervals the air frays. A patch of lattice will momentarily reveal the raw coordinate grid of its own formation, then resolve again into mineral leaf-forms. A vent will stutter, cycling the same thermal breath forward and backward in a loop that lasts minutes before the normal direction of time reseals itself. These interruptions do not announce themselves; they pass through the space like weather too faint to disturb the sparse arrangement of crystals or the patient drift of rising heat. No creature moves. The only motion is the incremental lengthening of a single lattice spine, the fractional opening of a forming aperture, and the long, patient settling of new layers over what has not yet become a floor.
