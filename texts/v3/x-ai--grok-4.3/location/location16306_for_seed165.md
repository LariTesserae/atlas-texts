The highland stretches without boundary across a wind-scoured plateau locked at impossible altitude, where every ridge and basin has already surrendered its edges to time. Rock faces stand polished to a dull metallic sheen by ages of frost-wedging and settling dust; they lean neither inward nor outward but simply endure, their planes fixed like pages pressed flat beneath an unseen weight. Occasional seams of darker stone bear the faint, almost accidental geometry of long-abandoned cuts—half-buried lines that might once have been thresholds or maps but now read only as paler scars within the same unchanging gray.

Moisture arrives in thin, reluctant pulses. Condensation beads along the underside of overhangs, gathers into shivering droplets, then releases in brief, cold threads that trace down vertical walls before vanishing into hairline fissures. These threads leave behind short-lived silver stains that reappear in exactly the same places each cycle, as though the rock itself remembers where water is permitted to pause. Between these moments the air holds only a dry, biting chill that keeps the ground from ever softening.

Across the emptiness, vegetation clings in isolated colonies: low, armored mats of black-green lichen and needle-leaved cushions no taller than a finger. They occupy the lee sides of boulders and the narrow shelves where frost accumulates most thickly, never spreading farther. Between them the stone continues unbroken for distances that blur the eye.

Sound does not vary. A single, layered roar fills the plateau—wind channeled through fixed corridors of stone, augmented by the perpetual low grinding of ice against ice within deeper crevices. The noise neither rises nor falls; it simply is, pressing against eardrums with the same pressure at every moment, so constant that it begins to function as silence’s opposite rather than its interruption.

Here and there the continuum fractures in fixed ways. A ridge may end in a clean vertical plane beyond which the air itself appears faintly doubled, showing a second, motionless image of the same ridge slightly offset, like a reflection trapped in still water. Certain shadows do not move with the light but remain cast at exact, impossible angles, their edges sharp as knife cuts. These anomalies stay perfectly stationary; only the traveler’s changing viewpoint reveals their fixed misalignment with ordinary space. No new fractures open and none close. They are as settled into the landscape as the polished rock faces themselves.

Nothing larger than a hand moves across the distances. Traces—shallow, elongated depressions in the dust—appear at immense intervals, always aligned with the same ancient routes, always empty when examined. The light remains low and directionless, a perpetual half-glow that makes every surface seem simultaneously near and impossibly remote. The whole place holds its form without progression, its dampness arriving and departing on schedule, its fractures remaining exactly where they have always been.
