A high, half-formed continent floats beneath its own thin weather. Rock here is not yet finished: black basalt webs still extrude from the mantle in slow, glassy filaments that have not decided their final angles. Between these fresh seams, water weeps in constant silver threads that gather, fall, and re-condense against the chill. The air is sharp and heavy with suspended droplets; every surface is slick, every breath visible for an instant before it is reabsorbed into the same quiet cycle.

Across this newborn topography a city has already taken root, its towers spaced kilometers apart so that the raw geology remains the dominant presence. The buildings do not rest on foundations so much as continue them: their lower courses are the same dark, still-cooling stone, their upper reaches grown from lattices that rearrange themselves overnight according to stresses the rock has not yet expressed. Bridges and terraces hang between structures on filaments finer than spider silk, their surfaces faintly luminous only because the condensed moisture clings there and refracts the ordinary daylight. Nothing is crowded; emptiness itself is cultivated so the continent can continue to shift without resistance.

Low, silver-green tussocks and mats of cold-adapted lichen find footing in the narrow seams where water lingers longest. They are few, yet they are enough to draw fleets of silent, furred gliders that launch from the tower ledges and ride the condensing updrafts for days without calling. Larger, slower creatures move along the weeping fault lines, their broad feet leaving temporary prints that fill again within minutes. The city’s machines, embedded in the same stone, register every new fracture and every new pool, then quietly adjust the lattice tensions above so that nothing topples while the world beneath is still deciding its shape.

Wind rarely rises above a constant, low-pressure sigh. The only distinct sounds are the soft, regular impacts of droplets returning to water already pooled on horizontal ledges, and the faint crystalline report, felt more than heard, of fresh mineral filaments locking into place. Light arrives evenly from a sky that is never quite clear and never quite overcast; it simply illuminates the ongoing work below without emphasis. The entire arrangement remains spacious, cold, and unfinished, yet already inhabited at every scale that still has room to grow.
