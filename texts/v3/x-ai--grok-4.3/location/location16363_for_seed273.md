The heights here are not mountains in the ancient sense, nor are they the clean spines of skyscrapers left to rust. Instead, everything that rises has been poured, bolted, and joined into one continuous armature of terraces, struts, and load-bearing skins that step across dozens of kilometers without ever touching unaltered ground. The material is mostly a composite of poured stone and riveted iron, now softened at every edge by two centuries of slow settling; joints have opened just enough for roots and condensation to thread through, yet the overall frame remains rigid, its angles still true under the weight of its own accumulated additions.

Lush growth covers the surfaces in dense, overlapping layers. Vines with broad, waxy leaves drape from catwalks and railings, their foliage catching the constant bright light that arrives already diffused through high, thin mist. Smaller ferns and mosses pack the seams between deck plates, turning vertical walls into living tapestries that shift only slightly from season to season. The warmth keeps the metabolism of these plants high, so the air itself feels slightly sweetened and thickened by transpiration, even while the same warmth draws frequent condensation onto every shaded flange and conduit. Droplets gather, swell, and release in quiet, irregular rhythms—never quite rain, never quite dry—feeding shallow trays and channeled gutters that run the length of each terrace like slow veins.

Life moves through this lattice in abundance. Insects with metallic wing-sheens patrol the seams; small climbing mammals and multicolored birds nest inside unused ventilation housings and between cable bundles. Their calls and the steady percussion of wings mix with the low, constant thrum of distant machinery—pistons and flywheels turning at deliberate, unhurried speeds inside reinforced vaults. The overall soundscape remains loud, yet it registers as a single sustained chord rather than discrete events: the hum of generators, the sigh of expanding metal, the rustle of leaves against vents, the occasional shriek of a raptor riding an updraft between towers.

Because the entire substrate was fabricated rather than excavated, erosion here is not carving but polishing: wind and water have rounded every accessible corner over long spans of time, producing a surface texture like weathered bone. Structural members still stand exactly where they were placed, but their profiles have softened, their shadows grown slightly more generous. The scale of the place keeps revealing itself in stages; from any single platform one can see other platforms continuing upward and outward, connected by suspended walkways and broad freight ramps that eventually disappear into haze. Nothing closes in; everything simply continues at the same measured density, layer upon layer.

The motion of the world is patient. Maintenance crews still pass along fixed routes, but their interventions are small and infrequent, absorbed into the larger rhythm of plant growth and metal fatigue. New leaves unfurl across weeks rather than days; droplets migrate along gutters over hours; the great flywheels inside the lower vaults complete a single revolution while the light shifts from one side of the sky to the other. The result is an environment that feels both fully artificial and fully alive, held in a warm, bright, densely inhabited equilibrium high above any natural reference plane.
