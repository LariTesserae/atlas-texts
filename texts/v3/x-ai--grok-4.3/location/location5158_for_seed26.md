The Substratum Expanse unfolds not as a single vault but as a lattice of interlocking caverns whose ceilings recede into distances that swallow lantern-glow. Everything beneath the soles is alloy: ribbed plates stamped with serial grids, raised walkways bolted over drainage trenches, and colossal shear-walls whose rivets still gleam with factory oil. No soil, no stone older than the decade-old pour; the world is poured, extruded, welded into being only yesterday in geological time.

Water moves constantly through this lattice. It weeps from seams in the overhead ducts, beads along cooling coils, and drips onto the metal in a slow, arrhythmic percussion that never quite syncs with the heavier industrial rhythm. Condensation hangs in the air like a low fog that the ventilation fans have not yet finished herding; where the fog thickens, small rimes of frost lace the edges of stair treads and pipe brackets. The temperature stays just above freezing, cold enough that breath plumes and any exposed metal burns skin on contact.

Machinery fills the open intervals but never crowds them. Between each stamping press or turbine housing lies a span of empty grating wide enough for a rail-cart to pass; the emptiness itself feels deliberate, as though the builders expected future expansion that has not yet arrived. From those gaps rise thin, pale biofilms—filaments no thicker than thread that gleam faintly under the sodium lamps—clinging to condensate-slick surfaces the way moss might on stone. They are the only vegetation, and even they thin out beneath the brightest work-lights.

Light itself is scarce and begrudging. Overhead sodium strips flicker at half-power, their glow filtered through layers of mist and the oily film on every lens. Emergency beacons pulse red at irregular intervals, throwing momentary shadows that stretch the length of a loading platform before vanishing. In the deeper recesses, clusters of cold-lamps—glass orbs wired to chemical batteries—emit a blue-white gleam that makes the frost look almost lunar.

Sound presses against the ears from every direction at once. The constant bass thrum of generators rolls through the metal decking; it is overlapped by the higher, irregular clatter of sorting mechanisms, the hiss of pressure-release valves dumping vapor, and the liquid percussion of water striking tin. Occasionally a distant alarm klaxon answers another, their two tones forming a dissonant interval that lingers until the ventilation fans cycle again and sweep the sound away. Fauna is limited to maintenance automata shaped like elongated beetles; they scuttle along the undersides of catwalks, their single head-lamps sweeping the undersides for corrosion. They move in slow, purposeful arcs and vanish into access hatches before any observer can count them.

New edges catch the eye everywhere: the sharp bevels of freshly milled girders, the raised burrs left by plasma torches, the crystalline bloom of mineral scale where seepage has already begun to etch the steel. These young wounds have not yet softened; they glint under the dim lamps like teeth still cutting through gum. Pumps labor to keep the lowest galleries from flooding; their suction rhythm adds a wet, breathing cadence beneath the mechanical roar.

The Expanse continues past the reach of any single viewpoint. Walk far enough along any main concourse and the ceiling lifts another story, revealing fresh tiers of pipework, new catwalks still taped with builder’s markings, and the faint blue haze of more condensing moisture. The entire construct feels half-finished and already running at full capacity—an active, contradictory state that the cold, wet metal seems to register as a low, perpetual ache.
