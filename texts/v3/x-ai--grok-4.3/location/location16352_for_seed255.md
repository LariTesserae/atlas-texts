The basin stretches beyond sight in every direction, a single unbroken floor of jointed mineral plates that rise and interlock like the scales of some unfinished hide. Nothing grows here; the only surfaces are polished stone, fused crystal seams, and the matte residue where new courses have been set down and not yet worn. Condensation gathers constantly on the low vaults overhead, releasing in slow, deliberate drips that strike the plates with a ringing report, each impact followed by a soft sliding note as the water finds the shallow grooves already carved toward the nearest reservoir. The air stays cool and unmoving between rains, carrying only the mineral chill of deep excavation.

Structures stand everywhere at arm’s length—low, broad towers, half-finished ramps, and freestanding walls whose edges have not yet received their final beveling. Some lean at deliberate angles; others stop abruptly mid-course, their exposed laminations showing the hand-marks of tools no more advanced than chisels and levers. Between them the space is tight, almost stifling, yet the ceiling continues outward for miles, turning the basin into one continuous, roofed plain still claiming new territory. Occasional vents in the upper stone release thin curtains of mist that drift downward, beading on every surface and feeding the shallow, lens-shaped pools that collect against the bases of unfinished walls.

Sound never stops. The dripping never ceases, but beneath it runs a deeper percussion: the slow grind of plates settling as unseen counterweights shift, the occasional dry click of a joint locking into place, the low groan of a ramp being levered another finger-width higher. These noises overlap and layer until the air itself feels crowded. Now and then a single, sharper report echoes from far off—a wall course dropped into its bed or a reservoir gate reseating itself—followed by a long, ringing decay that travels between the close-packed forms.

Nothing moves that can be seen, yet the basin is not still. Water migrates along the grooves, plates adjust their seating by fractions, and somewhere a new course of stone is being lowered into darkness with a measured, deliberate cadence. The few living things that pass are small, pale, and brief: translucent mineral-skinned forms no larger than a hand, sliding between joints or vanishing into condensation vents. They leave no trace but a momentary ripple in the pools and a faint, glassy click before the larger sounds close over them again.
