High in the thinned air, where an ancient range has slumped over eons into a single broad dome of stone, a fracture no wider than a person’s shoulders opens onto a hidden pocket. The rock around the entrance is polished to a soft matte by inconceivable time; every edge has been rounded until the stone feels more like cooled wax than mountain. Inside, the pocket widens just enough to hold a shallow basin perhaps twenty paces across. Light arrives normally—diffuse daylight filtered through a single, irregular skylight—but it arrives after first passing through a slow lens of condensation that coats the upper rock, so the illumination feels both ordinary and strangely softened, like late-afternoon sun seen through breath on glass.

The floor is not level. It is a series of low, water-worn terraces that step downward toward a central dimple no deeper than a cupped hand. Moisture arrives in cycles rather than steady rain: every few hours a fine mist gathers on the concave ceiling, beads, and falls in soft, audible taps. Between falls the surfaces dry just enough that pale mineral rings mark the high-water lines. The air stays warm, close, and faintly sweet.

Vegetation rises in isolated, vivid clumps rather than continuous cover. Thick-leaved rosettes the color of new copper push from cracks; their undersides host constellations of tiny luminous nodules that brighten and fade with each drying cycle. Between the clumps the stone remains bare, so the eye travels across open rock that seems almost empty despite the lushness of what does grow. The same stone carries faint, perfectly circular depressions—remnants of small, once-embedded devices whose casings have long since merged with the rock. A single curved spar still protrudes near the far wall, its surface shifting between matte and mirror depending on the angle of view; inside it, something continues to hum at a pitch below hearing, turning the mist that touches it into brief, glittering filaments before releasing them again.

Fauna moves constantly. Iridescent, many-legged creatures no larger than thumbnails pour in living ropes along the terraces, clicking and chittering; their numbers make the sound continuous and loud even though each individual is quiet. Larger, translucent flyers the size of open hands drift between the clumps, their wing membranes tuned so that every beat produces a resonant chime. When the mist falls they gather beneath the droplets, drinking and adding layered, glassy tones to the overall roar. The air feels crowded with motion and with the small impacts of bodies landing, taking off, brushing stone.

Nothing here is hurried, yet nothing is still. Condensation cycles, mineral rings advance and retreat, the creatures follow their own overlapping rhythms, and the half-buried devices keep their subsonic conversation with the moisture and the light. The pocket remains exactly as large as it needs to be, warm, damp at measured intervals, loud with life that has nowhere else to go, and ancient enough that even its strangeness feels settled into final form.
