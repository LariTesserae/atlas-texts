The plateau stretches without boundary or interruption, a single unbroken shelf of pale stone lifted so high that the air itself feels thinned to a permanent chill. Sunlight arrives without angle or mercy, flooding every surface in an even, shadowless glare that never softens. The rock underfoot has been scoured for uncounted ages into low, rounded domes and long, flute-like ridges whose edges have worn until each fits the next with the precision of a joint left undisturbed for centuries. Between these forms lie distances measured in hours of walking, empty basins of the same pale stone that hold nothing but their own faint sheen of condensed moisture.

Small, shallow pans collect that moisture where the rock dips, each pan no wider than a stride yet persistent enough that a pale film of water remains. From these pockets rise thin, wiry stems no thicker than a quill, spaced so far apart that each plant appears an island to the next. The wind that crosses the plateau never varies in strength or direction; it moves constantly through the long channels between the domes, striking the fluted ridges and producing a single, continuous pressure of sound. The tone does not rise or fall. It simply is, filling the open space until the ears lose the ability to measure distance by it.

Small, quick animals occupy the same stone without altering it. Pale, long-limbed lizards flatten themselves into the shallow grooves, their bodies absorbing the constant glare and the steady vibration. Swarms of minute, translucent insects rise and fall through the wind like motes that have learned to ride rather than resist it; their collective wings add only another layer to the existing roar. Nothing builds here. Nothing is carried or placed. The stone, the light, the wind, and the creatures continue exactly as they have, each element locked into the others so that any single change would require every other to change with it—an equilibrium that shows no sign of beginning or ending.

At intervals the rock opens into deeper basins where the same condensation gathers into standing pools no larger than a body could cross. Around these pools the sparse stems thicken slightly, their roots sunk into hairline fractures that the wind has never succeeded in widening further. The animals drink, then return to the open stone. The sound does not pause. The light remains level. The plateau continues, unaltered, across distances that exceed any single view.
