The chamber is a single sealed pocket no larger than a modest hall, its walls of fresh basalt still showing the razor edges and hackly fracture patterns of its recent separation from the surrounding rock. Thin, blade-like ridges of mineral run across every surface—sharp enough to slice skin yet already dulled in places by the slow passage of countless small bodies. The air is warm, motionless, and perfectly dry; no vapor clings to the stone. Instead, the walls and floor are coated in a continuous but sparse carpet of translucent, plate-like growths, each plate no thicker than a leaf and veined with threads of pale sulfur. These growths neither move nor grow; they simply occupy their allotted space, locked in an unchanging mosaic that leaves wide stretches of bare rock between them.

Here and there, low cylinders of stacked stone—barely knee-high and crudely fitted by hand—stand half-buried in the crystal plates. Their tops are worn smooth by repeated contact, yet no dust settles on them; the equilibrium of the pocket preserves every surface exactly as it was left. Scattered among the cylinders lie smaller artifacts: a single obsidian flake, a length of braided fiber now rigid as wire, a shallow bowl whose interior holds only a faint metallic sheen. They are few, and the empty floor between them feels deliberate.

Light is a faint, sourceless glow that seems to rise from the plates themselves, enough to reveal forms but not to cast shadows. The same glow outlines the countless small fauna—segmented, many-legged creatures no larger than a thumb—that move constantly across the crystal surfaces. They do not feed or fight; they simply traverse fixed routes, their legs striking the plates in an unbroken, metallic rhythm that fills the entire pocket with a loud, continuous chiming. The sound never rises or falls, never pauses for breath or night; it is the audible signature of a system that has reached perfect stasis. Every impact, every echo, every faint rattle of a dislodged plate is instantly replaced by the next identical note, so the chamber is never silent yet never changes its song.

Because nothing is consumed and nothing is added, the fauna and the crystal plates maintain an exact spacing and number. When a creature dies, its husk remains where it fell until another creature carries it precisely to a designated ridge and leaves it there, where it slowly mineralizes into another plate of the same translucent material. The process is invisible in any single moment; only the result—an eternal, crowded yet sparse geometry—can be seen. No entrance or exit exists. The pocket is its own sealed world, warm, loud, and perfectly still in time.
