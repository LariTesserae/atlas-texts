The terrain spreads in a low, open basin of stratified mineral flats, each layer smoothed by long cycles of settling rather than violent carving. Broad shelves of pale, silica-rich stone descend in gentle steps toward shallow depressions where condensation gathers in slow, repeating films, leaving the rock faces perpetually filmed with a cool, metallic dampness. No green or soft growth interrupts the surfaces; instead, fine vein-like ridges of quartz and feldspar mark the only visible patterns, their edges rounded and balanced against one another like a finished puzzle left in place for ages.

Darkness holds the entire plain. Only the faintest gradients of shadow differentiate one horizon from another, enough to reveal that the flats continue without interruption, widening into further basins and low ridges that lose themselves beyond reach. The air itself carries weight—dense enough that moisture beads on any exposed contour and the cool temperature clings rather than dissipates. Small pockets of standing water collect in the eroded troughs, each one reflecting nothing, each one briefly disturbed when the ground vibrates.

Movement fills every interval. Countless small, mica-plated forms swarm in overlapping sheets just above the stone: translucent, wafer-thin bodies that flex and glide without wings, brushing one another in constant, layered passage. Their passing generates the sound—an unrelenting, high-frequency abrasion like countless sheets of glass dragged across one another, punctuated by deeper, resonant clicks when larger clusters collide or settle momentarily onto the rock. The noise does not rise and fall; it is continuous, pressing against the ears from every direction so that distance itself becomes difficult to judge. Occasionally a thicker pulse travels through the stone itself, a low vibration felt through the feet rather than heard, produced when a denser mass of the same creatures tunnels briefly beneath the surface before re-emerging.

Nothing here is still. Condensation forms and drips in slow, regular intervals; the mineral ridges shift by imperceptible fractions as micro-fractures adjust to the cool air; the swarms reorganize according to invisible currents of moisture and vibration. The creatures themselves seem to feed on the trace films of water and the exposed mineral surfaces, their bodies leaving behind faint etchings that are already being re-smoothed by the next migration. In this darkness and density the basin feels simultaneously empty of familiar life and impossibly crowded, a single continuous process of abrasion, condensation, and resonant motion without beginning or visible end.
