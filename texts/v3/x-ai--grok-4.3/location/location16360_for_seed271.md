In a shallow basin no wider than a cathedral nave, the land lies cupped within low earthen rims that have long since lost their sharpness. The ground is a patchwork of compacted silt and pale stone, its surface smoothed by uncounted seasons of intermittent moisture that arrive as brief rains or slow-beaded condensation rather than flowing streams. Between the stones, shallow pans no deeper than a hand’s breadth hold clear water for a day or two after each wetting; then the warmth draws most of it away again, leaving behind only faint mineral rings like ghosts of earlier visits.

Scattered across this floor are small, low hummocks of tough, gray-green vegetation—more leather than leaf—rooted in the thinnest seams of soil. They stand well apart from one another, leaving broad stretches of bare earth between them. Nothing taller than a knee rises anywhere; the basin holds no thickets, no canopy, only these isolated cushions that seem to have grown to exactly the size the available dampness will support and no more.

The air remains warm and still. Light arrives from above as a muted wash, filtered by a high, unbroken layer of thin cloud that neither thickens nor clears. Shadows are present yet never sharp; they rest on the ground like a second, paler skin. Because nothing moves—no insect flight, no leaf tremor, no distant call—the silence feels less like absence and more like a held note. The only sounds, when they occur, are the soft, widely spaced impacts of a condensing droplet striking a stone or the almost inaudible sigh of warm air shifting against the basin rim.

Across the open ground lie faint, linear marks half-sunk in the silt: low ridges no higher than a finger’s thickness, their edges rounded by long weathering. They suggest the memory of earlier boundaries or alignments, though whether they were raised by wind, water, or some older ordering of the land itself is no longer possible to tell. They do not intrude; they belong to the same equilibrium as the stones, the sparse plants, and the shallow, temporary pans of water. Nothing here arrives or departs. The basin simply continues, warm, dim, and balanced between its modest dampness and the dryness that surrounds it.
