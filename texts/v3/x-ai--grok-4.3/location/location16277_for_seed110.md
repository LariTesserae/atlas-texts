Here the mountain still remembers its own birth. High on an interior flank, where wind and weather have not yet found purchase, a seam no wider than a forearm opens into a hidden cavity no larger than a modest chamber. The rock around it is not old; it is still arriving. Pale, unoxidized stone weeps clear droplets from pores that have barely finished forming. Each bead swells in silence, detaches, and falls the short distance to the uneven floor, where it joins a shallow film that never deepens and never dries.

The air remains warm, almost body-heat, and perfectly still. Moisture hangs suspended in countless invisible layers, so that moving even a hand through the space feels like brushing through unseen curtains. Nothing grows thickly; only thread-fine filaments of a pale, lightless mold trace the lowest seams of the rock, spaced far apart, each filament drinking from the constant condensation and giving nothing back. The same sparse film clings to the single artifact left inside: a palm-sized shard of harder stone, its edges worn by patient rubbing rather than by time or water. The marks on it are shallow, repeated, patient, as though someone long ago sat here and smoothed the rock simply to feel it yield.

Nothing moves except the droplets. No current stirs the film on the floor; no draft lifts the mold threads. The cavity holds its contents in a fixed suspension—temperature, saturation, mineral growth, even the slow lengthening of a single crystal spear descending from the ceiling—all balanced so that change has no room to begin. Sound is almost absent. Only the soft, spaced impacts of water on water reach the ear, each one identical to the last, spaced at intervals that never shorten or lengthen.

In this unchanging pocket, a handful of minute, eyeless creatures rest motionless along the lower walls, their bodies flattened into the rock pores like pressed leaves. They do not feed, do not breed, do not stir. They are simply present, as dense in their stillness as the suspended moisture is in the air. The rock continues its quiet emergence around them, adding microscopic layers of new mineral that will never erode, never be seen by daylight, and never disturb the equilibrium already achieved.
