The place is a sealed industrial lattice suspended high in warm, pressurized water, every surface manufactured and interlocked without a shred of untouched substrate. Massive I-beams, pressure-sealed arches, and riveted decks form a compact nest of chambers no larger than a cathedral nave and its surrounding transepts. The steel and alloy skins have never weathered; their edges remain knife-sharp, surfaces still bearing the faint mill scale of recent fabrication. Between and across these rigid geometries grow thick mats of slick, rubbery vegetation—broad leaves veined with metallic threads, stalks anchored directly into bolt holes and pipe seams, root-like fibers threading through conduit runs. The growth is so dense that moving from one chamber to the next requires pushing through layer upon layer of fronds and floating spore-clouds that drift without current.

The water itself is body-warm and still, carrying only the faintest, almost imperceptible drift that turns a single leaf or loosened petal through a slow arc over several minutes. Light arrives evenly from recessed phosphor strips along the ceiling ribs, neither dim nor harsh, illuminating the packed interior without shadow or glare. No animals move through the foliage or along the struts; the only visible life is the vegetation, which releases tiny, glittering seeds that hang in place before settling onto new metal at a glacial pace. Machines stand integrated into the same structure—rows of pressure turbines and filtration drums whose blades turn once every few hours, their motion marked only by the soft flexing of adjacent leaves. Pipes glow faintly where slow chemical reactions cycle within them. The overall effect is of a single organism whose organs are both plant and engine, grown into each other while everything remains utterly quiet.

Occasionally a pressure valve opens with a muted sigh that fades before it can become sound, or a rack of leaves unfolds by a few centimeters across an hour. The entire arrangement feels held in perfect, patient suspension: fully occupied, recently completed, and already inseparable from its own vegetation. Boundaries are crisp—walls, floors, and ceilings close the space on all sides, and beyond them the water simply stops at sealed viewports, showing nothing but uniform warm haze. Nothing rusts, nothing decays, nothing arrives or departs. The place continues its single, shared process without hurry or noise.
