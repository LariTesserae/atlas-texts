The place is a sealed vertical fissure no wider than a crouched body, driven like an irregular blade-cut straight down the flank of a cloud-scraping ridge. Its walls are pale, salt-burnished stone that has long since stopped eroding; every surface has settled into the same faint, satin texture, as though the rock itself decided centuries ago that further change would be redundant. Light arrives only as a single persistent filament from a hairline crack at the top—enough to silver the edges of things without ever warming them.

Clinging to both walls and to one another is a dense continuum of mineral growths that require no liquid. They are cool to the touch, faintly opalescent, and branch in tight, coral-like fans and curtains that interlock until almost no air gap remains. Some formations mimic fronds; others form rigid lattices or bulbous clusters that press against the fissure like packed lungs. Their chemistry is silent but perpetual: they draw trace atmospheric reagents and exhale nothing detectable, maintaining the same volume and hue from one millennium to the next.

Embedded among the growths are the remains of an industrial micro-system that once fitted the fissure exactly. Small turbine housings, now half-swallowed by the mineral matrix, still turn—imperceptibly, at the rate of one revolution per day—driven by temperature micro-gradients that never quite equalize. Their bearings emit a single, unwavering low-frequency drone that fills the entire crevice and never modulates. The sound is not loud in volume but saturates the space so completely that it feels like a pressure against skin and stone alike. No other noises exist; the equilibrium is absolute.

Tiny access plates and bracket stubs protrude here and there, their metal surfaces etched to the same satin finish as the rock. Nothing moves except the turbines’ glacial rotation and the imperceptible thickening of the mineral fans. The fissure holds its contents—rock, growth, machinery—in a single, unchanging occupancy; each element has grown around the others until separation is impossible. In this pocket the world is finished, fully occupied, and without gradient or exit.
