The landscape unfolds across a high, fractured plateau suspended in permanent half-light, its knife-edged ridges and broad sunken basins carved by ages of wind and frost into rounded, almost deliberate forms. Rock here is not merely stone but a dense, matte-black substance veined with faint crystalline seams that catch what little light remains and turn it into slow, liquid glints. Between the ridges lie immense empty distances—kilometers of open air and bare, wind-smoothed pavement—interrupted only by occasional solitary pillars or hanging mats of vegetation.

Those mats are the source of the lushness: thick, rubbery leaves and braided root systems that dangle from the cliffs like living cables, each leaf surface beaded constantly with condensation that rolls downward in patient rivulets. The water never gathers into rivers; instead it collects in shallow, glass-smooth pools cradled in the rock, each pool ringed by a different species of pale, cold-adapted growth that drinks the mist and releases thin curls of vapor in return. The air stays cold enough that the droplets sometimes freeze into needle-fine frost before melting again at dusk.

Across these distances move the fauna—small, many-legged creatures that travel in loose, drifting schools, their soft footfalls barely audible. Larger forms appear only as slow silhouettes against the far ridges: long-necked browsers whose breath condenses into faint silver plumes, or low, armored shapes that scrape mineral films from the rock with deliberate, mechanical patience. None of them call. Sound travels only as the occasional soft click of shifting crystal, the drip of water into still pools, or the low internal thrum of certain thick-stemmed plants whose hollow chambers expand and contract like bellows, drawing in mist and releasing a barely felt current of warmer air.

The plateau itself feels machined by time rather than by hands. Long avenues of stone have been polished into gentle channels where wind funnels moisture from one basin to the next; elsewhere, veins of the crystalline rock widen and narrow in repeating patterns that suggest valves or conduits. No artificial structures interrupt the scene, yet the living systems within it move with the steady, repetitive rhythm of a vast and mostly silent factory: leaves unfurl in measured pulses, mineral nodules grow and are harvested by the browsing fauna, frost films form and sublimate again. The emptiness between these processes only makes their quiet industry more noticeable, as though the entire region were a single slow engine turning beneath layers of darkness and cold.
