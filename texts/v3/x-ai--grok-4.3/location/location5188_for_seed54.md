The landscape is a high, open plateau of raw black stone and slowly assembling crystal, lifted above an endless dark. Moisture from the thin, cold air condenses directly onto every surface, beading into lenses and threads that never quite freeze or evaporate; instead they seep along microscopic channels already etched into the mineral fabric. These threads gather in low places, forming shallow, ink-dark pools whose surfaces tremble with the subsonic pressure of whatever moves beneath them.

Nothing grows here in the usual sense. The ground is not soil but an interlocking sheet of volcanic glass and engineered lattice that continues to knit itself together at a rate visible only across centuries. Where the lattices are furthest along they rise into tiered, hive-like masses whose edges are still half-sunk in the plateau, their unfinished facets glinting like wet obsidian. Between and within these masses run narrow galleries and suspended walkways of the same material, dense enough that from any single viewpoint the structures appear to overlap in depth, a single volume crowded with planes and struts.

Through every crevice and along every ledge moves an overwhelming population of small, hard-shelled entities whose bodies are mostly mineral themselves. They do not feed or reproduce in recognizable ways; they simply migrate in slow, continuous rivers, their jointed limbs tapping and scraping as they test and reconfigure the lattices. The sound of this movement is constant and loud, a layered metallic hiss and chime that travels through the stone and pools in the low places like water. Individual motions are almost imperceptible, yet the collective pressure they exert on the structures produces a low, resonant drone that never ceases.

Condensation drips from the highest unfinished spires, each drop falling several long seconds before striking the surfaces below; when the drops land they do not splash but spread in deliberate, branching films that the mineral entities immediately incorporate. Over time these films carve new micro-channels whose direction changes according to the slow rearrangement of the entities themselves. Nothing here hurries. The city-scale lattice continues to accrete outward from raw ridges still half-buried in the plateau, while the entities’ migrations trace paths that will become infrastructure only after many human lifetimes have passed. The darkness remains absolute; the only illumination is the faint internal glow of stressed crystal where the entities have worked longest, a bioluminescence that registers more as temperature than light.
