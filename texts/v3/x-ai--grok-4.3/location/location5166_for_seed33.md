On a highland plain the size of a small continent, the ground rises in successive tiers so sheer that each new level seems to have been cut from the one below by an enormous, patient blade. The tiers continue upward until their rims dissolve into the dim, unchanging half-light overhead, each band hundreds of meters tall and striped with alternating bands of chalk-white salt rock and granular black glass. Between the tiers enormous empty distances stretch—kilometers of level mineral pavement marked only by occasional wind-sculpted ridges no higher than a person’s knee. Nothing grows; the surface is bare except for the faint crust of evaporated minerals that cracks underfoot with the sound of distant paper tearing.

Scattered across the plain are clusters of upright slabs and hollow cubes whose proportions mimic streets and buildings, yet they are continuous with the surrounding stone. Long ago erosion and slow settling carved rectangular voids and straight avenues through what was once a single immense deposit; the remaining forms are so regular that walking between them feels like entering a city whose inhabitants stepped away only moments before. The avenues are wide enough for many figures abreast yet remain almost empty. Only now and then a slender, translucent filament—mineral, not organic—rises from a crack, waves once in a slow, cold breeze, and retracts.

The air is cool and perfectly dry. It carries neither scent nor moisture; breathing it produces a faint metallic dryness at the back of the throat. Light arrives from no visible source and falls at angles that shift when one looks away; shadows sometimes lengthen toward their own objects rather than away from them. At irregular intervals a low geometric tremor passes through the ground. The slabs realign by a finger’s width, the avenues subtly narrow or widen, then everything settles again into its previous state of weathered equilibrium. The movement is silent except for a brief, high harmonic that fades before it can be located.

Farther out, toward the horizon that never approaches, the same pattern repeats on a larger scale: new clusters of geometric mesas appear, separated by still greater empty tracts. Occasionally a single dark shape—low, mobile, faceted—crosses one of these tracts at walking pace and vanishes into a crevice without leaving tracks. The crevice closes behind it with a soft click of stone on stone. Nothing else moves. The entire expanse remains suspended in a state of active, almost imperceptible change, its forms continually refined by forces that have already finished their work and are simply continuing it forever.
