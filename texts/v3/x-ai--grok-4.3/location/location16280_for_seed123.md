In a crease no wider than a curled body, the rock has not yet learned how to be old. Fresh fissures still carry the bright, splintered edges of their first cracking, and the low ceiling sweats beads of condensation that swell, drop, and are instantly replaced. The air stays warm, thick with the mineral smell of recently opened stone and the faint ozone bite of current moving through wet copper.

Everything inside is pressed together. Thin stalks of pale, nearly translucent vegetation—more mineral than leaf—grow directly from the joints of small iron housings. These housings are scattered rather than arranged, half-sunk into the floor like teeth that never finished erupting. Some still tick with slow, deliberate motion; tiny pistons no larger than finger joints slide in and out of sealed sleeves, each stroke pushing a drop of warm water along a capillary channel. The water travels only a few hand-lengths before it soaks into the living rock again.

Fauna fills the remaining space. Pale, many-legged things no bigger than coins move in constant, soundless negotiation along the surfaces of the machines and the rock between them. Their bodies are faintly luminous where they press against one another, exchanging brief sparks of static that vanish as soon as they separate. Larger, slower creatures—armored and low—occupy the shallow basins where condensation pools, their exoskeletons serving as additional condensation surfaces. Nothing hurries, yet nothing is still.

Light arrives normally from somewhere above and behind the viewer’s head, a steady, ordinary illumination that nevertheless makes every surface look slightly wrong, as though the eye keeps misjudging distances inside the tight volume. The only sounds are the soft, rhythmic kiss of water meeting metal and the almost inaudible rasp of tiny limbs against stone. The machines continue their minute labor; the creatures continue their minute traffic. Both seem to have grown here rather than been placed, and the warm, damp pocket continues to hold them all without offering even a finger-width of unused space.
