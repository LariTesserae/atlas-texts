The place rises first as a high, open basin ringed by low mineral ridges that have the look of cooled slag. From any vantage the eye travels far across flattened ground that once held more, now marked by widely spaced towers and broad platforms of fused stone and layered metal. Between these structures the distances feel deliberately empty, as though something intended vast unoccupied volumes rather than streets or plazas.

Water is everywhere yet never pooled for long. It gathers in thin films on every horizontal surface, beads on the vertical faces of the towers, and runs in slow, persistent threads down the edges of old walkways. The air itself is heavy with it; condensation forms continuously on cooler metal ribs and drips in a quiet, irregular rhythm onto the stone below. The heat keeps the moisture from settling. Steam rises in faint, transparent veils wherever a drip strikes warmer ground, and the whole basin carries a low, constant haziness that dims the light further. What light does arrive is filtered and indirect, arriving late in the day as a dull bronze wash that barely defines the tops of the tallest towers.

The constructions themselves are old enough to have reached a kind of equilibrium with wind and water. Their edges are rounded where they once were sharp; seams have softened into continuous curves; entire sections have slumped or spread into low fans of fused mineral. Yet the forms remain legible—broad ramps, tiered platforms, and hollow vertical shafts that still channel both air and water according to some original geometry. In places the metal has grown crystalline excrescences, pale and glassy, that catch the dim light and throw it back as small, steady glints. These outgrowths give the structures a faintly living surface, though nothing grows on them.

Sound is sparse. A low, steady hiss of steam escaping from joints in the towers punctuates the silence at long intervals. Occasionally a deeper note sounds when water finds a vertical shaft and falls the full height, striking an echoing basin far below. Between these events the air is nearly still. The few moving things pass without voice: slow, low-bodied mechanisms that follow fixed grooves across the platforms, their wheels depositing thin trails of condensed moisture that soon evaporate. At greater distances, dark flecks occasionally cross the haze—solitary flyers whose wings make no audible beat. They descend only to drink at the weeping ledges and depart again without lingering.

The basin remains active in its own measured way. Heat rises from vents hidden beneath some platforms, driving slow updrafts that keep the condensation cycle turning. Water that reaches the lowest layers seeps into shallow mineral aquifers and is drawn upward again through capillary action in the crystalline veins of the towers. The structures themselves continue a faint, almost inaudible expansion and contraction with each temperature shift, their joints releasing minute quantities of trapped steam. Nothing here is still, yet nothing hurries. The emptiness between the towers and platforms simply holds the motion at arm’s length, letting the slow processes continue without interference.
