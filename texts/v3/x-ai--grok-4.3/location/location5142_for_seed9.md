The place is a single high terrace no larger than a cathedral nave, its edges dropping into cloud so that the world beyond is only rumor. Everything here has been poured or bolted or grown into place; there is no raw stone or soil visible, only layered panels of warm composite that have been seeded with capillary channels. Moisture condenses constantly along the undersides of these panels and beads downward in slow, deliberate drips, feeding a dense understory of engineered vines that climb every vertical surface and interweave overhead into a living canopy. The vines are thick with broad, glossy leaves that never quite touch but stand a hand’s width apart, each leaf trembling with the weight of its own collected water.

The air is warm and still, yet never stagnant; faint convection currents move the vapor in slow spirals between the foliage layers. Because the terrace is fully enclosed and newly completed, the materials have not yet begun to weather. Joints are crisp, surfaces retain the faint sheen of the molds that shaped them, and the only erosion present is the deliberate micro-channels already scored into the panels to guide water. These channels are embryonic—still filling, still learning their own gradients—so occasional overflow creates miniature falls that land on the next tier of leaves with soft, regular percussion.

Light arrives evenly from above through a translucent ceiling membrane, diffused enough to erase shadows yet bright enough that every droplet on every leaf refracts a tiny spectrum. The only sounds are water in motion and the low, continuous murmur of hidden circulation pumps that keep the capillary feed moving. No animals have arrived yet; the space is too new and too high for them. Only the vegetation and the machinery that sustains it share the volume, layered so closely that a single step forward requires choosing which stalks to press aside and which to step over.

At intervals the vines part to reveal small industrial nodes—manifold junctions where warm fluid is exchanged, or banks of thin resonators that emit a sub-audible hum to encourage root growth. These nodes are already beginning to collect their own condensation skins, so metal and leaf appear to be exchanging surfaces. The entire terrace feels at once finished and still assembling itself, a manufactured marsh caught mid-gesture, warm, dense, and self-contained above the clouds.
