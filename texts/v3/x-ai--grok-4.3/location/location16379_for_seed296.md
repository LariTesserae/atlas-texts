The plateau stretches outward in every direction, its surface worn smooth by epochs of wind and water until only the gentlest undulations remain. Stone that once formed jagged peaks now lies in broad, low ribs half-buried beneath layers of moss and slow-creeping vines. Condensation gathers overnight on every exposed surface, trickling into shallow stone bowls that never quite empty; thin ribbons of water follow the ancient grain of the rock before vanishing again into mist. The air stays cool enough that breath becomes visible, yet the constant dampness keeps the vegetation thick and blue-green, crowding against every vertical face and horizontal plane.

Buildings rise in dense, staggered tiers from this worn foundation. They are not stacked so much as grown, metal frameworks and concrete shells swallowed by rope-thick roots and flowering creepers that climb from one level to the next. Pipes and conveyor housings thread between the vegetation like arteries, carrying faint mechanical pulses that barely disturb the quiet. At this scale the city reads less as a single settlement than as a continuous band of habitation stretching across several valleys, its edges softening where factories merge with terraced gardens and then with open mist.

Light arrives filtered through low, fast-moving cloud. Everything remains half-shadowed, colors muted to slate, fern, and oxidized copper. The few animals that move here do so at the edges of sight: pale, long-limbed insects that crawl along the undersides of pipes, and once in a long while a solitary gliding form that appears only when the mist thins. Their presence feels accidental rather than resident.

Machinery runs on schedules measured in days rather than hours. When a turbine spins up, the sound is a low, wet thrum that travels through the rock and then disappears again into dripping water and leaf rustle. Rain arrives in brief curtains, enough to pearl every leaf and then gone, leaving the air heavy with the scent of wet stone and crushed greenery. Nothing feels finished; the plateau’s ancient flattening continues beneath the newer layers, while the city and its vines keep rearranging themselves around the same few stable contours. The overall impression is one of slow, continuous adjustment rather than any single dramatic event.
