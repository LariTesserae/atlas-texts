The city stretches farther than any horizon can contain, a single unbroken lattice of mineral mass and engineered void that fills an entire continental basin and then some. From the flattened low ground, layered slabs of quartz-rich stone and fused silicates rise in overlapping planes, each level slotted so tightly against the next that no true gap remains. The structures do not sit on the land; they replace it, so that every surface—wall, floor, ceiling, thoroughfare—has been shaped and re-shaped until the original substrate is only a memory beneath meters of cumulative construction.

Water moves through this density without ever pooling into lakes. It condenses constantly on every upward-facing plane, forms slow rivulets along the seams, and weeps from capillary fissures the builders left open for exactly that purpose. The air stays warm and close, almost thick enough to feel against the skin, carrying the moisture upward again in slow convection so that the entire plain remains in a perpetual state of light, drifting damp. Nothing grows here; the mineral surfaces have been tuned or doped in ways that prevent even the hardiest lichen. Instead, thin crystalline tubules and lattice networks—part machine, part self-organizing precipitate—thread through every block and girder, carrying power, data, and micro-currents of the same water that keeps the temperature steady.

Sound does not travel through this place so much as occupy it completely. A single sustained roar rises from the constant re-arrangement of matter: vast actuator arrays that shift entire city blocks by centimeters each hour, adjusting for subsidence and thermal expansion; the low thunder of water vapor flashing through micro-valves; the overlapping pulses of colossal resonant engines buried kilometers deep. The noise is so total that it becomes a kind of pressure, felt in the teeth and the bones. Occasional sharper cracks break across it when a layer of stressed silicate finally shears and is immediately sealed again by autonomous repair swarms.

Fauna moves within and between these shifting strata. The creatures are not numerous in the ordinary sense—they are simply never absent. Long, segmented forms the color of wet stone slide along the condensation channels, their bodies tuned to the same mineral frequencies as the city itself. Smaller, quicker things flicker through the capillary networks, leaving momentary iridescences where they disturb the thin water films. Larger entities, more like ambulatory reefs than animals, drift across the broader plazas on cushions of ionized air, their mineral carapaces accumulating new layers of condensate and dust as they move. They feed on the waste heat and stray electromagnetic fields of the city’s hidden machinery; nothing here eats anything that was once alive.

The turbulence is not weather in the planetary sense but something engineered and then deliberately allowed to exceed its own controls. Blocks rotate, tilt, and reseal according to rhythms that no single intelligence tracks. Entire districts rise or subside by a hand-span over the course of a day, altering the flow of condensed water and the paths of the larger fauna. The advanced systems that maintain structural integrity also generate the instability, cycling materials through states that current physics can measure but not fully predict. Erosion here is not decay but an ongoing, balanced process: surfaces are abraded by water and by the passage of the reef-like creatures, then rebuilt at exactly the same rate by the lattice networks, so that the city’s form remains recognizable even while every atom within it is eventually replaced.

In the perpetual warm half-light that filters through the shifting upper planes, the distinction between built and grown, between structure and inhabitant, has long since blurred into a single, saturated, roaring continuum.
