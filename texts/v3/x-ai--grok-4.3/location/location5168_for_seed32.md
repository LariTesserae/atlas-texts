Inside this sealed pocket, every surface is a facet of interlocking crystal grown from the same dry mineral breath. The lattice rises a few body-lengths at most yet fills every direction without gap or void: translucent hexagonal columns lean against one another at precise angles, each one worn by time into gentle, flowing ridges that match exactly with its neighbors. No mortar exists; the forms have simply continued until they met and fused. The joints are rounded and polished, edges softened into long, continuous curves that record an equilibrium reached long ago and never left.

Sparse filaments of white mineral fiber cling to the upper faces of the columns like frost that decided never to melt. They are the only living tissue, motionless, each strand exactly as long as the next, spaced so that no two touch. Their pale color catches the light and returns it in flat, even sheets. The light itself is constant and total, arriving from everywhere at once because every surface refracts and passes it onward; there is no shadow, only a uniform glare that makes the cool air feel solid.

Sound fills the same space the crystals occupy. A single low chord, dense and unvarying, issues from the lattice as the columns transmit minute stresses through one another in perfect phase. The tone does not rise or fall; it simply persists, a continuous pressure against the ears that never resolves into separate notes. Because nothing moves, the chord cannot change; because the lattice is complete, the chord cannot cease.

No creature stirs. The only motion is the steady refraction of light along the worn ridges and the unchanging tone that the ridges themselves sustain. The entire arrangement sits embedded within a larger, unseen host mineral; its boundaries are the outer crystal walls of the pocket itself, grown thick enough to seal away every trace of moisture or fluctuation. Inside, temperature, pressure, and alignment have all settled into one another so completely that the landscape can be read as a single finished object, already finished when the first light arrived and still finished now.
