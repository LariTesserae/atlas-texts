Beneath a low, unbroken sky the plain stretches without relief, a single continuous plane that has been shaped rather than found. Its surface is not soil or stone but a pale, faintly translucent membrane stretched tight over countless shallow vaults and conduits, the whole expanse still thickening as it grows. Where the membrane has not yet sealed, clear fluid beads and slides, then gathers into long, slow-moving threads that follow the faint depressions already scored into the plane. These threads carry warmth rather than coolness; each one steams gently in the heavy air before sinking again through pores that open and close at irregular intervals, returning the moisture to the living lattice below.

The membrane itself is sparsely marked by low, angular ridges—embryonic walls that rise only a handspan before they stop, their edges still soft and incompletely fused. Between the ridges the surface remains almost featureless, interrupted only by occasional blisters where condensation has gathered beneath the skin and lifted it into shallow domes. From these domes the heat rises in slow, visible shimmers; the air above them wavers, then settles again, carrying a faint, sweet scent of sugars just beginning to polymerize. No true plants stand upright; instead, thread-thin filaments of the same pale tissue lie flat against the membrane in radial patterns, feeding on the condensate and adding microscopic layers each time the cycle repeats. Their presence is visible only as a faint, almost mineral dullness against the brighter surrounding sheet.

Far ahead the same membrane continues, unmarked by horizon or curvature, simply receding until distance and heat-haze erase detail. Here and there, wider depressions have already collected enough fluid to form temporary shallow pools whose surfaces reflect the even sky before the pools are drawn downward again through valves that dilate and close without sound. Each intake produces a single, soft percussion, like the distant tap of a membrane against wood; the sound travels little distance before the heavy air absorbs it. Between these events the plain is nearly silent, the only movement the slow advance of the fluid threads and the minute, continuous tightening of new tissue.

Because nothing has yet lived or died on this surface, no dust has settled, no footprints remain, and no wind has carved channels. The geometry that exists is therefore the first geometry, still guided only by the internal pressures and flows that continue to organize the living sheet into channels, vaults, and the first faint outlines of something larger. The heat remains steady, the light unbroken, the scale beyond any single vantage; the plain simply continues, thickening, sealing, and occasionally releasing another thread of water that will travel a short distance and then disappear once more into the unfinished architecture beneath.
