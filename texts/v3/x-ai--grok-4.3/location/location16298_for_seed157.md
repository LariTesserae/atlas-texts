High in the folded shoulders of an ancient range, a single cirque no larger than a cathedral nave holds itself apart from the wider slopes. Its rim is a continuous lip of pale, water-stained stone, rounded by long weathering until every edge reads as intentional yet untouched. Inside, nothing rises or falls; the basin has reached its final shape and keeps it.

The air stays cool and motionless, heavy with the scent of wet stone and bruised greenery. Water gathers constantly on every surface—seeping from hairline cracks in the rock, beading along the undersides of leaves, condensing into slow, soundless drops that descend through the half-light and vanish into the mat of growth below. The vegetation fills every vertical interval without gaps: layered fronds, thick cushions of moss, and broad, vein-thick leaves that press against one another so closely they form living walls and ceilings. Between them there is no empty space, only more plant. The darkness is not total but stubborn; what little light reaches the floor arrives already filtered and dimmed, carrying no warmth.

Creatures occupy the remaining intervals. They are numerous beyond counting yet nearly silent: translucent salamanders flattened against dripping stone, clusters of minute pale crabs that drift rather than scurry across the foliage, and soft-bodied slugs whose slow progress leaves glistening trails that never dry. Their presence is continuous and saturated; at any moment a dozen bodies occupy the space a hand might pass through, yet the only audible sign is the faint, wet friction of their movement, absorbed at once by the leaves. No calls, no rustling wings. The equilibrium extends to them as well; they neither increase nor diminish.

Nothing here alters. The same drop completes its fall along the same leaf-edge it has followed for unmeasured time. The same arrangement of fronds admits the same thin shaft of light. The rock continues its imperceptible weeping; the moss receives it without swelling or receding. The basin therefore feels less like a living place than like a finished arrangement that has been left exactly as it must remain—dense, cool, dark, and full.
