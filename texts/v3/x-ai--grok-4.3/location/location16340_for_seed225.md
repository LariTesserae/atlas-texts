A single vast chamber lies beneath the surface, its boundaries marked by a ring of pale stone that has been worn into soft, undulating waves over unmeasured time. The floor dips and rises in gentle swells no higher than a person’s waist, each hollow holding a shallow lens of water that reflects the light pouring from fissures high above. These openings—narrow vertical slits and jagged rents—admit shafts of intense, steady illumination that cross the empty air like slow, silent blades, never quite reaching the opposite wall at the same moment. Condensation forms along the upper rims of the fissures and falls in deliberate, spaced droplets that strike the pools with a sound no louder than a fingertip brushing cloth.

The stone itself is a pale, fine-grained matrix threaded with darker veins that have been polished to a dull satin by long cycles of moisture and evaporation. Nothing stands upright; every surface curves or recesses. Between these curves the chamber remains almost empty, the air holding only faint, persistent warmth that never sharpens into heat. Small pockets of darker, cooler air linger in the deeper concavities, carrying a faint mineral breath that rises and settles again over hours.

Vegetation appears only as thin, translucent sheets that cling inside the narrowest crevices, their edges curled like dried leaves yet never fully dry. They drink from the condensation film rather than from the pools and grow so sparsely that a single crossing of the chamber may reveal fewer than a dozen such growths, each separated by wide stretches of bare stone.

The fauna is present yet unobtrusive: clusters of minute, slow-moving crustaceans the color of milky quartz traverse the film of water along the floor, leaving traceries that vanish within minutes. Pale, elongated insects drift on faint convection currents beneath the light shafts, their wings catching the illumination in brief, glassy flashes before they settle again on the stone. Larger, flatter creatures—dimpled and nearly translucent—rest motionless inside the shallow pools, rising only when a droplet lands nearby, then subsiding without ripple. Their movements accumulate across long durations rather than punctuating any single hour.

No constructed trace interrupts the stone. The only tools are the hand-like hollows already shaped by water and the faint scoring left by the slow passage of the crustaceans. Sound remains minimal: the occasional soft strike of condensation, the low, almost inaudible rasp of a carapace against stone, and, once in a long while, the collective whisper of many tiny wings adjusting their angle to the drifting light.

Change registers only as gradation. A pool edge migrates by the width of a finger across many cycles of light and dark; a new condensation streak appears on one wall while an older one fades on another. The chamber holds these shifts without urgency, its hollow volume permitting each alteration to unfold at the pace of weathering rather than weather.
