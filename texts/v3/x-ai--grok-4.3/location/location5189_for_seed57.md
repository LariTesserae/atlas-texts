Inside the narrow seam where two colossal, slow-drifting root-masses have pressed together for centuries, a pocket of water has collected that is not quite part of the greater sea. The cavity is no larger than a cupped hand, yet once entered it unfolds in every direction at once; distances refuse to stay fixed, so that a tendril seen a moment ago may now brush the far wall that was never there before. Everything is saturated—water threaded with drifting filaments of plant matter so fine they read as mist until they brush skin or scale. The vegetation is dense enough to feel solid, a living felt of translucent leaves and branching fronds that glow with a faint, sourceless cool light, more like stored moonlight than life. Their edges are knife-sharp, recently unfurled, the world still learning how to keep them in one shape.

The temperature holds steady just below what the body expects, a constant cool pressure that slows every motion. Currents inside the pocket are almost imperceptible; when a frond finally releases a spore it takes long minutes to travel the width of a finger. Sound arrives only as the softest particulate hush, countless minute impacts of drifting matter against other drifting matter, the occasional wet sigh when a leaf uncoils. No larger creatures move here. What life exists is vegetable or half-vegetable, locked into slow negotiations with the dissolving boundary between flesh and fluid.

Now and then the pocket’s own geometry shivers. A straight line of stems will suddenly continue behind the observer, or two clusters of roots will trade places without moving. These shifts leave a brief metallic taste in the water and nothing else; the vegetation simply re-knits around the new arrangement. Scattered through the tangle are faint, ribbed impressions in the root walls—concave impressions the size of fingerprints—shaped like the negative spaces left when something once rested there and then was absorbed. They are the only marks that the pocket has ever held anything that was not root, leaf, or water.

Nothing here is in a hurry to finish. The erosion that carved the seam outward is still occurring, but its progress registers as the gradual rounding of an angle over decades rather than the sudden fall of stone. Darkness is not absolute; it is simply the dominant medium, interrupted only by the diffuse, cool phosphorescence of the crowding plants. One can remain inside the pocket for a long while and still feel that one has only just arrived, because arrival itself is a process the space refuses to complete.
