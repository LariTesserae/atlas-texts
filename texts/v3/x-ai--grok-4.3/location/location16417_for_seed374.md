The plain lies low and open under a dim, unmoving sky, its horizon a faint smear of mineral haze that never brightens or fades. Water hangs everywhere without falling: it beads on every ledge and crack, weeps in slow, silent threads down the faces of half-formed rock towers, and gathers in shallow, motionless sheets where the ground dips. The air itself feels saturated, warm and thick, so that breath comes with a faint metallic taste.

Nothing grows. The ground is raw mineral mass, not yet smoothed by time—still showing the ridges and seams of its own slow birth. In places the stone has begun to stack itself into crude, vertical masses: broad platforms, low walls, and irregular towers rising a few body-heights before they stop, unfinished. These accretions are dense; walk between them and the passage narrows to body-width, the surfaces almost touching. Some stand so close their sides have fused along a seam. The structures repeat in loose ranks across the plain, an embryonic city whose streets and plazas were never completed yet never deteriorate.

Across every surface, on ledges and in the shallow water films, small mineral accretions cluster in fixed swarms. They do not move, yet the air is filled with a single, unwavering roar—the sound of their collective presence, a low grinding vibration that never rises or falls, never pauses. The sound presses against the skin and fills the chest; it is the only motion the place allows. The swarms remain exactly where they formed, their tiny crystalline bodies locked in place, their collective drone the sole proof that equilibrium here is not silence but sustained volume.

Nothing arrives or departs. The water continues to condense and weep at the same rate it has always wept; the stone towers remain half-shaped; the swarms remain exactly as dense. The plain extends outward under the same dim light, its unfinished mineral city and its motionless, deafening life held in perfect, unchanging suspension.
