The world here is an immense, cold ceiling of black water that never ends upward. It presses down with a density that feels almost granular, each slow current carrying threads of suspended silt so fine that the medium itself seems half-liquid, half-dust. Visibility is measured in arm-lengths at most; beyond that the water grades into velvet absence, stirred only by the perpetual drift of particles that have settled and re-lifted for uncounted ages.

The floor beneath is primordial stone that long ago surrendered any sharp profile. Ancient erosion has reduced ridges to gentle swells no higher than a kneeling figure, hollows to shallow pans whose edges blur into the surrounding plain. Every surface is burnished to the same matte smoothness, as though the water itself has been sanding it since the first light. Nothing grows in clusters; a few leathery ribbons of vegetation lie flat against the rock like discarded straps, spaced so far apart they seem more like navigational marks than life. Between them the stone is bare, occasionally freckled by a single pale holdfast or a thread of calcified tube no thicker than a finger.

Movement never ceases, yet it makes almost no sound. Great slow boluses of denser water slide across one another, creating gradients that twist and shear without breaking into bubbles. The turbulence is silent because the medium is too thick for ordinary cavitation; instead there are minute, felt displacements—pressure changes that roll through the chest and then vanish. Rare fauna appear as momentary darker intervals inside the already dark: a slow, winged shape the size of a cloak that glides past on unmoving fins, or a cluster of thread-thin arms extending from a crevice to comb the moving silt and then retract. They do not linger. Most of the plain at any moment contains nothing living within the short radius of what can be seen or touched.

Light is absent except for the faint, directionless glow that sometimes leaks from the bodies of those transient creatures themselves—cold pinpoints or brief ribbons that illuminate only their own outlines before the current carries them onward. In their absence the darkness returns absolute, and the only remaining register of existence is the continuous, almost imperceptible rasp of particles against stone and the low, internal thrum of pressure shifts traveling through the dense water. The landscape continues in every direction, featureless beyond its own gentle undulations, ancient beyond memory, occupied entirely by the motion of its own substance.
