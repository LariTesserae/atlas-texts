Beneath the surface, the place begins as a single, low-ceilinged gallery whose walls still carry the fresh chisel-marks and fracture-planes of recent excavation. The rock itself is pale and sharp-edged, its surfaces barely softened by the first thin films of mineral dust that have settled since the last cutting. Moisture gathers in these minute irregularities—slow beads that swell, drop, and are replaced by new condensation drawn from warmer air rising through fissures far above. The water is never plentiful enough to flow, only enough to keep the air heavy and the surfaces slick in narrow, repeating cycles measured in hours rather than seasons.

Between these fresh-cut walls, buildings have been fitted like teeth in a jaw. They rise only two or three narrow storeys, yet they occupy every usable span, their masonry keyed directly into the living rock so that roof edges nearly brush the ceiling. Catwalks, pipe runs, and stair towers stitch the gaps, creating a continuous three-dimensional lattice whose density leaves almost no open volume. The stone of the structures matches the surrounding matrix so closely that at first glance wall and building seem continuous, until lantern-light reveals the deliberate joints and the faint vibration of metal within them.

Heat rises steadily from the lower levels where massive, slow-turning engines are housed. Their flywheels revolve at intervals measured in minutes, each rotation advancing a ratchet or driving a long belt that feeds a distant press. The machinery itself runs warm rather than scorching; its warmth, trapped beneath the low rock ceiling, maintains the constant damp that nourishes the vegetation. Vines with broad, translucent leaves trail from every ledge and railing; their roots have already found purchase in the mortar lines and in the thin sediment that accumulates in the angles of the pipes. Moss mats thicken on horizontal surfaces, brightening from olive to a deeper, wet green wherever a drip strikes them. The growth is orderly in its own way—plants follow the geometry of the infrastructure rather than overwhelming it, filling interstices the builders left open.

Light remains low. A few caged industrial lamps burn with steady, orange filaments, their output deliberately throttled so that shadows stay long and edges blur. In compensation, certain vines and wall-growths emit a faint, cool phosphorescence whose spectrum shifts almost imperceptibly across the course of a day, following some internal rhythm independent of any clock. The combined illumination is sufficient to navigate but never to banish the sense that the ceiling is nearer than memory suggests.

Sound moves through the same narrow channels as the air. The dominant tone is a low, patient thrum from the engines—one cycle every several minutes—accompanied by the soft, regular percussion of condensation striking metal or leaf. No voices carry; any speech that occurs is deliberately muted by the architecture itself, absorbed by the dense growth and the absorbent stone. When a distant valve opens or a counterweight descends, the noise is felt first as a slow change in pressure against the skin rather than as an audible event.

Fauna is scarce. The only consistent presences are pale, elongated isopods that traverse the pipe runs at intervals measured in days, grazing on the condensation films and leaving behind faint silver trails that evaporate within an hour. Occasionally a larger, solitary form—something adapted to the narrow vertical shafts—may cross a catwalk and vanish again, leaving only a momentary disturbance in the moss.

Over longer spans the entire arrangement continues its patient adjustment. The sharp fracture faces of the rock gradually acquire a thin, darker patina where moisture lingers longest. Vines thicken their grip on the masonry, subtly redistributing structural loads. The engines maintain their measured cadence, their heat output varying only with the slow seasonal pulse of water seeping from above. Boundaries remain distinct: beyond the last row of buildings the gallery narrows again into raw, unfinished tunnels whose purposes have not yet been claimed by either industry or growth. Within the settled core, however, the rock, the structures, the vegetation and the damp heat have already begun to read as a single, slow-moving organism whose parts are still learning the precise geometry of coexistence.
