In a razor-edged fissure no wider than a forearm, wedged into the flank of a newborn volcanic horn that still steams from its own recent birth, lies a pocket of almost suffocating intimacy. The walls are black glass and fresh pumice, fractured yesterday by cooling stresses; every facet is knife-bright, untouched by wind or rain, the fractures so clean they still ring with the memory of the split. Condensation gathers on every upward-facing plane, trembling droplets that swell, fall, and are instantly replaced by the next exhalation of heat from below. The air itself feels thickened by its own moisture; breath moves through it like something half-solid.

Nothing green or rooted exists here. Instead the surfaces are colonized by dense crusts of mineral exudates—opalescent films that behave like living tissue, swelling and contracting with each pulse of warmth. These films crackle softly when they split, releasing threads of vapor that braid and unbraid in the narrow light. Between and beneath the films, smaller presences seethe: clusters of glassy mites no larger than sand grains, their legs jointed from the same fresh silicate as the rock. They move in layered sheets, one stratum crawling over the next, each individual’s motion almost imperceptible until the collective drift shifts the entire surface by a visible degree. Their passage leaves faint, temporary etchings that heal within minutes as new mineral oozes up from below.

The temperature hovers at the edge of tolerance; heat radiates upward from unseen veins of magma only meters deeper, while the fissure’s mouth, higher still, admits ordinary daylight that refracts through the rising vapor into soft, directionless glow. Sound is almost absent. The mites’ movements transmit as the faintest sub-audible vibration felt first in the teeth, then the inner ear. Occasionally a droplet detaches and strikes another; the impact is a single, damped click that is swallowed at once by the dense air.

Within this confined volume, space does not behave consistently. A droplet falling from the ceiling may, for a breath, continue its descent after it has already struck the floor; a patch of mineral film will seem to lie both on the left wall and, simultaneously, across the back of an outstretched hand. These discrepancies last only until the observer blinks or shifts weight, at which point the geometry corrects itself with a faint internal pressure, as though the pocket has chosen to become coherent again for its own convenience. The mites navigate these folds without hesitation, their trails sometimes appearing to cross over one another in three dimensions that later prove to be the same two.

No hand has ever altered this space. The only tools are the mites themselves, whose crystalline mandibles abrade fresh surfaces and, in the same motion, reseal them with fresh precipitate—an economy so immediate it leaves no trace of work, only continuous becoming. Heat, water, and motion braid together without pause; when one process slackens another quickens, maintaining the saturation inside the fissure like a held note. Outside, the mountain continues its first slow collapse; inside, the pocket remains sealed, active, and almost silent, a single dense thought the rock has not yet finished thinking.
