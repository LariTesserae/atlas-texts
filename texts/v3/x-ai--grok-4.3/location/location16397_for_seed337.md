In the dim hush beneath the ridge-line, a city is being born from stone that has not yet decided what it wants to be. The rock here is still young in its shaping—soft enough that hand-held chisels and bone wedges can bite into it, leaving pale, floury scars that have not yet darkened with age or polish. Passageways open without pattern, some no wider than a man’s shoulders, others suddenly flaring into vaulted chambers where the ceiling still carries the ragged tooth-marks of the last excavation team. Moisture moves through these raw galleries in slow, secretive ways: a film of condensation that gathers on the colder faces at night and slides downward in patient threads, collecting in shallow basins cut deliberately into the floor so the settlement can drink without descending to the deeper aquifers. The air stays cool and carries the faint mineral bite of wet limestone.

Nothing grows thickly here. A few pale root-threads—pallid, almost translucent—find purchase in the cracks where a pocket of soil has been carried down by seeping water; they hang like forgotten string but produce no leaves, only a faint damp scent when brushed. Between these threadbare strands the city presses close. Walls of freshly dressed stone stand barely an arm’s length apart, their surfaces still gritty with drill dust. Doorways are low, framed with timber that has not yet darkened. In places the rock itself is being left in rough pillars to bear the weight above, and around these pillars people have already begun to lash woven screens and hide partitions, turning the negative space of excavation into rooms, storage niches, and narrow lanes that force bodies to pass in single file.

Light is steady rather than bright—slow-burning reed lamps set in wall niches, their flames protected by thin slate baffles so the draft does not carry smoke far. The illumination is enough to work by, enough to recognize a face at ten paces, but it leaves the higher vaults in soft shadow. Footsteps are muffled by the constant thin film of moisture on the floor; voices travel only a short distance before the unfinished surfaces swallow them. The only persistent sound is the patient, irregular tap of tools still at work somewhere farther along the new tunnel, and the soft, single-note drip where a condensation channel has not yet been carved to carry water away.

The settlement feels both crowded and incomplete. Families sleep on reed mats laid directly on the living rock; cooking fires are kept small in shallow depressions so the smoke can be drawn upward through natural fissures rather than purpose-built chimneys. Children play between the unfinished pillars, their games bounded by ropes strung with chipped stone markers. Because the rock is still being opened, new chambers appear every few days, and the geometry of the place shifts—an alley that yesterday ended in solid wall today terminates at a freshly cut threshold where someone is already fitting a door frame of green timber. Nothing here feels permanent, yet nothing feels empty; the density of bodies and intentions fills every interval the stone has so far yielded.

Above, the surface world remains distant and irrelevant. Down here the only weather is the slow migration of moisture from ceiling to floor, the only season the gradual hardening of the rock as new faces are left exposed to air. The city continues to carve itself outward, one careful removal at a time, turning absence into architecture while the water keeps its quiet schedule and the pale roots continue their patient, lightless reach.
