Beneath the crust, the world is still deciding what it wants to be. Raw stone buds like living tissue, ridges and nodes pushing outward in slow spasms that rearrange corridors within hours. Where the rock has not yet sealed, broad galleries open—wide enough that a herd could turn inside them—yet the air stays thick with movement. Shifts in the mineral lattice release faint puffs of warmth that bead moisture along every new fracture; droplets gather, fall, and leave trails that etch and re-etch the same surfaces before the next tremor erases them.

No green or even fungal threads exist here. Instead, the stone itself carries faint internal patterns, vein-like discolorations that glow the steady color of midday when light from surface fissures or from the animals’ own shell-lamps reaches them. Water collects in lens-shaped pockets cupped inside the newest folds; some last only minutes before a fresh fracture drains them, others linger long enough to grow a skin of mineral film. The air smells of wet chalk and heated quartz.

Life moves in constant layers. Armored slugs the size of small boats glide along the ceilings on secreted tracks of glassy paste, their undersides rasping new grooves that become tomorrow’s channels. Smaller clusters of six-legged mineral borers pour through every temporary seam, their mouth-parts ticking like loose pebbles in a flood. Larger, translucent herd beasts the color of milky agate travel in dense, slow rivers, their footfalls producing a rolling thunder that never quite dies; when one stops to drink at a pocket, hundreds more press against it, bodies overlapping until the whole mass seems to breathe as one. The noise is continuous: grinding stone, clicking chitin, the slap and hiss of water hitting newly opened rock, and, underneath it all, a low, irregular booming whenever a gallery wall gives way and collapses into the chamber below.

Scattered through this churn are the first crude marks of use. Shoulder-high rings of piled rubble mark places where the herd beasts have shouldered loose blocks into rough windbreaks; the barriers are already half-swallowed by fresh mineral growth. Thin, hand-wide scrapes on certain ledges show where the borers once paused to widen a passage before the ceiling shifted again. Nothing lasts long, yet nothing is ever entirely abandoned—the same seams keep reopening, the same pockets reappear a few body-lengths away, so the marks accumulate like half-erased tally strokes on living slate.

Light behaves normally once it arrives: it travels straight, casts ordinary shadows, and is only colored by whatever mineral dust happens to be suspended at that moment. Yet the turbulence keeps that dust moving, so beams appear to flicker and drift as though the air itself were breathing. Warm currents rise from deeper fractures, carrying both moisture and the faint vibration of still-deeper change. In this place, formation and destruction are the same ongoing motion; every creature, every droplet, every new crystal is both agent and witness of the instability that refuses to settle.
