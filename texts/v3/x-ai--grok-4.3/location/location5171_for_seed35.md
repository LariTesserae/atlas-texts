The chamber is a single, sealed blister of mineral no larger than a modest cathedral nave, its boundaries fixed by knife-edged ridges of fresh gypsum and anhydrite that only lately parted from the surrounding bedrock. Light arrives thinly from veins of low-grade phosphorescent salts embedded in the ceiling; the glow is perpetual and directionless, enough to silver the edges of things but never to cast a true shadow. Warmth radiates from the same salts at a steady body temperature, dry as kiln brick, carrying only the faint metallic scent of long-unweathered stone.

Across the floor and up the walls, dense congregations of small, jointed creatures—each no bigger than a thumb—occupy every square span. Their translucent carapaces are veined with copper-colored filaments that pulse in perfect unison, never advancing, never retreating, yet producing an unvarying metallic rasp that fills the space like a single sustained chord. Between their ranks rise sparse, hair-fine stalks of crystalline vegetation, each filament no thicker than a wire and terminating in a single dry seed-pod the color of old bone. The stalks do not sway; they simply exist at the precise interstices left by the creatures.

Scattered among both life-forms are the partial relics of an earlier industrial presence: a single gear-toothed sleeve half-swallowed by the wall, a length of conduit whose rivets have fused with the host gypsum, a low transformer block whose coils still emit a faint, unchanging electromagnetic whine. These artifacts show no corrosion, no accumulation of dust; they simply share the same static equilibrium as the fauna and the crystal growths. No gradient of moisture, temperature, or population ever develops. The air does not stir. The creatures’ rasp does not rise or fall. The faint phosphorescence neither brightens nor dims. The chamber holds its inventory of presences in exact proportion, as though the moment of its opening has been extended indefinitely into a single, self-replenishing present.
