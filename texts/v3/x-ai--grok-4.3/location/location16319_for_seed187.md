The basin is a low, ancient hollow no larger than a single cathedral nave, its floor worn by eons of erosion into a single unbroken plane of compacted, living matter. Nothing rises more than a few body-lengths above that plane; the whole space has settled into its final form, roots and stems and leaves having long ago replaced any trace of mineral ground. The air stays cool and still, carrying only the steady drip of condensation that forms along the undersides of the highest leaves and falls at unchanging intervals, each droplet landing with a soft, repetitive percussion that never varies.

Between the colossal, hollow trunks that function as the chamber’s architecture there is mostly absence: wide, empty volumes of cool air where a person might walk for many paces without touching anything living. These voids are the dominant feature. The plants themselves have grown into continuous, self-supporting masses that serve every structural purpose—floor, wall, ceiling, conduit—yet they leave the interior almost vacant, like the nave of a living building whose columns have been withdrawn. Pale, translucent sheets of membrane stretch between distant supports, damp and faintly luminous under ordinary daylight that filters through the single high canopy. Water gathers in shallow, persistent pockets along the membrane folds and along the thick root-lips that mark the boundary of the space; the pockets never overflow and never dry.

Nothing here has been made by tools, yet an industrial order is constant and audible. Within the thickest trunks, vascular channels pulse in slow, synchronized rhythms that produce a low, continuous mechanical drone, the sound of sap forced through successive chambers at steady pressure. The drone does not fluctuate; it is the acoustic signature of a system that reached equilibrium long ago and has no reason to change. Around and inside these pulses move abundant small creatures—winged, crawling, burrowing—whose combined activity raises the overall sound level to a sustained roar that fills the voids without ever altering its character. Calls, scrapes, and wingbeats overlap into one unbroken texture, loud yet static, as though the same instant of noise is being repeated without progression.

The temperature remains cool enough that breath condenses on the inner membrane surfaces and runs downward in thin, constant rivulets. Light arrives evenly from above, neither dim nor bright, revealing every surface as plant matter in various states of maturity: fresh translucent growth alongside older, darker, fully hardened wood that has stopped expanding yet never decays. The entire arrangement is self-contained, bounded by the gentle inward curve of the encircling root ridge that marks the edge of the original river course. Beyond that ridge the same plant mass continues, but the description ends here, at the scale of one stable, self-sufficient chamber where moisture, plant, sound, and motion have reached a final, unchanging balance.
