In the reached-far end of an ancient mountain’s final flattening, a single chamber persists like a held breath. Once a high summit, the stone has worn itself toward level through unthinkable durations until only this modest vault remains, cathedral in proportion yet intimate as a sealed grove, its boundaries defined by the slow meeting of floor and lowering ceiling. The rock is warm to the touch, not from any outer sun but from a patient seeping that condenses along every fracture and drips in long, irregular intervals. Water lingers near every surface, beading on stone that never fully dries, threading in thin films that catch the edge of sound rather than light.

The air is thick with vegetative presence yet nearly empty of bulk. Between thickets of root and stalk lie wide, open volumes where a body might pass without brushing anything for many paces. Lush mats of dark-adapted growths—ribbon-like algae, tumescent fungi, and shoulder-high stands of fleshy, lightless leaves—rise in isolated clusters from floor and wall, drawing what they need from the constant film of moisture and the faint metabolic warmth stored in the rock itself. Their surfaces are soft and yielding; they do not rustle in any breeze because no breeze reaches here. Instead they respond across hours, minute shifts in curvature or spore release timed to the slow drip cycles of water from above.

Fauna move through the same emptiness in abundance yet with vast distances between each presence. Pale, long-limbed forms—segmented crawlers, membrane-winged gliders no larger than a hand, sinuous blind swimmers in the shallow films—navigate almost entirely by vibration. Their calls are sudden, resonant bursts that travel the length of the chamber and return altered, a layered chorus that registers as loud because the stone itself acts as amplifier. Each cry hangs for many heartbeats before the next begins, so the soundscape feels both continuous and unhurried. Between calls the chamber feels profoundly still, the quiet stretched thin across the open space.

Scattered through this life are remnants of something older than the mountain’s flattening. Low, curved shells of an unnameable alloy rest half-sunk in sediment or lean against living pillars; their surfaces are smooth, unmarred by rust or lichen, yet they bear no visible mechanism. At irregular, centuries-long intervals one of them emits a subsonic thrum that momentarily stills the fauna and causes the nearest growths to tighten their leaves. The devices do not appear to consume anything visible; they simply persist, maintaining an equilibrium the surrounding stone has long since accepted as its final shape.

Nothing here changes quickly. Water continues its patient descent. Leaves adjust their angles over days. A single animal call may echo and re-echo for the length of a human life before its pattern is replaced by another. The chamber remains exactly itself, small enough to contain in a single slow walk, large enough inside to feel like a world that has already finished becoming.
