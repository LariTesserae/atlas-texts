The chamber is no larger than a cathedral nave, yet it feels larger because almost nothing occupies it. Its ceiling is a single low vault of recently fractured basalt, still shedding hairline shards that drift downward through humid air like black snow. Where those shards strike the floor they ring once, a high metallic ping that lingers and multiplies, because the entire volume is designed to carry sound. Nothing absorbs it: no carpets, no curtains, only the faint continuous percussion of water falling from weeping fissures overhead.

The floor itself is not bedrock but a translucent membrane of engineered lattice, cool and faintly warm by turns. It flexes underfoot with the slow breathing of pressure cycles that run beneath it, cycling condensed moisture upward again so that the air remains saturated without ever pooling into lakes. From the lattice rise slender pillars of the same living stone, spaced farther apart than seems rational for any city. Between them the space is simply empty, a deliberate void hundreds of meters across, yet the settlement claims this emptiness as its primary architecture. Walkways and narrow balconies spiral outward from each pillar at irregular heights, connected by threads of light rather than railings. The lights are not lamps but slow-moving filaments of plasma that crawl along the threads and pulse when moisture beads on them, dimming the chamber further rather than brightening it.

Against the pillars cling dense mats of vegetation whose leaves are darker than the stone. They drink from the weeping rock and from the lattice’s cyclic exhalation, growing in tight spirals that never stray far from their host pillar. The air smells of warm petrichor and something sharper, like ozone just after lightning. No insects drone; nothing moves among the leaves except the slow rotation of the plasma threads and the infrequent drift of stone dust.

Every surface is new. The fractures in the vault are still bright at their edges where the rock split only days or weeks earlier. Nothing has had time to soften. Even the vegetation shows knife-edge growth, each new frond unfurling with geometric precision before it begins to curve. The only motion that repeats is the breathing of the lattice and the measured fall of water beads that strike the floor and are instantly reclaimed.

Sound never stops. The lattice hums a low chord that shifts with each pressure cycle; the plasma threads hiss when they pass through denser pockets of vapor; falling droplets ping and echo until the next cycle overwrites them. The combined result is loud, yet it is the loudness of an instrument being tuned rather than of any living creature. Nothing answers the sound back. The chamber simply continues, recently opened, still settling, already occupied by an urban logic that treats emptiness itself as the central district.
