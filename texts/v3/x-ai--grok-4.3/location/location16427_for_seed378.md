The high plateau stretches wide under a relentless white glare, its surface a single unbroken sheet of dark volcanic glass fractured into shallow, bowl-like depressions. Heat rises from the mineral in slow waves, bending the air so that distant ridges shimmer and dissolve. Despite the temperature, water is everywhere close: it beads constantly on every exposed face, gathering into clear rivulets that trace the low points before sinking again into hairline fissures. The rock itself seems to sweat; at intervals a heavier drop falls from an overhanging ledge and strikes with a single, muffled note that is quickly absorbed by distance.

No green or brown interrupts the palette. Instead, pale mineral salts have leached outward in fan-shaped stains around every seep, and thin crusts of translucent crystal grow along the edges of the rivulets, recording the slow passage of moisture and heat. The ground is firm yet porous; beneath the glass skin, older layers have already weathered into rounded pebbles and fine angular grit that shifts underfoot in dry, soundless trickles.

Scattered across this expanse stand the remnants of an industrial settlement, each structure isolated by hundreds of paces of bare rock. Low, windowless blocks of cast stone and riveted iron rise only one or two storeys, their sides streaked with rust and salt. Narrow channels of open pipe link some of them, carrying condensed water to small cooling basins that steam faintly in the bright light. Between the buildings the plateau remains nearly empty, the eye traveling uninterrupted to the next faint silhouette on the horizon. No roads cross the intervals; only faint, wind-smoothed tracks show where heavy machinery once moved.

Life moves through the spaces between these outposts. Small, translucent creatures the size of fists scuttle across the glass, their bodies formed of the same crystalline salts that coat the rock; they leave momentary trails of darker moisture that evaporate within seconds. Larger forms, ribbed and segmented, rest motionless in the shallow basins, absorbing heat and water through mineral lattices that line their undersides. Their movements are deliberate and nearly silent; only the lightest scraping or the occasional soft expulsion of steam marks their passage. At intervals a vent hidden beneath a ledge releases a plume of warm vapor that drifts a short distance before vanishing, stirring the air into brief, visible eddies that subside as quickly as they formed.

Nothing speaks or calls. The only persistent sounds are the quiet, spaced drips from the weeping ledges and the low, continuous sigh of water seeping through fractures deep inside the plateau. The whole scene feels both settled and unfinished, as though the processes of heat, water, and slow crystallization have reached an equilibrium that will persist until the next shift in the rock or the next arrival of machines.
