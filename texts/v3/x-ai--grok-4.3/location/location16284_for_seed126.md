The surface is an unbroken continental lattice of engineered feldspar and memory-metal, fused at the nanoscale into a single, seamless shell. Over uncounted ages the once-towering geometries have subsided until only the faintest, continent-long undulations remain—low rises and shallow basins whose relief is measured in meters rather than kilometers. Under a cloudless, glaring sky the lattice radiates a steady, dry warmth; fine motes of suspended crystal drift just above the surface, never quite settling, never quite rising, forming slow, silent weather of their own.

Nothing here grew; everything was placed, then left to endure. The same self-replicating substrate that forms the ground also extrudes, at sparse intervals, rigid stalks of translucent silica that flex and realign with each thermal cycle, harvesting the constant light and storing it as minute voltage imbalances. Between these stalks the surface remains empty for kilometers, yet the emptiness is deceptive: beneath the smooth skin, layer upon layer of dormant circuitry and dormant life continue to operate. Occasional pressure fronts—dry cascades of charged particulates—travel across the plain, briefly illuminating dormant traceries that flare and subside like sheet lightning seen through stone.

Movement is rare and deliberate. A single translucent hexapod might appear on the horizon, its legs articulating in absolute silence as it follows an ancient patrol loop; days later it will have advanced only a few body lengths. Where two such machines cross paths they exchange packets of light along whisker-like antennas, then continue without altering course. No vocalization occurs; the only audible phenomena are the low, crystalline stress tones produced when the lattice expands at noon and contracts at dusk—thin, glassy notes that travel for leagues before fading.

Because the entire world is constructed and self-maintaining, erosion has long since reached its terminal state: every fracture is healed, every lost atom is replaced from internal reservoirs. The result is a landscape that feels simultaneously finished and still in motion—vast, warm, densely patterned at every scale from molecule to continent, yet quieter than any natural desert. The rules are simple: light enters, data and charge circulate, nothing is added or removed. The surface continues its slow, bright breathing, patient as stone and as precisely engineered as a clock that has forgotten it was ever wound.
