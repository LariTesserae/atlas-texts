The chamber is small enough that three strides cross it end to end, yet it feels crowded. Every surface—floor, low ceiling, and the curved walls that meet like the inside of a kettle—has been plated in pressed sheet metal the color of old nickel. The seams are still bright where the rivets were driven yesterday or the day before; faint tool marks catch what little light there is. Condensation gathers along the upper plates and drips at irregular intervals onto the lower ones, each drop ringing once before it is absorbed into the thin film of water already moving across the floor toward a narrow channel cut along the baseboard.

Machines stand almost shoulder to shoulder along the walls: squat electric motors coupled to worm drives, pressure vessels no larger than milk cans, and bundles of braided hose that loop and re-loop between fittings. None of them are finished; open access plates reveal coils still wrapped in factory tape, and one motor housing has been left with its armature half-inserted, as though the assembler stepped away mid-task. A single low lamp, its filament dimmed to a dull orange by voltage drop, hangs from the center of the ceiling on a short gooseneck. It throws more shadow than illumination, so the machines appear to lean inward, their unfinished edges almost touching.

Nothing grows here by intention, yet pale threads—perhaps mineral bloom, perhaps some accidental polymer leakage—have begun to web across the coolest corners where pipes meet plates. The threads are sparse, fragile, and the constant cool draft from a grille near the floor keeps them from spreading far. No insects crawl among them; the air is too cold and too filtered for that. The only motion is mechanical or fluid: a slow peristaltic pump pulses somewhere behind a bulkhead, pushing a thin lubricant through transparent tubing; valves tick when pressure equalizes; water droplets continue their patient migration toward the drain. Between these small events the chamber holds a dense, muffled quiet, as though the metal itself is still deciding what sound it will make when it is finally complete.
