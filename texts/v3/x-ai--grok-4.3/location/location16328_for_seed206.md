A low hollow gathers itself beneath an unbroken canopy of broad, vein-thick leaves, the sort that have grown into one another until the space beneath feels like a single breathing chamber. Water beads constantly on the undersides of those leaves and falls in slow, deliberate drops that never quite pool; instead they trace pale channels through the mat of living root-flesh that forms the floor. The ground is neither soil nor stone but a continuous quilt of interlocking rhizomes, some as thick as forearms, others hair-fine, all warm to the touch and faintly throbbing with internal circulation. Between these larger masses the rhizomes thin, leaving irregular clearings of pale, almost translucent membrane through which the slow seep of groundwater is visible—an intimate, never-rising tide.

The air stays warm and motionless, heavy with the smell of chlorophyll and warm sap. Condensation gathers along the curved inner surfaces of the largest leaves and runs in fine rivulets toward the center, where it is reabsorbed by the same tissues that released it. Nothing grows taller or wider; nothing withers. The entire pocket exists at a fixed stage of maturity that has apparently lasted longer than any outside season. Scattered across the clearings stand a few thicker upright stems, each one containing a slow, visible peristalsis: a dark pulse of fluid travels upward, pauses, and returns, repeating the same circuit without variation. The motion produces a low, rhythmic thrum that fills the space like the interior of a vast, soft machine.

Small, iridescent insects and translucent amphibians move through the gaps between the stems, their numbers high yet their individual paths widely spaced. Their wings and limbs beat against the membrane floor and the veined walls, generating a continuous soft clatter that layers over the deeper circulatory pulse. The light that reaches them is ordinary daylight filtered to an even, shadowless gray; it arrives from no visible opening yet never fades or intensifies. The whole arrangement feels both self-contained and embedded, as though this warm, wet, unchanging plant-room were tucked inside a larger body that no longer acknowledges the boundary between itself and what it contains.
