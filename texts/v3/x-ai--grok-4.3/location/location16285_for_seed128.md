This pocket unfolds like a single breath held inside the ribcage of something far larger—an alcove no wider than an outstretched arm, yet choked with presence. Everything within it has been grown or printed or coaxed into place long ago; there is no raw substrate left. The walls and floor are the same seamless continuum of matte, faintly iridescent composite that behaves like cooled magma: smooth enough to slide along yet textured by centuries of micro-abrasion until every surface carries the same softened, almost organic curvature. Water does not fall here so much as gather. It condenses in invisible films along the upper margins, then migrates downward in slow, branching rivulets that weep from hairline seams, turning the lower surfaces dark and slick. The air remains warm, almost skin-temperature, heavy with the smell of wet composite and the faint ozone bite of whatever keeps the micro-climate alive.

Light is scarce and indirect, arriving only as a dull ember glow from thread-thin veins embedded just beneath the surface. Those veins pulse once every few minutes, never bright enough to cast shadows, only enough to pick out the nearest layer of forms. The result is a perpetual half-darkness in which depth collapses; every object seems pressed forward against the retina.

Within that dimness the space is crowded to the point of intimacy. Tiny engineered life-forms—scarcely larger than midges—move in layered sheets: some crawl across the weeping seams drinking condensate, others hover in vibrating clusters whose wings produce a continuous, many-voiced roar that fills the pocket completely. The sound is not layered over the environment; it is the environment. It presses against eardrums, rattles loose droplets from overhangs, and sets the very walls into a sympathetic micro-tremor that feels like breathing. Between the swarms, sparse filaments of engineered growth—more like metallic lichen than true vegetation—cling in single-species mats no thicker than frost, their edges already softened by the same slow erosion that has rounded every manufactured ridge and joint.

Nothing is still. Water migrates along pre-carved capillaries that flex minutely, redistributing moisture according to unseen schedules. The midge-like swarm ebbs and flows in response, a single organism of many bodies. Where the condensate grows too heavy it beads, falls, and is wicked away again through porous lower strata, only to reappear higher up; the cycle is endless and audible, a low, constant percussion beneath the wings. The pocket exists in a state of settled exuberance: every surface worn to its final comfortable shape, every process balanced against every other, yet none of them quiet. The technology sustaining it is older than any language spoken outside its walls, operating at scales where matter and instruction have become indistinguishable. In this small, loud, warm wetness, the distinction between made and grown has long since collapsed into the same living patina.
