The chamber exists at the peak of an artificial rise whose foundations were laid so long ago that even the idea of foundation has worn smooth. From outside it would register only as a low, dark bulk against the sky, but inside it becomes everything. Walls, floor, and ceiling are indistinguishable in material and purpose: a single continuous lattice of hand-shaped mineral blocks, each one fitted to its neighbors with such precision that no seam remains visible. The blocks are the color of cooled volcanic glass, threaded with faint veins of warmer stone that seem to hold the ambient heat. There is no substrate beneath or beyond them; the lattice is the place.

Light arrives in narrow, fixed shafts from apertures too small and high to be called windows. What little illumination reaches the interior is the color of dusk, enough to reveal edges and hollows but not to separate one layer of construction from the next. The temperature stays steadily warm, as though the blocks themselves exhale a constant, dry breath. Nothing moves. Nothing grows. The lattice fills every volume: vertical stacks rise like petrified reeds until they meet horizontal bridges that cross and recross, leaving only the narrowest of capillaries between them—passages just wide enough for one body to slide through sideways, and only if that body never turns.

Every surface carries the same fine, time-polished texture, as though endless hands once pressed and rubbed until no roughness survived. Some blocks are pierced with regular perforations arranged in spirals and grids; others are left solid but scored with shallow channels that align from one course to the next. These channels and holes do not appear to serve any living function. They simply exist, and through their alignment they sustain a single, unwavering resonance—a deep, layered drone that fills the chamber completely and never rises or falls. The sound is not produced by wind or movement; it is the acoustic signature of the lattice itself, locked into its final arrangement. Any new arrival hears it at once as a physical pressure against the skin and bones, yet the pressure never changes and nothing stirs.

There is no water and no trace that water was ever present. The mineral blocks are dry to the touch and hold no moisture. Nothing organic—neither plant nor animal—has ever taken purchase; the surfaces are too uniformly hard and the interstices too exact. Only the blocks remain, locked into an equilibrium that was reached so long ago the chamber registers as ancient even to itself. The entire construct occupies no more volume than a large cathedral, yet within that volume every cubic span is occupied. Nothing is left empty, and nothing is permitted to alter. The warm dark air, the fixed dim light, the ceaseless drone, and the mineral lattice simply continue, each confirming the others without need for progression or decay.
