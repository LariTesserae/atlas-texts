A vast, high plateau of fitted stone rises into thinner air, its horizon a flat line of pale sky broken only by the low angles of distant ridges. Everything here is made: the ground is a seamless pavement of hand-quarried slabs, set without mortar yet so precise that no blade can pass between them. From this pavement rise walls, basins, low towers, and open colonnades, all of the same pale mineral, their edges still crisp because the work is young. Water gathers constantly on every surface. It does not rain; instead the bright, cool light pulls moisture from the air itself, beading along carved ledges and trickling down fluted channels that were cut to guide it. The channels cross and re-cross at right angles, feeding shallow rectangular pools whose surfaces hold perfect reflections of the empty towers.

Between the structures there is no gap, only further construction: narrow stairs that end in alcoves the size of a standing body, cylindrical vents no wider than an arm, blind windows set flush with the stone. The density is total; the eye cannot find a single patch of bare substrate. Yet the scale remains open—the plateau continues for many miles, repeating the same interlocking patterns until they soften at the limit of sight. Light falls hard and clear from above, throwing knife-edge shadows that move only with the slow passage of the sun. Nothing grows. Nothing moves except the water.

Occasionally a larger drop gathers on an overhanging lip and falls into a pool with a single, soft report; after that the quiet returns. The stone itself is faintly translucent in places, and when the light strikes through thin panels it reveals internal striations that continue the surface patterns into the depth of the material. These striations shift over years rather than days, mineral slowly rearranging itself along the lines the builders first cut. Water follows the shifts, finding new routes through hairline seams that were not there a generation earlier. The whole plateau therefore changes, but so gradually that the eye perceives only stillness.

High, cool, saturated with its own constructions, the place holds its silence and its water together beneath the unchanging glare.
