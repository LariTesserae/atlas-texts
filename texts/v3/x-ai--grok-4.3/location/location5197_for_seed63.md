The chamber sits inside the flank of an immense, wind-scoured spire that rises alone from a plateau of fractured stone. Its walls are not rock in the ordinary sense but a single, continuous mass of fused mineral—bands of rose quartz and black tourmaline swirled together like frozen smoke. Over uncounted ages the material has been softened and re-shaped by its own slow heat, so that the once-sharp angles of the cavity have rounded into gentle, overlapping curves. Light never reaches here; the only illumination is a faint internal glow that leaks from seams in the floor where pressurized gas escapes, a dull orange that pulses once every few minutes and then fades.

The air is thick, hot, motionless except where narrow currents rise from those same seams. Nothing grows. Instead the entire volume of the space is occupied by dense, interlocking masses of dark, glassy nodules that have grown directly from the walls and from one another. Each nodule is roughly the size of a clenched fist, faceted, and slightly warm to the touch; when it is disturbed it contracts into itself with a dry, almost inaudible click. These nodules are the fauna—lithic colonies that metabolize dissolved silicates carried upward by the rising heat. They are packed so tightly that in places they form bridges from wall to wall, leaving only narrow, twisting conduits a body must push through. Movement within those conduits is constant but so slow and deliberate that the sound never rises above a faint, continuous whisper of shifting grit.

Scattered among the nodules are older, larger forms: a single slender pillar whose surface has been carved with shallow spirals now almost erased by the same mineral accretion; a low, rectangular platform whose edges have slumped into rounded steps; and, against the farthest curve of the wall, the suggestion of an arch whose keystone has long since fused into the surrounding mass. These remnants are of the same mineral as everything else, only differently arranged. No tools remain, only the shapes they once imposed.

Heat radiates upward from the floor in slow rhythms, causing the upper nodules to expand minutely and then contract again, a breathing motion measured in hours rather than seconds. When the pressure seams brighten, the nodules nearest them turn translucent for a moment, revealing thread-like veins of liquid mineral moving inside. The light fades; the veins darken; the soft, dry shifting resumes. The chamber never empties, never floods, never cools. It simply continues, occupied to its last crevice by the same patient, heat-driven life that first sealed it from the outside world.
