The place exists beneath the skin of the world, inside a network of chambers that opened only recently—sharp ridges still fresh where the rock split, edges that have not yet dulled into curves. Water moves through everything. It weeps from ceiling seams in long, quiet threads, gathers in shallow pools that reflect the light filtering down from distant fissures, and lingers as mist against cold stone. The air itself feels saturated; every surface carries a thin film of condensation, and small droplets form and fall again in slow, regular rhythm.

Lush growth covers every plane. Thick mats of pale moss and cold-adapted vines coat the walls and floor so completely that the original rock is seldom visible. Broad leaves, veined with white and blue, overlap in dense layers, their surfaces beaded with water. Roots and runners cross and re-cross one another, knitting the chambers into a single living mass. There is no gap between one plant and the next; the vegetation fills the space as tightly as mortar.

The temperature stays low enough that breath shows in faint plumes. Ice crystals rim the edges of some pools and glitter along the undersides of overhanging ledges, yet the plants continue to grow, drawing warmth from the constant moisture and from one another. The scale of the place is deceptive: individual chambers feel enclosed, yet openings lead onward in every direction, revealing further halls, terraces, and sunken basins that stretch for distances equivalent to a small city quarter or valley reach.

Fauna moves through this packed fabric. Small, furred creatures thread between leaves and along root bridges, leaving narrow trails that close behind them. Larger forms, low and slow, shift among the deeper foliage, their passage marked only by the soft rustle of displaced stems. Nothing is loud. The dominant sounds are the steady drip of water, the faint creak of expanding roots, and the occasional whisper of wings or scales against wet stone. Even the movement of larger animals stays muted, absorbed by the saturated air and the intervening growth.

No tools or devices exist here. Walls and ceilings have been shaped solely by water, cold, and the pressure of roots forcing stone apart along natural fractures. Where inhabitants have altered the space, they have done so by guiding living growth—training vines into archways, encouraging certain mosses to thicken into platforms—yet the result still feels continuous with the surrounding rock and plant. The whole arrangement remains young; collapse scars are fresh, and new channels are still being carved by trickling water. Life continues its cycles in quiet abundance, water feeding growth, growth holding water, cold preserving the balance inside the open dark beneath the surface.
