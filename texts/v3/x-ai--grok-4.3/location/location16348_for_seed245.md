In the dim half-glow that passes for daylight here, the plain stretches without horizon or curvature, a single low shelf of made matter that could be a continent or the floor of an unfinished planet. Nothing grows; nothing moves of its own accord. The surface is not rock but a continuous skin of fired ceramic panels, each one still faintly tacky at the seams, their edges held together by crude iron staples hammered flush by some earlier hand. Between the panels run shallow gutters no wider than a wrist; from these gutters rises a constant film of condensation that thickens, beads, and finally falls back as droplets the size of coins. The air itself feels pressed, carrying both the metallic bite of fresh kiln-work and the heavier scent of water that has nowhere colder to go.

Far apart—so far that a walker might lose sight of one before the next appears—stand the only vertical things: squat kiln-towers of the same ceramic, their walls still soft enough to take a thumbprint. Some lean at angles that should topple them, yet they hold, because the ground beneath them is still deciding its own slope. Now and then a tower shortens by a hand-span, its upper courses sinking into the softening surface as though the whole structure were being gently re-swallowed; moments later another length extrudes upward in a slow surge of fresh material, the new panels clicking into place with soft, dry reports that echo once and are gone. These changes produce no wind, no tremor felt through the soles, only the quiet stutter of matter rearranging itself.

Heat collects near the surface and does not rise. It gathers in pockets between the panels and in the open mouths of the half-formed kilns, turning the condensing film to steam before it can fall. The steam drifts in low, coherent sheets that never quite dissipate, turning the middle distance into a series of overlapping veils. Through these veils, distances double or halve without warning; a tower that stood three strides wide a moment ago may appear again as a thin line of darkness, then as a lattice of nine towers spaced like fence posts. The eye learns not to trust any single measurement. When the veils part, the same tower is often simply gone, leaving behind only a circular depression already beginning to fill with fresh condensate.

Sound is scarce. The only constant is a faint, high ringing at the limit of hearing—the settling of the ceramic as it cools and reheats in endless succession. Occasional brittle pops mark the sudden appearance of a staple or the collapse of one that has become redundant. Nothing calls, nothing breathes, nothing answers. The turbulence of the place is therefore almost entirely visual: forms emerge, drift, and are revised in silence, like a diagram being redrawn by an unseen hand that cannot decide whether to finish or begin again. Across the plain the same process repeats at every scale, so that the horizon itself may be only the next iteration of the same unsettled seam.
