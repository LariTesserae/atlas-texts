The plain stretches without interruption, an immensity so complete that its own horizons feel like slow, internal weather. Underfoot the stone has been reduced by unthinkable time to a single unbroken membrane, neither rock nor soil but the final residue of both, polished until it accepts no new mark. Water lies everywhere and nowhere: it seeps upward through hairline fissures in endless clear beads, it condenses in the warm air until the light itself seems to bead and run, it gathers into low sheets that slide across the stone without ever deepening into true pools. The sheets migrate in slow, purposeful rivers only a few centimeters high, their edges fraying and re-forming so that the ground is never truly dry and never truly submerged.

Nothing grows tall. Patches of dark, almost metallic lichen cling in the shallowest depressions, each colony separated by leagues of bare membrane; between them rise occasional transparent filaments no thicker than spider-silk that catch the dim light and quiver though no wind touches them. These filaments appear, persist for minutes or hours, and then retract into the stone as if the plain were breathing them back in.

The air is saturated with more than moisture. Space itself feels occupied: translucent shards drift at every height, each the size of a fallen leaf or a distant tower, edges ragged where they have torn from some larger continuum. They rotate, dissolve, and re-coalesce without pattern, sometimes passing through one another, sometimes locking momentarily into lattices that hum before shattering again. The plain is therefore never empty even when it appears vacant; every direction is cross-hatched with overlapping transparencies whose constant re-arrangement produces a low, continuous pressure against the skin.

Sound arrives from every layer at once. The sliding water sheets whisper and click against the stone; the drifting shards emit thin, glassy chimes when they shear; the membrane underfoot answers each distant impact with a sub-audible groan that travels for hundreds of kilometers. All of it is magnified until individual textures collapse into a single unbroken roar, a white noise so total that silence has become unimaginable. Within the roar, fragments occasionally cohere into something like voices or storms or the cracking of bone, only to dissolve back into undifferentiated thunder.

No animals move across the open stone; the few that exist do so inside the fissures or within the temporary shards themselves, their forms glimpsed only as momentary distortions when a shard passes overhead. Scattered across the plain are the last, unreadable traces of earlier presence: low rings of fused glass where something once concentrated heat, shallow depressions whose rims have been softened into perfect circles by the patient sheets of water, and, rarer still, upright blades of the same ancient membrane standing a hand-span high, their edges already beginning to round.

The light never brightens. It arrives filtered through layers of migrating vapor and drifting shard, arriving as a slow, amber half-glow that makes every surface appear farther away than it is. Changes occur without warning: a region of membrane may blister upward into a momentary hill, shed a swarm of new transparent filaments, and subside again within minutes; elsewhere a sheet of water may invert without losing contact with the stone, becoming a ceiling of still liquid a meter overhead before it falls back as soundless rain. These events leave no lasting trace. The plain absorbs them, flattens them, and continues its enormous, eroded hush beneath the permanent, deafening churn of its own becoming.
