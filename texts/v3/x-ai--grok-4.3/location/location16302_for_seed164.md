The high plateau stretches without boundary, a single unbroken tier of rock that rises from the world’s deeper layers like a shelf left unfinished by whatever process once carved the planet. Its surface is not ancient stone worn smooth; instead, the strata stand fresh, their edges knife-clean, facets sharp enough that light catches them in hard white lines. Between these ridges lie intervals of nothing—kilometers of flat, open stone where the eye finds no interruption, only the faint refraction of air that has never learned to hold dust.

Across that emptiness, industrial districts sit at irregular distances, each one a dense knot of towers, conduits, and foundries. Pipes the color of oxidized copper loop between the clusters like frozen rivers; their joints are newly welded, bright beads of metal still visible beneath the inevitable film of condensed moisture. Every district runs continuously—pistons driving in measured cadence, turbines emitting a single sustained chord that travels outward across the plateau. Because the intervals between settlements are so wide, the chord does not fade; it simply joins the next identical chord, forming one unbroken note that fills the entire shelf. Nothing interrupts it. Nothing answers it.

Water never falls as rain. Instead it gathers on every upward-facing plane—on the roofs of the factories, on the tops of the sharp rock fins, on the broad leaves of the vegetation that grows only at the margins of the structures. The vegetation is dense wherever it occurs, a matted weave of broad, rubbery fronds and low, branching shrubs whose stems carry a faint internal phosphorescence, visible even in ordinary daylight. The plants do not sway; they hold their positions exactly, leaves beaded with droplets that roll inward along veins and disappear into the stone at the base. The cold is steady, low enough that breath clouds briefly before thinning into the same motionless air.

No tracks cross the open stone between districts. No wings disturb the condensation that continually reforms on every surface. The only motion is the slow, internal cycling of the machines themselves, their rhythms locked so that each stroke occurs at the precise interval required to keep the overall sound unchanging. The plateau therefore contains both vast emptiness and permanent occupation, both industrial density and the silence of absolute distance, both cold and the constant presence of water that never freezes or evaporates. Over centuries the arrangement has remained identical: the same sharp ridges, the same separated cities, the same low, unvarying note traveling outward across stone that still looks newly formed.
