A vast, motionless plateau lies submerged beneath kilometers of cool, weightless water, its elevation so enormous that it forms a hidden continent suspended in the dark blue middle-depths. Here the ocean is not empty: it is entirely filled by a single, unbroken organism of plant tissue that extends in every direction for horizons beyond sight. Towering columns of rigid, leathery fronds rise hundreds of meters, their surfaces veined with glassy conduits that slowly pulse water and dissolved minerals. These conduits branch and rejoin in perfect, unchanging patterns, forming vaulted galleries and narrow, echoing shafts through which light from the distant surface arrives as steady, pale shafts.

Between the great stems the plant-mass is thinner, leaving wide, drifting volumes of clearer water—spaces large enough for schools of slow-moving creatures to pass without touching the living walls. The creatures themselves are abundant yet oddly still: translucent, many-limbed forms that drift along fixed routes, their bodies etched with fine mineral lattices they have grown rather than built. They never stray from their corridors. Nothing hunts, nothing breeds, nothing dies; the entire arrangement has reached a state in which every flow of water, every exchange of gases, and every slow ticking of internal valves maintains one another in absolute balance.

The only movement is hydraulic. At regular intervals the giant conduits constrict and release, producing a continuous low thunder of water forced through narrow throats and across ridged diaphragms—sound like distant turbines or the ceaseless exhalation of enormous engines. The temperature remains the same at every depth and season, a heavy, metallic coolness that never rises or falls. Because nothing decays and nothing grows anew, the plant architecture shows no gaps, no collapse, no fresh shoots; every surface is etched and scoured to the same smooth, settled finish, as though the entire plateau has existed unchanged for the age of the ocean itself.

Light falls evenly through the upper canopy, neither bright nor dim, revealing the endless repetition of column, conduit, and drifting corridor. Around the edges of each open volume the water occasionally eddies into slow, standing vortices where mineral particles glitter like suspended dust, then settle again into perfect stillness. Nothing arrives from elsewhere; nothing leaves. The plateau simply continues, a single living machine of plant and water turning over on itself in unchanging, thunderous quiet.
