The valley floor spreads wide under a perpetual half-light, a low basin where the horizon never quite arrives. Fresh stone still carries the crisp edges of its formation—jagged ridges and splintered blocks that look as though the ground only recently decided to hold still. Between these sharp planes, broad plazas stretch empty for hundreds of meters before another cluster of structures appears, each group separated by long intervals of open, faintly steaming ground. The stone sweats in the warmth; tiny beads of condensation gather on every upward surface and then slide into hairline channels that feed shallow, intermittent pools no deeper than a hand’s breadth. The pools never connect into rivers; they simply appear, linger, and evaporate again within an hour or two, leaving behind a faint mineral film that glitters like frost before it dries.

Vegetation pushes up from the cracks in deliberate, isolated stands—broad-leaved canopies and hanging curtains of fronds that glow with their own low bioluminescence. The leaves are thick and waxy, almost rubbery, and they trap the dimness beneath them, creating pockets of deeper shadow where moisture lingers longest. Yet between each stand lies empty pavement or bare, newly set rock, so the overall impression remains one of deliberate spacing rather than crowding. The warmth keeps the air soft and slightly heavy; droplets form on the undersides of leaves and fall with soft, spaced plinks that echo farther than they should across the open plazas.

The city itself is present but never dense. Slender towers rise in widely separated clusters, their façades composed of materials that refuse ordinary description—surfaces that appear matte until light shifts across them, then reveal microscopic lattices shifting in coordinated waves. Walkways and platforms hover a few centimeters above the ground without visible support, adjusting their height in slow, almost imperceptible increments as though breathing with the moisture cycle. Occasionally a tower will unfold a new level or retract one, the motion soundless except for a faint, collective sigh of displaced air. These changes register as geological rather than mechanical, another layer of the same young landscape still settling into itself.

Light remains low and diffuse, as though the sky is permanently veiled by a thin film of its own making. No shadows are sharp; everything blends into a continuous half-tone. In this dimness, faint motes drift upward from the mineral films left by evaporating pools—nanoscale structures released by the condensation cycle, rising in slow, almost contemplative spirals before dissipating again.

Fauna is scarce and nearly invisible. Once in a long interval a translucent filament drifts across a plaza, contracting and expanding like a single nerve fiber tasting the air. It leaves no track and makes no sound. The dominant presence is the quiet itself: the soft, rhythmic drip of condensation, the almost inaudible vibration of the hovering walkways, and the low, steady pulse of the living rock as it continues its slow redistribution of mass. Rain does not fall in sheets; instead the atmosphere occasionally thickens, producing a fine, directionless mist that coats every surface and then withdraws again, feeding the scattered pools and the patient vegetation without ever breaking the prevailing hush.

The entire basin feels half-formed and half-aware, an urban organism still testing its own architecture while water and warmth move through it in patient, repeating patterns. Nothing hurries. Nothing crowds. Yet everything continues to adjust, layer by nearly invisible layer, beneath the constant dimness.
