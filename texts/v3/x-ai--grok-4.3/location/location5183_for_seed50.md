A hidden pocket of primordial ocean exists high within a raw, half-formed massif of black crystal and cooling stone that has not yet decided what shape it will take. The entire microenvironment occupies the volume of a single vast chamber, perhaps large enough to hold a cathedral yet small enough that a swimmer could cross it in a few dozen strokes if the currents allowed. From outside it is undetectable; only a raw fracture in the mountain’s flank reveals the interior when one already knows the way.

Water fills every crevice, dense and cool, yet it is not still. Violent, localized gyres twist and slam against the unfinished walls, the turbulence so constant that the liquid itself seems to be sculpting the geology rather than the reverse. Walls of water rise and collapse within arm’s length of one another, smashing against embryonic ledges and half-extruded crystal spines that have only begun to grow. The sound is a single, unbroken roar—impact, cavitation, and the low groan of stone still deciding whether to fracture or flow. Normal daylight filters through a translucent membrane of mineral at the pocket’s upper limit, turning the churning water the color of weak tea and making every bubble flash like a small sun before it bursts.

Between the vortices, the water is nearly empty. Vast clear volumes separate the few solid presences: a single drifting ribbon of pale, almost transparent vegetation that looks more like frozen breath than plant matter; a scatter of fist-sized, hand-shaped nodules of polished stone that may once have been tools or may simply be the next stage of the mountain’s slow self-assembly; and, very rarely, a pale, translucent creature no larger than a hand that jets between safe pockets of relative calm, its body nothing but a membrane and a thread of darker tissue. The ceiling and floor remain unfinished. New mineral is still precipitating in slow curtains that the currents instantly tear apart again, so the space feels both ancient and only a few heartbeats old.

The atmosphere—if it can be called that inside the water—is one of perpetual becoming. Nothing has settled into its final form. Currents carve temporary canyons that are erased moments later; the sparse life clings to momentary eddies and must constantly relocate; the few artifacts drift until they lodge in a new crevice and begin to be incorporated or re-dissolved. To remain here is to feel the world still negotiating its own dimensions, sound, and scale inside a chamber only the mountain remembers is there.
