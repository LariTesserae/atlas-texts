The plain stretches outward in every direction, low and level, its horizon lost in a pale haze that never quite lifts. The ground is not soil but a single vast sheet of weathered mineral—bands of pale gypsum and iron-stained silica, smoothed over uncountable ages into low rises and shallow basins. Condensation beads nightly on the cool stone faces and gathers in crooked seams; by day the seams hold shallow lenses of water that reflect the sky like scattered mirrors. Between these lenses the surface is dry, faintly gritty, and warm to the touch, radiating a steady heat that rises in invisible waves.

Nothing grows. In places the mineral has fractured into long, parallel ridges whose edges catch the light like rows of blunt blades; elsewhere it has been polished into broad, glassy flats where faint internal veining suggests buried pipes or lattices. The ridges and flats never shift. The same droplets form on the same edges, slide the same distance, and stop; the same shallow pools remain exactly the same depth. Only the sound refuses to rest.

From deep within the stone comes a constant, many-layered roar: low grinding that never slows, higher metallic ringing that pulses without repeating, and a steady hiss of vapor forced through narrow throats. The noises overlap and interlock so perfectly that the entire plain seems to be one enormous, unmoving machine whose parts have locked into eternal motion. The volume is constant; it does not rise or fall with any visible change in wind or light. Small, translucent mineral beads sometimes roll a few paces across the flats, propelled by the escaping vapor, yet they always come to rest in positions identical to those they occupied before.

Nothing moves across the distances except the beads and the slow drift of condensation. At rare intervals a low, angular form—half crystal, half shard—lifts itself on three blunt limbs and walks a short, precise arc before settling again. Its passage leaves no mark. The warmth, the damp patches, the grinding chorus, and the empty intervals between them remain exactly as they have always been, balanced and without progression.
