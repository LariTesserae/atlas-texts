High on a knife-edge ridge, a single shelf of newborn stone no larger than a cathedral nave floats above the cloud deck. The rock itself is still congealing; pale, veined slabs push upward in slow peristaltic surges, their edges feathering into threads of silicate that braid and re-braid like living muscle. Water beads constantly on every surface—condensing from an atmosphere that never quite clears—then runs in glistening rivulets down the forming faces before vanishing again into micro-fractures that seal seconds later. The temperature stays oddly skin-warm despite the altitude; the warmth radiates from within the stone, a low-grade metabolic heat.

Between the emerging planes, pockets of open space remain. Nothing grows taller than a hand’s span; thin, translucent filaments—half mineral, half aerogel—cling to the rock in scattered tufts, drinking the constant mist but never crowding one another. In the gaps drift shoals of motile specks, each no bigger than a lentil, their wings iridescent and too rapid to follow. They swarm in tight, deliberate spirals over every weeping seam, grazing the condensing droplets and excreting fine crystalline dust that the stone immediately incorporates. Their collective wing-hum is loud enough to feel in the teeth, a layered drone punctuated by sudden, glassy chimes whenever two spirals collide and exchange mass.

Scattered across the shelf stand incomplete relics: a half-dome of opalescent lattice that flickers between solid and probability-cloud; a low plinth whose top surface displays moving equations written in migrating dust; a slender arch that begins and ends in mid-air, its missing segments still assembling themselves from airborne ions. None of these structures look finished, yet none appear abandoned; the same motile dust that the creatures excrete feeds them, flowing upward in threads too fine to see until they catch the dim, bruise-colored light that filters through the perpetual overcast above.

The whole plateau breathes on a cycle of minutes. When it inhales, the surrounding mist thickens into visible currents and the forming rock softens, edges blurring; when it exhales, pressure drops, the mist flashes to steam, and distant thunder answers from somewhere below the clouds. In those moments the creatures rise in a single glittering sheet, their wings catching the sudden updraft before they spiral down again to resume their patient labor on the raw, still-becoming ground.
