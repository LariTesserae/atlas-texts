The place is a single vesicle of hardened mineral folded inside something larger, though the containing skin is never visible. From within, it presents as a high, open table of fused silicates that stretch without horizon, yet every step taken returns the walker to the same shallow basin after a few dozen paces. The rock itself is pale and finely crazed, its surface etched by long, slow abrasion into rounded terraces and low ribs no higher than a handspan. These forms have reached equilibrium: the same curves repeat at every scale, descending into microscopic fluting too fine for touch to register.

Light arrives as a single, unchanging wash the color of tarnished nickel, arriving from no direction. It is enough to read the grain of the stone but not enough to cast a true shadow. Temperature sits just above the point where breath would condense, a dry, penetrating chill that leaves minerals brittle yet never lets frost form. No moisture exists anywhere; the chemistry of the plane runs on different bonds, perhaps on lattice defects or long-chain mineral polymers that shift only across centuries.

Scattered across the terraces are small intrusions of engineered matter. Some appear as hairline filaments of darker crystal that terminate in nodes no larger than seeds; others are shallow depressions whose edges are perfectly circular and whose depths fall away into optical paradox—bottomless from one angle, only a hand’s breadth from another. These remnants are not ruined; they are simply no longer performing whatever function once required them to be whole. Their surfaces record faint interference patterns that slide across the visible spectrum when the slow light changes its angle by the smallest fraction.

Between them the plateau is nearly empty. Distances open without effort. A single crystalline blade might stand thirty paces from its nearest neighbor, both of them identical down to the last fracture line. The emptiness is not barrenness but a deliberate spacing, as though the mineral matrix itself refuses denser occupation. Changes occur, but only at the scale of the entire formation: a thread of darker mineral lengthens by a molecule every few decades; a circular depression contracts by the width of a wavelength over the same interval.

At the periphery of awareness the rock sometimes thins. A walker who stares too long at any single terrace may notice its edges beginning to repeat, the same arrangement of ridges appearing again, slightly rotated, as though the plane were tiling itself into identical copies that fail to match at the seams. These mismatches do not open into elsewhere; they simply stop being there, leaving a brief interval of absolute non-being before the next faithful copy continues. The effect produces no sound. The only audible events are occasional, almost sub-audible clicks as a lattice defect migrates a single atomic spacing, or the soft grind of two mineral faces settling against each other after another year of mutual pressure.

Nothing grows, nothing moves under its own power, and nothing arrives from outside. The pocket remains exactly as large as it needs to be to contain the present arrangement of stone and engineered residue, and no larger. Over durations measured in rotations of distant stars, the whole vesicle will finish its present configuration and become another, equally sparse and equally self-similar.
