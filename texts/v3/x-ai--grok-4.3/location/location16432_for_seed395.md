The expanse is a single, unbroken organism-city that carpets a low, planetary flatland. From horizon to horizon the ground rises only in gentle, almost imperceptible swells, each swell the slow heave of living tissue rather than rock. What passes for streets are narrow, winding fissures between towering, sessile growths whose trunks and buttresses have fused wall-to-wall; there is no empty space left, only the continuous, saturated fabric of the city-organism itself.  

These growths are not trees in any familiar sense. Their outer shells are plates of woody chitin laid down in regular, rectilinear courses that mimic masonry, while their inner architectures are networks of vascular bundles that swell and firm over decades into chambers, galleries, and load-bearing columns. The newest tissue is still soft and pale; older districts have darkened to leathery browns and deep umbers as successive generations of the same organism overgrow and harden what came before. Because the underlying mineral crust is still young and only partially lithified, the living mass is simultaneously foundation and superstructure, slowly secreting calcareous binders that turn soft sediment into something stone-like beneath the weight of the city above it.  

Moisture never pools. Every surface is covered in minute, hair-like absorptive filaments that harvest the faint night-time humidity and channel it inward through a capillary web; the result is a landscape that appears lush—broad, leathery fronds and curtains of photosynthetic tissue in saturated greens, ochres, and indigos—yet remains entirely dry to the touch. Temperature stays within a narrow warm band, enough to keep metabolic rates steady but never high enough to desiccate the outer membranes. Light falls normally, filtered only by the highest canopies into a steady, leaf-dappled glow that reaches even the lowest fissures.  

Fauna moves through this saturated matrix without sound. Legions of soft-bodied grazers, no larger than thumbs, glide along cytoskeletal rails grown into the walls; larger, jointed browsers drift from level to level on gliding membranes, their footfalls muffled by the velvety outer cuticle of the host organism. Predation occurs through slow encirclement and chemical dissolution rather than sudden strikes. The only audible phenomena are occasional, almost subsonic creaks as older structural plates settle and re-knit, or the dry whisper of thousands of tiny spiracles opening and closing in unison with the daily metabolic pulse.  

The entire formation is actively self-assembling. Budding nodes appear overnight along existing fissures, extruding fresh scaffolding that will become new “buildings” within a few seasons. Older sectors undergo quiet apoptosis, their tissues softening and collapsing inward to be reabsorbed, leaving temporary voids that are already being colonized by adjacent growth. In this way the city-organism maintains its packed density while still expanding across the low plain, a single, continent-scale biological conurbation whose geology, architecture, and ecology remain indistinguishable from one another.
