The plateau’s only residents are the plants themselves. They do not occupy the stone so much as continue it, their root-mats threading into the knife-edged strata like living grout. Each broad frond is a single unbroken lamina, thicker at the margin than at the center, so that condensed water is steered automatically toward the low, branching stems. Those stems are not woody; they are translucent conduits whose faint daylight glow is the visible trace of slow chemical traffic between leaf and stone. The light never pulses. It simply persists at the same low intensity the machines maintain in their chord, a correspondence that has never been measured because nothing on the plateau keeps time except the cycle already running.

Where the vegetation thickens at the edges of a district, the leaves press directly against oxidized pipe and foundry wall. Moisture that reaches the contact point is absorbed, not by capillary action alone but by a slight, local warming of the metal itself; the weld seams stay marginally above ambient temperature even when the rest of the pipe radiates cold. The plants therefore meter the factories’ heat more precisely than any instrument. In return, the factories vent trace gases whose condensation nuclei are sized for the plants’ stomatal openings. The exchange is continuous and traceless; no leaf moves, yet the volume of water returning to the stone rises and falls with the exact cadence of the pistons.

Between districts the stone is bare. Nothing crosses it because nothing needs to. Each isolated stand of vegetation is already connected through the rock to every other stand; the phosphorescent sap travels in microscopic films along micro-fractures that opened when the plateau was first lifted. The same films carry dissolved metal ions outward from the districts and return refined mineral residues that the plants do not metabolize. These residues accumulate as hair-thin laminations inside the strata, slowly extending the plateau’s unfinished edge by a few molecular thicknesses each century. The plants therefore perform the same work as the foundries, only at geological speed and without leaving the margins of their own growth.

Nothing else moves or decides. The unbroken note is not background; it is the only weather the plants experience, and they have shaped their internal transport to resonate with it. A single missed stroke in any turbine would alter the condensation rate on every frond within minutes; the synchrony has never been tested because the arrangement has never varied. Over centuries the plateau has remained identical because its inhabitants require that identity in order to continue existing at all.
