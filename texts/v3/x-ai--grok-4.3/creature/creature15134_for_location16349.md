In this suspended room of stone and mist, life has been pared to its most enduring forms. The cushion plants form the true residents—low, patient domes of interlocking stems and leaves that have spent centuries learning the exact angle at which a single night’s condensation will bead and linger before rolling inward to the pooled depressions. Each mat is both individual and part of a larger weave; where one colony ends, another begins a body-length away, their boundary marked by a hair-thin seam of exposed rock where roots have already begun their microscopic prying. The leaves themselves carry a faint internal iridescence, not from pigment alone but from the way their waxy cuticle catches and holds the light that filters through the brief daily veil of mist, turning the green momentarily silver before the mist thins again.

Between these emerald islands the black lichen paints its own slower claim, a dust-fine crust that records the passage of every condensing drop and every grain of wind-driven gravel. It needs almost nothing, yet it alters the color of the entire floor, shifting the basin’s perceived temperature from pale stone to something darker, more receptive to the thin warmth that arrives only at midday.

The segmented travelers—flat, jointed bodies the precise shade of water-darkened slate—move like living punctuation between these fixed stations. Their motion is tied strictly to the moment vapor begins to gather on every horizontal surface; they emerge from the narrow fissures beneath the tilted slab clusters, drawn by the same moisture that the plants hoard. Each creature pauses at the rim of a circular depression, drinking with the same unhurried rhythm that governs everything else here, then continues its circuit. Their passage leaves nothing visible save a faint, reversible polish on the gravel and the soft, dry rasp of chitin against stone that threads through the low chord of the wind. They do not disturb the cushions; the plants seem to register them only as another source of incremental shadow, another brief interruption in the long settling of light.

Nothing else moves by choice. The mountain’s final posture—the clustered slabs themselves—functions as shelter rather than habitat, their channels guiding stray trickles downward into the unseen galleries where the deeper booms originate. Life here does not colonize so much as it consents to remain, held in place by the same slow arithmetic of frost, wind, and evaporation that continues to rearrange the basin even as it shelters what little has learned to stay.
