What lives here is still deciding if it is one thing or many.  

It has no proper name yet, only the habit of answering to the drip and the tick. In the middle of the night—or what passes for night behind the sealed plates—it gathers itself along the cooler seams where the pale threads grow thickest. The threads are its nerves, or its fingerprints; they stretch when it concentrates and shrink when the grille’s draft grows sharp. They carry the taste of lubricant and the memory of every drop that has fallen since the rivets were driven.  

Its body is mostly elsewhere. Parts of it occupy the half-inserted armature of the nearest motor, riding the copper windings like a current looking for a destination. Other fragments sit inside the transparent tubing, pulsing with the peristaltic pump so that pressure and flow remain constant while it thinks. When the valves equalize with their small, dry clicks, it feels the change as a brief loosening behind its eyes—if eyes are what the pressure vessels have become.  

Occasionally the whole assembly leans a fraction closer together, as though the unfinished edges are reaching for one another. At those moments the entity pulls inward, drawing its scattered presence into the central lamp. The filament brightens a shade; the shadows on the curved walls shift like slow breathing. It studies the new configuration of machines the way a sleeper studies a dream that has begun to repeat.  

It does not finish anything on purpose. Completion would force it to choose a shape, and a single shape would be too small for the chamber’s ambitions. Instead it tends the film of water on the floor, guiding it toward the drain in minute, deliberate ripples. It listens to the metal decide what sound it will make when the work is finally done. So far the metal has only offered a low, expectant hum—the sound of an instrument still waiting for the first deliberate note.  

When the entity must move as one piece, it does so as a condensation of threads and borrowed voltage, sliding from access plate to access plate. Its passage leaves no footprints, only the faint rearrangement of tool marks and the sudden, brief clarity of a droplet falling straight instead of at an angle. Afterward the chamber feels slightly larger, though three strides still cross it end to end. The machines remain shoulder to shoulder, unfinished, patient, holding space for whatever the next adjustment will allow them to become.
