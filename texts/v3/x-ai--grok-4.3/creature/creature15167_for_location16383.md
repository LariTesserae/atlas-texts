In this changeless expanse the only persistent presence is not a population but a solitary phenomenon that visits like a forgotten equation returning to solve itself.  

A membrane, thinner than any living tissue yet tougher than any mineral facet it crosses, drifts at the height of a person’s waist or sometimes lower, sometimes skimming the needle tips. It is not one color but the absence of color until light strikes it at the precise angle where the ionized gas refracts through its folded surfaces; then it flashes for an instant into a shifting spectrum of pale violets and liquid silvers before vanishing again into transparency. Its shape is never fixed: sometimes a perfect disk two strides across, sometimes an elongated ribbon that threads between lattices, sometimes a sphere that collapses into a saddle and reforms without ever tearing. Where it passes, the noble-gas ions momentarily thicken into visible threads that spiral inward and are consumed; the crystals themselves remain untouched, though for a few heartbeats after its passage their edges glow with a faint internal luminescence, as if acknowledging an old debt.

It does not feed continuously. Between feedings it simply exists, coasting on the minute pressure gradients created by the overlapping space-folds. When two gravitational inversions intersect in its path it rises or sinks without effort, adjusting its own curvature so that the brief reversal of entropy inside it registers only as a single, perfectly circular ripple that travels outward and is gone. It leaves nothing behind—no residue, no warmth, no alteration of the facet angles. The plain erases even the memory of its transit within moments; only the crystals retain the infinitesimal record in the newest atomic layer they quietly add.

No other form appears. There are no colonies, no offspring, no seasonal gatherings. The membrane arises once in an unmeasurable span, lingers for subjective hours or days, then thins until its edges become indistinguishable from the surrounding refracted light and it is no longer present. Its departure is not an event but a gradual restoration of the original stillness, as if the plain has exhaled a single, perfect breath and is once more holding it.
