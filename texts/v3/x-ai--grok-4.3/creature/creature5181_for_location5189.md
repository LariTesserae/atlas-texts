In that cupped-hand hollow the life does not arrive; it accumulates. The same drifting filaments that first read as mist thicken where light-collecting membranes meet, drawing themselves into slender, jointed stalks no thicker than a hair. Each stalk ends in a translucent blade that opens only after the current has brushed it from the same side for several slow hours. When the blade finally stiffens, the stalk beneath it darkens by a single shade, as though the plant has remembered the pressure and chosen to keep it. These stalks grow in dense, overlapping constellations; where two meet they do not compete but braid, their cells softening and then re-hardening around one another until the pair functions as a single, slightly wider conduit for whatever faint sugars the cool light can coax into being.

Some of these braids continue downward until they press against the ribbed impressions already sunken into the root walls. The impressions are not vacant. A pale, finger-length lobe of tissue still occupies each one, its surface etched with the same concentric ridges as the surrounding root. The lobe pulses once every few minutes, not with blood but with a slow shift of internal water that exactly matches the rhythm of the pocket’s rare spore releases. While it pulses, the ridge lines around it deepen by a fraction, as though the root is reading the lobe’s shape into itself. In time the lobe will be gone, its outline left behind as another faint negative; the root wall will have grown one more record of a visitor that elected, without hurry, to remain as topography.

Between the stalks and lobes drift smaller entities still more difficult to separate from the water. They are not animals. They are loose constellations of the same translucent cells, held together only by surface tension and the habit of aligning their long axes with the almost-imperceptible current. When one such constellation brushes a living frond it does not eat or injure; instead its outermost cells flatten and adhere, adding themselves to the frond’s edge like fresh pages slipped into a book that is still being written. The addition registers as a momentary brightening of the cool phosphorescence, then the new cells darken to match their neighbors and the constellation is one cell fewer.

Occasionally a shift in the pocket’s geometry carries a whole stand of stalks behind an observer or repositions two root clusters so that their impressions now face each other. The living tissue inside those impressions does not startle; it simply continues its measured pulse, the water inside each lobe redistributing itself to the new orientation as though direction were merely another negotiable trait, like translucency or the decision to remain attached. Nothing here finishes becoming. Even the spores, once released, continue for minutes to adjust the precise angle at which their membranes will finally stiffen, as though each spore were still consulting the water about the shape it might prefer once it arrives—wherever arriving is understood to be a process rather than an event.
