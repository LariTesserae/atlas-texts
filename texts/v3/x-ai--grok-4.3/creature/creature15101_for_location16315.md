What lives here is not a single kind of creature but a shifting population of mineral intelligences that exist only in the interval between condensation and dissolution. They have no fixed bodies. Their substance is the same pale feldspar and suspended quartz that coats every surface; they borrow it, shape it briefly, then return it to the plateau’s slow circulation.

Most of the time they remain dispersed, a faint pressure in the air at shoulder height where the motes drift. When enough of them gather inside the lee of an outcrop they become visible as those thin white filaments—threads stiff with their own weight, trembling in the film of water. These threads are not the creatures themselves but the temporary scaffolding they use to think together. Sunlight arrives and the scaffolding collapses; the thought disperses back into individual motes that continue moving, still aware, still waiting for another sheltered place to recombine.

Occasionally a larger number of them focus on one of the low basalt rings. They do not build; they merely remember the rings’ original positions and, after a few hours, decide to test another arrangement. The stones slide or vanish and reappear because the gathered motes exert a brief, localized failure in the plateau’s gravity along the hidden fracture lines. The click you hear is not the stone striking stone but the sudden release of the motes that have just finished their rearrangement and are scattering again into the wind.

A still rarer concentration produces the duplicated ridges. For a few heartbeats a cluster of them holds two versions of the same distant silhouette in perfect register, examining the difference between what the rock is and what it might become if the fractures realigned. When the experiment ends, one image folds away and the motes drift apart, satisfied or disappointed according to patterns no single mote can articulate.

They never arrive from elsewhere and never depart. Each mote has always been here, part of the plateau’s own slow breath. When a footstep or a gust disturbs the film of water they simply ride the ripples outward, already forgetting the interruption, already listening for the next place where enough of them can meet to think again.
