In the intervals between the towers, the slow jointed forms continue their measured traversals. Each is a single unbroken body of the same dense amalgam as the plain, though its surfaces show a finer grain, as if the mineral had been compelled to arrange itself along countless parallel filaments that allow precise flexion at every joint. These articulations never grind; they simply realign, the facets sliding past one another until the required angle is restored and then locking with the same soft, single click that is felt more than heard. When one of them stops, its segments settle into the exact geometry needed for perfect stillness, and for hours it could be mistaken for another fixed framework until, without preamble or acceleration, it resumes its line.

They do not appear to steer. Their paths are predetermined by the same equilibrium that fixed the plain itself; they simply follow the invisible rulings that run between the distant sheds. Occasionally two such forms approach each other on crossing lines. They do not slow or acknowledge one another. At the calculated instant their nearest segments rotate just enough to clear, then rotate back, and the forms continue outward until curvature reclaims them.

A rarer sort moves on a different scale. These are smaller, low assemblages that travel only within the largest sheds. They consist of stacked crystalline plates connected by short, dense rods that extend or retract by minute degrees. Their motion is almost imperceptible; over long observation one notices that a plate which had faced inward now faces slightly outward, or that an entire low cluster has shifted a hand’s breadth closer to a turning flywheel. They leave no residue. When one plate finishes its adjustment it becomes indistinguishable from the surrounding stillness until the next imperceptible shift occurs.

Nothing is born among them, yet new forms appear at intervals so vast that the interval itself has become part of the architecture. Where two filaments cross for centuries without fluctuation, a fresh jointed shape eventually detaches from the intersection, already aligned to a fresh straight line, already moving at the established pace. The plain does not register the event as change; it registers only the continuation of the same mineral distribution, now occupying one additional set of coordinates.

These entities, if they can be called that, do not gather. They do not communicate by any outward sign. Each maintains its prescribed vector or its prescribed adjustment until the configuration of the whole requires otherwise. In the intervals between their motions the plateau remains exactly as it has remained: warm, motionless, occupied by the same slow, jointed presences that have always been there and will always be there, until mineral equilibrium itself is superseded by something the plain has never recorded.
