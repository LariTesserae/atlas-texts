The chamber is inhabited by a sparse population of lithic symbiotes—tall, narrow beings whose bodies are grown rather than born. Their skeletons are extensions of the same living basalt that forms the pillars, their skin a continuous sheet of the dark, spiraling vegetation that drinks from the weeping rock. Because they are so few, and because they spend most of their long lives motionless against their chosen pillar, the chamber’s emptiness remains almost absolute. When one of them does move, it is only after a pressure cycle has completed; the lattice flexes, the individual detaches with a faint tearing sound like wet slate, and walks along a chosen thread of plasma. The plasma dims respectfully as the body passes, registering the brief density the way a seismograph registers a distant footfall.

They communicate almost entirely through the existing acoustics. A single phrase may take hours to shape, each syllable formed by the controlled release of a droplet from a fingertip or the slow brushing of a frond against the membrane floor, so that the resulting series of pings and harmonics carries exact meaning across the entire volume. Because the space is so large and so absorbent of its own sound, every utterance is also a public record; nothing said is ever lost until the next pressure cycle overwrites it. Consequently, most of them choose to remain silent for decades at a time, listening instead to the slow tuning of the chamber itself.

Their city therefore consists of three concentric absences. The first is the void between pillars. The second is the long intervals between movements. The third is the stillness they maintain even while present, their bodies indistinguishable from the pillar until they choose otherwise. Only during the rare moments when several of them converge on adjacent balconies does the plasma brighten noticeably, as if the chamber itself were acknowledging a temporary crowding. Then the figures stand motionless for days or weeks, exchanging long, overlapping chords of arranged water and stone dust, after which each returns to its pillar and the emptiness reasserts itself with the same deliberate care that opened the chamber in the first place.
