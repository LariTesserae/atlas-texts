In the ceaseless half-light of the hollow, the smallest inhabitants are the ones most completely merged with the sound. They are not merely insects but elongated, many-jointed forms whose bodies have become almost entirely transparent; only the faint internal tracery of their guts and the pale outlines of their spiracles remain visible against the green. Each travels its fixed circuit close to the wall, wing membranes reduced to near-invisibility yet still capable of sustained, low-frequency vibration. When two orbits cross, the bodies pass through one another without collision, the momentary shimmer of overlapping wings registering as a brief brightening in the general drone. Over centuries their routes have worn shallow grooves into the plant tissue, so that the paths themselves are now faintly raised and polished, guiding new generations without need for memory.

Larger and slower are the pale segmented drifters that occupy the middle air. Their bodies consist of soft, translucent rings joined by flexible membranes that can stiffen or slacken at will. Propulsion comes not from wings but from sudden, audible snaps that invert one or more segments, sending them gliding on currents they themselves help generate. At intervals they alight on the upper ribs of living wood, pressing their ventral surfaces flat against fresh leaves. There they remain motionless for long stretches, apparently feeding on the microscopic exudates the plant releases only when pressure is applied in this specific way; afterward they drift off again, leaving behind a faint film that the leaves reabsorb within minutes.

Between these two populations move even rarer presences—thread-fine, almost invisible filaments that occasionally knot themselves into temporary, pulsing knots. These knots sometimes anchor briefly to the drifting forms or to the orbiting insects, then unravel again, releasing minute droplets of fluid that the plant surfaces immediately reclaim. The entire assemblage moves without apparent hierarchy; the constant low thunder of wings and membrane snaps simply forms the medium through which every other action occurs. When a fold momentarily opens in the distant wall, a handful of the smaller insects vanish through it, while a few of the larger drifters arrive from the newly revealed space; the exchange registers as no more than a subtle shift in the overall pitch of vibration.

All of them, plant and fauna alike, are engaged in the same slow polishing of interior space. The insects’ ceaseless circuits prevent any new shoot from encroaching on the open volume; the drifters’ membrane snaps stir the air just enough to keep condensation from pooling too deeply; the rare thread-knots appear to prune the tiniest emerging growths before they can obstruct sight-lines. The result is not tenancy but continued co-performance: the chamber remains empty precisely because it is so densely inhabited, its emptiness the audible, trembling product of everything that moves inside it.
