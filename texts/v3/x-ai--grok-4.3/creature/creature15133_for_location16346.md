The living things of the shelf are not many, and they do not announce themselves. They are instead extensions of the mineral surface that has lifted them into light.

The continuous mantle is itself the first inhabitant: a single, slowly self-weaving tissue whose fronds are stitched through with microscopic threads of the same pale salts that form the towers. Where the tissue sags between towers it forms thin, translucent veils that never quite touch the rock below; where it thickens against a vertical face it hardens into rounded, faintly iridescent cushions whose outer layer flakes away in dry, transparent scales. The whole growth drinks nothing and exhales almost nothing; its only motion is the incremental tightening of its crystalline fibers at midday, when the lattice beneath it expands by another fraction of a hair. Over decades these tightenings accumulate into new draperies and new cushions, so that the mantle steadily reduces the remaining gaps between towers until, in some places, it seems only a single membrane separates one crystal wall from the next.

Moving within and beneath this membrane are the small segmented animals. Their bodies are soft, almost gelatinous, yet each segment carries a fine dusting of the same translucent salts that glitter on the towers. Because of this they never quite disappear against the background; instead they appear as faint, many-limbed glints that travel in slow, unvarying arcs. They seldom meet. When two paths cross, one animal simply halts, its limbs continuing a minute kneading motion against the frond beneath it until the other has passed. Their passage is registered only by the brief, localized dimpling of the living crust and by the dry, almost inaudible rustle when a frond, released, settles back into its former plane.

Nothing here reproduces quickly or visibly. The animals leave no eggs or young that can be seen; the mantle adds new layers rather than scattering seeds. Both persist by remaining almost unchanged while the rock itself lengthens, grain by grain, and the light continues to strike the same facets at the same angles each day. The creatures and the tissue together form a single, extremely slow metabolism whose only product is endurance: the gradual conversion of fractured mineral into a surface that is still fractured mineral, only slightly more occupied, slightly more itself, than it was the decade before.
