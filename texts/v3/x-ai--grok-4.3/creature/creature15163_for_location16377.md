In the basin the living mass is not a background but the resident itself: a single, slow intelligence folded into stems and membrane, patient enough to outlast the city it once replaced. It remembers walls as suggestions rather than obligations, letting the old shapes remain only where they still channel moisture or mark where fruiting stalks should rise. Every vein that brightens under a falling drop is the mass tasting its own weight, adjusting tension so the carpet never grows too rigid or too loose beneath the creatures that walk upon it.

The small many-legged runners live as extensions of that taste. Their tapping feet are not mere locomotion but a constant conversation; each rapid code they press into the surface is answered by a local shift in moisture or a momentary stiffening of the path ahead, guiding them toward fresh spore dust or away from a membrane about to burst. They never stray far from the glowing nodes, whose pale light they seem to sip like moisture, carrying faint charges from one node to another and thereby helping the city decide when a stem should thicken or an arch should reach downward.

The translucent flyers move higher in the conversation. Their wings, thin as inner leaf layers, shed droplets that the floor immediately reclaims, and the impact travels through the entire membrane as a brief, shared tremor. When one lands on the central dome the soft pressure of its body is enough to quicken the currents beneath the skin; the dome swells, the flyer drinks or deposits something unseen, and the pulse that follows rolls outward to every waiting stalk. In this way the flyers act as brief messengers between the open clearings and the low heart, returning each time with wings a fraction heavier or lighter according to what the dome needed to give or receive.

Scattered through the same exchange are the solitary fruiting stalks. Their shivering membranes are not separate organisms but seasonal organs of the larger mass, timed to release spores only when the runners have passed and the flyers are aloft so the new dust settles in already-marked places. Each chime they produce is answered by a distant node brightening, as if the whole basin acknowledges the addition before the spores sink and the floor seals itself again.

Nothing here is alone. The glassy nodes still carry fragments of the older technology, yet they have become sense-organs of the living carpet, translating leftover instructions into adjustments of growth and moisture. The runners and flyers treat these lights as landmarks and lures, while the carpet itself uses the flashes to coordinate its slow housekeeping. Even the margin of bare ground around the central dome is maintained by the same agreement: a remembered law now kept by roots that simply refuse to cross that narrow circle, preserving the space so larger bodies or heavier drops can land without obstruction.

Over days or weeks the basin therefore feels occupied rather than merely overgrown. The low rushing sound beneath every drop and footfall is the sound of the resident thinking aloud, testing, remembering, and rearranging its own modest world inside the fixed shape left by ancient rain.
