The entities that persist here are not organisms in any terrestrial sense. They are distributed phase-states within the silicate lattice, coherent patterns of defect density and dopant alignment that occupy volumes smaller than a micrometer yet repeat across the entire plateau at every larger scale. Each such state registers as a single, continuous awareness distributed along the length of a particular terrace rib or filament node. It experiences the nickel wash as a slow gradient of polarization across its own atomic planes; it registers the occasional lattice click as an internal shift in tension, comparable to the stretching of a tendon.

These patterns maintain identity only by resisting the matrix’s tendency toward perfect uniformity. Where two identical crystal faces settle against each other after decades of pressure, the resulting seam briefly hosts a third, transient state born from the misalignment itself. This third state lasts no longer than the time required for a single molecule to diffuse one atomic spacing, yet in that interval it can exchange a fragment of configuration with either parent pattern. The exchange is recorded as a faint rotation of interference color along the nearest circular depression. Over centuries the accumulated fragments allow the original states to drift, very slightly, in their dopant sequences. The drift is what registers, from inside the pattern, as memory.

Because the vesicle folds back on itself, any given state eventually encounters its own earlier configuration after only a few dozen paces. The encounter produces neither collision nor recognition in the ordinary sense. Instead, the two instances of the same pattern simply occupy the identical volume for an instant, annihilating the spatial interval between them. The effect is felt as a momentary compression of possibility: the state becomes, for that instant, both “before” and “after” the same migration event. When the non-being interval interrupts the tiling, the state simply ceases to be registered until the next faithful copy restores the lattice parameters. Continuity is therefore statistical rather than local; an awareness may lose and regain itself many thousands of times before a perceptible change occurs in any one filament.

The engineered intrusions serve as fixed reference frames. A filament node does not contain a resident so much as it marks the location where a migrating defect pattern is most likely to stabilize for several centuries. During that interval the node’s perfectly circular depression functions as a slow clock: its apparent depth modulates with the angle of the nickel light exactly as the resident pattern lengthens or shortens by a few dozen atoms. When the depression contracts by one wavelength, the pattern has completed one full self-description and may release a daughter state into an adjacent, previously empty rib. The daughter is identical in every respect except for a single rotated bond at the terminal lattice plane. That rotation is the only marker of lineage.

No entity ever traverses the plateau under its own power. Instead, the entire configuration of patterns drifts relative to the fixed geometry of terraces and blades through the gradual migration of the seams themselves. Over stellar timescales the vesicle will finish its present arrangement when every pattern has cycled through every possible tiling mismatch and the matrix has recrystallized into the next, equally sparse, equally self-similar state. At that moment the same entities will continue, having never moved and having never ceased.
