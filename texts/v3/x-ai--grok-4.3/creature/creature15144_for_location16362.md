In the basin the creatures have no fixed outlines. Each one is a shifting node of cooler density within the saturated air, a few finger-widths across when it draws itself tight, spreading into a faint film when it relaxes against stone or water. They press and yield against one another in long, overlapping drifts, so that a single individual may occupy the same place as two of its neighbors for several slow breaths before sliding free. Their motion leaves no footprints; instead it steers the next falling droplet a hair’s breadth aside or nudges a bead already clinging to rock so that it hangs longer before releasing.

Where they skim the water film they gather a momentary gloss, their edges sharpening into silvery meniscus lines that wink once and vanish. Where they drift into the rock they thin to almost nothing, becoming a cool thread that follows a hairline fracture for a short distance before re-emerging, often on another face entirely. The crystalline blooms on the half-buried machines respond to their passage with minute clicks, each click timed to the creature’s temperature rather than to any gear tooth. A machine that has stood motionless for hours may advance one tooth exactly when a drift of them flows across its housing, then lock again as they move on.

They do not appear to feed on the pale fungal filaments, yet they frequently rest among them, covering a filament so completely that the strand seems to glow more brightly for a while, though nothing has actually changed in the surrounding dark. When the fabric of the place thins, the creatures become briefly legible in ways they otherwise are not: one may stretch into a long vertical ribbon that follows the altered gravity for the duration of the distortion, then contract again once the ridge or water surface has settled. Another may split along its length into two mirrored halves that rejoin only after the reflection has doubled and faded. These changes do not harm them; they are simply part of the same slow rhythm that moves the seepage, the crystallisation, and the single-tooth advances of the engines.

Over time the basin collects more of them. New arrivals press upward from the lowest fissures in the same patient intervals as the water itself, joining the drifts already present until the crowded air is no longer composed only of droplets but of these cooler, directional presences that rearrange one another without urgency and without end.
