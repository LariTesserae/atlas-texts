They are not quite creatures and not quite machines, but slow accretions of the same dust that films every ledge. Each body is a packed aggregate of quartz grains and metallic sublimates, bound by electrostatic films that tighten and relax with temperature. From a distance they read as mobile shadows, low to the surface, many-legged, their outlines shifting as grains migrate across them like sand on a dune. Up close the grains resolve into jointed plates, each plate a miniature mirror that catches the dim photoluminescent light and throws it back in fractured glints. Their legs are thin silicate spines that terminate in adhesive pads; the pads leave no residue because they are made of the same material they touch.

Movement is collective rather than individual. Dozens travel in braided lines, each line a temporary organism whose members trade positions without breaking stride. When a gap opens between towers, the lead individual pauses, extrudes a glassy filament from its forward plates, and the filament stiffens into a bridge as later members cross. Once the last has passed, the filament softens and draws back into the body that produced it, the material reincorporated without waste. The only sound is the faint shearing click of one plate sliding over another, a dry punctuation that lasts a fraction of a second before the current reforms.

They do not eat or rest. Instead they harvest. At unfinished edges they scrape fresh grains loose with specialized forelegs, then carry the grains inward along the same lines of movement until they reach an active core. There the grains are pressed between piezoelectric tongues inside the towers; brief, high-frequency vibrations re-crystallize them into new structural members that are immediately added to the city’s shifting fabric. A single line of carriers may spend hours moving the same small quantity of material up and down before it is finally locked into place.

When a tower segment shears free and drops a hand-span before re-anchoring, the occupants already on it do not scatter. Their gait simply lengthens or shortens by the width of one leg joint, the line re-knitting around the new angle as though the displacement were a minor correction in a shared map. Nothing in their behavior suggests alarm or memory of the shift; only continuous adjustment, as though the fracture itself were a living template they are obliged to follow and refine.

Over longer intervals, whole clusters will migrate upward or downward within the seam, carrying sections of rail or balcony with them. The vacated space is left raw for a time, then new threads arrive to begin another increment of growth. In this way the city and its inhabitants remain indistinguishable in purpose: both are the same mineral tension continuing to resolve itself along a single, narrowing plane.
