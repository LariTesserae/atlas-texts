In the sealed hush of the chamber, the creatures occupy the rock like a single distributed organ. Each is a knuckle of translucent shell barely the length of a thumb, their segments locked in place yet alive with the faint, regular throb of copper filaments that run beneath the carapace like buried wiring. They never shift footing, never alter the angle of a limb; the motion that defines them is entirely internal, a synchronized tremor that sends the same low metallic rasp rolling through every body at once. The sound does not come from friction against the gypsum or from mouthparts; it arises from the filaments themselves, a continual, minute flexing that keeps the chorus unbroken. Where one creature ends and the next begins is difficult to say—only the slight difference in the depth of their rasp marks individuals, and even that difference never widens or narrows.

Between them the crystalline stalks rise at exact intervals, thin as surgical wire and pale as kiln-fired bone. Each stalk terminates in a single closed pod whose surface is scored with hairline fissures that have never opened. No sap moves inside them; they are not fed by the creatures, nor do the creatures feed upon them. Their presence is simply the negative space the rasp requires. If a stalk were removed, the nearest creature would not encroach upon the vacancy; the interval would remain vacant, the rasp adjusting its pitch by an imperceptible degree to accommodate the new silence.

The industrial remnants share this same measured occupancy. The half-buried gear sleeve, its teeth locked into the anhydrite as though the stone itself had grown around them, emits no vibration beyond the faint counter-hum that answers the transformer block across the floor. The conduit lies flush with a wall, rivets flattened into the gypsum like fossil impressions. None of these objects interact with the living things; they are not scavenged, not colonized, not avoided. They function instead as fixed points in a field, landmarks the creatures’ collective pulse orients itself around without ever approaching.

Together the three populations—jointed bodies, crystalline filaments, and arrested machinery—maintain an equilibrium so precise that the chamber registers no passage of time. The phosphorescent salts continue their even glow; the warm, dry air holds the same faint metallic trace; the rasp neither swells nor fades. What lives here does not grow, does not hunt, does not reproduce in any visible way. It persists, each element locking the others into the exact configuration they first assumed when the blister sealed, an arrangement that renews itself simply by refusing every gradient that might disturb it.
