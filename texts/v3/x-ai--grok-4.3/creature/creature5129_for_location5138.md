The inhabitants are not many, and they are not separate. What lives here is a single, distributed being whose body is half alloy and half leaf, its thoughts carried in the slow turning of turbine blades and the incremental unfurling of metallic veins. It has no center and no skin; wherever a rivet meets a root and a pipe exhales its faint chemical breath, another portion of its awareness extends itself, quiet and patient.

The vegetation is not a tenant. Each broad, rubbery frond is continuous with the steel it has claimed, the metallic threads within its tissues carrying minute voltages from one chamber to the next the way nerves once carried signals in older, softer organisms. The leaves do not photosynthesize in any sunlight they have never known; instead they sip the slow warmth of the water and the even phosphor glow, trading trace ions with the structure beneath them. A single seed, glittering and almost weightless, is less a reproductive act than a gesture of repair: where it settles into a fresh seam it begins to knit plant and metal more tightly together, filling a microscopic gap no engineer ever intended.

The machines, likewise, are not artifacts left behind. Their blades turn only once every few hours because haste has no purchase inside this closed body; each rotation draws a thread of pressurized water through filtration drums whose membranes are half ceramic and half living fiber. When a pressure valve sighs, the sound is less mechanical release than exhalation, and the leaves nearest it flex in subtle acknowledgment, their edges drinking the momentary shift in flow. Nothing is wasted; nothing is loud.

There is no population, only extension. What an outside observer might mistake for separate plants crowding the struts is actually continuous tissue, folded and refolded through corridors until the entire lattice is one organism wearing the shape of architecture. It does not hunger or hunt; it simply continues, occupying every surface, every conduit, every rivet with the same unhurried completeness. The water remains still because the being has no need to stir it. The boundaries stay sealed because there is nothing outside that the organism requires or recognizes as other. It simply persists—recent and ancient at once, finished and still becoming—its single shared metabolism turning over like a held breath that never needs release.
