In the deepest seams and undersides, where capillary water meets crystallized coolant, the encrustations have thickened and reorganized themselves over uncountable cycles. What began as salt-tolerant films now form layered, leathery mats several centimeters thick, their pale dendritic patterns no longer merely following drips but directing them, etching new micro-channels into the iron with slow, metabolic acids. These mats breathe. They expand and contract with the pressure cycles in the buried mains, their surfaces dimpling and releasing thin veils of vapor that drift upward to join the perpetual distillation overhead. In the jaundiced intervals of the sodium lights they appear almost translucent, shot through with darker veins that pulse in time with the distant piston roar.

Where the mats have grown thickest, around the elbows of major coolant lines, they develop raised nodules that occasionally split to extrude delicate, glassy filaments. These filaments reach across gaps in the plating and anchor themselves, forming living bridges no wider than a finger. Small, bead-like clusters travel along them—aggregates of the same pale growth, but mobile, inching forward on successive expansions and contractions. They do not move quickly; a single transit between two adjacent flanges can take most of a blackout cycle. When the arc lamps gutter out they leave faint blue traces behind them, chemical phosphorescence absorbed during their passage and released again in darkness, mapping their slow migrations for any observer patient enough to remain still.

Deeper in the structure, where thermal fatigue has opened hairline voids between plates, larger forms persist. They are not animals in any ordinary sense. They resemble flattened, many-limbed turbines of accreted scale and crystallized salts, their “limbs” actually bundles of wire and relict cabling overgrown by the same living film. These entities shelter in the shadowed pockets beneath gantry tracks. When a district exhales its scheduled cloud of super-cooled vapor, they emerge, dragging their weight across the tessellated floor on dozens of delicate, scraping feet. They pause at weeping vent stacks to drink, their bodies ringing softly with absorbed vibration, then retreat again as the next pressure wave arrives. Their passage leaves temporary channels through the thinner mats; within hours the films regrow across the disturbance, erasing the tracks.

Nothing here reproduces in the old biological manner. Instead, pieces detach—small flakes of the living scale, or spherical droplets encased in glassy sheaths—and are carried along fluid hammers or condensation strings until they lodge in new crevices. There they unfold over years, incorporating whatever rivets or pipe fittings they find, until another slow turbine-shape begins to drag itself across the plain. The entire megastructure participates in this process: every shift of a hydraulic actuator, every sigh of vapor, every sympathetic tremor of a catwalk becomes part of the same extended metabolism. The plain is not inhabited so much as incompletely transformed into a single, continent-scale organism whose organs are presses and whose blood is coolant, still dreaming, in its vast, staggered respiration, of purposes long since worn away.
