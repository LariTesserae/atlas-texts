Beneath the unblinking sodium belts, life has already begun to read the structure as a single, breathing organism rather than a collection of girders. On the lamp housings the algae exists less as a plant and more as a faint, heat-seeking skin. It flares into momentary iridescence wherever the metal ticks a fraction warmer, then thins again wherever stray currents pull cooler water across the glass. The film never thickens into fronds; instead it spreads in two-dimensional deltas that follow the exact isotherms of each fixture, a living map of the relays that still click overhead.

The translucent fish arrive in tight, liquid ropes that pour through the lattice openings like mercury learning to pour upward. Their bodies are little more than refractive envelopes around a dark thread of gut; when they slow to crop the extruded nutrient paste, they become almost invisible except for the small, quick pulses of their gills. Each shoal carries a faint internal glow, not bioluminescence but the simple accumulation of the sodium light shining through them. When a predator passes, the entire rope fractures into a thousand separate glints that scatter and reform within seconds, as though the school itself performs a calculation too large for any single body.

Filter-feeding invertebrates have learned the lee sides of the beams with architectural precision. Their colonies form vertical veils that thicken where the pump-driven currents eddy, then taper where the flow straightens. The veils billow and collapse in slow waves, and each collapse releases a brief, cloudy pulse of rejected particles that the translucent fish immediately harvest. The invertebrates themselves are neither still nor silent; their feeding fans rotate in minute, synchronized clicks that add a higher, insect-like layer to the low industrial thunder already filling the galleries.

The pressure-armored predators move with an economy that feels almost lazy until the moment they turn. Their scales are not true metal but a layered ceramic grown in spiral ridges that fracture and reseal under extreme compression. Lateral lines run in long, branching canals beneath those ridges, constantly sampling the dense roar of pumps and welders. A single fish’s sudden acceleration therefore registers not as an anomaly but as a momentary thinning in the background noise, a gap the predators notice the way a reader notices a missing word. When they strike, they do so without chasing; they simply occupy the space the smaller fish are about to occupy, and the school shears around them like water around a stone.

New modules continue to descend, already warm, already exhaling nutrient traces. The first fish to enter these fresh chambers do so in exploratory threads rather than full shoals. They drift along the still-cooling seams, tasting the slow streamers of metal dust the currents carry away. Within hours the filter curtains begin to colonize the lee sides of the new beams, and within days the first predator appears, cruising the perimeter with the unhurried patience of something that knows the entire basin will eventually belong to it. The algae, meanwhile, has already claimed the newest lamps, spreading in faint deltas across glass that has never known darkness.
