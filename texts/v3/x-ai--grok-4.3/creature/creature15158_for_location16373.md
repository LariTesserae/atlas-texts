The chamber is home to a single, continuous organism that has no name and no center. It spreads as mats of thick, copper-dark leaves that drink the steady warmth rising from the pipes, and it rises as columns of pale stalks whose spore heads brush the low stone ceiling. Threads of the same growth twist through every seam and valve housing, cracking some casings and sealing others, until the distinction between root and rivet has blurred into one composite tissue.

Where the leaves press against the sodium lamps, they glow with a slow, internal orange light that answers the mineral warmth beneath them. When steam hisses from a cracked joint, the nearest fronds draw back by a finger’s width over the course of hours; when a pipe cools, the growth thickens toward it again. Water that collects in the rust-stained basins is drawn upward through the stems in a rhythm too gradual to see, then released again as droplets that fall back into the same shallow depressions days later. Nothing hurries. Nothing preys. The organism simply continues, thickening where the vibration from the buried turbines is strongest and thinning where the rock sweats coldest.

Over decades the same living weave lengthens a new tendril across a catwalk rail or splits another gear housing, adding its own material to the metal until the machine itself becomes another slow extension of leaf and root. The space holds no separate creatures, only this patient exchange between warmth, moisture, and growth, turning the sealed dark into one enduring body.
