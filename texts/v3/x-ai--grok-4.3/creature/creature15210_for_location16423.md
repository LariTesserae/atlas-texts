In the vaults, life moves at the pace of stone remembering itself.

The smallest beings are the veil-drifters: palm-wide membranes of translucent tissue stretched over internal gas sacs that thrum with the crystal light. They do not so much fly as loiter in the faint updrafts created by the rock’s slow respiration. Their undersides are furred with adhesive cilia that comb microscopic flakes shed by the living seams; each feeding leaves a momentary constellation of silver motes that glow for a few breaths before fading. When the vaults heave, the drifters rise in coordinated sheets, their gas bladders dilating until they press softly against the higher haze, then sink again like settling ash once the tremor passes.

Larger and more deliberate are the hook-walkers. Their six limbs end in single recurved claws the color of burnt flint; the claws never quite touch the floor at the same instant, so their progress around plaza edges produces only an irregular, almost thoughtful ticking. Between steps the middle pair of limbs drag lightly across the fused rock, tasting the thin film of moisture the stone exhales. They are not predators in any ordinary sense; they orbit the plazas in long, overlapping spirals that gradually erode stray mineral dust into faint radial patterns—new, accidental ornament for the unfinished city. When two walkers meet, they pause, raise opposing claws, and exchange a brief, dry rasp before continuing, as though confirming that the emptiness between them remains properly maintained.

Deeper in the haze, almost indistinguishable from the crystal veins themselves, move the seam-tenders. These are not separate organisms but extensions of the living stone: pale, branching threads that can lift a hand’s width above the floor for a few decades before subsiding again. Where they rise they brush against drifting veil-drifters, exchanging microscopic passengers; where they sink they leave behind shallow grooves that later fill with condensation and evaporate into new tracery. The seam-tenders respond to the vaults’ breathing by flaring brighter, as though the rock’s slow inhalation excites them. During these intervals their filaments knot and unknotted in slow dances that rearrange loose grit into low ridges no higher than a claw’s reach—temporary thresholds across the plazas that collapse again when stillness returns.

Occasionally a different scale of presence makes itself known. During the strongest tremors, certain monoliths briefly grow warm to the touch and emit a low, sub-audible tone. At such moments the hook-walkers freeze mid-spiral and the veil-drifters press themselves flat against the floor. Nothing visibly emerges, yet for several slow heartbeats the air feels thicker, as though an awareness larger than any single creature has leaned forward to inspect the state of its plazas. When the light dims again, the monoliths are cool, the walkers resume their circuits, and the drifting sheets of membrane rise once more.

Together these forms continue the original project without ever completing it. The veil-drifters keep the air in gentle motion; the hook-walkers keep distances between structures legible; the seam-tenders keep the stone’s surface alive and slightly uncertain. The vaults therefore remain exactly as their builders left them: roofless, half-empty, and still in use.
