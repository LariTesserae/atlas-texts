In this sealed pocket, life has thinned itself to the thinnest possible thread. The mold is not a carpet or a fur; it is a scatter of single filaments, each one no thicker than a hair and spaced so that a hand could pass between them without brushing more than two or three. They grow only where the rock meets the shallow film of water, and even there they grow by subtraction rather than addition—each filament drinks the condensing moisture and returns nothing, leaving behind a faint etching in the stone that will never be seen. Their pale color is not the white of new growth but the absence of any color at all, as though the mold has surrendered pigment along with every other expenditure it could no longer afford.

Lower on the walls, the creatures lie pressed into the pores like dried leaves sealed between pages. They are smaller than the smallest fingernail paring, translucent except for the dark coils of their folded limbs. In the perfect stillness they appear less like animals than like intricate shadows the rock itself has cast. Because they never move, the new mineral layers forming above them simply continue over their bodies, incorporating them grain by grain into the growing stone. Their eyeless heads rest against the rock as if listening to the faint percussion of droplets a few centimeters away; their sides rise and fall so slowly that the movement might be mistaken for the breathing of the mountain rather than their own.

Nothing else enters or leaves. The shard of smoothed stone lies where it was last set down, its rubbed surface now carrying a faint film of the same condensation that sustains the mold. No larger animal has ever found the seam; the temperature and saturation remain too constant for anything that still requires choice or motion. What remains here is only what can persist without altering the balance: filaments that ask almost nothing, and bodies that have consented to be included among the forming minerals. The cavity holds them the way a closed book holds pressed flowers—preserved not by intent but by the simple absence of any force that could disturb them.
