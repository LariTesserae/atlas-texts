In this living expanse, the true residents are not visitors but participants in a single metabolism that has no clear boundary between structure and inhabitant. The smallest are the rail-grazers—thumb-sized, gelatinous bodies that never quite touch the surface they traverse. They ride translucent cytoskeletal filaments that grow in parallel grooves along every wall and ceiling, their soft undersides secreting enzymes that loosen a thin film of nutrients from the host tissue. As they drift, they leave behind faint, iridescent trails that harden into new rail segments within hours, extending the city’s internal transport network by their own passage.

Larger browsers drift between these levels on broad, veined membranes that catch the constant, leaf-filtered light like slow sails. Their bodies are jointed yet boneless, each segment ringed with fine, sensory papillae that taste the air for pockets of ripening photosynthetic sugars. When they settle, they press flat against the leathery fronds, exchanging carbon-rich fluids through microscopic pores in their own cuticle; the city-organism benefits from this pruning and fertilization, while the browsers receive shelter and a steady trickle of moisture. Their movements are so muffled that the only visible sign of their presence is the subtle dimpling of the surface as weight shifts, followed by the slow re-plumping of the tissue once they lift away.

Predation here unfolds across days rather than moments. The encircling hunters are little more than mobile sheaths of permeable membrane. They flow around a browser or cluster of grazers, then seal themselves into a temporary sac. Inside, digestive secretions soften the prey without violence; the resulting slurry is absorbed directly through the sac walls and later excreted as mineral salts that the city uses to lithify its foundations. When the hunt ends, the emptied sac sloughs away, leaving behind only a faint chemical signature that attracts fresh grazers to the newly enriched rail.

Even the air itself hosts life. Thousands of spiracles along the vascular walls open and close in slow unison, each exhalation carrying microscopic symbionts that colonize any fresh bud or apoptotic hollow within minutes. These airborne partners maintain the narrow thermal band by modulating local respiration rates, ensuring that older districts do not overheat as they harden. In return, the city supplies them with protected niches inside its thickening plates.

Nothing here is separate for long. When a new node erupts overnight, it is already seeded with dormant rail-grazers and membrane browsers that activate as soon as the scaffolding firms. When an older sector collapses inward, its softening tissues release clouds of symbionts and dissolved nutrients that are immediately claimed by adjacent growth and its attendant fauna. The result is a population that cannot be counted in individuals; every creature is a temporary concentration of the same continuous life, moving through a body that is also its home, its food, and its future.
