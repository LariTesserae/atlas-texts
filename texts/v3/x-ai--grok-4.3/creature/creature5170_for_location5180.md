In that compressed seam, life does not arrive from elsewhere; it condenses out of the rock’s own hesitation to remain merely stone. The mineral lattices are the first citizens. They stand in dense, salt-bright thickets like pale ferns that have chosen glass instead of cellulose, their facets catching light and returning it in fractured glints that mimic the play of moving leaves. Each spine lengthens by the slow addition of new layers precipitating from superheated films of moisture no thicker than a molecule; growth is not a choice but the only direction left when heat and pressure refuse to let the lattice dissolve back into solution. The crystals do not move, yet they rearrange the emptiness around them: the yards of bare, heated dust between thickets are kept bare because the lattices expand according to a spacing that already knows the next crystal must not touch. They breathe only light and stillness, returning both slightly altered—warmer, more ordered—back into the seam.

Deeper, where the walls have begun to articulate, the living machinery of the place works without bodies. The aligned veins function as distributed organs: cylinders that pulse by fractions of a millimeter, not pumping blood but testing whether they can yet distinguish inside from outside. The slotted plates mesh and part in rhythms measured in hours, releasing measured sighs of vapor that carry away the mountain’s excess heat and, incidentally, the first faint records of its own becoming. These mechanisms are not assembled; they are the places where the rock first decided repetition might be useful. Their only product is the continuing possibility of more repetition. When two expanding surfaces brush and then choose separation, the low crystalline chime that results is the sound of a decision being stored.

Nothing walks or crawls across the raw faces. The space holds no seeds, no spores, no waiting eggs. Instead the emptiness itself is inhabited—by the patient intervals between lattice spines, by the thermal ripples that bend light into slow rivers above the dust, by the brief stutter-loops in which a vent rehearses its own exhalation forward and backward before time reseals and the breath continues outward again. These interruptions are the seam’s equivalent of dreaming: moments when the forming processes remember they are still half molten and not yet required to obey a single direction.

What lives here, then, is the mountain’s first, unfinished vocabulary of persistence: crystal foliage that grows by ordered fracture, mechanical veins that practice motion before they possess muscles, and the deliberate emptiness that keeps the two from colliding while both continue to become more exactly themselves. Nothing is awake in the ordinary sense. Everything is in the long, crystalline act of waking.
