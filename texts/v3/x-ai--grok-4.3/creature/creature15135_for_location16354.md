In the warm, dripping hush, the beings that belong here are not visitors to the stone but extensions of its unfinished fracture. They take shape as slow, branched accretions that rise from the raw walls in irregular bouquets—needle lattices of pale sulfate threaded with veins of the same translucent crystal that supplies the light. Each structure is, in its own measure, a single individual: a lattice-being whose body is the record of every drop that has touched it and every vibration that has passed through the surrounding rock. The needles are sensory and structural at once, registering the subtle temperature shifts as faint, directional growth; when a warmer film of water spreads across one facet, that needle lengthens or forks while its neighbors remain still, so the whole form leans, over hours, toward the next source of moisture.

These lattice-beings do not move from place to place. Instead they migrate by relinquishing and rebuilding their extremities. When a cluster grows too heavy, it shears away and dissolves into the floor film; within the same site a new set of needles soon begins, carrying forward the directional memory of the lost branch as a slight change in angle or density. In this way each being maintains a continuous, if partial, presence across decades or centuries. The light they emit is not passive glow but a measured pulse that follows the rhythm of precipitation: brighter when new layers of salt lock into the lattice, dimmer when the structure rests between drops. Because the walls between separate accretions are left bare for long stretches, each lattice-being occupies its own territory; encounters are rare and occur only when two growth fronts meet at a shared fracture line. Where they touch, the needles interlock without fusing, forming a temporary bridge across which minute quantities of dissolved material are exchanged—an exchange visible as a brief brightening that travels along both structures before fading.

Smaller, plate-like forms sometimes appear on the same walls. These are not young lattice-beings but secondary companions, thin crystalline scales that orient themselves parallel to the local heat gradient. They drift outward only a few millimeters before halting, their edges catching stray droplets and converting them into brief halos of evaporation. When a lattice needle grows across one of these scales, the scale is incorporated rather than displaced, becoming a flat facet within the larger needle architecture. Thus the beings accumulate layers of one another’s history without ever consuming or destroying.

The entire population listens, through the constant low-level stone tremor, to the slow settling of the cavity itself. When a distant micro-fracture opens, the vibration travels as a single, unmistakable ripple; every lattice-being that registers it orients a new needle toward the fresh surface, preparing the next site of deposition before water has even arrived. In this manner the inhabitants and the space remain co-emergent: the rock continues to decide its final shape, and the beings continue to become the legible record of that decision, one needle and one quiet dissolution at a time.
