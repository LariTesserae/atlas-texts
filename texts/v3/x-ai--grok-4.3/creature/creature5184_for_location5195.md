In that sealed basalt pocket, life—if the word still applies—has learned to proceed at the speed of evaporation. The inhabitants are the mineral threads themselves. They begin as hair-thin extrusions weeping from the same fractures that bleed water, emerging first as clear beads that harden into filaments no thicker than a spider’s dragline. Over decades these filaments lengthen, branch, and knit together into loose, translucent veils that hang between ledge and lintel like unfinished lace.

Each thread is both creature and dwelling. As water sheets across it, dissolved calcium and iron accrete along its length; the thread grows by burying its own older surface beneath newer mineral. What moves, moves imperceptibly: a filament may take three human heartbeats simply to detach from a neighboring strand, another dozen to drift the width of a finger before settling again. When many such threads shift together they alter the dim, sourceless light by a fraction, so the chamber seems to breathe once every few minutes, though no lungs are present.

Where threads cross the low door-lintels or crawl across the terraced roofs they leave permanent white scars identical to the glaze already coating every stone; an observer would never know a living passage had occurred. Their only appetite is for the steady drip. When a droplet strikes a thread it is absorbed, carried along internal capillaries no wider than light itself, and redeposited farther along as fresh carbonate. In this way the threads maintain the city’s slow polish, repairing what the ceaseless water would otherwise dissolve.

They are not numerous. A century may pass between one generation and the next. Yet they occupy every surface at once, layered so finely that the entire chamber is, in effect, a single slow organism still finishing itself exactly as its long-vanished masons intended. Nothing else enters, nothing leaves, nothing grows faster than the glaze. The threads and the city are the same continuing act of deposition, indistinguishable except by the patience required to notice the difference.
