In the gaps between the blocks, where the shallow pools hold their own thin skins of mineral oil and iron, the first residents reveal themselves only by displacement. Broad, segmented fronds the color of oxidized bronze rise in irregular fans, but they do not sway with any current of air. Instead they tilt, degree by slow degree, toward each fresh pulse of steam that rolls out from the nearest ventilator grille. Their undersides are not leaf-veined but reticulated with fine, hair-like tubes that drink the condensate directly, drawing it inward until the surface tightens and beads again on the outer cuticle.

Between these fronds move smaller presences. They are almost the same temperature as the surrounding mist, so they register first as distortions—a momentary clearing in the drifting curtains, a brief brightening of the copper-tinted light where something has drawn moisture into itself. When they settle on the polished concrete they leave no footprint; they simply thicken the existing film of scale, adding another translucent lamina to the leathery surface. Over years these accretions have raised the walkways in faint terraces, each step no higher than a finger’s width, so that the entire plaza now slopes in imperceptible gradients toward the grated sumps.

Larger forms occupy the shadowed intervals beneath the elevated gantries. They adhere to the undersides of the pipe runs like heavy, flattened sacs of braided tissue, neither animal nor machine but something that has grown along the rivets and learned their spacing. Every few hours one of them contracts, forcing a measured expulsion of warmer vapor through pores that align exactly with the vent patterns of the buildings below. The released breath joins the general mist, drifts, and is drawn back into the intakes, completing a loop the structures themselves appear to expect.  

The crane at the center does not operate alone. Its hook, when it descends, meets not only the rising leaves but a slow, deliberate uncoiling of deeper growths that have pushed through the grate in thick, rope-like bundles. These bundles part at the hook’s touch, then reknit once it rises, their cut ends already exuding fresh mineral cement. The motion is not repair but conversation: the machinery acknowledges the encroachment, the encroachment concedes a measured path. Nothing hurries.  

Farther out, where the concrete meets the reed-choked water, the boundary itself is inhabited. The reeds are not simple plants; their stems are sheathed in thin metallic laminations that ring softly when stray droplets strike them. At the waterline these laminations continue downward as root-like cables, threading through the basalt seams and emerging again inside the weeping rivulets. The water that flows along those paths therefore carries both dissolved stone and dissolved intention; by the time it reaches the sumps it has been tasted, sorted, and faintly rewritten.  

Nothing here breeds in the usual sense. Presence accumulates. Over decades the plazas have grown slightly more reflective, the steam pulses slightly more synchronized, the intervals between blocks slightly more resonant with a low, continuous tone that is neither machine nor growth but the two of them gradually becoming indistinguishable. The residents remain, thickening the air, polishing the stone, keeping the distance the garden and the machinery have agreed upon.
