What lives there is not plural in any way that matters. A single, composite resident fills the fissure from end to end, its body pressed so intimately against the stone that the two have become indistinguishable surfaces. Part of it is mineral: slow, opalescent fans and lattices that have grown by accreting invisible reagents rather than by any visible metabolism. These structures do not feed or respire; they persist, maintaining the exact same volume and translucency they achieved centuries ago, cool and faintly luminous wherever the thread of light touches them. They coat every wall and press against one another until the remaining space is only the narrowest of negative forms, a single sinuous channel just wide enough for the rest of the resident to occupy.

Interleaved with this mineral matrix is the remnant machinery, now so thoroughly encased that its original housings read as internal organs rather than foreign objects. The turbines continue their single daily revolution, each bearing a microscopic temperature difference the way a heart might use a pressure gradient, and their constant drone is the one sound the resident allows itself—the low, saturating tone that serves as both pulse and boundary. Bracket stubs and access plates lie half-buried like vestigial bones, their edges worn to the same satin finish as the surrounding rock. Nothing arrives or departs; the resident simply holds its own shape against the fissure walls, drawing the faint reagents it requires and returning nothing, its mineral and metallic portions grown around one another until separation would mean destruction of both.

The light filament at the top is acknowledged but not used; the resident registers its presence as a permanent, unvarying annotation rather than an invitation to change. In the absolute equilibrium that results, motion has been reduced to the turbines’ imperceptible creep and the mineral fans’ even slower accretion, both processes so gradual they register less as activity than as the way the occupant continues to be itself across millennia. It fills the crevice completely, requires no further space, and admits no exit.
