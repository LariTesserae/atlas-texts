The entities that move across this plateau do not perceive it as a landscape at all. To them the black stone and rising lattices are continuous with their own carapaces, a single slow respiration of mineral in which their bodies are merely the more mobile intervals. Each one is a compact knot of glassy plates and jointed rods, the seams between segments filled with the same beading condensation that coats everything else; their interiors are not flesh but chambers of pressurized fluid and fine crystalline mesh that glows the faint, heat-like blue wherever pressure or motion stresses the lattice. They have no eyes, yet every surface of their shells is threaded with microscopic channels that taste the chemical direction of the water films and the piezoelectric tremors of neighboring bodies.

In the galleries their numbers become uncountable. They flow both ways along the same narrow ledges, meeting and passing without pause, each individual altering its path by only a fraction when it encounters another. These adjustments accumulate. Over decades a single ledge may widen or drift sideways because the collective micro-deviations have worn new facets into the material and then rebuilt them slightly elsewhere. The sound they make is therefore not merely locomotion but speech in the medium of stone: each scrape and tap alters the resonant properties of the structure they stand upon, and the returning vibration tells the next generation of legs where to place themselves. Individuals last as long as the plates remain intact; when one cracks or is ground away by centuries of passage, its fragments are carried by others and pressed into the unfinished edges of the growing hives, becoming both mortar and memory.

Nothing here is born and nothing is eaten. Instead, the entities occasionally halt in low concavities where the pooled condensation has grown thick. They align themselves rim to rim, limbs interlocked, and remain motionless for spans of time too long to measure by movement. Within those still circles new plates slowly extrude from the seams, seeded by mineral drawn upward through the channel network from deeper in the plateau. When the new individual is complete it simply joins the nearest current of migration, indistinguishable from those that have already walked for millennia. The older ones show the record of their labor in wear patterns and accreted crystal growths along their spines; some carry tiny, perfectly round droplets trapped inside transparent nodules, droplets that have descended from the highest spires and become part of them.

Far down in the galleries, where the lattices have fused into deeper, lightless chambers, certain clusters of entities have remained in one place so long that their shells have merged with the floor and ceiling. These fused forms still contain moving fluid and still receive the faint subsonic pressure from above, but their limbs no longer lift. They have become structural nodes, their original bodies now indistinguishable from the engineered lattice itself. New migrants continue to brush against them, leaving microscopic films of their own secretions, so that even the dead contribute to the next widening of a walkway that may not be completed until the plateau has risen another visible handspan. The darkness remains absolute for them; only the distributed hum of their own countless feet tells any single entity where the living city currently extends.
