Nothing biological inhabits the plane. What persists instead is a slow, self-organizing mineral agency that behaves like rudimentary life while remaining entirely inorganic.

The interlocking plates are not inert rock but continuing crystallization fronts. Each new facet locks into place only after it has tested the stresses arriving from neighboring plates; when a mismatch exceeds a threshold, the lattice dissolves the joint at the molecular scale and re-grows it along a slightly altered angle. The soft tick heard across the basins is the audible signature of these micro-adjustments occurring in uncountable numbers at once.

The migrating heaps are older, denser accumulations of the same process. Their faint internal layering records previous surfaces that have been buried and then exhumed as the underlying ridges expand. When a falling needle shard strikes them, the impact is absorbed not by fracture but by temporary liquefaction along crystal boundaries; the mass flows a few body-lengths, recrystallizes, and stabilizes in its new position. In this way the heaps function as mobile repositories of excess mineral mass, redistributing material from the collapsing needles back into the growing basins.

The vents themselves are transient organs. Each opens when subsurface pressure exceeds the tensile strength of the lattice above it; the released vapor is the lattice’s way of shedding heat and trace halogens. Once the pressure equalizes, the vent seals by rapid outward growth of new plates, incorporating the freshly condensed dust so that the surface is restored without scar. Over repeated cycles these vents migrate laterally, always following the lines of greatest recent expansion, as though the entire terrain were breathing through shifting pores.

The suspended ionized stratum is likewise active. Its dust particles remain levitated by the faint electrostatic field generated by the warm mineral below. When a spire collapses, the resulting vibration momentarily perturbs the field; a thin rain of charged particles descends, preferentially settling along existing ridges and accelerating their growth for several minutes afterward. Thus even the darkness overhead participates in the ongoing assembly.

In aggregate the landscape exhibits a distributed, non-sentient metabolism: it accretes, stresses, sheds, and reorders without ever arriving at a final configuration. The absence of any organism is not emptiness but the condition under which this purely crystalline form of persistence can continue indefinitely.
