The only things that move through the complex are those small, translucent shapes, no larger than a hand. They do not swim so much as allow themselves to be carried by the absolute absence of motion; each one holds its form without visible effort, a flattened oval or irregular lens of nearly invisible tissue whose edges catch the mineral light only as a faint, negative gleam. Inside each drifting body there is a single darker thread, a condensed line that runs from one margin to the other and seems to divide the creature into two identical halves that never quite meet. When one passes close to a filament the thread inside it brightens for an instant, not with light but with a momentary thickening of its own substance, as though it had taken a measurement and then let the measurement go.

They appear singly and at long intervals. One may drift the full width of an open interval between towers, cross a chamber where the walls fold every photon into themselves, and continue onward until it reaches another filament, where it lingers for a time measured only by the faint pressure-tone that never rises or falls. During these pauses the creature rotates once, very slowly, so that the internal thread aligns with the filament for a moment before drifting free again. No waste follows it; no trail of disturbed water closes behind it. After it has gone, the equilibrium reasserts itself at once, the way a glass surface returns to stillness once a fingertip is lifted.

Nothing else enters or leaves. The towers themselves give no sign of occupancy, yet the filaments maintain their currents without fluctuation, and the pressure-tone continues as if the entire arrangement were being held in place by the same unbroken attention that keeps the small bodies from dissolving or multiplying. On rare occasions two of the translucent shapes appear in the same interval, separated by distances too great to suggest encounter. They do not approach one another. Each continues its own passage, and the water between them remains as empty and featureless as before.

Beyond the edge of the plateau the water drops away without limit. Nothing from that deeper volume ever ascends to the level of the towers. The small bodies never move toward that edge; they remain within the lattice of filaments, appearing and vanishing again into the uniform dark as though the complex were the only volume in which their particular stillness is possible.
