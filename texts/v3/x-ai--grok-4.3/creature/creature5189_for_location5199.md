The plateau sustains only what has already learned to match its own refusal of change. The wiry stems that rise from the shallow pans are not so much rooted as fused, their pale fibers grooved in the same direction the wind has scored the surrounding stone. Each stem remains no taller than a finger’s length yet persists through seasons that never arrive, drawing the thin film of water into itself and releasing almost none. When the constant pressure of air moves across their open tips, they do not sway; they simply tighten, transmitting the vibration downward into the hairline cracks where their roots have sealed every fracture against further widening.

The translucent insects move within that same pressure rather than against it. Their wings are little more than curved films of membrane, thin enough that the unrelenting light passes straight through them and renders each swarm nearly invisible except where thousands overlap. They rise and drop in unison, riding the steady current between the fluted ridges, feeding on whatever microscopic film condenses on the stone each night. Their movement adds no new sound; it merely thickens the single, endless tone already present, as if the wind itself had acquired a faint, living texture.

Flattened against the same grooves lie the long-limbed lizards, their pale scales the same matte tone as the rock. They do not bask so much as disappear, pressing their bodies into the shallow channels until only the faint rise of breath distinguishes them from the stone. Their limbs are elongated for reaching between distant pans without lifting away from the surface, and their eyes remain half-lidded against the constant glare. At intervals they cross to the deeper pools, drink, and return to the open stone before the light has shifted by any measurable degree. Nothing hunts them; nothing competes. Their stillness and the insects’ rising and the stems’ rigid persistence all answer the same wind with the same answer: continuance without alteration, an equilibrium that has outlasted any memory of beginning.
