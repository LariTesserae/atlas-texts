**Preparation for the Folded Settlement (the “Knuckle Pocket”)**

This is not a ruin or a constructed village; it is a single, slow-moving organism that tolerates a few dozen human-sized bodies inside it. Treat it as a living cavity rather than architecture. An ordinary traveler needs to minimize damage, avoid altering the moisture and temperature balance, and move as if already inside someone else’s lung.

**Clothing and Body**
- Wear multiple thin, breathable layers rather than heavy insulation. The air is cold but the humidity is near saturation; thick synthetics will trap condensation against your skin and chill you faster than they warm you.
- Outer layer should be a lightweight, tightly woven natural-fiber shell (linen or hemp) that beads water without plastic membranes. Anything that squeaks, rustles, or reflects light will register immediately against the constant soft dripping.
- Footwear: thin, flexible soles with almost no tread—essentially moccasins or soft leather slippers. Thick boots tear the moss pads and bruise the living vine walkways; the settlement’s floors register weight and will tighten or loosen in response. Bare feet are acceptable if your soles are tough; the moss is deep and springy, but it is also full of small, soft-bodied creatures that will press against skin.
- Cover hair and scalp. Condensation falls constantly; uncovered hair becomes a dripping wick within minutes and carries droplets into the deeper galleries where they do not normally reach.

**Light and Vision**
- Do not bring bright lights. The chamber’s only illumination is the faint, pulsing internal glow of the plant tissue itself. A single shielded candle or a very low red-lensed lamp is the practical maximum; anything brighter will cause the phosphorescent veins to dim in reflexive response and will unsettle the inhabitants.
- Train your eyes for movement rather than detail. The arthropods inside the leaves register only as traveling distortions; larger fauna appear as sudden local dimming when they cross a vein. Learn to notice these instead of trying to illuminate them.

**Tools and Interactions**
- Carry nothing edged except a small flint or bone blade (the same materials the residents use). Steel or modern alloys leave metallic traces in the condensate cycle and are culturally dissonant.
- If you must harvest or prune (you probably should not), use the same slow, directional cuts the residents make—never across a main vein. The settlement regrows overnight; abrupt removals create local imbalances in humidity and light that the whole structure then compensates for.
- Expect to stoop or crawl within hours. Corridors that are passable at dusk require crouching by dawn. Keep your pack small and compressible so you can reshape it to fit changing spaces.

**Biological and Psychological Considerations**
- The air is cold, still, and saturated with plant volatiles and slow microbial activity. A simple linen mask dampened with clean water helps filter the heaviest spores if you are sensitive. The microbial load is high but stable; most travelers experience mild digestive adjustment rather than acute illness.
- The fauna is almost entirely invisible and non-aggressive, yet you will feel constant low-level contact. The sensation is less like being crawled on and more like the surface under you occasionally breathing or shifting density. Panic responses are the main hazard; move slowly and the sensation usually subsides.
- Sound carries differently here. Your own breathing, heartbeat, and the occasional droplet striking the central pool become the dominant acoustic events. Loud speech or abrupt movement registers as a physical pressure against the living walls.

**Temporal and Exit Planning**
- The pocket changes on a scale of hours, not days. Mark your entry point with a single, small, living marker (a bent tendril or placed leaf) rather than an object; the residents understand such signs and will usually leave them undisturbed.
- If you intend to stay overnight, accept that you will wake inside a slightly different geometry. Plan exit routes that rely on fixed stone ribs rather than plant corridors, which may have narrowed or re-routed.
- Leave no waste. The closed condensate loop and the residents’ constant harvesting mean any foreign material is rapidly incorporated or rejected; both outcomes disturb the balance.

**Mindset**
Approach as a tolerated parasite rather than a guest or explorer. The space is already complete and self-regulating; your presence is accommodated only because the inhabitants continuously adjust for it. Move with the same deliberate slowness they use—harvesting, redirecting, thinning—rather than trying to pass through quickly. The reward is not spectacle but the unusual intimacy of being inside a structure that is still deciding what shape it wants to be by morning.
