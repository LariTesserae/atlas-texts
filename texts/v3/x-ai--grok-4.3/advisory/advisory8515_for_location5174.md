**Preparation for the Alloy Plateau**

This is not a landscape you visit so much as a surface you are temporarily permitted to occupy. Treat it as an active, low-power megastructure whose indifference is absolute. An ordinary human arrives already at a disadvantage: the place was not built for biology, and it does not adjust.

**Physical orientation and scale**  
The horizon is functionally infinite and perfectly flat to the eye; once you leave the arrival point you will have no natural landmarks. Carry a personal inertial reference—mechanical or optical, not electronic—because distances and angles can fold without warning. The three-to-thirty-heartbeat distortions are unpredictable and silent; if you feel ear pressure or notice a gantry suddenly doubling its apparent distance, stop moving until the geometry resolves. Mark your path with something that cannot be absorbed into the alloy (bright filament tape or micro-drones that hover rather than land). The plateau will eventually “forget” any object left motionless long enough.

**Light and thermal management**  
Perpetual noon is not metaphorical. There is no night cycle, no relief from the hard geometric shadows. The alloy itself stays cool, but the light is unrelenting; bring polarized full-spectrum filters rather than ordinary sunglasses, and assume any exposed skin or organic material will suffer accelerated fatigue. The persistent shadows are not empty; the small crawlers aggregate there. If you must rest, do so in shifting light rather than deep shadow.

**Atmosphere and water**  
Air can thin locally without visible cause. Carry supplemental oxygen or at least a rebreather that does not rely on ambient pressure being stable. Water exists only as condensate. Every vertical shaft is ringed with collection pans; you can drink from them, but the level changes on cycles longer than a human attention span. The grooved channels are sharp enough to bead water rather than wet the surface—do not use them as handholds if you value your gloves or skin.

**Local life and integration risk**  
The six-legged crawlers move slowly but deliberately along every rail and conduit. They will investigate an unfamiliar warm mass and may attempt to treat you as another surface on which to glide. Larger kite-forms drift above; their turning produces pressure changes rather than sound—learn to read that change as an early warning. The pale lichen in the exhaust slots is harmless but indicates active thermal cycling below the skin. None of these organisms are aggressive, yet the plateau itself has a demonstrated tendency to absorb inert objects. Any crawler that stops moving eventually crystallizes and becomes part of the structure. Treat immobility as the greater danger.

**Spatial and temporal awareness**  
The “impossible wear” grooves and the folding geometry suggest the material is still adjusting to stresses that predate your arrival. These effects are real, not optical. Sleep in short cycles; the slow realignment of the overhead cranes occurs every few hours and registers first as a change in cranial pressure. If you remain motionless for too long, the structure may begin to treat you the way it treats the crystallized crawlers.

**Minimal kit recommendations**  
- Redundant mechanical navigation.  
- Polarized eye and skin protection.  
- Small pressurized water collector that can fit over a pan rim.  
- High-visibility, non-metallic path markers.  
- Low-mass shelter that can be erected and struck in under a minute.  
- Ear protection capable of registering sub-audible pressure shifts.  

The plateau does not welcome or repel; it simply continues its minute adjustments. Most visitors leave because they realize the place has already begun to classify them as either temporary disturbance or potential component. Prepare accordingly.
