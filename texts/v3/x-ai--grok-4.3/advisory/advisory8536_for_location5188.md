**Preparation for the Black Plateau Lattice**

This is not a place for casual visitation. It is an unfinished megastructure the size of a mountain range, still under construction by processes measured in centuries, inhabited by a single, continent-scale population of mineral entities whose entire existence is the act of assembly. An ordinary human can survive here with rigorous preparation, but the margin for error is narrow because every environmental factor is hostile to human physiology and psychology.

**Atmosphere and Thermal Management**  
The air is thin and consistently sub-zero. Condensation occurs on every surface, including your skin and clothing, but does not freeze. Bring a sealed, heated rebreather or positive-pressure suit with active moisture management; ordinary parkas will saturate and turn into ice shells within hours. The condensation itself is chemically aggressive enough to etch glass over long periods, so all exposed materials (boots, gloves, helmet seals) must be rated for prolonged contact with mineral-laden fluids.

**Sensory Protection**  
The constant layered metallic scraping, chiming, and subsonic drone is physically loud enough to cause hearing damage and will transmit through your boots and any surface you touch. Industrial-grade noise-isolating headphones or a full helmet with active noise cancellation are mandatory. The darkness is total; the only native illumination is a faint thermal glow from stressed crystal that registers more as warmth against closed eyes than visible light. Bring multiple redundant light sources, but favor infrared or low-power red filters. The entities respond to temperature gradients. White light or high-heat sources may cause localized migration surges around you.

**Mobility and Navigation**  
The terrain is a three-dimensional maze of overlapping planes, struts, and half-formed galleries whose apparent solidity is deceptive. Surfaces are slick with condensation films that the entities continuously modify. Use crampon-style footwear with replaceable spikes and magnetic or adhesive assists for the smoother crystal sections. Carry physical ranging tools (laser or ultrasonic) rather than relying solely on visual landmarks; the overlapping architecture makes conventional maps useless within minutes. Mark your path with passive, non-luminous beacons that do not alter surface temperature.

**Interaction Protocol with the Entities**  
The small, hard-shelled beings move in dense, slow rivers. They do not appear to perceive humans as relevant, but they will flow around or over any obstruction. Do not step on them in large numbers or attempt to clear paths; their collective pressure can subtly shift lattice sections. Treat them as a slow-moving fluid. If you must cross a major migration route, pause and let the flow divert around you rather than forcing a channel. Their limbs can exert surprising purchase on crystal; avoid trapping limbs or equipment between individuals.

**Temporal and Psychological Factors**  
Nothing here operates on human timescales. Condensation drips take seconds to fall, lattice accretion is invisible in real time, and any change you cause will be overwritten or incorporated within hours to days. This can induce profound disorientation. Limit exposure to four to six hours maximum per visit. The combination of unrelenting mechanical sound, absolute darkness, and the sense of standing inside an incomprehensibly vast, disinterested process produces rapid psychological fatigue. Carry stimulants only as emergency measures; the preferred approach is strict time discipline and pre-planned exit routes.

**Contingency Measures**  
The plateau is raised above an endless dark. Falling is likely fatal. Every walkway or ledge should be treated as potentially incomplete. Carry a lightweight harness and tether system rated for dynamic loads. If the entities begin concentrating around your location in unusual density, withdraw immediately along your marked path. They are not hostile, but their slow rearrangement can seal exits over the course of a day.

In essence, treat this as an extreme industrial environment that happens to be alive and still under construction. Your goal is not exploration or interaction but temporary, low-impact presence. The lattice will continue its work whether you are there or not.
