This is a place of perfect mineral equilibrium, so preparation centers on protecting human sensory systems that evolved for change, movement, and contrast.

**Vision.** The total, directionless glare will overwhelm eyes accustomed to shadows and depth cues. Bring high-quality, heavily tinted goggles or glacier glasses with side shields; standard sunglasses will be insufficient. Consider also a lightweight white cloth or veil to drape over the head and further diffuse the light. Do not rely on shadow-based navigation—bring a mechanical compass and small lengths of colored tape or chalk to mark your path across the crystal, as perspective and distance will be difficult to judge.

**Hearing.** The single, unbroken chord will press against the ears continuously and transmit through bone. Pack high-attenuation foam earplugs rated for industrial use, plus over-ear muffs if possible. The sound is structural rather than airborne, so complete silence is impossible; the goal is only to reduce its intensity enough to prevent headache and disorientation. If you are particularly sensitive to low-frequency vibration, test short exposures to similar tones beforehand.

**Temperature and air.** The air is still and cool, with no convection currents. Wear layered clothing that can be adjusted without removing outer layers—once inside, you will not want to create unnecessary motion or disturbance. A simple dust mask or respirator (even an N95) is advisable in case the white filaments release fine particles when disturbed, though they appear inert.

**Movement and surfaces.** Every surface is hard, curved crystal. Bring thin-soled shoes with good grip (approach shoes or climbing shoes) rather than thick boots that reduce feedback. Carry a short walking staff or pole to test footing and to help maintain balance on the polished ridges. Gloves are useful for bracing against columns.

**Psychological framing.** The absence of any change or living reference point can induce a peculiar time-disorientation within minutes. Decide in advance how long you intend to stay and set a timer; do not rely on your internal clock. Many visitors report a growing sense that the lattice is “finished” and that their presence is an error in an already completed system. Treat this sensation as environmental rather than personal.

**Exit protocol.** The pocket is sealed. Establish a reliable return method (portal marker, timed extraction, or companion outside) before entry. Nothing inside will help you leave.

The experience is essentially one of standing inside a finished geological chord. Expect the visit to feel less like exploration and more like being briefly tolerated by an object that predates and will outlast any human timescale.
