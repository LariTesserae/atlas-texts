**Preparation for the Plateau of Residual Fields**

You will be stepping onto a place that has not finished cooling from its own creation and whose smallest details are still under the influence of something older than its geology. Treat it less like a hike and more like entering a half-dormant piece of precision machinery the size of a cathedral.

**Footwear and movement**  
Ordinary hiking boots are insufficient. The basalt fractures are still sharp enough to slice leather or skin on contact. Bring rigid-soled boots with thick Vibram or equivalent soles and consider light titanium or carbon-composite shin guards. Every step must be placed with intent; the cushions of vegetation are not to be stepped on, and the empty stone between them carries faint residual lines that occasionally flare. Move slowly and test each foothold. Gloves are advisable for balance, but remove them when near the water veils—the lattice responds to skin oils and salts by slightly altering flow patterns.

**Hydration and moisture management**  
The air is saturated yet the plateau offers almost no drinkable water. The seeping sheets are in constant contact with the residual fields and should be considered non-potable. Carry all water you will need. Condensation will form continuously on skin, lenses, and metal; bring absorbent cloths and a small supply of silica-gel packs for optics. The humidity never quite becomes rain, so you will not get wet in the usual sense, but everything you carry will feel perpetually damp within minutes.

**Protection from the lattice**  
The invisible fields that hold vapor and redirect heat are the most unpredictable element. They do not appear to harm human tissue on short timescales, but they do interact with moving objects. Small ferrous items (watches, steel buckles, multitools) may tug or drift slightly toward certain fracture lines. Leave most metal behind or secure it. A simple compass is useless here anyway. If you carry any electronic device, keep it powered off unless needed; the spectrum shift at the edges of sunlight sometimes produces brief, localized static on sensitive circuits.

**Observation tools**  
Bring a small prism or diffraction grating to examine the altered sunlight; the prismatic fringes around shadows are not optical illusions but actual refraction changes. A pocket spectroscope will reveal more than a camera. For dusk, a red-filtered headlamp on its lowest setting is safest—the internal glow of the metallic leaf veins lasts only until the sun fully sets and is worth witnessing unlit. Do not use white light after the glow extinguishes; the sudden darkness is absolute except for the quartz seams.

**Minimal interference**  
The spacing behavior of the vegetation suggests the lattice still enforces certain boundaries. Do not place objects across the straight etched lines between cushions, even temporarily. The hovering water spheres in the coaxed bowls are extremely sensitive to disturbance; merely breathing directly above one can collapse it. If you wish to hear the faint glassy tone they produce, stand well back and wait for a natural formation.

**Timing and duration**  
A single visit should last no more than two hours. The first hour is best spent simply standing still and letting the private weather cycle reveal itself. The second hour, near dusk, is when the residual fields become most visible in the leaf veins. Depart before full darkness. The plateau offers no shelter, and the temperature drop once the lattice stops actively managing heat is rapid.

**Psychological note**  
This is not wilderness. It is a maintained equilibrium that has outlasted its makers. The plants, the water, the tiny segmented creatures, and the fields are all still negotiating with one another. Your presence is tolerated only because you are too large and too brief to register as a variable. Move with the same deliberate restraint the vegetation shows toward its neighbors, and the plateau will remain legible. Move carelessly and it will simply become incomprehensible or briefly hostile in small, precise ways.
