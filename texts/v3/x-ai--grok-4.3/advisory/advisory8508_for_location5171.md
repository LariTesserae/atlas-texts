**Preparation for the Gypsum Blister**

An ordinary traveler should treat this as a zero-gradient, closed-system environment that has eliminated every variable except its own sustained equilibrium. Assume that any introduced change (body heat beyond the ambient 37 °C, exhaled moisture, vibration, electromagnetic noise, or simple displacement of the creatures) will be immediately opposed by the chamber’s tendency to restore exact proportion. Therefore preparation centers on minimizing disturbance while staying alive inside an atmosphere that has never circulated.

**Atmosphere & Life Support**  
Bring a closed-loop rebreather with CO₂ scrubber and at least six hours of reserve. The chamber contains no convective currents; any local depletion of oxygen or buildup of humidity around your body will not diffuse away. The metallic “stone” odor suggests an anoxic or low-oxygen fraction—do not rely on the air. A lightweight positive-pressure suit with sealed gloves and boots is advisable; the creatures’ translucent carapaces and fine crystalline stalks will abrade skin and eyes if contact is prolonged.

**Acoustic & Sensory Protection**  
The metallic rasp is a single, unwavering chord produced by tens of thousands of synchronized pulses. Ear protection (active noise-cancelling muffs rated to at least 35 dB) is mandatory; prolonged exposure without it produces rapid fatigue and disorientation. Because light is emitted isotropically from ceiling veins, shadows are absent; depth perception collapses. Carry a small, narrow-beam lamp (preferably red or deep amber) and a set of matte-black reference rods or ranging sticks to restore parallax. Without them, simple tasks such as stepping over the hair-fine stalks become hazardous.

**Mobility & Access**  
The entrance is a recently parted knife-edge of gypsum and anhydrite. Pack a short pry bar or expanding wedge made of a softer metal (aluminum or copper) so the rock does not shatter and release fragments that would violate the floor’s precise occupation density. Once inside, move with extreme deliberation—treat every footfall as a potential perturbation. Magnetic or adhesive-soled overshoes can help maintain contact without crushing the thumb-sized fauna.

**Thermal & Electromagnetic Considerations**  
The salts already radiate body temperature; additional heat from electronics or lights must be minimized. Use passive thermochromic indicators rather than powered instruments where possible. The half-swallowed transformer still emits a faint, unchanging whine; carry a non-contact EMF meter and keep ferrous tools at least 30 cm from any fused conduit or coil to avoid inducing micro-currents that could register as change.

**Psychological & Temporal Framing**  
The environment is engineered to feel like a single instant indefinitely prolonged. Travelers commonly report a mounting impression that time itself has halted. Bring a mechanical watch with a clear second hand and a written checklist of exit criteria (e.g., “leave when oxygen reserve drops below 30 % or if two consecutive pulses of the creatures deviate from unison”). Without external temporal anchors, it becomes easy to remain far longer than planned.

**Contingency**  
Should the chamber interpret your presence as an imbalance, the only reliable exit is immediate, non-panicked retreat along the same narrow path used on entry. Do not attempt to “restore” anything—any attempt to reposition creatures, stalks, or artifacts will only increase the deviation. Seal the ridge behind you with a temporary plaster or expanding foam if the multiverse transit requires the blister to remain intact for future visits.

In short: arrive self-contained, acoustically and visually augmented, and prepared to treat the chamber’s perfect stasis as a fragile and hostile equilibrium rather than a peaceful one.
