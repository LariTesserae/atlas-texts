**Preparation for the Manufactured World**

Pack as if entering a single, kilometer-thick machine that has been running without interruption for geological time. The environment is not hostile in any dramatic sense, but it is relentlessly indifferent to biology and will test edges, seals, joints, and attention spans.

**Clothing and personal protection**  
Choose garments with no exposed fabric edges or dangling elements. The surface is covered in perfect right angles and unmachined cooling fins that will slice cloth, skin, and webbing without resistance. A single layer of tightly woven, matte-finished technical fabric (or light armor cloth) covering torso, arms, and legs is sufficient; add reinforced panels at knees and forearms. Footwear must have rigid soles and no tread patterns that can trap the small mechanisms—smooth or finely stippled rubber works best.  
Because the air maintains a constant high humidity and deposits micro-droplets continuously, bring a lightweight hooded shell that is vapor-permeable yet water-shedding; you will be damp, not soaked. Gloves should be thin enough for dexterity yet cut-resistant; you will be handling or steadying yourself against edges constantly. Eye protection (clear, non-fogging) is recommended; the droplets refract light in unexpected ways and can sting after hours of exposure.

**Navigation and orientation**  
The most dangerous feature is visual and spatial repetition. There are no landmarks, no variation in horizon line, and curvature is the only scale reference. Carry at least two independent position-tracking methods: a high-precision inertial navigation unit (or dead-reckoning watch) and a simple physical means—colored, non-reflective markers or a spool of thin line—to create a personal reference trail. Without these, it is easy to walk in what feels like a straight line and return to your starting point after many hours without realizing it.  
A small mirror or retro-reflective patch on a pole can serve as a distant aiming point when the haze and droplet sheen erase contrast.

**Supplies and physiology**  
All water on the surface is standing condensate; it is chemically pure but biologically inert and will not sustain human needs over time. Bring your own potable water and calorie-dense food for the entire intended stay plus a 50 % margin. Digestion continues normally; waste does not interact with the local mechanisms in any useful way, so carry disposal capability.  
The uniform warmth and continuous moisture encourage minor skin maceration and chafing. A simple barrier ointment and breathable underlayers mitigate this. The air appears oxygenated and non-toxic, but prolonged exposure to the constant aerosol will leave a faint mineral residue on teeth and mucous membranes; bring a mild oral rinse.

**Interaction with the mechanisms**  
The small jointed devices are not curious and will not divert from their routes to investigate you. They treat a human body exactly as they treat any other obstacle: they climb over or around it if the geometry permits, or wait in patient queues until a path clears. The only risk is accidental damage to their standardized interface ports if you sit or step directly on them. A light walking staff lets you gently displace clusters without injury to either party.  
Do not attempt to exchange modules or plug any personal equipment into the flush ports; the connectors are dimensionally precise and will damage anything not manufactured to the same specification.

**Psychological considerations**  
The acoustic environment is dominated by spaced condensation impacts and the subsonic motor hum. After several hours this produces a peculiar form of sensory monotony that can erode time sense. Many travelers report a compulsion to count drips or steps. Schedule deliberate rest periods with recorded audio or speech to anchor duration. The lack of any organic motion or scent also removes normal circadian cues; if staying longer than a local day, maintain an artificial light/dark cycle.

**Exit planning**  
Because nothing arrives or departs, departure is entirely under the traveler’s control. Mark your entry point with multiple redundant indicators (color, line, inertial coordinate) before moving any distance. The surface offers no natural shelter from its own weather; if a multiversal transit requires time to establish, erect a minimal insulating pad rather than lying directly on the alloy.

In short, treat the world as an infinite, weatherless, perfectly finished factory floor whose only processes are maintenance and crystal accretion. Everything else—food, orientation, bodily comfort, and the preservation of ordinary human scale—must be imported and actively maintained.
