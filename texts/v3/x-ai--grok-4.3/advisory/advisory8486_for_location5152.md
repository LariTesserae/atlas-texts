**Preparation for the Chamber**

This is not a place that rewards haste or modern expectations of control. It is a single, slow, living machine the size of a cathedral, suspended so high that the lowest struts vanish into cloud. An ordinary traveler should treat it as an environment that has already reached equilibrium with itself; your job is to pass through without breaking that balance.

**Footwear and movement**  
Wear boots with aggressive, flexible soles that can grip wet composite mesh without catching on rivets or ribs. The floor is a single vast plate, but it has texture, slight give in places, and occasional shallow trays of standing water. Do not bring anything with hard, slick soles or aggressive lugs that could damage the mesh or the thin biological crust that has formed in the low spots. Carry a lightweight staff or pole—three meters, non-conductive—for testing footing around pipe runs and for steadying yourself when a distant piston shifts and sends a low-pressure wave through the structure. The chamber is so large that you will walk for many minutes between machine-islands; plan routes by visible landmarks (a particular camshaft, a thick vine curtain) rather than by distance.

**Clothing and microclimate**  
The air is warm, humid, and smells of warm oil and stone. Dress in lightweight, quick-drying layers that wick moisture yet protect skin from constant fine condensation. A light hooded garment with a stiff brim helps against the deliberate, heavy drips that fall from the upper vaults at irregular intervals. Bring nothing that will absorb and hold oil; the mist that rises from the engines coats everything eventually. A simple cloth mask or breathable scarf is useful near active pipe runs where the oil smell is strongest, though the air itself is breathable.

**Sensory adaptation**  
The engines turn on geological time. Their sound is not loud in the usual sense but pervasive: a continuous low-frequency pressure that travels through the mesh and into bone. Most people acclimate within an hour, but sensitive travelers should bring soft earplugs for the first passage across open floor. At the same time, the sound carries information; learn to distinguish the normal breathing from the rarer, sharper notes that mean a valve has reseated or a linkage has finally bound. Vision is high-contrast: bright shafts through the quartz lenses during daylight, then dim city-glow reflection at night. A small, warm-spectrum headlamp with a red filter preserves night vision without startling the pale moths.

**Biological considerations**  
The fauna is already established. The large plate-winged moths are slow; they avoid moving objects but will brush past if you stand in their patrol paths. The lizards and metallic beetles stay close to warm housings and are not aggressive. Do not crush them underfoot or attempt to collect them. The gardens are self-watering; disturbing a vine curtain or stepping into a tray can send a pulse of water down through the mesh and alter local humidity for days. Walk around the living architecture rather than through it.

**Tools and supplies**  
Carry only what you can sling across your body. A small sealed container for water (the chamber’s water is already in use by the plants). A compact multi-tool with ceramic blades—metal against metal here has a way of leaving lasting marks. No drones, no bright lights, no adhesives or markers. If you must leave a trace, use loose stones or bits of slag already fallen from the structure; the chamber absorbs them.

**Mindset and duration**  
The machines move in hours and days, not seconds. Sit for a full engine cycle on one of the drier catwalks if you want to understand the place; most visitors leave too quickly and remember only the scale and the sound. The chamber does not need you, but it will tolerate a careful visitor who moves at its pace, keeps to the open mesh where possible, and accepts that condensation will fall, engines will turn, and vines will continue their slow work of turning abandoned machinery into architecture.

You are entering an organism that has already finished becoming. Arrive ready to be small, quiet, and temporary.
