**Preparation for the Ridgeline City**

Pack for constant cold damp and surfaces that are never fully dry. Bring layered wool or synthetic base layers that wick condensation, topped with a lightweight, seam-sealed shell that can shrug off brief vertical rain without holding water. Footwear should be stiff-soled boots with aggressive tread and a high rand; the rock is still shedding sharp quartz and mica flakes, and the fern ledges are slick with perpetual film. Carry a pair of lightweight crampon-style overshoes—the kind used for verglas—because even the iron scaffolds collect a thin, treacherous glaze.

The air is thin and carries a faint metallic bite that lingers on the tongue after reality glitches. Bring a simple particulate respirator (P100 or equivalent) and a small supply of electrolyte lozenges; the aftertaste can turn mildly nauseating when fractures in perception occur. Vision will be compromised at all times. A headlamp with a deep red filter is useful for preserving night adaptation, but rely primarily on a chemical glow-stick or a low-output blue chem-light baton—the locals’ lanterns already use this spectrum and your eyes will adapt faster to it.

Expect the ground itself to behave like wet paint. Include a compact walking staff or ice axe with a wrist leash; when a length of railing suddenly casts two shadows, the extra point of contact helps maintain balance until the glitch resolves. A small mirror or polished metal card can serve as both signaling device and quick check for multiple reflections. Carry one mechanical watch (quartz or automatic) and one wind-up analog clock; sound sometimes arrives before its vibration, so being able to compare elapsed time against perceived delay gives an external reference when the ridge groans and the mills sigh out of sync.

Fauna requires modest precautions rather than weaponry. The six-legged grazers are skittish but their hooves scar stone; keep at least two body-lengths of clearance on fern ledges. The beetle-turbine creatures under the platforms are drawn to vibration—your footsteps on metal will be felt through the soles—so move deliberately and pause often. The luminous larvae are harmless in small numbers but their collective hum can mask the deeper ridge-settling sounds that warn of new fractures. A lightweight mesh hood or wide-brim hat keeps drifting larvae out of eyes and mouth during evening travel.

Navigation and shelter are the real variables. The city accumulates rather than expands, so maps are useless after a few days. Carry a coil of 50 m of 7 mm static line and a handful of pitons or clamp anchors; platforms and scaffolds are still being accepted by the mountain and may shift. A compact bivy that can be clipped to scaffolding is preferable to looking for enclosed rooms—the foundry sheds exhale cool vapor that pools and reduces visibility to a few meters. Most structures remain half-open by design.

Finally, adopt the local tempo. Nothing mechanical here moves faster than one tooth per hour. Walk, speak, and decide at that same deliberate rate. Rushing increases the chance that a fracture will open underfoot or that a shadow-duplication will leave you momentarily disoriented on an exposed cantilever. If you move like the ridge is still negotiating its own shape, the place will tolerate your presence while it finishes drying.
