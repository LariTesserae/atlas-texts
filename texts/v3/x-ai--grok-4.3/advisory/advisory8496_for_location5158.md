**Preparation for the Substratum Expanse**

This place is an active, unfinished industrial undercroft—cold, wet, deafening, and built entirely of fresh metal that still feels like it’s vibrating from the day it was poured. An ordinary traveler needs to treat it like a cross between a half-built submarine, a meatpacking plant running at full speed, and a cave system that was never meant for people.

**Thermal and contact protection (non-negotiable)**  
The temperature sits right at freezing with constant evaporative cooling from all the dripping water. You will lose heat fast through your feet and hands. Wear:
- Insulated waterproof boots with aggressive tread (the grated walkways get slick with condensate and the occasional mineral slime).
- Thick, lined gloves you can still work in. Never touch any metal surface bare-handed; the alloy holds cold like a heat sink and will instantly stick to skin.
- Layered synthetic or wool clothing under a waterproof shell. The air is humid enough that cotton will soak through and stay wet. Bring spare gloves and socks; once your hands are cold and wet, dexterity collapses quickly.

**Hearing and vision**  
Industrial noise is constant and layered: bass thrum through the decking, high-pitched valve hisses, irregular alarms, and the never-ending drip percussion. Good over-ear protection that still lets you hear warnings or approaching machinery is essential. The light is terrible—flickering sodium strips behind mist, occasional red strobes, and sparse blue cold-lamps. Bring a reliable headlamp with a red filter option (to preserve night vision in the darker sections) plus a handheld light with a wide beam for fog penetration. The mist scatters light; narrow, focused beams cut through it better than floods.

**Footwear and movement**  
The environment is full of sharp edges, raised burrs, and open drainage trenches. Sturdy, closed-toe boots with ankle support are mandatory. Watch the gaps between walkways—some are wide enough for a rail-cart and drop into lower flooded galleries. Move deliberately; everything is metal on metal and the surfaces have almost no give.

**Respiratory and contamination awareness**  
There’s visible oily film on many surfaces and a faint chemical tang in the air from whatever the machinery is processing or leaking. A simple particulate respirator or even a good dust mask cuts down on breathing in aerosolized oil and whatever the biofilms are shedding. The water dripping everywhere is industrial runoff—do not drink it, and assume any open wound exposed to it needs cleaning later.

**Navigation and orientation**  
The lattice is deliberately repetitive and the ceiling keeps rising in new tiers. Bring a simple compass or inertial tracker; magnetic or inertial navigation works better than trying to follow sightlines through the fog. Note the builder’s tape and markings on newer catwalks—these are the only real landmarks. The maintenance automata (the beetle-shaped ones) follow fixed routes; they will ignore you unless you block an access hatch, but stepping into their path can spook them into defensive behavior.

**Sensory and psychological preparation**  
Expect a low-grade sensory assault. The sound never stops, the light never settles, and the scale makes everything feel slightly off. If you’re prone to disorientation or mild claustrophobia/agoraphobia, this combination can be unpleasant. Short visits with frequent returns to a better-lit staging area are wiser than long explorations on the first trip.

**Minimal kit additions that pay off**  
- A small thermos of warm liquid (your hands will thank you).
- Zip ties or tape for quick repairs on gloves or clothing torn by burrs.
- A whistle or loud personal alarm—useful if you slip into a lower level and the machinery masks normal shouting.
- Something bright and reflective to mark your route back; the sodium lighting washes out most colors.

The Expanse rewards slow, methodical movement and punishes haste or exposed skin. It is not actively hostile, but it is indifferent and mechanically indifferent—exactly the kind of environment where small oversights compound quickly. Pack like you’re spending a cold, wet shift in an active shipyard that was built yesterday and never quite finished.
