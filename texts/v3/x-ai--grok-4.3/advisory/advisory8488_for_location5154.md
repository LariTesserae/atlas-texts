**Preparation for the Basin of Equilibrium**

This is a place that has finished happening. The silence is not an absence but a completed state; every surface has already reached its final configuration. Treat the visit as an intrusion upon a finished system. Your goal is not exploration or discovery but to pass through without adding any new events.

**Physical Preparation**

Wear a sealed, low-abrasion suit with integrated hood and full-face respirator. The air carries sub-visible mineral motes that settle and do not rise; ordinary clothing will trap them against skin and fabric, and within hours they produce a dry, itching film. The respirator should filter particulates down to the sub-micron range while allowing normal breathing. Bring your own water and food in sealed containers; nothing here is potable and the dryness accelerates dehydration.

Footwear must be stiff-soled with excellent ankle support. The floor consists of overlapping mineral plates that never shift under weight but create irregular, low steps. Soft soles will conform to the edges and produce fatigue. Gloves should be thin but durable; you may need to steady yourself against monoliths, and the compacted salts and silicates are not kind to bare palms.

**Visual and Spatial Preparation**

The suspended shells will occlude and reveal themselves depending on viewing angle. This creates a constant, low-level visual noise that the brain interprets as motion even when nothing moves. Spend time beforehand training with environments that produce similar effects (dense wire sculptures, lenticular prints, or calibrated VR) until the disorientation becomes familiar rather than alarming. Carry no bright lights; illumination should be diffuse and low. Sudden brightness causes the shells to flash opaque, which can produce momentary vertigo.

The duplicated terrain patches are stable. When you encounter a vertical seam where the world appears twice, cross it at a steady walking pace without looking back. The seam itself produces no physical sensation, but lingering near it increases the chance of noticing further duplications that were not previously visible. Do not attempt to map the overlaps; they are not errors but permanent features.

**Interaction Rules**

Do not touch the suspended shells. Their positions are part of the fixed arrangement; displacing one will produce a soft click that propagates through the density until the configuration re-equilibrates. The sound is the only audible event you are likely to cause, and it will feel louder than it should because of the surrounding silence.

The cairns and wedged tools are not artifacts in the archaeological sense. They are also in equilibrium. Leave them exactly as found. If you must move past one, step around rather than over; the plates they rest upon are stable only in their current stacking.

**Temporal and Psychological Preparation**

There is no day-night cycle or weather. Your subjective sense of time will stretch or compress depending on how long you remain still. Bring a mechanical watch rather than any electronic timepiece; the duplicated zones can produce minor electromagnetic inconsistencies. Set an absolute exit timer before entry and honor it without debate. The temptation to remain “just a little longer” to observe the lack of change is surprisingly strong.

The silence and visual stasis can produce a dissociative calm that makes ordinary urgency feel distant. Counter this by rehearsing a short, factual checklist to recite aloud at fixed intervals: “I am visiting. I will leave. Nothing here requires my presence.”

**Exit Protocol**

When you decide to depart, retrace your exact incoming path rather than seeking a new route. The duplicated patches make new paths unreliable for return. Once you have crossed the outer ring of monoliths, pause for several minutes and inspect your suit and gear for any mineral motes or shell fragments before removing the respirator. Nothing from this location should be carried elsewhere; the equilibrium does not travel well.
