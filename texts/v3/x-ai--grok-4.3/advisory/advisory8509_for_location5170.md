**Preparation begins with accepting that you will enter an organism, not a place.** The chamber is not a cave that happens to contain plants; it is continuous living tissue. Every surface will treat your presence as a local change in humidity, pressure, and chemistry. You cannot remain external to it.

### Physical conditioning and clothing
Train in sustained high-heat, high-humidity conditions (sauna protocols or equatorial fieldwork). The air does not move; sweat will not evaporate efficiently. Lightweight, loose, long-fiber natural clothing (linen or hemp) is preferable to synthetics, which trap moisture against skin and promote maceration. Footwear should be thin-soled and flexible; stiff boots will puncture or bruise the leaf canopy and root terraces. Carry no metal buckles, zippers, or frames—the tissue will slowly grow around or over them if you remain still for long periods.

Pack nothing that requires dryness. Electronics will fog internally within minutes. Paper maps will wilt. Bring only what can be discarded or absorbed: small quantities of salt, glucose, and a single waterproof note in plant-based ink if you must leave a marker. The chamber recycles its own water in closed loops; any water you introduce will be absorbed and redistributed by the bladders and mycelium.

### Biological and medical considerations
Expect continuous 30–35 °C heat and near-100 % relative humidity. Core body temperature will rise steadily. Plan for no more than four to six hours of continuous exposure on a first visit, with mandatory exit and re-acclimatization. Fungal and bacterial skin infections are probable; apply a broad antifungal barrier before entry. Inhale the air through a light organic-fiber mask on first entry—spores and mycelial fragments are present even if macro-fauna is absent. The translucent mites and beetles are harmless at human scale, but their gut flora is not.

The constant uniform illumination will suppress melatonin production. Bring a truly opaque sleep mask and ear protection; the only external sound is irregular dripping that the tissue muffles into near-silence. Without these, circadian disruption occurs within a single prolonged stay.

### Navigation and psychological framing
The space offers almost no visual differentiation once inside. Older woody ribs and former cistern depressions exist, but they are already being incorporated and will look increasingly similar to newer tissue after hours of exposure. Mark your path only with organic, non-toxic dyes that the mycelium can eventually metabolize; otherwise you leave permanent foreign inclusions. A compass is useless—the chamber has no magnetic reference and no consistent verticality once leaf terraces begin to slope.

Mentally rehearse that time moves differently here. Growth is occurring, but at rates invisible to human perception. You will feel as though nothing is changing, yet upon exit you may discover that small cuts or abrasions on your skin have been lightly colonized by the same white mycelial felt that coats the mineral veins. The sensation is usually described as a faint, persistent itching that ceases only after thorough external decontamination.

### Exit protocol
Do not linger until fatigue sets in. The equilibrium state of the chamber does not become hostile, but it does not become helpful either. Once moisture has saturated your clothing and hair, thermoregulation becomes increasingly difficult. When you leave, the abrupt return to moving air and directional light will feel violent; allow several minutes of stillness at the threshold before re-entering ordinary environments.

The chamber does not want visitors, but it will not resist them. It will simply continue its existing process with you inside it, adjusting locally and forgetting you were ever there.
