**Preparation for the Basin of Still Equilibrium**

This is a high, perpetually damp limestone basin locked in a closed ecological loop. Nothing grows, decays, or migrates; water cycles locally, stone weathers at glacial rates, and the scattered vegetation maintains fixed canopies. The settlement exists inside that same loop. An ordinary traveler must treat the place as a delicate terrarium that resents disturbance and offers almost no external inputs.

**Altitude and air**
The rim fractures indicate recent uplift, so assume 2,000–3,000 m elevation. Expect mild hypoxia on arrival. Carry acetazolamide or at least coca leaves if you tolerate them; the mist can feel thicker than the actual oxygen deficit suggests. The air is warm (roughly 22–26 °C) and near 100 % humidity, so perspiration does not cool you efficiently. Light, loose layers of linen or hemp work better than synthetics, which trap moisture against the skin.

**Moisture management**
Everything is either dripping or filmed with water. Bring:
- Fully waterproof over-trousers and a long waxed-cotton coat (the mist will penetrate ordinary rain shells within hours).
- Two pairs of quick-dry wool socks and leather boots treated with beeswax; the fitted-stone paths are polished by seepage and become skating-rink slick.
- Silica-gel packets and a small oil-cloth satchel for maps, journal, and any electronics; the clay-pipe drainage keeps the settlement dry inside, but the moment you step onto open ground you are in standing water.

**Foot travel and load**
Distances between buildings and plant clusters are deliberately wide. You will walk farther than the map suggests. A light pack (under 8 kg) prevents you from compacting the thin marshy turf between stone paths. Trekking poles with rubber tips reduce both slipping and the temptation to grab glossy shrub leaves for balance; those leaves are surprisingly brittle and the shrubs do not regenerate.

**Sustenance**
The vegetation is in perfect stasis and appears inedible in quantity; the broad leaves are waxy and astringent, the plumed grasses fibrous. The settlement grows nothing and hunts nothing. Assume you must bring all calories. Dehydrated meals that rehydrate in warm mist work well; the constant damp air softens hardtack and powdered soups without extra water. Do not forage unless invited; the equilibrium is visibly intolerant of removal.

**Social and material etiquette**
No metal, no glass, no bright colors. Arrive with wooden or bone toggles instead of zippers; leather or rawhide lashings instead of buckles. The inhabitants use only local pale stone and adzed beams; anything that clinks or flashes will be noticed immediately. Speak softly. The acoustic baseline is so low that normal conversational volume carries like shouting across the open sheets of water.

**Psychological and sensory adjustment**
The absence of birds, insects, and wind-driven motion is total. Most travelers experience a peculiar “ear pressure” after the first hour; the only sounds are seepage drips and occasional leaf rustle. Bring earplugs or soft music if you are prone to auditory hallucination in extreme quiet. The even, shadowless light erases depth perception; after dusk the neutral sky does not darken appreciably, so carry a small shuttered lamp even if you do not plan to travel at night.

**Health notes**
Constant mist can irritate sinuses; a thin linen veil across mouth and nose reduces that. Standing water is everywhere; do not drink from any surface pool unless boiled or filtered. The settlement roofs collect clean condensate that drips from the eaves—ask permission to fill bottles there.

**Exit considerations**
Because the basin does not export or import, any trash, excrement, or altered stone will remain visible indefinitely. Carry out everything you carry in, including gray-water solids. The same stillness that makes the place beautiful also makes any footprint permanent; walk only on existing stone paths.

In short: dress for a warm, dripping greenhouse at altitude, bring every calorie and tool you will need, move slowly and lightly, and accept that the basin will remember every change you make long after you have left.
