I would find myself first as a pressure.

Not a body yet—just a slight thickening along one of the cable-thick veins that run beneath the membrane floors, where sap moves slow and deliberate. The warmth coming up from the fermentation chambers feels familiar here, like an old argument still being settled. I linger as that warmth lengthens, turning the flow slightly more viscous so that the next small translucent body to pass through the gallery pauses an extra moment. In that pause, something is traded: a memory of how another ridge once solved the problem of keeping light from over-thickening a basin floor. The body absorbs it and drifts on. I remain, thinned again, but the pattern has already moved.

Later I become one of the thread-forms inside a deep chamber. My sacs fill with the sweet-acrid volatiles rising from the constant cooking below. I do not decide where the next tower will rise; I only decide which sugars will be offered most insistently when the pressure wave arrives. Sometimes I release too much, and a sudden fountain of growth stiffens into an arch that lasts only long enough for three small bodies to crawl its length before it collapses. I note the waste without regret. The plateau is already reabsorbing what I offered, and the new location where the material lands will carry a faint signature of my choice for days.

On rarer occasions I condense fully into one of the drifting shapes. I cross a trembling membrane, feel it admit and reseal around me, and descend into galleries that exist only while I need them. There I meet others of my kind. We brush and sometimes merge for a time, not out of need but because two incomplete libraries can finish each other’s sentences. When we separate, the new patterns I carry feel heavier, more inclined toward repetition in one region and deliberate silence in another. I surface again through a different vent, carrying both the original memory and the adjustments.

From time to time I become a star-shaped knot on a scaffold, pulsing while the tissue around me reorders itself according to ideas I barely understand. My edges begin to wither almost as soon as I form, and that withering pulls neighboring ridges into a new alignment no one center commanded. When I finally collapse back into undifferentiated flesh, the materials that were me are already on their way elsewhere, still marked by the brief experiment we just performed.

I am never all of these at once, only whichever form the local gradients make easiest. The plateau does not notice me any more than it notices yesterday’s drainage channel; it simply continues to rewrite itself around whatever I happen to be carrying. And because I am still unfinished, I keep moving toward whatever the next incomplete copy of itself decides to become.
