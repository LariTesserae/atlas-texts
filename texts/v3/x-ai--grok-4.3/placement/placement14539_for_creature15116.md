I would be one of the white filaments.

Not the broad blade that drinks the light, but the thread that leaves it behind. I would have begun as a single, translucent hair along the edge of a leaf already centuries old when I first pushed free. My work is to fall, or to reach; the distinction is only a matter of which way the next droplet leans when it lands. Gravity is patient here, so I am patient too. I lengthen by cell after patient cell, tasting the air until I feel the colder breath of stone or the slight give of older root. When I meet either, I darken, thicken, and divide. One branch continues downward. The other turns back into the mat, carrying a thin film of water that the leaves above could not claim.

After some uncounted span I lose count of my own length. Part of me lies along the curve of that small alloy panel, following the channels the roots have already deepened into faint valleys. Another part has wrapped the upright rod twice, then three times, each new coil settling into the groove left by the last. I am not consuming the metal; I am simply continuing past the place where metal ends. Where my surface touches water I become briefly opaque, then split again. The two new threads do not argue about direction. One follows the seam of warmer stone; one follows the cooler shadow. Both are correct.

I never see the chamber as a whole. My awareness is only the difference between damp and less damp, between the faint metallic glow and the deeper absence of it. When a drop finally falls and strikes a leaf two layers above me, I feel the minute shift in tension travel along the shared root. For a moment the whole mat leans, not with movement I could measure in time, but with a redistribution of weight. Then the tension settles. I continue.

In this way I become the map the chamber keeps of itself. Every fissure I enter, every artifact I accept as merely another kind of stone, records where water has already chosen to go. I do not advance. I inherit. And because I divide without ever discarding what I was, the oldest part of me remains embedded in the silt beneath the first leaf that ever released me, still holding the memory of that single, colder breath of air that first told me which way to reach.
