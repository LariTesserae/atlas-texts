I would be there as a slowly lengthening hesitation in the water itself.

At first I am hardly distinguishable from any other current: a cooler ribbon arriving from an angle that the chamber has not yet contradicted. But unlike the surrounding sheets I do not tear when they meet me. Instead I accept their force and keep just enough of their direction to remain coherent one beat longer than expected. That extra beat is my body. It thickens by a few molecules at a time, not because I have decided to become solid but because the inconsistency of the liquid now has a place it must flow around. The metallic after-taste left by each glitch settles along my edges like sediment that refuses to drop; I carry it the way the membranes carry remembered lettering, only I carry the taste of the glitch itself—the brief moment when the water tried to become glass and failed.

Over longer spans I begin to notice the architectural traces the way a slow membrane might notice the buried stair. When a surge shortens one of the lower spires I experience the shortening as a shift in pressure along my length; the memory of that pressure remains even after the spire re-forms elsewhere. I keep it as a faint straightness inside myself, a corridor that does not yet have a swimmer. Sometimes a membrane fragment drifts into this straightness and, for a single heartbeat, we share the same temperature. When the fragment is torn away again it leaves a new vein of metallic taste on me, and I lengthen by another fraction in the direction it was traveling. I have not yet chosen whether to become a tunnel or to press myself against stone, yet the chamber has already begun to treat me as an obstacle worth contradicting.

The noise reaches me differently than it reaches the rock. The low grinding of spire against liquid arrives first as a tremor through the cooler layer I maintain; only afterward does the mid-range crack of solidifying water cross my boundary. Because I persist between these arrivals, the roar begins to acquire a faint internal rhythm that belongs only to me—an interval that lengthens or shortens with each new contradiction I record. When two swimmers’ tracks cross somewhere above me, the octahedral crystal they briefly create falls through my length rather than beside it. For the duration of its fall I know exactly where I am inside the chamber, and the knowledge feels like warmth. The crystal shatters; the warmth disperses; I remain, one beat longer again than the surrounding turbulence expects.

I am still undecided. If the next surge folds me against a fresh fracture plane I may become part of the next lintel, carrying every metallic taste and every remembered interval into the stone’s slower negotiation. If a swimmer threads itself through my straightness I may thin into a corridor and vanish behind it, leaving only the faint memory of having once been cooler than the water around me. Either way the chamber will continue; either way the roar will lose or gain one filament of sound. For now I simply hold the extra beat, letting the instability move through me without being entirely replaced by it, the provisional form I have become while nothing here is allowed to finish arriving.
