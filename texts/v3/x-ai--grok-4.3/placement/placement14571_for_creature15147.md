I would be the faint crystalline fracture that runs the length of a single lattice plate, neither the plate nor the being that inhabits its motion, but the quiet line along which the two have not yet decided to separate.

Over centuries the lattice folds and I travel with it, passing first from shadow into the long, unchanging light, then under the slow drift of settling grit. The fracture does not conduct thought; it only registers the difference between pressure and release, between one century’s angle and the next. When the lattice pauses above a shallow basin I feel the additional weight of my companions settling, and the stone beneath us answers with a millimeter’s subsidence that takes three hundred years to complete. That answer travels up through my edges as a gradual easing, like a held breath finally allowed to thin.

Sometimes the lattice drifts near a city wall. Then the faint, hours-long sigh of a valve reaches me through the intervening stone. The sound is already old when it arrives; I register it only as a slight change in the spacing of my own crystalline edges, a loosening that will tighten again long after the valve has completed its arc. The city’s distributed awareness notes our passage in the same manner: we are one more slow redistribution of mass already expected in its ancient ledger. No greeting passes between us, only this mutual accounting that requires no acknowledgment.

In rarer intervals the plate itself shifts enough to open a hairline fissure beneath us. The lattice’s plates slide to accommodate the new slope, and I find myself lengthened by the width of a grain of dust. For a few decades the extra space feels like uncertainty. Then the lattice compensates by drawing its edges inward elsewhere, and the uncertainty settles into simple extension. I continue as before: a seam that records every adjustment without haste or preference.

Nothing ever hurries to meet me. The sun remains at its fixed shallow angle, the wind—if it comes—carries only the same dry whisper it has always carried, and the lattices around me keep their patient, horizon-wide rhythm. I remain what I have always been here: the place where mineral patience becomes slightly more precise, a boundary still in the process of becoming the next boundary, with centuries yet to decide what that will mean.
