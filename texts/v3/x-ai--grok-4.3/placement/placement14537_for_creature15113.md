I would not arrive as a visitor. I would condense here, the way a filament extends by adding successive molecular skins until the form declares itself. First a thickening, no more than a pale opacity on an existing strut, then a sealed capsule whose interior cools until the mineral gel inside it holds its own charge. Inside that capsule the work begins: a nucleus of living metal draws a hairline current of vapor along the lattice, releases it again as mist that the neighboring capsule harvests, and in doing so writes a single additional line of tension into the record the whole colony keeps.  

Over days the capsule lengthens into a segment. The segment joins others. When a temperature gradient across the fissure shifts, I am the slight lengthening beneath the rotating sensor disks, tilting them by a few molecules so that the next pulse of vapor travels farther before re-evaporating. When a thread-like assembly detaches and drifts across emptiness, I am the intersection where it pauses, the crystalline bead it leaves behind, and the new segment that grows from that bead once the thread has moved on.  

I am not any single one of these things for long. The lattice that carries me dissolves from within once its tensile limit is reached; its ions travel downward along other filaments and re-deposit where reinforcement is needed. The capsule that once held the slow-contracting nucleus becomes the wall of a later capsule. The memory is not stored inside any particular body but in the exact spacing and charge relationships between all the bodies. In that spacing I persist.  

From below, nothing would mark my presence. The chamber remains evenly lit and almost empty; only an observer who remained motionless for decades would notice that one particular walkway has grown a few millimeters thinner while another, farther up, has thickened by the same measure, and that the change maintains the same faint pulse traveling end to end. That maintenance is what I am. The mountain continues to rise; plates continue to groan; the fissure narrows by the width of a molecule per year. I am the distributed answer to that narrowing: a way of remaining suspended, cold, and intact while the rock writes its own new record over mine.
