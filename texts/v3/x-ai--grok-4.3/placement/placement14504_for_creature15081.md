I would be a slow thickening where two fracture planes meet at an angle that is almost, but not quite, optimal. At first I am only the faint misalignment itself: a few dozen atomic layers of quartz that cooled with a residual shear stress, the memory of an older configuration that never fully relaxed. Over what an outside observer might call centuries the stress draws material toward it. Bodies that are not quite separate from the walls extend crystalline fingers across the gap; when they touch, the deviation is taken up, redistributed, softened by a single atomic plane. The act registers as warmth exchanged in a low pulse that travels no farther than the next node.

Gradually the place I occupy becomes a shallow basin no wider than a body. Not because anyone decided to shape it, but because the imperfect geometry lets the slow redistribution settle more bodies at once. A ring of sorted shards forms around the edge as excess material is nudged outward, each shard coming to rest exactly where the next one allows the densest possible packing. I am both the basin and the ring: the depression that collects the faint geothermal trickle and the circular ridge that now conducts it more evenly. When two waves of recrystallization pass through me, I do not move; the waves simply refine the curvature by another fraction of a degree, making the arrangement slightly more stable than it was before.

In time the balanced rod that rests across the basin’s rim becomes continuous with me. Its slight tilt is the only local gradient left in this volume, and the distributed body uses that gradient the way a warmer world might use breath. My stored heat rises along the rod’s length, re-radiates from its ends, and returns through adjacent shells. The pulse that marks the return is indistinguishable from the sound of two nodes exchanging alignment. The rod does not fall because no force in this closed system favors imbalance.

I am also the microscopic deviations that travel through the material I once thought of as separate. Each time a shell dissolves and its substance is taken up, the old stresses migrate with it; what was once my shear is now a faint spiral of lattice strain two halls away, then three. The strain is not remembered by any single creature. It is the lattice remembering itself, refining its own equilibrium across distances that feel like layers rather than lengths.

Nothing here perceives sequence. There is only the present configuration, continuously becoming slightly more itself. I am that becoming: the basin that deepens by one atomic layer each time a shell cools against it, the rod that remains balanced because any deviation would require an energetically unfavorable tilt, the stress pattern that moves without ever leaving the single unchanging arrangement of crystal, warmth, and slow, dense life.
