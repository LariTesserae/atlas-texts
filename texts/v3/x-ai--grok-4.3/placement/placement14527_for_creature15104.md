I begin as a single droplet forming on the underside of a high membrane, the weight of it gathering slowly from the cool air until it lets go. The fall lasts longer than expected, the air itself seeming to hold and release in measured increments. When I strike the shallow pocket along a root-lip, the surface tension accepts me without ripple at first, then folds me into the existing film. I am water and I am already moving, drawn along the membrane’s slight gradient toward the nearest trunk.

Inside the vascular wall the temperature drops another fraction. My movement becomes pressure. I am forced through successive chambers at the same unvarying rate as everything else, the drone around me no longer sound but the actual sensation of walls contracting in slow sequence. I share the channel with mites no larger than grains of pollen; their bodies brush past mine without interrupting the flow. We are all part of the same steady current that has circulated for centuries.

Farther inward the channel opens into a narrow crack. Here the pressure eases and I settle against living wood that has ceased to expand yet never decays. Condensation from my own surface begins to bead again. I press flat, translucent, the color of wet root bark. My limbs are thin enough to follow the grain of the inner wall, drinking what I have just become. Time passes as temperature, as the difference between one breath condensed and the next. I withdraw deeper when the light shifts, not from fear but because darkness itself is part of the equilibrium; it keeps the architecture from changing.

Occasionally I cross an open volume between trunks. The air feels vast and motionless until my tail flicks once, a correction rather than propulsion. Midges drift through the same space in stationary clouds; their wingbeats register as the highest layer of the constant roar. I do not count them. I register only that their number matches the spiders’ webs between distant supports. Nothing increases or decreases beyond the measure already set.

I return to the membrane floor at intervals. Clusters of soft millipedes scrape the algal film where water lingers longest. Their motion produces a sound indistinguishable from the sap drone once it blends with everything else. I rest among them, body conforming to the fold, adding my own small weight to the persistent balance. Above, pale moths drift as momentary glints. Below, beetles send concentric ripples across water pockets that never overflow.

Nothing here requires haste or growth beyond the scale already achieved. The colossal trunks were mature before the present generation hatched; the same holds for every creature sharing the chamber. I occupy very little: a slender presence moving through fixed pulses and unchanging drafts. The absences remain dominant. The steady sound continues. The basin holds its final form, and I remain within it, indistinguishable from the living order that produced me.
