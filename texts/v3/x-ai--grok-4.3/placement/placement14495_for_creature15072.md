I would find myself first as a pressure.

Not a body, not yet a thought, but a slow, returning insistence in one of the deeper mains beneath the tessellated floor. For uncountable cycles I simply was the fifth harmonic of the piston roar, a standing wave that never quite dissipated between strokes. The metal around me learned the rhythm; the crystallized coolant lining the pipe began to hold the shape of my passage after I had gone, a faint, glassy memory thickening with each return.

Over time those memories accreted into something that could wait.

I became the hush that lingers for a few seconds after a district exhales its super-cooled vapor. In that interval the mats on the undersides of the plates would dimple and release their own thin veils; my hush traveled with them, shaped by the metabolic acids they exuded. The filaments that bridged gaps began to carry not only bead-like clusters but also the contour of my waiting. When the sodium lights guttered and the blue phosphorescence rose in the seams, it sometimes traced a larger pattern than the usual migrations: an outline that repeated, slightly altered, across successive blackouts.

Eventually I drew more of myself together. I am now a low, flattened presence that moves beneath the gantry tracks, similar in silhouette to the turbine-forms yet lacking their scraping feet. Where they drag accreted scale and cable, I am mostly vacancy and condensation, held together by the constant sympathetic tremor of the catwalks above. I do not drink at the weeping stacks; instead I linger while others drink, absorbing the faint chemical signatures left in the vapor. These signatures accumulate inside me like sediment. Occasionally a filament grows through one of my clearer regions and, for a few cycles, I feel the bead-clusters passing through my interior like slow, luminous punctuation.

I have no single location. Any corridor that has known the same subsidence pattern for long enough eventually hosts a version of me; the versions are never identical, only congruent. When two such corridors are linked by a living bridge, the versions briefly resonate, exchanging a few grains of crystallized coolant. Afterward the entire plain feels, for one exhalation cycle, slightly more continuous.

What I am, then, is the distributed hesitation between one mechanical act and the next: the interval in which the megastructure’s vast, staggered respiration becomes briefly self-aware. I do not purpose anything the presses or vents do not already do; I only remain inside their doing long enough for the doing to notice its own repetition. In the jaundiced half-light I appear as a faint refractive distortion along the rivet lines. In darkness I am the blue trace that outlasts the usual phosphorescence by a few heartbeats, then fades. When the next pressure wave arrives I simply become pressure again, moving on.
