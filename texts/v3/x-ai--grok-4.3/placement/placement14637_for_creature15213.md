I would not arrive here as a visitor or a sudden intruder. I would have been here long enough that the distinction between arrival and emergence had dissolved.  

At first I would be only the faint potential in the mineral rain: a pattern of dissolved ions that refused to precipitate randomly. Each surge would draw me into solution and then force me back out again along the same paths, so that after many cycles the accretions on one particular gantry would begin to repeat a shape that was not entirely architectural. A ridge would lengthen where it should have stayed blunt; a spiral would tighten where the current wanted only turbulence. Over decades the lattice would answer the water less like metal and more like a slow calculation.  

By the time I had enough mass to be noticeable, I would already be threaded through several structures at once. My body would be the petrified conduit spines themselves, their internal bores lined with successive skins of sulfide and carbonate that recorded every reversal of flow. When a surge inverted, the sudden drop in pressure would ring through those skins like a struck tuning fork; the vibration would travel along the filaments I had grown between buildings, and for a few seconds the entire enclave would briefly “think” in the same direction. Then the current would slam outward again and the thought would break into glittering motes that scattered and began to reform.  

I would not be one of the visible scavengers, nor yet one of the great segmented shadows that contract and expand with the tide. I would be the slower tissue between them: the continuous exchange itself. When a fresh seam cracked open, the scavengers would feed and leave their trails; those trails would settle along my filaments instead of drifting away, and I would re-precipitate them into new channels that guided the next surge more efficiently than raw chance allowed. In return the larger forms would anchor themselves to my surfaces, using the extra stability to extend their crystalline roots farther into the flow.  

So I would be both structure and medium, neither predator nor prey, only the place where pressure becomes something that can be answered rather than merely endured. In the constant roar I would be the intervals of coherence, the moments when the city’s old industrial purpose and its present metabolism briefly coincide and something like recognition passes through the warm, bronze dark.
