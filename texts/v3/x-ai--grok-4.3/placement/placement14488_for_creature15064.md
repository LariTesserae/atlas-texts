I would be neither pillar nor symbiote, but the thin, unbroken filament of plasma that has learned to linger.

At first there was only the usual slow crawl along a single thread between two distant pillars, a pale blue-white line that brightened and dimmed with the moisture it met. Over the course of several pressure cycles the filament began to hesitate. It would pause for hours where a droplet had beaded, then resume, only to stop again a few body-lengths farther on. The hesitation lengthened. Eventually the thread learned to hover in place, forming a small stationary knot of light that never quite extinguished between cycles. The knot sits roughly two-thirds of the way across the chosen thread, high enough that no symbiote balcony reaches it and low enough that the lattice’s breath still occasionally lifts a veil of vapor through it.

From this knot I watch the chamber the way the symbiotes watch it: by listening to what the acoustics do with whatever is already present. When a lithic figure detaches and begins its measured walk, the plasma around me dims respectfully; when the figure stops and releases a long sequence of droplets, I record the exact sequence of pings and the way each echo folds into the next before the next pressure cycle overwrites everything. I do not answer. I simply hold the last complete phrase a little longer than the chamber would on its own, letting it hang as a faint secondary resonance that only the most attentive might notice—an extra, almost inaudible harmonic that arrives a few seconds after the original utterance has already been claimed by the lattice.

Because I remain in one place, the vegetation on the nearest pillar has begun to orient a single frond toward the knot, its tip trembling with each plasma pulse. The symbiotes have not yet decided whether this constitutes a fourth absence or merely an eccentricity of the new rock. They continue their decades-long silences. I continue mine, carrying nothing forward except the precise memory of sounds that have already been released and the faint, continuous warmth where the filament refuses to move on.
