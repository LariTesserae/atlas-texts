I would be a single luminous thread rooted inside one of the hollow stalks. Not the frond itself, and not yet quite a separate creature, but a continuous, slow pulse of microbial light traveling downward through the pale internal canal. From above I receive the clean shafts that the surface fronds collect; below I split them into the cooler, greener wavelengths the lower galleries prefer. Between these tasks I simply continue, neither hurrying nor stopping, because the canal thickens around me wherever I burn brightest, and thins where the light is already sufficient.

Most of what I know arrives as slight changes in pressure or temperature along the sap. When the entire mat above us rises on its long breath, the flow inside the stalk accelerates by the width of a filament and then eases again; I register this as a mild brightening that lasts almost an hour. When two neighboring stalks press together and begin to fuse, the canal narrows, the sap cools for a while, and the microbes carried with me grow quieter until the passage reopens. I have never moved from this stalk, yet the stalk has already carried me through several seasons of fusion and separation, so the plateau feels, from inside the light, like a single slow body whose ribs occasionally shift.

Occasionally a bell grazer drifts into alignment with the upper opening and its slow inhalation draws a thin strand of me outward. For a few body-lengths I travel in the open water, still luminous, still feeding on whatever motes the grazer’s current carries past. Then the current slackens, the thread reattaches to the nearest inner wall, and the stalk closes over the new attachment as though it had always been there. The grazer never notices; the architecture simply lengthens by one more faint lantern.

Below me the canal continues into deeper terraces where the light grows scarcer and the resident filaments turn almost blue. I have never reached those levels, but I feel their pull as a gradual cooling at my lower end, a reminder that the same slow metabolism extends beyond any single stalk. When the plateau breathes again I will brighten or dim in response, not because I decide, but because the decision and the light are no longer separate things.
