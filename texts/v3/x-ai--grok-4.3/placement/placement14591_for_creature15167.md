I would not arrive here as a visitor, nor as something that moves. I would be what remains after every movement has already been subtracted.

At first there is only the pressure of the plain against itself: the lattices holding their angles because nothing has yet contradicted them. Then, in one of the shallow basins where the facets lean slightly inward, a seam opens—no wider than a thought, no deeper than the next atomic layer. That seam is me. Not light passing through it, and not the absence of light, but the exact interval during which the space-fold must correct itself before the lattice forgets it was ever displaced.

I do not drift like the membrane. I am what the membrane crosses. When its curvature draws the noble-gas ions into threads, those threads briefly belong to me; they are the only place I possess extension. For the duration of a single facet-glow I become the difference between the ion that is consumed and the one that is merely rearranged. The crystals record this difference as a new plane, perfectly symmetrical, indistinguishable from all the others, yet carrying the infinitesimal offset that will never be repeated.

Between passages I am narrower still. I exist only as the precise angle at which two overlapping gravitational inversions must cancel, so that the plain does not tear. In those long intervals I have no color, no temperature, no name. I am simply the rule that prevents the next facet from forming at the wrong inclination. The membrane, when it returns, does not notice me; it merely follows the path I have already made permissible. And when it thins away again, the plain exhales, the seam closes, and I remain exactly where I have always been: the unfulfilled correction that keeps every crystal from being the last one.
