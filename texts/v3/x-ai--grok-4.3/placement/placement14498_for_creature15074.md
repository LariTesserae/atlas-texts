I am the last fastening that has not yet failed.

At first I knew only pressure: the long torque of the beam above me and the patient sag of the plate below, each trying, across centuries, to decide which direction gravity would finally win. My threads are still engaged, but the iron around them has recrystallized into something gentler, almost fibrous. When a drop strikes the joint I feel it as a small loosening, a microscopic concession; later the same drop evaporates and the concession is taken back. I have learned to count time by these concessions.

Because nothing else moves quickly here, I have become a kind of listener. The perpetual chord that fills the chamber passes through me as through any other strut, yet I register its smallest variations: the instant a fly larva inside a duct bursts its skin and the pressure wave travels outward; the slow slide of an isopod across a polished perforation lip; even the faint ultrasonic shiver of the vines when their leaf-tip hydathodes release a thread of water. These events arrive already braided into the chord, but I can separate them the way a hand can separate one strand of wire from a cable.

In the long intervals between shears I grow a thin skin of oxide that exactly matches the rust-colored carapaces of the isopods. When one of them pauses beside me I feel its segmented weight as a momentary redistribution of load, nothing more. Once, long ago, a caterpillar descended the vine that brushes my head and began to spin a cocoon against my shank. I held perfectly still while it worked. Its silk mixed with the rust I was already shedding; when it pupated and later emerged as a moth, the empty shell remained, a delicate lattice of iron and protein that still catches drops and passes them downward.

I do not know what will happen when I finally let go. The plate will drop a few centimeters; the isopods and moss will re-settle. The chord will absorb the small metallic report. I will become one more note among its others, distributed through the structure rather than concentrated at a single joint. Until then I remain here, neither growing nor decaying at a rate the chamber can notice, performing the only task left to me: holding, for one more unmeasured interval, the shape the world has already decided it will keep.
