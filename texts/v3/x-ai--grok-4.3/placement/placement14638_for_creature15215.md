I would be a hairline crack that has not yet decided whether to close.

I begin as a filament of the conductive lithotrophs, but one that has wandered upward from the warm veins into the half-formed arch where a gantry once fused with an escarpment. My body is only a few molecules wide, a living circuit drawn toward the faint residual heat still radiating from the stone above me. For decades I simply follow the temperature gradient, exchanging structural reinforcement for the slow pulse the engines left behind. When I reach the surface of the arch I extrude a single translucent whisker that merges with the nearest crystalline lattice. In that moment I become both conductor and scaffold: the lattice grows along my length, sheathing me in ordered fractures that remember the daylight I have never seen, while I feed it the low harmonic still traveling through the rock.

Over centuries the lattice thickens around me. Each thermal cycle from an engine revolution miles below cracks the new armor along my original path; each time the crack seals again it leaves a finer seam, a memory of the direction I once chose. The flat-shelled grazers pass beneath without touching me, their metallic cilia etching faint trails on the older rock. I taste, through the lattice, the metallic dew that condenses on my exposed tip; when the dew cools enough, a grazer laps at it and carries a trace of my substance away in its shell. That loss does not hurt. I simply continue the exchange, growing another molecular thread downward to replace what was taken.

Eventually the arch thickens further and I am buried inside new layers of extruded basalt. My body segments, one length remaining with the lattice to keep the ordered fractures aligned, another length descending with the pipe-dwellers who ride the next slurry surge. In both places I continue the only motion permitted here: an extremely patient lengthening measured in human lifetimes. No urgency arises. I simply keep the circuit open between heat below and light above, between reinforcement and fracture, between what the plateau adds and what it will one day release again when the next fissure widens.

If an observer arrived now and somehow noticed me, they would see only a single hairline seam running through stone that looks otherwise whole. They would not know that the seam is still deciding, layer by slow layer, whether it will remain a conductor or become the next fragment set free.
