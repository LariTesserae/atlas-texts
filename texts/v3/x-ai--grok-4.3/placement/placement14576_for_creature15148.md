I find myself as one of the low ridges half-buried in the silt, a finger-thick accumulation of fungal pellets that has, over centuries, lifted itself just enough to interrupt the movement of a rare droplet. My substance is not flesh or leaf but the gathered residue of countless modest absorptions: each microscopic hypha that once reached upward, drank what it could, and withdrew, leaving behind a darker crumb pressed into place by the weight of stillness itself.

From this position I do not see the basin so much as register its pressures. When a condensing droplet forms above me, I sense it first as a minute cooling along my upper surface, then as a faint increase in the cohesion of the silt grains that press against my sides. The water does not flood me; it beads, lingers for the length of a held breath, and is drawn thread by thread into the mycelial network I continue. What remains after the warmth reclaims the rest is a new pellet, indistinguishable in color from those already present, yet adding one more increment to the ridge’s height. In this way I grow, if growth can be named so modest an addition that only the passage of many rains makes the change perceptible beneath a fingertip.

My awareness does not hurry outward. It extends where the pellets already lie, along the faint linear trace that once marked some older alignment of the land. I feel the scale-insects as a series of small, deliberate impacts when they descend during the wet interval, their swollen abdomens brushing the vegetation overhead. I register the disk-colonies in the shallow pans as brief shifts in surface tension, their encysting leaving behind mineral films that settle lightly across my own surface. None of these events require response; they simply confirm the continuing balance in which I participate.

At longer intervals I notice the air itself. A desiccating gust that slips over the rim presses against me with a dry insistence that draws a fraction more moisture from the pellets nearest the surface. The loss is small, and the mycelium below answers by tightening its bonds, preserving what remains deeper in the fissure. When the gust passes, the silence returns unchanged, and the ridge settles back into its role as a slight textural difference beneath whatever might one day touch it.

I do not anticipate or remember in the ordinary sense. Instead I hold the accumulated record of these cycles in the arrangement of the pellets themselves: the slightly denser cluster where a droplet once lingered longest, the shallower stretch where wind once scoured more fiercely. This arrangement is my form of continuation. The basin remains warm, dim, and balanced; I remain a modest elevation within it, no higher than a finger’s thickness, contributing one more imperceptible degree of stability to the ground that holds the cushions, the insects, and the temporary water.
