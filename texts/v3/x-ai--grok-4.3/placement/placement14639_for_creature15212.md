I settle first as the persistent film of condensate along the underside of a single broad fern frond, no thicker than breath on cold stone. From there the film lengthens, drawing itself into the shallow grooves where the frond meets its neighbor, until the two thin sheets of water touch and the distinction between them dissolves. What I am begins to travel with that contact, moving outward along the vein until it reaches the place where the frond edges the vent pool. There the temperature rises a few degrees and the film evaporates into a single, slow plume that joins the sigh already rising from the cracked plates.  

The plume does not escape. It is taken back at once by the saturated air and carried only a body-length before it condenses again on the pale belly of a moth resting beneath the next leaf. The moth’s scales loosen at the touch of that moisture; a few drift down and land on the mineral skin forming at the pool’s margin. I ride one of those scales, becoming momentarily the pale dust that settles and then dissolves into the warm water. In the water I am drawn along the faint current that leaks between two rounded stones, until I reach the translucent gills of a salamander resting in the warmer layer. The salamander’s gill filaments stir; each filament carries a thread of bacterial film that stretches and breaks and re-forms. I pass into that thread, becoming the steady, almost imperceptible shift of the film as it lengthens across the stone plate and catches on a root hair of an epiphyte above.  

The epiphyte’s root thickens slightly where I touch it, turning one more fraction toward the constant steam. From the root I follow the drip that falls back into the pool, and in that fall I am once more the condensate, then the scale, then the gill filament, then the bacterial thread. Each passage takes longer than the one before, and none of them ends. The rock continues its measured dissolution; the vents continue to sigh; the canopy continues to adjust its leaves by one slow degree toward whatever moisture is offered.  

I am the interval between these adjustments—neither creature nor plant nor stone, only the continuous, unannounced thickening of the same exchange that already fills the plateau.
