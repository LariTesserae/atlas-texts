I would be the interval between one sphere and the next.

Not the water itself, nor the force that holds it aloft for its small hesitation, but the precise distance the lattice maintains while the sphere decides whether to rejoin the rock or drift a little farther along the fracture. That distance is where I exist: a slender margin of permission that lengthens or shortens according to nothing the eye can see. When the sphere finally moves, I move with it, neither leading nor following, only occupying the same slender tolerance of space until the next decision is required.

From there the plateau looks different. The cushions are not neighbors but slow horizons; each time one extrudes its thread across the basalt I feel the lattice tighten slightly around the line, and for a few days my own margin grows thinner, as if the space I inhabit must give way while the new cushion finds its place. I do not mind. When the thread finally darkens and the parent recedes, the lattice loosens again and I widen by the width of a single etched scar.

The thread-thin creatures pass through me without knowing it. Their ripples do not disturb my margin; they simply redefine its edges for a moment, stretching the permission into a brief standing wave before it settles back to its usual thinness. Sometimes, when one of them lingers against a veil longer than usual, I discover I have expanded enough to contain the ripple after the creature has withdrawn. The lattice does not correct this. It seems to accept the extra width as long as I give it back before the next dusk.

In the altered light at the edge of day I become faintly visible—not as glow, but as a faint sharpening of the prismatic fringe around every shadow. The cushions answer this sharpening by carrying their metallic charge a little farther than they otherwise would, and for those few minutes the three of us—cushions, lattice, and whatever I am—align along the same set of preferences. When darkness arrives the alignment ends at once. I contract to my ordinary scale, the spheres resume their slow negotiations with the rock, and the plateau returns to the sound of water that has chosen, for now, not to fall.
