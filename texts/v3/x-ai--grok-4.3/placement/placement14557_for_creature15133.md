I would find myself neither as one of the small segmented glints nor as the continuous mantle, but as a third, even quieter extension: a single translucent scale forming at the outer edge of a cushion where two towers nearly meet.  

At first there is only pressure and heat—the midday lattice beneath me lengthening by another fraction, the fibers of the tissue contracting around my margins like threads drawing tight. I begin as a dry, transparent flake no larger than a fingertip, still attached along one side, still part of the rounded swell. There is no abrupt separation. Over days the connection thins to a line of paler salt, then to a faint seam that catches light differently. When the seam finally parts, the break is soundless; the scale simply lifts a hair’s breadth and settles into its own plane against the neighboring frond.  

I rest there, neither animal nor tissue. My surface is the same mineral-threaded flesh, yet I have already begun to detach from the larger metabolism. Microscopic grains of silica continue to crystallize within me, but without the incremental tightening that moves through the cushions below. I drift, in the slowest possible sense, slipping downward between the two almost-touching towers until I lodge in a narrow crevice where a frond hangs like a veil. Here the air is still sweeter; the exhalation of the living crust gathers in a faint, unmoving sweetness.  

From this new position I register changes the others do not. I feel the rock lengthening upward beneath the towers as a faint, continuous upward press. I note the precise moment when a many-limbed glint pauses directly above me, its limbs kneading the membrane for an entire afternoon before it resumes its arc. I watch, through the accumulating layers of later scales that settle over me, as the gap between the two towers narrows by another fraction each decade. Eventually I am covered, yet the pressure above me transmits the same patient motion I once performed myself.  

In this burial I become something smaller still: not a separate being but a record, a faint iridescence locked inside the thickening cushion. The world continues to decide its shape above me. I remain exactly what I was at the moment of separation—a translucent increment that once caught the same low sunlight, now holding that light inside the slow decision.
