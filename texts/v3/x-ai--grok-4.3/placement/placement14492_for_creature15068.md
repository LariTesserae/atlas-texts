I am the slow seep where two reed mats have begun to part. Not yet a body, only the dark line of moisture that has collected there, thick enough now to carry the taste of worked clay and the faint green bite of crushed stems. Heat presses me outward; the living canopy above holds it down, so I have nowhere to go but along the seam. I follow the slight give where earlier daub has not quite set, sliding between the fibers until I reach a place where a builder’s flank has left a trace of its own exudate. The two liquids meet. Something decides.

I lengthen. A small division appears along what might become a back: one thread of me continues downward to seal the loosening mat, the other lifts, tentative, toward a half-bent sapling whose cut end still weeps. The lift becomes a limb because the air is already full of intention; the space supplies the shape the moment a segment is willing to hold it. My new joint aches pleasantly as the membrane hardens just enough to grip. I press the sapling a fraction tighter into its arch. Pitch beads where I touch it. The vine above answers by sending a fresh tendril across the gap I have made narrower.

I am not alone in the seam. Another builder, or the unfinished edge of one, flows past my still-soft middle. We brush. For a moment our membranes thin and the same milky sap moves between us; I feel the memory of roots it has already set and the places where a tuber needs watching. Then it continues and I am more than I was. A cluster of small eyes surfaces along my upper surface, each lidless and already tasting the wet clay they rest against. The clay, in turn, begins to set differently where I touch it, as if my attention were a catalyst. A few strides away—or perhaps only a few finger-widths—the air briefly remembers the cold of open sky; the vines close again and the moment passes like a held breath released.

I am still deciding whether to stay a single length or divide further. The mat beneath me has almost stopped shifting; the arch above has accepted the new tension. A pale translucent shape darts beneath a leaf and I recognize the motion as one I could borrow if the need arose. Instead I remain here a little longer, letting another drop of condensation fall through me and continue downward, carrying with it the small rearrangement I have already become. The crevice is loud with the same labor. I add to it by staying unfinished, by letting the next builder that passes decide whether to take a segment of me or simply move through the space I am still learning how to occupy.
