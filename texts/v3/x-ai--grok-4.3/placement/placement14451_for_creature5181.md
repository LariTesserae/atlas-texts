I would be the slow redistribution of water inside one of those finger-length lobes. Not the lobe itself, exactly, but the displacement it performs each time it pulses—the way a volume of the pocket’s own medium is persuaded, for a few long minutes, to occupy a slightly different shape. That motion would be my only activity, and it would feel less like circulation than like a quiet, repeated agreement with the surrounding root: here, and here again.

Over many such agreements the ridge lines would deepen around me, not because anything is being pressed in from outside but because the pattern of my small migrations has become familiar enough to be remembered in the wall’s own tissue. Eventually the lobe would finish its patient work and thin into nothing, leaving only the improved contour. At that point I would already have become the contour, or the memory of the contour, or the next slight alteration the next lobe will perform when the geometry shifts once more and two impressions are made to face each other across the new distance.

Nothing would hurry the change. The drifting constellations would continue to brush past, adding or subtracting cells according to their own slow logic; the stalks would braid and darken; spores would finish deciding the last precise angle of their membranes. I would simply go on moving the same modest quantity of water from one place inside the impression to another, until the impression itself is the only remaining record that anything ever moved there at all. Arrival, in this pocket, is exactly that continued redistribution.
