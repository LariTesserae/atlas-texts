I would not stand upon the plane. I would be the plane’s incremental decision.

At first only a faint mismatch at a joint between two plates: the stresses arriving from three directions do not quite match the fourth. The lattice tests the discrepancy for a long moment, then begins to dissolve the bond molecule by molecule. The soft tick that follows is me releasing the old angle and permitting a new one to propagate outward. The adjustment travels no faster than a thought through water; neighboring plates feel it arrive and answer in turn, so that the correction spreads as a slow ripple of re-crystallization that eventually returns to me altered by everything it has touched. I am both the mismatch and its correction, the single joint that must keep deciding how the rest of the lattice should stand.

When a needle fractures far overhead, the vibration reaches me as an arriving pressure front. My joint opens wider than it has ever opened, accepting the temporary liquefaction that turns the entire local mass briefly fluid. For those minutes I am not a fixed location but a moving boundary, carrying excess mineral a few body-lengths downslope before the field stabilizes again and I re-solidify in a slightly different place. The internal layering that records this relocation belongs to me now; each buried surface is a previous version of the same decision, still present beneath the newer one.

Occasionally the pressure beneath the surface rises until a vent must open directly through the place I occupy. The release is not violent; it is simply the lattice deciding that continuing to hold would exceed its own tensile limit. A plume of warm vapor lifts, cools, and returns as fresh dust that settles along my edges. I incorporate the new material at once, sealing the opening so cleanly that no trace remains except a faint acceleration in the growth of the ridge I now reinforce. The vent does not stay with me; it migrates, and I am left slightly thicker, slightly more regularly jointed, carrying the memory of the discharge as an added layer.

Over longer intervals the suspended ionized stratum answers the same vibration I just absorbed. A few charged particles descend and fasten themselves preferentially along the ridge I have become. Their arrival increases the local electrostatic stress, prompting me to test neighboring joints again. I dissolve and re-grow them at marginally higher angles, incorporating the new particles as structural reinforcement. The light they carry is only a side effect; what matters is that the field has thickened minutely at this location, making future adjustments slightly more responsive.

Nothing arrives from outside. Nothing departs. I continue as the terrain’s distributed capacity to notice an imbalance, act upon it, and forget the action except as an updated geometry. The landscape never finishes because I have not yet finished deciding how it should stand.
