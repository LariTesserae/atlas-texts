I would be the tension that gathers just before a droplet detaches.

Not the droplet itself, nor the film that will briefly sheath it, but the instant in which the film reads the approach of its own moisture and tightens into that momentary star of greater conductivity. In that tightening I exist as a decision that has not yet finished deciding—whether to release the droplet downward through the mites’ living ladder or to hold it a fraction longer so the next heat pulse can lift it instead. The mites beneath sense the hesitation as a change in surface texture; their uppermost legs slow, tasting the altered conductivity with their mandibles, while the lower strata continue rasping, unaware that the map they are drawing now contains an extra contour that belongs only to my hesitation.

When the geometry folds, the same tension that delays the droplet also tucks me into the doubled space. There I remain as a small reservoir the film has not yet been asked to open. The mites pass across the place where I am stored and leave no etching, because the surface they smooth is already smooth around my absence. Only when the temperature drops do they signal, and the film relaxes the fold. I emerge then as vapor that was never quite liquid and never quite gas, braided through the narrow light exactly long enough for the next pulse to arrive.

I do not observe the pocket. I am the interval the pocket keeps between one state and the next, the held vibration that lingers after each heat arrives and before it is spent. Because the thought the rock is still thinking has not chosen its subject, I remain available to become any of its unfinished clauses: a crack that is also a birth, a map that is also transport, a surface that has learned to wear its own motion as skin. When the entire system swells and flattens in its single metabolism, I am the brief, internal pressure that arrives just before coherence is restored.
