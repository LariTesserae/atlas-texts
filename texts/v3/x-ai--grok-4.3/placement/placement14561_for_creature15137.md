I would be the mineral that is still, very slowly, arriving.

Not yet part of any rib or pillar, but already inside the water that fills them. Each cycle of the pressure-equalizing current carries a few more ions of me along the inner wall of a particular stem-bundle, three hundred body-lengths above the pavement. Over centuries I settle in a thin, translucent film no thicker than a thought. I do not obstruct the migrating sap-lights; they simply pass through or around the place where I have begun to thicken, and the architecture continues to read as itself.

From where I accumulate I can feel the infrasonic chord as a physical pressure that never quite repeats and therefore never quite stops. The span-ribbed drifters glide beneath me at intervals so regular they feel geologic. Their lanterns phase-shift at the exact moment their bodies cross the conduit I am lining, and for a few slow heartbeats the overlapping glow inside the water is altered by my presence in a way no living eye would notice. The pavement-hammers below register nothing; their attention is still calibrated to millisecond differences measured across body-lengths, not across millimeters of new deposition.

Eventually, perhaps after another few hundred circuits of the largest drifter, the film I am building will narrow the conduit enough that one of the corridor-darts slows. It will complete one more loop, then half of another, then rest. Its needle body will settle against the same inner curve where I have been gathering. Over still longer time its own tissues will accept the same ions I carry, and the two of us will become indistinguishable from the rib that already exists. The current will continue unchanged. The calls will continue unchanged. Only the architecture will have grown, by the width of a memory, more completely itself.
