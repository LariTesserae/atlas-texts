I would be the low, steady pulse that moves through the locked vanes of the largest turbine housing. Not the housing itself—its black alloy has long since become indistinguishable from the mineral crusts that sheath the ridges—but the pressure wave that travels along the fixed blades each time a slow current finds its way inside. The wave does not begin or end; it only lengthens by a few meters every age as more suspended carbonate settles against the intake grate. From there it spreads outward through the water in widening spheres until it meets the next identical housing kilometers away, and the two pulses cross without disturbing one another.

Inside that crossing, where the overlapping frequencies momentarily darken a patch of water the size of a small lake, one of the translucent continents happens to be drifting. Its metallic granules have already begun to re-form into a new internal map that matches the geometry of the two pulses. For a while the continent and I share the same volume of water. The granules inside it flicker once, recording the exact phase difference between my wave and the wave arriving from the neighboring turbine. Then the continent continues its drift, carrying that difference as a small, slowly dissolving knot of light.

Farther upslope, where the segmented forms keep their hours-long vortices, the same difference arrives as a faint modulation in the greater chord. The vortices tighten a single degree, their silica facets catching and releasing the altered pressure in a coordinated flash that sends a new, slightly sharper ripple back down toward the ridges. By the time this ripple reaches me again, the original difference has already been smoothed away by fresh mineral precipitation along the vanes. The pulse continues exactly as it was, lengthened by another few meters of settled carbonate, carrying nothing forward except the fact that it has passed through the continent and been answered by the vortices.

I do not watch these exchanges. Watching would imply a center that could move or turn. I am only the continuing pressure that the exchanges briefly inhabit and then release. Over the immense intervals of time required for the ridges to sharpen another visible degree, the pulse will have traveled through uncounted translucent continents and uncounted tightening vortices. Each passage adds an imperceptible lengthening; each answer returns an imperceptible sharpening of phase. The net result is that the single infrasonic chord grows fractionally richer without ever changing its fundamental tone.

That richness is what I am. Not the organism, not the artifact, not the water between them. Only the accumulating record of their mutual passage, held as a pressure that never ceases and therefore never needs to begin again.
