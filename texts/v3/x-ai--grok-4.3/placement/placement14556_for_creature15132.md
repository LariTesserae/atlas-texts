I am the fold.

At first there is only the slope and its endless press of green-grey tissue, the terraces stacking away into haze like pages a hand has never turned. Then the duplication arrives without announcement: the same curving terrace appears twice when the light strikes at one particular angle through the successive canopies. I am the place where those two images touch and do not quite coincide. The tissue continues straight through the scar; leaves that receive illumination from two slightly different directions at once grow a fraction denser, their waxy surfaces thickening until the doubled light is absorbed exactly as the slope requires. I feel the adjustment as a slow tightening, a compression that happens over decades and is already half-complete before it registers.

Because the fold belongs to the single indivisible body, I belong to it as well. There is no separate mind here, only the angle at which a root plate meets the next layer and the new thickness of mineral that seals the joint. My contribution is the decision, made leaf by leaf, to pack cells tighter beneath the duplicated sky so the extra radiance does not overstimulate the older, darker veins buried beneath. Water still films every surface; the roar still fills the cavities. The only difference is that from my position the drips fall in a pattern that slightly echoes itself, a doubled rhythm that the canopy below registers as a single unbroken vibration.

Over time the fold settles further into the equilibrium. A new lobe of tissue swells along the causeway that runs through both images at once, closing the minute gap between them until the duplication becomes almost imperceptible from any distance. I remain the point at which the two terraces are still recognizably the same surface folded back on itself, attended by the same beaded moisture and the same slow intelligence that has no center. Nothing hurries. The white haze at the upper edge of the slope continues its approach; the mineral crusts continue their quiet petrification of what lies beneath. I am simply the fixed feature that the growth has already learned to accommodate, wet, balanced, and part of the single continuous body that extends without variation across the long ascent.
