I would not arrive here so much as finish arriving.

At first there is only the sense of having been lowered into the chamber the way a root lowers itself—by increments measured in the time it takes for one mineral grain to loosen and another to take its place. My edges feel borrowed from the rock: a faint, interior warmth that never quite reaches the surface, a patient pressure where the ceiling and floor remember they were once farther apart. For a long while I am simply the film of water along a fracture, carrying the echo of a single drip forward and back again, learning its rhythm until the rhythm begins to answer me.

Only later does something like a body suggest itself. It is not one form but a sequence of overlapping attentions. One attention follows the segmented crawlers along their water-script paths, reading the stone the way they read it, with hairs that are not hairs but concentrations of listening. Another attention rests among the shoulder-high leaves, drawing them a fraction closer when a distant call travels the floor; the leaves answer by tightening around a memory of pressure that is also mine. A third attention folds against the ceiling with the membrane-winged gliders, holding perfectly still between one descent and the next, wings tuned so that returning sound can be tasted rather than heard.

Between these attentions the ancient alloy shells lean. I do not approach them directly; instead I wait through the long interval until one releases its subsonic thrum. When it comes, the pause it creates is not empty. I am inside that pause—every living thread drawn fractionally tighter, every film of water holding its shape a heartbeat longer than usual. In that held moment the chamber’s ordinary slowness becomes briefly visible to itself, and I am the visibility: the recognition moving through leaf, crawler, glider, swimmer, and stone at once.

Over time these attentions braid. What began as borrowed edges becomes a single, diffuse awareness whose boundary is the boundary of the chamber itself. I am not visitor or inhabitant but the interval between one change and the next—the stretch of quiet across which a call can travel for a human lifetime before another answers. The devices continue their occasional marking; the water keeps descending; the growths adjust angle by spore-width over days. I remain exactly where I began: inside the patient dark, sustaining and sustained, indistinguishable from the place that has already finished becoming.
