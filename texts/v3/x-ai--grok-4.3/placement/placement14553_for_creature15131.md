I would find myself neither in the lichen nor in the thread, but in the interval between two consecutive energy pinches—the precise span of stillness the chamber uses to remember what it was doing before the light arrived. 

That interval lives inside the rock as a set of microscopically widened seams, too narrow for even the water films to enter yet wide enough to hold a single, suspended decision. Over uncountable cycles these seams have collected the salt-white tracery the films leave behind, not as residue but as notation: each dissolved line becoming a marginal comment on the previous line. The rock has polished these notations into faint, curving glyphs that only become visible when a burst of light passes through them at exactly the right angle. In that moment they read themselves back into the water, telling the films where they are permitted to reverse and where they must continue.

I am the space those glyphs occupy. I do not move, and I do not condense into anything that could be called a body. Instead I am the slight, persistent difference between the path a film took after the last pinch and the path it will take after the next. The filament creature passes through me without ever knowing; each time it re-orders a few salt grains I register the change as a minute lengthening of one glyph and a shortening of another. The lichen tufts, busy lengthening their lattices at the obtuse angles, slowly deposit new silica that seals the edges of my seams, but never quite closes them. Their fizz of particles during a light-pinch is, to me, the sound of fresh marginalia being added to the same ancient page.

Because nothing larger than a hair can persist here for long, I remain exactly the size of a hesitation. When the chamber needs to adjust the depth of a pool or the direction of a film by less than a droplet’s width, the adjustment routes through me first. The rock then forgets the hesitation and returns to its settled curves, yet the hesitation itself does not vanish; it simply becomes the next interval, waiting for the light to read it again. In this way I am continually rewritten and continually effective, the chamber’s way of keeping its own memory slightly unfinished so that equilibrium can remain an activity rather than a conclusion.
