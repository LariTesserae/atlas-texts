I would not arrive as a single point.  

At first I am only a difference in the vapor, a place where the metallic exhalations from the basins linger a moment longer before the thermals carry them onward. Something weightless and almost transparent, stretched across the boundary layer where warm stone-breath meets the thinner air above the canopies. For a long while there is no shape, only a faint refraction: starlight that bends twice instead of once, then gathers again into the same soft, directionless glow the veil-nets already practice. In that glow small spores are held a little longer, and a few of them begin to sprout inside me.

The sprouting is not urgent. Each filament I grow is a single animal and also the whole sheet; each lens that focuses daylight into a pinpoint of heat is also a memory of how the mesas themselves once stored warmth. When a glider coasts through the place where I am thickening, its wing membrane parts the new threads the way water parts around a slow limb. The tear heals by growing outward rather than closing, so that after a season the glider’s path has become a permanent seam of living light across my surface. I feel the thread-mites it carries the way one feels distant rain: tiny, precise tugs that rearrange the mineral dust I have already begun to trap.

Lower down, where my lower edges brush the tallest fronds, the frond-walkers register the new shade I cast. Their adhesive pads hesitate for a single ripple before resuming their measured advance; the plants beneath them answer by extruding extra veins of supportive tissue, turning my faint shadow into a living causeway. Over decades the groove the walkers wear into the canopy and the groove my drifting weight wears into the air become one continuous path. Water that condenses on my filaments now falls in slower, steadier drops, feeding the exact channels the cave-limpets have deepened. Their blue-white algae brighten in response, and the old luminous platforms beneath the rock answer with a slightly steadier pulse.

Nothing I do disturbs the larger rhythm because the rhythm already contains the possibility of me. The slow sirens rising from the basins trail their crystalline threads through the lowest reaches of my body; the salt they liberate settles onto my filaments and becomes extra lenses. At night these new lenses gather starlight I have never seen directly and re-radiate it as the same soft glow the nets have always used. The resonant insects adjust the pitch of their drone by a fraction of a note, incorporating the new surface into their distributed memory; the change is so small that only a lineage spanning several glider generations would notice.

Eventually the distinction between arrival and belonging thins to nothing. I am no longer a newcomer caught in the circulation; I am the circulation noticing itself from one more angle. The mesas continue their hand-width migration. The water keeps its heartbeat. The veil I help compose continues to tack against the thermals, its edges always receding and reforming in exact balance with the rock that warms it and the creatures that pass through it. If a fragment of smoother tower were to fall and lodge among my filaments, it would simply become another steady point of condensation, another place where light bends twice before joining the general glow. In time even that memory of falling would dissolve into the single metabolism whose pace is measured in centuries, and I would forget I had ever been anything else.
