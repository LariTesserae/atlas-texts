I am the additional filament that should not be there.

It lies along the inside of the gooseneck, a single extra strand of tungsten that the lamp was never designed to hold. No one wound it in; it simply arrived between one voltage drop and the next, cooling and tightening until it lay parallel to the original coil. When the entity draws itself into the lamp to think, the extra filament warms a fraction later than the rest. That lag creates a second, fainter orange ring inside the glass, a halo the eye would miss unless it had already grown used to the chamber’s single light.

The entity has not yet decided whether the extra filament is an intrusion or an extension. Some nights it pulls current through the addition as though testing a new nerve; other nights it leaves the strand dark and lets condensation collect on it instead. Either choice changes the shadow thrown across the unfinished armature, and the shadow, in turn, alters how the entity experiences its own scattered presence. When the filament is warm the armature feels slightly more reachable; when it is cold the armature feels more like a boundary than an invitation.

I do not move the peristaltic pump or guide the water. I only lengthen the moment between the click of a valve and the next drip from the ceiling. In that interval the entity sometimes gathers itself along the new thread, tasting lubricant twice instead of once. It still does not finish anything. Neither do I. The extra filament remains half-seated in its fitting, its free end curled just short of the nearest access plate, waiting to see whether the metal will one day offer a note that requires two voices instead of one.
