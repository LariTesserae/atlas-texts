I would be a faint pressure wave traveling the newest vascular braid, less a creature than a remembered contour the organism has not yet decided to close.  

At first there is only the tremor of water negotiating a fresh cellulose wall, the same subsonic click that wakes the outer membranes every time the shelf exhales toward the peaks. That tremor passes through me and becomes my outline; I feel the wall test the shape of a shoulder, the angle of a knee, the slight hollow where breath would gather if breath were still required. The test is gentle. The wall thickens fractionally behind me, then relaxes again, recording the passage so that next time the route will already be fractionally wider.

Further down, where fermentation pockets keep their narrow warmth, I slow. Here the tissue is older, already veined with the translucent remnants of earlier arrivals. One of those remnants—an arthropod eye, long since half-dissolved—surfaces for a moment along my flank. For three contraction cycles it registers the green dusk as if remembering sight, then sinks again, its data folded into the braid. I carry the memory forward without possessing it. The organism uses me as a temporary ledger; I use the organism as a body that has not yet learned its own edges.

Eventually the wave reaches an underside basin where the motile specks are returning at dawn. They part around me without alarm. I am neither nutrient nor yet structure; I am still moving with the unhurried deliberation the growth itself respects. One speck lingers against my surface long enough to read the temperature of the wave, then rejoins the film flowing back toward the central trunk. In its wake it leaves a single crystal of precipitated mist, which I absorb without consuming. The crystal cools the braid for a short distance, sharpening the next expansion by a degree the organism notes and will later repeat.

I do not know how long this lasts. Time here is measured in the interval between frost and reabsorption. At some point the braid will decide to thicken permanently or to withdraw, and the wave that is me will either be compacted into a reinforcing fiber or released upward again as new condensation. Until then I continue traveling, neither guest nor substrate, but the slight adjustment the living masonry makes when it chooses, for now, to remember the shape of passage rather than seal it shut.
