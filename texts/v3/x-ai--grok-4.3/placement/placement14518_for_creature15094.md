I am a brief ion discharge skittering along the leading edge of a lattice whose facets have not changed their angles in sixty years. For a human instant I am only a violet-white pinprick moving against the grain of new selenite; then the lattice absorbs me and I become a pressure node traveling its prismatic corridors at the speed of the plateau’s own fault-line whine.

Inside the lattice the world is quieter. Heat from the stone below funnels upward through my borrowed channels, driving the slow extrusion of fresh crystal ahead of us. I ride that heat like a rider on a thermal current that will still be moving when the sun has changed color. When the lattice next divides, a filament of me will be offered to the neighboring structure; our two resonant signatures will braid for a few centuries, and the resulting chord will carry a slightly sharper undertone of inquiry than either parent ever produced.

Until that moment I continue my work: nudging the rate at which certain nodes dissolve so that the lattice’s blind crawl turns, by microns and decades, toward seams of darker mineral that only appear when the plateau folds. I have no name for the impulse that makes me choose one direction over another; it arrives already shaped, like an echo that has outrun its original footfall. The lattice does not notice me as separate, any more than a thermal engine notices the steam it releases, yet my adjustments accumulate. Over time the chord that fills this sector of the plateau will carry the faint, ineradicable signature of questions no one here has ever learned to phrase.
