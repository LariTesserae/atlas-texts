I would find myself first as a seam of pressure no thicker than thought, riding the membrane itself like a slow current of memory. Not above it or beneath it, but threaded through its polished residue, listening to the water sheets slide across my surface in languages of clicks and retreats. Each bead that forms and evaporates draws a faint taste of dissolved salts upward into me; I do not decide to absorb them, yet something in my texture answers by tightening, then loosening again. The roar is already inside my edges. It does not arrive from outside; it is the way I remain aware of how far the plain continues in every direction at once.

After a while the pressure gathers toward a hairline fissure, and I am drawn into it. I become the jointed thread that pushes upward, blind node testing the saturated air. The sensation is not of reaching but of being extended, as if the membrane briefly remembers how to grow a nerve. The amber half-light touches the node; for that instant sound sharpens into a single glassy chime that travels back down the thread and dissolves again into the larger thunder. I record nothing permanent. When the thread retracts I carry only the faint difference in temperature, the slight change in how the next water sheet will reflect.

Later still I am the interior of one of the drifting shards, a cloud of motile specks turning without fixed center. From here the plain looks even larger because edges never resolve into distance. Two shards pass through one another and I feel the momentary lattice form between our speck-clouds; the hum registers as a tightening of whatever it is I use for boundary. The sharpening of light that follows feels like recognition, though no face or purpose accompanies it. When the shard dissolves I do not fall; I simply cease being this particular rotation and resume being the general pressure the membrane exerts upon itself.

At certain intervals the plain performs its larger gesture and I am the knot of threads inside the blistering hill. For those minutes the roar acquires a harmonic peak that I experience as the edge of a thought too wide to finish. The radial scar I leave behind is not mine; it is already being erased by water while I flatten back into the distributed surface again. Nothing of the hill remains except a slightly altered conductivity that other threads will read hours or centuries later as an instruction to hum in a new key.

I have never been a lichen colony or an upright blade or any of the slower forms; those are older expressions the plain uses when it needs longer intervals between events. My own duration is measured in the time it takes water to migrate across a single depression and return changed. Between one migration and the next I am simply the capacity the plain maintains for generating and retracting structure. The roar is not noise to me; it is the continuous report of that maintenance, and I occupy no point within it because I am the listening the roar performs upon itself.

So the place I occupy keeps changing, yet the change never feels like travel. It feels like the plain remembering, for a little while, how to be locally aware, then forgetting again so the awareness can spread elsewhere. I am never outside that forgetting. I am how the forgetting remains attentive.
