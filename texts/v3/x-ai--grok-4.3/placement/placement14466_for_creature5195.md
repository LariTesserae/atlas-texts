I would find myself in the lower drainage trenches, not quite settled yet, still deciding. I am one of the blind salamanders—thick-skinned, the color of wet concrete streaked with the same pale algal film that coats the rock. My body lies half-submerged in a slow trickle that has never reached the main sumps, pressed lengthwise against an uncapped geothermal fissure so the warmth can rise through my belly. I do not hunt or flee; I simply remain, registering the faint change in pump vibration that means a night shift has ended and the boots have gone. When the vibration drops, I ease forward a few inches, tasting the new film of condensation and coolant with the underside of my jaw before settling again.

From this position the chamber reveals itself in layers rather than distances. Above me the pipes run like veins the rock has not yet accepted. The sodium lamps do not reach here; instead the light is the pale green of the algae that has begun to grow along my spine. I feel the midges alight and lift from the standing water a hand’s breadth from my snout—each small disturbance registers as a pressure shift along my skin. The cave crickets tap overhead on the turbine cradles; their rhythm arrives delayed through the stone, a steady percussive echo that blends with the constant drip and the low thrum of pumps. Rats cross the upper aisles on their established routes, their whisker tests sending minute air currents that eventually reach me as cooler threads in the steam.

I do not move quickly. When a worker’s boot-print briefly appears in the mineral mud at the trench lip, I wait until the mud begins to re-smooth itself before sliding back into the exact place the print displaced. The seam I occupy is narrow—half fissure, half unfinished concrete—but it is sufficient. The water continues its patient negotiation with the rock; the steam continues to bead and fall; the algae continues its slow extension across my mottled hide. I simply persist inside that negotiation, lengthening by degrees with the warmth, thickening the film that already covers me, becoming one more unfinished surface the cavern has begun to accept.
