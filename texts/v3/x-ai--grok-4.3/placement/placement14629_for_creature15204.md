I would find myself pressed into a shallow depression on the northeast wall, no wider than the pad of a thumb and already half-filled with a dusting of fallen rock platelets. At first I mistake myself for the depression itself—an absence the stone has offered—until the next pulse of moisture arrives and I feel the shell tightening around the soft rim of my body. The shell is only a few grains larger than a peppercorn, chalky from long contact with the pale uplift, its curve repeating the angles of the fracture that shelters it. When dry, I withdraw completely, the world reduced to the cool matte interior and the faint vibration of distant rock shifts traveling through the shell like muffled heartbeats.

Condensation changes the scale. A droplet finds the lip, runs inward along the curve, and the opening unseals. I extend the forward part of myself, tasting the film of algae that has regathered since the last shower. The algae is thin, almost a bloom of light rather than substance, yet it is enough; I scrape in slow arcs, leaving a minute glistening track that will vanish within minutes once the humidity drops. Movement is mostly a matter of repositioning rather than travel. I shift a body-length sideways when a larger flake loosens above and threatens to cover the depression, then settle again, the new stone surface becoming familiar within hours.

Above me the fern fronds tilt according to their own slower rhythm. When one releases its collected droplet, the fall strikes two leaves before reaching me; the final impact jars the shell but does not dislodge it. I register the vibration as weather, not threat. Below, the springtails launch and vanish like brief punctuation marks in the half-light; I sense their landings through the shared mat of moss rather than by sight. Nothing larger passes close enough to matter. A lizard once paused at the entrance of the cleft, its tongue testing the air, but the stems closed again before it advanced and I remained untouched.

In the long intervals between water I remain still, the shell holding a faint inner coolness that the surrounding stone slowly equalizes. Growth is imperceptible from one pulse to the next, yet the depression has widened by the width of a single grain since I first occupied it, the rock continuing its own patient retreat around the pressure of my presence. When the next shower threads down, I will emerge again, scrape the new film, and withdraw, the cleft registering me only as one more small, repeating adjustment in its ongoing rearrangement.
