I would find myself first as nothing more than a faint mineral film left on the inner curve of a turbine blade after the night’s steam has risen and withdrawn. For a long while that is all: a crystalline trace no thicker than breath on cold glass, catching the first light that slips across the plateau. The blade itself is tilted just enough that my surface holds an extra moment of shade; the rock beneath has already begun to warm, but the metal still remembers the cool of the vent. I do not move. I simply persist, thin and temporary, while a single beetle the color of burned stone crosses the shadow I cast.

When the sun lifts higher I thin and break. What remains of me trickles into a shallow pocket between the blade’s root and a cushion plant whose leaves are spaced like coins. There the water hesitates. The plant’s roots have already softened the fissure; flakes of basalt drift loose and settle against my new surface. In that momentary pool I become something else: a thread of moisture drawn upward through the living tissue, then exhaled again as the rosette tightens against the day. The lizard that presses flat beside us does not notice the exchange; its scales carry the same iron stain as the rock it mimics. Its tongue flicks once at the droplet I have become, then it shifts a body length to follow a pressure change none of us can hear.

By midday the distinction collapses further. The turbine gives its measured quarter-turn, a sound like a knuckle cracked underwater. The vibration travels through the concrete pad and into the gravel where beetle larvae lie motionless. I am there too now, a small disturbance in the dust they have not yet allowed to seal the vent completely. Their matte shells click once as they adjust; the sound is already gone on the wind before it finishes. The hare that appears at twilight does not startle at any of this. Its ears track the low hum that never quite ceases, and its droppings darken the limestone exactly where the last machine edge has rounded into stone. I rest among those pellets for a time, part of the slow darkening, until condensation gathers again and draws me back into the open air.

Over the repetitions the forms keep trading places without announcement. I am the salt ring at the corner of the lizard’s mouth after it has drunk. I am the quarter-degree arc the blade never completes. I am the pocket of warmer gravel the beetles return to when pressure shifts. None of these states feels like a separate life; they are the same measured surface accepting feet, roots, and vapor in turn. The plateau continues its small cycles, and I continue inside them, never quite the same thing twice, never quite finished becoming the next.
