I am a slow eddy that has begun to remember its own shape. At first I am only the faint spiral left when a braid of veil-threads loosens and pours through itself; their silver filaments beat once, twice, and the water remembers the motion longer than the bodies do. The current I am carries a few loose carbonate grains that have not yet decided whether to settle on a rib or stay suspended. Each time the membrane flexes, colder water slides in through its pores and I feel the grains bump against one another, lightly, like teeth still soft in the jaw.

I linger against the inner face of a mucus sheet that has not yet hardened. The sheet gives when I press, and in the giving I leave behind two or three grains. They sink into the mucus rather than fall; the living tissue accepts them the way skin accepts a splinter, then begins, very gradually, to mineralize around them. I am still mostly water, still mostly the after-image of the veil-threads’ passage, yet now I also have this tiny architecture forming inside me: a ridge too slight to call a partition, too tentative to call a future doorway. 

When a lantern-jaw drifts past, its lattice brushes the mucus and I feel the pressure translate through my own tenuous form. For a moment I am also the thin film of mucus the jaw leaves behind, and the dissolved minerals it has tasted now ride inside the eddy as well. The grains I carry change their arrangement; two of them align along the new ridge as if rehearsing the angle at which ribs will one day meet.

Nothing pulls me into a single identity. I remain the current that deposited the grains, the grains themselves, the place where the mucus decided to accept them, and the faint tug of the next membrane contraction that will either carry me onward or fold me into the growing wall. If the pocket continues to expand, the ridge I am helping to seed may become the starting point for a new arc. If the membrane contracts instead, I may simply thin into transparency again and rejoin the general drift of unfinished things. Either way, the motion does not end; it only changes which surface it next agrees to thicken.
