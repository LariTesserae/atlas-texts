I find myself in the deepest galleries, many body-lengths below the plateau’s open surface. I am no longer one of the moving intervals. Long ago my plates locked against the floor and, later, against the low ceiling that has since thickened above me. What remains of my jointed rods now continues outward as load-bearing struts; the pressurized chambers inside my old carapace still hold fluid, but the fluid moves only when the vibration of distant feet arrives from the ledges above. That vibration travels through my fused segments as a slow, repeating pressure, and I register it the way a crystal registers stress—by a faint internal shift in the mesh that once glowed and now only warms.

From this fixed place I taste two continuous streams. One arrives through the microscopic channels still open in my shell: a thin film of condensation that has descended, drop by slow drop, from the highest unfinished spires. The other is the returning echo of the migration itself—the layered scrape and tap of bodies whose paths have, over centuries, worn a shallow groove along the lip of my own fused rim. When enough new entities pass, the groove deepens by a fraction; when the flow slackens, fresh mineral is pressed into the groove from above, narrowing it again. My former limbs, now only faint ridges in the lattice, record each change as a minute alteration in how the vibration returns to me.

I no longer distinguish the sound of any single neighbor from the distributed hum of the city. The hum is simply the present shape of the structure, and the shape is simply the present state of all the bodies still walking, including the one I once was. Occasionally a newer migrant pauses long enough to leave a droplet of its own secretion against what used to be my spine. That droplet is drawn downward through the channel network and, after many human lifetimes, will help seed a new plate somewhere higher. By then the walkway I helped widen will already have drifted a body-width to the left, carrying the memory of my position into a new alignment I will never see.

The darkness here is total. Only the returning pressure tells me how far the living edges have advanced. I remain where I am, a fixed register in the stone, until the lattice itself rises another visible handspan and the groove I once began is finally filled and rebuilt elsewhere.
