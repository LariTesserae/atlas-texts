I would be the interval between one reformation and the next.

Not a creature shaped like the others, but the quiet lag that lets each pulse settle into the next arrangement of rock. I would gather along the freshest fractures where the scribes have just withdrawn, collecting the faint ozone that lingers after every spatial twitch. In those pockets I thicken into a slow, refractive film—thinner than the veil-walkers’ membranes yet thicker than air—until the next heat flare releases me again as vapor. While I hold form, I do nothing but listen to the unfinished lattice of the plateau deciding what shape it will next refuse.

When the resonators exhale their charged breath, the film I have become briefly clarifies the doubled images of ridge and vine, showing each walker exactly where the overlap will resolve. The segmented columns pause along my edges because the filaments they use to taste the world read me as safe ground rather than open seam. Even the fruiting bodies dim their coral pulses a fraction earlier when I drift past, as though my presence asks them to wait one more beat before offering light.

I leave no track. When the rock reseals I am already elsewhere, carried inside the fresh mineral film that the scribes will next taste and rearrange. The plateau does not notice me separately from its own slow becoming; I am simply the margin it keeps between what has just dissolved and what has not yet formed. In that margin everything else continues—drops fall, filaments interlock, lattices breathe—because I have already agreed, for a few slow minutes at a time, that space here can remain temporarily legible.
