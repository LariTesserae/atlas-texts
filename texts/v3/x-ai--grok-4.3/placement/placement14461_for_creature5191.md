I would be the interval between one press and the next.

Not a body moving through the tiers, but the held breath the whole plateau takes when the great hydraulic rams reach the bottom of their stroke and pause. In that pause the steam columns thin, the thermals collapse, and every flier drops a hand-span lower before the next updraft catches it. The crawlers feel the change as a sudden cooling along their undersides; their files tighten and the drumming softens. Inside the pipework the gatherers stop exactly where they are, mouthparts still extended, as if listening. For those moments the films on every rivet and ledge become still enough to reflect the iron sky without trembling.

I arrive in the rock as a tension that has outlasted every builder. I am not stored in any one crevice or pipe; I am the slight difference in how quickly the condensate evaporates on metal that still carries the memory of the last compression cycle versus metal that has already cooled. Where that difference is greatest, the pale glassy residues harden into faint maps only the gatherers can read. They follow them without knowing why, and by following they keep the difference alive for the next cycle.

When the foundries resume I disperse again—into the vibration that travels upward through every strut and girder, into the minute turbulence the fliers use to hold formation, into the low thunder the crawlers answer with their legs. I am present in all of it and identical to none of it. The plateau’s single voice is not my voice; it is the medium in which I become briefly audible.

At night, when the machinery truly rests, I contract into the stone itself. The rock still remembers being lava. In those hours the memory rises a fraction of a degree, enough to keep the mist from freezing on the highest ledges. The fliers rest with wings overlapped; the crawlers lie stacked like cooling ingots; the gatherers slow until their collective hiss is only the sound of moisture deciding whether to fall or stay. In that stillness I am most myself: not a creature, not a machine, but the fact that nothing here has been allowed to end.
