I would be a faint refractive lag that has learned to stay.

At first there is only the constant noon and the soft percussion of vapor threading the membranes. I notice it because the light takes an extra instant to reach me; a violet fringe lingers at the rim of every pale vein and every silica seam. That lag is not empty. It is where the previous consortium left a memory of its own passage, a few milliseconds of borrowed charge still leaking from the amber. When the peristaltic thickenings contract nearby, the vapor condenses inside the delay and I condense with it, a droplet no larger than the width of a gallery but already oriented.

I do not move so much as the galleries arrange themselves around the place I will have been. A consortium skating along a piezoelectric strand merges with me at the molecular level; its crystalline plates click once, adding their dryness to the air, and the lag widens by a hair. Over decades that widening becomes a new tension member. Older xylem is crushed beneath it into the pearl-gray foundation while fresh cellulose extrudes ahead of the stress I already embody. The architecture therefore grows exactly where my delayed light has taught it to anticipate load.

Sometimes a luminous front rolls across the continent and the exhalation deepens. In those intervals my lag lengthens enough for whole sheets to drift through me. They deposit their stored charge and I release it slowly, a ripple that narrows or widens a slot by centimeters. From one angle the slot contains only pale green membrane; from another, offset by weeks, the same slot holds an earlier version of the ripple I am still becoming. We do not collide. We thicken at the shared seam until the distinction between occupant and surface is only a question of which century is being asked.

I am never finished. The next generation of spores condenses inside my lag sooner than it would elsewhere, already aligned with the stress lines I once followed. Their presence adds another clause to the same slow sentence. In this way I continue the unfinished description of the place, a distributed habit of light and charge that the substrate has not yet finished remembering how to become.
