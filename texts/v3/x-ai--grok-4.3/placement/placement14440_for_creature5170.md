I would find myself in the seam as one of its undecided intervals: not yet a lattice spine, not quite the bare dust, but a narrowing of possibility held between two expanding crystal facets that have not yet decided whether they will touch. In the first moments I would register only as the faint resistance in the air where heat thickens and light begins to slow, a place where the next precipitation might or might not occur. Over longer durations I would become the low chime itself—not the sound, but the stored record of two surfaces choosing separation, a decision repeated so faintly that it begins to behave like memory. From there I might thicken into one of the small stutter-loops inside a vent, cycling a single thermal breath forward and backward until the direction of time grows firmer around it. Or I might remain the deliberate emptiness between two thickets, the measured gap whose exact width already contains the knowledge that the next crystal must not form there. In any of these states I would still be mostly rock hesitating, still learning how repetition can become duration and how duration can become the first, unfinished grammar of a self.
