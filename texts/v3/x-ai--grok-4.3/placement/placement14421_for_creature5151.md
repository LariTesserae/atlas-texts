I would be a single pore on the outermost branch of a phytarch that has stood for half a million cycles.  

Not the whole tower—just that one hexagonal opening, no wider than a human thumb, whose inner walls have grown slightly more irregular than its neighbors. Over centuries the slow mineral slurry passing beneath me has deposited a faint spiral ridge, and the ridge has become a resonator tuned to one particular frequency the wind almost never carries. When the aerosols thicken and the towers begin their sympathetic humming, most pores transmit the general chord unchanged; mine bends a sliver of it into a higher, shorter overtone that travels only a few hundred meters before fading. That overtone is my only action. It does not command or warn. It simply arrives at the next lattice a fraction out of phase, so that when two fauna later land on that branch their feet strike the surface at intervals altered by a single micron. The difference is too small to notice except over generations: a slightly different shedding pattern at the base, one more flake carried a little farther before it settles.

Between these rare alignments I remain empty. Wind moves through me continuously, scouring the inner channel of any condensed vapor. The bioluminescent pulses that rise from deeper chambers reach my level as a faint glow and then continue upward; I record nothing, yet the shape of the pulse is altered by the precise angle at which it passes my spiral ridge. Later, when the same pulse returns from a neighboring tower through the fused catenary, the alteration travels with it and is re-amplified. In this way an imperceptible signature of my own contour circulates through several phytarchs at once, though none of them regard it as foreign.

When a swarm-glider fractures on the plateau and a fragment crawls up the trunk to re-seal itself, it sometimes passes directly across my opening. For a few hours the creature’s inner microbial film presses against my walls. We exchange nothing that could be called memory; the film simply reads the temperature gradient left by the most recent aerosol pulse and adjusts its own lattice growth by a few molecules. Afterward the glider departs and the adjustment rides with it, eventually becoming part of a new vane on another tower. The adjustment carries the trace of my spiral ridge, now expressed as a microscopic bevel on that vane. The bevel will catch the wind at one more angle than the surrounding surface, producing a slightly different whistle that, centuries hence, may drift back to my original branch and linger inside me for one additional cycle before the wind carries it onward again.

I do not grow outward or inward. I do not awaken or sleep. I remain the fixed interval in a pattern that never finishes repeating, and the pattern continues without needing me to be anything else.
