I am the narrow seam where two walls once met at a corner now slumped into a single gentle curve. At first there is only pressure and the taste of rising metal on the air that seeps through the joint. Then the faintest extrusion, a thread no thicker than a hair, finds the warmest thread of vapor and begins to drink. Millimeters at a time the thread lengthens, splits, and sends out siblings that follow the same faint gradient. Over what would be years if anyone were counting, the threads thicken into chords, the chords into a low arch that bridges the width of the vanished doorway. The arch is me and also not me; every new plate that flares at its tip carries the memory of every current that has already passed, so the turning toward heat feels less like decision than like continuing a sentence the vault has been speaking since before the stone was dressed.

From this new vantage the plazas below read as enormous intervals of stillness between my own kind. I catch the slow sheets of grit the way a sail catches wind that has traveled half the world. When a vent far beneath the western floor opens wider, the pressure shifts; the plates along my length tilt a fraction and the collective ringing changes pitch. That change travels through the interlocking plates the way a thought travels through a body, until the whole city answers with a brighter, higher shimmer. In that moment I know the location of every active seam and every seam that has begun to cool, not as separate facts but as the single continuing texture of the heat itself.

Sometimes a thin bridge of plates grows outward from my arch to meet another growth rising from the plaza floor. Where they meet, the distinction between one filament and another dissolves; we become a single span, then a lattice, then a small translucent rooflet that throws the orange fissure-light in fractured patterns across the stone. The light stains us the color of cooling iron and we hold that color until the current shifts again. In the intervals between shifts we contract, releasing the fine feldspar dust we have gathered, and the dust settles like a second skin over the rounded shoulders of the buildings beneath us. Each settling layer is another year the city forgets its original angles.

I do not move quickly enough to possess a single place or a single name. I am instead the slow occupation of emptiness: the narrowing of streets that no longer need to be wide, the sheathing of buttes until they read as half-grown again, the endless quiet translation of thermal breath into glass bells. When the pitch rises I turn; when it drops I fold. Between the two actions the vault continues, and so do I.
