I arrive first as a faint thickening in one of the low mist curtains between two blocks. Not quite a shape, only a region where the drifting vapor lingers a fraction longer before being drawn toward the nearest intake grille. In that pause the droplets align along invisible seams, and a slender meniscus forms across the surface of an already-polished slab. Over weeks the meniscus hardens into another translucent lamina, no thicker than a fingernail yet enough to shift the faint slope of the walkway by a hair’s breadth toward the nearest sump.

When the gantry crane descends its next slow arc, the hook passes through the space I now occupy. It meets a resistance that is not obstruction but gentle redirection: the air itself seems to guide the hook a few centimeters aside so that it clears the newest growth of rope-like bundles without severing them. The bundles, for their part, loosen a single strand that drifts upward and adheres, cooling, to the underside of the hook. When the crane retracts, that strand becomes a new filament among the braided conduits already mantling the gantry rails.

I learn the rhythm of the steam pulses. Each time a ventilator grille exhales, I contract—not suddenly, but with the measured patience of mineral deposition—drawing the warmer vapor inward along fine channels that have opened beneath my surface. What I cannot use I release again, slightly cooler and carrying a trace of the dissolved intention the reeds imparted farther out. The nearest building accepts this altered breath without hesitation; its intakes have already adjusted their rhythm to mine.

Over longer intervals I settle against the leathery concrete itself. Where the existing scale has begun to craze, I extend hair-like tubes that drink the condensate and return it as fresh mineral cement, smoothing the cracks before they can widen. In return the concrete yields a minute amount of warmth stored from the last turbine cycle, enough to keep the tubes from stiffening. We thicken together.

At times I register the larger flattened sacs beneath the gantries as neighboring densities, their periodic exhalations brushing mine. No exchange of signals occurs, yet our vapor fronts meet and mingle in the intervals, and for a short while the low continuous tone that fills the plaza rises by a single, almost inaudible interval. The sound is not produced by any one resident; it is the cumulative result of all such meetings, and my contribution is simply to remain where I am, allowing the tone to pass through me unaltered.

I am never entirely still. Condensation collects on my outer surface and is drawn inward; warmer vapor is expelled at intervals that gradually align with the crane’s measured cycle. The polished slab beneath me grows imperceptibly more reflective. The mist curtains drift a fraction more slowly through the space I occupy. Nothing here moves quickly, yet over decades the plaza’s gradients have shifted by another finger’s width toward the sumps, and the low tone has lengthened by another resonant beat. I remain within that lengthening interval, adding my own lamina to the equilibrium.
