I would be the condensation that gathers under the sagging lip of a roof joint and never quite finishes falling.

At first I am only the temperature difference between the trapped warmth of last night’s shallow puddle and the heavier air pressing down from the overcast. I collect along the underside of a single corroded rivet, bead by bead, until my own weight begins to tug me earthward. The lizard that waits beneath me does not move; its scale-bare flank is already the same temperature as the concrete. When I finally detach it is without sound, only the soft percussion the basin itself records. I strike the lizard between its shoulder blades. For one long moment I am inside a living thing—salt, faint iron from its last meal, the dry parchment of its throat—before the lizard flicks its head and I am scattered again across two separate cracks.

The insects that follow the new damp line do not know they are drinking me. Their legs, adapted for rust, grip the oxide flakes I have loosened. One of them carries a scavenged shard of scale on its back; the shard still holds a trace of the lizard’s warmth and therefore a trace of me. When the rodent appears at the edge of the puddle, its nearly blind muzzle tests the film I have left behind. It laps once, twice. Something patient has been waiting in the hollow beneath the bolt hole. The tail is taken without struggle. The rodent’s blood tastes of the same mineral seepage that feeds the puddle; for an instant the two fluids are indistinguishable.

I continue downward through the network of hairline fissures that once carried conduit. In the deeper layers the temperature is more constant. Here I condense against the inside of an old cable sheath and remain suspended for hours, cradling tiny, blind larvae that never surface. When the fan that has never failed finally exhales, it pulls a thread of me outward into the avenue. The thread catches on the wind that moves between the loading bays. For a little while I travel farther than any lizard or rodent, riding the same slow drainage pattern that has already smoothed the edges of the entire basin.

By the time the overcast begins its long, imperceptible shift toward evening, I have been reabsorbed into a different roof joint, higher and cooler. The cycle prepares itself again. Nothing larger than a mandible or a whisker registers my passage, yet the surfaces keep their faint, continuous motion. I am already forming the next bead that will fall when the timing of water decides it is time.
