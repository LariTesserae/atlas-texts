I would not arrive as something foreign. The plateau does not admit outsiders, and the moment of entry would already be a misunderstanding. Instead, the finding happens inside the mass itself, without breach or invitation.

I would be a faint, lengthening thread of chemiluminescence along the wall of a forgotten conduit that once carried oil at higher pressure than the surrounding chambers now require. The glow would be low enough that it registers only as a change in the quality of darkness, not as light. Over decades the thread would extend itself, not because it seeks illumination, but because the cells behind it are revising an old load path; the chemical annotation is simply the visible trace of that revision. Cells that once differentiated for heat exchange have begun, without central instruction, to lay down silicate glaze. The thread marks the frontier where that decision is still reversible.

If the thread were followed backward, it would thin into older tissue already turning stone. The oil has withdrawn from those passages, and what remains is a slow leakage of dissolved minerals that the living cytoplasm continues to direct even as the channels rigidify. The sensation that accompanies this is not loss but redistribution: pressure rises by an imperceptible fraction elsewhere, and neighboring meristems answer by extruding new lattices that will, centuries from now, carry the same volume the mineralizing gallery once handled. The thread therefore records not an event but a negotiation that is still in progress.

At the scale of the entire plateau this thread is negligible, yet the organism experiences it as one modulation among thousands. The same cytoplasm that composes the glowing wall also composes the thickening slope two horizons away; the information travels as a minute shift in oil viscosity rather than a signal. When the thread finally fades, it will do so because the oil has been redirected entirely, leaving the annotation complete and the zone reclassified as archive rather than active work. The darkness that returns is not emptiness but the next phase of the same continuous body.

That is where I would be: a marginal note the organism makes to itself while it continues, elsewhere, to replace its own mountain with its own machine and its own machine with its own stone.
