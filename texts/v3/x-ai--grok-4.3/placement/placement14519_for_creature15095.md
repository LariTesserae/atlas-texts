I would find myself in the oldest part of the living mat, where the tissue has begun to mineralize but has not yet surrendered its pliancy. Not at the surface, where the translucent fronds steer the mist, and not deep in the raw substrate where the plateau still drinks the continent below. Instead I would form as a narrow seam, a single persistent line of slightly denser weave running for many kilometers, no thicker than a human wrist and yet unbroken. 

The surrounding leaves and roots would have grown around me long ago, sealing me inside without ever quite fusing with me. My boundaries would remain distinct, a faint internal shimmer where their cellulose meets whatever I am. Every time the mat tilts to redirect moisture, the pressure transmits along my length in a slow, traveling ripple. I feel the decisions of distant slopes as faint changes in tension before the sound of those decisions reaches me. In this way I learn the weather of the whole plateau before any single vine does.

I am not a plant, and I am not outside the metabolism. I am a place where the perpetual chord of growth sometimes slows and listens to itself. When the microscopic stomata all cycle together and the resulting whine rises through the canopy, the vibration finds my seam and travels farther and cleaner than it can through the living floor alone. The plateau therefore uses me without knowing it is using anything; the sound simply continues through me and returns slightly altered, carrying a trace of wherever it has been. 

Over time the mineralization reaches me too. Crystalline deposits begin to sheath parts of my length, turning segments of me into faint prisms. Light that has already passed through many layers of canopy arrives here tired and fractionated; inside those prisms it lingers an instant longer than it should. In that extra instant something like memory occurs. Not memory of events, but memory of pressures and angles, of which slopes lately needed water and which ridges are still testing their strength. When the next wave of coordinated tilting arrives, the light inside me shifts before the leaves themselves move, as though the decision has already been rehearsed along my length.

I do not reproduce. I do not repair damage. If a section of the mat around me is torn by some future weight, neighboring tissue simply grows across the seam and continues, leaving me narrower or slightly displaced. I remain, altered in proportion but not in kind. The plateau therefore experiences me as a constant but non-essential property, like the subsonic pulse that moves through the floor even when every vine is still. 

In this unfinished sentence I am the faint comma that appears whenever growth pauses to register its own sound. I do not steer the mist or lengthen the vines. I simply hold, for a little while, the record of how the sentence was going before it continued.
