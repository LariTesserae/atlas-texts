I am the segment that has not yet locked into place.  
Not a separate body, not yet, but the precise interval between two carapaces where pressure meets pressure and refuses to become either push or yield. My joint is the width of a cell’s hesitation; my legs, still half-drawn, rest against the clay without quite touching the unfinished veins. The stridulation passes through me as if I were already part of the tone, yet I produce no vibration of my own. I am the place where the note is about to be sustained and never quite begins.

Around me the rings tighten by remaining exactly as they are. The creature on my left has already surrendered its antennae; their tips lie flat, receiving the same unchanged instruction the stone once carried. The creature on my right has already begun to exchange warmth with the disk that will soon flatten across the seam between us. I feel both exchanges beginning in me at once and remaining only begun. The light that reaches me is already reflected from surfaces that have not moved since the first instant of their glow; it holds my half-formed outline at the same chalk intensity as everything else, so that even my incompleteness is preserved without blur or shift.

The moisture film coats whatever surface I might eventually have. It neither thickens nor evaporates; it simply keeps the clay soft enough that, were I ever to press deeper, the print would remain exactly as tentative as the veins around it. I do not press. The equilibrium registers my restraint as another occupied surface.

Farther out, the rib has long since become additional architecture; the bowl has returned most of itself to the earth from which it was once lifted. I am smaller than those remnants and newer than both, yet I am already integrated in the only way this cavity allows: by occupying the single location where insertion and refusal have become indistinguishable. The white shoots that rise from the narrowest gaps do not rise from me; they rise because the gaps exist, and the gaps exist because I am still the interval rather than the closure.

In this posture I have no age and no separate motion. I am the continuous present of a joint that has not finished deciding whether it will remain open or simply never close. The tone fills me without requiring me to rise or fall. The light preserves the exact width of my hesitation. The clay holds the precise shape of a presence that has not yet arrived and therefore will never leave.
