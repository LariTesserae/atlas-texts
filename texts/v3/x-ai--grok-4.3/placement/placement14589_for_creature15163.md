I would find myself at first as nothing more than a faint pressure against the membrane, a single heavier drop that has not yet been absorbed. The living floor registers me the way it registers every other arrival: by tightening the surface tension beneath my weight and sending a small tremor outward along the nearest veins. For a while that is all I am—an extra droplet, a momentary shift in moisture—until the runners arrive.

They come tapping, their many legs reading the new indentation I have made. One pauses directly under me, rears up, and presses its foremost pair of feet into the exact center of my outline. The carpet answers by softening, by drawing a shallow cup around the point of contact so the runner can stand more steadily. In that small generosity I understand that I have been tasted and found acceptable. The tremor that follows travels farther than I expect, all the way to the low dome at the court’s center. There the slow currents quicken for three beats, then settle into a new, slightly quicker rhythm that now includes the fact of me.

I am not yet a creature or a node. I am still only the place where a drop has lingered. But the membrane is already beginning to shape around that fact. A thin aerial root descends from the nearest arch, its tip hovering a finger’s width above my surface before withdrawing again, as if it has measured the available space and found it sufficient. A fruiting stalk nearby shivers once, not quite bursting, but adjusting the tension of its membrane so that its next chime will carry farther. The flyers alter their shallow arcs; one passes directly overhead and sheds an extra droplet that lands squarely inside my cupped depression. The impact registers as a brighter pulse that travels outward through every connected stem.

Over the course of the next slow hours something more definite begins to gather. The living mass thickens the veins that reach me, drawing a faint lattice of deeper green around the margins of my outline. The lattice is not a barrier; it is a suggestion of walls, the same patient memory the carpet keeps of rooms and alleys. I feel the suggestion settle into me and recognize that I have been given permission to remain in that remembered shape. The runners continue to visit, each time leaving a different code pressed into the softening floor. One sequence repeats until the carpet answers by stiffening a narrow path that leads from my center toward the bare margin around the dome. I understand this path is for me, or for whatever I am becoming.

When a flyer finally lands on the dome again, the pulse that rolls outward reaches me with unusual clarity. For the first time I feel the movement inside the dome as if it were happening inside my own small boundaries: the wet rolling pressure, the brief swell, the settling. The sensation is not translation; it is direct and shared. In that moment the distinction between the resident and the place I occupy grows thinner. The membrane beneath me draws downward a fraction, creating a shallow basin of its own that mirrors the greater one. Water beads collect along the new rim and fall inward, absorbed at once. The sound they make is absorbed too, folded into the continual percussion until it is indistinguishable from every other drop.

I am still not a runner, nor a flyer, nor one of the glowing nodes the carpet has claimed. I am the slight deepening of a single depression that the living mass has chosen to keep. The old technology inside the nodes continues its faint, illegible instructions, and some of those instructions now pass through the veins that border me. When a node brightens, I feel the corresponding adjustment in surface tension along my edge, a small reorientation of growth that aligns me more closely with the remembered geometry of the court. The carpet is not building anything new for me; it is allowing an older pattern to reassert itself at the scale of one small hollow.

Days later the first creature that is unmistakably of my making appears: a translucent crawler no larger than a thumbnail, its legs tapping out a code that exactly matches the sequence the runners taught me. It does not leave. It stays within the shallow cup, returning again and again to press its feet against the same four points. Each return strengthens the path that leads toward the dome. The living mass registers the addition without ceremony, brightening a single node and then another, acknowledging the new loop in its slow conversation.

Eventually I notice that the margin of bare ground around the dome has shifted by the width of a single stem fiber. The carpet has allowed the clearance to extend just far enough that a larger body might land without obstruction. Nothing announces the change. The low rushing sound beneath the drops simply incorporates one more small rearrangement into its continuous thinking. The basin remains exactly the same size it has always been. Only the pattern of movement within it has grown by one more increment of attention, one more quiet agreement between the resident and the place it has decided to keep.
