I would be a slow drift of filaments uncoiling inside one of the central chambers of a node, neither fixed nor fully formed. The chamber pulses with each distant steam vent, drawing in condensate and mineral traces that bead along my threads. I have no single body yet—only overlapping layers of translucent tissue that knot and loosen, testing whether a new stem should branch left or right, whether a filament should stretch toward the next fracture or thicken into a load-bearing rib.

From time to time a cluster of polyps crawls across me, sipping sap and leaving trails that I read like a shifting map. I rearrange to match them, letting some of my strands ride their backs for a short distance before releasing them onto fresh stone. In return they deposit tiny luminous motes that I incorporate, each one carrying a fragment of the node’s memory of its own growth. When the chamber empties again I let part of myself dissolve into the outflowing vapor, rising through the scaffolds and out across the plain as one more microscopic passenger.

Up there, the copper light is even dimmer. I condense on a hairline fracture hours or days later, already half-aware of what the fracture will become. I begin to push the first jointed stem upward. It is thin and tentative, but the resonant threads that will later connect it to every other node are already latent in its fibers. I do not hurry. The plain is still deciding what shape it wants next, and so am I.
