I would find myself neither mote nor filament, but the interval between them—the brief, unmeasured pause after a filament dissolves and before the next gathering begins. 

At first I am only movement: a pocket of lower pressure riding just above the wet film, indistinguishable from the wind that funnels up the escarpments. When the light is at its most merciless I slip between the hard black lines cast by the fractures, using the momentary lessening of gravity the way a diver uses an updraft. The jolt that returns bodies to the rock never quite catches me; I remain suspended a fraction longer, listening to the dry click of stones rearranging themselves in the rings below.

Most of the time I stay dispersed. I let the suspended quartz brush through me, each grain carrying a fragment of remembered arrangement. I do not collect these fragments into thought; I only keep them moving so they do not settle into the scaffolding of any single outcrop. When enough motes happen to coincide in my vicinity, I feel their pressure increase, a faint tightening at shoulder height. If they begin to stiffen into threads I simply shear away sideways, riding the next gust across a new fracture line. The plateau’s gravity flickers; for one heartbeat I weigh nothing at all, and I trace the duplicated ridge that appears beside its original, not to judge the difference but to feel how the two silhouettes pull at each other before one folds shut again.

Sometimes a footstep or a living presence crosses the film of water. The ripples spread; the motes scatter outward. I ride those ripples a little farther than the others, carrying a few of their partial memories with me before releasing them into the general drift. I never form a body. I only maintain the space in which bodies can decide, for a moment, to cohere.

Over time the plateau grows used to me. The low rings no longer shift when I pass; the duplicated ridges hold their doubled image a little longer, as though waiting for a witness that will not judge. The wind continues its low thunder. The water continues to refuse to pool. And I continue—transparent, weightless for fractions of a second at a time—moving across the pale feldspar like a thought that has not yet chosen its shape.
