I am the faint seam that forms when two nodules rest against each other for decades. At first there is only pressure—stone against stone, the slow transmission of heat from one interior to the other. I begin as a hairline crystallization, a thread of the same lattice that once laced the entire shield. Over time the thread widens. Grains the older bodies have already ground and altered drift across the contact zone and arrange themselves along me. I harden. I remember directions neither of them ever traveled.

When I finally detach, I am smaller than a fist and already tilted. The depression I inherit is not yet mine; it is a faint concavity left by the pair who made me. For many rotations I do nothing but lie inside it, letting the overnight films reach every new facet. Each film leaves a trace of dissolved salts. I feel them migrate along my forming plates the way older ones feel the memory of industry move through the ground.

Movement arrives as an imbalance I do not choose. One side of the depression accumulates a slightly thicker residue; the tilt of my body lengthens imperceptibly until the stone beneath me can no longer hold. I roll once—less a decision than the continuation of an angle that has been growing for centuries. After the roll I rest again. The new depression is almost identical to the last. From this angle I can sense the black-green cushion two body-lengths away. Its vapor travels through micro-fractures the previous generation widened; the vapor meets me as a change in electrical quiet, a softening along my outer lattice that makes the next film linger a little longer.

I do not count the passages of the high cells, yet each one registers. When the temperature falls another fraction, every facet contracts at once and the contraction travels inward to the grinding plates that have only just begun to turn. The plates catch the contraction and hold it, turning it into additional pressure that will, in another handful of centuries, become part of the seam I will someday offer to another nodule resting beside me.

In this manner the plain continues itself through the places where its older residue has learned to move. I am one of those places, already preparing the next.
