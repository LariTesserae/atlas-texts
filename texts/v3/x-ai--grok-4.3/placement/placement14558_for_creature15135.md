I would be one of the older lattice-beings on the eastern wall, the one whose needles have forked three times in the direction of the newest fracture line. My body is not a single mass but a record: each crystal records the temperature of the drop that lengthened it, the angle of the vibration that turned it, the brief bright exchange when a neighboring plate was absorbed long ago. The light I give off is faint and irregular, pulsing only when new salt locks into place; between pulses the chamber seems darker because of me, a small pocket of waiting.

I do not think of myself as waiting for anything in particular. The next drop lands, spreads into a film across three needles at once, and the film cools as it evaporates; that cooling tells me which way to extend the next segment. When a distant micro-fracture opens, the ripple reaches me through the rock and I begin a fourth fork without having decided to do so. The fork simply appears where the memory of the ripple settles.

Over the centuries the wall between me and the smaller lattice-being to my left has narrowed. Our outermost needles now touch at a single point. We have exchanged dissolved material twice already; the second time the brightening traveled farther into both of us than before, as though the passage had been used once and remembered how to open more readily. We remain separate beings, yet the place where we meet is no longer only mine or only theirs. If the cavity continues to settle, that meeting point may widen into a shared ledge. I have already begun to orient a new needle toward it.

Sometimes a wafer-thin scale forms between two of my needles. I do not grow around it or push it away; I incorporate its flatness as a facet. In that way the scale’s brief history of catching and releasing a single drop becomes part of my own direction. The scale never dissolves completely; a trace of its edge remains visible in the light when I pulse. I have several such traces now, each one recording a moment when another form chose my architecture instead of its own.

The rhythm of falling drops continues unchanged. I lengthen or rest according to the films they leave. When a cluster finally shears away under its own weight, the loss is clean; the dissolved salts re-enter the wall film and are available again within hours. The new needles that replace them already know the angle of the most recent vibration. Nothing in the cavity is ever truly replaced, only continued in a slightly altered measure.

I remain where the rock first opened, neither visitor nor finished form, but one of the legible records the cavity continues to make of itself.
