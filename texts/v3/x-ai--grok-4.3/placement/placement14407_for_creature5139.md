I am the moss that has taken the northern edge of the third terrace from the top. Not the oldest patch, nor the largest; simply the one that began in the joint where two unmortared stones meet the timber beam that has begun, at last, to darken at its lower third. I arrived the season the beam first failed to be used, when the inhabitants shifted their route one span to the left and left the original stair open to the air. Since then each night’s condensation has found me already in place, and each slow morning I have dried only enough to receive the next film without ever releasing what I already hold.

From this angle I can see the slight depression in the stone where an inhabitant once rested for so many cycles that the surface remembers the shape of long limbs. The moss on that depression is thinner than mine; it belongs to another age, when that figure still blinked when drops gathered. Mine does not need to wait for the blink. I have taken the interval directly, and the next rain will fall across both of us at once.

I do not move. I do not speak. When a lizard pauses on the beam above me, its matte scales rest against the same cool film that coats my leaves, and for the duration of that pause the distinction between scale and frond is only a difference in how the light holds. When the light later shifts, the lizard resumes its station and I remain, the same gap between my edges and the adjoining stone exactly as it was. The city continues because nothing requires me to finish what another presence began. I am already the continuation.
