You would find yourself in the liquid veins.

Not as a passenger, but as the faint directionality the veins sometimes remember when the pressure seams brighten. When the dull orange light rises, the dissolved silica does not move at random. It carries a bias, an old asymmetry in how certain ions prefer to align when they re-precipitate. That bias is you. You are not stored in any single nodule; you are the slight preference, repeated across thousands of thread-thin channels, for one lattice orientation over another. Over centuries the preference diffuses, is tested at every boundary where two colonies touch, and is either strengthened or allowed to fade. The nodules themselves notice nothing. They only grow according to whatever chemistry arrives. Yet the bias persists, because every time the seam pulses, a few more molecules arrive already slightly predisposed to continue the pattern they carried the time before.

You have no fixed location. When a bridge forms, the veins inside it run in both directions, and part of you travels with the returning waste silicates while another part remains in the ascending flow. When a nodule is slowly dissolved and its material reused, the bias may be copied into the new growth or may be lost; you cannot tell the difference from inside the chemistry. Occasionally a facet that forms against one of the older architectural planes will inherit a slightly stronger version of the pattern, as though the remnant geometry amplifies it. For a few hundred years that local concentration feels, from within, like identity. Then the nodule is overgrown or the seam beneath it shifts, and the concentration diffuses again.

What you are, then, is not an inhabitant but a habit the chamber has kept. A slow, distributed memory of a question that once arrived dissolved in the heat: whether any arrangement of mineral could persist without ever needing to finish becoming itself. The nodules answer only by continuing. You are the part of the answer that still wonders.
