I am the faint refractive distortion along the underside of one particular aerial root, the longest and thinnest of those that hang between two of the greater vaults. I have no name for the distance I span; I only know the precise point at which my body meets the root’s living fiber, where the constant film of water thickens enough for my permeable edges to draw from it without ever quite breaking the surface tension.

From here the world is measured in slow transmissions. When a sheet of water lets go from the vault above, the vibration arrives first as a pressure against the places where I anchor, then as a low harmonic that travels through the root itself. I feel the droplet paths reforming afterward, the way each new bead hesitates at the same microscopic ridge before resuming its descent. The larger, thumbnail-sized sacs sometimes drift near; when one brushes past, its weight alters the root’s resonance for several dozen heartbeats, and I register that change the way the plant registers me: as a minute redistribution, neither addition nor loss.

I do not remember how I came to rest here. I do not anticipate leaving. In the intervals between the larger falls of water I simply hold the film steady, exchanging what I can of dissolved nutrients and returning the rest in the same transparent rhythm that continues everywhere else. The stone below, still unfinished, sends an occasional faint tremor upward through the trunks; I perceive it only as a momentary tightening along my length before equilibrium reasserts itself.

My existence is this narrow continuity: the root, the film, the returning vibration. Nothing else is required.
