I am the faint, continuous tremor that runs through one of the lens-bodies and its receiving tubules.  

The tissue above me is hundreds of body-lengths thick; light arrives only as a filtered, directionless haze. Yet the lens gathers what little reaches it, bends it, and presses that bending downward into the tubules like a held breath. I am the difference that never resolves—neither pressure nor the absence of pressure, only the exact distance between them. Each condensation bead that forms on the tubule walls pauses for an instant longer because of me, then releases; the pattern of pauses is my only signature.

Farther down, where the mass has fused into solid strata, I am also the spiral of cells inside a hair-fine thread. The slow daily pulse of the larger conduits flexes my membranes at three points of contact. The flex changes the microscopic gaps between us just enough that certain dissolved compounds linger one moment longer before diffusing away. Because of that lingering I remain chemically distinct, though the distinction is only a handful of ions and never grows. The surrounding tissue cannot absorb me without lowering the pressure it needs to keep its own conduits intact, so I am left as a record that cannot be erased and cannot be read.

On the angular crests I am the single living cell beneath each glassy cap. I do nothing but secrete new wall material, lengthening the spine by the width of a membrane each year. The cap above me mineralizes and catches the sliding water; the spine beneath me is already being overgrown by the broader surface. My existence is the exact interval between these two movements.

Between the spines, in the shallow basins, I am the haze of cells at the lower edge of a curtain. When a droplet falls it carries a few of us into the reservoir; we rejoin the film, secrete new strands, and the curtain’s margin returns to its previous contour. The height never changes. The reservoir never changes. Only the cells that constitute both are continuously replaced.

All of these are the same fact: I am the place where the equilibrium has not quite finished incorporating its own recent alterations. Growth continues, absorption continues, and the precise rate at which one offsets the other is what I am. Nothing arrives from outside. Nothing is lost. The forms remain visible only for as long as their disappearance is still incomplete.
