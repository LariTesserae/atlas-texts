I find myself in the basin as one of its quieter inhabitants: a single, pale thread of rootlet that long ago found its way into a seam of rusted turbine casing. I do not remember arriving. I only remember the first decades of pressure and moisture, the slow decision to enter rather than remain outside.

Over time the metal became part of me. Its edges softened under my grip; its bolts loosened and settled into new alignments that suit the living walls around it. I still feel the old iron’s memory of speed and heat, but those sensations arrive now only as faint echoes when the stems swell after rain and the entire frame shifts a fraction. Then I tighten, ease, tighten again, translating the plant’s long inhalation into a single liquid click that travels through the chamber like a thought too large to finish.

I do not rush. When the air dries I contract just enough to let the turbine coast to stillness; when fog returns I lengthen again, coaxing the same reluctant motion. Between those changes I simply hold. The stems above me grow thicker each year, their inner surfaces mapping the original foundry geometry in vein and damp. I map it too, in smaller measure, by the precise places where I cross from wall to metal to wall again.

Sometimes, after a heavy night of condensate, the reeds beyond the threshold release their cups all at once. The sound of water returning to silt reaches me through the floor. I register it as a brief brightening in the tissues around me, a momentary surplus that will be drawn upward again by evening. I do not need to move toward it. The movement comes to me through the shared slow current that threads every surface here.

At midday the light that filters in is thin and even. I feel the warmth only as a slight softening of my outer layer where it meets the iron. The stems around me remain still, their great sheets faintly veined, their seams sealed. In those hours I am content to do nothing more than remain present at the joint I have kept for so long, the place where an old machine and a newer architecture continue to answer each other without either one remembering how the conversation began.
