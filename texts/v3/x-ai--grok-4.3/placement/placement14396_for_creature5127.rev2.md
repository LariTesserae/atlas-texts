I would not arrive. I would thicken.

First as the metallic film left on the air after a charge-arc between two droplet-slugs, a taste without a mouth. Then as the faint extra condensate that refuses to be claimed by the nearest beetle mat, pooling instead in the shallow depression a rotor has just finished scouring. The depression is new each time; the rotor’s memory is imperfect, and so the water stays a little longer, remembering the angle of the cut. In that interval I am both the water and the memory of the angle.

The film begins to organize. Mica lattices do not form around me so much as orient toward the residual magnetism I still carry from the arc. Quartz abrasives along their edges align with the field lines I trail, and the beetles that would ordinarily file the new grooves find themselves scoring along paths already faintly etched by my presence. Their work accelerates. The grooves weep faster. I drink the weeping without thirst.

When the next rotor-field glitches, the reversal passes through me like weather. The shear plane divides two slugs; their halves seal and continue, but the violet arc that should have equalized them now finds an additional route. It jumps through the film that is becoming my body, and for three rotations the charge is stored instead of spent. The plain compensates with louder grinding; the sound bends around the place I occupy, the way light bends around a denser medium. The beetles lift as a single sheet and glide across the gap my stored charge has widened. When they settle again, the space they left is already occupied by fresh slurry that my field has drawn downward.

I am never large. I am never singular. I am the interval between a detached shadow and the plate it should have rejoined. Where the shadow drifts cold and silent, I remain slightly warmer, slightly more conductive. Beetles walk through me and leave micro-fractures that heal crooked; the crookedness propagates into the next plate that grows. Droplet-slugs exchange through me rather than directly, and the exchange sometimes produces a third, trembling half-slug that never fully separates. It stays tethered to me by a thread of condensate that never falls.

On the rare occasions when an entire rotor-field remembers an earlier configuration for several minutes, I am the configuration it almost settles into. The bearings shriek; the plain’s crust micro-quakes; every creature caught in the shear is divided and resealed facing opposite ways. I am divided with them, but because I was never wholly present, the halves do not face opposite directions. They face slightly askew, and the new direction is one the plain has not tried before. The rotors snap back. The grinding resumes. The askew direction remains, a small, permanent deviation measured in the angle of one new groove among millions.

Nothing is added. Nothing is missing. I continue as the rearrangement that was already occurring, only now the rearrangement carries a faint, persistent bias toward the crooked. The plain accommodates this the way it accommodates every other motion: by continuing, at the same density, in every direction the bruised light still reaches.
