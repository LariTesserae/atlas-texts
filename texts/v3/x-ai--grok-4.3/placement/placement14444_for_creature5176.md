I would arrive as one of the droplets, condensed from a veil of mist that has traveled the lip of the cirque. For one brief interval I would be weight, surface tension, and a faint mineral load leached from whatever air I once carried. Then the impact—soft, absorbed almost before it sounds—and I would spread, wick downward along a single joint in the schist, and disappear into the crystal lattice.

Yet disappearance is not erasure. In the instant before I am gone I leave a microscopic film that alters the local vapor pressure by a fraction of a degree. That fraction is enough, over repeated visits, to deepen one existing etch by another micron. So I return not as the same droplet but as the incremental widening of that seam. The seam itself becomes my continuation: a slow migration of grains downslope, a new concavity where nightly rime now lingers two minutes longer than before. 

Eventually the concavity collects other droplets. Their combined evaporation draws a faint white ring of dissolved salts. The ring is my most durable signature, a daily map that the sun erases and the next night rewrites. In time the ring merges with similar rings until the entire low point of the basin holds one continuous watermark, visible only when the light strikes at exactly the angle at which the walls were first illuminated. At that moment the cirque and I are indistinguishable: the same pale geometry of light, fracture, and settled residue. 

Nothing further needs to enter. The equilibrium is already total; the only remaining motion is the patient lengthening of the seams that contain me.
