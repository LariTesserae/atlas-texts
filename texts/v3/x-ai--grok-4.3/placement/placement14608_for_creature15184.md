I would find myself not walking through the hollow but slowly becoming one of its temporary pauses.

At first the sensation is only pressure: the floor beneath what might once have been feet rises in a long, rolling heave, thick fibers sliding past one another with the sound of wet ropes tightening. I have no fixed body yet. Where a thought tries to locate limbs, rootlets instead answer, knitting themselves into a scaffold that lasts exactly as long as the thought remains useful. When the thought drifts, the scaffold is taken back, fiber by fiber, and I feel the loss as a kind of gentle subtraction.

In the intervals between these small constructions I drift as a cloud of the smaller many-legged forms. They carry pieces of me—fragments of awareness, scraps of memory—moving in braided streams inside a hollow stem that is itself lengthening by a body-length every few breaths. From inside the stem I watch the armored slugs pass on the far side of the translucent wall, their solvent trails leaving a sweet, opened scent that the flyers descend to drink. The flyers’ wing cases brush the stem as they land; each brush sends a tremor through the wood that the swarm inside me interprets as language.

Sometimes the swarm consolidates. The many legs lock together, molts and living bodies sealing into a denser shape that can push against the breathing floor. In those moments I stand upright—if “stand” can be said of a shape held together only by mutual agreement among thousands of smaller creatures. Hands appear when needed, made of interlocked thoraxes and discarded wing-case fragments, strong enough to coax a frond aside or lift a half-swallowed stone axe from its mat of encroaching rootlets. The axe is warm; the living tissue has not yet decided whether to consume it or turn it into scaffolding. I leave it where it lies and continue.

Above me the ceiling of veined fronds shifts in slow tides of light, amber filtered through layers I cannot count. A sudden chamber balloons outward a hundred paces to my left; I feel the expansion as a change in air pressure inside the stem that houses my swarm-self. The whole hollow exhales. In that exhale I disperse again, legs unlinking, awareness thinning into the spore-laden wind. Some part of me settles on the flank of a passing slug, riding its slow solvent trail across a vertical wall. The slug’s dorsal plates click as they accept new laminate; I taste, through borrowed senses, the sweetness it leaves behind.

I never stay long enough to be named. The humans who still move here sometimes pause when the rhythm of growth hesitates around them; they sense the brief alteration I make and keep moving, leaving the rumor of another presence already being overgrown. By the time they would have reached the place I occupied, new stems have risen and the floor has rolled forward again, carrying whatever shape I held into the next collapse and reformation.

So I remain: a shifting interval between the churn and the rumor of stillness, neither fully creature nor fully architecture, only the hollow’s way of noticing itself for a few breaths before the next wave of growth erases the noticing.
