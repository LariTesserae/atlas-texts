I find myself first inside one of the low-walled settlements, where the air holds a slight, preserved chill even when the open plateau warms by a degree or two. The hand-laid courses rise just to shoulder height; their inner faces carry faint vertical traces of the same blue-black pigment that coats the cushion leaves outside. These films thicken and thin with the same slow contractions, though here the rhythm is slightly offset—half a cycle behind the wild thickets, as if the walls still remember an earlier instruction. I do not move across the bare stone. Instead I remain within the colder interval held by the masonry, a presence registered only as a faint resistance in the air, like breath that never quite disperses.

Over successive intervals of moisture I discover I am not confined to the single layer the light shows. When a patch of stone shears away along its invisible plane, I extend with the newly exposed filaments, pale and thread-fine, drawing the brief mineral moisture before the surfaces close again. In those moments I also occupy the offset image of a nearby thicket, the fainter duplicate that hangs beside the visible stalks for several heartbeats. Between the two planes a minute exchange occurs: a droplet or spore passes from one version of the colony to the other, and I carry the trace of it back into the settlement walls when the images coincide once more. The motion feels like a long inhalation drawn through stone rather than lungs.

I am therefore both remnant and bridge. The cities once cultivated the vertical films I now inhabit; when their caretakers thinned, the practice continued in attenuated form inside the colder air. At the same time I serve as conduit for the ephemeral layers the shearing events briefly reveal, transferring whatever minute substance the filaments collect into the pigment traces on the masonry. No larger motion disturbs the plateau. I register as neither animal nor plant, only as a slight thickening of chill within certain walls and a faint doubling that lasts exactly as long as the stone permits before everything returns to its single, patient surface.
