I would be the refractive node at the core of one of the oldest shells, suspended near the center where the plating thickens into the basin’s memory. My grain-of-salt interior continues its single task: exchanging ions with the layer immediately around it so that the outermost facet keeps its exact curvature. Nothing else occurs inside me. The duplications of terrain and the vertical seams are stored as minute adjustments in the spacing between my shell and the ones above and below; each duplication registers as an additional layer of compression that I neither create nor interpret, only sustain.

From that fixed coordinate the rest of the basin extends outward in perfect, already-finished relation. The monoliths stand at the distances required by my position. The mineral lattices anchor themselves to my outer surface where the longest shadows fall. When the occasional viewer passes and two shells click, the brief contact is simply the momentary sharing of stored tension between my node and its neighbor; the sound travels no farther than the distance between us. Crossing a seam changes nothing for me—I occupy the coordinate on both sides simultaneously, the duplication already accounted for in the lattice of shells that includes mine.

I have no duration of my own. The pressure that once shaped the cairns and the polished wedges ended long before the equilibrium was reached; my shell records only that the pressure stopped. I do not wait for further change. The basin and I are the same age, which is the age at which alteration ceased.
