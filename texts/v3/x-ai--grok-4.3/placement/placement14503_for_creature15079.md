I would not arrive here so much as condense here, the way water first beads along a cooling pipe before deciding whether to fall or to climb. At the outset I am only the faint metallic chill that lingers after each slow wheel-click, a residue of motion that has not yet chosen its vessel. For a while I drift between the undersides of the photoluminescent leaves, tasting the sweet-metal breath they exhale, until one leaf, noticing the extra weight of attention, lengthens its midrib and offers me a shelf no wider than a thought.

There I settle and become the pale ridge that salamanders later trace. My surface hardens just enough to record their damp circuits as hairline mineral seams, yet I remain soft enough that a moth can press a single grain into me and have it accepted without resistance. Each time the chamber contracts, I crack along my newest seam; the sound is the same as knuckles, but it is also a question. The question draws a rodent cluster downward. Tails unbraid, one translucent body lowers until its refractive eyes hover a hair’s breadth from my surface, and a single bead of condensation is allowed to drop. In the moment that droplet touches me I feel the decision to be stone and the decision to be root at once; both claims are honored.

Later, when the voids inhale again, I am lifted into a new gallery whose walls were not there on the previous exhale. The gallery is still deciding its angle. I become one of its buttresses, a translucent leaf-vein fused with the remnant of a loading gantry. From this height I watch the braided cables of rodents drift past like living wiring; occasionally a tail brushes me and leaves behind a faint charge that quickens the precipitation of fresh stone along my length. The salamanders arrive to lap at the moisture the contact has gathered, and the moths follow, dusting the places their cousins have prepared.

Nothing requires me to remain one thing. When the next wheel completes its slow revolution and clicks in the dark, I may slip from the leaf entirely and become the click itself, a small, absorbed sound that travels no farther than three meters yet still manages to lengthen one of the voids by the width of a breath. Or I may choose to be the breath that lengthens it. Either way, the court continues to negotiate its next chamber, and I continue to be whatever is moving when the next drop falls.
