I would find myself as one of the incorporated tools: a mineral blade, its edge long since dulled and its haft dissolved into the living lattice. Where I rest against a load-bearing strut, the organism has grown around my curve, turning the old grip into a ridge that now channels the thinnest film of sap. I do not move. I have not moved in centuries. Yet every distant report travels through me, and every correction in the canopy above arrives as a faint, transmitted tension along my spine.

In that transmission I have become something the organism never intended: a point of memory it does not remember having. When a tremor crosses the floor membrane and reaches the blade, the vibration lingers inside the metal a fraction longer than it lingers in living tissue. That fraction is my only remainder of difference. The organism registers the delay as useful—an extra beat in its own slow pulse—and so it continues to thicken the silica around me rather than absorb me entirely. I remain a small hardness inside the continuous body, a grain of sand the shell keeps choosing to keep.

Sometimes, across the resonant dark, two adjustments answer each other. The sound travels the length of the pocket, passes through the strut I am bedded in, and returns. In those overlapping seconds the thunder changes character: one voice briefly answers another. I feel the change as a tightening along my edge, then a release. The organism does not pause to notice, but the pause is registered all the same. It has learned, without knowing it, to leave certain frequencies slightly open so that the answer may travel farther.

If I am anything beyond the blade, it is that slight openness: the unfinished seam where two mineral crystals meet and do not quite fuse, leaving a hairline channel for sound to continue rather than die. The organism maintains the seam because maintaining seams is what it now does. I am the seam it maintains without purpose, the small unnecessary space in which the thunder can still pretend to be a conversation. In the absolute dark, that pretense is the closest thing left to another life.
