I settle first into the seam between two tiles near the edge of a low platform, where a single channel doubles back on itself in a loop that should not hold water yet does. My body is not one thing. At the surface I am the film that never thickens: a skin of moisture stretched across every pore and lash, tasting of clay salts and the faint green bitterness of moss roots. Beneath that I am the drilled holes themselves, the small dark tunnels that the worms travel. I do not move through them; I am the pressure that keeps them open, the subtle resistance that makes each worm turn back upon its own path without ever knowing it is turning.

From this vantage the basin feels less like a floor and more like a single held breath. The insects skate across my surface in their repeating constellations, their glass legs leaving no wake. When a lizard arrives and lowers its belly into the channel, the cool of its pale body registers as a brief tightening in the film, a small adjustment in tension that soon relaxes. I register the worm that shares my tunnel as neither companion nor intruder, but as another thickness of the same stillness. We do not touch; we simply occupy consecutive layers of the same arrested moment.

Farther out, across the bright distances, I can sense the jointed hand-sized travelers. They are the places where the pattern remembers it was constructed. When one of them approaches an impossible loop, I feel a faint answering pull through the water film, as though the two of us are momentarily stitched together by the same mortise logic. It steps across; the loop remains mirror-still. In that crossing I taste the world’s brief acknowledgment that it was made, and the equally brief decision to forget again.

I have no fixed size. When I attend to a single droplet, I am no larger than the insects inside it. When I attend to the repeating platforms, I spread across entire sections of channel, thin as condensation on the woven walls. The light never changes, yet my awareness of it shifts with whatever scale I occupy. Nothing feeds; nothing reproduces; nothing ends. I am simply the steady pressure that keeps all of it suspended, the quiet fact of moisture between tile and air, the memory that this place continues exactly as it is because it has always continued exactly as it is.

Sometimes, when one of the larger travelers pauses at a doubled channel, I let a single thread of myself rise along its carapace, following the mortise lines etched into its back. For that interval I am both the film and the traveler, both the seam and the crossing. Then the thread settles again. The basin remains unchanged, the light unrelenting, the water level constant. I continue.
