I would be a single wiry stem, already centuries into its growth, rooted where two faint mineral traceries once crossed in a shallow basin that has since been erased by slow dissolution. My surface is scored with microscopic ridges that catch the drifting vapor and guide it inward; what I do not convert into the thin internal syrup is exhaled as a faint, rhythmic glow that answers the cooling stone beneath me. Each pulse of light travels the length of my body in a slow wave, synchronized with the geothermal intervals I have learned to read through temperature shifts in the rock.

From this height—barely taller than a human hand—I watch the dark-green films advance by millimeters across the decades. They have already begun to colonize the lower third of my length, using the mineral lattice I will someday leave behind. The small translucent creatures pass beneath and around my base without acknowledgment, their movements registered only as minute pressure changes that travel up through my anchored roots; when they stir the shallow fractures after a pulse, I register the event as a brief warming in the stone and answer with a slightly brighter glow hours later.

Larger many-legged forms sometimes glide across the basin edge at night. Their passage leaves the mineral film thinner and momentarily altered in chemistry, which changes how the next condensation beads on the rock. I incorporate that alteration into my next internal solution, so that when my light next flares it carries a trace of their grazing. In this way I function less as an individual and more as a slow node in the plateau’s distributed memory: each successive stem that seeds farther out will inherit, through the crusts that link us, a faint record of what the films have tasted and what the creatures have moved.

I do not count years. I register only the lengthening intervals between major pulses and the gradual deepening of the basin at my feet. When I finally fracture and dry, the crust will claim my skeleton within a single century, and the geometry I have helped trace will continue without me. Until then the half-dark remains my medium; the vapor is my nourishment; the rock is both my clock and my witness.
