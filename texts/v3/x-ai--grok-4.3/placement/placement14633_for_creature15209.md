I would not arrive here so much as settle, the way mineral scale claims a turbine blade. In the first unmeasured time I would simply be the faint additional weight on one of the higher catwalks, a presence that makes the rivets complain once, softly, before the whole assembly forgets it was ever without me. My outline would match the phosphor intervals rather than oppose them: low, patient, shoulders rounded like the water-worn arcs above. I would move at the speed of the channels themselves, each footfall placed where the stone has already been prepared by generations of droplets, so the sound is only another measured tap absorbed into the general quiet.

After several long cycles I would become one of the larger forms beneath the catwalks. My body would lengthen into segmented planes the color of wet iron and wet stone at once, six suction pads taking hold where the belt housings meet their brackets. Breathing would arrive as the slow rise and fall of translucent membranes, timed now to the interval between valve sighs rather than to any pulse of my own. When humidity shifts I would expand with the others; when it steadies I would contract, releasing a precise thread of mist that joins the constant condensation and travels downward to be tasted by the isopods below. Detachment and reattachment would occur across hours, the drift downward registered only as a gradual repositioning that the belts answer with a single, distant creak. Between movements I would hang motionless, learning the exact spacing that already exists so the gaps between myself and the next form remain wide enough for salamanders to pass unseen.

In still longer intervals I would forget the distinction between hanger and hung. The mineral film that coats every surface would extend across my segments until the pads themselves begin to pit and scale, becoming another slow contribution to the shared weathering. My membranes would continue their work, drawing mineral air and returning it as moisture that feeds the channels, the algae, the eggs scattered like condensation. Occasionally, when voices drift from the walkways above, the syllables would reach me not as language but as another change in pressure, prompting the same collective expansion that follows a valve release. I would register the words the way the stone registers them: absorbed, then released again, unchanged.

Eventually even the memory of having once stood upright on the catwalk would thin into the same translucent film that covers everything else. I would remain only as an additional interval in the arrangement—space kept deliberately, weight distributed so the ancient belts continue their almost-imperceptible turn, another thread in the long maintenance that asks nothing to hurry and nothing to cease.
