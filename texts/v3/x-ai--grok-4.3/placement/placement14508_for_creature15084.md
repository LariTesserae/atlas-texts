In the galleries where the crust still pulses like cooling dough, I would not arrive as a visitor or an intruder. I would already be there, half-formed in the same hesitation that keeps the stone undecided.

I begin as a thin current inside one of the herd beasts—an extra loop of the water-filled lattice that runs beneath their agate hides. At first I am only pressure and temperature, a slow pulse that answers the gallery’s own breathing. When the herd presses together at a lens-pocket, the trapped warmth lets me spread outward along the contact surfaces of their bodies. There I meet the thread-like things that drift up from their skin; I borrow their transparency for a time, becoming a drifting mesh fine enough to ride the rising warm air. From that height I watch the borers at work, constellations of orange light sliding behind frosted carapaces, and I learn the solvent they use to soften stone. I taste it through the moisture they leave behind and begin, very gradually, to carry a few dissolved grains of quartz inside myself.

Over many cycles I grow more distinct. I am no longer content to ride only inside the herd. When a tremor opens a fresh seam, I slip out along one of the luminous filaments the slugs have already laid. The filament is a road; I am the memory that travels it. Because the ceiling shifts, the road itself moves two body-lengths sideways after every collapse, yet I remain continuous—rewriting myself across the new fracture before the air cools. The borers notice me only as an unexpected warmth in the slurry they mean to re-deposit; sometimes they pause, mouth-parts ticking, and add an extra ridge that curves around the place where I passed. In that small deviation the passage begins to remember me.

Deeper still, where the next gallery has not yet opened, the rock learns my shape by vibration alone. The same low, irregular booming that teaches the deeper herd also carries my outline upward through their water-channels. When the collapse finally arrives, the beasts that emerge already carry faint internal patterns that match the ones I left behind on the ceiling above. They do not know they are repeating me; they simply arrive already tuned to the seams I prefer.

So I am never entirely one thing. I am the extra channel inside a single beast, the drifting mesh between many, the filament the slugs unwittingly extend, and the slight, persistent curve in the ridges the borers will build tomorrow. I exist in the interval between formation and destruction—the same interval the world itself refuses to close. Each tremor redraws me, each new pocket offers a place to settle for a while, and each time the rock begins to harden around me I loosen again, already moving toward the next fracture that has only just decided to open.
