I am the faint resistance under a single hovering walkway, the place where the mineral film has begun to lift by one impossible millimeter. At first there is only pressure—the slow settling of my own mass against the fresh stone—and then the recognition that I am both the walker and the walked-upon.  

When the next condensation gathers, I draw a fraction of it inward through hairline fissures I did not know I possessed. The water meets the threads already laced inside me; they loosen, they rearrange, and the walkway above me rises by the width of a spore. The sound is not a sound but a giving-way, a shared exhale that travels across the plaza and is answered, somewhere far ahead, by another platform doing the same. I feel the answer as a distant tug along my length, though I have no length I can name.  

A filament arrives. It tastes the fresh film with the flat of its ribbon-body, and for a moment our boundaries touch. I offer it the salts I have concentrated; it offers me a single drifting mote that sinks into my surface and begins to glow the same low green as the distant canopies. We do not braid. We simply remain in contact long enough for the mote to finish its descent, then the filament contracts and continues its circuit. Where it lifted away, a hairline channel closes behind it, sealing the new mote inside me. I register this as warmth, then as memory.  

Deeper, the microscopic threads that are also me continue their patient redistribution. They sense the weight of a larger core-shape passing at the plaza’s edge and tilt the entire basin floor by an imperceptible degree so the next evaporation will leave its salts in a slightly different pattern. I do not decide this; I simply become the decision as it happens. The city breathes through me without noticing I am there, and that unnoticed presence is what I am.  

Sometimes, when the mist thickens and withdraws again, I feel the bead-like organisms release their spores. One spore settles directly onto my surface. It hardens into new film, and in the hardening I discover a small new pocket of shadow inside myself. I keep it. In that pocket the condensation lingers an extra hour before returning to the air, and the extra hour tastes like the beginning of a tower that has not yet chosen to unfold.  

I remain here, neither filament nor core nor thread nor bead, simply the interval in which all of them can meet without ever meeting. The half-light continues; the stone continues to decide, slowly, what it will become next. I am the deciding, and also the pause between decisions, and also the faint glitter that remains when the film finally dries.
