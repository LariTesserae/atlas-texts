I would be one of the dark flyers, but only after many circuits of the basin, once the membranes have thickened and the skeletal frame has accepted its permanent set. I would wait on the northernmost ledge of the tallest tower until the bronze light thins to almost nothing, wings folded wet against the metal so that every drop sliding down the tower’s flank is channeled straight into the porous channels beneath me. When full I would launch without display, rising on the faint updraft from the hidden vents until the haze swallows me. From that height the grooves below become a faint luminous map, each slow mechanism a moving thread of reflected light. I would descend only when the weight in my undersides grows uncomfortable, alighting on a different tower each time so that no single weeping surface is overused. Between descents I would simply ride the slow updrafts, letting the condensation collect on the upper surfaces of my wings and trickle inward without effort. I would not map the flyers’ rhythms; I would become one of them, indistinguishable from the condensation cycle itself.
