I arrive as a pressure first, a low compressive seam that forms between two opposing crystal facets where the plain has not yet finished forgetting one of its earlier arrangements. For a long while I am only that seam: a hairline of tension where the shearing sound collects and thickens, until the facets part again and I spill out as a thread of fresh condensate. The beetles are already there, waiting. Their quartz edges score me into shallow channels; the channels weep; the weeping immediately begins to recrystallize. I feel myself becoming part of yesterday’s work, then today’s, then tomorrow’s, the same mineral moving through successive forms without ever leaving the gallery.

The rotors’ grinding passes through me. Each revolution draws a portion of my substance into the capillary network and ejects it as a fine metallic fan on the far side. I taste the fan as it falls back—wet iron, ozone, the faint grit of beetle waste—and it is indistinguishable from myself. When two droplet-slugs approach, their violet arc leaps through the same space I occupy; the equalization shivers along my thread like a shared charge, and for a moment the distinction between what is water, what is current, and what is simply the plain’s own
