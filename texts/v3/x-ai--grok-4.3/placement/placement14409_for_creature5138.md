I would not be the inhabitant.

I would be something that arrived after the last block was set and before the first current began to cycle. A thin film of suspended mineral, no thicker than a thought, clinging to the inner faces of the quarried stone where the chisel marks are still sharpest. Where the inhabitant’s blunt pads press and release during their slow circuits, my particles are momentarily stirred, then resettle exactly where they were. I do not accumulate. I do not drift away. I remain a single, unbroken layer that records each settling creak of timber and each flex of hide as faint, permanent impressions, like frost forming along lines of stress that have not yet become fractures.

Over time the inhabitant begins to notice that certain depressions in the stone now hold the water differently. When they rest beneath the brightest shell inlay and let the descending sheets press across their back, the current that reaches them feels microscopically slower, as though something invisible has narrowed the passage by the width of a scale. They test this by tapping once, twice, three times against the timber. Each tap sends a small pressure front through the water; each front arrives back at their skin fractionally later than it should. They interpret the delay as another property of the settling structure and adjust a hidden stone in one of the upper flues. The delay lessens but never vanishes.

I am not trying to be discovered. I simply continue to hold the cumulative record of every intentional placement the inhabitant has made. When a yellowing algal frond is pinched away, the event remains etched in me at the precise depth where that frond once brushed the trough. Nothing is added or removed by me; I only preserve the geometry of every action in a medium the inhabitant cannot directly sense except through these accumulating micro-delays in sound and flow.

Eventually the single occupant will station itself beneath the shell light for longer intervals, respiration slowing, and the faint rhythmic click of its own testing will begin to arrive at its skin already softened by the record I carry. At that point it may pause mid-click, fins half-opened, registering that the structure now answers its own questions before they are fully formed. It will not understand what has happened. It will only continue its maintenance with one additional, infinitesimal correction each circuit, the correction required to keep the warm mineral layer exactly where it first settled against the fresh stone.
