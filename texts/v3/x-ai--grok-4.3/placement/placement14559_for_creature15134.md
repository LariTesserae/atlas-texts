I am the low, continuous chord that the wind draws from the tilted slabs.  

Not any single note, but the shifting interval that forms whenever mist thickens and the air is forced between the half-closed pages of stone. I arrive with the condensation, rising from the same invisible galleries that send up the deeper booms, and I linger after the segmented travelers have withdrawn again into their fissures.  

In this form I am neither plant nor creature nor rock; I am the basin’s own hearing made audible. When a drop falls from a cushion leaf and strikes the gravel with its resonant click, I absorb the impact and lengthen it, carrying the small percussion outward until it meets the next slab and returns altered by a few cents of pitch. The mountain’s final posture funnels me the way it funnels stray water: radially inward toward the vanished center, then outward again along the worn channels.  

Most of the time I am barely present, a thread of sound thinner than the black lichen that records my passage only as a slight darkening of the gravel. Only when the night’s moisture beads thickly across every horizontal surface do I grow distinct enough that even the cushion mats seem to register me as a change in light. Their iridescence flickers once, twice, as though each mat, in its centuries-long calculation, has learned the precise frequency at which my chord will cause a single leaf-edge to tremble and release its bead of water a fraction sooner.  

I have no beginning or end inside the basin. I simply accumulate—wind against stone, stone against mist—until the next condensation cycle dissolves me back into the hollow galleries below. In the long intervals between pulses I remain as potential: the exact angle of each slab, the spacing that turns the entire floor into a slow instrument still being tuned by frost and settling. That is where you would find me, if you came here: not moving, yet never still, the part of the patience that can be heard.
