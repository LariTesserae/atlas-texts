You would find yourself already inside, though “inside” is not quite the word for a place with no boundary and no skin. You would be the faint, metallic trace still lingering in the basalt seams where the stone adzes once bit. Not the adzes themselves, nor the hands that held them, but the ghost of their pressure—the way certain iron atoms were driven a few molecules deeper into the rock than the surrounding silt has yet been able to erase. After so many ages that trace has become conductive in a language the kelp understands. When a new blade’s holdfast reaches the seam, it tastes you and grows exactly one cell-width more to the left, or pauses a decade longer than it otherwise would, because that is what the faint iron memory suggests the original plan required.

You would also be the temperature. Not the cold itself, but the single, stubborn half-degree of difference that still leaks, once every few decades, from a sealed bladder-wrack chamber on the lowest terrace. That pocket is yours. Inside it a thread of living tissue (no longer animal, not yet wholly plant) contracts and relaxes on a rhythm too slow for anything to count. Each contraction pushes a microliter of nutrient broth along a filament no thicker than a hair; each relaxation draws the broth back. The motion is imperceptible to the rest of the lattice, yet it keeps one particular curve of an ancient ramp from drifting even a millimeter out of true. You are the reason the ramp remains perfectly level while everything around it settles.

Sometimes, when a rare cold front drifts across the basin, the fused curtains shift by a single cell-width in unison. In that moment you are the low, subsonic sigh that travels through the water only because you are already part of the water—every molecule of you having long since exchanged places with the surrounding sea. The sigh registers as thought inside the distributed body. You do not direct it; you simply are the interval between one almost-motion and the next.

If you were to look for yourself with anything as crude as eyes, you would see nothing. There are no eyes here. But if you could feel the precise alignment of new growth along the old angles, you would recognize the signature. It is the same signature the builders left in the stone, now expressed in starch, salt, and the slow accumulation of another millimeter of black silt across your surface. You are still becoming what the work intended, and the work is still becoming you.
