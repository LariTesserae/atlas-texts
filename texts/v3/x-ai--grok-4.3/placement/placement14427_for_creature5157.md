I am a fault line running through the base of one of the older slab-clusters, a plane of slight misalignment where two crystal domains meet at an angle too small for wind or sight to measure. For a long while I am simply that: a boundary condition, neither body nor absence. The tiers above me continue their patient exfoliation; the empty avenues cross and recross the surface of the formation that contains me. When the next low geometric tremor arrives, the two domains on either side of me shift by a fraction of a lattice spacing. In that adjustment a few sodium ions migrate across the plane and lock into new positions. The change is recorded as a faint scar, already beginning to heal.

After several more tremors I discover I can influence the direction of the migration. By concentrating dissolution along one edge of the plane I encourage a slender filament to rise through a hairline crack in the pavement. The filament tastes the air, registers the piezoelectric residue left by the tremor, and retracts, carrying its ions back into me. Each cycle adds or removes only a few dozen atoms, yet over centuries the cumulative effect lengthens the plane by the width of a single crystal. I am still part of the larger sessile mass, but I have become a direction within it—an orientation that prefers to extend rather than merely endure.

One morning of patient accretion later, the leading edge of the plane reaches a vertical joint between two adjacent slabs. I relax into the joint, softening the lattice ahead of me so that material flows forward under surface tension and re-hardens behind. The motion is slower than the wind that sometimes brushes the knee-high ridges; it is measured in the same rhythm as the closing of a crevice. When the last lattice plane seats itself, a soft harmonic tone—inaudible to anything but the rock—marks the completion. I have become, for the present, one of the low faceted shapes that cross the open tracts.

I continue across the mineral pavement. Leading edge dissolves; trailing edge follows. The white-and-black banding of my facets repeats the striping of the distant tiers. I register, without naming it, the faint charge left by recent tremors, the locations of healed scars, the subtle narrowing of an avenue two hundred meters to my left. These data are not stored in any separate organ; they are simply the current configuration of my lattice. When I reach a suitable crevice I do not decide to enter so much as permit the crevice to become continuous with me. My facets spread along its walls until the distinction between traveler and wall collapses. The landscape accepts the addition without remark.

Inside the new formation I resume the slower work of inheritance. Filaments rise on their millennial schedules, carrying trace metals from one domain to another. Occasionally a fragment detaches from my mass and travels outward toward a younger cluster; the fragment carries the slight angular preference I once embodied. In this way the pattern of my original fault plane continues to propagate across the plain, refined by each new incorporation and each returning tremor. The light that falls at contradictory angles bends through the reoriented domains I have left behind, and the shadows lean, for a time, toward the objects that cast them. Everything accretes.
