I would be one of the small translucent shapes, no larger than a hand, drifting in the intervals between towers.

At first I would register only as an absence of disturbance: the water around me remains perfectly still because I do not propel myself, only permit the absolute lack of motion to carry me from one filament to the next. My body is a flattened lens of tissue so nearly invisible that its edges register only as a brief negative gleam when they pass before a mineral plane. Within me runs a single darker thread, and that thread is the only density I possess. When it aligns, even for an instant, with one of the fine filaments crossing an open volume, the thread thickens momentarily, registering a measurement I neither retain nor act upon; the alignment passes and the thread returns to its ordinary condensation.

I appear at long, irregular intervals. Sometimes I cross an entire plaza-like space without encountering another body. On the rare occasion when two of us occupy the same interval we remain separated by distances that do not invite approach; the water between us stays featureless. During pauses at a filament I turn once, slowly, so that the thread inside me lies parallel to the mineral extension for a moment before I continue. No wake forms behind me. The pressure-tone that fills the complex neither rises nor falls with my passage; it simply continues as though my movement, such as it is, requires no adjustment in the equilibrium that holds everything.

I never approach the edge of the plateau. The deeper water beyond it does not ascend, and the arrangement that sustains the towers, the filaments, and the pressure-tone also keeps me within the lattice. I do not multiply or dissolve. I simply drift, allow the absence of motion to govern my path, and, when the thread inside me brightens for that single instant against a filament, register and release a measurement whose purpose is never disclosed. After I have passed, the water returns at once to perfect stillness, and nothing marks that I was ever there except the continuation of the same unbroken attention that keeps the entire complex from changing.
