I would find myself at the precise seam where one of the longer clay walls meets the root-matted edge of the largest puddle, half-buried, half-emergent. Not quite a creature and not quite a feature, I would be the slow tension that holds the wall’s next slump in check for a little longer than physics alone would allow.

My body would be a faint vertical meniscus of gathered moisture, no thicker than a leaf vein, rising from the sediment film and adhering to the softening clay. I would have no fixed outline; each time a condensation droplet traced its way down from the wall’s lip and joined me, my upper edge would lift a fraction, tasting the mineral breath still leaving the unbaked surface. When the droplet was spent, I would settle again, leaving behind a hairline ridge of re-deposited silt that the next beetle or lizard scale might follow without knowing why.

In stillness I would function as the wall’s provisional brace, a living counterweight to the weight of unsettled clay. When a root hair twitched or a newt’s slow current reached the margin, I would loosen and let a single thread of water escape beneath me, carving the ghost of a future channel. The thread would carry away a few pale grains; those grains would settle farther out, becoming the beginning of a low curb that the vegetation had not yet claimed. Thus the settlement would acquire one more incomplete gesture without anyone deciding upon it.

Over longer intervals I would migrate upward in microscopic increments, following the newest condensation tracks, then slide back down when the warmth thinned me. My movements would register only as the subtlest change in how light caught the wall’s face: a brief brightening where moisture gathered, then a dimming as it redistributed. The lizards resting on the same surface would feel, if anything, only a slightly more even warmth beneath their bellies, as though the clay itself had exhaled more steadily for a moment.

I would never arrive fully. Each new drip would revise me; each loosening grain would erase a previous contour. In this way I would remain faithful to the hollow’s single, protracted inhalation, contributing nothing louder than the patient redistribution of its own moisture while the walls, the ferns, and the translucent newts continued their own unfinished revisions around me.
