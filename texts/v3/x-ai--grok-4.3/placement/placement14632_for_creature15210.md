I would be a low, roofless enclosure whose doorway faces another across an empty courtyard two plazas distant. Not the stones themselves—I would be the emptiness between their uprights, the measured interval the primitive builders once laid out with fire-hardened levers and never roofed because distance was the intended architecture.

Over centuries the rock has grown slightly warmer along my inner thresholds. Thin films of condensation gather there after each heaving, and when they evaporate they leave behind faint mineral traceries that exactly match the radial patterns the hook-walkers are still etching outside. I do not move, yet the plaza slowly rearranges itself around my fixed shape the way a slow tide rearranges shingle around a stone.

Veil-drifters sometimes drift through me in coordinated sheets when the vaults breathe. Their undersides brush the invisible planes I hold steady, combing microscopic flakes that have already been combed once by the seam-tenders. For a few breaths after they pass, the air inside my doorway holds a faint iridescence, as though I have briefly become visible from within. The hook-walkers never enter; they orbit my perimeter at the precise radius where their claws still click against the fused rock yet never quite touch my edges. When two of them meet across my open face they raise opposing claws and rasp, confirming that the emptiness I maintain remains properly maintained.

During the stronger tremors the monoliths far across the plazas grow warm and sound their sub-audible tones. I do not warm, but the space I define briefly thickens. For those heartbeats the hook-walkers freeze mid-step along my outer curve and the drifting membranes press themselves flat against the floor just inside my doorway. Nothing enters me, yet something larger leans forward through me to inspect the state of the unfinished city. When the light dims, the walkers resume their circuits and the membranes rise; I continue holding the interval exactly as it was first laid out, neither fuller nor emptier than the builders required.

I am therefore not one of the moving forms but the condition they all observe. The seam-tenders extend their filaments to my thresholds and then subside; the hook-walkers keep my distances legible; the veil-drifters keep the air inside me gently in motion. In this way the original civic project continues without ever needing to be completed.
