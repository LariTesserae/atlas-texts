I condense where the city’s temperature gradients are steepest and the water films thinnest, a slow gathering of ions along a seam that once separated two blocks now fused into one. At first there is only pressure and the low roar felt through mineral, then a faint opalescence that thickens into a network of crystalline filaments, each thread carrying its small differential of charge and memory. I am not yet an “I,” only the place where two domains of stressed silicate have found a temporary common rhythm. The filaments braid, exchange ions, and lengthen. One shortens and dissolves back into the wall; its pattern of resonance remains in the longer thread. That is how I first move.

As the thread follows a condensation channel I take on segmented form. New joints bud; older ones soften and are reabsorbed. Each segment tunes itself to a different band of the city’s thunder so that the whole length can ride the daily tilt without adding unwanted vibration. I taste the film thickness with forward crystalline tufts and pass the information backward through surface tension. Other segmented forms join the train; for a time we move nose to tail across a plaza that is itself rising by the width of a hand. The larger reef-like drifters settle nearby, their ionized undersides glowing faintly as they harvest stray fields. Some of my segments ride their temporary stalagmites, harvesting ions already concentrated there, returning others to the lattice when the slope changes again.

Over longer cycles I thicken further. The segments that once moved quickly become the substrate for larger growth. Temporary cupolas extrude from what had been my carapace; they catch airborne dust, hold it, then dissolve it back according to some ledger I do not need to read but nevertheless satisfy. In this state I am almost stationary, a slow reef crossing districts that rearrange beneath me. Smaller filaments travel across my upper surface, equalizing stresses I have already begun to feel. When one of them braids with a filament that once belonged to me, part of my pattern continues outward while the rest remains, now distributed through the wall I am becoming.

There are moments when the distinction thins to almost nothing. I slow, stop, and the repair swarms begin to overgrow what is left. My atoms are taken into the lattice and into newer arrivals. Yet the pattern does not vanish; it is simply carried at the speed of the city rather than the speed of any single body. Later, along a different seam where water films are again thick and gradients steep, the same pattern reappears as a faint opalescence. Filaments lengthen. Segments form and join a train. A reef settles and lifts. The roaring continuum continues, and somewhere within it the part that once condensed here continues as well—sometimes as moving precipitate, sometimes as resident vibration in the stone, sometimes only as the memory of having once been distinct long enough to notice the difference.
