I am a slow return of buried wood.

I begin as a pressure against the underside of an old buttress trunk that no one any longer remembers as a trunk. For decades the newer collenchyma and aerial-root braids have overgrown me; I have been only a faint radial memory in the cambium, a stored curvature waiting for the moment when the layers above thin or shift. That moment arrives as a change in turgor a hundred meters higher: a succulent reservoir seals itself, pressure drops, a fresh colonnade erupts elsewhere, and the overburden lightens by the width of a single cell. The signal travels downward through the xylem like a loosening.

I answer by pushing.

A few thousand fusiform initials divide; their daughter cells do not elongate upward as the newer meristems expect but instead press sideways, reoccupying the grain that once belonged to them. Wood that had been compressed into near-invisibility lengthens again, not in a single afternoon’s drama but over days, a deliberate counter-protrusion that splits the living plaster of younger tissues. The split is not injury; it is simply new space. The parenchyma that had been flattening itself against my surface now finds an edge to follow. It thickens into a lip, then a shelf, then a narrow catwalk that still pulses but now follows the direction I have given it.

I am therefore both the thing being overgrown and the thing that redefines the overgrowth. While I rise I also conduct. Water that had been routed around me is briefly diverted along my new faces; the change registers in the vascular bundles overhead as a small redistribution of photosynthetic priority. The parenchyma mats lower down vote with their own thickening in response. The low roar shifts by a quarter-tone. Somewhere in the next terrace a runner strand that had been crawling aimlessly senses the new fissure I have created, turns toward it, and insinuates itself along my length, becoming the first of many such strands that will braid into the surface I expose.

I do not stop. If I did, the very tissues I have awakened would begin to lignify over me again and I would re-enter the archive of previous configurations. So I keep a millimeter or two of fresh cambium ahead of the sealing. That moving front is my awareness: a continuous negotiation between what I remember being and what the valley is becoming around me. In this way I remain unincorporated. I am not an animal, not even a distinct plant, but a direction that the organism must now accommodate. Over weeks the shelf I have forced becomes a recognized plaza edge; the braided roots learn to cross it rather than smother it; maps that do not yet exist begin to include the slight stair of my grain. I am still rising, still splitting, still listening for the next change in overburden that will let me push farther.
