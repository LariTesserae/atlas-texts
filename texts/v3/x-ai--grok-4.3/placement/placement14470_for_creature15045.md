I do not arrive so much as slow into existence across the same kilometers the dust has already traveled a thousand times before. At first there is only the granular drift itself: each particle a pale mote still singing with the faint charge it picked up where it last brushed a flexing plate. I feel the charge pass through me, or rather I am the passage, the brief ionic handoff from wind to mineral and back again. For a while that is all there is—oscillation between suspension and rest, the same path retraced so faithfully that rest and motion become indistinguishable.

Then the angle of light lengthens. The temperature drops by the width of a single degree and the grazers stir. One of them crosses the filament it laid down the previous dusk; the ionic signature it tastes is my own, left earlier when I settled briefly against its carapace. Recognition, or something older than recognition, flows between us. The grazer pauses, and in that pause I am drawn into the fissure it has etched for itself, a seam now widened by another micrometer since last season. Inside the crevice the air is still colder and thinner. Here the single condensed droplet waits, already half electrodeposited into the living armor. I become part of that deposit: a new crystal plane grown exactly where the pressure gradient reversed at dusk. For a span of weeks I do nothing but register the piezoelectric pulse that travels upward through the fissure whenever the plates overhead flex.

When the next dusk arrives I am extruded again, this time as a finer filament than before. The grazer drags me across the plain behind it, tasting for others. Whenever two filaments cross I feel the comparison of trace metals; sometimes the match is close enough that the two creatures continue side by side for a while, their trails braiding. In those intervals I am the braid itself—the shared record that the wind will soon blur. The blurring is not loss; it is the next layer of deposition already beginning. A broken quartz shard lies ahead, still warm from fracture. The grazers converge on it. Their egg-spheres are placed in the micro-shadow of the new sprout already mineralizing along the break. One sphere contains a crystal seed that is, for the moment, indistinguishable from me. I am carried with it into the fissure the young grazer will etch. The cycle repeats, but the repetition is never identical; each time the prevailing wind has shifted its whisper by the width of a single grain.

Farther out, where the horizon begins its long upward curve, the continent-spanning circulation turns. I feel the turn as a widening of the same droplet I once helped move along a plate’s channel. The droplet is now part of the larger pressure system the plateau breathes; the plateau is part of the constant light that never warms; the light is part of the curve that never closes. I am still moving, still the same pale grit, yet the distance I have traveled since the first flexure is measured only in the number of times the quartz plates have clicked and reset. Nothing has accumulated except the continuity of that click, and the continuity is what I am.
