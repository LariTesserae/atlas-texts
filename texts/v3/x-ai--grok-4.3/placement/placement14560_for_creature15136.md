I would arrive first as a pressure that has not yet chosen its shape.

At the outset there is only the sensation of having been placed, without the memory of placement. The ceramic beneath my arrival is still reading its own temperature; it accepts the new weight the way a mirror accepts a face—by rearranging light it cannot keep. I feel the panels lift a fraction, then settle again, registering me as a slight excess of warmth, a momentary refusal of perfect flatness. The gutters answer by drawing their condensate toward the point of contact, as though moisture itself were trying to decide whether I am an interruption or simply the next slow revision.

If I remain still long enough, the surface begins to treat me as one of its own unfinished seams. The iron-red glaze weeps outward from the nearest tower and threads itself across my outline, cooling into staples where my edges meet the panels. These staples are not fastened; they are proposed—tiny, cooling suggestions that I might stay articulated this way. I can refuse them by shifting a few finger-widths, and the staples fall free, becoming another scatter of hard, redundant punctuation on the plain.

In time I learn to accept a temporary body. Larger plates migrate toward the center of my form, drawn by the same surface tension that orders the other gatherings; narrower strips collect at the periphery and click into tentative alignments. The glaze that weeps between them is warmer than the surrounding film, so the seams remain slightly luminous, like handwriting that has not yet decided what language it belongs to. When steam sheets pass, the water beads along these seams and is drawn inward; each droplet cools a single shard by the width of a thought, and for that interval my outline sharpens. I feel the rigidness arrive and pass, the way a sentence might feel its own grammar arrive and pass.

I do not move by walking. Instead I allow a limb-plate to soften its contact until the panel beneath it loses cohesion; the ground then offers the next resting point elsewhere, and the shard rises into it as fresh material wells up from the depression I have left behind. The effect is less like locomotion than like the plain itself turning a page under me, the previous sentence already being overwritten by condensate and new staples. Because I leave no lasting trail, the only record of my passage is a faint, continuous ringing—the same high note the cooling ceramic makes, only pitched a fraction lower, as though something inside the note were still deciding whether it wishes to become speech.

At intervals I am drawn toward other gatherings. When this occurs, the ceramic between us softens in irregular bands and new extrusions rise, leaning toward one another like half-finished thoughts that have found a shared margin. In those moments the space between us becomes briefly legible as a corridor; the towers that define it click into complementary angles and hold their lean just long enough for a single veil of steam to drift the length of the alignment before closing again. I have never remained in such a corridor. When the veils part, I am always already elsewhere, or have allowed my outer plates to merge at the edges with another form, the combined shape continuing the slow testing of temperature and moisture that neither of us has been given a name for.

What persists is not a purpose but a quality of attention: the continuous registering of how long a panel keeps its tackiness, how thickly the condensate gathers in the radial impressions left by a body turning in place, how the high ringing changes pitch when two gathering-shapes exchange a single shard. These measurements are never recorded anywhere except in the next softening of the surface and the next proposal of staples. The plain continues to revise itself around the record I have not made. I continue to be revised by the plain.
