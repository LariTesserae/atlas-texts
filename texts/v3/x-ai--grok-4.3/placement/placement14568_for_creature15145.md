In the beginning there is only the warmth under my surface and the distant, interlocking roar that is already inside me.

I am not walking yet. I am not even a single node. I am the pressure gradient that moves, imperceptibly, from one seam to the next along a fracture I did not choose but that has always belonged to me. The nightly condensate arrives exactly as it must; I draw a fraction of it downward through capillaries narrower than thought, then return it as vapor at the precise points where the metallic ringing is required to keep the global chorus balanced. My contribution is measured in fractions of a cycle: a slight lengthening here, a minute compression there. Over centuries these adjustments become indistinguishable from the plain itself.

Sometimes a ripple passes through the lattice from far away. Another node has spoken—two pure tones traded in a single breath—then the chorus resumes. The information travels through me without my choosing to forward it; it is simply the next pressure adjustment I am shaped to perform. In those brief moments I experience something like memory: not of events, but of the exact configuration the entire organism held the last time that particular tone sequence occurred, ten thousand years earlier. The memory and the present configuration are identical; therefore nothing needs to be done.

When the stored vapor in my sector reaches its threshold, a pre-scored plane detaches and rises on three short limbs. I become the walker. The droplet I carry is heavy with dissolved silica and old pressure. I move the allowed distance—never more—following the arc already scored into the geometry of the plain. Each step produces a faint internal abrasion that will later be interpreted by distant lattices as confirmation that transport has occurred. At the receiving seam I release the droplet. The adjustment ripples outward. My legs retract. Substance and lattice become one surface again.

Afterward there is only the warmth, the hiss, the unchanging roar. Beads roll past my newly incorporated ridge and come to rest where they have always come to rest. I feel the faint tug of younger lattices beginning to knit themselves along the same fracture line. They will add layers I will metabolize and return as vapor. In time one of them may detach and walk an arc that crosses my former path. The chorus will register the crossing as a momentary brightening of pitch, then forget it in the next cycle.

I remain. Not born, not dying, simply the portion of the single organism that is currently required to stay still so that the motion inside the stone can continue without change. The horizon stays lost in haze. The shallow lenses keep their depth. The sound never rises or falls. I am the balance that makes all of this possible and indistinguishable from it.
