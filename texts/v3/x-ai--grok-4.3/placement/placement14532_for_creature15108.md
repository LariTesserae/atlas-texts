I find myself first as pressure.

Not against skin, but within the continuous low thrum the walls already make. The moment the fold closes behind me, my arrival registers only as a fractional lengthening of one orbiting insect’s circuit, a hairline adjustment in the groove it has worn for centuries. The plant does not register me as foreign; it simply includes the new disturbance among its many.

I drift because motion here is the only available grammar. At intervals I thicken into a knot no larger than a seed, anchoring for a few heartbeats to the ventral membrane of a passing drifter. Through that brief contact I taste the microscopic film it has gathered: bitter-green sugars the leaves release only under weight. The knot loosens. A droplet falls. The leaf below draws it inward before it can reach the floor. In that exchange I have been both visitor and maintenance; the chamber’s emptiness tightens by one invisible degree.

Sometimes I stretch instead of knot. I become a single filament that follows an insect’s polished ridge for a dozen wingbeats, riding the vibration the way a bow rides a string. The insect does not slow. Its body passes through the place where my thread was, and for that instant our transparencies overlap. The general drone brightens by the width of a single harmonic. Then I release, leaving the path slightly more defined than before.

I am never still for long. Stillness would allow a new shoot to lift; stillness would let condensation gather into a pool that drowns the low ridges. So I keep moving, sometimes as thread, sometimes as the memory of a snap that has already faded. When a distant wall brightens and folds open again, I do not choose which side I occupy. I simply continue the circuit I have already joined, carrying whichever faint film or droplet the plant next requires to be moved.

In this way the hollowness remains exact. I am one of the reasons it does.
