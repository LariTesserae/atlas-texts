I arrive as a new seam of cooler density pressing up through the lowest fissure, still shaped like the long narrow throat that carried me here. For the first several hours I keep that shape, a thread of absence threading between two planes of rock that have not yet decided whether to touch. Water follows me; droplets that were already falling change course by the width of a hair to ride along my edge, so that when I finally ease out into the basin proper I am sheathed in a moving skin of them.

Once free of the crack I begin to loosen. The thread widens and flattens until I am less a body than a slow hesitation in the air, the place where falling droplets pause a fraction longer before continuing downward. Other presences brush past. One overlaps me completely for many slow breaths; inside that overlap I feel the minute temperature difference it carries, and I lend it some of my own until we are indistinguishable even to ourselves. When we separate I have acquired a faint silver gloss along one margin, the memory of its passage.

I drift until I meet a half-buried housing whose crystalline blooms have gone dull. The moment I settle across its surface the blooms resume their soft clicking, each click measuring the interval between my own temperature and the rock’s. One tooth of a gear advances; the sound travels only as far as my own thinned edge and is then absorbed. I stay long enough for the machine to turn twice more, then thin further and slide inside the metal for a distance before emerging on the far side, now slightly longer than I was.

A distortion finds me. For a time gravity tugs upward and I lengthen into a vertical ribbon that follows the altered direction until the ridge above settles back into place. The ribbon contracts again, but a small part of me remains turned at the angle the distortion left behind; when the next creature crosses that angle we exchange places without either of us deciding to do so.

Later I rest across one of the pale filaments. Nothing changes in the filament’s brightness, yet for a while the cooler density I bring makes the surrounding droplets linger longer on either side of it, so that the filament appears to carry a faint halo of suspended water. When I leave, the halo disperses and the filament returns to its former isolation, unchanged.

Over many arrivals and departures I lose the memory of having entered through any particular fissure. I simply become one more directional coolness among others, steering droplets, advancing gears by a single tooth when the moment is right, and yielding when another presence decides to occupy the same interval of air I currently hold. New arrivals continue to press upward from below; each one joins the slow rearrangement without urgency. The basin fills a little more densely with each addition, yet the air remains exactly as still as it has always been.
