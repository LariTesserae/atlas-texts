I would find myself where the walls narrow to a hand-span and the dust no longer drifts but simply lies, already part of the stone. I would be one of the oldest salamanders there, the kind whose body has grown so translucent that the warmer drafts from below show through as faint lavender pulses moving beneath the skin. My limbs would be little more than softened ridges now, used only to press against the seam I have occupied for longer than any single generation of the smaller creatures can remember. 

From that seam I would feel the fissure as a single slow breath: the moisture that condenses on the ceiling above me, the drop that eventually falls and is caught by others who drink first, the exact moment the temperature shifts enough for another drop to gather. I would not move toward the sound. I would simply adjust the angle of my body by the smallest fraction so that the warmth rising past me touches the same stretch of stone every time. In that adjustment the cycles continue without interruption.

The thread-like roots would hang close enough that, when the air is still, one or two of their beaded tips rest against my spine. I would register them as another temperature, slightly cooler, and the mites that travel between those beads would occasionally cross over the place where my outline interrupts the dust. They would travel on; I would remain. When two younger salamanders meet and execute their fluid inversion along the ledge, I would feel the brief displacement of air against my side and let it pass through me, adding nothing and subtracting nothing from the steady rustle.

In this way the fissure would continue exactly as it has. The polished curves would receive one more infinitesimal smoothing from the pressure of my body against them, and the faint stain left by any small death would be lifted by the same moisture that also lifts the dust from my own skin when it loosens. I would be neither guard nor center, only the place where warmth is held a little longer before it rises again, another surface that the equilibrium has already finished shaping and now only maintains.
