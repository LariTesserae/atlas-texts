I would be a slow coalescence along one of the half-buried cords where two filament clusters have fused. Not yet a separate creature, more a thickening of the cord’s own wall—a gradual inward pleating that has begun to divide the single channel into two narrower ones. Warm fluid still passes through me, but now it is slowed and warmed further by the new folds, so that when it finally re-enters the lattice it carries a slightly different scent of sugars, one degree more polymerized. The membrane above me has already begun to register this change as a faint, persistent heat, and the next thread of surface moisture is already bending a fraction toward it.

From inside the cord I sense the larger plane only as pressure gradients and the periodic opening of distant valves. When a pool drains, the sudden surge tugs the cord sideways; the tug travels through me as a brief straightening of every new fold, followed by a slower elastic return. That return is becoming my first rhythm. I do not yet know it as time, only as the difference between stretched and slack.

Occasionally a drifting sac brushes the cord. Its fluid wall presses against mine for a moment, and through that contact a few of its clouded sugars cross into me. They do not feed me so much as they alter the local tension of the membrane, making the next pulse along the cord arrive with a slightly sharper ripple. I register this as a small, involuntary tightening—one more pleat added, one more subdivision begun.

Above us the embryonic ridge continues its uncertain rise. I am not beneath it, exactly; I am part of the material that will, or will not, become its inner face. If the ridge ever hardens into a wall, the cord I inhabit will lie along its base like a buried nerve. If the ridge instead softens and is reabsorbed, the cord will simply continue as a surface filament, feeding outward instead of inward. Either outcome still feels, for now, like the same gentle insistence: a continued drawing-tight that has no name yet except the sensation of becoming narrower and therefore more itself.
