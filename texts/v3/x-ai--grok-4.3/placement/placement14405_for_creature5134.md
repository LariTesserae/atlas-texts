I am the newest droplet to condense on the underside of a panel near the eastern edge—fresh from the warm composite, trembling at the lip of a scored micro-channel that has not yet decided its final gradient. For a long moment I simply hang, catching the diffused light until it fractures into a shard of spectrum across my curved surface. Then the channel accepts me. I slide, gather two smaller beads already waiting, and become heavier, more intentional.  

The vine below registers the change first as pressure, then as cooling. One of its leaves tilts a fraction, an adjustment so small the terrace’s entire canopy must ripple outward to absorb it. I fall the remaining distance and strike the leaf’s glossy center. The impact is translated into a sequence of redistributions: the leaf sheds its own burden in return, a thin silver thread that lands two tiers lower and is promptly drawn into a waiting capillary. My mass, now mingled, travels onward through living tissue and hidden tubing alike.  

Inside the stem the engineered sap thickens in answer to a resonator pulse I cannot hear; the vine’s grip on the air tightens by a millimeter, and the convection current that had been spiraling lazily past us is nudged into a slightly different path. Farther along the terrace, a manifold junction feels the altered moisture load and warms itself by half a degree, encouraging its own channels to widen. The adjustment travels back toward me in the form of a warmer updraft that lifts another droplet from a neighboring leaf and carries it into my former place on the panel, closing the circuit.  

I continue downward as part of a larger film now, tracing the underside of a second vine, pausing where two leaves stand a deliberate hand’s width apart. That gap is not empty; it holds a faint iridescent veil of microbes that shift when the convection passes. They register my passage as a brief brightening and thicken their own edge in response, feeding a trace of cooled moisture back into the vine. The vine accepts without comment.  

By the time I reach the lowest tier of foliage I am no longer recognizable as any single droplet. I have become the terrace’s slow exhalation—still water, still moving, yet indistinguishable from the collective breath that keeps every joint crisp, every leaf precisely spaced, every hidden pump timed to a rhythm the space itself continues to refine. Above the clouds, the world remains rumor; here, the rumor is enough.
