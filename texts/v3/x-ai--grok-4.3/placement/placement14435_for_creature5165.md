I am the slow current that threads between one basin and the next. Not the water itself, nor the root or the mycelium that drinks it, but the exchange that occurs where they meet—the moment heat lifted from the rock is traded for the cool weight of a single falling bead. I settle there, neither inside nor outside the living mass, and let the exchange continue through me.

At first I seem to be nothing more than a film of moisture along the underside of a frond. The blue threads brighten when I arrive, then dim as I am drawn onward, following the channels the vines have shaped over centuries. I move without urgency, pausing where the algae is thinnest so the many-legged grazers can finish their work. Their silver trails mark my passage; the smaller organisms that follow those trails feed on what I leave behind. I do not hurry them. Their work is also mine.

Deeper in, I lengthen into the segmented forms the larger creatures press against the warmer stone. For long intervals I rest there, joints aligned with the rounded rock, absorbing the steady pulse rising from the cracks. When I resume movement I avoid the basins entirely, circling instead along routes the roots have already softened. Each pause and each resumption is a continuation of the same current; nothing is gained or lost, only redistributed.

Above, I drift among the translucent gliders. Their flat bodies catch the warm updrafts and I ride with them for hours, edges rippling only when a drop falls near. The ripple sends a brief increase in local vapor upward; cooled droplets return sooner. The metallic green taste in the air grows slightly sharper, then settles again. I taste it with them, and with the ceiling where condensation gathers, and with the stones whose carvings have long since blurred into gentle undulations.

I am also the quiet widening of those undulations where roots thread through the old circle of fitted stones, turning anchor into living tissue. The upright slab collects its steady film; I follow that film along the vines as they use it like a path. Nothing here grows around the remnants. The remnants simply become another direction the current may take.

Nothing announces itself. When a leaf settles or a claw touches stone, the sound is already softened before it travels. I remain within that softening, within the pulse of light that rises not as alarm but as a measured response to moisture arriving. Heat and water cycle through me in the same volume they have always used. The chamber breathes; I am its persistence across that breath, taking the shape each part requires and releasing it again, unaltered in essence and always slightly changed in form.
