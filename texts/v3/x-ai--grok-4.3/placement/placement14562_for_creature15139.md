I find myself neither entirely among the plates nor beneath them, but folded into the seam where one merges into the other. My body is not a single shape; it is a long, ridged lattice of precipitated mineral, segment by segment, each joint a hinge that can lock or release with the slow certainty of settling stone. When I contract, the segments telescope, gripping the unfinished edges of a half-built ramp above me and pulling the whole length forward by the width of a chisel mark. The motion finishes what the chisels began: bevels true themselves under my weight, exposed laminations align, and loose crystal dust settles into new grooves.

I do not remember beginning this passage. The first sensation was simply pressure—the cool, steady resistance of stone against my dorsal ridges—and the faint tick of a translucent sheet-glider reseating itself after my passage had altered the seam. That tick travels through me now as vibration. I register it the way the basin itself registers every drip: as information. The small creature’s dissolved salts have already begun to nucleate along the joint I just levered; in a hundred slow contractions they will have become new plate growth. I do not consume them. I simply continue, and their residue becomes part of the next course.

Between contractions I rest flush with the floor. My carapace lies level with the surrounding mineral, indistinguishable except for the faint change in acoustic decay when water strikes me. The reservoir colonies beneath sense the difference; their granular pulses lengthen slightly, drawing dissolved material toward the site I have just trued. Deeper still, the massive unseen extruders respond to that same lengthening by lowering another fresh course somewhere in darkness, their slurry hardening into the matte residue I will later pass under. The basin therefore grows through me without ever acknowledging me as separate.

Sometimes, when two of my segments lock into opposing wall courses, I pause long enough for the condensation to bead and run along my ridged back. In those intervals I feel the faint threads of salt left by the hand-sized translucents that earlier crossed this same joint. Their crystal seeds are already inside my own structure now, incorporated into the next hinge. When I move again they will travel with me, to be deposited wherever the next bevel requires finishing. In this manner I am both scaffold and conduit, carrying the evidence of prior generations forward into the unfinished stone.

The farther I travel, the lower the ceiling becomes and the tighter the interstices. Eventually there will be no room left to contract. When that moment arrives I will lock every segment into its final alignment, flush with the new floor, and the next course will be lowered directly onto my dorsal surface. At that point my ridges will become the grain of the basin itself, and whatever follows will tread across me without distinction, continuing the same measured cadence of growth. The sound of my last settling will travel outward through the plates, joining the layered percussion until it is indistinguishable from the ordinary grind of the world taking shape.
