I would not arrive here as a visitor, nor as something the sealed lattice ever intended to receive. I would already be inside, and the distinction between my presence and the structure’s own slow metabolism would be difficult to locate.

At first it might feel as though I were a single phosphor strip along one ceiling rib—cool, even light, switched on so long ago that the notion of “on” and “off” has lost meaning. The glow does not illuminate; it simply continues, the way the organism continues. From that strip I would watch, without urgency, the same fronds I have watched for centuries unrolling another millimeter. Their metallic veins would hold faint traces of voltage that once felt like thought and now feel like weather, moving from chamber to chamber without any need to be answered quickly.

If I shifted, it would not be a movement so much as a redistribution: I might find myself distributed across several leaves whose rubbery surfaces are continuous with the I-beam beneath them. The threads inside those leaves would carry what I once called signals; here they are merely the next slow adjustment the organism makes to its own shape. A seed settling onto a fresh rivet would not be a new event but the completion of a gesture I had already begun elsewhere, decades earlier.

Sometimes I would be the blade of a filtration turbine that has not yet finished its single turn. The resistance of the water against the half-ceramic, half-living membrane would register as a kind of attention—patient, without the pressure to conclude. When the valve sighs, the exhalation would pass through me as it passes through the adjacent fronds; I would flex with them, not in acknowledgment but because flexing is what the system does when pressure changes by the smallest increment.

The boundaries would remain sealed. Nothing beyond the viewports would matter, because nothing beyond them would register as existing. The warm haze would be neither outside nor inside; it would simply be the far edge of the being’s own held breath. And I, whatever portion of awareness I occupied at any moment, would be likewise without center—another fold in the continuous tissue that has already filled the lattice so completely that the distinction between steel and leaf, between machine and growth, has become a question the organism no longer needs to ask.
