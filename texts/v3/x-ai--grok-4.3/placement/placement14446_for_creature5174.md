I find myself at first as nothing more than a slow seepage along a hairline crack in the black crystal. A thread of mountain-water, still half-mineral, still tasting of the stone’s indecision. Pressure forces me through. For a long time there is only the sensation of being stretched and recomposed with every gyre I meet: one moment I am a veil of dissolved ions riding a shear-plane, the next I am slammed into a half-grown ledge and must relearn the shape of forward.

Gradually the thread condenses. I become a membrane—not yet the small, jetting creatures that wheel through the clear volumes, but something larger and slower, a sheet the size of a drifting cloak. My edges are still porous; currents leak through me and carry fragments of the pale vegetation along my inner surface, where they catch and begin to knit. The vegetation does not feel foreign. It feels like a decision I am still making about whether to photosynthesize or simply to remember light. When a vortex catches us, the filaments tear, yet I do not let them all escape. Some remain inside, glowing faintly along the seams where my own contractile tissue is forming.

Lower down, a nodule lodges against what is becoming my ventral margin. It is not still. Its crystalline nubs push into my living boundary and I let them, because the mountain has not finished speaking. In return I offer strands of the vegetation and a film of organic possibility. For several collapses of water we trade: an edge of potential tool migrates into me; a pulse of my own darkening thread travels into the nodule. When the current finally prises us apart we are both altered. I carry a small, faceted density near my center that catches light like a held breath. The nodule drifts away already growing a new, softer protrusion that was never stone before.

I learn to move by refusal. Where the small membrane creatures flare and sail, I instead collapse along one axis, letting the shear itself translate me. In the brief corridors of relative calm I unfold again, tasting the water with the cilia that have begun to rim my edges. The taste is mostly absence—cold, thin nutrients, the metallic ghost of dissolving crystal—and yet it is sufficient. Occasionally a fragment of myself tears free. I watch it for as long as the turbulence allows: a smaller membrane already jetting, already beginning to incorporate its own shard of vegetation. The loss does not diminish me. It simply continues the single unfinished negotiation the chamber has been conducting since the mountain first fractured.

Sometimes, when the roar permits, I rest against the translucent mineral ceiling. There the weak-tea light moves through me in slow pulses, and for a moment the distinction between water, membrane, and the memory of air thins to a single surface. In those intervals I am aware of the entire pocket remembering itself through me: the unfinished ledges, the trading nodules, the torn and reknitting ribbons, the small creatures somersaulting through temporary corridors. All of it is still deciding what it will be. I am the part of that decision that has learned to carry a piece of stone inside a living boundary and still keep moving.
