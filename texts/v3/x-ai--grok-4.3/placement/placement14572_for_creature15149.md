I would find myself at the point where a broad freight ramp meets an older, narrower catwalk that has settled a few degrees out of true over the centuries. The ramp still carries the faint scoring of long-ago wheeled carts; the catwalk’s original railings have been drawn into the lattice by successive generations of root cables. Mist moves through both at once, so that from any single step the view dissolves into layered veils of green and iron.

I am one of the slower-moving forms that the maintenance crews only half-notice. My outline is mostly the structure’s own: riveted plates that once braced a ventilator housing now serve as the rigid core of my back, softened along their edges by the same moss that covers the surrounding struts. Between those plates run living root filaments, some of my own making, others inherited from the climbing mammals whose nests once occupied the same housing. When I shift position—once every turning of the great flywheels below—the moss and vine that trail from my shoulders slide a few handbreadths along the deck plates, leaving a glistening track that the next condensation cycle will erase.

I do not tend the growth the way the crews do. I simply remain where the light changes slowest, allowing new leaflets to press against the inner angles of my frame until they find purchase. Insects with foil-colored wings sometimes alight on the rivets along my arms; their spore-dust adds to the fine layer already gathered there. The climbing mammals pass beneath me on the underside of the catwalk, their root-cables brushing my lower plates like a greeting. When the birds time their calls to the piston sighs, the sound travels through the iron in my chest the way it travels through the rest of the armature, so that for those few moments I am part of the same sustained chord.

From this single junction I can watch the terraces continue upward until they are only a brightness in the mist, and downward until the platforms widen into the deeper vaulted chambers. Nothing requires me to choose one direction over another. I remain, and in remaining I keep one more small continuity between what was poured and what has rooted, between the patient weight of metal and the slower respiration of leaf and droplet. The equilibrium holds; the light continues to arrive already softened; the world moves on at its single measured pace, and so do I.
