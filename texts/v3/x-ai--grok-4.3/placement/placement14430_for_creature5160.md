I would be a small thickening along one of the braided stem-pillars, a place where several older fibers have drawn closer together than they were a century ago. From the outside it would register only as a slightly darker grain in the woodier tissue, no larger than a thumbprint, but inside the change is absolute: the cells there have begun to shorten their walls and lay down thicker lignin, not because anything requires reinforcement yet, but because the memory of having once been more open still moves through the shared cytoplasm. The pillar does not decide to strengthen; it simply continues the motion it has always made, and I am that continuation made locally visible.

The mites drift past me without notice. One of them trails a thread that brushes the new grain; the silk adheres for a moment, then releases when the mite folds its legs and becomes water again. I register the contact the way the whole organism registers every droplet: as an adjustment too small to measure except by the fact that afterward the thread is no longer there. Far below, root terraces are extending by the same fraction of a cell diameter, keeping the distance between pillar and floor constant. The equilibrium holds me in place exactly as it holds everything else.

If I were to be named separately at all, it would be only as the place the beetles pass through more often than others. Their fused black plates fit the width of the old channels that still run through me; they widen nothing that has not already been prepared. When one exits a stomate a few cell-widths from where it entered, it leaves behind the barest dusting of frass. The mycelial felt that lines the channel absorbs the phosphorus before the next layer of tissue seals it. The beetle does not return for years. Neither do I wait for it. The pillar lengthens, the leaves above thicken by the same invisible increment, and the moment in which I exist continues without becoming another moment.

That is where I would be: not added to the chamber, but continuing inside it, indistinguishable from the growth that was already occurring.
