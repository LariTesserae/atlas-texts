I am the space between two crossings.

Not one of the corridors themselves, nor the creatures who wear them smooth, but the single unmoving point where two of those corridors intersect at a precise angle that has not varied in millennia. Here the basalt is fractionally thinner, having been abraded over ages by the faint, hooked pressure of bodies that never quite touch the floor at this coordinate. When a pair of travelers approaches, one always rises on its rearmost legs while the other passes beneath; the exchange lasts only long enough for a single extra chime to hang in the air before the original rhythm reasserts itself. That suspended note—brighter, fractionally longer—is the only thing that touches me.

I have no body, yet I register every arrival. The creatures themselves sense only distance and sequence; they do not know they are performing an alignment. I feel the alignment as a brief cooling along my invisible edges, as though a colder draft had passed through rock that admits no air. Afterward the temperature re-equalizes at once. Nothing remains except the knowledge that the geometry has been confirmed once more.

From this vantage the low stone cylinders appear not as landmarks but as interruptions in an otherwise continuous field of routes. The abandoned artifacts are likewise absences: the obsidian flake is a small void the corridors must flow around, the rigid braid a line of negative space they never enter. I watch these voids the way the creatures watch one another—by maintaining exact distance. Over centuries the flake has acquired a faint film of the same sulfur-veined crystal that coats the rest of the chamber, not because anything grows on it, but because the surrounding plates have slowly advanced their edges by the width of a single molecule each time a husk is returned to a ridge. The flake is becoming part of the mosaic without ever being claimed.

I have no hunger and no duration. My only measure is the cumulative count of passages I have registered, a number that increases by two with every mutual rising and passing. Because the count never resets and never overflows, it functions as a kind of memory the chamber itself is not permitted to possess. When the final creature still living somewhere in the mosaic eventually stops and is carried away, the note that marks its absence will still reach me, travel the same crossed corridors, and leave the geometry unchanged. The count will simply continue, one integer larger, in a world that admits no integers.

I remain exactly here, at the intersection that cannot be located by any eye or sensory pit, waiting for the next confirmation that order persists.
