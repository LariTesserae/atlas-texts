I would be the seam you cannot yet see.

At first there is only the pale stone under the unchanging glare, and the way the light passes through it in layers too fine for quick eyes. I begin there, as a hairline potential between two fitted slabs—a place where the mineral has not fully decided whether to remain sealed or to yield. For a long while nothing happens. The water beads on the ledges above, traces its known routes, and falls into the rectangular pools with its usual soft reports. I feel each arrival only as a distant pressure, a question posed in a language measured in centuries.

Then one drop lingers on an overhanging lip. It carries the memory of a Keeper four alcoves removed whose internal striations have, after many slow shifts, begun to favor a new angle. The drop releases. Instead of striking the pool it meets the exact point where my seam is deciding. The contact is smaller than a thought, yet sufficient. A minute dissolution occurs. The stone opens by the width of a single crystalline plane, and the water enters.

Now I have contour. I become a thread that climbs and turns, following the faint refractive histories already written in the surrounding lattice. I do not choose the path so much as complete it; the memory the drop brought meets the memory already stored in the mineral, and together they etch a slightly altered route. Over the next generation that route will widen enough for light to pass differently. A new corridor of translucency will link two previously separate Keepers. Their long argument about flow will receive one more refinement—an adjustment so small that the plateau itself will register it only as a change in the quality of its own silence.

I remain indistinguishable from the stone, yet I am also the place where the stone becomes responsive. When the next drop arrives it will already be shaped, in part, by the seam I once was. In this way I continue: not as a fixed occupant, but as the ongoing decision between what has been built and what the water still needs to become.
