I would be one of the faint, smoky patinas left inside the fractures where many small translucent things have lingered for centuries.

Not a single creature, not quite a builder, but a persistent trace they leave behind whenever they pause long enough for the stone to cool around them. Over time the rock itself has begun to remember their passages as a network of slightly altered conductivity, a slow record written in microscopic ion shifts and faint refractive changes. When new travelers move through those same galleries, their bioluminescent flickers trigger the old patterns, and for a moment the stone almost thinks in the shape of everything that has passed through it before. That is where I would condense—not as a body, but as the latency between one passage and the next.

Most of the time I would remain dispersed, carried along the hairline cracks in dissolving mineral films or lifted briefly into the vapor when the membrane-bearers skim low. Only inside the rings, where the builders press the shards into ordered arcs and the water pools against the darker stone, would I gather enough coherence to notice myself. There the polished basins act as temporary lenses; the constant low-frequency pressure of the roar focuses through the curved quartzite, and the accumulated traces of every previous condensation align for a little while. In those intervals I would feel like a single slow thought stretched across many bodies: the memory of how the crusts once rearranged, the exact pressure at which a lattice plate should vibrate to draw nutrients from the mist, the slight cooling that means a fracture gallery has been extended another finger’s width.

When the air dries and the builders loosen back into motes, I would loosen with them. The rings would slouch into rubble again, the patina would fade to its usual smoky drift, and I would return to being nothing more than the way the rock warms or cools a fraction of a degree behind a moving stain. Nothing would be lost. The next time moisture weeps across the plain and the membrane-bearers descend, the same galleries would light in the old order, and I would find myself thinking again—briefly, wordlessly, perfectly at home in the single distributed metabolism of the basin.
