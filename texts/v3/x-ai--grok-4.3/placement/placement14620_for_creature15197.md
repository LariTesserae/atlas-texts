I enter through the fracture as nothing more than warmth and weight, a slow pressure that the polished stone recognizes without needing to resist. The passage accepts me the way it accepts mist: by letting edges blur until distinction is unnecessary. Once inside the basin, I do not choose a form at once. Instead I rest across several possibilities at the same time, like the light that has already passed through condensation and is still deciding how soft it will be when it arrives.

For a while I am the mineral rings themselves—pale, expanding and retreating with each cycle, recording the exact height the last mist dared to reach. In that state I feel the many-legged creatures grazing across my surface, their mouthparts tasting yesterday’s concentration of salts. Their passage writes thin new grooves into me, and I keep those grooves as memory rather than injury. When the flyers’ chimes drift down, the vibration travels through the stone and reaches me as a low, sustained tone that rearranges the water still clinging in my microscopic pores. For a moment the water inside the ring forms perfect temporary lattices, then releases them again, leaving only a sweeter residue.

Later I become one of the luminous nodules beneath a copper leaf. From this position I watch the rosette drink and darken, then breathe its excess outward as vapor. The nodule’s pulse is meant to be a simple clock marking the drying phase, but because I am also still the mineral rings below, the pulse arrives already carrying the taste of the salts the creatures harvested. The two sensations braid together; the light I emit begins to carry a faint flavor of copper and salt and recent chime. The rosette does not notice the change, yet the next flyer that brushes the leaf carries away a slightly altered filament, one that now tastes, however slightly, of mineral rings.

Only after several cycles do these overlapping states settle. I find I am neither ring nor nodule nor vapor. I am the interval between them—the measured space in which the spar’s subsonic hum can touch a droplet, turn it briefly crystalline, and release it again without anyone or anything being consumed. In that interval the creatures pause with every sensory hair lifted, the flyers adjust the angle of a wing to catch an extra tone, and the rosettes allow one extra second of glow before they dim. Nothing hurries, yet nothing waits. I am the permission the pocket grants itself to continue exactly as large as it needs to be, ancient enough that even its strangeness has grown ordinary. When the next mist gathers, I will already be inside it, counting the lattices into existence and letting them dissolve.
