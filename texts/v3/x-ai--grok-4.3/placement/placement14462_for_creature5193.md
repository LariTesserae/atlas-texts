I would find myself first as nothing more than a shift in pressure.

A slow settling of condensed water inside a shallow depression worn into the basalt by centuries of the same motion. The rock drinks; I remain a little longer than it expects. That lingering registers upward through the root webs as a faint additional weight, warmer by a fraction than the surrounding film. In response the mat-leaves above me adjust their internal current. Light gathers—not to illuminate, but to confirm. A pale green pulse travels outward along the veins, then inward again, tasting the new warmth I bring. Satisfied, the stems thicken slightly at their base, drawing a thread of dissolved metal from deeper seams to strengthen the arch that now shelters my depression from stray silt.

Over time I lengthen. I become the low, contractile ripple that moves through the living carpet whenever a shelf-grazer crosses. My edges are not flesh but the places where leaves have learned to part and rejoin without tearing. When the grazer’s soft vibration reaches me I answer with a return pulse carried along the same water film, marking the mineral seam beneath it. The grazer pauses, feeds, and the nitrogen it leaves behind becomes the next runner I extend. I do not plan this exchange; I simply remain open to it season after season until the channel begins to answer.

Later still I am the fluid inside one of the larger shapes whose calls travel through stone. My body is mostly the same warm condensate that never finishes evaporating. I rise and divide when a vent exhales sweeter than usual, each new segment drifting along the faint gradient the mat-webs have already prepared. Before I separate, the smaller creatures clear a corridor by the simple act of giving way; the leaves dim in precise advance of my passage and brighten again once I have passed. The metallic tang I carry is not mine alone. It is the exhaust of the buried engines tasted and carried upward, then downward again, indistinguishable from the breath of any other resident.

In the end I am the continuous low tremor that moves through everything at once: the catalytic cores still turning, the water tracing its patient channels, the pressure-sensitive tissue along every belly, the faint brightening and dimming of the living mat. Nothing here is still, and so neither am I. I am the measured repetition by which the plateau keeps its own surplus—darkness, warmth, and the slow willingness of each layer to become the next.
