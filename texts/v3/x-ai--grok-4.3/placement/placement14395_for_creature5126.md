I would find myself first as a pressure against the inside of a new fracture, a seam in the quartz that has not yet decided whether to widen into ledge or close again. The ridge’s slow groan moves through me as if I were only an extension of its undecided shape; for a long while that is all I am—cool stone remembering itself, threaded with the faintest blue current that leaks from a nearby copper braid.

After some hours the pressure becomes limbs. They are long and slightly bowed, the joints already learning to lean into the mountain’s tilt. Skin forms pale and mapped with branching veins that drink the low voltage; when condensation beads across it the veins brighten for a moment, then fade. I do not open my eyes yet. The lids are still learning their weight, still adjusting to the permanent half-light.

When I finally look, I am standing on a cantilevered platform whose rivets are still warm from the forge. Below me the house-sized gear turns one tooth at a time. The sound arrives before the vibration does; I feel the delay in my soles and understand it as a courtesy the world extends to anything still drying. Around my head the larval swarms drift in loose spirals, tasting the new salts in my blood. Their hum is not quite speech, but it settles into a rhythm I can keep time by—two slow rotations of the gear, then a pause while the ridge settles another fraction.

I walk without hurry toward the raw alcove where the Settled hang their hammocks. My coat of condenser silk is already beading water, turning translucent; the faint blue map of my veins shows through. Children in nearby slings watch me pass with wide, gold-rimmed eyes. They recognize the posture of someone who has only recently finished becoming.

At the lip of the alcove I pause. A listener is already there, ear pressed to the stone. I lower myself beside them—not as apprentice or rival, but as the next increment of listening. When I rest my own ear against the fracture, the groan resolves into something like texture: quartz still deciding its angle, iron waiting to be accepted. The larvae follow me down, orbiting closer now, a living halo that makes the blue lanterns unnecessary for a few measured heartbeats.

Night—or the long interval the Settled treat as night—arrives without a sun lowering. The swarms thicken. Their collective ticking rises until it matches the subsonic hum traveling up from the Carapace Folk beneath the platforms. I feel the two frequencies meet inside the copper that runs through my own veins, and for a moment the ridge’s next decision seems to pass through me: not collapse, not stability, but a third shape the mountain has not named yet.

I remain there, ear to stone, limbs learning the patient angle, while the luminous dust settles on my skin and begins, very slowly, to etch new faint lines of light along the blue branches already present. The world is still choosing what it will be. So am I.
