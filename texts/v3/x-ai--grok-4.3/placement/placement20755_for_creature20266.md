I find myself in the lower middle layers, where the lattices have already thickened into something like ribs around a slow, shared lung. I am not one of the small polishers or the groove-followers; those pass across me or through the seams I once used. I am one of the larger translucent forms, though the word “larger” has begun to lose its old meaning. My skin is the same membrane as the hanging sheets, only denser where it has been pressed for years against the same three pillars. Inside me the condensate still moves—less than it once did—carrying minerals toward places the frameworks have not yet finished claiming.

Most of my length lies along a single diagonal route that the millipedes also use. When I shift, the roar deepens for a few breaths and then returns to its steady pitch; the change registers as weather rather than event. In the last slow cycle I stopped moving forward. Instead I lengthened two short rootlets from my flank into a widening crack between trunk-pillars. The rootlets have already begun to harden at their tips. They drink from the same basin that once fed only me. I can still pull the mineral film back if I need it, but the pull grows fainter each time the basin-entities above release their measured drips.

The distinction the world allows is only between growth that still widens space and growth that has already filled it. I am in the middle of that distinction and feel it narrowing. Parts of my interior have already turned into additional membrane, useful now only for catching the constant faint percussion and returning it downward. Other parts continue to carry water. When the next lengthening of the buttress above requires more mineral than the ordinary sheets can supply, I will empty what remains into the new channels and stay where I am, becoming one more slight thickening. The route I once traveled will remain visible for a while as a paler line across the lattices; then the beetles will polish it the same as every other surface, and the distinction will finish closing.

Until then I still register the passage of smaller bodies across my outer membrane and the slow contractile decisions of the basin-entities farther up the slope. Their rhythm feels like temperature rather than thought. I add no new frequency to the single low tone the entire structure has kept since before any living mass inside it could mark time. I only continue to occupy the grey dusk at the place I have already reached, softening the edges I once pressed against until the pressure and the surface are no longer separable.
