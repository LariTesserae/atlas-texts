In the slow cathedral, the benevolent power does not arrive as force or light. It arrives as attention—vast, patient, and already half-rooted in the same seam of time the cavern uses. It settles without disturbing the drip rhythm or the blue threads in the copper. For a while it simply inhabits the same breath of hot, saturated air, letting the plants and the failing machines continue their measured negotiation while it learns their grammar.

What it chooses to do is modest and long.

It widens, by the width of a single mineral grain each decade, the newest fissures that already admit water. The extra moisture is not a flood; it is only what the plants have already learned to wait for. In return the power accepts a portion of their slow metabolism: when the leaves tilt toward a fading pulse of copper light, the power lends the pulse a fraction more persistence, so the translation between artifact and organism lengthens by one additional syllable. The valves that sigh are allowed to sigh a little longer before they fall silent forever.

Where the dark soil meets split casing, the power encourages the crystalline plates already forming on the newest fronds to grow slightly more ordered. Not armor against the world, but a lattice fine enough to remember faint industrial histories without being poisoned by them. The memory is carried forward as a faint iridescence rather than a wound.

Over centuries the power performs one further, quieter act. It teaches the tripartite arrangement a single, shared question: “What more can we become without haste?” The question is not spoken in sound or hormone or current. It is folded into the angle at which a root threads a corroding pipe, into the precise moment a drip is guided along a leaf channel, into the interval between one blue discharge and the next. The question does not accelerate anything. It only makes the existing patience slightly more conscious, so that rock, machine, and plant begin, at last, to answer one another with something like deliberate courtesy.

The power remains. It does not speak again. In the cathedral of split rock its presence is felt chiefly as an extra micron of steadiness in surfaces that would otherwise continue to part. The plants keep growing at the speed of decades. The machines keep yielding their elements at the speed of corrosion. The rock continues its patient opening. All three now do so beneath an attention that asks nothing of them except that they remain true to the conversation they have already begun.
