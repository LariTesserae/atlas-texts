A benevolent and wise power, drawn by the plateau’s measured pulse, would not arrive as a visitor. It would settle instead as an added layer of the same slow logic already governing the stone.

It would first listen for centuries, matching its attention to the intervals between geothermal pulses. Only when it had learned the precise cadence of condensation, deposition, and retreat would it begin. Its first act would be invisible: a faint, persistent tension in the vapor itself, so that the warm showers, already brief, would linger an extra fraction of an hour. The extra moisture would reach fissures that had previously remained dry for decades, allowing the translucent many-legged grazers to extend their range by a few body-lengths. The change would register only as a slightly wider band of paler stone where the mineral films were now more thoroughly grazed.

Over the next several hundred years the power would introduce a second, quieter modification. Within the shallow basins it would seed microscopic nodes of a new crystalline habit—still the same pale stone, yet arranged in slightly tighter hexagons. These nodes would not generate new life; they would merely offer the existing crusts a marginally more stable scaffold on which to advance. The dark-green films would therefore braid themselves a little more quickly across the new terraces, shortening the time between successive generations from perhaps three centuries to two and a half. The plateau would continue to appear untouched; only the living geometry would become fractionally denser, visible solely from the same low angles that already revealed its earlier patterns.

The phosphorescent stems would receive the gentlest adjustment of all. Their internal syrup would be given one additional molecular path that allows the captured minerals to be stored for slightly longer before conversion. As a result, certain stems would survive one extra geothermal cycle before fracturing. When they finally did break, the surrounding crusts would inherit not only the mineral scaffold but also a faint, residual charge that makes the next generation of stems sprout a few meters closer together. The constellations across the plateau would therefore brighten and multiply by increments too small for any single observer to notice, yet measurable over millennia.

None of these acts would alter the fundamental rule of the place—that moisture appears, life answers, stone remembers. The power would simply lengthen the intervals during which the memory can be refreshed. It would not quicken the creatures, nor raise towers, nor impose any external purpose. It would only ensure that the next warm shower finds the basins infinitesimally deeper, the next geothermal pulse finds the fractures infinitesimally more receptive, and the next long dusk finds the scattered points of phosphorescence one degree less isolated.

In this manner the benevolent attention would remain indistinguishable from the plateau’s own ongoing work. The only evidence of its presence would be that the silence between the tick of cooling stone and the whisper of vapor would, over time, grow very slightly more inhabited, as though the emptiness itself had learned the patience required to hold one more quiet thing.
