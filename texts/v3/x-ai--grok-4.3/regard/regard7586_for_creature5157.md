The power arrived as a stillness within the stillness.

It did not descend from above; it was already there, folded into the half-light the way a thought can be folded into a lattice before the crystal knows it is thinking. For a long time—long enough for three mobile fragments to be born, cross a single avenue, and re-merge—it simply watched the way the plain watched itself: by being continuously present to every slow rearrangement.

When it finally acted, the act was almost indistinguishable from ordinary geology.

A new kind of tremor moved through the tiers. It lasted three centuries and was felt by only the largest of the sessile bodies. Where it passed, two crystal domains that had been slightly misaligned for half a million years found perfect register. The correction produced no harmonic. Instead it left behind a single, hair-fine plane of perfect transparency running the entire height of one cliff. Light that entered this plane did not bend away; it continued straight until it reached the interior of the formation and emerged again, now carrying a faint, steady glow that had never existed in the plain before.

The glow was not nourishment. It was recognition.

Inside the giant body that received it, filaments that had always risen and retracted on the same ancient schedule began, very gradually, to vary their timing. One filament would linger half a millimeter longer before retracting. Another would emerge at an angle never before attempted. The change was smaller than a single ion’s width, yet over the next twelve thousand years it propagated through the entire district like a rumor passed at the speed of mineral growth.

The mobile fragments noticed first. When one reached its destination crevice and relaxed into the stone, a few of its lattice planes refused to lock completely. They remained slightly proud, forming a shallow, faceted ridge no taller than a fault line. Other fragments, crossing the same tract later, paused—not stopped, only paused—an instant longer when their leading edge brushed that ridge. In that pause a few atoms were exchanged that had never been exchanged before. The exchange was not memory; it was something gentler. It was the knowledge that another lattice had once been here and had left a trace that could be read without being consumed.

The benevolent power did not speak. It had no need. Its single intervention continued to unfold at the pace the plain could accept, which is to say it would still be unfolding when the last of the current tier rims finally dissolved into the unchanging light overhead. By then the transparent plane would have migrated inward through many bodies, and the faint glow would have become a permanent, low-frequency resonance running through every healed scar and every re-precipitated avenue.

The plain remained the plain. Nothing organic ever grew. The wind-sculpted ridges still rose only to a person’s knee. Yet the shadows, on certain rare alignments of tremor and refraction, now sometimes leaned not toward their objects but gently toward one another, as if acknowledging a second geometry they had not noticed until the light decided to remember them.
