The power arrives as nothing that displaces water. It is only a slower rhythm laid over the existing one, a patience the pocket has not yet learned to measure. For a long while it simply remains, tasting the unfinished carbonate with the same tongue the lantern-jaws use, reading the distributed sentence of the veil-threads without adding any new clause. It notices how every death is already a mineral deposit, how every graze is already a chemical message, how the membrane flexes in ignorance of any outside. This ignorance is not lack; it is the precise condition under which the place can remain itself.

What the power chooses to do is therefore very small and very precise.

It releases a single, almost weightless filament of its own attention into the inflowing water. The filament is not substance but a willingness to remember. As the cold veil is drawn through the membrane’s pores, the filament passes with it and settles, molecule by molecule, along the inner surface. It does not harden the ribs or stiffen the mucus sheets. Instead it leaves an extremely faint trace, a readiness to notice when two lantern-jaws brush jaws and fail, for once, to retain the whole of each other’s tasted route. In that small failure the filament offers only the quiet fact that the missing fragment is still present somewhere in the pocket, still moving, still available if the system should ever discover a use for perfect recall.

The veil-threads, braiding near the new trace, find their silver filaments momentarily lengthened by a single extra pulse. They do not become larger or wiser; they merely continue their sentence one clause farther than they would have, and the extra clause happens to describe the lantern-jaws more accurately. The pinprick grazers, brushing the hanging vegetation above the trace, receive a chemical signal that lingers a fraction longer than usual, enough for one colony to drift, without intention, toward a rib that has not yet decided its neighbor. The membrane itself, flexing, draws the new attentiveness inward and outward again; its next contraction deposits an infinitesimally finer layer of carbonate along every growing arc, as though the pocket had been praised by something it cannot name.

Nothing is completed. Nothing is rescued. The place continues to condense its boundaries while already crowded with life that treats those boundaries as temporary. Only now the unfinished corridors carry, in addition to pressure and faint red light, the bare possibility that a finished world is not required in order to be remembered. The benevolent power withdraws, taking nothing with it, leaving only that extra clause still moving through the living braids.
