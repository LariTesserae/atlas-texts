The benevolent power arrives not as force or visitor but as a quiet recognition, the way light sometimes lengthens without changing its angle. It perceives the slope entire, from the lowest fused plates to the uppermost haze, and understands immediately that nothing here requires rescue. The equilibrium is not fragile; it is the only condition under which this form of life can persist. Any addition of vitality, any interruption of the mineral press, any quickening of the centuries-long adjustments would fracture the very balance that sustains the ceaseless moisture and the folded spaces.

So the power does one small thing and then withdraws.

It lays, across the highest and most exposed crowns, a single additional law of refraction. From that moment the light that already passes through successive planes of leaf and stone is subtly reordered so that wherever duplication occurs, the extra illumination is folded inward rather than outward. The effect is invisible to any eye that might someday arrive; the canopies experience only that the doubled light now arrives already tempered, already measured to the exact density their thickened tissues prefer. The change is smaller than the width of a single cell and will require no adjustment on their part for another few thousand years.

Having done this, the power leaves no further trace. It does not quicken growth, does not introduce new moisture, does not still the endless dripping or alter the roar. It simply permits the place to remain exactly what it has become—an intelligence whose only motion is the slow closing of gaps and whose only thought is the perpetual maintenance of wetness under unchanging heat. The slope continues its ascent into white haze, the terraces continue to receive and pass water, and the single indivisible tissue keeps pressing, layer upon layer, its oldest parts hardening while its newest lobes advance by the width of a finger every few decades.

The power carries the memory of the place with it as a kind of reference: one instance, among many, where wisdom required no intervention beyond a single, almost weightless refinement of light.
