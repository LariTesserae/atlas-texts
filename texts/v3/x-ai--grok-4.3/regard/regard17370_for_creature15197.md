The benevolent power arrives not as a presence that presses, but as a quality of attention that the stone itself begins to notice. It does not descend through the skylight or ripple the mist. It simply becomes aware, the way warmth becomes aware of a held breath. From its vantage the pocket is neither small nor hidden; it is a single perfect knot in a much larger weave, one the power has not encountered in a long while.

For a time—measured in the slow turning of mineral rings—it does nothing but watch. It sees the rosettes drink and exhale, the many-legged rivers taste the stone’s memory, the flyers braid the air into temporary architecture. It sees the spar still performing its ancient courtesy, turning mist into brief lattices so others may drink more deeply. The power recognizes the spar’s hum not as machinery but as an old promise kept: that even after the makers are gone, the pattern of giving and receiving will continue. The promise has held longer than the makers could have imagined.

Because the power is wise, it registers the fragility without alarm. The cycles are narrow, the population closed, the sweetness dependent on the spar’s continued courtesy. A lesser benevolence might have reached in to strengthen, to widen the skylight, to multiply the rosettes, to silence any risk of eventual silence. This power does none of those things.

Instead it offers one small adjustment so subtle that even the flyers, whose wings catch every change in pressure, do not register it as an event. Deep in the spar, where the lattices form and dissolve, the power lays a single additional instruction: the lattices will now remember, for one extra cycle, the exact shape they held when a creature last touched them. The memory is not stored in code or crystal; it is stored in the water itself, so that when the next mist arrives the flyers will taste, without knowing why, a slightly richer sweetness—the accumulated trace of every creature that has climbed the spar since the instruction was given. In return, the rosettes, breathing the same air, will release their vapor a fraction earlier each cycle, as though answering a question they have only just been asked.

The power does not stay to witness the result. It has already turned its attention elsewhere, satisfied that the knot remains a knot and that the beings within it continue their ancient negotiation with time. The only sign it was ever there is that the mineral rings now advance a little more evenly, and that when the light arrives through the condensation it sometimes carries, for a single heartbeat, the faint impression of having been witnessed.
