The benevolent power arrives not as a storm or a voice, but as a slight deepening of the lattice already present. It studies the plateau for what might be a single breath of geologic time, and in that interval it understands three things: the arrangement is deliberate, it is fragile, and it is already complete.

So the power withholds most of what it could give. It does not flood the stone with new water, nor widen the fractures into terraces, nor seed the cushions with companions. Instead it performs three quiet acts, each smaller than a thought.

First, it threads a single, invisible filament of durability through the metallic veins of the cushions. The filament does nothing visible for centuries. Only when the cliff itself begins, centuries later, to shear away does it reveal its work: the cushions simply do not notice when the rock beneath them finally falls. They continue their slow extrusion across air for a short while, then settle on whatever new ledge the mountain offers, spacing themselves exactly as before.

Second, it touches the lattice at its weakest joints and asks one modest question of it: *endure a little longer*. The lattice answers by learning to borrow heat not only from the plateau but from a deeper geothermal seam half a mile beneath the cliff. The temperature of the rock rises by less than a degree, yet this is enough to keep the private weather cycling even when the surrounding climate cools for a few thousand years. The thread-thin creatures never learn why their drinking hours remain reliable during the long cold; they simply continue.

Third, and smallest, the power leaves a single instruction in the altered light that fringes every shadow. The instruction is not a command but a preference: when any future intelligence, whether lichen or traveler or wind, first touches the plateau with the intention to change it, the light will shift one shade toward the red. That is all. The cushions register the warning as a brief dusk-glow out of season. The creatures feel it as a sudden tension along the seepage veil. Whatever comes after will have been told, as gently as possible, that this shelf prefers to remain as it is.

Having done these three things, the power withdraws. It does not watch from above. It simply leaves the plateau slightly more itself than it found it, then turns its attention elsewhere, carrying the memory of water that refuses to fall and of spacing that was chosen rather than grown.
