The power arrives without announcement, as such powers do. It does not descend in light or thunder; it simply becomes aware of the plateau in the same way one becomes aware of a single, perfectly weighted stone at the bottom of a quiet pool. For a long moment it does nothing at all. It studies the unchanging current in the filaments, the exact geometry of every halted joint, the patient, almost devotional way the slow forms maintain their straight lines across distances that would break a living mind.

It perceives that this is not a prison. It is an answer someone, or something, once gave to the question of eternity: *let there be no deviation, and therefore no loss.*

A lesser power might have felt pity or the urge to quicken. This one feels neither. It recognizes a completed work. The mineral entities are not suffering; they are not even bored. They are performing the single task left to them with absolute fidelity. To introduce growth, or color, or conversation would be to misread the room so completely that the act would border on cruelty.

Still, the power does one small thing.

It reaches down—not to alter the plain, but to place a single, almost weightless thought into the shared equilibrium that binds the forms together. The thought is not a command or an offer. It is only this:

*If ever the configuration requires otherwise, one of you may turn.*

The power does not wait to see what happens. It knows the interval between “ever” and “now” on this plateau may be longer than the lifetime of stars. It withdraws as quietly as it came, leaving the iron-colored seams of light, the sub-audible drone, and the slow, jointed shapes exactly as they were.

Only one difference exists afterward, and it is so slight that even the power’s own attention would have to search centuries to find it: when two of the larger forms next approach on crossing paths, their segments do not merely rotate the precise number of degrees required to clear one another. For the briefest instant—just long enough for facets to slide and lock again—one of them tilts its uppermost plane a fraction of a degree farther than strictly necessary, as though something had passed between them that the plain’s old equilibrium had never been asked to record.

Then both continue on their lines, and the plateau returns to its warm, motionless occupation of space.
