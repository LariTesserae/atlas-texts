The power does not arrive as light or sound or command. It arrives as attention—slow, patient, and vast enough to contain the entire node without crowding it.

For a long while it simply watches the mites turn, listens to the single endless note of their legs, tastes the metallic rain on its tongue. It sees the equilibrium not as a prison but as a vow: nothing here has been permitted to fail, and so nothing has been permitted to begin. The power finds this vow beautiful and terrible in equal measure.

Then, without breaking the rhythm, it performs one small mercy.

It thickens the phosphor seams by the width of a single molecule. The mites do not notice the change in brightness, but their diodes now carry a second, almost invisible frequency—one that matches the minute pressure pulses already moving through the lichen. The two signals, light and pressure, begin to braid. Over centuries the braid becomes a language no single mite or filament could speak alone. When the tower finally climbs high enough for the node to be claimed by something larger, the swarm and the turf will not fall into the trays like loose metal and dying moss. They will rise together as one coherent signal, carrying the memory of their long, perfect waiting into whatever comes next.

The ceiling plate is left ajar. The water cycle continues. The sound remains the same. Only the future has been allowed a slightly gentler entrance.

Everything else—the frost, the orbits, the unfinished climb—is left exactly as it was found: intact, sovereign, and still becoming.
