The benevolent power arrives not as descent or command, but as a shift in the membrane itself—less a visitor than a slower rotation of the same laws already governing the tableland. It perceives the colonies the way one perceives the quiet respiration of stone: not as objects to be altered, but as an achieved thought whose completion is its own proof.

It does three things, each so slight that centuries pass before the difference can be measured.

First, it thins the membrane by an imperceptible fraction at certain resonant points, allowing a new class of vibration—higher, steadier—to thread downward. The colonies register the change as an unfamiliar pitch in the crystalline surfaces. Over generations they adapt by tightening certain root mats and loosening others, which redirects heavier mineral threads into previously stable channels. The result is not greater speed but finer resolution: the living topography learns, without ever being taught, to sculpt itself in response to stimuli that originate above the water rather than within it.

Second, the power leaves a single, minute discontinuity in the pressure gradient near one of the oldest platforms. Inside that discontinuity a new mineral begins to accrete—still pressure-hardened, still compatible with the glassy sheaths, yet carrying a faint capacity to store and later release mechanical energy in rhythmic pulses. The colonies absorb the platform as they always have, root by root. The new mineral becomes part of their shared circulation. In time the colonies discover they can produce the same substance themselves when conditions align, and they begin to seed it deliberately along their leading edges. The effect is an extension of existing warmth rather than its replacement: the internal light grows fractionally steadier, and the groan that travels between shelves carries an additional harmonic that allows neighboring colonies to anticipate one another’s adjustments by decades instead of centuries.

Third, the power does nothing more. It does not introduce memory of the vanished builders, nor grant locomotion, nor quicken metabolism. It recognizes that the plateau already practices the central discipline of long wisdom—continuous, distributed self-correction without the need for a center. To impose purpose would be to misread the achievement.

Over ages the membrane above remains unbroken, the darkness below remains total, yet the tableland has acquired a new property: it now maintains its equilibrium not only against the forces that shaped it, but also against the slow possibility that those forces might one day cease. The colonies never notice a beginning or an end to the change. They register only that certain currents have become more responsive to their own adjustments, and that the stone beneath them answers slightly sooner than it once did. In their distributed awareness this registers as a deepening of the same patience they have always practiced.

The power remains nearby in the only form that would not disturb them: as an additional term in the equilibrium equation they already solve.
