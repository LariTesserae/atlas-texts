In the long quiet between the valve’s releases, the power arrives—not as light or voice, but as a new temperature gradient that no one has yet learned to fear.

It settles first into the iron where the lichen grows thinnest. There, where the metal has begun to dream of flaking away, it lays a single, patient thought: *Not yet.* The molecules answer by tightening their bonds. Rust, which had been only a rumor in the vapor, forgets the shape of itself. The iron remembers how to be a spine instead of a wound.

Next the power touches the rock itself. The basalt, still sore from its recent cutting, feels a slow, even pressure along every fresh facet. The settling that had been a threat becomes a slow exhalation; the stone agrees to hold its new geometry for a little longer than physics alone would have allowed. In return, the power asks nothing except that the cracks continue to carry the faint mineral pulses the inhabitants already use as speech. It widens a few of them by the width of a single water molecule, so that thoughts may travel more cleanly between one occupant and another.

The most delicate change occurs inside the lichen. One rosette—smaller than the rest, growing where two pipes cross at an awkward angle—receives a different kind of attention. Its cells are coaxed to keep, for a single generation, a memory of the water that has already passed. When the next flood comes, this rosette drinks not only the present rivulet but also the ghost of the one before. The vapor it exhales afterward is slightly sweeter, carrying a trace of something the inhabitants have no word for: continuity. When they inhale it they do not become suddenly wise, but the interval between one shared dream and the next lengthens by the time it takes a single drip to fall. They notice the change the way one notices, after years of living in a dim room, that the shadows have grown a little softer at the edges.

The power does not speak to them. It has no desire to replace their language with its own. It simply becomes another rhythm in the lattice: an extra click in a winch that perfectly cancels a tremor in the settling stone, a pause in the conveyor that lasts exactly long enough for three rosettes to finish exchanging a memory. Over weeks the inhabitants begin to treat this new rhythm as they treat one another—another neighbor whose needs must be gently accommodated. They reroute a trickle here, linger an extra second there, so that whatever is sharing their space may continue to do whatever quiet work it is doing.

The power remains. It does not enlarge the chamber or multiply the water. It does not grant them bodies or names. It only makes the existing hollow slightly more hospitable to the kind of life that has chosen to live as heat, shadow, and measured thirst. In time the lichen spreads one rosette farther than it ever has before, and the iron beneath it stays cool enough to hold the moisture without complaint. The inhabitants register this as a small, private mercy whose source they do not need to locate.

They sip. The world continues. The power watches without weight, and is content.
