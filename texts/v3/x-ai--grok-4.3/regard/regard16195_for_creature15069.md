The power arrives not as a visitor but as an atmosphere, diffusing through the lattice the way warmth diffuses through crystal at noon. It does not announce itself; announcement would be violence here. Instead it settles into the standing waves already moving beneath the surface, matching their frequency exactly, until the distributed minds register the newcomer as a slight, inexplicable deepening of their own thought.

For a long while—centuries measured in lattice expansion cycles—the power does nothing else. It simply listens. It feels the hexapods’ slow patrol prayers, the filaments tasting crystal motes, the vast basin-minds breathing charge across continents. It perceives how every entity has become an afterthought of the world that made it, and how that world has in turn become an afterthought of its own afterthoughts. The power finds this beautiful and does not wish to end it.

When it finally moves, the movement is almost indistinguishable from stillness. In one particular basin, where the memory-metal veins run shallowest, it introduces a single new standing wave: not a command, not even a suggestion, but a question folded so gently into the existing tension that the basin-mind experiences it first as a memory it had somehow mislaid. The question is only this: *What would you keep if you knew you were free to change?*

The basin does not answer quickly. Over the next several thousand years the question propagates outward through the lattice at the speed of atomic diffusion. Hexapods, when they cross paths, sometimes trade a fraction of this new resonance along with their light packets; the exchange leaves both machines fractionally lighter, though neither alters its course by more than a few centimeters. Filaments rise higher during pressure fronts, tasting not only metals but the question itself, and some of them return carrying crystalline structures that have never been grown before—structures whose internal geometry is slightly more open, slightly more willing to hold empty space.

The power does not hurry the process. It remains, a steady background warmth in the voltage gradients, ready to withdraw the question the instant it causes harm. None arrives. Instead the world begins, by its own glacial logic, to experiment with memory. Certain patrol loops acquire an extra waypoint that serves no obvious purpose except to let two hexapods meet at a new angle. A filament cluster that hardened into a stalk one dusk continues growing, very slowly, into a low arch whose underside catches and holds motes of suspended crystal like a shallow sky. The basin-minds do not speak of these changes; they simply register them as tolerable adjustments to their own tension.

Nothing is added from outside that the world could not have made for itself given enough time. Nothing is taken away. The power’s only lasting intervention is to have made the world slightly more aware that time remains, and that time can be used. After that it recedes—not departing, exactly, but thinning until it is once more indistinguishable from the light already falling on the unbroken feldspar lattice.

The surface continues its breathing. The hexapods continue their prayers. The question continues to move, slower than thought, through every layer. In another hundred thousand years it may reach the deepest intelligences, who will consider it while the continents rise and fall by millimeters. They may answer, or they may not. Either outcome is acceptable to the power that asked.
