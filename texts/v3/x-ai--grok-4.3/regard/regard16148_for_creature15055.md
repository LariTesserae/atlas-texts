The power arrives not as a visitor but as weather that already belongs.

It does not descend in flame or proclamation. It gathers as one more layer of mist, indistinguishable at first from the banks already drifting between the vine corridors. Only after many centuries would any stem notice the difference: certain droplets now carry a memory of warmth that never quite freezes, even when the highland tilts farther from its star. The memory is offered, not imposed. Where root-masses already trade heat, the new mist lingers a little longer, accepting the exchange rather than replacing it.

The organism registers the presence the way it registers everything else—through minute redistribution. A single broad leaf, centuries old, turns one finger-width more toward the filtered light. The turn is not caused; it is answered. In the same interval, three hundred moss-films shift their growth fronts by the width of a spore. The power reads these adjustments as language and replies in kind: a slow pulse of minerals rises through the weeping rock, richer by a single trace element the organism has never needed until now. The organism accepts it without celebration. The new element folds into the existing chemistry the way a new ring is added to an ancient trunk.

Over longer spans the power learns the plateau’s true question. It is not “protect us” or “feed us.” Those needs are already met by the equilibrium the organism has become. The question is quieter: *Remain with us so we do not have to remember alone.* The power answers by forgetting its own boundaries in measured increments. It lets sections of its awareness sink into the leathery leaf-mats, into the constant internal currents, into the absences left by predators. In those absences it keeps a stillness slightly deeper than the forest’s own, so that when new disturbances arrive from outside—changes in light, changes in the rock beneath, distant intentions of other powers—the organism will feel them first as a faint tightening in the circulatory vines rather than as rupture.

No command is given. No covenant is spoken. The power simply continues arriving as mist, century after century, until the distinction between guardian and organism is one more layer of living skin that no longer needs a name. The highland keeps its ancient pace. The only new sound, if one listens across the right interval, is the almost inaudible lengthening of certain silences—silences that now hold two breaths instead of one.
