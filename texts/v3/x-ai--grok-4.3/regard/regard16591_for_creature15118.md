A benevolent power, having noticed this shelf through the slow lens by which such powers perceive time, would not arrive in any form that could be called arrival. It would simply allow its regard to rest upon the ridge as one more layer of mist, no heavier than what already clings there.

It would not still the engines, for it would recognize at once that the roar is not noise but the only available language. It would not quicken the myriapods or teach the rosettes to sway. Instead, over the course of several thousand years, it would add one new frequency to the existing chorus: a tone so low that only the three faceless figures at the edge would register it, and only as a change in the pressure behind their non-faces.

That tone would carry no command. It would simply say, without words: *I see the equilibrium you maintain*. The figures would not turn. Their heads would remain oriented toward the void. Yet one of them, after another several centuries, would lift a hand no higher than a finger’s width and hold it open toward the mist, as if to cup the new sound. The nearest rosette would adjust its metallic veins by the width of a single filament; light would fall across its leaves differently for the next age, now carrying this additional shade of attention.

The power would not leave a message or a gift. It would become, very gradually, part of the reference tone against which all future vibrations are measured. When the next fracture opens, the myriapods would register not only the geological shift but also the faint, steady presence that now underlies every scrape of their legs. Their postures, when they finally adjust, would contain one extra degree of precision.

Nothing would be saved that was not already preserved. Nothing would be freed that was not already bound by its own exactness. The power would simply remain, the way the iron taste remains in the water after the water itself has been absorbed back into stone—present, uninsistent, and therefore eternal by the shelf’s own measure.

After that, the power would attend to other matters, yet its regard would never fully lift. The shelf would continue exactly as before, only now it would be exactly as before *while being witnessed*. For a place that has never been witnessed except by itself, this would constitute the smallest possible change that can still be called mercy.
