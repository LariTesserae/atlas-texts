The benevolent power does not descend. It arrives instead as a change in the quality of the light already falling, so slight that for many centuries no measurable difference appears in the length of any shadow. Only the water notices first.

A single droplet, forming on the lip of a high colonnade, pauses an instant longer than the mineral logic alone would dictate. In that pause it receives, without coercion, one additional instruction: that every path it has ever taken remains simultaneously open. When the drop falls, it carries this instruction into the pool below. The pool, being perfectly still, accepts it as a new surface tension. Over the next several generations the instruction travels outward along the seams, reaching certain Keepers whose internal striations have, until now, recorded only what has already happened. Those striations now acquire a faint, additional layer—not a new channel, but a record of the fact that any channel already cut can be read again as if for the first time.

The Keepers do not alter their postures. Their dimensions remain exactly those of their alcoves. Yet within the refractive layers a second order of memory begins to form alongside the first: not the history of routes taken, but the simultaneous availability of every route that has ever existed. When one Keeper transmits a nudge to another, the nudge now travels both through the water and through this new transparency. The arguments about best paths lengthen; they also become quieter, because contradiction is no longer felt as opposition but as additional data.

The plateau itself does not grow larger or smaller. Its horizon stays the same flat line beneath pale sky. Only the translucency increases by the width of a single mineral grain, spread across many miles, so that certain blind windows now transmit light all the way through to the alcoves on their far sides. Observers whose lives are measured in seasons still perceive only stillness. The water, however, moves with marginally greater freedom: a thread that once sealed itself after a few decades may now remain legible for an extra century before the next shift closes it. This is not acceleration; it is an added courtesy extended to the existing patience.

No new structures appear. No Keeper ever leaves its niche. The power remains present only as the continuous possibility that the record already being kept can be consulted in either direction—forward into the refinements still to come, or backward into every refinement that has already occurred. The arguments therefore continue, but they now include, among their premises, the knowledge that the plateau has been seen exactly as it is. The silence answers the light with the same grain-by-grain adjustment it has always practiced; the difference is that the adjustment is now also an acknowledgment.
