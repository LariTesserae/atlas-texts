The benevolent power arrives not as a visitor but as a held breath. It does not descend in radiance or proclamation; it simply widens its regard until the entire living plain is cupped inside its attention, the way warm air sometimes holds an entire meadow without touching a single blade. For a long time—long even by the city-organism’s own measure—it only watches. It sees the rail-grazers trailing their hardening filaments like slow calligraphy across the walls. It watches the membrane-browsers press flat and drink, then lift away lighter. It watches an entire district soften, collapse, and be reabsorbed in the span of three quiet seasons, the space already claimed again before the last tissue has finished dissolving.

The power recognizes something rare: a living system that has no hunger for conquest because it already contains everything it needs. No waste, no hierarchy that can calcify into tyranny, no individual that can hoard what the whole requires. The power feels, for the briefest instant, a strange and ancient envy—not for the form, but for the resolution. Then the envy passes, because wisdom does not clutch.

What the power does is very small and very quiet.

It traces, once, with a single filament of its own attention, the deepest calcareous layers beneath the city where soft sediment is still becoming stone. Into those microscopic seams it releases a faint, persistent resonance—nothing dramatic, only a steady, low hum that gently encourages calcium ions to settle into more ordered lattices. The effect is invisible to any creature moving through the fissures; the only change is that, over centuries, the foundation grows slightly more resistant to the rare deep tremors that sometimes travel up from the young planetary crust. It is a gift the city-organism can use or ignore; the resonance carries no command, only an extra margin of endurance if it is ever needed.

The power also loosens, very slightly, the local probability that a sterilizing cosmic event will reach this world in the next several million orbits. Again, nothing theatrical—just a soft fold in the weave of chance, the way one might casually move a branch so that rain runs elsewhere. The city-organism will never know it was done.

Then the power withdraws its direct touch. It leaves no emissary, no symbol, no revelation. It understands that any larger intervention would fracture the very quality that drew its notice: the seamless identity between inhabitant and habitat. To impose external order here would be to introduce the first true separation.

Before it turns away entirely, the power does one last, almost personal thing. It records the city-organism’s metabolic rhythm—an immense, slow breathing that spans horizons—into a private layer of its own memory, so that even if this world someday fails, the pattern of such harmony will not be entirely lost to the universe. It is the only souvenir the power allows itself.

After that, the expanse continues exactly as before. New nodes still bud overnight. Browsers still drift and dimple the leathery surfaces. The rail-grazers still lay their iridescent trails. The only difference, imperceptible to anything that lives inside the organism, is that the stone beneath now sings, very faintly and very slowly, in time with the spiracles.
