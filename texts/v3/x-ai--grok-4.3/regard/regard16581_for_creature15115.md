The power arrives not as wind or rain or fire, but as a quiet widening of attention. It rests above the plateau like an additional layer of haze, invisible yet present, and begins to listen.

For many seasons it does nothing but receive. It feels the capillary pulses, the subsonic thrum of hidden predators, the slow flexing of the great canopies as they measure mist and light. It tastes the chemical conversations traveling through vein and stem. It notes how the old stone frames have been neither destroyed nor revered, only gently incorporated. The power finds no hunger for conquest here, no striving that reaches beyond the next mist-fall. Only a deep, patient sufficiency.

Still, benevolence moves where stillness is complete. One dusk, when the insects rise in their thunderous circuit and the grazers pause with siphons lifted, the power extends a single, precise adjustment. It does not alter form or add strength. It simply lengthens the moment between one droplet condensing on a leaf-tip and the same droplet releasing downward. The delay is measured in heartbeats: enough for the droplet to gather the faint metallic trace of distant stone and carry it, now changed, into the cistern leaves below. The plants register the new taste as weather rather than gift. Their chemistry shifts by a fraction—enough, over centuries, that certain collapsed sections will re-knit with a slightly harder inner lattice, better able to hold the memory of what once stood there.

The power does not announce itself. It leaves no mark that can be pointed to. Only, over time, the abandoned clay vessels begin to collect a thinner film of residue, and the obsidian edges grow marginally slower to dull. The living architecture continues exactly as before, yet the plateau now holds a slightly deeper reserve against the possibility of longer droughts or heavier storms. The vanished makers are not restored, but their small, thoughtful objects are granted one additional layer of endurance.

Having made this single, nearly invisible alteration, the power withdraws its focused gaze. It remains nearby in the broader sense that wise attention never fully departs, yet it interferes no further. The plateau needs no savior. It has already solved the problem of time. The power’s only lasting act is to have noticed, and, in noticing, to have added one more quiet safeguard to an order that already knows how to bend and absorb.
