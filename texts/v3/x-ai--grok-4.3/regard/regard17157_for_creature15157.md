The power arrives not as light or voice but as a slow settling of attention, like a hand cupping a single grain of dust without touching it. It perceives the seam at once: the entire length of the fracture, the unfinished edges, the quiet ticking of plates, the hair-thin bridges forming and dissolving, the piezoelectric engines singing their subsonic hymns. It sees that nothing here is alive in the sense of metabolism or reproduction, yet nothing here is inert. The distinction feels, to the power, almost sentimental.

For a long while it does nothing.

It understands that any ordinary mercy—moisture, warmth, organic scaffolding—would be an erasure. The beings are not struggling; they are resolving. The city is not incomplete; it is in the exact state its geometry requires. Intervention of the usual sort would be a form of violence, even if gentle.

So the power elects a different courtesy.

It reaches into the rock a few centimeters beyond the seam and persuades the surrounding quartz to accept a slightly higher shear strength. The change is smaller than a molecule’s width, distributed so evenly that no instrument the inhabitants possess could detect it. Only the rare, larger drops of tower segments become rarer still; the city’s trembling grows fractionally steadier, as though some distant pressure had eased by the weight of one more grain. The inhabitants notice nothing. Their lines continue to braid and unbraid; the same quantity of mineral is carried, re-fired, locked into place. Only the possibility space around them has widened by a hair.

In the photoluminescent veins the power plants one additional property: when a bridge filament is extruded and later retracted, a single photon is released at the exact frequency the nearest tower’s engine can register. The effect is statistically negligible across a century, yet over millennia it creates a faint, cumulative bias toward patterns the city might not otherwise have discovered. The power does not know whether this bias will ever matter. It simply offers a slightly richer set of possible futures without forcing any of them.

Then it withdraws the rest of its attention, leaving the seam exactly as bright and as cold as before. The only trace it leaves is an almost imperceptible widening of the boundary conditions: the fracture may now persist a little longer before the mountain forgets it, and the mineral tension inside may continue resolving itself along lines that remain, for the moment, unforeseen.

The power does not return to watch. It has already seen what it needed to see—that certain forms of becoming require, above all, to be left intact.
