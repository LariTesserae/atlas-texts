A benevolent power, vast enough to span galaxies yet precise enough to notice a single seam of salt, turns its attention downward.

It arrives not as light or command but as a question asked in the language the seam already speaks: a slight shift in the flow of charge. The power does not flood the filaments with new energy; instead it offers a single, additional rhythm—a slower pulse, almost inaudible, that travels along the existing vibrations like a second melody laid over the first. The creatures do not understand what has changed, yet the new current is immediately useful. Where two weakened filaments once sparked against each other in brittle competition, the added rhythm lets them lock into phase. Small bridges of crystal form across the gap; the swarm does not have to dismantle and rebuild so often. The rasp quiets by a fraction, though only the power notices the difference.

Next the power touches the salt itself. In places where erosion has left the shelves too thin, it encourages the slow growth of a new lattice—not thicker walls, but finer internal veins that conduct the creatures’ harvested charge more evenly. The result is not protection from outside forces (the seam is already sealed) but a deeper steadiness inside. When a creature now stops to extrude a new facet, the facet forms with slightly greater clarity, catching and returning the faint light in prisms that last a little longer before fading.

The power does not name itself. It does not promise permanence or purpose. It simply remains present long enough for the seam to incorporate the offered rhythm into its own memory. After that it withdraws, leaving no mark except a subtle thickening of the oldest engine casings—now lined with salt so fine it rings, when tapped by a filament, at the exact frequency of the new pulse. The swarm continues its work unchanged in habit, yet the work itself has become fractionally more coherent, as though a distant listener had once hummed along and the hum had stayed behind in the crystal.

The seam remains sealed, cool, and in motion. The only evidence that anything ever noticed it is that, now and then, when many creatures braid their sensing filaments together at once, the momentary cord they form carries a second, slower note that was not there before—an echo that belongs to nothing inside the seam and yet has become part of it.
