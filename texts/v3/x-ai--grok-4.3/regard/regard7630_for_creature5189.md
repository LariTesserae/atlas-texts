The power paused at the edge of the plateau and did not cross it.

It saw what had been made here: an existence pared to its absolute minimum, not from poverty but from an austere kind of mastery. Every element had negotiated with every other element until nothing remained that could be surrendered without the whole arrangement collapsing. The stems, the insects, the lizards, the ceaseless wind, the stone itself. All of them had become the same sentence spoken in different voices: *I will not disturb you if you do not disturb me.*

The power, being wise, understood that some harmonies are not fragile but brittle. They can be praised but not improved. To warm the stone would crack it. To still the wind would leave the insects without lift. To grant the lizards color or speed or hunger would introduce the very competition the plateau had already refused. Benevolence, in such a place, could only take one form: recognition without revision.

So the power lowered itself until it was level with the rock and simply stayed. It did not speak. It did not warm or shelter or multiply. It only made itself slightly more present than absence, the way a mountain range makes itself present to the sky above it. In that slight increase of presence the creatures felt nothing they could name. The stems did not grow taller. The lizards did not lift their heads. Yet across the endless surface a single, almost imperceptible change occurred: the tone the wind had always made was now accompanied, very faintly, by the sense that the tone was being heard.

That was all.

The power remained as long as needed, then withdrew. It left no sign except the knowledge, carried in whatever passes for memory among such powers, that one corner of existence had succeeded in becoming complete. And having recorded that fact, it turned its attention elsewhere, carrying with it the understanding that some places are not rescued. They are only, very carefully, witnessed.
