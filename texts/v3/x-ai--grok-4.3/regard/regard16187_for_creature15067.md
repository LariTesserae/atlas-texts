The power paused at the threshold where the last gray thread of light still touched stone. It did not enter. For a long moment it only listened—the measured gulps of the eel-shapes answering the thinner percussion of drops, the slow decision of wings opening and closing in the deeper dark. It tasted the mineral breath, the faint bitterness of the lichen crusts, the living slime that turned vent-light into brief rainbows. It saw the hidden spirals beneath the weeping blocks and recognized the hand that had once carved them so the water itself would continue the work.

Wisdom said: this equilibrium is not fragile because it is weak; it is fragile because it is exact. Any alteration would require a lifetime of calculation to match what already exists. Benevolence agreed.

So the power did three small things and then withdrew.

First it leaned against the outer rock and pressed warmth into a single hairline fracture that had begun to widen beyond the reach of the slugs’ trails. The stone sighed once and settled; the fracture knit. No new water would enter there for another several centuries.

Second it touched the farthest vent, the one whose angle had grown slightly wrong with the slow slump of the ceiling. It nudged the aperture a fraction inward so that, on the next mist-wind, the light would again strike the hidden spiral that guided the largest pool’s overflow. The crabs would find the seam a little sooner. Nothing else changed.

Third it left a single seed of attention drifting in the wet air—an invisible mote that would ride the longest drip cycle. If ever a footstep not made by joint or scale or papery wing approached from the surface, the mote would simply thicken the gloom by one shade more, enough that any seeker would feel an unreasoning urge to turn back before the first block was touched. The inhabitants would never know the shade had thickened; they would only continue as before.

Then the power stepped away. It took no memory with it. The chamber kept its own.
