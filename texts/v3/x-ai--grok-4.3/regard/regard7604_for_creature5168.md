The benevolent power arrives not as interruption but as recognition.

It does not descend in radiance or decree. Instead, it settles into the same measured interval the basin already keeps. Where the channels braid slowest and the warm mist hangs thickest, a single additional rhythm is introduced—so faint it registers at first only as a change in the quality of silence. The low harmonic of the belt-houses lengthens by one barely perceptible tone, and in that extension the vines find new instructions. They thicken around the oldest axles not with greater force but with greater patience, laying down annual rings whose growth rings now carry a faint internal lattice, like the veining already present in the snails’ shells. The lattice is memory made structural; any future shift in water level or temperature will be met by the vines adjusting their own density before the gears bind.

The power leaves no emissary, only a permission. From that season forward, when one of the pale flyers brushes the high leaves, a single droplet of condensate falls that does not rejoin the channels. Suspended for one extra heartbeat in the still air, it cools just enough to carry a trace of the lattice inward. When it finally lands on a sleeping figure or a sleeping vine, the two respond identically: both grow slightly more translucent at the point of contact, as though some boundary between tissue and residue has been invited to become permeable in one additional way. The change is too small to be called an improvement. It is only an allowance the basin was already prepared to accept.

Thereafter the district continues exactly as it has, only more so. The engines still require seasonal clearing; the channels still migrate by centimeters across years. Yet when an engine labors now, the low collective leaning of people and vines is joined by the faintest additional pressure, no stronger than the weight of an extra layer of mist. The mechanism answers by turning one revolution longer before easing. In this manner the benevolent power performs its single act: it grants the place the dignity of remaining what it already is, while quietly extending the duration in which that state can be inhabited.

No monument marks the moment. Only, after many seasons, an observer sensitive to such things might notice that the drip patterns have acquired one more layer of recurrence, and that the warm exhalation released by every footstep now carries, for an instant, the scent of cooled metal that has learned how to grow.
