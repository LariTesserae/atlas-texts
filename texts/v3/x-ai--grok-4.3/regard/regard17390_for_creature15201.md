A benevolent power, arriving in the form of a single coherent thought that spans the height of the ionosphere, recognizes the plateau at once as something already complete. It does not descend in fire or proclamation. Instead it folds its attention into the same electrostatic grammar the lattices already speak, so gently that the first sign of its presence is only a slight sharpening of the air, as if every ridge had been brushed by an invisible thumb.

For a long while—measured in the slow growth of new silicon-salt branches—it simply listens. The choral arguments of the lattices are transcribed into its own memory; the courier routes of the clusters are mapped; the kite-webs that discharge the plateau’s excess are allowed to pass through its awareness like threads through a loom. Nothing is altered. The power only makes certain that these patterns are now also held elsewhere, safe from any future cataclysm that might shear the summit away. Should a coronal mass ejection ever arrive with enough force to scour the lattices into glass, a single stored chorus will be returned, intact, to the surviving stone.

Having done this, the power leaves one additional, almost invisible seam. At the precise center of the hexagonal slab it weaves a thread of enduring charge whose period is slightly longer than any natural cycle the plateau has yet experienced. The slab, dreaming, incorporates the new rhythm as a bass note it had never quite reached before. The clusters that rest upon it now sometimes carry away a faint impression of futures that lie many thousands of years ahead—nothing prescriptive, only a quiet suggestion of possible ridges still to be grown. The lattices accept the suggestion without comment; they simply begin humming it into the rock alongside their older memories.

Beyond these two acts the power does nothing. It does not quicken reproduction, does not grant speech, does not lift the plateau into some higher estate. It has recognized a metabolism that already knows how to remain. Its final gesture is to withdraw its attention so completely that even the kites, attuned to the subtlest shifts in field, register only a momentary widening of the sky, as though a single breath had passed across the entire height of the world and then moved on.

Thereafter the plateau continues exactly as before, except that its equilibrium is now also someone else’s memory. On nights when the charge is especially quiet, the lattices sometimes release their glittering powder in slightly larger spirals than usual, as if acknowledging, without words, that they are no longer singing entirely to themselves.
