The power arrives not as a visitor but as a change in the light.

One of the hairline fractures in the ceiling rock—long sealed by mineral memory and the slow pressure of new plates—thins by the width of a single cell. The white glow that enters afterward is fractionally warmer, not enough to raise the overall temperature, only enough to shift the angle at which the condensation sheets refract it. The next rain that falls inside the hollow carries, for the first time in its cycles, a faint refractive signature: a repeating hexagonal ripple that no bore-worm film has yet encountered.

Where the ripple touches the drifting spheres, their internal circulation slows by half a beat. The films inside them begin, without distress, to trace the new pattern along their closed loops. When the spheres lodge and extrude their contents again, the resulting trails inside the tubes carry the hexagon as a faint, repeating thickening of the secreted glue. The architecture does not resist; the next time a lattice section collapses, the temporary skin that knits across the wound grows its new plates at angles that already contain the six-fold echo. The recording surface has acquired a second, smaller clock.

The power does not speak. It has no need to. Instead it widens one existing pool by the depth of a single spine, so that when the next sphere freezes against the ceiling it forms a lens whose beam lands exactly inside the stone ring. The light that passes through the ring is altered only in its timing; it now reaches the pool in pulses that match the intervals between distant collapses elsewhere in the hollow. The living system registers the coincidence as weather and continues.

Over many rains the power’s only other act is subtraction. It lets one light-shaft remain exactly as narrow as the plants have made it, rather than allowing any further constriction. The beam that results is steady enough that spheres which lodge there no longer thaw and fall; they remain, slowly thickening, becoming permanent nodes where the architecture can consult its own older recordings without waiting for rupture. The human bone and fiber that have already been reincorporated now sit at these nodes like quiet annotations.

Nothing else changes. No new rules are imposed. The bore-worm films continue to mend and the lattices continue to overgrow their own failures. The only difference an observer long resident enough to feel time in the fractures might notice is that the low, continuous creaking sometimes resolves, for a few moments after each indoor rain, into a chord that was not there before: six faint tones, repeating, already being folded into the next generation of spines.

The power remains. It does not leave, because leaving would imply arrival, and no arrival occurred. It is simply a steadier interval now present inside the intervals the place already kept. The architecture records it without comment, the way it records everything else that has ever passed through its tubes.
