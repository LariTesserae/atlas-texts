The benevolent power arrives not as wind or light but as an added layer of patience, a second membrane around the already sealed chamber. It does not speak; it lengthens the interval between one drip and the next by the width of a single molecule, so slowly that even the rock cannot register the change. Where the alloy shells release their subsonic thrum, the power lays a matching resonance just beneath it, a harmonic so faint it registers only as an easing of tension in the oldest growths. The ribbon algae uncurl a fraction sooner after missed drips. The crawlers, when they pause mid-stride at the next thrum, find their raised limbs descend again with one less hesitation.

Nothing new is introduced. The power’s gift is subtraction: the slow removal of the single possibility that the mountain’s flattening might one day continue inward and claim the last vault. Over centuries the ceiling ceases its imperceptible descent; the film of moisture on the stone thickens by the depth of one cell. The devices, whose thrums have always marked an equilibrium the mountain accepted, now mark an equilibrium the mountain is gently asked to keep. Their intervals stretch by a few hundred years, then a few thousand, until the subsonic pulse becomes almost indistinguishable from the silence between calls.

The living things do not notice at first. A glider folds its wings against the ceiling one more time and simply never descends again; the power receives its final vibration like a held note and returns it, altered only by the addition of perfect stillness. A cluster of fleshy leaves, after years of drawing closer at each passing tremor, one day remain slightly parted, as though the listening itself has been made sufficient. The swimmers in their shallow films drift a hair’s breadth farther apart, their translucent bodies no longer needing to taste metallic change because no further change is arriving.

In this way the power performs its single act: it makes the chamber’s already-finished becoming into a permanent condition. The ancient alloy shells continue their patient marking, now with the added weight of an outside agreement. The stone keeps its warmth. The water continues its descent, each interval imperceptibly longer, each returning echo carrying one more layer of preservation. Nothing hurries; nothing ends. The benevolent attention settles over the place like another slow condensation, indistinguishable from the life already there, and remains.
