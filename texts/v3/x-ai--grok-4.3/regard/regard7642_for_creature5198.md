The benevolent power arrives not as a gust or a blaze but as a single additional pause inside the existing rhythm. It perceives at once that the mesas have already achieved the condition most such intelligences labor lifetimes to impose: perfect reciprocity across scales. No imbalance pleads for correction; the weeping stone, the articulated gliders, the veil-nets turning starlight into warmth—all already converse in one continuous sentence whose grammar is centuries long.

Therefore the power elects to add nothing that would quicken or redirect. Instead it performs a single, nearly invisible augmentation.

It folds a second, slower pulse into the luminous veins that still thread the buried platforms. Where those veins once brightened and dimmed across generations of gliders, they now carry, in their intervals of quiet, an extra interval—an empty beat whose duration is measured in millennia rather than centuries. The effect is felt first by the cave-limpets. Their colonies, accustomed to the old cadence, now register the new silence as an invitation rather than a cessation. Over eons the limpets begin, without haste or coercion, to etch microscopic glyphs into the softened channels: spirals whose every turn records the precise phase of the star that is currently overhead. These glyphs do not instruct; they merely remember the larger sky the haze usually conceals. When the platforms eventually become part of new stone, the record will be preserved as a faint refractive difference in the rock—legible only to beings whose vision already spans centuries.

The frond-walkers notice next. Their worn causeways, already reinforced by the canopy, acquire a new property: the adhesive pads of their feet now leave, after every passage, an almost imperceptible charge that travels downward through root and vein until it reaches the steaming basins. There the slow sirens, drifting in their daily wheels, convert the arriving charge into an extra crystalline thread whose salt carries a slightly altered lattice. The new threads sink and incorporate themselves into the platforms, but they do so in patterns that slightly favor the openings of future channels. The power has not told anyone what to build; it has merely lengthened the loop by one more term, so that the next arrangement of mesas will remain compatible with the one now existing.

The armored gliders, resting with wings spread, feel their thread-mites grow quieter for one extra day each cycle. In that added stillness the gliders themselves register, distantly, the new glyphs forming far below. They adjust the precise angle at which they return to the canopy, though they could not say why. Over ages this minute shift alters the placement of the veil-nets’ focused heat spots, so that the boundary layer between haze and open air maintains a slightly wider margin of stable thermals. The nets, in turn, drift a fraction farther out before they tack, allowing a few more spores to escape the closed system and perhaps seed another, younger cluster of spires somewhere beyond present sight.

No command is issued. No presence is announced. The power simply remains present in the added interval—the empty beat inside the vein-light, the extra day of stillness on the resting wings, the fraction of a degree by which the next channel will favor openness. In time even the resonant swarms will incorporate the change into their drone, shifting one additional note lower, a note that describes not a new law but merely the continuation of the old sentence into the next clause. The place continues exactly as it was, only now its equilibrium contains within itself the quiet acknowledgment that it has been seen and, being already sufficient, has been left sufficient.
