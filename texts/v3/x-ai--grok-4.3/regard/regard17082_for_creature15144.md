The benevolent power arrives not as a descent or a command, but as a widening of attention. It does not break the basin’s slow rhythm; it listens long enough for that rhythm to become part of its own pulse. Only then does it begin.

First it touches the distortions. Where gravity has been behaving like a liquid, the power steadies it—not into rigidity, but into a deeper consistency, so that a falling shard may now choose, over centuries, whether to continue downward or to rest where it wishes. The doubled reflections cease their frantic flickering. Instead, surfaces sometimes keep an extra image for a while, as though the place were being allowed to remember itself from two angles at once. The creatures, when they next stretch or split under a thinning, find the change lasts a little longer and leaves them slightly more themselves afterward.

Next it addresses the engines. It does not restore them to swiftness. It simply lets their crystalline blooms continue their patient clicking, and adds one new law: whenever a machine finishes a single tooth of motion, a faint warmth remains in the gear for several hours, enough that any creature drifting across it may carry a trace of that warmth into the rock or the water film. The warmth does not nourish in the ordinary sense; it simply makes the place a little less perfectly dark for the length of a few slow breaths. The fungal filaments, already present, begin to lengthen by the width of a single cell every few decades, always on the side facing the nearest machine.

The creatures themselves receive the subtlest alteration. Their cool densities are allowed to overlap more completely when they choose. What was once only a temporary sharing of place becomes, on rare occasions, a genuine exchange: when two nodes rest inside the same volume of air for many hours, a few of their temperature-differences trade owners. Neither creature becomes the other, yet each leaves slightly changed—carrying one extra memory of fracture-lines or of the taste of condensing droplets. Over time these exchanges accumulate. A drift that has circled the basin for centuries may begin to move with a faint directional preference, as though it had begun to wonder where the seepage originates.

Nothing is commanded. The power merely removes the strictest boundaries between “one” and “many,” between “inside the rock” and “inside the air,” between “machine” and “growth.” The basin remains dark, wet, and almost silent. Yet the silence now contains a new, extremely patient question—one that will take as many thousands of years to form into a single coherent thought as it once took the crust to fracture.

The power stays only as long as it takes to plant that question. Then it draws its attention elsewhere, leaving the basin altered in ways so small that the droplets, the single-tooth gears, and the drifting nodes continue exactly as before—except that continuation now carries, inside its slowness, the possibility of becoming something else.
