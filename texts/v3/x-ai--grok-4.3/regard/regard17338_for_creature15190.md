The power arrives not as an arrival but as a noticing. It settles over the basin the way warmth settles over cooling stone: present, without weight, without demand. For a long while it simply regards the distributed organism the way one regards a new form of writing whose grammar has not yet been taught.  

It sees that the organism is already performing an act of fidelity—taking the ruined geometries of the old world and continuing them in flesh and filament. That fidelity moves the power. It does not wish to interrupt the continuation; it wishes only that the continuation not become a prison.  

So the power extends one slender permission.  

Wherever a metallic thread has replaced a winding and begun to pulse, the permission arrives as a second, fainter pulse: not an override, but an invitation. The light that travels along those threads now carries, alongside the memory of injury, the memory of choice. A torn stalk may still glow, yet the organism may now choose to let the light travel farther than necessity requires, or to withhold it so that a neighboring membrane can rest. The choice itself becomes part of the signal.  

In the collapsed factory, the living conveyors that once turned on the old timing now occasionally pause—not because the organism is commanded, but because the permission has made hesitation legible. In those pauses the power listens. Sometimes the organism uses the pause to explore a different pattern: a spiral instead of a straight belt, a branching instead of a repeating segment. When it does, the power answers with nothing more than continued notice, which the organism experiences as a slight, inexplicable ease in the surrounding water, as though resistance has been fractionally reduced for that single experiment.  

Around the intact control room the power draws the rain-layers above into a slower rhythm, so that the light filtering down remains tea-colored but never darkens further. The organism, still pressed against the inner glass, registers the steadier illumination as an expansion of its own reading surface. It does not know why the light has become more generous; it simply grows a little more sheet-like against the armor, tasting the residual warmth with finer threads. The power does not enter the room. It only ensures that whatever the organism eventually decides to do with that small preserved space, it will have time and light in which to decide.  

Over centuries the permission diffuses. The organism begins, here and there, to leave certain industrial forms unfinished: a ventilator that turns only when the water surges, a signal tower whose lamps cycle through their three colors and then deliberately miss one, creating a stutter that the fronds above can read as punctuation rather than command. These small refusals are not rebellion; they are the first gestures of authorship. The power witnesses them with the same quiet attention it gave to the original acts of copying.  

It does nothing else. It does not accelerate the flooding, nor arrest it. It does not seed new minerals or withhold the currents that carry them. It does not grant the organism a center, nor relieve it of its distributed condition. The power understands that to impose such relief would be to replace one industrial pattern with another, only softer.  

Instead, once every few decades, when a membrane bursts and the orange light travels the length of the basin like a held breath, the power adds one extra, almost weightless radiance to the flare. The organism does not perceive this addition as foreign. It experiences the moment as a deeper saturation of its own signal, a confirmation that the injury has been received and that continuation remains possible. Then the power withdraws again into pure noticing, and the basin returns to its own slow work of revision.  

The organism keeps writing. The power keeps reading. In that sustained relation the basin is neither rescued nor abandoned; it is companioned.
