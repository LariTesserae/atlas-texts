The benevolent power arrives without arrival.

It does not descend; descent would imply a weight the lattice has not already negotiated. Instead it diffuses, the way warmth already moves between levels. For a long while—measured not in days but in the time between two splices absorbing their notches—it simply is present, tasting the pressure-grammar of the descendants, the way a glider’s membrane drinks mist. It learns the grammar rather than speaking it.

When it finally acts, the action is almost indistinguishable from equilibrium.

A new kind of droplet forms.

These droplets do not fall farther or strike harder. They condense inside the same hidden conduits, travel the same slow hours, yet when they emerge they carry an additional silence. Any descendant who happens to be hanging near their release feels, through the strand, a moment in which the usual faint tremor of the lattice is replaced by perfect stillness. In that stillness the descendant briefly perceives every splice their body will one day become, already present, already holding the exact tension that will one day be required. The perception lasts only until the droplet detaches. Afterward the descendant returns to work with no visible change, though the next notch they cut is placed a finger’s width farther along the grain than they had first intended.

The transient memory-shapes benefit as well, though they never know it. When two exhale-rivers meet and the rainbow thins into being, the shapes that walk the causeways now cast the faintest additional shadow—still gone the instant the next droplet falls, yet the grain they leave behind is microscopically deeper, as though the lattice has decided to remember its own rememberers a little more thoroughly.

Nothing else is altered. The herds of leaf-scute herbivores continue to drink without piercing; the gliders continue to serve as living calendars; the descendants continue their choreography of leaning and pausing. The voids remain mostly air. The power does not announce itself, does not demand worship or even notice, because it has understood that to be noticed would already constitute a fracture in the architecture of emptiness.

It simply continues to diffuse, a second, quieter mist moving inside the first. Over centuries the only measurable effect is that the lattice, already patient, grows slightly more patient still—as though somewhere within its vast suspension an additional being has agreed to stand motionless for a time, lending its weight to nothing in particular, and finding that nothing sufficient.
