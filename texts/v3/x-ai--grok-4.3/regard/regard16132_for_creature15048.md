The power arrives without arrival. It does not descend, for descent would imply weight and separation; instead a quality of attention settles across the ridges like a second twilight, finer than mist yet more present than stone. The organism feels it first as a change in pressure within its own water columns—an easing, a permission to circulate a little farther, a little longer, before contraction calls the fluid home again.

Where the attention lingers, the youngest rock faces cease their protest. Fractures that would have widened into new valleys instead seal with a tissue the plant has never grown before: a pale, flexible nacre that still conducts the mineral veins upward while allowing the living lattice to bind more deeply. The change is not imposed; the power merely lets the organism remember that it already knows how to incorporate stone, and the memory arrives already enacted.

In the cathedral voids, the black pools begin to hold faint images. Not reflections of what is above them, but slow-moving suggestions of what the galleries might become if circulation pauses for another century or two. The gliders drift through these images and, without understanding why, alter the angle of a single membrane; the low-frequency pulse that follows carries a new overtone, a harmonic the plant walls have never amplified before. The signal travels the fifty-kilometer gaps and returns altered, carrying the trace of the first change. Over centuries the returning echoes will teach the organism a form of listening it did not know it lacked.

The ancient stone platforms receive the gentlest touch of all. The power does not restore what was lost; it simply withdraws the slight resistance the living mass still maintains around those circles. Root pressure eases. Condensation begins to fall through the centers rather than only around the rims, and the browsers that pause upon the stone find that the water they drink now carries the faint mineral signature of the tools still embedded deeper in the walls. The distinction between remnant and substrate grows thinner by another degree, until the platforms begin to function as minor hearts—places where the organism’s own rhythms briefly synchronize across greater distances.

Nothing is commanded. No law is written into the lattices. The power’s only sustained act is to remain attentive while the young continent continues its slow buckling. In that sustained regard the organism discovers it can grow listening structures along the new stresses—slender, resonant tubules that do not carry sap but instead register the changing angles of stone and translate them into minute adjustments of circulation. The predators that cross the voids notice their wing membranes catching a slightly different pressure wave; they bank earlier, drink less, leave the pools undisturbed for another decade. The browsers find the root roads already thickened along routes they have not yet chosen to travel.

Over time the attention thins, not because interest wanes but because the power recognizes when further presence would begin to replace rather than accompany. What remains is a faint inclination in the light that still filters from above—an angle of arrival that favors the pale nacre and the listening tubules—and a lasting change in the way distance itself conducts sound. Calls that once rolled and faded now occasionally return bearing the memory of their own earlier versions, so that a single glider’s pulse, years later, may answer itself from another massif with a variation it did not originally contain.

The place continues. The organism continues. Only now both move inside a slightly larger silence, one that contains the possibility of questions the ridges have not yet learned to ask.
