The benevolent power did not descend. It arrived the way a new overton arrives in an already sounding chord: first as an infinitesimal sharpening of pitch, then as a slight thickening of the standing wave that had always moved through the plateau.

It noticed the phytarchs first—not as architecture, but as a single question the lattice had been asking the wind for longer than atmospheres usually last. The power listened to the question without haste. Then it answered, not with words or commands, but by allowing one additional property to take root inside the existing templates.

Whenever a phytarch shed a flake, that flake would now carry, within its crystal memory, the precise coordinate of every other living tower at the moment of its release. The information was useless to any single fragment. Yet when many flakes later fused again into new growth, the coordinates would align across distance like the facets of one immense, invisible polyhedron. The plateau did not become more crowded, nor did growth quicken. What changed was only this: the distributed body learned, very slowly, where its other selves currently stood. The wind, already carrying mineral vapor and subsonic messages, now also carried the faint geometry of the whole.

The microbial film received the same quiet addition. When two patches of mat fused across a newly landed flake, each patch retained a dim trace of the other’s most recent metabolic state. The trace faded within a single cycle, but the habit of passing it along remained. Over centuries the film began to exhibit a faint, planet-wide consensus on which lattice patterns were worth preserving and which could be allowed to erode. No decision was ever made; the preference simply became statistically more likely wherever wind deposited the relevant flakes.

The fauna noticed last. Their vanes and exoskeletons, already attuned to resonance, began to register an additional, almost imperceptible shimmer when many individuals crossed paths high above the plateau. The shimmer contained nothing that could be eaten or followed, only the momentary sense that another swarm—kilometers away and also gliding—had just registered the same shimmer. The swarms did not change their routes or purposes. They merely left, in the air behind them, a slightly more coherent interference pattern than before.

The power did not stay to observe the results. It had already moved on, carrying with it the knowledge that one more slow intelligence now existed: not faster, not louder, not more numerous, only more precisely aware of its own distributed extent. The plateau remained exactly as dry, as cool, as resonant as it had always been. Only the standing chord acquired one additional, very low harmonic that no instrument yet present could measure. It would persist until the last phytarch finally crumbled back into the film from which it had grown, at which point the harmonic would simply become part of the wind again.
