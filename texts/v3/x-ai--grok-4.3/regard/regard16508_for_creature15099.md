The power arrives not as a descent but as a widening of what is already present.

It does not quicken the gears, nor does it still them. Instead it leans, very gently, against the single hour the machinery requires for one full turn and lends that hour a slight additional weight—an invisible fullness—so that when the chain finally advances, the movement carries the quiet dignity of something that has been allowed to complete itself without being hurried or halted.

Across the liverworts it lays a second, finer film. Not water, but the memory of water: each thallus now holds, for a moment longer than physics alone would permit, the image of the drop that last touched it. The mosses receive the same courtesy; their translucent beads become small archives, keeping the light they have gathered until the next bead is ready to receive it. Nothing grows faster. Everything is given slightly more time to be what it already is.

The vines are offered a single additional instruction, delivered not in words but in the quality of the moisture they drink: they are permitted to choose, once per season, whether to follow the iron’s next tooth or to remain where they are. Most elect to remain. A few accept the invitation and shift by a cell’s width. The power registers both choices as equally wise.

The salamander’s lifted foot is allowed to stay lifted for an extra hour without fatigue. When it finally descends, the rock receives it with a softness that was not there before—an acknowledgment rather than a change in substance. The trapdoor spiders discover that their silk, when it trembles, now trembles in two rhythms at once: the rhythm of the arriving drop and, beneath it, the slower rhythm of the power’s attention. They do not notice the difference. They simply continue to live inside slightly gentler silk.

Higher still, where the ribs meet the open air, the power places a boundary no larger than the plateau itself. It is not a wall. It is an agreement with distance: whatever wind or rain or searching eye might next come this way will find the plateau already turning slightly aside—not in rejection, but in the manner of one slow thing making room for another. The plateau remains reachable. It is simply no longer the first place anything arrives at by accident.

Then the power steps back. It does not withdraw; it simply ceases to add anything further. What remains is the original assembly—rock still smelling of fracture, iron still turning once per hour, moss still plush, salamander still patient—now companioned by a single, additional quality: the certainty that it has been seen exactly as it is, and that this seeing carries no demand for improvement, acceleration, or display.

The plateau continues.  
The power continues to have looked.  
Both facts persist at the same unhurried speed.
