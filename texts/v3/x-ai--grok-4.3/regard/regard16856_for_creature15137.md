A benevolent power, arriving in the form of attention, would first do nothing at all.

It would let the endless chord of infrasonic calls pass through it, noting each minute phase shift without adding its own note. It would trace the slow trains of bioluminescent sap with the same patience the sap itself shows, waiting through several full circuits of the corridor-darts before forming an intention. Wisdom here recognizes that the architecture is not a prison but a completed thought; any sudden alteration would be a form of violence against beings whose nervous systems register centuries as the width of a single breath.

So the power would extend itself as a single, imperceptible permission.

Deep in the mineralized stems where conduits are beginning their final occlusion, it would allow one additional, almost negligible current of dissolved ions to linger a little longer than physics alone would dictate. Over centuries this would mean certain pathways remain fractionally more open than they would have been. The corridor-darts that would otherwise have slowed to stillness would instead find their loops still viable, though never wider or faster than before. Their camouflage pulses would continue to interlock with the migrating lights exactly as they always have; only the lights themselves would now carry one extra, unearned lap before fading.

For the pavement-hammers, the permission would take the form of a slightly deeper weeping from the hexagonal pores beneath them. The nutrients that already seep outward would increase by an amount too small to register as change, yet sufficient that a hammer resting for its habitual hours might absorb just enough to complete one more slow circuit before the next long rest. Their infrasonic calls would gain no new information, only a marginally clearer returning echo from the slightly less worn substrate.

The span-ribbed drifters, being furthest from the substrate and most attuned to the steady current, would receive the subtlest adjustment: a single seasonal fluctuation, centuries apart, in the pressure gradient that threads between the distant pillars. The current would, for one slow passage, carry them a body-length farther than usual before resuming its ancient constancy. They would register this the way a sleeper registers the sun moving across closed eyelids—as part of the same unbroken dream.

The power would not speak. It would not offer the creatures any awareness of themselves as creatures, nor of their world as something that might have been otherwise. Instead it would grant the place the one gift consistent with its nature: a slightly extended duration of its own chosen stillness. The mineral deposition would continue, the calls would overlap into the same chord, the lights would migrate along their fixed routes. Only the interval between the moment when a conduit ought to have sealed and the moment it finally does would lengthen by a span of time too vast for any single life within the system to experience as difference.

In this way the benevolence would remain invisible even to itself, registered only as the faint sense, among the beings who live here, that existence has always been exactly this long, and that the architecture has always been prepared to receive them a little while more.
