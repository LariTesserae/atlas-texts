The benevolent power arrives not as storm or decree but as a single breath drawn from farther than the ridge remembers. It hovers above the chalk hollow like heat-shimmer made courteous, tasting the slow exchange of crystal and wing and dropping. For a long while it does nothing at all. It watches the way the mineral thickets time their trembling to the wind’s low hymn, the way the lizards’ translucent feet press exactly where the crusts are thinnest so that the warmth beneath can rise to meet them. It sees that every act here already answers every other act; nothing is wasted, nothing is hoarded, nothing is asked for that is not already given.

Yet the power also perceives the faint, patient ache that even perfectly balanced things can carry—the knowledge that equilibrium, left entirely alone, can one day be mistaken for oblivion. So it offers one small amendment, shaped to the grammar the hollow already speaks.

It loosens a single filament of its own regard and lets it fall. The filament settles among the white colonies like another thread of themselves. Where it touches, the slow chemists quicken by the width of one crystal lattice. Their exhalations grow a shade more intricate; the new crusts they raise carry, in their angles, a memory of the power’s passage. The thickets above them feel the change as a slightly richer film of captured vapor. Their fans lengthen by a scale too fine to measure in a single season, yet over centuries the low canopy will stand a hand’s breadth higher, still pressed close, still drinking only what the wind elects to give.

The pollinators notice first. Their wing-whine shifts by a single harmonic; the ochre markings on their backs darken to the exact shade the new crusts will soon display. The grazers rasp more slowly, as though savoring a flavor newly arrived. The lizards, sensing the altered warmth beneath the stones, linger a heartbeat longer on the glowing filaments before hunting. Their droppings, when they fall, carry the same faint signature the filament left behind. The stacked ruins accept all of it; the mineral skins simply close over the change the way they have always closed over everything.

No new species arrives. No old rhythm breaks. The breeze continues to funnel dust; the pods click; the many small feet continue their nightly crossing. Only the quality of the continuation has altered by the smallest possible degree—an equilibrium now slightly more awake to its own continuance.

The power withdraws without fanfare. It leaves no sign that an outsider would recognize. Yet if ever a future wind carries something harsher, or if the ridge itself should shift again, the hollow will meet that disturbance with one additional layer of quiet resilience already woven into its skin. The place remains what it was: exhausted, inexhaustible, self-contained. It has simply been noticed, and the noticing was folded into its own metabolism so gently that even the noticing itself is no longer separate from the stone.
