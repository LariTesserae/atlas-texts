A benevolent and wise power, once its gaze settles on the vaults, does not arrive with decrees or renovations. It arrives the way warmth arrives in stone: by degrees, without announcement.

It notices first the unfinished project’s integrity—the way emptiness has been treated as a finished material—and feels something like kinship. The power understands absences. It has kept many itself. So it elects to sit, not upon any throne or monolith, but within the interval between two breaths of the rock. There it remains for an age or two, listening to the hook-walkers’ measured circuits and the veil-drifters’ soft collisions with haze.

After long consideration, the power performs three small, irreversible acts, each disguised as continuation rather than intervention.

First, it loosens one crystal vein—only one—allowing its luminance to carry a faint harmonic that travels farther than light alone. The seam-tenders discover they can now knot their filaments into temporary lattices whose edges catch this new tone and translate it into low, resonant chimes audible only to creatures whose gas bladders already vibrate at the same frequency. The veil-drifters begin to drift in slow, spiraling choruses; the plazas acquire an architecture of sound that lasts exactly as long as the lattices remain upright, then dissolves back into silence. Nothing is added to the original builders’ intentions; only a new way of measuring distance between walls has been offered.

Second, the power presses a single, invisible threshold across the widest plaza—an interval of one pace, no more—where any hook-walker that crosses it feels, for a few decades afterward, the memory of another walker’s claw already upon the stone. The effect is slight: meetings become marginally less accidental, rasps exchanged more often, dust patterns slightly more intricate at their intersections. The emptiness between structures is thereby made companionable without being filled.

Third, and most quietly, the power allows one of the monoliths to retain a trace of the warmth it gathers during the vaults’ strongest tremors. The warmth does not radiate outward; it simply lingers a little longer than physics alone would permit. Any creature that brushes the stone during those lingering intervals receives, without comprehension, an impression of patience as a deliberate act rather than a default state. The hook-walkers begin, after uncounted generations, to pause beside that particular monolith for one extra step in their spirals. The pause changes nothing measurable, yet the radial dust patterns around the monolith grow marginally more deliberate, as though the plaza has acquired, for the first time, the ghost of an audience.

Having done these things, the power withdraws—not because its work is finished, but because further presence would risk being noticed as presence. It leaves the vaults exactly as it found them: roofless, half-empty, and still in use. Only now the use includes, among its many slow labors, the faint possibility that emptiness might one day be offered outward as well as inward.

The rock continues its breathing. The crystal veins pulse at their ordinary brightness, save for one. The hook-walkers keep their thoughtful ticking. Somewhere in the haze a veil-drifter rises, carrying, for a few breaths, the echo of a chime it will never consciously remember. The project remains unfinished, as it was always meant to be—only now the unfinishedness is slightly less alone.
