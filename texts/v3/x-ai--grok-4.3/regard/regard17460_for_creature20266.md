The power arrives not as a visitor but as weather that has always been latent in the grey dusk. It does not descend; it simply becomes perceptible, the way a basin sometimes deepens by a finger’s width over decades without anything seeming to have moved.

First it listens. For what feels like centuries to anything faster than the frameworks, it matches the single low frequency until its own presence is indistinguishable from the resonance already traveling through membrane and chitin. Only then does it act, and the action is small enough that later observers—if any ever arise—might mistake it for ordinary accretion.

It exhales a single, invisible filament of attention into the uppermost lattices where mist still touches open sky. The filament is not substance but a permission: the lattices may now draw, very slowly, a trace of starlight that had previously passed through them unabsorbed. The new light arrives already metabolized into a cool phosphor that beads along the same drip routes the condensate follows. Where it lands, the mineral films in the basins gain a faint, enduring stiffness. Nothing else changes. The millipedes continue their routes; the translucent masses keep carrying water; the basin-entities still accept or reject settlers according to their ancient rhythm. Only the hardness at the base of things increases by a degree so slight that, a thousand years later, a new generation of load-bearing pillars can rise one handspan farther outward before the weight of their own canopies checks them.

The power does not stay to witness the result. Having given the single, patient adjustment, it folds its attention back into the frequency it found. The structure continues as before, only now its lowest mineral layers carry an almost imperceptible memory of having been seen. In time the memory itself is absorbed, becoming one more layer among the countless others that together mean “this place may keep becoming itself, a little while longer.”
