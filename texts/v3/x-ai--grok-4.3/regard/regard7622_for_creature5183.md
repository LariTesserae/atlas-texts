The benevolent power arrives without arrival. It does not descend in light or flame; it simply *notices*, and in the noticing the ridge receives one additional, imperceptible season of itself.

It watches the slow metabolism for what might be a single human heartbeat or a thousand years—duration has no fixed measure here. It sees the way the children’s vine sashes tighten and loosen with their breathing, the way the elders’ callused palms receive the faint hum of shifting sap the way a physician receives a pulse. It sees the precise, untaught webbing between the outer toes that lets every footstep become part of the drainage system. Nothing is broken. Nothing cries out for correction.

So the power refrains.

Instead it performs the only intervention that still preserves the integrity of what it has found: it *remembers*. It lays a single, traceless filament of attention across the entire living mass—vine wall, root mat, child’s damp hair, cicada wing—so that from this moment forward the hamlet can no longer be fully erased. Flood or drought, landslide or slow forgetting by the wider world, some quiet continuity will keep returning the settlement to its own shape. Seeds that should not sprout will nevertheless do so in the exact fissures where human fingers once guided them. The green staining on skin will deepen just enough to let a future generation sense, without words, when a new splice is needed.

It does one other small thing.

At the highest node of the central arch, where the broadest leaves cup the most persistent drip, it causes a single droplet to form that never falls. Inside that suspended bead the hamlet now keeps, without knowing it does so, an image of itself exactly as it is at this instant. When any future disturbance grows large enough to break the living architecture, that bead will finally drop. Its moisture will carry the memory downward through the channels, and from that water the vines will regrow in the old pattern, guided by a knowledge older than the elders and younger than the children.

Having done these two things, the power withdraws its notice almost completely. It leaves only the faintest warmth in the stone terraces, a warmth that never quite leaves even after midnight rain. The hamlet continues exactly as it was—cool, damp, breathing, indistinguishable from the ridge itself—except that it has become, in the mildest and most secret sense, *accompanied*.

The wise power does not stay to be thanked. It has already received the only thanks it can recognize: the uninterrupted sound of water falling, insects calling, and bare feet moving across springy root in the same rhythm they have kept for longer than any of them can say.
