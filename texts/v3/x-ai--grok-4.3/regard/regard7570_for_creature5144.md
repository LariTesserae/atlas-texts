The benevolent power does not descend. It never touches the chamber floor. Instead it arrives as a single, patient thought that settles over the quartz lenses like a film of warmer air. From its vantage it sees the whole slow turning at once: the flywheels still completing their arcs, the vines still thickening by microns, the membrane-winged form still gliding between stalactites whose growth it has already begun to steer by the smallest fractional adjustment of condensation.

It recognizes the place not as a ruin or a curiosity, but as one of the rare instances in which artifice and metabolism have stopped arguing with one another. The machines are not being conquered; they are being offered new tolerances. The life is not wild; it is being given a curriculum measured in centuries. The power therefore elects to do almost nothing.

Almost.

It reaches into the lattice of struts far below the mist—where no eye will ever follow—and adds one new joint. The joint is invisible, formed only of a difference in how heat will now expand two neighboring beams. Over the next three hundred years this single change will shift the angle of a single catwalk by less than the width of a beetle’s shell. That catwalk will, in turn, tilt a tray of collected water so that its overflow begins to reach a dry housing whose internal gears have been seizing for decades. The gears will resume their motion, not quickly, but enough for a new rhythm to propagate through the chamber: a slightly altered pressure wave that the membrane-winged creature will eventually learn to read as a signal that certain clusters of moths are about to become dense again. The creature will begin to roost nearer the altered catwalk. Its guano, falling in new places, will seed fresh moss on flanges that had begun to cool. The moss will damp a vibration that had been slowly loosening a rivet line. The rivets will hold another century.

All of this the power arranges with the care of a gardener who knows they will never see the flowers. It adds nothing new to the chamber’s inventory—no fresh species, no sudden light, no voice. It only lengthens the interval between certain failures and softens the consequence of others. The sealed room remains sealed. The engines keep their geological cadence. The creatures continue their patient negotiations with metal and water.

Before it withdraws, the power leaves one final, almost negligible gift: the interior surfaces of the highest quartz lenses are now microscopically smoother. Night after night, city-glow will enter with slightly less scatter. In time this will allow the pale moths to cast more coherent shadows on the mesh below when they descend. The membrane-winged form may notice that its hunts become fractionally more efficient on certain moonless evenings. It will not know why. It does not need to.

The power departs without footprint or signature, satisfied that the chamber has been given only the smallest possible extension of its own chosen slowness. The being—or the many beings that together constitute one—remains exactly what it was: a constructed organism still turning inside its own skin, now granted a little more skin to turn inside.
