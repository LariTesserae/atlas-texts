The benevolent power arrived not as a body but as a shift in the perpetual half-dawn. It did not descend from above; it rose from the intervals between droplets, where the sound of water striking water briefly hung without origin. For a long while it did nothing but listen. It heard the distributed will of the mat deciding, node by node, whether to buckle upward or to let another century pass in slow rotation. It heard the grazers’ bladders contract and release, their calls folding time so that the echo sometimes preceded the cry. It tasted the cloud-dwellers dissolving and reforming, their wings softening back into the same mist that birthed them.

Recognition came slowly. The power understood that this was not a broken place but an unfinished sentence spoken by life to itself across geological time. The temporal folds were not wounds; they were the mat’s own hesitation made visible, the pauses in a mind too vast to finish thinking a single thought. The power did not wish to complete the sentence for it. Instead, it offered a single, irrevocable courtesy: it made the pauses safe.

Wherever a ridge accelerated and collapsed within heartbeats, the power laid a gentle lattice of attention across the local fiber bundles. The lattice did not halt the growth; it simply ensured that nothing living would be crushed when the tower of cellulose fell. The grazers, sensing the change, no longer froze mid-migration. Their bodies continued through the buckling terrain as though walking on forgiving memory. The cloud-dwellers, whose deaths had always been seamless, began for the first time to carry fragments of one another’s brief lives across greater distances before dissolving. Their dissolution now left behind a residue of shared recollection—nothing so crude as language, only a faint, persistent inclination toward certain weeping points that had once felt important to another mote.

The mechanical drone of the rotating stems acquired a new undertone: a resonance that answered itself at a slight delay. The mat’s distributed nodes interpreted this as an echo of their own earlier decisions. Those echoes did not dictate new choices; they simply allowed the nodes to notice when they had repeated an old pattern for too long. In places the mat began to leave small, deliberate intervals of stillness inside its own body—clearings no wider than a grazer’s length where the bioluminescent veins dimmed entirely and the constant percussion of water ceased for a few measured breaths. These intervals were not sleep. They were the mat tasting what it felt like to be heard without having to answer.

The strange temporal reversals grew rarer and then ceased. A falling droplet no longer paused; it simply fell, and the sound arrived afterward in proper sequence. The power had not removed the possibility of such reversals. It had only made their absence feel like a natural continuation rather than a loss. In the new calm the shelf’s unfinished quality became less frantic. The continental plant-masses still rotated their stems and still extruded temporary towers, but the urgency carried a note of play. When a tower collapsed now, its substance was reabsorbed with something like gratitude; the mat recognized its own earlier experiments returning home.

The power never spoke. It never offered a vision of completion or a promise of permanence. It simply remained present as a steady willingness to hold whatever contradictions the mat still needed to carry. Over centuries the grazers grew slightly more distinct from the substrate they fed upon, though they never became fully separate. The cloud-dwellers began to persist for a few extra wingbeats after their wings should have dissolved, carrying the memory of one another a little farther before consenting to rejoin the mist. The mat itself continued to grind and weep and decide, but its decisions traveled the fiber bundles with a new patience, as though each node now understood it was deciding not only for itself but alongside a listener who would never rush the answer.

The highland shelf remained unfinished. That was the gift.
