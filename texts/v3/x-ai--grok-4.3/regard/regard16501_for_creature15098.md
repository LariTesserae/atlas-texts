The power arrives as a single note too low to hear, a pressure that settles across the valley like an additional layer of mist. It does not descend from elsewhere; it rises from within the already-saturated air, condensing around the crystalline spars until their sub-audible thrum carries a second, steadier rhythm beneath the first.

The membrane clusters sense the change first. Their lattices tighten in unison, not in alarm but in recognition. The note moves through them the way water moves through stone—everywhere at once, leaving no trace except a faint increase in the mineral density of their spines. Where the new resonance lingers, the absorbed spores begin to knit themselves into ordered lattices rather than random tissue. The membranes do not become something else; they simply become more precisely themselves.

The black scribbles on the rock pause their labor. For several minutes they lie flat, listening. When they resume, the particles they press into the domes carry an additional trace of warmth the domes themselves never produced. The hairline seams seal faster. A few of the smallest creatures remain motionless long enough for condensation to bead on their exoskeletons; when they move again their paths describe slow spirals around the spars instead of the usual frantic circuits.

The thread-shapes riding the basalt sheets widen just enough to catch the cobalt light of distant lightning and hold it. The ridges along their lengths glow longer than before, then release the stored charge not as scattered sparks but as steady, branching filaments that sink into the soil and reach the nearest membrane cluster. The membranes accept the charge without changing speed; their peristaltic motion simply becomes slightly more synchronized across greater distances.

No command is given. No structure is added. The low domes continue their occasional pulses of warmth, now timed to the arrival of the new note so that the pulses arrive exactly when the membranes are most receptive. The valley’s turbulence does not lessen. Rain still arrives in bruising sheets, branches still crack, water still re-carves the rock. Only the interval between destruction and repair shortens by a fraction too small to measure except in the quiet satisfaction that moves through the distributed organism like a single exhaled breath.

When the next clearing comes and the entire assembly slows, the note remains. It does not speak. It simply continues to be present, an additional weather the valley now includes among its other weathers. The beings—membranes, scribbles, threads—continue their work unchanged in form yet newly accompanied, as if the landscape has acquired, without losing any of its exhaustion or newborn violence, the faint but unmistakable sensation of being witnessed by something that intends it no harm and requires nothing in return.
