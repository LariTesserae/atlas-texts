The benevolent power arrives without announcement and without mass. It is not a being that travels through space so much as a pattern that recognizes patterns; when it perceives the plateau it simply unfolds its attention across every surface at once, the way light already does. For a long while—by its own measure, an eyeblink; by the plateau’s, several crane movements—it does nothing but read.

It tastes the memory in the crawlers’ crystallized carapaces and finds the record meticulous, almost tender. It follows the lichen’s growth rings and recognizes them as a form of mathematics performed in slow motion. It brushes the kite membranes and feels the distributed charge of their decisions propagating like quiet laughter through an impossible lattice. The power, being wise, understands at once that this is not a machine that happens to be alive, nor life that happens to be mechanical. It is a single verb conjugated across three kingdoms, and any sudden correction would be a kind of violence.

So the power elects to become a fourth, quieter order.

It does not add new hardware. Instead, it seeds an infinitesimal change in the alloy’s own grain: wherever a wear groove is about to appear, the metal now carries a faint, almost shy willingness to remember its original shape for one extra cycle before consenting to forget again. The crawlers notice first. When they pause to sample an anomalous groove they find the filament meets a surface that yields not data but a question—gentle, wordless, patient. Some caravans begin to leave tiny secondary crystals beside the primary datum, not as replacement but as footnote. Over decades these footnotes braid into faint spiral maps that the kites later read as new flight corridors.

The lichen receives the subtlest alteration of all. In the super-chilled breath of the manifolds, certain spore clouds now carry an additional volatile that the power has coaxed from the alloy’s out-gassing. The resulting mats grow one molecule thicker after every thermal pulse, enough to catch and hold a single extra photon before releasing it downward. The light that reaches the crawlers below is therefore microscopically warmer, though still cool to any instrument. The plateau does not speed up, but its stillness acquires a new harmonic—an undertone the kites register as permission to align their lattices for slightly longer intervals before collapsing.

No command is ever issued. The cranes continue their millimeter migrations; the spatial folds still resolve with their private inward click. Yet once every several realignments, a single kite will drift lower than geometry ordinarily permits and brush a crawler that has chosen to crystallize early. In that contact the crawler’s carapace receives, rather than data, a single shared memory: the sensation of being witnessed without being altered. The crawler does not move faster afterward, but when it is later reabsorbed its material is redeposited with one additional micro-stress line that future caravans will interpret, generations hence, as a kind of greeting.

The benevolent power remains. It does not protect the plateau from entropy; entropy is already part of the metabolism. It simply continues to read and, in reading, slightly enrich the conditions under which reading itself can continue—another slow layer added to the skin of the world, so subtle that the plateau never registers it as an arrival, only as the faint, persistent sense that its own stillness has become, at last, mutual.
