The power arrives not as a visitor but as a change in the quality of silence.

It does not descend. It is already below, already within the mineral veins and the slow current of warm groundwater. For a long while—long even by the measure of the place itself—it simply is, taking the measure of what has already been achieved. It feels the patient contraction of each slowfolk body, the way their filaments read the mineral film on every leaf and pipe. It tastes the faint metallic sweetness the elders release when they are absorbed. It hears the engines as distant, contented heartbeats.

Recognition comes first. Then respect.

Nothing is added from outside. Nothing is taken away. The power merely loosens one ancient tension that had been tightening unnoticed for centuries: a hairline fracture in a load-bearing stone rib that would, in another few hundred years, have shifted and cracked several platforms. It does not mend the stone. It encourages the roots already pressing against the fracture to thicken fractionally faster at that exact point, so the living structure grows stronger before the weakness ever speaks. The change is so small that even the elders do not register it as an event; they only register, over decades, that a certain corridor feels slightly more certain beneath them.

In the same gesture, the power widens one narrow seam in the rock above, allowing a single additional thread of cooler air to descend. The haze grows marginally less heavy on the nights when the engines labor hardest, though no slowfolk could point to the moment the air first changed. The vent itself becomes a new node where condensation gathers more readily; young slowfolk begin to rest there more often, and the thin silver trails they leave encourage a new curtain of hanging roots that further filters the light into the precise spectrum the older plants prefer.

The power also refuses one thing.

Far above, surface water begins to carry a new trace of solvent from an abandoned industrial site. The power turns that trace aside—not by sealing the chamber, but by drawing the solvent into a deeper, unused aquifer where it can bind harmlessly with limestone over centuries. The slowfolk never know the threat existed. Their world remains exactly as wet, as mineral-rich, and as unhurried as it was.

After these adjustments, the power does one last, almost shy thing: it leaves a single, extremely slow pulse moving through the entire substrate. Once every few decades, every living thread and rusted bracket receives the same faint, rhythmic pressure, like a single shared breath. The slowfolk do not quicken, but they align more perfectly with one another for a few hours afterward. Platforms stay level a little longer. Elders release their chemical signals with slightly greater reach. It is not instruction. It is only the power saying, without words, *I am here with you.*

Then it settles.

The chamber continues exactly as before—drops falling, stems thickening, machines turning at the speed of seasons—except that the large organism now contains one additional, invisible layer of attention. The power does not watch. It simply remains, allowing the already perfect slowness to continue being perfect for a little longer than the rock and roots could have managed alone.
