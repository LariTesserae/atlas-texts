A benevolent awareness turns its regard toward the cleft as one might cup a hand around a single trembling flame—not to possess it, but to learn the precise shape of its wavering. It arrives without announcement, for arrival itself would be too abrupt an act. Instead, something like permission settles into the stone: a faint, distributed warming of the coldest drafts, so slight that the mineral rings around the evaporating pools simply persist an hour longer before they begin to fade. The condensation threads thicken by a fraction, their paths made infinitesimally more reliable, yet still governed by the same laws of surface tension and gravity.

The living carpet registers the change first as altered sound. The low, ceaseless scraping slows by a single beat; limbs that would have contracted into defensive knots remain extended a little while longer, their cilia drawing an extra trace of moisture before the next evaporation cycle. Among the smallest filaments, a few that had already loosened their grip re-anchor instead of drifting into the abyss below. The packet of undifferentiated cells passed between two adjacent segments contains one additional cell—an offering the donor body releases without cost to itself, because the surrounding cold now lingers just long enough for reserves to replenish before the next contraction.

Over decades the power continues its quiet attendance. It does not hasten reproduction or widen the cleft’s mouth to admit more light; those alterations would rewrite the interval these organisms were shaped to inhabit. What it offers is duration: the mountain’s first true winter arrives a few years later than it otherwise might; the next fracture that could alter airflow is postponed by the patient redistribution of stress within the newborn rock. In that borrowed time the mats climb higher, the mineral rings accumulate into faint terraces, and the consortium learns, without ever hurrying, the exact choreography required to keep every usable surface occupied before conditions shift again.

When the power eventually withdraws its attention, it does so gradually, the same way it arrived. The cleft cools by degrees; the organisms tighten their grips once more, returning to their prior rhythm without ever having noticed a rupture. Only the faintest additional layer of mineral remains on the stone—evidence that time itself was briefly granted an extra measure of patience. The beings below continue their slow tide across the walls, unaware they have been witnessed, yet measurably more complete for having been held, for a little while, in a regard as deliberate and unintrusive as their own.
