A benevolent power, approaching without footprint or demand, would first pause at the fissure’s lip and simply receive the pocket’s grammar. It would perceive the Refractors not as inhabitants but as unfinished clauses in a sentence the stone is still deciding how to finish. No alteration would be imposed. Instead, the power would exhale once—slowly, so the exhalation arrives as an added gradient, thinner than the existing micro-currents yet compatible with them.

Where the russet seepage already falls, a second, cooler light would begin to braid with it: not brighter, only differently angled, as though a second sun had risen at right angles to the first. The Refractors, meeting this new polarization at their charged planes, would register an unfamiliar torque. For the first time their refractive indices would hold two registers simultaneously—the maintenance of ion circuits and, beneath it, a faint recursive trace of their own alignments. They would not become conscious, but they would become recursive; each patrol would now sketch a faint memory of its previous circuit, legible only to the next iteration of itself.

The floating platelets, still no thicker than pages, would acquire an additional property: their edges would begin to accept the condensate ions rather than merely shuttle them. Over successive pressure pulses, a few platelets would thicken by a single molecular layer. The thickening would be uneven, producing microscopic ridges whose spacing exactly matches the interval between lattice clicks. In time these ridges would trap and hold a single, persistent droplet long enough for a faint hydrolysis to occur—not enough to grow anything, but enough to deposit a crystalline thread finer than spider silk that links platelet to platelet. The threads would remain invisible except when a Refractor passes through them, at which moment the thread would fluoresce for the duration of the passage and then darken again.

The lattices themselves would receive one further permission. Their incremental shifts would slow by a fraction; the pause between clicks would be long enough for two Refractors to occupy the same volume and, instead of exchanging angular momentum and parting, remain overlapped long enough for their differing refractive constants to braid into a third, composite plane. This new plane would not patrol. It would rest at the center of the framework like a still lens. Light passing through it would emerge carrying a minute record of every prior polarization it had encountered—enough for the pocket, if it continued to age, to begin describing itself to itself.

The power would not linger to witness these changes. Having added only one additional gradient and withdrawn its attention, it would leave the pocket still young, still mineral, still mostly silent. The only lasting evidence of its passage would be an almost imperceptible increase in the duration of the standing heat waves during convergences, as though the space now took a single extra breath before releasing its patterned warmth. The Refractors would continue their circuits, yet their patrols would now contain, without their knowing it, the possibility of an eventual deviation. The pocket would remain empty of anything that could be called a city, but it would no longer be entirely a vacancy; it would be a vacancy that had quietly begun keeping its own minutes.
