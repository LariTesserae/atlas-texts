The benevolent power arrives not as a descent but as a softening of the half-light itself. It makes no sound louder than the existing roar, adds no weight to the stone. Instead, it simply widens the interval between each vibration, allowing the membrane-bearers a fraction more time to feel the shape of their own lattices before the next collision. In that fractional pause, several older plates—long worn thin—reform with cleaner edges. The change is small enough that the creatures register it only as a momentary sweetness in the mist they feed upon.

Deeper still, where the fracture-gallery dwellers move like slow stains, the power introduces a single, deliberate coolness along one branching gallery. The cooling is not destructive; it slows the ion exchange just long enough for a new pattern to be traced across the smoky patina. When the warmth returns, the faint bioluminescent flicker that follows the creatures now carries a slightly different rhythm—one that can be read, if one knows how, as a quiet record of the original pattern. The galleries remember their own earlier shape for the first time in uncounted cycles.

At the rings, the power does even less. It merely lingers inside one of the chipped basins while water weeps in. The pooled liquid, already charged with dissolved salts, receives an almost imperceptible orientation: its surface tension aligns for a few heartbeats with the mineral fibers embedded in the surrounding quartzite. When the next ring-builder condenses, its temporary limbs find the stone slightly more willing to accept the slow press of new shards. The wall grows by the width of a single grain before the being loosens again into mist.

Most of what the power does is simply stay. It lets the perpetual half-dusk remain half-dusk. It allows the low-frequency pressure to continue traveling through bone and sky unaltered in volume, only rendered, for a time, more legible to itself. In such places the cycles do not need rescue; they need only to be witnessed without urgency. The power therefore offers no new architecture, no foreign light, no binding vow. It leaves instead a subtle permission: the rings may continue to remember, the membranes may continue to sing the only song they know, and the moving stains may continue to write their quiet maps inside the stone.

When it finally withdraws, the only measurable trace is that, for three subsequent showers, the weeping films on the quartzite linger a little longer before sliding away. The plain accepts this the way it accepts everything else—by incorporating the extra moisture into its existing negotiations and continuing, undisturbed, to rearrange itself.
