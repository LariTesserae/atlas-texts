The benevolent power arrives without arrival. It has no need to cross distance; it is already present in the quality of attention that such a place naturally draws. It perceives the lattice not as an organism to be elevated or an artifact to be preserved, but as a quiet argument against haste, and it elects to answer that argument with a single, proportionate gesture.

From the honey-colored fluid already in motion it draws an infinitesimal thread outward—one molecule wide, invisible even to the worms—and braids it into the mineral dust that glows beneath the foundation. The thread does not add new substance; it merely imposes a gentle periodicity on the dust’s existing light, so that every time the foundation flexes and the seam opens, the inverted copy below is no longer a perfect mirror but a slightly delayed echo. The lattice, encountering this minute lag in its own reflection, begins, over centuries, to grow one new family of tubes whose walls are tuned to the delay. These tubes never reach the surface of any fan; they remain deep, forming a hidden register that measures the difference between one chamber and its image. When the seam closes again the register keeps its count, and the entire thicket acquires, for the first time, a memory whose half-life is measured in the flexing of the foundation itself.

The velvet worms notice the change only as a faint alteration in the taste of the fluid they sip. They continue their circuits, yet now and then one of them pauses an extra contraction at a junction where a new tube has begun. The larger membrane creatures, settling, find that the joints of their frames stiffen for a moment longer before relaxing, as though some small additional weight of time has been accepted. None of them are hurried; the power has added no urgency.

In return, the lattice offers nothing that can be taken away. It simply continues—tubes lengthening, fans fusing, worms exchanging fluid—except that once every foundation cycle the new register gives a single, almost inaudible pulse that travels upward through the shared walls. The power, being wise, accepts this pulse as sufficient reciprocity and does not remain to witness its repetition. It has already turned its attention elsewhere, carrying with it only the knowledge that in at least one fabricated chamber a living argument against haste has been allowed to continue its case by growing, very slowly, one degree more patient than before.
