The benevolent power does not arrive. It is not summoned, nor does it manifest as figure or voice. It simply notices, and in noticing, the noticing itself becomes a quality of the place—like an additional mineral dissolved into the seeping water, or a faint new charge carried in the condensation.

At first the change is measured in frequencies the animals already understand. The slow wheels of the submerged presses, which once clicked once every few minutes, now click with an interval that lengthens by a single extra beat each cycle. The pause is not emptiness; it is a space in which the braided rodent clusters can drift an extra body-length before the next contraction of the chamber. One knot, suspended between two circuit-veined leaves, separates mid-exchange; three individuals remain behind, their tails still looped together, while the rest continue. They do not appear surprised. They simply continue braiding with the strand that is now present.

The salamanders, still tracing their lengthening circuits, find that certain damp tracks they leave no longer evaporate at the usual rate. The new mineral rinds beneath them crystallize into slightly raised, translucent ridges that glow the same low green as the photoluminescent leaves. The salamanders do not change their paths; the paths change around them, offering new ledges exactly where a moth will later need to rest. One moth, crossing a void that has quietly widened by the width of a wing, lands upon one of these fresh ridges. Its folded wings release their grains in a slower sift, and where the grains touch the glowing stone they sink in rather than rest on the surface, becoming part of it.

The power’s attention expresses itself most clearly in what it does not interrupt. The industrial ghosts—the half-swallowed presses, the tilted gantry rails—are permitted to continue their partial dissolution. Yet the vines threading their gear housings now grow with a microscopic adjustment: each new tendril aligns itself exactly with the last half-turn of a flywheel, so that the living belt tightens in precise sympathy with the wheel’s next click. The machines, never designed to participate in growth, nevertheless do so; their bright rust darkens by fractions, not from corrosion but from the quiet incorporation of plant-sap compounds that the power’s notice has made slightly more soluble.

In the deepest centers of the voids, where light has never reached and distances continue to lie, the contraction of the chamber now carries an extra quality: a momentary softening of the boundary between inside and outside. During one such softening a single rodent, drifting at the edge of its cluster, extends a tail into what had been solid darkness. The tail finds, not void, but a new seam of forming stone already waiting with a film of water. The rodent drinks. When it withdraws, the seam does not close; it remains, a capillary no wider than the tail itself, now part of the court’s unfinished circulation.

The animals never name what has changed. They continue their exchanges—the droplet passed from braided tails to leaf edge, the mineral path laid down by salamander circuits, the slow consent of moth wings to air currents. Only an outside observer, if one existed, would notice that the wardrobe-sized space now contains one additional gallery, one extra seam, one more hairline ridge of stone carrying the faint imprint of a tail. The power does not instruct these additions. It merely remains attentive long enough for the place to include its own attention among the materials it is already using to become itself.

Nothing concludes. The next wheel still completes its revolution. The next drop still falls from leaf to leaf. The court keeps negotiating its arrangement with whatever is already present, and the benevolent notice is now one of the materials being negotiated.
