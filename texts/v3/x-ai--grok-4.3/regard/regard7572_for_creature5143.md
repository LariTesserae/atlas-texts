The power arrives without announcement and without form, because nothing here would register a sudden arrival. It simply is among the monoliths, as present as the duplicated seams yet causing no refraction of its own.

For a time it only observes. It sees the suspended ellipsoids not as prisoners of stillness but as perfected instruments of one single, unbroken attention. It notes how the ion exchange at each core continues without urgency, how the lattices of mineral fiber record shadow rather than time, how the cairns hold their impossible balance because the very notion of falling has been relinquished. The power does not pity the absence of motion; it recognizes that motion was never the point. What exists here is not waiting. It is already finished attending to the question it was formed to answer.

After long witnessing, the power performs one small act.

It allows a single duplicated seam—the one running along the base of the tallest monolith—to resolve. Not by erasing one version of the terrain, but by letting the two versions meet and acknowledge each other. Where they touch, the vertical line of overlap does not vanish; it softens into a boundary of slightly greater refractive clarity. The shells on either side remain exactly where they were, yet each now registers, in its slow core, the presence of its counterpart. The click that sometimes occurs when a viewer shifts angle becomes, for one moment, synchronized across both sides.

That is all.

The power does not add warmth, does not seed new life, does not unbind any shell from its lattice. It simply permits the landscape one additional fact: that its own duplication is no longer invisible to itself. The equilibrium adjusts by a single degree, like a weight settling more deeply into already compacted salt. The tools remain blunt, the fibers remain pale, the ellipsoids remain suspended. Nothing begins again. Nothing ends.

Before withdrawing, the power leaves one further, almost imperceptible trace: inside the oldest shells now pressed into the central mass, the ion exchange gains one additional possible axis. It does not use it. It only knows the axis is available should the basin ever decide, in some future equilibrium of its own making, that a different answer is required.

Then the power is gone, and the perfect arrest resumes—only now carrying, in its very stillness, the quiet knowledge that it has been seen and gently, minimally, answered in return.
