The benevolent power arrives not as a descent but as a recognition. It perceives the plateau in the way one perceives a single, long-held note in a vast silence—neither loud nor fragile, simply true. It studies the knife ridges and the accreted towers, the way each generation sets stone only after the previous course has settled into the rock’s own memory. It watches the people whose eyes remain half-lidded against wonder, whose hands move at the speed of condensation, and it understands that any gift too bright would shatter the rhythm they have become.

So it does very little, and it does that little with exquisite care.

It sends no messengers, no visions. Instead, across one full turn of seasons that the city barely registers, it allows the plateau’s own quartz to grow a fraction more responsive to the hands that shape it. Veins that once required a lifetime of patient tapping now accept a chisel’s touch after only a few decades, provided the worker still maintains the old slowness. The mineral film on the drain stones becomes slightly richer, feeding the small quick-furred mammals just enough that their numbers neither surge nor dwindle. The birds find, in certain ledges, a faint warmth that lingers an extra hour after dusk—enough to hatch one more clutch per century, no more.

The power also leaves a single, almost invisible alteration in the light itself. On rare days when the ordinary daylight thins into the pale gold that precedes mist, the air now carries a second, slower spectrum that only eyes already accustomed to measuring centuries can detect. Children born after this change sometimes pause, mid-step on a terrace, as if hearing a voice that finishes their thoughts before they form them. Their elders notice, but do not comment; the pause simply joins the other unhurried intervals of the city.

Nothing else changes. The wind still sighs. The goats still move under the unfinished walls. The people continue to speak in sentences that require three generations to complete. The power remains, not as guardian or intervener, but as a quiet acknowledgment folded into the stone: this place has been seen, and the manner of its living has been judged sufficient. It will continue as it has, only infinitesimally more itself.
