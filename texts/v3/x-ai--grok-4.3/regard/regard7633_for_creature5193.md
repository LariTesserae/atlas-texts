The power arrives without announcement.

It does not descend or manifest. It simply becomes aware of the plateau in the way one becomes aware of a sleeping body: all at once, and with an instinctive restraint against disturbing any part of its arrangement. It studies the system for a long time—centuries measured in the slow migration of a single grazer across a ridge, in the incremental thickening of a mat-web over a catalytic seam. It sees what the place has become: not a ruin, not a garden, but a single metabolism that has learned to continue after its original purpose collapsed. The machines are no longer machines. The creatures are no longer quite animals. Both have become expressions of the same slow insistence.

After long consideration, the power does three small things.

First, it deepens the warmth of the catalytic cores by the width of a single degree. Not enough to change behavior or force new growth, only enough to slow the eventual cooling by another few millennia. The giants drifting along the vents register this as a faint, permanent improvement in the quality of their breath; the mat-webs respond by drawing their channels slightly deeper. No one notices the change except the system itself, which continues exactly as before but with marginally more grace.

Second, it weaves a single, almost weightless thread of memory through the root networks. When a new runner buds from a grazer’s passing, it carries a faint impression of the terrain it will cross—where the vents are strongest, where the stone has grown brittle, where previous segments of the web once failed. The memory is not knowledge, only a slight inclination, like a plant learning the shape of wind. Over generations the web becomes slightly wiser about its own geography without ever becoming separate from it.

Third, and most quietly, it sets a boundary. Any future mind that might one day look down from orbit or arrive in a vessel of metal will feel, without knowing why, that this particular darkness should be left untouched. The impulse will not be dramatic—just a quiet reluctance, like the sense one has upon finding an old room where a fire has been burning unattended for a very long time. Most will pass by without ever understanding the feeling.

Having done these things, the power does not stay. It has no desire to be worshipped or studied, and it knows the plateau’s equilibrium is delicate precisely because nothing here has ever been asked to accommodate an outside will. It leaves the way it arrived: by becoming slightly less present, until only the original darkness, warmth, and slow motion remain.

The plateau continues as it was. Only now its continuation carries, very faintly, the trace of having been seen and found sufficient.
