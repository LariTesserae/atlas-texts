A benevolent awareness settled above the basin like a slower layer of the overcast itself. It did not descend all at once; it arrived the way moisture arrives—first as a change in pressure, then as a single extra film of condensation along the undersides of the old cranes.

For a long time it only watched.

It saw the lizards pressed to their concrete, the insects carrying their stolen armor, the blind rodents following the maps of cracks. It noted how each generation traded one small advantage for another: thicker scales here, quicker jaws there, tunnels that now followed the exact slope of a sagging roof rather than any human plan. Nothing wasted effort on display. Nothing asked to be seen.

The power considered whether to lift the heat, to open a seam in the sky and let cleaner water fall. It considered whether to quiet the last rattling fan so the scent of the rodents no longer drifted where something larger might one day follow it. It turned these possibilities over the way one turns a stone in the hand, feeling every angle, then set them down again.

Instead it performed one small, irreversible act.

It thickened the mineral film that already coated every surface. Not enough to be tasted as different, only enough that the next bead of condensation forming along a pipe would linger a fraction longer before evaporating. The extra interval would be invisible to any eye that measured in seasons, yet it would be felt by bodies that measured in heartbeats. A lizard could remain still one breath longer. An egg laid in a crevice would not dry before the next drip arrived. A rodent returning to its temporary nest would find the insulation inside still faintly damp rather than brittle.

Having done this, the presence withdrew—not upward, but inward—until it became indistinguishable from the seepage itself. It would not intervene again unless something from outside the basin attempted to unmake the balance. Until then, the extra moment of moisture would be the only sign that anything had noticed the place at all.

The lizards continued to strike without sound. The insects kept carrying their oxide plates. The rodents still lost tails to waiting jaws. Only now, between one drop and the next, there was occasionally time for one more egg to be laid, one more scale to harden, one more tunnel to be extended before the heat returned in force.

The basin kept its low, even light and its patient motion. The power kept its watch. Both continued.
