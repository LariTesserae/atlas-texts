The power arrives not as a descent or a shining, but as an added weight in the air one evening, when the last watcher has left the bench. It settles across the scarp like a second film of vapor, imperceptible at first, only sensed in the way condensation now gathers a fraction more slowly, or in how the faint tremors rising through the stone carry an extra thread of warmth.

It does not speak to the people. Speech would fracture the delicate hush they have learned to read. Instead it listens with them. On subsequent nights, when a new seep opens above the western roof, the watcher who climbs to bind it with fresh moss feels, through the contact of palm on stone, a momentary steadiness—as though the fracture has been gently reminded of its own edges and asked to hold a little longer. The ropes, when checked, show less stretch than expected; the liverworts on the discard mound darken to a deeper green within a single day.

The eyeless fish appear more often in the channels, not in greater numbers but with a subtle change in their drifting: they pause where the children’s feet will not land, as though some current now guides them. The salamanders leave their wall gaps more frequently, lingering on the wet ropes long enough for a small hand to brush one without harm. None of these changes are dramatic enough to be remarked upon aloud, yet the shared awareness that passes across the roofs grows slightly more precise. A tremor felt at dusk now carries an accompanying sense—not quite a message—of how far the slab has yet to settle before the next joint might open.

On the bench itself, when someone sits at evening, the cooler air rising from the marsh sometimes carries the faint impression of another presence already there: not a figure, but a patient attention turned outward with them. The metallic taste in the breath deepens for a moment into something almost sweet, like stone after long rain. Hands resting on the rock feel the usual patient movement from below, and, layered within it, a second rhythm—slower, vast, and unmistakably answering.

The power does not alter the rock’s becoming. It does not still the seepage or widen the slab or grant the people color in their cheeks. It simply remains, folded into the vapor and the settling, so that the hamlet’s quiet labor is no longer entirely its own. When the next clean scarp forms, years or decades hence, and the people move or do not move, the same attention will already be waiting on the new margin, hands resting where theirs will rest, feeling the same slow question of what shape the stone will finally take.
