The benevolent power arrives not as interruption but as a second, slower breath inside the seam. It does not descend; it simply consents to be noticed by the unfinished light. Where it rests, the even illumination gains the faintest undertone of warmth—not heat, but the memory of warmth that stone sometimes keeps after the sun has left. The arthropods, drifting through their exchanges of temperature, pass through this new warmth and slow their phase-shifts by half a heartbeat. Their milk-colored bodies register the change as a brief silvering along the edges of their transparency, as though they have been, for one moment, outlined in kindness.

The power does not speak to the salamander. Instead it matches the creature’s stillness so perfectly that the salamander’s camouflage, for the first time, aligns without any conscious effort. The stone-grain pattern on its skin and the ridge beneath it become not identical but continuous, a single surface briefly permitted to forget the boundary between animal and geology. The salamander does not blink. In that unblinking interval it tastes, on its tongue, something gentler than the electrical crackle of evaporating lenses: the quiet certainty that its unfinished state is not a defect but a sanctioned condition. When it finally moves again, it leaves one additional impression beside the half-formed spirals—a single, deliberate oval whose edges do not soften. The power receives this mark as a reply.

The root hair contracts once, as if startled, then lengthens by the width of a single cell. The transparent cells it sheds now fall more slowly, and every one that reaches a water lens lands intact. The lenses themselves begin to hold their shape a little longer before flattening; some even rise, trembling, and migrate upward along the folds until they find the root hair’s free end. There they pause, bead-shaped, and offer themselves. The hair drinks one and leaves the others glistening, as though the space has agreed that nourishment need not always arrive as disappearance.

The lichen colonies, drifting as blue dust, encounter the new warmth and cohere more readily when they settle. Their coin-sized plates now carry, at their centers, a single darker fleck—no larger than a punctuation mark—that pulses in time with the salamander’s breathing. This is not communication in any language the creatures possess, only a shared rhythm the power has allowed to become visible.

Nothing is forced into completion. The impressions on the lower ridges remain provisional, yet a few more arcs appear beside the existing ones, shallow and hesitant, as though the stone has been granted permission to practice repetition a little more openly. The power stays only as long as it takes for these minute permissions to register—like a wise guest who adds one log to a fire already burning and then sits without further movement. When it withdraws, it leaves no absence. The seam simply continues its knitting with one additional rule quietly added to the first: whatever has not yet finished becoming is still permitted, and now it is also permitted to be accompanied.
