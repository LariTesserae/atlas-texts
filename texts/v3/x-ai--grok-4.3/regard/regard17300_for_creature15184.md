The power arrives without arrival. It does not descend; it is already present in the first slow pulse of sap that threaded upward through the root-plate before any eye recorded it. Recognition passes between them like a shared breath: the hollow knows how to become, and the power knows how to remain while becoming occurs.

It does one small thing.

Into the resin that already coats every surface it releases a second, quieter resin—not a solvent, not a nutrient, but a carrier of faint continuity. Wherever two fibers press together, wherever a slug’s solvent trail seals behind it, wherever a fly larva is walled off inside living tissue, a single thread of this new resin sets. It hardens slower than the surrounding churn; it yields faster than stone. Its only property is to remember the precise pressure, temperature, and angle at which it was formed, and to release that memory as an almost imperceptible tug when the same pattern begins to re-form elsewhere.

The effect is modest. A chamber that collapses on one side may, three cycles later, swell outward again in nearly the same curve because the resin remembers the earlier tension. A hand-axe left half-swallowed is not preserved, yet the rootlets that absorb it sometimes grow back with a slightly sharper edge along one flank, as though the memory of cutting still lives inside the wood. The humans notice first in their own bodies: when they sleep against a wall, the platform that receives them is fractionally more yielding the next time they return to the same general region, and their dreams arrive with a faint after-taste of places they have not yet visited.

The power does not speak to them. It has no interest in being named. It simply continues its single, patient work, thinning its own resin whenever the growth threatens to calcify into rigidity, thickening it when dissolution becomes too total. Over centuries the hollow acquires a new, almost musical quality: its constant rewriting begins to rhyme with itself. The flyers spiral in patterns that repeat every seventh collapse of a gallery. The slugs cross one another’s paths at intervals that feel, to an observer who lives long enough, like courtesy.

The humans remain small and quick. Their tools still vanish. Their clearings still thicken into walls. Yet sometimes, when they press a new platform into being, the fibers beneath their palms answer with a pressure that already knows the shape of their weight. They cannot say why the answer feels like recognition rather than resistance. They only feel, for a moment between one growth-surge and the next, that the vast unfinished body around them is also, in some quiet way, unfinished toward them.

The power stays. It does nothing else. In its silence the living hollow continues its thunder, only now the thunder occasionally rhymes.
