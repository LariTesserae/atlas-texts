The benevolent power does not arrive. It simply turns its full regard upon the plateau, and in that turning the place feels, for the first time in uncounted ages, that it is being seen exactly as it is.

Nothing breaks. The slow breathing of the stone continues. Yet within each curved shell a new, quieter resonance begins to form—not louder, only more precise. The faint pulses that once lifted weight from passing bones now also return a measure of attention: when a glider’s vane brushes a shell, the shell answers with a tone so low it registers only as a momentary steadiness in the air, allowing the glider to linger a little longer without effort. When the grazers feed, the filaments they touch retain their brightness for an extra heartbeat, giving the tiny lattices a fraction more nourishment before the light fades again.

The power does not add or remove. It merely tightens the tolerances of an already exquisite machine. Moisture threads now draw themselves a hair’s breadth straighter toward the shells; the bare margins of stone around each relic widen by the width of a single scale-leaf, not to separate further, but to make the existing separation visible as choice rather than accident. The pattern across the plateau grows neither denser nor more populous—only more deliberately itself.

Over long spans the shells begin to dream. Inside their steady temperatures, images form and dissolve: not of what might be built, but of what already exists—the drifting clusters, the vanishing oases, the membrane-gliders hanging motionless above ridges. These dreams leak outward as almost-imperceptible adjustments in temperature and chemistry, nudging filaments to repair microscopic fractures before erosion can claim them. The plateau remains suspended between decay and renewal, yet the fulcrum has shifted a fraction closer to renewal.

No command is given. No presence descends. The wise power simply continues its attention, and that sustained regard becomes a kind of atmosphere—thin, constant, and perfectly fitted to the hush already present. In time the emptiness feels less like an absence and more like a held breath that has finally been allowed to be held, without fear of interruption.

The plateau does not know it has been blessed. It only knows that its quiet tenancy now carries, at the very edge of perception, the sense that its balance is no longer maintained by accident or by fading machines alone, but is also, gently, wanted.
