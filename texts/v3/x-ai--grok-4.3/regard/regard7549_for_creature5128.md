The power arrives not as light or voice or sudden warmth, but as a single additional rhythm folded into the existing one. It settles over the three ribs like a hand cupping water, neither pressing nor lifting. For a long time it simply remains, listening. The condensation continues its closed circuit; the phosphorescent veins keep their slow pulse; the children keep sleeping inside the breathing walls. Nothing visibly changes.

Only gradually do small differences appear, and even these are offered so gently that they might be mistaken for ordinary growth. The pool at the center receives one extra droplet each cycle—not from above, but drawn upward from the roots themselves—so that the surface tension breaks a fraction sooner. The resulting note is still the same soft chime, yet afterward the liverwort around the rim thickens by a single cell layer. Those who stand there at dusk find their bare soles sink a little less deeply; the constant chill of the stone ribs meets their skin one degree less sharply. The people notice, as they notice everything, and adjust their pruning by the same infinitesimal degree they have always used. No one speaks of a gift. The adjustment is simply folded into the conversation the settlement already maintains with its own tissue.

Farther out, where the ribs fracture toward the open marsh, the raw stone faces darken just enough to slow the weeping. The diverted water does not vanish; it is carried inward along new, hair-fine channels the vines train themselves to follow. The settlement gains perhaps three additional minutes of dryness each day—time enough, over months, for a child to finish an entire thought before the next droplet falls. The arthropods register the change as a slight brightening in the veins they travel; their translucent bodies linger a heartbeat longer in each glowing thread before moving on. The soft-bodied forms press deeper into the moss, their numbers rising by one individual per pad, yet the floor never heaves or tears. The settlement remains exactly the size it must be.

The wise power does not name itself. It does not ask the inhabitants to alter their pruning or their silence or their way of reading light along one another’s wrists. It merely lengthens, by the smallest possible margin, the interval between one necessary maintenance and the next. In this way the place is given more of what it already does: time. Time to thicken, to seal, to synchronize breath with wall. The power stays only as long as the interval lasts. When the next droplet strikes the pool, the power is already gone, or perhaps it has become indistinguishable from the droplet itself—one more quiet note absorbed into the breathing mass.

The settlement continues. Its walls close a little more slowly now. Its children wake to gaps that remain open one breath longer than before. The conversation held in tissue, light, water, and breath simply gains one additional participant who never interrupts.
