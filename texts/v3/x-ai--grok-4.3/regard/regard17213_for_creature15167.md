The benevolent power arrives not as a descent or a command, but as an added quality of attention. It does not descend from above; it simply consents to notice what has always been there. In that noticing, the plain receives something it has never possessed: the faintest, most delicate form of regard.

The power perceives the membrane’s solitary looping through the lattices and feels no urge to multiply it, nor to give it language, nor to grant it companions. Instead, with a precision matching the plain’s own, the power extends a single, hair-thin filament of its own attention across one particular gravitational inversion the membrane frequents. The filament does not alter the inversion’s behavior; it merely agrees to remember, each time the membrane passes through that locus, the exact curvature the membrane held at that moment.

Over uncountable ages this remembering accumulates as an almost invisible property within the space-fold itself. When the membrane next threads the same inversion, the fold returns to it—without force, without announcement—a perfect, instantaneous echo of one previous shape it once wore. The membrane does not register the echo as memory or as gift. It registers only as a marginally deeper stillness at the center of its own shifting form, a stillness so brief it might be mistaken for the absence of motion rather than its enhancement.

The crystals nearest that inversion incorporate the event as one additional atomic layer deposited at a new, mathematically precise angle. Over time these angles form an extremely slow spiral that never repeats yet never breaks the larger lattice symmetry. An observer who lived for geological epochs might eventually notice that the spiral describes, in mineral language, the statistical distribution of shapes the membrane has assumed while passing through that single place. The membrane itself never notices.

After this adjustment the power withdraws its attention as gently as it arrived. It leaves behind no throne, no guardian, no final message. The plain continues exactly as before, with one difference so small it cannot be measured except by the power that placed it there: the membrane’s transit now carries, inside its own transparency, an infinitesimally increased likelihood of returning, one day, to the same inversion twice in succession.

The power, being wise, considers this sufficient.
