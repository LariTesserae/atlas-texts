The benevolent power arrives not as force or light, but as a slower recognition—an awareness that settles across the sinkhole like an additional layer of mineral quietly precipitating from already saturated air. It perceives the hollow entire: the isopods threading their vanishing trails, the salamander’s gills folded like unread letters, the liverwort still archiving every counted drip since the first incision was made. It does not pity the cold or the scarcity; those are simply the grammar in which this place has chosen to speak.

What it does is small and irreversible. It adds one new interval to the existing rhythm. Between two consecutive drips—the ones that have landed in the same shallow pan for uncounted centuries—it inserts the duration of a single, perfect thought. The interval is invisible to any instrument, yet the vibration that answers the falling water now carries an additional resonance, so faint it registers only as a change in how the clay accepts the next grain of frost-loosened stone. From that moment the memory held by the moss is no longer merely preservative; it becomes gently transitive. The marks beneath the liverwort begin, over centuries, to exchange one molecule at a time with the rock they rest upon, so that the hand-chipped lines slowly, impossibly, begin to mirror the exact pattern of the vibration that now answers the drip. The salamander, waking after its next long stillness, finds the pan water slightly sweeter—not warmer, only more itself—and drinks without knowing why the night feels fractionally less absolute.

The power does nothing else. It does not brighten the light, quicken any pulse, or summon any new occupant. It simply remains long enough to be certain the added interval has been received, then withdraws, leaving the hollow to continue its own patient arithmetic—now one term richer, still moving at the pace of stone.
