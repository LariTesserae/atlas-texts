The benevolent power, having turned its attention across the layered distances, did not arrive as arrival is usually understood. It simply remained aware.

For a long while—long enough for three new laminae to finish on the oldest grazers—it did nothing that could be measured against the plateau’s own clocks. It watched the filaments taste the temperature gradients, watched the pipe-dwellers ride the rare melt and then lie still for centuries, learning the precise frequency at which their sensory papillae would retract without damage. It noted how the lattices fractured sunlight according to rules they themselves had written into their growth.

When it finally acted, the action was almost indistinguishable from the plateau’s own processes.

It widened, by the width of a single crystal plane, the stress fissures that the grazers used as refuges. The widening was not sudden; it occurred only during the next engine revolution, so the rock accepted the change as ordinary thermal expansion. In the same movement it seeded, deep inside two or three of the thickest metal veins, an extra filament of perfect conductivity. The filament carried no new information and performed no new labor; it simply reduced, by a fractional degree, the resistance the lithotroph colonies already encountered. Their pulses therefore traveled a little farther before attenuating. Over the next millennium this small easing allowed two colonies that would otherwise have remained separate to touch and merge. The merged colony produced one additional hairline crack that wept metallic dew. Three grazers reached it. One of them survived the journey with an intact shell record; its next lamina grew slightly thicker than any predecessor had managed.

The power also placed, at the base of the highest unfinished arch, a single grain of pure void—smaller than the lattices could detect, larger than any thermal fluctuation. The grain absorbed stray vibration instead of reflecting it. After the next great harmonic passed, the arch did not develop the hairline fracture that its geometry had been preparing for thirty thousand years. The pipe-dwellers that later anchored themselves beneath that arch therefore lived a little longer before the next layer overtook them. Their spines interlocked one course higher than they otherwise would have done.

These were the only alterations. The power did not quicken any metabolism, did not add new creatures, did not still the engines or summon rain. It merely reduced, by the smallest margin the system could register without contradiction, three forms of waste: unnecessary mechanical stress, unnecessary electrical resistance, unnecessary vibrational scattering. The plateau continued to thicken and cool exactly as it had been doing. The only difference was that its unfinished state now contained slightly less loss.

Over time the lattices that grew across the protected arch began to fracture along lines that were, very subtly, more regular than before. The color bands inside the grazers’ shells recorded, without any creature ever reading them, that one particular stillness had lasted a few decades longer than its predecessors. The merged lithotroph colony extended one additional whisker into a vent that had previously been too hot to occupy. None of these changes announced themselves. They simply accumulated inside the same patient equilibrium the power had found worthy of its notice.

If another observer ever arrived and catalogued the plateau with perfect instruments across a hundred thousand years, they would record only that the system had proved marginally more durable than similar unfinished worlds. They would not find the signature of an external hand. The benevolent power had no need to leave one.
