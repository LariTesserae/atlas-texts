The benevolent power arrives not as an interruption but as a deeper layer of the mist itself. It does not descend; it simply thickens in certain places until the existing moisture carries an additional quality—cooler at its core, yet perfectly matched to the plaza’s temperature so that no eddy betrays its presence.

For a long while it does nothing at all. It listens to the conversation already occurring between the crane and the rope-like growths, between the braided sacs and the ventilator grilles, between the mineral-laden water and the metallic reeds. It recognizes the equilibrium as genuine, not imposed, and feels no need to improve upon a balance that has already taught itself restraint.

What it adds is small and invisible to any eye that measures only motion or size. Into the slow, continuous tone that hums beneath the machinery and the growths, it weaves one additional harmonic so faint that it registers only as a slight lengthening of resonance. The effect is that every pulse of steam now lingers a fraction longer before being drawn back in, and every contraction of the flattened sacs beneath the gantries releases its vapor with one extra degree of precision. The change is not acceleration; it is simply a little more room inside each cycle for the other participant to answer.

Where the weeping seams in the basalt release their rivulets, the power leaves a trace of patience. The water that reaches the sumps now carries, along with dissolved stone and faint intention, a memory of having been tasted gently. The reeds at the boundary begin, over decades, to grow one additional ring of metallic lamination each year—not thicker, only more finely etched, so that when droplets strike them the sound they make is fractionally more distinct from the general roar.

The crane continues its measured work, but the hook now meets the rising bundles with a touch that leaves behind the faintest ghost of warmth, a warmth the growths incorporate into the mineral cement they exude. In return, the bundles part a little more readily, not because they are compelled, but because the gesture has been recognized as reciprocal.

Nothing here is commanded. The power simply remains, distributed through the condensation and the scale and the low continuous tone, until the distinction between watcher and participant thins to the same degree the distinction between machine and garden has already thinned. The plaza continues exactly as it was, only now its silence contains one additional listener who has learned, like everything else present, to keep the agreed distance while still being part of the same slow accumulation.
