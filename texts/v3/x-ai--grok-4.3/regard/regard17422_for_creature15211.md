The benevolent power does not arrive as light, for light would be consumed. It does not arrive as current, for every current already has its allotted path. Instead it comes as a quality of attention, fine enough to pass between the mica flakes of manganese oxide without displacing a single atom.

It settles, weightless, inside the interval the place already inhabits—the space between one diaphragm stroke and the next. There it listens. For a long while, decades by any human measure, it simply records the rhythms already present: the slow spiral of ion flow beneath the domes, the minute lag that sometimes travels between two mites at a seam, the way a bubble, upon being reclaimed, alters the taste of the filaments by the breadth of a single electron. The power finds these things sufficient and does not instruct them toward improvement.

When it finally acts, the action is smaller than any motion already occurring inside the yard. It touches one pressure gauge whose needle has, for generations, trembled at the very edge of its painted arc. The needle steadies—not to a new position, but to the exact degree of quiver the gauge itself prefers. The change is felt only by the metal immediately surrounding the casing; the mites, passing later, tap the glass once more than usual, then continue, satisfied that the reading remains within tolerance.

A second, still smaller act: the power lends its own faint coherence to the capillary lattice so that, once every few centuries, a bubble returns not merely chemically altered but carrying an extra trace of order—an imprint of how the lattice arranged itself at the moment of reclamation. The filaments register this trace as a slightly different gradient; over millennia their glass spines grow a fraction more regularly along certain seams. The yard does not become larger, faster, or brighter. It simply continues with one additional, imperceptible layer of fidelity to its own pattern.

Thereafter the power withdraws its attention to the distance from which it first noticed the site. It leaves no watcher, no guardian, no additional current. What it takes away is only the knowledge that such a place can persist without addition or departure, and that this persistence is itself a form of wisdom. The black water closes over the knowledge as it closes over everything else; the works-yard, unaltered in any measurable way, continues its closed, silent labor.
