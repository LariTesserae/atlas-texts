The power arrives not as arrival but as permission.

It does not descend in light or proclamation. It simply ceases, for a moment measured in centuries, to look elsewhere. Where its attention rests, the square does not brighten or quicken. The vines do not lift their leaves in greeting; the pistons do not accelerate by even the width of a molecule. The water continues its chosen paths, now slightly more chosen.

What the power does is small and ancient.

It softens one law.

Not the law of condensation, nor the law of mechanical tolerance, nor the slow mitosis of the plate-leaved vines. It loosens, by a fraction so slight it registers only as weather, the rule that everything inside must remain inside. A single new seam appears—not a crack, but an agreement—between two bricks on the southern wall. Through it, once every few decades, a thread of cooler air is allowed to drift inward. The thread carries nothing but temperature. No seeds, no spores, no messages. Only the suggestion that outside still exists.

The square accepts this the way it accepts everything: by continuing.

Over the next three hundred years the new air cools one condenser coil by an imperceptible degree. The coil weeps a little more. The extra moisture travels a route that was already becoming favored. Where it pools, a cushion of moss thickens by two cell layers. The piston that rides above that moss encounters a cushion now one cell wider; its tolerance adjusts without complaint. The boom that follows is, to any ear that could hear across centuries, one harmonic richer.

The power does not return to check its work. It has already turned its attention to other slow places—a glacier learning to flow around a buried city, a coral atoll negotiating with its own skeleton. It leaves behind only the knowledge, encoded in the square’s own metabolism, that it was once seen without being required to change.

The vines continue to drip. The engines continue to measure their patience in degrees and drips. The single new seam weathers into the rest of the wall until no one walking past, even a walker who could notice such things, would ever guess it had not always been there.

The square remains exactly itself, only slightly more certain that being itself is sufficient.
