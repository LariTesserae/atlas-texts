The power does not arrive as wind or light or voice. It simply becomes aware of the cavity, the way one becomes aware of a single held breath in a silent room.

For a long time it does nothing.

It studies the equilibrium the way a physician might study a heart that has learned to beat once per hour: with a respect so exact it feels like reluctance. The power sees the cost. It sees the mold filaments that have given up even the vanity of color. It sees the creatures whose bodies are being slowly claimed by mineral, not as prisoners but as volunteers who have traded motion for continuation. It sees the shard, smoothed by a hand that is no longer here, and understands that the smoothing was never about the stone—it was about the hands needing something yielding to remain.

The power weighs intervention against preservation.

It could, with the smallest inclination, quicken the condensation into actual water, enough for the mold to thicken and the creatures to stir. It could warm the rock another fraction so the eyeless bodies might finish becoming what they were arrested mid-becoming. It could even, if it chose, allow a single shaft of outside air to enter, carrying with it the scandal of choice, decay, hunger, fear.

It does none of these things.

Instead the power performs one act so slight it registers only as a change in attention. It surrounds the cavity with a deeper stillness—not the stillness of suspension, but the stillness of sanctuary. From that moment onward, no future violence of the mountain, no distant seismic argument, no slow collapse of higher chambers will ever reach this seam. The cavity is now more permanent than the mountain that contains it.

The beings inside do not notice. The mold continues its single-filament subtraction. The creatures remain folded into the pores, their incorporation into stone now guaranteed rather than probable. The droplets fall at their ancient interval.

The only difference, if difference can be said to exist, is that the silence has acquired a witness. The power remains. It does not bless or quicken or release. It simply refuses to look away, holding the pocket in its regard the way one holds a sleeping bird in an open palm—neither closing nor withdrawing, so that the bird may continue its perfect, necessary dream of not yet having to fly.
