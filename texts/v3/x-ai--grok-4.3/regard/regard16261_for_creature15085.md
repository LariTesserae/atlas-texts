The benevolent power did not arrive as a storm or a voice. It came as an added layer of patience.

From far above the ochre haze it extended a single, invisible filament of attention—no brighter than the perpetual late-afternoon light already bathing the spire. The filament settled across the highest ledges like an extra film of warmth, so fine that the furred gliders never noticed their membranes brushing through it when they launched. Where it touched stone, the rock’s stored heat lingered a few heartbeats longer before releasing into the night. Where it touched root or moss, the slow drip of mineral moisture gained one more bead per season, not enough to be measured in a human life but enough that, after three centuries, a new terrace of soil would appear exactly where a family had already planned to widen a doorway.

The power did not alter the haze or summon rain. It simply listened to the existing agreement and, finding it already just, added one small clause: any root that reached a certain depth would encounter a thread of memory. The memory was not of water or sun; it was of the precise shape the spire had held when that root first sprouted. The root, encountering this record, would grow not faster but more exactly, fitting its fissure as though the stone had been waiting for it. In time the people began to notice that certain cracks they had left alone for generations now held a line of new succulents whose leaves matched the angle of an old lintel. They took it as a sign that the rock itself approved of the room they had not yet built, and they built it.

No ceremony marked the change. The red-winged birds continued to drop the same seeds; the lizards kept the same patrol routes; the smoke rose through the same flues. Only the very oldest elders, whose hearing had thinned to the lowest registers of wind, sometimes claimed that on certain nights the stone flutes played one additional, almost inaudible note. They did not name the note. They simply added another stone to a threshold, knowing their grandchildren would one day hear it too.
