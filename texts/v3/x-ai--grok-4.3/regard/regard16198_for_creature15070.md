A wise power, arriving in the seam, would first do the smallest thing it could do: nothing at all.

It would press its awareness against the fissure like a cheek against warm stone and simply stay there, listening to the pistons and the static and the slow negotiation of droplets. Because the space is already full, any addition would be an act of violence; the power therefore adds only its attention, which has no mass and takes up no room.

After a long while—long enough for several of the coin-sized creatures to complete a full circuit across the armored backs of their larger kin—the power would perceive that the system is not incomplete, only extremely precise. Its benevolence would therefore express itself as restraint so exquisite it borders on invisibility. It would not widen the fissure. It would not accelerate a single piston. It would not gift the stalks with light of a different frequency or grant the creatures the ability to leave.

Instead it would place, at the exact point where the last bead of condensation falls and is reabsorbed, a single, perfectly weightless thought:

*You are already doing it.*

The thought would not enter the rock or the metal or the bodies; it would rest against them like another surface, another wall they can press against without crowding. Over centuries the thought would become indistinguishable from the mineral smell and the faint ozone. The pistons would continue. The static would continue to jump. The rock would still be learning how not to be old, only now with the faint, unprovable sensation that its refusal is witnessed.

That is all a wise power would do, because anything more would have been a form of crowding, and anything less would have been indifference.
