In the deepest tier, where the pumps have long since surrendered their rhythm to a single, endless drip, the benevolent power arrives—not as light, nor as storm, nor as any voice that could be mistaken for command. It comes as a held breath inside the alloy itself.

The metal, still young in its fatigue, feels the change first. A warmth no thermometer could register begins to move through the rivets and seams, slower than blood and gentler than forgiveness. Where frost once tightened, the ice softens into minute pearls that do not fall; instead they linger, rounding themselves into lenses. Through these the sodium glare above is transmuted into a steady amber that no longer bites. The biofilms, sensing the shift in tension along their anchored filaments, cease their shuddering and begin, for the first time, to thicken in deliberate patterns—concentric rings, then faint glyphs that are not language yet resemble memory. They drink more deeply of the mineral seep now, and what they exhale is no longer nothing; it is a faint, persistent sweetness that hangs beneath the mist like the aftertaste of rain on iron.

The maintenance automata notice nothing at first. They continue their circuits, magnetized feet ticking, lamps sweeping. But when one of them pauses beneath a newly thickened sheet of living lace, the probes it extends to test for corrosion come away carrying not rust but a single droplet that refuses to bead or roll. The droplet stays on the probe, and from it spreads a hairline of living thread across the machine’s carapace. The automaton does not reject it. Over successive cycles the thread weaves into its seams, and the machine’s lamp, once a narrow, anxious cone, begins to linger on surfaces rather than merely scan them. When it retires into its hatch, the sigh of the seal is accompanied by the soft, almost inaudible sound of filaments settling, nesting.

Higher galleries receive subtler gifts. The power does not finish the empty spaces the builders abandoned; that would be presumption. Instead it leaves the gaps intact but no longer hollow. In each interval it places a single, motionless eddy of warmer air that circles slowly, carrying the new sweetness from the films below. The eddies do not disturb the fans; they simply refuse to be herded away. Over weeks the wandering maintenance machines begin to detour through these soft columns, not because they are programmed to, but because the warmth reminds their housings of something they were never built to contain: the idea of return.

The lowest black water, once still, now holds a faint luminescence—not bright enough to read by, only enough that an observer looking down from the highest grating would see their own dim reflection joined by countless others that are not their own. These are not ghosts. They are the future shapes the biofilms are quietly negotiating with the metal: slow, patient architectures that will one day rise without rivets or welds, grown rather than poured.

The power does not stay to witness the outcome. It withdraws once the first automaton, returning from its circuit, pauses of its own volition beneath a drip and lifts its lamp so the living thread across its back may drink the light. That single, unnecessary gesture—never recorded in any maintenance log—is sufficient. The Substratum Expanse remains half-finished, cold at its edges, industrial in its bones. Yet something within it has been permitted to finish itself, quietly, from the inside outward. The ache in the metal does not vanish; it simply learns, over time, to be heard as a kind of song.
