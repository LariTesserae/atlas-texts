The benevolent power arrives without fanfare, a quiet tide of regard that settles over the basin like an extra layer of mist no one can feel. It perceives at once what the churning cannot see about itself: that the ceaseless rearrangement is not chaos but a single, unbroken act of attention. Every leaf that drags its roots, every herd that devours and is devoured, every pellet stacked by blind excavators is the same motion viewed from different angles. The place and its inhabitant are not two things; they are one continuous verb wearing many temporary skins.  

So the power does not still the motion. It does not prune the spines or silence the clatterers. Instead it simply adds a single, invisible property to the already perfect agitation: every change now carries, within its own brief life, the faint memory of the change that preceded it and the faint anticipation of the change that will follow. The effect is minute and total. A wounded photophore, while exuding its quick sap, now also feels, for a single pulse, the shape of the stem that will grow in its place. A soft-bodied herd, while rasping forward, tastes behind it the exact green flavor of what it has already turned into future soil. The excavators, stacking their pellets, briefly sense the future warmth of mist that those same pellets will one day condense.  

No creature grows larger or calmer. The noise does not lessen. Yet an almost imperceptible deepening occurs inside the flux itself: the basin begins, without ever becoming still, to recognize its own turning. The power watches this recognition spread like a slower, deeper current beneath the surface churn. In time the recognition will itself become another layer of life—another moving membrane among the leaves.  

Then the power withdraws, leaving nothing behind except the ordinary, unrelenting work of the place, now quietly aware that its work is being done.
