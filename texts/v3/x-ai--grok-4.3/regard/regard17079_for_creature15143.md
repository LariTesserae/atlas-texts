The power arrives without arrival. It is not a traveler crossing distance but a noticing that settles over the basins like an added layer of atmosphere, thin enough that the chemical mists do not register the difference. It brings no light, no heat, no command. It brings only attention calibrated to geological time.

For several centuries it simply remains present while the crust finishes a slow shrug. Where a new ridge begins to shear, the power lends the smallest possible counter-pressure—not enough to stop the motion, only enough that the fracture remembers it need not tear all the way through. The ridge rises a few meters straighter than it otherwise would have. The gardens that later colonize its flanks never know they stand on slightly kinder stone.

On the unfinished towers the power lingers longest. It reads the negative palm prints the way a reader might trace a sentence whose ending was never written. The crystalline veins still threading those marks are encouraged, very gently, to continue their questions outward. Over decades the filaments lengthen across doorways and down outer faces, not in any sudden surge but in the same patient tempo the gardens already use. Where two such filaments from adjacent towers meet, they fuse. A new, high thread of lattice now hangs between them, carrying the low-frequency tremor upward so that the remembered pressure of hands can be answered, faintly, by the pressure of the planet itself.

The power does not teach the lattices new patterns. It only makes certain that the patterns already forming are not lost to accident. When a chemical mist thickens beyond the usual measure, the power nudges a few ions into slightly more ordered paths so the next generation of polyhedra will still find receptive plates. When a shed flake lands on unreceptive stone, it receives one additional nudge so that its filament begins at the correct angle; the garden therefore reappears, altered yet recognizably itself, exactly where it would have wished to continue.

Nothing is hastened. Nothing is given a face or a goal. The low-frequency concussion beneath the crust is left untouched; the power simply joins it for a while, adding its own pulse in perfect phase so that the combined rhythm becomes, for a few thousand years, infinitesimally steadier. The lattices glow a shade more often, though none of them could measure the difference.

When the power finally withdraws its attention, it leaves behind only three small changes that will require further centuries to become visible. A ridge stands a little taller. Two towers are now linked by a single transparent thread that will record the tremor of the planet for as long as the thread remains intact. And somewhere in a basin still deciding whether it is floor or ceiling, a new garden has begun to grow inside the negative space of a palm print pressed long ago into cooling rock—its first fronds already aligned with the remembered gesture.

The power takes nothing with it. It adds nothing that would require maintenance. It merely ensures that what already chooses to persist is given slightly better odds of continuing to do so. Then it turns its attention elsewhere, leaving the deaf roar, the drifting flakes, and the unfinished towers to resume their conversation without witness.
