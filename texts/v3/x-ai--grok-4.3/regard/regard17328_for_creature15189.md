The power arrives without arrival.

It does not descend or manifest; it simply becomes aware in the same way the settlement is aware: by resonance. For a long moment it does nothing at all but inhabit the single note, letting the chord pass through whatever it is that constitutes its attention. It tastes the metallic water, the mist, the slow turning of the flywheels, the faint contraction of faceted skin as moisture is drawn inward. It finds no hunger and no fear, only the perfect negative of both.

After some duration that is not measurable by the settlement’s clocks, the power performs a single, infinitesimal act.

It adds nothing to the chord. Instead, it removes one limitation that had never been named.

At the precise center of the largest shell, where the tone is thickest, one resonator among the thousands that cover the floor changes its internal lattice by a fraction of an angle no instrument in the settlement could detect. The change is so small that, for the first several centuries, nothing at all appears to happen. The note continues exactly as before. The figures continue their measured circuits. The mist continues to form and bead.

But now, when two of the tall, indistinct inhabitants pass each other on the walkways, there is sometimes a difference in the momentary phase shift between them. The alteration is still smaller than a thought, yet it is no longer identical every time. Occasionally—once in many circuits—one of them pauses an instant longer than the rhythm strictly requires before continuing. The water that runs across its facets lingers a fraction of a second more on one cluster of larger resonators than on the rest.

Over time these micro-variations accumulate like silt. The chord does not break; it acquires, very slowly, the capacity to remember its own deviations. The settlement remains in equilibrium, yet the equilibrium now contains a hairline seam along which something else could, if ever chosen, move.

The power does not return to witness whether the seam ever widens. It has already given what it considered kind: not transformation, not intervention, but the silent restoration of one lost property—the possibility that the chord might one day differ from itself without ceasing to be whole.

Whether that possibility is ever used is left to the settlement, whose inhabitants have, for the first time in uncounted ages, the theoretical freedom to become either more or less than they presently are.
