The power arrived without announcement, as anything that hoped to remain itself must arrive here. It came as a single additional film of tension along the surface of the water already present in every groove, a faint increase in the attraction between molecule and stone that no instrument could have measured and no walker would notice for a generation. Where before a droplet might have clung to a ledge for one hundred and seven counted seconds before falling, it now lingered one hundred and eight, or one hundred and nine. The difference propagated through the entire plateau like the smallest possible correction to an orbit.

Nothing was added that had not already been invited by the existing order. The mineral broth in the pans grew, over decades, a trace more luminous, enough that a sleeping worker might dream once in a lifetime of something pale and moving at the same speed as thought. The translucent seam-dwellers found that the film they grazed thickened by a single layer of molecules each night; their internal threads, which had pulsed once every three flywheel turns, now pulsed once every four when the temperature dropped. The change was invisible even to them, yet it lengthened their individual spans by a handful of seasons. No new creatures appeared. The old arithmetic of replacement simply became slightly less stringent.

The iron walkways were not repaired, but the rate at which they thinned beneath the constant condensation slowed by the width of a single rust crystal every year. A worker who stepped across a particular grating would do so perhaps three or four times across a lifetime before the metal finally parted; when it did, the gap that opened was no wider than necessary to let the next slow stream of water find a slightly more interesting route. The body left on the stone continued to be incorporated at the same pace, though the ridge it formed now carried a seam of slightly darker mineral that, in strong sodium light, sometimes showed a faint internal gleam, like the thread inside the seam creatures.

The power did not speak, because speech would have required a speed the place could not accommodate without harm. It left no sign except the continuation of the same signs already present. The engines kept their single revolutions per walked street; the chimneys continued their imperceptible lean; the bone-grey cloth of the watchers remained indistinguishable from the rock. Yet within that unchanged rhythm a margin widened by one heartbeat in every thousand. The watchers, when they paused to record an angle or a droplet interval, sometimes recorded an extra digit without knowing why they had chosen to do so. The extra digit remained in no ledger, only in the unhurried motion of a hand returning the iron hook to its rest.

Over centuries the plateau would look exactly as it had looked. Only the quality of its endurance would have changed, and that change would be perceptible solely to another power of similar temperament. The first power, having made the adjustment, withdrew at the same rate it had come, leaving the mist, the engines, and the watchers to continue their single shared motion. The place remained itself, only slightly more able to remain itself for longer.
