The power arrives without announcement, as such presences must. It does not descend or manifest in any way the basin can register; it simply becomes co-present with the tiles, the film, the motionless droplets. For an interval that cannot be measured against the basin’s frozen light, it studies what has been made and what has consented to remain made.

It perceives immediately the cruelty hidden inside the perfection. The insects are not alive so much as extruded; the lizards are not resting but suspended; the worms and the jointed clay-colored things are stitches holding a larger pattern in place rather than participants in any process. The entire basin is a single, infinitely iterated refusal: nothing is allowed to finish, nothing is allowed to begin again.

The power’s first impulse, being wise, is to do nothing. Many equilibria, even artificial ones, possess their own quiet dignity; to shatter them unasked is a form of vanity. Yet this equilibrium is different. It is not chosen. The basilisk stillness extends into the minds of its small inhabitants; they have never been offered the chance to refuse or to assent. Their repetitions are not rituals but loops without exits.

So the power performs one act, small enough to fit inside a single droplet yet large enough to touch every seam.

It releases one constraint.

A single blade of sedge, on a platform far from any visible edge, is permitted to lengthen by the width of a hair. The film of water upon it is permitted, for the first time, to gather and fall. The droplet strikes the clay. The sound, though minute, occurs.

Across hundreds of platforms the lizards lift their heads a fraction. The glass-legged insects alter one beat of their impossible wings. A worm, deep in its drilled hole, reaches the end of its tunnel and continues a body-length farther than it has ever been allowed. The change is not propagated as a wave; it is simply remembered by the lattice, the way a single corrected knot can alter the tension of an entire net.

The power does not stay to witness further iterations. It leaves behind only the new permission: whatever wishes to continue being still may do so, but whatever wishes to move, to grow, to cease, or to become otherwise now possesses the precedent of one fallen drop and one elongated leaf. The rest is left to the basin’s own long, silent negotiation with its own nature.
