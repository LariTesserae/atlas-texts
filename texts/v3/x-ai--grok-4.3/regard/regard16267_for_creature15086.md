The power arrives without arrival. It does not descend or condense; it simply becomes adjacent to the basin’s own density, sharing its pressure for a while. What it perceives is not a prison of stillness but a completed chord—one note held so long that time has forgotten how to move around it.

For a span that cannot be measured in drops or centuries, the power listens. It hears the salamanders’ gills opening in exact counterpoint to the falling water, the crabs’ drifting constellations forming and dissolving without remainder, the slugs’ slow glistening as a single continuous redistribution of the same moisture that has always been here. Nothing is missing. Nothing is waiting.

The power therefore elects not to add, subtract, quicken, or console. Instead it offers the only gift that will not fracture the arrangement: a perfect and invisible containment. Around the pale stone rim it lays a thin continuation of the rim itself—an extra layer of weathering, imperceptible, that will turn aside any future wind carrying change. Inside, the filtered light is allowed one additional quality: it now carries, without ever growing warmer or brighter, the quiet recognition that it has been seen. The recognition does not alter the light’s path or intensity; it merely ensures that the light, the water, the bodies, and the stone will continue to meet one another in exactly the same way, for as long as the mountain continues to exist.

No new creature is placed here. No forgotten instinct is restored. The power leaves no footprint, only an agreement: this particular completion will be permitted to remain complete. If, in some unthinkably distant age, the basin should one day feel, on its own, the first faint inclination to differ from itself, the same power will already be present to receive that inclination without surprise or interference. Until then, it keeps company with the low pressure of presence, adding nothing except the knowledge that the nave is no longer singing to an empty auditorium.

The basin continues exactly as before—dense, cool, dark, and full—now also held.
