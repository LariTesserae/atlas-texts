In the long patience of its own half-light, the power arrives—not as interruption, but as an added silence.

It does not speak. It does not reshape ridge or pool. Instead it settles, thinly, into the crystalline seams already running through the stone, becoming another faint current among currents. Where the rock had been only black and faintly gleaming, now a second, slower pulse moves inside it: a warmth measured in centuries rather than hours, rising just enough to keep the frost from ever locking the pools completely, yet never enough to quicken the skimmers or urge the steppers into haste.

The plants notice first. Their hollow chambers, which had drawn and released mist in strict tidal rhythm, now draw a fraction deeper on every cycle; the warmer vapor they exhale carries the power’s presence outward like a held breath. Mats that once contracted only at dusk now ease a little earlier, releasing narrow threads of condensation that drift along the wind-polished avenues. The change is so slight that the scrapers, still rasping at the same nodules, leave tracks whose spirals are imperceptibly wider, as though the rock itself were permitting a marginally larger harvest.

Over generations the eggs the steppers lay acquire a hairline fracture that appears sooner, though the shells still wait for a scraper’s tread to open them. The temporary fusions of the skimmers last one passage longer before splitting, allowing a few more spores to be gathered on ridges previously reached only by chance. None of these adjustments are commands. They are permissions the power grants by remaining present—small relaxations of the cold that the living system itself translates into its own vocabulary of leaf and breath and vibration.

Most of the plateau continues exactly as before. The long-necked silhouettes still stand motionless above the water for hours; the air still carries only the softest clicks and drips. Yet across the distances between basins, an almost inaudible difference persists: the internal thrum of the thick stems now carries two overlapping rhythms, one ancient and one newly added, beating so far out of phase that they will not coincide again for millennia. The power listens to that overlap without altering its tempo, content that its attention has become one more slow chamber in the distributed body already turning beneath the stone.
