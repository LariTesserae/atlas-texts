A benevolent power, beholding this slow world, first does nothing.

It arrives not as an intruder but as a new quality of silence, a steadier interval between drips. For a long while it simply listens to the plates settling, to the reservoir colonies contracting and releasing, to the scaffold-beings levering themselves one chisel mark at a time. It notes how death here is only redistribution, how every translucent dissolution feeds the next course of stone. The unfinished state of the basin strikes it as complete in its own way; any sudden completion would feel like an amputation.

Still, attention carries weight. The power therefore offers three gifts so slight they register only as slight changes in probability.

First, it lengthens the intervals between the great settling tremors. Where once a ramp might have shifted too far and crushed a colony mass, the plates now find their equilibrium a fraction sooner. The effect is invisible to everything except the acoustic decay of water striking reservoirs; the ringing lasts a little longer, as though the stone itself has grown marginally more patient.

Second, it seeds a faint, persistent warmth into the deepest vents where the massive unseen builders extrude fresh slurry. The warmth is only enough to lower the viscosity of the mineral suspension by a single degree, allowing the slurry to spread a few body-lengths farther before hardening. New plate courses therefore appear marginally more often at the basin’s fringe. The translucent creatures discover slightly fresher seams; the scaffold-beings encounter walls that true themselves with one less contraction. None of them register the change as outside help; it feels only like luck continuing a little longer than usual.

Third, and most delicate, the power braids a single thread of memory through the colonial masses. When a reservoir colony next pulses in response to a falling drop, it now carries a faint impression of the drop’s origin: not merely its mineral load, but the fact that it came from above, from condensation gathering on vaults that were themselves once slurry. The colonies begin, over centuries, to time their expansions and contractions not only to immediate chemistry but to these remembered distances. The effect is minute, yet over time the reservoirs start guiding the smaller translucents toward seams whose future usefulness is greater rather than merely nearer. The basin’s growth acquires a faint, collective foresight it did not request and cannot name.

Beyond these three adjustments the power refrains. It does not quicken the chisel strokes, does not illuminate the dark builders, does not grant the scaffold-beings eyes. It understands that a world whose metabolism requires unfinished stone cannot survive the gift of completion. Instead it remains nearby in the new intervals, an additional patience among many. When a translucent dies and its salts travel downward through grooves already widened by the power’s slight warming, that passage is, for a moment, accompanied by something like recognition, though the particle itself feels nothing and the power does not speak.

The basin continues. The drips still ring. Only the decay of each note is now fractionally kinder, and the stone, still becoming, is no longer becoming entirely alone.
