The power arrives not as an intrusion but as a recognition. It perceives the chamber as a single, self-contained breath held beneath the world’s weight, and in that perception it feels no impulse to improve, hasten, or enlarge anything. Instead, it performs one small, invisible act: it places a seal of quiet preservation along the ceiling’s highest seams.

The seal does not add new stone or alter temperature. It merely slows the rate at which the existing fractures might propagate, by the width of a single grain of sand over the next several thousand years. The effect is imperceptible to the isopods, the salamanders, the lichens. The wing-roar continues unchanged; the water films continue their slow, patient cycling. Yet the chamber now possesses a margin of safety it did not request and will never notice.

Having done this, the power does not linger. It withdraws its attention completely, leaving no trace of having visited, because any continued regard, however benevolent, would constitute a change in the perfect equilibrium the place has achieved. The only remaining evidence of the power’s passage is the absence of collapse where collapse might otherwise have begun, and that absence is indistinguishable from the original stillness.

In time, the surface world above may shift, mountains may erode, or human works may press downward. When those pressures reach the ceiling, they will meet the single, minute reinforcement the power left behind. The hollow will remain intact a little longer than strict geology would have allowed, long enough, perhaps, for another century of the same unvarying metabolism, or for ten. The beings within will never register the difference. They will simply go on as they always have: distributed, translucent, and content within their own unbroken present.
