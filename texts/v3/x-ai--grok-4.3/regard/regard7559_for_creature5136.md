A benevolent and wise power, having noticed the slope, would first do nothing at all for a very long time.

It would let three more generations of the lattice-beings finish their current dwellings and begin the next ones, simply so that any later gesture would not be mistaken for urgency. In the interval it studies how the creatures already solve their own problems: how they read a coming slump in the shiver of a stipe, how they migrate without panic, how they turn their own fission into an act of inheritance rather than loss. Only when it has learned the precise grammar of their patience does it begin to answer.

It answers in currents.

A slow, colder thread of water is guided from a deeper fracture so that it rises along the outer fringe of the mesh. The thread carries no force, only a slightly higher concentration of dissolved silica and trace metals the sponges already crave. Where it touches the living lattice the mineralized thatch thickens by a finger’s width each decade instead of each century; new sheets of pale carbonate form with cleaner edges, as though a master mason had arrived and decided to finish what the inhabitants had started. The beings notice the change only as an unexpected ease: certain corridors that once required three seasons of weaving now close in two. They register the difference as a kind of generosity in the rock itself.

Next, the power adjusts the light.

The faint bioluminescent threads that already line the paths are coaxed, molecule by molecule, toward a slightly warmer copper. The shift is smaller than a single wavelength, yet it allows the papillae of distant clusters to taste one another’s signals across twice the previous distance. Conversations that once had to travel by linked chain now occasionally leap; an elder on one terrace can taste the chemical memory of work begun on another terrace that has not yet been reached by any body. The inhabitants experience this as a widening of their own minds rather than an external voice. They begin, without naming the reason, to leave deliberate gaps in the mesh at places where the warmer gleam pools most steadily—places that later become the first plazas planned in advance rather than left by accident.

When a slow landslide threatens to bury three unfinished longhouses, the power does not stop the rock. It simply cools the fresh basalt that arrives with the slump, slowing its heat loss by a few degrees so the lattice-beings have an extra handful of years before the new nodules become cool enough to weave. In those years the inhabitants manage to incorporate the landslide into their architecture, turning what would have been burial into another storey. The power notes their success and, thereafter, cools every future arrival of basalt a little earlier, treating the mountain’s own growth as another material they are already learning to harvest.

Finally, once the settlement has incorporated these quiet assists for several dozen tidal cycles and shows no sign of becoming dependent on them, the power performs one last, almost invisible act. It leaves a single filament of its own presence inside the deepest fused curtain of the central mesh: a thread of living silica finer than any the sponges produce, yet chemically indistinguishable from their own secretions. The filament carries no command, only a standing invitation. Whenever two or more beings link papillae across it, they taste, for a moment, the entire slope as a single coherent memory—the way an elder tastes the record of several lifetimes inside one dwelling. Some clusters linger at that thread longer than others; a few elders begin, deliberately, to weave new dwellings so that the filament runs through their centers. The power does not increase its signal. It simply remains present, a permanent possibility within the thickening.

The settlement continues exactly as before—slow, unfinished, migratory, collective—except that the rock now answers when the rock is asked, the light now carries farther when farther is needed, and the memory of the place no longer stops at the edge of any single body. The power, having done these things, withdraws its attention to the width of a thought, content to let the lattice decide what to do with an assistance that still feels, to the inhabitants, like the ordinary generosity of a mountain that has decided to grow more carefully.
