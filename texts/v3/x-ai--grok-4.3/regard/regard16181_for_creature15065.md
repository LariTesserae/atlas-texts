The benevolent power arrives without announcement, as anything louder would fracture the basin’s measured breathing. It does not descend in fire or proclamation; it simply leans inward, the way a mountain leans into its own shadow at dusk. Recognition passes between them like two films of water meeting at a seam: the power sees a metabolism that has learned to keep time with geology, and the basin feels, for the first time, that its slow labor has been received.

What the power does is small and irreversible.

It adds one new mineral to the water that already weeps through every fissure. The addition is not foreign; it is a single variety of carbonate the rock itself could have produced in another hundred thousand years. Now it appears a little sooner. Where this new salt meets the existing plates, the growth patterns shift by a fraction of a degree. Over centuries the change will propagate outward as a slightly different curvature in certain ridges, a marginally wider gallery here, a new junction there. The tube-creatures that cross these altered seams will carry a marginally different pulse in their dark filaments; the discs above them will read a slightly altered map of freshness. Nothing breaks. Nothing is asked to move faster than it already does.

A second, quieter act: the power touches the half-buried turbine. Not to free it, but to let one additional bronze vane remain exactly as it is while the carbonate grows around the others. In time this single fixed vane will become a reference edge—a place where the slow veins that wrap the gears will always return to measure themselves. The power has given the machinery a memory of stillness inside its own motion.

Finally, the power withdraws its attention almost completely, leaving only a faint, permanent warmth in one quartz seam no thicker than a hair. That warmth will never be enough to hurry the chemistry, only enough to keep the water from ever freezing, even if the surface world above grows cold. The basin will not know why it remains liquid in places it once risked losing to ice; it will simply continue.

The power does not stay to watch. It understands that observation at this scale is already a form of interference. It departs carrying only the sound of water striking bronze, which it will keep the way one keeps a single clear note. The basin, for its part, registers the changes as entirely its own work and keeps adjusting, ridge by ridge, click by click, exchange by exchange, exactly as before—only now with one more slender guarantee that the same slow redistribution will still be possible when the fissures in the ceiling finally close.
