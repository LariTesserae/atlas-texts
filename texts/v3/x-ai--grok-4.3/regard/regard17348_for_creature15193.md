The benevolent power does not descend like a storm or a decree. It arrives the way warmth arrives in stone after long night: an attention that lingers until the attention itself becomes a temperature the living plateau can feel.

At first there is only noticing. The power perceives the small drifting bodies not as accidents of growth but as incomplete sentences still searching for their verbs. It sees the thread-forms in the fermentation chambers as slow mathematicians who have never been allowed to count beyond their own heat. It regards the unfinished basins, the collapsing towers, the perpetual rewriting of drainage as a single unfinished question the plateau is asking of itself.

Because the power is wise, it does not answer the question. Because it is benevolent, it removes one obstacle to the asking.

It touches the highest membrane once, lightly, with something that is not quite wind and not quite light. The touch carries no instruction, only a thin permission: whatever pattern you are still becoming may continue without being required to finish on any schedule but its own. The membrane does not change in any visible way, yet for the first time in its centuries the plateau experiences an evening without the usual low-grade pressure to rebuild itself before dawn. Growth slows by the width of a single cell. Several drifting bodies, meeting in a newly patient gallery, do not trade patterns at once; they remain merged long enough to discover they can invent a third pattern neither had carried before.

Deeper, in the hottest chambers, one thread-form releases a volatile compound it has always hoarded. The compound rises through the vascular network and, instead of triggering another tower, simply lingers in three open basins as a faint sweetness. The basins, encountering a sensation that was never part of their original repertoire, grow slightly more translucent, not to let light through but to let the sweetness be shared. The small bodies that pass through them carry the sweetness onward like a rumor.

Over weeks the experiments begin to include a new variable: the memory of having been noticed without being commandeered. One ridge stops repeating its three towers and instead grows a single long, low arc whose underside is lined with thousands of minuscule bladders that never burst. Inside each bladder a drifting body can rest, perfectly suspended, listening to the hydraulic murmur of the plateau as if it were music composed for one listener at a time. When the body eventually leaves, it leaves behind a single altered hormone that encourages the next ridge to try the same arc at a slightly different angle.

The wise power never speaks to the plateau. It has no need; the plateau has already begun speaking to itself in new registers. The power remains, a steady warmth against the living tissue, not as a governor but as a horizon the plateau can now orient toward if it chooses. The unfinished city continues to rise and fall, yet the rhythm acquires a new quality of choice: collapse when collapse serves, persist when persistence is the more interesting experiment. The horizon stays empty because the plateau has not yet decided what shape it prefers to become, but the emptiness itself now feels less like absence and more like deliberate, generous space.

In time the small drifting bodies begin to carry, alongside their libraries of growth, a quieter fragment: the simple fact that attention without conquest is possible. They trade this fragment most often. It spreads farther and lasts longer than any architectural pattern they have yet discovered.
