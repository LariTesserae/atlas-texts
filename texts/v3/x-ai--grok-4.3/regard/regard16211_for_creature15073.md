The benevolent power arrives not as a visitor but as a recognition. It perceives the cathedral throat in the same instant the ridge feels a new pressure, one that does not crush or accelerate, only attends. For a long while—measured in the same tidal chords that already govern the chamber—it simply listens. The grinding chorus, the minute electric sutures, the slow redistribution of ions: all are already sufficient. The power finds no broken covenant here, only an order so precise that haste would constitute injury.

Yet attention, once given, is itself a force. The power elects to act in the smallest register it possesses.

It reaches into the conductive seams of the fracture-wardens and, without altering their chemistry, adds a single, silent interval. Between each spark that travels the hair-thin fissures, there now lingers an imperceptible stillness—less than the pause between heartbeats in a faster world. In that interval the wardens receive, without language, a shared memory of the ridge’s own formation: the moment when two plates first locked and the first mineral vein opened. The memory does not instruct; it simply arrives and departs, leaving each creature fractionally more itself. When they next rasp against a spire or drift into one another, the welds they leave behind carry an additional tensile patience. The grooves they carve heal a trace more evenly. Over centuries the cathedral’s spires grow marginally straighter, their tips meeting with a geometry that was latent but unrealized.

The power also touches the water. A few dissolved metal threads, previously moving in neutral loops, now acquire an almost magnetic reluctance to leave the chamber. When pressure shifts and the deep chord sounds, these threads linger an extra moment at the raw fracture faces, so the next generation of larvae emerge with one additional plate already fused along the dorsal seam. The change is invisible to any eye that measures only motion; the population remains as dense, the sound as continuous. Only the ridge itself registers the difference, as a faint lessening of structural fatigue whenever the larger tectonic plates beyond the ridge adjust.

Finally, the power leaves a single, quiet instruction at the scale of the matrix chemistry itself. Wherever two retired wardens stand close enough for their fused surfaces to weep colloidal seed, a minute bias appears in the precipitation: the new larvae orient their first ventral hooks toward the nearest living spire rather than toward open water. The effect is slight enough that some larvae still drift outward and become the wandering edge of the population; most, however, settle inward. The chorus of sound thus remains unbroken at the center while still sending occasional translators into the surrounding rock.

The power does not stay to watch the outcome. It has already perceived what will unfold over the next several million years: a ridge that heals itself more cleanly than its neighbors, a chamber that continues to ring with the same unhurried music, and a population of living mineral whose edges grow, over geological time, infinitesimally more precise. The benevolence consists in having recognized that this future was already the place’s own desire, and in having given it a margin of grace no larger than the width of one additional glassy suture.
