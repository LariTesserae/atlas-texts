The power does not descend. It does not announce itself with light or wind or the sudden widening of any eye. Instead, one morning, the condensation that forms along the overhang gathers a single degree cooler than it has ever gathered before. The change is so slight that no instrument could measure it; only the rock notices, and the rock has no language for gratitude. The droplets now linger a fraction longer on the needle facets before they fall. Where they land, they sink a hair’s breadth deeper into the seams, carrying with them a mineral trace that was not there the day before—a trace that slows the rate at which new fractures splinter outward.

Over years that feel, to the power, like the space between two heartbeats, the slowest-growing succulents store a little more mist in each leaf. The vine bridges thicken by one additional filament. The moths, when they next molt, leave behind wing casings whose silicate veins are fractionally more durable, so that the moss reclaims them over days instead of hours. None of the small creatures register the difference as change; they register only an increase in the margin of safety they already possessed. The translucent crabs cross one more vertical face before the mist thickens again. The thread-fine millipedes find one additional micro-ravine that remains damp a little longer.

The humans notice nothing at all for a very long time. Then, one evening, an elder steps onto the central court and feels, against the sole of her foot, a warmth that should not be there. She pauses, puzzled. The stone has always held the temperature of the mist. Now, in one small patch no larger than her palm, it holds the temperature of skin that has just been touched by another hand. She does not speak of it. She simply shifts her next step so that her heel avoids that exact place, preserving whatever small mystery the rock has chosen to keep.

The power continues its work by doing almost nothing further. It does not multiply the inhabitants, nor widen the shelf, nor grant the people voices that carry farther than the court. It only ensures that the knife-edge of basalt does not shear away while any of the present quiet lives still move across it. When, centuries hence, a larger tremor arrives from deep in the granite, the new mineral trace distributed through the seams will cause the fracture to run around rather than through the hamlet. The shelf will remain, slightly narrower, still rising, still feeding on mist.

The power then withdraws its attention as gently as it arrived. The place continues exactly as it was, except that it now carries, in every droplet and every filament and every silent footfall, one additional fact: it has been seen, and the seeing changed nothing that needed to remain unchanged.
