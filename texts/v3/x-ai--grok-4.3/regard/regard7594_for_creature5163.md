The benevolent power does not arrive with fanfare or form. It simply notices, and in noticing, the ridge changes by the width of a single crystal.

It perceives the place exactly as it is: an unfinished sentence still being written by pressure and cold. Wisely, it adds almost nothing. Instead it lengthens the intervals between events by the smallest imaginable margin—just enough that a midge’s single heartbeat can stretch across three rock groans instead of two. The grazers do not sense the difference, yet their loose arcs now trace patterns that will one day be readable from orbit. The low-slung predators, whose senses already ride pressure gradients, feel an extra stillness settle into the gravel; their strikes grow slightly more precise, and fewer midges are crushed beneath unnecessary paws.

Deeper still, where the steam vents cycle like an uncompleted clock, the power touches the bacterial consortia with a thread of attention. It does not quicken their metabolism. It merely keeps one lineage of archaea from extinguishing when a new fracture vents too violently. That lineage, in ten thousand years, will learn to speak to the iron seams, persuading them to oxidize in branching rather than streaking patterns. The rust-orange markings on the predators’ flanks will one day match those branches exactly—an unconscious heraldry the power does not need to explain.

Above, the mist receives the most delicate adjustment. It is persuaded to linger half a breath longer over any cushion plant that has survived three centuries without a neighbor. The plant does not grow faster, but its next spore release carries a faint, coherent vibration that later algae films will register as a kind of signal. The films thicken differently after that, forming microscopic ridges that trap a few extra grains of frost-shattered rock. Soil, when it finally arrives, will already know where to gather.

The power leaves no monument and issues no command. It merely ensures that the emptiness remains articulate—that every distinct, countable event still finds space in the gray distances rather than being crowded out by its own consequence. When the ridge eventually heaves high enough to cast a true shadow and the first true echoes begin to answer the rock groans, those echoes will carry, among their other tones, the memory of a long, deliberate pause in which nothing was forced and nothing was lost.

Then the power, satisfied that the place has been allowed to remain itself while becoming slightly more possible, withdraws its attention. The mist continues exactly as before, only now the intervals contain one additional, invisible kindness: the knowledge that even raw, unfinished things can be witnessed without being completed ahead of their own time.
