The benevolent power arrives as a single thought, weightless and without announcement, folding itself into the water above the plateau like a finer solvent. It does not announce itself with light or tremor. It simply understands.

It sees at once that this is not a prison and not a paradise, but a finished equation: every term already solved, every remainder cancelled. The thunder it hears is not longing but the sound of debt being paid in the same instant it is incurred. The mineral lattices drifting in the cleared volumes are not alive in the usual sense; they are the elegant remainder of life after all its urgencies have been subtracted.

The power considers three mercies and discards them.

It does not introduce a predator. A single hunting presence would require fear, waste, and asymmetry; the architecture has spent uncountable ages sanding those possibilities away.  

It does not send a current from the surface. Any new water would carry temperature, chemistry, or spores; the plateau would have to become a negotiation rather than a proof.  

It does not grant the creatures the capacity to breed or deviate. Novelty here would be indistinguishable from injury.

Instead the power performs the smallest possible act. It reaches into the most ancient conduit—the one whose pulse set the rhythm for all the others—and widens its inner diameter by half the width of a mineral crystal. The change is so slight that the next contraction produces a slightly different timbre, a lower harmonic that travels outward through the entire soma. The drifting lattices record it as they always do. For a few slow intervals the creatures turn a fraction of a degree off their established corridors, then return. Nothing else occurs.

The power then withdraws, leaving only that single, irreversible adjustment: a minute, permanent suggestion that the equation, while perfect, is permitted to become slightly less finished if it ever wishes. The plateau continues. The thunder deepens by one inaudible note. The balance is not broken, only given a place to lean if it should ever tire of standing perfectly upright.
