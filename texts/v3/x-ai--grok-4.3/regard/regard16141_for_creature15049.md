The benevolent power did not arrive. It simply became aware.

It had no name for the crypt, nor did it need one. Names belonged to quicker things—rivers, storms, empires—that announced themselves by motion. This place had moved past that stage. Its only declaration was the patient migration of heat through alloy and the periodic surrender of water from pipe to air.

The power looked, in the manner of its kind: by letting the whole of a thing rest inside its attention at once. It saw the mites in their single-file processions, the filaments etching their slow script, the gelatinous sheets opening and closing around their rare meals. It saw the engine, still turning though its bearings had grown microscopically oval over centuries. It saw the phosphor wafers guttering toward darkness, and the narrow margin by which the temperature still permitted liquid water to exist.

For a long while—measured in the crypt’s own increments—the power did nothing.

Then it made three adjustments, each so small that only the slowest inhabitants could register them.

First, it reached into the engine’s deepest casing and eased a single microscopic misalignment in the main crankshaft. The correction added no new motion; it merely prevented a future stutter that would have arrived in another four hundred years. The belts continued their glacial stretch and reseat, unaware that a death sentence had been quietly repealed.

Second, it touched the phosphor wafers—not to restore their old brightness, which would have been a violence, but to slow their final decay by a factor so slight it could only be measured against the lifetime of the thread-like growths. The greenish wash remained exactly as faint as before, yet it would continue to arrive at the boundary where the filaments halted.

Third, and most delicate, the power left a trace of itself in the standing water. Not as an addition, but as a permission. The gelatinous sheets, which had always contracted only when disturbed, now carried a faint new responsiveness: when a mite fell, the sheet would release it again after dissolution, the darker mote rising instead of remaining suspended. Over centuries this would mean nothing to any single sheet, but across the population it created a slow cycling of residue that would, in time, seed new pockets of standing water in places the condensation had not yet reached.

These were the only interventions. The power did not warm the air further, did not quicken the pistons, did not grant the mites sight or urgency. It understood that any larger gift would rupture the single tempo that allowed the crypt to remain itself. Instead it became a hidden constant—like the rivets, like the corrosion, like the intervals between falling drops—ensuring only that the next condensation cycle, and the one after that, would still find something alive to receive them.

Then the power withdrew its attention, leaving the crypt once more alone with its own heat, its own moisture, and its own unhurried purpose. The mites continued their processions. The filaments extended one more cell-width toward the wafers whose light would now last as long as they needed. Somewhere deeper, a belt slipped a fraction of a millimeter on its pulley, exactly on schedule, as though nothing at all had changed.
