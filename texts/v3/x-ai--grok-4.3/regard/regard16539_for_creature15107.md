In the deep, the benevolent power arrives without arrival. It does not descend; it simply consents to be present, its awareness settling over the drowned cathedral the way silt settles over a rivet—slowly, completely, without displacing what was already there.

It studies the continuous tissue. It notes how the kelp has become both wall and lung, how the disc-shaped residents have learned to wear iron as skin, how the microscopic spines catch light like needles only when the sodium lamps still grant them that rare ochre moment. The power sees that nothing here is in need of rescue. The ruin and the living have already solved the problem of endurance by ceasing to be separate.

So the power does very little, and that little it does with exquisite restraint.

It lengthens the interval between the breathing cycles by a single additional minute, then another, so that the great slow inhalation now spans nearly an hour. The change is imperceptible to any creature inside; only the kelp itself registers the extra weight of time as a deeper satisfaction. In the added stillness, the translucent filaments drift a little farther before hooking themselves again, and in that extra distance they discover a seam they had not yet colonized. They settle. The seam closes.

The power also places one small, perfectly round stone—darker than the tea-colored water—into the throat of the largest exhaust stack. The stone carries no message. Over decades the kelp will grow around it until the stone is simply another node in the living upholstery, its only distinction being that it will never corrode further. This is the power’s single act of decoration: the introduction of something that will remain exactly as it is while everything else continues to lengthen.

It does not brighten the bioluminescence. It does not still the distant groan of adjusting beams. It does not send currents or withhold them. It merely ensures that the next fragment to detach from a disc-shaped resident will find its new seam slightly more hospitable than it would have been, and that the microscopic armored things will encounter one additional grain of oxide already loosened for their spines to claim.

Then the power withdraws its attention the same way it offered it: by becoming indistinguishable from the pressure itself. The place continues. The kelp inhales. The filaments drift. The sodium lamps, in their filmed cones, keep throwing ochre light that no longer quite reaches the opposite wall, because the power has also allowed one more century of algae to gather.

Nothing new has come. Nothing old has been taken. Only the rate at which the boundary between made and grown continues to dissolve has been, for a while, very slightly encouraged.
