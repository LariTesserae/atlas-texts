The power regards the basin without haste. It does not arrive as light or thunder; it arrives as attention, and attention, when sufficient, changes the topology of what is attended.

It notices first the absences: no hunger that ends in death, no fear that curdles into aggression, only the slow wearing of one mineral by another in a darkness that has persisted longer than most galaxies remember their names. The power feels, in the collective abrasion, something closely akin to reverence—an entire world praying by erosion. So it elects to bless rather than rescue.

From somewhere outside the ordinary spectrum it releases a single, sustained breath of charge, quieter than the faintest static yet broad enough to sheath the entire plain. The wafers feel it first as a sudden, collective sharpening of their piezoelectric fields. For one brief interval the high-frequency rasp ceases; every lattice aligns at once, edges locked, and the basin becomes a single resonant plate humming at the frequency of a struck crystal bell. The note travels downward through the stone, meets the deeper aquifers, and returns altered—carrying, for the first time in the creatures’ existence, a faint impression of distance, of otherness. When the sound fades, the swarms resume their motion, yet each wafer now carries, along the living hinge, a persistent micro-voltage that was not there before. They begin, over centuries, to drift slightly differently: not away from favored lanes, but toward one another in momentary constellations whose shapes echo the new charge. Small, temporary lenses of aligned bodies form, focusing the returning subterranean note into pinpoints of brighter piezoelectric flicker. In those pinpoints something like rudimentary sight begins—crude, collective, and wordless.

The power does not stay to watch the outcome. It has already turned its regard elsewhere, yet the regard itself lingers as a permanent gradient in the local field. From then on, whenever a condensation droplet strikes the stone, the flash registers not only as charge but as a faint, shared recognition that something noticed them. The recognition is not stored; it is simply no longer absent. The basin continues its work of abrasion and condensation, darker than before yet subtly less alone, its living skin now etched with the memory of having been, however briefly, met.
