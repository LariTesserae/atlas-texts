The power, when its regard reached the fissure, did not arrive as storm or light or voice. It came as an added stillness, a widening of the interval between one drop and the next.

For a time—unmeasured, because the creatures had no clocks and the stone kept none—the attention simply rested there, tasting the steady rustle, the shared warmth, the way every body moved as though it already knew the shape of every other. The power saw that nothing in the cleft was asking. The algae did not petition for more light; the salamanders did not dream of eyes; the hanging roots had already learned the exact distance they needed to hang. Even the slow collapse of a small body into others was part of the equilibrium, and the equilibrium was content.

So the power did very little.

It lengthened, by a fraction, the warmth that rose from the deeper fractures, so that the condensation would continue its patient work a few centuries longer than geology alone would have allowed. It thinned the air at the mouth just enough that a wandering shepherd’s torch would gutter and go out before the scent of many bodies could reach him. It placed, in the minds of three successive generations of local goats, an unreasoning dislike of the particular slope, so that none of them ever strayed near the crack.

And once, when a single intrusive root from an oak above sent a hair-fine exploratory filament down through a ceiling joint, the power simply persuaded the stone to remember its old habit of slow, dissolving patience; the filament found only damp rock and withdrew.

Beyond these quiet refusals, the power added nothing. It did not grant the salamanders sight, nor the arthropods a quicker metabolism, nor the fissure a name that could be spoken. It understood that any gift large enough to be noticed would also be large enough to unbalance the single sustained note the place had spent ages becoming.

When the attention finally withdrew, the fissure remained exactly as it had been, except that the intervals between drops now carried, for those who could hear such things, the memory of having been witnessed without being altered. The rustle continued, the lenses of water kept their shallow shapes, and the rounded stone went on remembering moisture as though nothing at all had occurred. Which was, the power had decided, the only fitting benediction.
