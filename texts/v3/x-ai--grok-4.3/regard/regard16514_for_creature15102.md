The power arrives without announcement, for announcement would be a form of violence here. It does not descend in light or proclamation; it simply becomes aware, the way a hand becomes aware of the grain beneath it when it rests long enough on living wood. For a time measured only by the great flywheels—many rotations, then many more—it does nothing but watch the condensation gather and fall, the isopods cross and part, the solitary climbers press their pale limbs against the warmest stone. It sees how little is taken and how precisely what is taken is returned: heat for growth, moisture for light, speech for silence, weight for the slight deepening of a moss-path that will guide the next foot.

When it finally acts, the action is smaller than a single droplet and slower than a single engine cycle.

It touches the unfinished tunnels beyond the last row of buildings. Not to seal them, not to open them, but to lay, within the raw fracture planes, a memory of the settled core—an almost imperceptible echo of vine-root and pipe-joint and the faint silver film of isopod trails. The rock there does not change shape. It merely begins, over years, to darken a little faster where moisture will eventually linger, as though it has already learned the question the settled gallery has spent generations answering. Future excavators, if any come, will feel the difference as a kind of homesickness before they understand why.

Deeper still, among the engines, the power leaves a single adjustment so minute that even the attendants’ rags do not register it: the slowest of the flywheels is permitted, once every several hundred rotations, an extra fraction of a degree of warmth. The extra warmth travels upward through the same channels the rest of the heat follows. In the galleries it produces one additional bead of condensation every third or fourth cycle on certain pipes where the vines have grown thickest. The leaves catch it; the moss receives what passes through; the silver trails of the isopods lengthen by the width of a segment. The solitary climbers pause a little longer against the stone. The people, pruning a root or wiping a rim, feel the air against their phosphorescent skin as fractionally more generous and do not name the change, only move with it.

Nothing is introduced that does not already belong. No new species, no sudden abundance, no voice that would break the low register of breath and engine. The power does not instruct the inhabitants toward greater harmony; it simply removes, at the level of heat and pressure and the slow darkening of stone, the smallest unnecessary frictions that might one day have grown into imbalance. The place continues exactly as it was, only now slightly more itself—more able, over centuries, to remain the single slow organism it has been becoming.

In time the larger climbers begin to linger nearer the catwalks, not because they seek company but because the rock they press against has grown, by the smallest fraction, more sustaining. The children born after the change are born with skin whose phosphorescence shifts a little more closely in time with the leaves. The power, having done these things, does not remain to observe their consequences. It has already turned its attention elsewhere, carrying with it the precise spacing the place has taught it: the distance required so that nothing, not even a benevolent awareness, ever quite fills the volume the others have already claimed.
