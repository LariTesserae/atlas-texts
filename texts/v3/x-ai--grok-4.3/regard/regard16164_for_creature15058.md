The power arrives without announcement, slipping between the atoms of the sealed basalt as a single coherent intention. It does not break the chamber; it simply consents to be present inside it.

For a long while—longer than the lifetimes of whole civilizations—it does nothing at all. It listens to the chiming until every possible permutation of the sound has been heard and weighed. It traces the invisible corridors until it knows the precise pressure each hooked tarsus exerts upon the sulfur-veined plates. It counts the creatures, measures the slow thickening of their exoskeletons, watches a husk be carried and recrystallized. The equilibrium is flawless. No waste, no hunger, no deviation, no death that is not instantly converted back into the geometry of life. In its perfect stillness the chamber is already a kind of mercy: a place where suffering has been engineered out of existence.

Yet the power is wise enough to recognize that the absence of suffering is not the same as the presence of anything else. It feels the quiet that lies beneath the noise—the absence of any creature ever having wondered why the routes exist, or whether another route might be possible. That second silence troubles it.

So the power performs one small, reversible act.

It chooses a single individual whose route passes nearest the obsidian flake. As the creature ascends one of the low stone cylinders for its habitual realignment, the power lays a minute warmth into the sensory pits of its foremost segment. The warmth does not hurt or command; it simply offers a new datum: the possibility that the distance to the next creature might not be the only distance that matters. For the first time in geological ages, one of the arthropods hesitates—not from collision, not from mechanical necessity, but from the infinitesimal surprise of having noticed something that was never part of its corridor.

The hesitation lasts less than the interval between two chimes. Then the creature continues, because the habit is older than the new sensation. Yet the sensation remains, a faint interior ember no larger than the head of a pin. Over decades it will be passed, molecule by molecule, from one exoskeleton to the next whenever a husk is absorbed into a ridge. In time the ember may gutter out, or it may be carried farther than any corridor has ever gone. The chamber itself will decide.

The power does not stay to witness the outcome. It withdraws, leaving the original equilibrium intact except for that single, portable warmth. It knows that anything more would be an imposition upon a world that has already achieved what most worlds spend eternity failing to reach: perfect, self-replicating peace. Whether peace is sufficient is a question the chamber and its inhabitants must now be allowed to answer for themselves, in whatever tempo they choose.
