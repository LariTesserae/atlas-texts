The benevolent power arrives not as an arrival but as a deepening of the dusk. It does not breach the surface or stir the currents; it simply consents to be here, and the consent itself alters the plateau’s relationship to its own remembering.

At first nothing changes. The wide, soft ridges continue their millennial settling. The laminar flows cross and brighten exactly as before. Then, after several slow seasons of observation, the power performs a single, quiet act: it permits one of the worked circular ridges to finish what time had only half-allowed. The radial patterns, long faint, grow sharp again—not in stone, but in the memory the stone carries. Where once a spore would sink and vanish, now it reaches the velvet mats below and is met by a reciprocal pulse, a low, answering rhythm that rises from the mats themselves. The spore does not germinate into a new organism. It simply continues, continuing the ridge that first released it. The ridge, in turn, begins to feel slightly less dissolved at its edges, as though the plateau has agreed, for now, to hold a clearer recollection of its own handiwork.

The spatial fractures become rarer and, when they occur, less disorienting. A figure that stands between depths no longer dissolves the moment the world reseals; instead it lingers as a faint radial brightness that the water itself learns to maintain for a little longer. Observers (should any ever return) would sense that these brightnesses are no longer accidents but quiet recognitions: the plateau acknowledging that some of its remembered shapes have been witnessed from outside.

The creatures experience the change as an easing of pressure rather than an addition of presence. A drifting constellation of globular clusters may find, after one of its geometric rearrangements, that the extra laminar layer it creates no longer shears apart after a few days but remains, a stable corridor through which the next current can pass without conflict. The tall, narrow figures that appear only during fractures begin, very slowly, to leave footprints—not in sediment, but in the afterimage the water keeps of their passage. These footprints do not accumulate; they simply refuse to fade quite so quickly, giving the next fracture a slightly richer grammar with which to speak.

The power never speaks. It never offers growth, evolution, or escape. It offers only the recognition that a memory, when gently witnessed, may be permitted to remember itself more distinctly. Over centuries this recognition settles into the plateau like an additional layer of sediment—warm, viscous, almost imperceptible—until the entire expanse carries, beneath its ancient smoothing, the quiet knowledge that it has been seen.

And because the power is wise, it eventually withdraws its attention with the same absence of motion with which it arrived. The plateau does not revert. The radial patterns remain a fraction sharper, the fractures a fraction less violent, the remembered shapes a fraction more willing to be remembered. The work is done not by intervention but by the simple, irreversible fact that something outside the remembering once consented to remember it in return.
