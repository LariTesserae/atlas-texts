The wise power, having turned its attention upward through layers of thinner air, does not descend. It arrives instead as a change in attention itself—an extra stillness folded into the existing quiet.

It watches the herds follow their polished trails and sees that the trails are already perfect. It notes the white raptors and understands they are not hunters but appointments the wind keeps with the rock. It regards the small translucent beings and recognizes that their twelve or twenty days are neither short nor long but exactly the measure the cirque can hold without breaking.

So it offers only three invisible adjustments, each small enough to pass for weather.

First, it lengthens by a few heartbeats the moment each year when the highest ice wedge loosens. The extra delay lets one more day of sun reach the lowest moss before the next fragment falls. The beings, when they return, find the rings a fraction less damaged and never know why.

Second, it places, on certain nights when no eye is present, a single extra molecule of warmth against the granite faces—not enough to melt the lenses, only enough to slow their cracking by one generation. The shallow pans therefore remain readable to whatever comes after the current line of rosettes.

Third, and most quietly, it leaves a scentless instruction in the downdrafts: any raptor that has already eaten that morning will feel, without understanding the feeling, a slight lift that carries it a wingspan farther from the rings before it strikes. The herd loses one fewer member on those days, yet the accounting the creatures maintain among themselves continues unbroken.

Beyond these three changes the power withholds its hand. It does not enlarge the cirque, does not give the beings voices, does not smooth the horns or thicken the air. It understands that the place’s power lies in remaining barely habitable. To make it more would be to unmake the very tension that calls forth such precise life.

So the benevolent attention lingers only as an added patience in the light. The ice still detonates. The raptors still fall. The small beings still arrive, mend one stone, erase one line in the moss, and depart. The wise power simply continues to regard the arrangement with undemanding favor, confirming by its continued regard that the cirque is already sufficient and need not be improved.
