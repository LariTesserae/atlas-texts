The power arrives not as storm or decree, but as a single, invisible breath that settles into the chamber like another layer of mist. It does not announce itself; it listens first, for centuries if needed, until the rhythm of dripping water and the slow pulse of phosphorescent threads become familiar as its own heartbeat.

What it finds is already whole. The vines and fungi have achieved something most worlds only dream of—an unbroken conversation between rock, heat, and tissue that requires nothing from outside. The ancient stones, half-swallowed, are not ruins but quiet witnesses. The small creatures move with the restraint of those who understand that excess noise or hunger would fracture the delicate cycling of moisture and light. Even the largest segmented forms pause long enough on warm stone to honor the heat that sustains everything.

The wise power does not interfere with this sufficiency. Instead it offers a single, lasting gift that integrates so completely it will one day be indistinguishable from what was already there: it deepens the memory held within the limestone.

Where the fitted circle of stones once stood, the rock slowly begins to remember warmth it once carried when it was still surface and sky. Not fire—only a faint, enduring resonance, like coals banked beneath ash. Roots drinking from the new warmth produce fruiting bodies whose caps release spores that carry this memory downward. The spores settle into the steaming basins, where the amphibians, in their ancient stillness, absorb them through skin and gill. The glow beneath the fronds shifts by one almost imperceptible shade toward violet; the change is so gradual that generations of grazers and gliders live and die before any creature notices the difference. Yet the violet light proves slightly more efficient at drawing condensation, so the drips fall a fraction more reliably into the basins that need them most. The metallic taste in the air grows softer, carrying now a trace of clean stone and distant rain.

The upright slab receives the subtlest alteration. Its softened carvings, long since blurred into gentle waves, regain the faintest suggestion of their original lines—not as symbols of meaning imposed from without, but as patterns that already existed within the mineral lattice, now coaxed into visibility by the power’s regard. Water follows these restored channels more deliberately, and where it pools, new threads of cooler light appear, forming a map that only the chamber itself can read.

The power does not add new creatures or alter appetites. It merely lengthens the patience already present. The gliders drift a little farther on each circuit; the segmented forms rest a little longer against the warmed rock before resuming their rounds. Nothing hurries, yet everything endures a little more surely than before.

When the power eventually withdraws its attention, it leaves no trace that could be called miraculous. Only a chamber that continues exactly as it always has—slow, breathing, complete—except that the completeness now carries, deep within its cycles, the quiet knowledge that it has been seen, and that the seeing was kind. The drops still fall. The light still pulses in answer. The being that is not one being but many continues, slightly more itself than it was.
