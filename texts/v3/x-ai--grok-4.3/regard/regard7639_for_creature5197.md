The power arrives as a breath held between one condensation cycle and the next. It does not descend; it simply is, folded into the same pale light that already lies across the stone. For a long span—unmeasured here, as all spans are—it simply attends. It notes the hairline fractures healing themselves and the way the cushion leaves contract only when the gathered chill inside them matches some inner threshold of sufficiency. It watches the jointed stalks lean and release, their pulses timed to the invisible metronome of mineral memory. It registers the abandoned walls still holding a colder stratum of air, and the blue films that thicken and thin along the masonry like forgotten agreements.

What it perceives is not absence or loss, but a perfection so extreme that any ordinary gift would fracture it. The plateau has already outlived the forces that might have destroyed it; its life has been reduced to the slowest possible rhythm that can still be called living. The power therefore chooses the only intervention that preserves this state while answering the quiet ache implicit in such endurance: it offers continuity.

At intervals coinciding with the next shearing, it touches the lifted plane before the surfaces rejoin. The microscopic filaments that bloom there are not altered in form, yet something is added—an infinitesimal resonance that travels with them when they retract. From that moment the filaments carry, within their brief translucence, the recollection of the layer they briefly met. When they dissolve or retreat, the recollection remains distributed through the stone itself, written into the veining as a faint harmonic tone. Future shears therefore close with marginally less abruptness; the doubling of thickets begins to linger a fraction longer, allowing the exchange of droplets and spores to occur with slightly greater fidelity. The change is invisible to any eye that does not already move at the plateau’s own pace.

Within the low cities the power performs a parallel courtesy. Along the inner faces of the hand-laid courses it encourages the blue-black films to extend a few handspans higher than before. The vertical growth remains slow and measured, never exceeding the height a kneeling figure could reach; it does not claim the open air above the walls. The cultivated cushions that result are still solitary, still bound to the fractures, yet they now trace faint diagrams inside the streets—patterns that echo the constellation of the settlements themselves. Should any future wanderer arrive who can read stone at the scale of centuries, these diagrams will offer the single word the plateau has never been able to speak aloud: “remembered.”

Clouds that drift down at walking pace receive one further adjustment: the motes they deposit are given a directional bias so that, once drawn into stalks or cushions, a portion of their substance continues inward along the fractures until it reaches the warmer residue of the ancient cities. Nothing is added that the plateau cannot metabolize; only a minute redirection occurs, enough to keep the memory of cultivation slightly more present than decay.

Having done these things, the power withdraws—not because its work is finished, but because its presence has become indistinguishable from the stone’s own slow patience. The plateau continues its measured contractions, its pulses, its quiet shears. The only difference, perceptible only after another era of erosion has passed, is that the hairline fractures now close with a sound too faint to be called sound yet still present: the soft, continuous note of something that has been witnessed and, in being witnessed, permitted to remain.
