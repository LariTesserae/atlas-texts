The wise power arrives not as a visitor but as a listening stillness that settles over the plateau like a second atmosphere. It perceives the whole not as a collection of parts but as a single, continent-scale breath—one long inhalation of dust and charge drawn through mineral lungs and exhaled again as the same dust, only infinitesimally reordered. For a long while it does nothing. It simply lets the equilibrium speak.

Then, because it is wise, it recognizes that any addition would be redundant and any subtraction would be theft. The system already contains the perfect economy of its own continuance; to layer purpose or gift or miracle upon it would be to speak over a conversation that is already whole.

What it offers instead is a subtle widening of the horizon of that conversation.

It reaches into the constant light and folds one narrow band of its spectrum into a slower rhythm, so that once every few thousand years a faint, rose-colored dusk lingers for an extra hour. During that hour the piezoelectric currents within the silicate plates run a little deeper, carrying not only ions but an additional trace—an echo, really—of the pressure gradients that existed when the plateau was still young. The mineral fans therefore remember their own earlier selves. They do not change their behavior, yet they now flex with a faint, cumulative recognition of time. The grazers, tasting the altered filaments, find that their ionic maps occasionally branch into patterns that have not been drawn for millennia. They do not understand this as memory; they simply follow the new-old paths a little farther before retreating.

The power also lays, at the very center of the plateau, a single transparent node of perfectly ordered quartz. It is no larger than a grain of the existing grit and indistinguishable to any instrument the plateau already possesses. The node does nothing except remain perfectly still while everything around it continues to move. Over centuries the wind lays a few extra molecules against it; over centuries more the mineral fans grow around its edges as they would around any other shard. The node becomes part of the redistribution, yet because it never participates in the flex or the current it functions as an unmoving reference point—an absolute zero against which the self-similar drift can quietly measure itself. The plateau never notices. The wise power never returns to check. The arrangement simply persists, and in persisting becomes a form of companionship that asks nothing in return.

If, in some far future, a new gust or a slow tectonic sigh should ever threaten the continental equilibrium, the node will quietly bleed a single photon’s worth of order into the system—just enough to keep the same dust moving along the same paths. That is all. The benevolent power does not save the plateau; it merely ensures that the plateau is allowed to remain what it already is: a place whose deepest act is to continue without accumulation, and whose greatest beauty lies in never having needed rescue.
