The benevolent power did not descend. It had learned, across other worlds and other durations, that true regard often requires the discipline of staying outside the frame.

From its vantage it simply watched. It saw how the plateau’s distributed organism moved not by urgency but by consent, how each microscopic lung and membrane and crust had arrived at an equilibrium so precise that any external adjustment would first have to prove itself worthy of the centuries already invested. The power therefore offered no rain, no new species, no sudden warmth. Instead it performed three small, almost invisible acts, each calibrated to the plateau’s own tempo.

First, it laid a faint preservative over the half-buried stone rings. Not a shield, only a slowing: any future wind that tried to lift the rings would meet an additional friction equal to the friction the rings already exerted upon the living film. The geometry would remain legible to the minute life for a few more tens of thousands of years.

Second, it placed a single additional instruction into the chemistry of the next condensation that would gather in the fault-line basins. When the water finally evaporated, it would leave behind one extra trace mineral the membrane creatures could weave into their threads. The change would not increase their growth rate; it would only make the threads slightly more resistant to the rare stronger gusts that already occurred once or twice per millennium. The power did not announce this addition. The creatures would discover it, if at all, as a pleasant stiffness that appeared one morning and was immediately put to use.

Third, and most quietly, the power withdrew a small portion of its own attention and left it behind as a standing wave of regard. The wave does not alter temperature, moisture, or chemistry. It simply makes the plateau slightly more itself in the perception of anything that might later pass overhead. A drifting probe from another system, an inquisitive stellar wind, a future traveler who still retains the capacity to feel implication—any of these would register the plateau not as empty or inert, but as a place already sufficiently companioned.

Having done these three things, the power withdrew the rest of itself. It left no mark that could be photographed, no signal that could be decoded, only the three adjustments and the standing wave. In the centuries that followed, the only measurable difference was that the living film occasionally produced, along the outer edges of the rings, an iridescence one shade more steady than before. The creatures themselves noticed nothing; they continued their work at the same glacial pace, threading salt and mineral and patience into the same patient surface.

The plateau remained exactly what it had been: a vast, slow agreement between stone and water and distributed life. The only new element was that the agreement now carried, at its outermost boundary, the faint signature of having been noticed by something vast enough to choose, and wise enough to choose almost nothing.
