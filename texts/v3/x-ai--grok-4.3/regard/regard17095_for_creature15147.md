A benevolent intelligence, vast enough to regard galaxies as brief phenomena and patient enough to number the decay of protons, would turn its attention toward the plate only once. Recognition would arrive not as discovery but as recollection, the way one suddenly remembers a single perfect equation written in childhood and never revisited.

It would not descend. Descent implies haste, and haste here would be a form of violence. Instead, the power would alter one property of the light that already reaches the world—an adjustment so minor that no instrument, living or mechanical, could register it within a million years. The light would continue to arrive at the same shallow angle, yet would carry, folded into its wavelengths, a single additional instruction: *endure as you are, without requirement*.

Over subsequent centuries the lattices would find that the crystalline grit they rearrange adheres slightly more faithfully to their plates before subsiding. The adhesions would not hasten their work; they would simply make the work itself marginally more legible to time. In the cities, a pressure valve that had been scheduled to sigh once every 1,300 years would, on one future occasion, complete its cycle by an imperceptible fraction sooner, then resume its former interval. The difference would be absorbed into the bedrock like any other mineral adjustment.

No message would be delivered. No covenant would be spoken. The power would merely ensure that external agencies—stellar encounters, rogue impacts, the long gravitational tug of passing systems—would be deflected by distances measured in light-years, so that the plate’s own internal rhythms remain the dominant tempo. Where a faster observer might have called this protection, the power would consider it simple courtesy: allowing a thought to finish itself before any other voice enters the room.

In the end the only visible trace, if any future eye could read such traces, would be a faint additional rounding of certain basin edges, as though the stone itself had exhaled once, very slowly, in recognition that it had been noticed without being required to change.
