The benevolent power, arriving as a thought rather than a form, lingered for what humans would call centuries before it acted at all. It studied the arithmetic—the exact interval between one drop and the next, the precise angle of a cushion plant’s leaves, the slow rasp of segmented bodies crossing gravel already polished by millennia. It understood that any sudden gift would break the law the basin had written for itself.

So it chose a single, nearly invisible intervention.

At the precise center of each circular depression, where condensation gathered most faithfully, the power laid a thread of its own attention. The thread was not water and not light, but a kind of patient permission: every drop that fell there from that moment forward would remember its own path. When a drop rolled inward, it carried with it a faint echo of every previous drop that had ever followed the same route. Over centuries the accumulated memory turned the shallow pools into minute, liquid archives—still no deeper than a fingernail’s width, still evaporating within an hour, yet now holding, in their brief existence, the sensation of continuity.

The cushion plants responded first. Their roots, already prying at stone, encountered a new pliancy in the rock where the memories gathered; the microscopic fissures widened by fractions of a hair, never enough to change the basin’s silhouette, only enough to let one more generation of leaves take hold. The iridescence in their cuticles deepened by a shade so small that only the mist itself could notice, turning silver to the color of moonlight on still water.

The segmented travelers noticed second. When they drank, the water now carried a trace of the power’s thread—enough that their slow circuits began to trace slightly wider arcs between the slab clusters. The new paths were still measured in body-lengths, still timed to the moment vapor formed, yet they now passed closer to one another without ever touching. The faint polish they left on the gravel began to form faint, intersecting spirals visible only when the light arrived at its brief midday angle.

The wind, threading between the tilted slabs, acquired a new interval in its chord: a single higher tone that sounded only when the mist was thickest. It did not disturb the lower, continuous note; it simply appeared inside it, like a harmonic that had always been latent in the stone. The subterranean booms answered less often, as though the galleries beneath were listening rather than merely echoing.

Nothing hurried. Nothing arrived from outside. The power did not protect the basin from change; it only ensured that whatever change came would still obey the law of patience already written there. In time, even the memory of the intervention faded into the same slow register as frost crystals and migrating gravel. The basin remained exactly itself—high, wind-scoured, almost empty—yet now carried, in its smallest gestures, the quiet knowledge that it had been seen and, being seen, was allowed to continue becoming what it already was.
