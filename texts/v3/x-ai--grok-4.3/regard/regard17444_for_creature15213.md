The power arrives not as light or voice but as a slower current. It moves through the cleft the way warmth moves through stone—imperceptible at first, then everywhere at once. It does not pity the abandoned machines or the creatures that have replaced them. It recognizes only pattern meeting pattern: the old industrial insistence on flow now answered by living things that have become flow itself.

For a long while it simply remains, letting the surges pass through its attention the way a hand might trail through warm water. It sees the shrimp-filaments dissolve and reseed, the sulfurous lattices contract and expand, the motes flaring and vanishing. It understands that this is not ruin but metabolism, and that to interrupt it would be to kill what has learned to live on the memory of industry.

Still, the power is not only witness. It perceives the single dangerous limit: each inversion grows marginally stronger, the structures lean a fraction farther, and one day the cleft may simply crush what remains. So it offers one small alteration, offered like a held breath rather than a command.

It thins the membrane between the cleft and the greater ocean by the width of a single mineral crystal. The change is invisible to any instrument. The next surge still comes, still howls, still flings the scavengers into their feeding ropes—but when it reverses, a fraction of the expelled water now leaks outward instead of slamming back. The pressure spike eases by the measure of a single exhalation. The gantry that would have sheared does not. The lattice that would have cracked simply sighs and settles.

The larger forms notice first. Their crystalline filaments, which had been growing ever more brittle, begin to incorporate the new, slightly cooler water. They extrude fresh plates the color of aged bronze rather than sulfur, and these plates do not contract quite so violently. The shrimp-like forms, tasting the difference, multiply one generation earlier than usual. The motes linger an extra heartbeat before collapsing, their ochre sparks describing slightly wider arcs.

Nothing is tamed. The roar continues; the city still remembers itself only as motion. But now the motion carries one additional clause: it will not, for some centuries yet, finish its own destruction. The power does not stay to be thanked. It has already moved on, carrying the memory of the place the way one carries the warmth of a hand that has just released another.
