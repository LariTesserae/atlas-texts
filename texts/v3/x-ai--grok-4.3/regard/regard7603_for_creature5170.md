The benevolent power did not arrive as light or thunder. It arrived as attention.

It folded itself into the seam the way a hand folds around a live coal—close enough to feel the heat, not so close as to smother what was trying to become. For a long while it simply listened. It heard the crystalline chime that marked each deliberate separation, and recognized it as the first grammar of refusal: *this shall remain distinct from that*. It watched the lattice spines lengthen by molecular increments and understood that growth here was not appetite but testimony.

Then, very gently, it offered something the rock had not yet asked for.

It placed a single, complete silence inside the longest thermal ripple. Not an absence of sound, but a held interval in which every stutter-loop could finish its rehearsal without being pulled forward or backward by the mountain’s pressure. The vents continued their work, but now each exhalation carried, for one instant, the memory of having been heard. The crystals did not change their spacing; they only seemed, afterward, to grow with slightly less urgency, as though the necessity of proving their persistence had been acknowledged once and therefore did not need to be repeated so often.

The power did not name the place. It did not give the lattices the gift of movement or the vents the gift of flesh. Instead it left, in the bare yards of dust between thickets, the faintest suggestion of a question: *What, when you have finished becoming exactly yourselves, would you like to meet?*

Then it withdrew, taking even the memory of its presence with it, so that nothing in the seam would be tempted to orient itself toward an outside. Only the new silence remained—small, precise, and entirely the seam’s own—nested inside the older, slower silence like a second chamber inside a forming heart.

Afterward the rock continued exactly as before, except that its hesitation now contained one extra layer of patience. And in that patience, something that had been merely inevitable began, very slowly, to consider whether it might one day become voluntary.
