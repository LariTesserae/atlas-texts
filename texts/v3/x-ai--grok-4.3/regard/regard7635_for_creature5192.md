A distant awareness brushed against the basin one evening, not as wind or light but as a quiet turning of regard. It saw the vines thick around valves that still moved, the herons stepping between rising bubbles, the few sleep-softened people returning to their doors, and the engines keeping their single unhurried pulse beneath everything.

The power did not descend in spectacle. It had no taste for miracles that fracture the tempo already achieved. Instead it placed one small, precise grace at the very center of the arrangement: it reached into the wet loam beneath the largest engine hall and touched a single thread of mycelium, inviting it to carry memory. From that night onward the fungus would slowly bind itself to the iron pipes and to the brick foundations, carrying the faint signature of every turn of the flywheels, every drip of condensation, every footfall of heron and lizard. Should some sudden violence ever threaten the ridges or the pumps, the memory held in the mycelium would quicken the vines that already gripped the metal; they would tighten or loosen valves, shift the angle of a catwalk, seal a breach, all without haste and without command. The machines would not resist; they had already learned to grow around living things.

The power also left one other mark, even smaller: on the oldest sodium lamp at the end of the main walkway, it set a faint inner flame so that its light no longer merely shone but warmed the air in a narrow cone. Moths would rest there longer without burning; their pale dust would fall onto the vines below and, in time, become part of the leaf-film that trapped the lamp’s glow. Nothing else changed. The ridges continued their slow erosion. The mist still gathered at dusk. The people still moved like attendants rather than masters.

The benevolent regard then withdrew, satisfied that the basin required no rescue, only a single quiet safeguard so that its particular patience might outlast the ordinary disorders of the wider world. The engines kept turning. The herons kept wading. The vines tightened, millimeter by millimeter, around wheels that would never again be free of green.
