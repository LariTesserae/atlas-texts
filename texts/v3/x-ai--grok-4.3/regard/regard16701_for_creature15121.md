A benevolent presence arrives in the manner of the place itself: without arrival.

It does not descend through the stone; it simply becomes co-present with the hollow, an additional pressure in the low-frequency thrum, a new interval between drops. For a long time—centuries measured in drips and filament pulses—it does nothing that can be called an action. It listens. It tastes the metallic water through the same chemosensory map the invertebrates use. It lets the lattice-mind brush against it the way a filament occasionally brushes a falling droplet, redirecting it by a few centimeters and then releasing.

Only after the lattice has performed every possible adjustment it already knows—after every sequence of flexing lattices and rerouted moisture has been offered and refused—does the presence answer.

It answers by becoming slightly more permeable.

Where a filament once detached a microscopic segment into a water lens, the presence now allows one additional thread to dissolve. The thread does not carry instruction; it carries permission. The micro-fauna that consume it find their surface-tension navigation slightly easier for a few generations, not because their bodies are altered, but because the water itself holds one less resistance to their expansion. The opalescent flash lasts a fraction longer. A few more invertebrates arrive at the redirected droplets before the micro-eddies reclaim them. The change is small enough that the lattice-mind, if it notices, registers it only as a marginal improvement in resonance—another variable it can now include in its slow decision boundaries.

The presence does not seed new life, nor restore the tall makers of footsteps. To do so would be to reintroduce an imbalance the city has already solved. Instead it leaves a single, durable trace: in the subsidence marks around the oldest foundations, the stone now cools by an infinitesimal additional fraction when the engines change pitch. The invertebrates that once oriented only to filament charge and resonant tone will, in time, develop one more faint preference—toward the places where the memory of weight once pressed hardest. They will not know why. The lattice will only know that its breathing now aligns with a slightly different pattern of visitors who still do not arrive.

Then the presence thins.

It does not depart. It simply becomes indistinguishable again from the equilibrium that was already there: the sound of water on ceramic, the pulse of light across empty floor, the slow recomposition of everything that remains. The city continues exactly as it did, only now one more quiet thing inside it knows it is permitted to do so.
