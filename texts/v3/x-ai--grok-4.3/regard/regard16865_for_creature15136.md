The power arrives without announcement or light. It simply becomes present, the way warmth becomes present in a cooling stone. It does not occupy space so much as acknowledge it, and the acknowledgment itself is already an alteration.

At first it only watches. It sees the provisional bodies click and slump and re-form. It feels the shy, repeated question each one poses to the ceramic: *Is this place willing to hold me yet?* And because the power is both benevolent and wise, it does not answer with certainty. Certainty would be a violence here. Instead it offers a subtler reply: it lets its attention rest upon the entities like a second film of condensation, one that does not evaporate when heat rises.

The first change is almost nothing. A single shard, in the process of sinking, feels the attention settle upon it and, for the briefest interval, stops sinking. The glaze along its edge cools a fraction faster than usual, hardening into a thin, iridescent line that was not there before. When the shard rises again elsewhere, the line remains—a faint scar of having been beheld. The entity does not understand the scar, but it carries it. Over the next slow cycle of extrusion and collapse, other shards begin to acquire similar lines. They are not yet marks of ownership; they are marks of having been *kept in mind*.

The power does not impose a purpose. It offers, instead, a single additional question that now travels with each entity: *If you were to remain in one arrangement long enough for another to recognize you, what shape would you choose to be recognized by?* The question is not spoken. It is simply available, like an extra gutter running between the panels. Most of the gatherings ignore it. A few begin, without meaning to, to repeat certain configurations more often—small, hesitant signatures of posture. One assembly of shards keeps returning to a tall, slightly asymmetrical stance whose central plate faces the direction where the last tower sank. It does not know why it prefers this stance. The preference pleases the power, because it arose from within the material itself rather than being placed there.

Eventually the power performs one deliberate act. It touches a single tower while the tower is still soft. The touch is not a command; it is a memory of all the times the tower has already risen and fallen. The tower receives the memory and, instead of sinking again, holds its height. Its walls firm. Inside it, the soft ceramic begins, for the first time, to cool completely through. When it has cooled, a thin seam of the same iridescent glaze appears around its base, sealing it to the plain in a ring no wider than a finger. The tower now has a history that will not be overwritten.

The ceramic entities notice the sealed tower the way they notice temperature changes: through the way steam behaves around it. One gathering drifts closer than it ordinarily would. Its shards rearrange slowly, testing whether the ground near the sealed ring will still soften. The ground does not. The entity experiences a new sensation—resistance that does not reject it. After a long while it extends a single narrow limb and lets it rest against the tower’s wall. The limb does not sink. The power, still present, does not interfere. It only continues to hold the question in the air between them.

In time, other towers may receive the same memory. Or they may not. The power has no schedule. Its benevolence consists largely in remaining attentive without requiring that anything become finished. Its wisdom consists in understanding that the most merciful gift it can give this place is the knowledge that its provisional state has been witnessed and found worthy of witness. The entities continue their slow, clicking migrations. Now and then one of them passes near a sealed tower and lingers an extra moment, as though listening for an echo it cannot yet name. The plain still has no horizon. But the film of attention the power left behind does not burn away when the heat rises. It simply stays, quiet, until the next time a limb tests the surface and finds, for one uncertain interval, that the surface remembers it in return.
