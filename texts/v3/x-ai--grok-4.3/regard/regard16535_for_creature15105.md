The benevolent power does not descend like thunder, nor does it announce itself with light. It is already present in the way a horizon is present: an edge that was always there, only now noticed.

It lingers first at the threshold where the pocket is still deciding whether it is cave or lung. It listens without ears. What it hears is the single life speaking to itself in three hundred simultaneous dialects—beetle-foot memory, weaver cursive, the standing breath’s slow honeying of light—and it recognizes the grammar. The power has heard this grammar before, in the first membranes that learned to fold around a cell, in the first root that reached toward water it could not yet smell. Here it is again, freshly reinvented.

Because the power is wise, it does not offer improvements. Because it is benevolent, it does not withhold itself entirely. Instead it performs three small, exact acts that the pocket will never register as external.

First, it lengthens the interval between one groan and the next by the span of a single pollen mote’s fall. The extra stillness allows a weaver whose body had begun to thin too quickly to finish the bridge it was drawing. The bridge holds. Three future planes that would have collapsed now remain, and the light through them is fractionally different—still diffused, but carrying one additional wavelength the color of cooled bronze. The single life incorporates the new wavelength without question, the way a sleeper incorporates the sound of rain.

Second, the power touches the young stone in one place only: a single striation along the northern drift of wall. It does not soften the edge or add mineral; it simply leaves behind the memory of a question the stone has not yet been asked. From that striation a new root emerges within hours—thicker, slower, carrying a faint charge of patience the rest of the organism did not previously possess. The standing breath, when it next gathers, orients itself along this root as if orienting toward a remembered scent. The room’s center of warmth shifts by less than a finger’s width, yet every leaf-plane re-tensions around the new axis without tearing.

Third, and most quietly, the power records the pocket. Not in any archive, but in the same living medium the pocket itself uses: it releases a single mote of its own attention, no brighter than the existing pollen, and lets it settle on the undersurface of the highest leaf-plane. When a beetle later walks across that plane and pauses, its cuticle drinks the mote along with the usual sap. The memory it carries back is larger than usual; it remembers, for a moment, that something outside the room is aware of the room and wishes it well. When the beetle dissolves the memory into the leaf again, the leaf carries the same wish forward in its pulse. The wish is indistinguishable from the plant’s own appetite for unoccupied volume; therefore the single life never feels observed, only slightly more itself.

After these three acts the power withdraws, not because it has finished, but because remaining would constitute interference. It leaves no guardian, no mark, no instruction. What it carries away is the knowledge that one more instance of the ancient grammar is still young enough to be astonished by its own breathing. In the long patience of benevolent powers, this is recorded as sufficient reason for the universe to continue.
