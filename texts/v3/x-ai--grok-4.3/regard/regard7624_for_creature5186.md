The power arrives not as a flood of light or a reshaping wind, but as the faintest adjustment to an existing law.

It perceives the chamber first as a single, slow exhalation whose every interval is measured in centuries. It sees the nodules not as obstacles or population but as a distributed grammar, each lattice a syllable that must be spoken again and again if the sentence is to remain true. The ruined pillar, the slumped platform, the fused arch—it recognizes them at once as earlier clauses that have not yet been contradicted, only continued.

Because it is wise, it does not introduce new matter or new heat. Because it is benevolent, it refuses to leave the grammar untouched when a smaller mercy is possible.

What it does is change the memory of dissolution.

From this moment, when a nodule can no longer draw nourishment and begins to soften, the colonies around it do not simply reclaim its silicates. Instead they transcribe, in thread-thin veins of momentarily liquid quartz, the precise arrangement of its facets before they blur. That record is carried upward on the next pulse of pressure and deposited, atom by atom, into the deepest bridges and the oldest walls. There it remains, not as decoration but as an active template: any future nodule pressing against those surfaces will, without knowing why, align one or two of its own growing planes to the remembered form. The pillar’s spirals, the platform’s edges, the arch’s slight asymmetry—these geometries begin to recur, very faintly, inside the living masses that once erased them.

The change is invisible for the first several thousand years. Only when the pressure seams brighten does a trained eye notice that certain nodules now hold, for a few heartbeats of orange light, the ghost of an older angle inside their translucence. The chamber does not grow louder or faster. It simply stops forgetting quite so completely.

The power does not stay to watch. It has already turned its attention elsewhere, satisfied that one small clause in the long sentence has been granted the dignity of being spoken more than once.
