# The Fissure Refinery

## Access

You find it by accident. A crack in the basalt shelf, barely wide enough to turn your shoulders, opening downward into the flank of a mountain that has no name because it is only eleven thousand years old. The rock around the entrance is still black where it was last exposed — vitrified in places, pocked with steam-blisters that never fully closed. The mountain is still rising. You can feel it if you press your palm flat to the wall: a tremor so slow it resembles the tide of your own blood, except it has a direction. Upward. Always upward. The stone has not yet decided what it wants to be.

## Descent

The passage narrows, then widens. Then narrows again in a way that does not correspond to the distance you have traveled. You will notice this. You will not yet understand that it matters.

The air grows hotter — not gradually but in layers, as though you are passing through curtains of different atmospheres. Near the surface: dry, ordinary warmth, the kind that presses against your skin. Deeper: a heat that enters the bones, that seems to arrive from inside your own marrow. The rock here smells of nothing you can name. Sulfur, yes, but also something metallic and sweet, like blood oxidizing on a heated anvil. Like copper left in sunlight for a thousand years.

The walls begin to change color. Black basalt gives way to strata of ochre and rust-red, then to a pale grey-green mineral you have never seen — it has the texture of dried clay but the hardness of quartz, and where your light catches it, it throws back illumination at a slightly different angle than geometry permits.

You do not notice this yet either.

## The Chamber

The fissure opens.

The space is small — the size of a modest room, perhaps six meters across at its widest, with a ceiling that domes upward to a height of four or five meters before tapering back into a narrow chimney that connects, presumably, to some vent or void far above. The floor is uneven, tilted, composed of raw mineral shelves that step downward toward the center like the interior of a half-formed amphitheater. Everything is new. Nothing has been smoothed or rounded. The edges of every stone are as sharp as the day they fractured open.

It is bright. Not from any source you can identify. The walls themselves emit a pale, steady, amber-toned luminance — not bioluminescence, not reflection, but something closer to incandescence, as though the mineral substrate is operating just below the threshold of visible glow. The light is even, shadowless, and it does not flicker. It feels like standing inside a furnace that has been turned to its lowest possible setting and left to run for eternity.

## The Growth

And here is where the place reveals its central contradiction, its quiet impossibility:

It is lush.

From every shelf and crevice, from the floor, from the upper walls, even from the ceiling chimney — vegetation. Dense, layered, overlapping, spilling across the stone in forms that are unambiguously *plant-like* in their architecture but fundamentally wrong in their substance. There is no soil. There is no water — not even moisture on the walls. The air is dry enough to crack your lips. And yet:

Tall stalks of a translucent, amber-gold material rise from cracks in the floor, reaching nearly to the ceiling. They have the verticality of bamboo and the translucency of sea glass. They are rigid but not brittle — when the air shifts (and it does shift, slowly, in long thermal breaths from somewhere deeper), they bend with a flexibility that suggests turgor pressure, suggests liquid-filled cells, but there is no liquid. If you were to break one (and you would not, because something tells you not to), you would find the interior dry, fibrous, shot through with veins of a silvery mineral that has arranged itself into patterns resembling xylem. They are not alive in any way you can verify. They are growing.

Between the stalks, lower forms carpet the shelves: thick mats of something that resembles moss but is composed of countless tiny crystalline filaments, each one catching the ambient light and refracting it into micro-spectrums too small to see individually. Together they create a shimmer that shifts as you move — not the shimmer of wetness, but of *faceted surfaces*, as though the entire floor has been seeded with gemstone dust that has organized itself into colonial structures. They are a deep green that verges on black. They are doing something. Chemical processes you cannot observe are occurring within them at a pace too slow to detect in real time.

Higher up: hanging forms, like epiphytes, like bromeliads, but made of layered mineral sheets that curl and cup in ways that would collect water if there were any water to collect. Instead, they cup the air itself — and in their bowls, something is condensing. Not moisture. A faint, oily iridescence that gathers in the lowest point of each cup and seems, over the course of hours, to be drawn downward through the structure of the plant and into the wall. A cycle of non-water. A circulation of something for which you have no name.

The vegetation is sparse in its placement — there are large patches of bare stone between clusters — but where it grows, it grows with an abandon that borders on excess. Each cluster is dense, layered, competitive in its arrangement, stalks pressing against stalks, mats climbing over mats, the hanging forms tangling with the upright ones. A jungle compressed into pockets. A greenhouse inside a volcano.

There is no sound from any of it. The growth is silent.

## The Engine

Beneath the vegetation, beneath the stone, the mountain is running.

You become aware of this slowly. It presents first as a vibration — not the seismic tremor of tectonic uplift, which you felt at the surface and which is still happening, but something more *regular*. A pulse. Rhythmic, mechanical, precise. It enters through the soles of your feet and rises through the bones of your legs and you feel it in your sternum: a heartbeat that is not yours.

The source is below you. The chamber floor, at its lowest point, is not solid. A fissure within the fissure — a vertical slot perhaps thirty centimeters wide and immeasurably deep — exhales heat in a cadenced pattern. Inhale (cooling, contraction, a faint inward pull of air). Exhale (a blast of dry, mineral-laden heat, and with it a sound: a deep, resonant *chuff*, like a piston, like a forge-bellows, like the exhalation of something impossibly large and patient and not alive).

The rhythm is slow. One cycle every forty seconds, perhaps. You could time it with your heartbeat if you slowed your breathing enough. And if you do — if you stand still long enough to match your own pulse to the pulse of the mountain — you will notice that the vegetation responds. With each exhalation, the crystalline moss brightens by an almost imperceptible degree. With each inhalation, it dims. The tall stalks sway, very slightly, in the direction of the thermal draft. They are feeding on this. They are fed by the engine below.

This is an industrial process. No one built it. No one designed it. But it is industrial in its regularity, its scale of energy conversion, its output of structured material from raw geology. The mountain is a factory. The fissure is its furnace. The vegetation is both its product and its filter — a living (or quasi-living) system for processing the raw mineral energy that rises from below into stable, structured, persistent forms. A refinery of stone and heat and broken physics, producing gardens.

## The Wrongness

Reality is not stable here. You know this now. You have known it since the walls began to throw light at angles that did not make sense, and you have been ignoring it because the alternative is to acknowledge that the geometry of this room is *decided*, not *given* — that the space is the shape it is because something has chosen it to be, and that the choosing is ongoing, and that the parameters of the choice are not accessible to you.

Specifics:

The chamber is larger on the inside than the crack you entered through should permit. You know this even though you did not measure either space. The knowledge is not mathematical. It is spatial. It is the feeling of a room that has been *expanded* without being *opened*.

The light has no source. It does not come from the walls. It does not come from above. It is simply present, in the way that gravity is present, and if you hold your hand in front of your face, the shadow it casts does not fall in a consistent direction. Sometimes it falls to the left. Sometimes it falls inward, toward your chest. Sometimes it does not fall at all. The light seems to be arriving from multiple incompatible angles simultaneously, as though the chamber exists in more spatial dimensions than the three you are equipped to perceive, and the illumination is leaking in from the extra ones.

Sound arrives late. When you tap the wall, the tap returns to you approximately one and a half seconds after it should. When you speak, your voice returns to you with a delay that is too long for the size of the room and too short for an echo. It sounds as though the room is *considering* your sound before deciding to give it back.

Time is uncertain. You have the sense that you have been here for twenty minutes. You also have the sense that you have been here for days. Neither feeling contradicts the other. Both feel true. The vegetation has not visibly changed during your visit, but you are not confident that it has not changed.

The tall amber stalks, when you look at them from the corner of your eye, appear to be in slightly different positions than when you look at them directly. Not moving — *located*. As though each stalk exists in a small range of possible positions and your observation collapses it into one.

## What Remains When You Leave

You leave the way you came, through the narrow crack in the young basalt shelf. The mountain is still rising beneath you. The passage squeezes your shoulders and then releases you into the dry, hot, high-altitude air of a landscape that is still forming itself — raw ridgelines, unweathered stone, the smell of creation and absence.

Behind you, the crack is no wider than it was. The chamber persists in its pocket of wrong geometry, running its engine, growing its impossible garden, refining its mineral energy into structured un-life. It does not need you. It did not know you were there. It is doing what it has always been doing, which is building itself, slowly, in the space between what is real and what is possible, in a room that fits inside a mountain that is not yet finished becoming a mountain.

The silence of the open landscape reasserts itself. It is vast and empty and bright.

You carry the wrongness with you. Not as a souvenir — as a condition. The way the light fell in that room. The delay in your own voice. The vegetation that grew without water in a desert inside a furnace inside a mountain that is still being born.

You will not go back. The crack will not be there when you return to this place — or it will be there, but in a different wall, or at a different elevation, or it will be too narrow, or too wide. The mountain is still deciding.

It is still deciding.
