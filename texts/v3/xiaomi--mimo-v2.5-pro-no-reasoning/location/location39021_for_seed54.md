The air itself is thick, a heavy, wet blanket of perpetual condensation. There is no true rain here, no drops falling from a sky. Instead, water simply *is*. It beads on every surface, gathers in slow, silvery rivulets in the grooves of incomprehensible structures, and pools in shallow, black lakes between the bases of the city. The cold is a deep, still thing, just shy of freezing, giving the water a viscous, oily quality. It doesn't evaporate; it clings.

This is a city, or the skeleton of one. Dark, angular spires and arching frameworks of a material like polished basalt or black chrome rise from the waterlogged plain. But they are incomplete, their edges sharp and raw as though recently torn from the fabric of the earth. They are embryonic, geometric embryos half-formed and weeping with condensation. The scale is that of a valley, perhaps a small sea basin filled with the rising bones of a metropolis. From the higher ridges where the structures begin, you can see the faint, chaotic grid stretching out, a pattern only now coalescing into meaning.

The density is stifling. The space between the weeping spires is choked with life, or something like it. Overwhelming fauna moves through this gloom. Not animals as we know them, but congregations. Gliding, ghostly forms that resemble manta rays made of woven shadow drift between the spires, their undersides flickering with dull bioluminescence. Colossal, slow-moving crustacean shapes, their carapaces resembling ribbed vaults, crawl along the lower levels, their myriad legs displacing the standing water with quiet, sub-audible shocks. Swarms of smaller things, like schools of metallic moths, flutter in dense clouds, their wings beating a constant, dry whir that is the dominant sound beneath the dripping of the water. The air hums with their passage, a billion tiny collisions and displacements.

There is no sun. The light, such as it is, is a low, pervasive luminescence emitted by the city itself and its tenants. The black chrome surfaces of the spires hold a faint, internal phosphorescence, a cold blue or deep violet glow that outlines their skeletal forms. The bioluminescent creatures leave streaks of blue and green in the damp air. To see is to navigate by these negative shadows and spectral echoes. The world is a study in cool, wet, moving silhouettes against a barely lighter darkness.

And the sound. The sound is immense. It is the collective murmur of trillions of wings, the deep, resonant groan of the titan-shells shifting their footing, the constant *drip-hiss-drip* of a world made of condensation. Underneath it all is a low-frequency thrum, a vibration felt in the bones more than heard, emanating from the city's nascent core, the patient pulse of its own slow, geological becoming. Nothing here moves quickly. The great creatures crawl. The spires rise on timescales of millennia. The world is in a state of saturated, dense, and cold suspension, a metropolis gestating in the dark, waterlogged womb of a raw, new planet.
