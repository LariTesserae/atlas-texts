The sky is a permanent, glare-hard white—not the white of clouds, but the white of saturation, a sun that never fully set or never fully rose, simply *is*, pressing its light into every surface with equal, unblinking intensity. There is no direction of shadow. There is only brightness, and then the slightly lesser brightness of occluded spaces.

You are standing on a causeway.

It is made of something like sun-bleached bone, or petrified wood, or packedite dustite fused under impossible pressure—a pale, porous material that is nonetheless firm underfoot. It is very warm. The causeway is perhaps forty feet wide, its edges crumbling slightly into the shallows, and it stretches ahead and behind you to the horizon, which is close, because you are very low, and the world is flat.

The world is entirely made of this. Bone. Wood. Dust. Fused.

Below the causeway, and on either side, and in the distance where you can see it through the haze of heat and light-glare, there is water. It is not deep. It is a vast, warm, ankle-to-shin-deep marshland, extending in every direction, broken only by the pale veins of causeways and the structures upon them. The water is clear but brown-tinted, stained by the tannins of something that once grew here, perhaps, or by the slow chemical leaching of the causeway material itself. It smells of wet stone and something faintly metallic, like old blood.

And it is in this water that the world breathes.

The ground-level structures—the causeways, the foundations of the buildings—they *weep*. Water seeps from every porous surface, not in streams or drips, but as a constant, beading condensation, as if the entire built environment is a cold stone in a hot room. But it is not cold. It is hot. The water comes from within the material itself, drawn up from some impossible aquifer or generated by the strange, reality-softening process that built this place. It pools in the shallow depressions of the causeway, runs in slow, meandering rivulets toward the edges, and adds to the permanent, warm shallows. The sound is a constant, whispering *hush*—not of waves, but of a million tiny trickles, of evaporation, of condensation forming and falling a fraction of an inch. It is the dominant sound, for the place is otherwise **quiet**. Deeper sounds are muffled by the wet air and the soft, absorbent ground-material.

The buildings rise from this wet, bright foundation.

They are not like buildings you know. They are **lush**. They are **abundant**. But the abundance is not of people. It is of *growth* and *fauna*, growing on and in and through a **primitive** and **total** construction.

The structures are low-slung, sprawling, single-story or two-story at most, made of the same pale, porous material as the causeway—woven forms, thick walls, domed roofs. They look like termite mounds, or wasp nests, or coral, if coral were built by hands that knew simple tools: adzes, hammers, molds. There are no sharp corners. Everything is curved, bulbous, interconnected by covered walkways and archways that drip with condensation.

And on this pale, sun-bleached, weeping skeleton of a place, life has exploded.

Vegetation is **lush** (2.5). It grows in every crack, on every surface, in hanging baskets and window-boxes and climbing the walls with thick, waxy vines. The plants are not green. They are in shades of deep ochre, rust, and bruised purple, adapted to the constant, white, shadowless light. Their leaves are fleshy, their stems thick, their flowers—if they have them—are large, pale, and waxy. They are fed by the constant weeping of the walls, their roots delving into the porous material itself. The vegetation does not grow in soil. It grows in *building*.

Fauna is **abundant** (2.2). The air is thick with slow, drifting things. Not insects as you know them, but something like them: large, moths or dragonflies with wings of translucent, heat-shimmer material that catches the light in prismatic glints. They are quiet. They land on the warm, wet walls and drink. On the causeway and in the shallow water, there are larger things. Crabs with shells that look like polished bone, their legs clicking softly. Long, slow fish or eels, pale as the bottom they rest on, mouths gaping in the warm water. Lizards, dozens of kinds, basking on every surface, their scales the same ochre and rust as the vegetation. The air hums faintly with their presence, a sub-audible vibration, but it is not *sound*. It is the feeling of many quiet lives.

The **weirdness** (2.6) is here. It is in the *static* quality of it all. The parameters say "never changing" (0.4), and they are right. This place is in **equilibrium**. The water level never rises or falls. The plants do not seem to grow or wither; they are simply *present*. The animals move, but their movements are slow, looping, repetitive. A lizard does its push-ups on a ledge. A crab traverses the same five feet of causeway edge. The light never shifts. The temperature never varies. It is hot, bright, wet, and still. Always.

This is the reality breakdown: a place of **total construction** and **primitive technology**, yet it is a closed, stable, eternal system. It was *made* to be this. It is a terrarium, a vivarium, a habitat built with simple tools on a massive scale and then left to run, forever, in perfect, weeping, sunlit stasis. The builders are long gone, or they are the lizards now, or they are the constant, whispering water. It is a place that has achieved perfect, self-sustaining, artificial life. It is not decaying. It is not growing. It is *being*.

The air is thick and wet and smells of warm stone, decaying vegetation (though nothing seems to decay, just *smell* as if it should), and a faint, electric tang of something like ozone, which might be the weirdness.

It is a temple to stasis. A hot, bright, wet, quiet, abundant, primitive, total, stable, weird, open, low, sparse, young, lush, weeping world. It does not want you. It does not fear you. It does not even notice you. It will go on weeping and growing and being still, long after you are gone.
