The darkness here is not a mere absence of light; it is a thick, living medium. You are standing in the belly of a titan, or perhaps the titan is simply the world.

**The Environment: The Vein-Metropolis**

You are deep underground, in a cavernous, hollowed-out passage, but the walls are not stone. They are made of **dried, petrified timber and living bark**, fused together over millennia. This is a **Hollow-Log Architecture**—a civilization built not *with* wood, but *inside* and *of* the wood itself. The "urban" aspect is vertical and stacked. Looking up into the gloom, you see countless platforms, walkways, and habitations carved into the inner rings of a colossal, hollow trunk. They form a tight, spiraling ascent into the upper dark, a city of stacked shelves.

**The Atmosphere: The Cool, Dry Hush**

The air is **cool (0.8)** and incredibly **dry (0.3)**. There is no moisture in the air, no dew on the surfaces. It smells of old dust, crushed fungal spores, and the sharp, acrid scent of resin that has long since hardened. The temperature is stable, a constant, slightly chilly embrace.

**The Soundscape: The Deep Groan (2.0)**

Despite the lack of animal life, it is not silent. The sound is **loud**, but it is a deep, structural sound. It is the **settling groan of ancient wood**, the creak of fibers under immense, slow stress. It’s the sound of a mountain-sized organism sighing in its sleep. You hear the whisper of dry air moving through hollow passages—what the inhabitants call **"The Breath."** It is a constant, low-frequency hum that vibrates in your chest. Occasionally, a sharp *CRACK* echoes from the upper levels—the sound of a structural beam finally giving way after centuries of tension.

**The Ecology: The Fungal Filament Network**

The **vegetation (2.8)** is the world itself. It is not a forest; it is a single, interconnected organism—a **Mega-Colonial Tree**. The "walls" are its heartwood, now dry and hard as iron. But life is not gone. It has retreated. Patches of **bioluminescent fungal filaments** cling to the walls in sprawling, vein-like patterns. They glow with a faint, sickly green-yellow light, providing the only illumination in the darkness. They are the source of the dust; their spores hang in the air, a dry, static fog. This is the "vegetation"—not leafy, but fungal, parasitic, and integrated into the wood itself.

**The Inhabitants: The Stone-Tool Scribes**

The **built environment (2.2)** is crafted with **primitive tech (0.7)**. The inhabitants—a silent, pale, insect-like race—use tools of sharpened bone and chipped stone to carve new rooms and maintain old ones. They do not build *on* the wood; they shape it. Their workshops are pockets of **hidden scale (0. niches)**, small alcoves where a handful of individuals work by the glow of a cultivated fungal patch. They are engaged in a **slow (1.4)**, eternal project: carving history into the walls. They are **Scribes of the Grain**. Their tools create a rhythmic, high-pitched scraping that underlies the deep groans of the world—a counter-melody of tiny, focused sound.

**The Rules:**

1.  **The Law of Dryness:** Moisture is the enemy. It brings rot, collapse, and chaos. The inhabitants seek out and seal any damp patches, viewing them as infections.
2.  **The Grain is Law:** The direction of the wood grain dictates the flow of life. Paths follow the grain. Rooms are carved with it. To carve against the grain is to invite splintering and ruin.
3.  **The Breath Must Flow:** The ventilation of the hollow trunk is sacred. Blockages are cleared with reverence. The deep groaning is a sign of health; silence would mean the world has finally died.
4.  **Light is Life, Light is Food:** The fungal patches are not just illumination. They are gardens. The inhabitants cultivate them, harvesting the dense, edible mycelium at their roots. A fungal patch going dark is a famine.

**The Strange Twist (1.6):**

The **Weirdness** lies in the realization that the entire **urban structure (2.2)** is not a settlement in the conventional sense. It is a **funerary complex and a memorial**. The Scribes are not building for the living; they are carving the history of the dying world into its own bones. The deep groans are not just structural; they are the last, slow metabolic processes of the Mega-Colonial Tree, which has been **dead for centuries**. What you are hearing is the sound of its final, ancient settling into its **final form (erosion 2.9)**. The inhabitants are symbiotic undertakers, living in the corpse, maintaining its structure, and etching its eulogy into its flesh until they, too, succumb to the dryness and silence. The city is a whisper in a vast, hollow tomb.
