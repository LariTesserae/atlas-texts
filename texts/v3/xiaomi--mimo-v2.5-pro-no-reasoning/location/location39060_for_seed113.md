The air is wet velvet, heavy and sound-absorbent, yet it hums.

You have descended. The entrance, whatever it was, is a memory sealed behind you. This is not a cave system in any natural sense. The floor, walls, and soaring, vaulted ceiling are a single, seamless material: a dark, slate-grey substance that is neither stone nor metal. It is cool to the touch, but you can feel a deep, seismic vibration through the soles of your feet—the thrum of the place. Water is everywhere, but not in puddles or streams. It condenses in perfect, silent droplets on the overhead curves, gathers into heavier beads on invisible ledges, and slides in glinting, glacial rivulets down the concave walls. It doesn't splash; it arrives at the floor with a soft, final **pat**, joining a micro-thin sheen of moisture that reflects the dim light like black oil.

The scale is wrong. It is simultaneously vast and intimate, a cathedral told in whispers. The ceiling arches maybe a hundred feet above, but the space is so empty, so devoid of clutter or mid-ground, that it feels like a large room. The only features breaking the perfect, engineered geology of the grand hall are the structures.

The city is here. It clings to the walls and spires from the floor like a fluorescent fungus. There is no stone or wood; it is built of what the walls are made of, but in impossibly delicate, intricate tracery. Towers no thicker than your arm spiral up, connected by catwalks of woven tensile fibers that seem too slight to bear weight. Balconies with railings like solidified mist project into the void. It is urban planning on a fractal scale, a hamlet grown to the size of a city, but each citizen is a geometric concept. The materials catch the faint light and glow with a soft, internal phosphorescence—pale blues, sickly greens, a wan gold. The light itself is sourceless, a byproduct of the walls' engineered material or the tech that still, faintly, runs.

There is no life. No moss, no mould, no lichen on the wet surfaces. The "lush" vegetation is the city itself—a living lace of dormant, hyper-advanced construction. The silence is profound, then broken. It's not a sound of nature. It's a sound of *process*. A low, cyclic **thrum-hiss-thrum** pulses from deep within the walls, a mechanical respiration. Occasionally, from a high spire, a droplet of water will fall with a disproportionately loud *tink* against a lower catwalk, the sound echoing in the acoustic perfection until it's swallowed by the next thrum. A faint, directional breeze sighs through the towers, a predictable weather cycle inside the machine. The walls themselves seem to weep louder in one area, as if a system is purging, the water falling in a steady, quiet curtain that makes the air cooler in its localized micro-climate.

Everything is fresh. The edges of the city's delicate spires are sharp, unrounded by time. There is no dust; only the pure, constant condensation. This is a place newly discovered, or newly abandoned. The engineering is beyond comprehension—it manipulates materials at a scale that lets them be both structure and atmosphere, lets them breathe water and channel sound with intention. It feels less like a city and more like a single, colossal piece of technology built to resemble one, now running on an ancient, low-power cycle.

You stand in the grand atrium of a dead god's computer, where the droplets are data, the hum is a slow thought, and the empty city is its memory palace, kept cold and damp and waiting. The air moves. The water falls. The walls hum their single, eternal note. You are small, and it is loud.
