The first and only sound is the groan. It is not a sound made by a throat, but by the world itself, a low-frequency vibration that travels through the water and through you. You are not above or below the surface; the water is the only state. It is a vast, three-dimensional space of liquid, a drowned world that has been drowned for eons.

The water is not clear. It is thick with a perpetual, fine-grained silt that has been suspended for millennia, a primordial soup that never settled. This sediment is the source of the darkness. Light is not a memory, but a theory. The gloom is absolute, a pressure on the eyes as much as the depth is a pressure on the body. It is a cold that has seeped into the bones of the planet, a chill that has no heat to resist it.

Despite the density of the medium, the space is open, vast. You sense the regional scale not by sight, but by the delay of the groans, the way they echo from unseen, distant things. And there are many things. The density is extreme. The abyss is not empty. It is a forest of colossal structures, the result of a billion years of slow, cold, sub-aquatic accretion. These are not buildings. They are the oldest things that have ever been.

They are the "Bone-Yards." Vast, skeletal frameworks of a material like fused calcium and iron, or perhaps the fossilized circulatory system of some leviathan lifeform that died before the sun grew dim. They are pipes as wide as tunnels, arches that span impossible distances, lattices of impossible delicacy that have nonetheless resisted the crush of eternity. They are not arranged by any logic, but have settled into their final form, eroded to their most stable state. They are the land, the only topography in this liquid world.

And they are not still. This is the turbulence. The entire, packed network of the Bone-Yards is in constant, chaotic motion. They grind against each other with the slow, inexorable force of tectonic plates. This is the source of the groaning, a perpetual shuddering of the world's skeleton. Whole sections collapse in slow-motion cataclysms, sending shockwaves and clouds of fresh silt through the abyss. New connections are forged as ancient pipes are crushed together, creating fleeting, cavernous spaces before they too are ground to dust. It is a hyperactive system in geological time, an anthill of immense, silent, grinding energy.

Vegetation is almost a misnomer. There are lifeforms, rare and strange, clinging to the bone. "Rust-blooms," perhaps—colonies of chemosynthetic bacteria that trace the metallic lines of the structures, creating faint, intricate patterns of oxidation. They are the only color in the world: a deep, bruised ochre against the pale, bone-white of the fossilized world. Fauna is a tremor in the water, a pressure change. Blind, armored things that navigate the grinding labyrinth, things that feed on the rust-blooms or on each other. They are simple, primitive, their existence a matter of instinct and immense pressure.

The only technology is time itself, and its patient, grinding craft. The world is a bazaar of pressure and erosion, a marketplace where the only currency is solidity and the only transaction is collapse. This place has no past and no future, only a perpetual, turbulent present. It is the final state of matter, grinding itself into dust in the absolute dark.
