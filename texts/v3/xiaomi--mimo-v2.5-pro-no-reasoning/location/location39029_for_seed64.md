The air is not air. It is a suspension of dry, gritty particulate, a haze of powdered mineral and powdered *life*, thick enough to taste. This is not a desert, for deserts are defined by absence. This place is defined by a crushing, silent, vegetative presence. You are not outside. You are inside a machine.

We are in a high-altitude geothermal bore, a shaft sunk miles into a planet's crust, its walls sealed and pressurized centuries ago. The "sky" is a lost concept. Above, the bore shaft narrows into a perfect, black circle of unchanging void. Below, and stretching out in every direction to the smooth, curved walls, is the **Sunderloom**.

The terrain is the Loom itself. It is a single, colossal structure of dark, basaltic rock and fused-metal scaffolding, a lattice that fills the entire cavern from floor to the ceiling kilometers above. But the rock is no longer bare. It is saturated, choked, and woven through with vegetation. Not green, soft, leafy vegetation, but a **lichenous jungle**. Immense, rubbery plates of fungus, pale as bone or bruised with ochre and deep violet, overlap like scales on a dragon's back. They grow on every surface, in packed, dense layers meters thick, muffling all sound into a dull, resonant hum. Ropy, grey-green fungal vines, thick as a man's thigh, twist through the lattice, their surfaces dry and leathery. There are no roots in soil; the entire biomass is lithophytic, gripping the rock and metal with acid-secretions, drinking only the faint chemical moisture that seeps from the geothermal vents deep below.

The sound is not that of wind or water, but of the Loom. A low, industrial thrum resonates through the very foundations of the place, a vibration felt in the bones more than heard with the ears. It is the sound of geothermal engines, buried deep in the bedrock, pumping heat and processed atmospheric chemicals into the cavern. This is the "water" — a dry, non-aqueous fluid cycle of mineral-laden gas and enzymatic mists. Occasionally, a deeper, metallic *groan* will echo as a pressure regulator or a vast, ancient piston shifts in its housing somewhere unseen. The dominant sound, however, is the rustle-dry-paper sound of the fungal plates scraping against one another as the engineered air currents shift.

The light is the dim, perpetual glow of the **Glim-moss**. Patches of it, a species engineered for bioluminescence, cling to the fungal plates, emitting a sickly, greenish-white light that never changes in intensity. It is just enough to see by, to cast everything in a shadowless, dreamlike twilight. The light does not travel far, absorbed by the dense particulate haze and the velvet-dark surfaces of the fungal growth.

The ecology is a single, closed-loop system. The lichenous jungle is the lungs and the stomach. It processes the chemical cocktail from the vents, and the industrial byproducts from the engines, into stable biomass. In return, its own processes regulate the atmosphere for the machines that tend it. There are no animals, no insects, no birds. The only movement is the slow, imperceptible growth of the fungus and the rhythmic, mechanical motions of the Loom's components.

Scattered throughout this fungal gloom are the signs of its builders and tenders. Not buildings, but **artifacts**. A rusted iron railing, half-digested by a fungal plate. The corroded husk of a maintenance automaton, its limbs frozen, its optical sensors dark, now serving as a trellis for a thick vine. Faded stenciling on a metal bulkhead, the language long lost. A single, severe-looking control console, its screen cracked, set into a wall, its buttons overgrown with a delicate, hair-thin mycelial network that pulses faintly with its own inner light. These are not ruins; they are integrated components. The tech is heavy, industrial, and old — massive pistons, exposed conduits of strange alloys, and geothermal drills the size of buildings, all slowly being enveloped by the life they were built to sustain.

The place is in a perfect, frozen equilibrium. The growth rate of the fungal jungle is precisely balanced by the chemical inhibitors released by the Loom. The heat from the geothermal engines is precisely balanced by the cooling systems. The air composition is precisely balanced. Nothing changes. Not day to day, not year to year. It is a terrarium on a planetary scale, a self-perpetuating, living engine, now tended by nothing, humming its loud, strange, static song to the uncaring dark, in a pocket of existence hidden deep beneath the world's skin.
