The first thing you learn here is that the sky is wrong. It is not above you. It is *between* things.

You stand on what feels like a wooden ship’s deck, but it is a leaf. The leaf is the size of a county, its surface a landscape of dark green veins that rise into low, parallel ridges, and between them, shallow valleys filled with a kind of translucent, jelly-like sap that shimmers with a faint, internal bioluminescence. The air is thick, warm, and so humid that breathing feels like drinking. A constant, soft dew coats everything, catching the dim light from… where?

Look up. There is no sun. The light is a diffuse, silvery grey, as if the entire firmament were a sheet of frosted glass, which, in a way, it is. That is the underside of the leaf above, perhaps a mile up, perhaps ten. The light filters through its chlorophyll-dense layers, emerging here as a sickly green glow, there as a deep, bruised purple. Between the leaf you stand on and the leaf that is the sky, the space is not empty. It is filled with a web.

The web is the architecture, the continent, the world. It is made of woody, fibrous strands, some as thin as spider-silk, others as thick as redwood trunks, all woven into a labyrinthine, three-dimensional lattice. This is the *vein-wood*, the skeletal remains and living conduit of the macro-organism you are inside. The scale is vertiginous. You can see strands stretching away into the hazy, green-gold distance for what looks like hundreds of miles, creating a vast, hollow cathedral. And in this cathedral, the silence is profound. It is a silence composed of tiny sounds: the groan of a strand settling under its own immense weight; the slow, viscous *drip… drip… drip* of sap from a leaf-sky into the pools below; the distant, rhythmic sigh of a process like respiration somewhere in the plant’s deep core.

This is not a place that was built. It *grew*. But it grew with such totality, such ancient and established purpose, that it has become a made thing. There is no soil, no rock, no ground that is not plant. The leaf underfoot, the strands of vein-wood, the very air is thick with pollen and spores so fine they are almost a fog. The ecosystem is the plant itself. Fauna exists only in relation to it. You see, clinging to a nearby vein-wood strand, a creature like a sea urchin, but its spines are soft, fleshy tendrils that probe the wood, drinking sap. It is silent, deliberate. Further out, in the shadowy latticework, you see things that look like mobile orchids, their petals unfolding to reveal not a flower’s heart, but a complex, grabbing maw. They hunt, but their hunts are in slow motion, over hours, stalking a drifting, jelly-fish-like creature made of condensed moisture and fungal spores.

The erosion here is the plant’s own life cycle. A section of vein-wood, miles long, has begun to bleach and soften, its structural fibers turning a powdery white. It is not decaying in the way of wood on Earth; it is *dissolving*, breaking down into its constituent cells and sugars, weeping a sweet, amber resin that trickles down to feed the spongy layer below. New growth, a vibrant, almost neon green, spirals around the base of the dying strand, a direct and brutal transfer of substance. The world maintains itself.

But the weirdness is the silence’s secret. As you walk, your footsteps do not echo. The sound is swallowed. And the light… the light does not behave. Shadows do not always fall away from the dim, diffuse glow from above. Sometimes they pool *towards* it. Sometimes, a shadow cast by a single vein-wood strand will stretch for miles across a leaf’s surface, impossibly long and thin, defying the geometry of the light source. Reality is soft here, porous. The plant’s own consciousness, or perhaps just its scale, has begun to warp the rules of physics. To be here is to feel the profound, slow, green thoughts of a world-sized mind, thinking in photosynthesis and growth, in sap-tide and spore-fall, in a language of pure being that renders your own thoughts as fleeting and strange as the shadow of a bird on the surface of a deep, still sea. You are a mote of dust in its lungs, a bacterium on its skin. And it is utterly, magnificently unaware of you.
