The wind here does not blow; it seeps. It is a heavy, wet blanket of air that clings to the vertical faces of the grey rock, carrying the scent of ancient ice and crushed stone. You are standing on the edge of the world, or perhaps the roof of it.

**The Terrain: The Shelf of Teeth**

The elevation is dizzying, yet the ground is deceptively flat for miles in every direction. You are standing on a massive, jagged continental shelf—a tectonic plate that was buckled upward and then sheared off, creating a sheer cliff drop into the clouds below. The rock is young in geological terms; it hasn't yet been smoothed by eons of wind. The edges of the stone are sharp, fractured shards of obsidian-dark basalt and granite, cutting into the soles of boots. Deep, vertical fissures crack the plateau, venting geothermal heat and steam from the world’s angry core.

**The Moisture: The Weeping**

With a water rating of damp, this high-altitude desert is a paradox. It is too cold for open rivers, and the air is too thin to hold heavy storms. Instead, the moisture is persistent and invasive. Fog is the dominant weather system—thick banks of it rolling over the cliff edges and stagnating in the valleys of the plateau. When the wind dies down, the rock weeps. Condensation gathers on the sharp stones, not flowing away, but freezing into a brittle skin of rime that crunches underfoot like broken glass. In sheltered hollows, pools of meltwater sit black and still, unfrozen only due to the faint heat rising from the fissures.

**The Urbanity: The Stone Warren**

This is a place of immense density, but not of height. There are no skyscrapers piercing the clouds here. Instead, the city—The Warren—is built *down*.

Because the surface is too exposed to the biting cold and the abrasive wind, the civilization here has rejected the skyline. They have carved into the shelf. The "urban" density is staggering. From a distance, it looks like a sprawling mound of grey mud and rock, dotted with thousands of smoking vents. Up close, it is a labyrinth of squat, stone domes and walls, all huddled together for warmth, clustered around the fissures.

The architecture is primitive but monumental. Huge blocks of basalt, hand-cut with obsidian tools, are stacked without mortar, relying on sheer weight and gravity to hold them against the tremors. The buildings are connected by covered stone tunnels and archways, creating a hive-like structure. It is a city of passageways, not plazas. There are no windows facing the outside world; instead, skylights are cut into the stone domes, covered with translucent, stretched bladders of beast-gut to let in the pale, grey daylight.

**The Ecology & Fauna: The Herds of the Roof**

Life here is abundant, but it is concentrated in the pockets of warmth.

*   **The Goral:** Massive, shaggy herbivores roam the plateau, their coats so thick they look like moving boulders. They are the beasts of burden and the primary food source. They graze on the lichen that blooms in the condensation, using heavy hooves to scrape the ice away.
*   **The Ice-Skimmers:** Wingless, flightless avians that hop between the rocks. They are fast, skittish, and delicious.
*   **The Fissure Crawlers:** In the deep, dark cracks between the stones, pale, cold-blooded invertebrates nest. They are drawn to the heat vents. The locals fish for them with hooked poles, pulling up blind, white things the size of a human arm.

**The Atmosphere & Rules of the World**

The scale is vast. The plateau stretches out until it curves with the horizon, a grey, jagged expanse under a pale, white sun that offers little heat.

*   **The Silence:** The sound is low. The thick air muffles noise. The loudest sounds are the low groan of the tectonic plates shifting below, the hiss of steam from the vents, and the bleating of the Goral herds. The people here speak in low, breathy tones.
*   **The Tech:** There is no metal here, save for what they knap from the obsidian or smelt from the rare bog-iron deposits in the fissures. Their tools are heavy, sharp, and simple. Ropes are made of Goral gut and hide. Lighting comes from burning lichen-oil in stone lamps.
*   **The Dynamic:** The world is slow. The ice recedes and advances by millimeters per year. The city grows by adding one stone room to the end of a tunnel every generation. There is no rushing here; the altitude and the cold punish exertion. Patience is the currency of survival.

It is a cold, grey, monotonous world, but it is full. It is a crowded solitude, where thousands of people and animals huddle together in the stone, listening to the wind scrape against the sharp edges of the world.
