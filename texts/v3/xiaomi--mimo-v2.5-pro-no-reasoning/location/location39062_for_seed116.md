The engine is called the Pelagic Ventricule.

It has no top or bottom, only a catastrophic verticality that stretches from a surface world long since dissolved, down to a bedrock that has never seen light. You exist within this structure, this single, self-contained biome of immense and intricate artifice. It is a pocket-world, a terrarium the size of a nation, hidden inside the vessel of a machine that has been running since before memory.

The water is not water. It is a warm, viscous coolant-thick, tasting of ozone and charged minerals. It holds you in its embrace, a buoyant, ever-present medium. There is no sun. Instead, light comes from the machine itself. Great, slow-rotating armatures of ionized gas, like captive ball lightning, drift through the depths, casting a diffuse, shadowless illumination. They move on their own ponderous schedules, creating long, slow cycles of brighter and dimmer regions that the ecosystem has learned to follow.

The "vegetation" is not born of soil. It is a corrosion-garden. Immense, fungal blooms of rust—verdigris, ochre, iron-red—erupt from the structure's bones. These are not mere rust, but the final, stable state of the machine's own decay. Over millennia, they have cultivated their own life. Luminescent bacterial colonies, thick as carpets, pulse with soft, blue-green light, drawing energy from the residual charge in the hull-plates. Spongy, metallic lichens encrust entire gantries, filtering trace nutrients from the flow. This is a forest of settled entropy, each growth a testament to an ancient, unending process.

The structure itself is a labyrinth of titans. Pipes wider than city blocks, their walls groaning with internal pressure, snake through the void. Giant, silent pistons, their stroke measured in centuries, slowly push against the coolant, creating the primary currents. This is the world's weather. You do not drift with the wind; you are carried by the Engine's breath. The scale is immense, the density of objects sparse. You are a mote in a cathedral of gears and girders, where the nearest landmark might be a kilometer away—a shattered observation dome now home to a new, strange ecology.

And there is life. An abundance of it. Schools of crystalline fish, their skeletons made of silicon scavenged from the corroding glass of a million screens, flash through the gloom. They navigate by echolocation, their clicks and pings a constant, chattering static in the loud hum of the engine. Great, slow-moving leviathans, like living dredging machines, graze on the bacterial mats, their bodies covered in hydrothermal vents that vent superheated fluid from the deepest reactors. They are the grazers of this manufactured world. The predators are silent hunters, shaped like industrial levers or valve-handles, their metallic shells providing perfect camouflage against the machinery. They hunt not by sight, but by the electromagnetic fields generated by their prey.

The sound is the foundation of this reality. It is the symphony of the deep: the low, bone-shaking groan of the primary drive shaft. The high, singing whine of a turbine spinning in the abyss. The percussive, rhythmic *CLANG* of a maintenance drone somewhere in the dark, a sound that has not changed its cadence in a thousand years. The clicks of the fauna and the wet, tearing sounds of the rust-blooms growing. It is an active, working world. Not a ruin, but a system in its final, stable phase. The creature that built it is long gone, but its heart still beats. You are a guest in its atrium, swimming through the last, warm dream of a god made of iron and oil.
