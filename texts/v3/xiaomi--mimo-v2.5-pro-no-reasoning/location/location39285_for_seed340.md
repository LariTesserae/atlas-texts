The first thing is the sound. It is not a sound that travels through air; it is a condition of the space itself, a pressure that vibrates in your bones before it ever reaches your ears. It is a layered, polyrhythmic roar. A foundational, tectonic groan of immense, slow-moving things, overlaid with the wet, fibrous *creak-and-snap* of growth pushed to its absolute limit, and punctuated by the high-pitched, multi-throated shrieks of things hunting and dying and mating and hatching all at once. It is the sound of a billion living things and the planet they inhabit all screaming, laughing, and dying in a single, endless, deafening chord.

You are in a chasm. Or rather, you are *within* a chasm. The walls are not rock, but life. They are the living architecture of a world that has subsumed all geology into its own being. The substance of this place is a chitin-plant hybrid, a resinous, fibrous material that is both trunk and exoskeleton, bark and bone. It is a warm, uniform ochre-grey, and it throbs with a slow, peristaltic rhythm, like the gut of some vast, slumbering beast. The air is thick, humid, and smells of ozone, fermenting nectar, and the sharp, acidic tang of spore-release.

Water is a memory, a concept as alien as vacuum. This world runs on other fluids. The "sap" that weeps from fresh wounds in the walls is a pale, viscous gel that sizzles on contact with the air. Other secretions are darker, syrup-like, collecting in hollows where they are immediately swarmed by skittering, multi-legged grazers no bigger than your hand, but present in a living, pulsating carpet.

This is the Great Germination. The erosion parameter was misleading; this is not erosion wearing things down, but *eruption* building them up. The place is embryonic. The walls are not static. In a sudden, violent convulsion, a section of the chasm wall fifty meters to your left bulges, splits, and a new "organ" erupts into existence. It is a pulsating, bioluminescent bladder, glowing with a soft, internal veridian light, connected to the main structure by a network of throbbing, translucent tubes. The temperature spikes in a wave of palpable heat. This is the turbulence, the constant, chaotic creation and destruction.

The fauna is overwhelming because the fauna is, in many ways, indistinguishable from the flora and the architecture. There are no clear lines. Creatures are born from the walls. A section of the chitin-plant softens, liquefies, and from the resulting puddle of organic matter, a new being claws its way out, taking its first breath and its first bite of its own birthplace in the same moment. Others are reabsorbed. I see a creature like a praying mantis made of polished obsidian, six feet tall, methodically eating its way into a wall. The wall does not bleed; it grows around the mantis, enclosing it, pulling it in. The mantis does not struggle. In minutes, it is gone, absorbed, and the spot where it entered is already healed, scarred over with a new, complex pattern.

The scale is regional, a network of these chasms and caverns, all interconnected. The light is normal, but its source is a mystery. It seems to emanate from the air itself, a diffuse, sourceless grey glow that filters through the humid atmosphere, catching motes of pollen, spores, and shed chitin in its beams. There are no shadows, only gradients of dimness.

Scattered among the living walls are the artifacts. They are not of this place. They are simple, crudely hewn. A sharpened shard of some dark, non-organic stone, its edge serrated, is jammed into a fissure. Further down, a small, lean-to shelter has been constructed from sloughed-off plates of the chitin-plant, lashed together with sinewy vines that are still faintly twitching. The people who made these are not present, but their mark is a fragile whisper against the roaring, deafening sentence of this world. They were here. They are probably still here, somewhere in the turmoil, their tools as primitive and desperate as a child building a sandcastle against the tide.

The weirdness is the totality of it all. This is not an ecosystem; it is a single, vast, biological mechanism. A Solaris-like entity that has decided to wear the shape of a planet. Life is not *on* this world; life *is* this world. The ground you stand on, a fibrous mat of interwoven tendrils, is alive. The air you breathe is thick with the exhalations of a trillion respirating surfaces. The light is the glow of a global metabolism. The sound is the sound of its own frantic, endless being. You are a mote of dust in the lungs of a god, and the god is breathing in, and out, and in, and out, forever.
