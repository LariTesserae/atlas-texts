# The Lenticular Dark

## Opening

You are beneath the world. Not beneath a world — beneath *the* world, the one you knew, the one with sky. Somewhere above you there is presumably still a surface, and above that a sun, but these concepts have become theoretical. They belong to a different geometry.

What happened above to send this much rock freshly open — to crack continental plates into a labyrinth of sharp-edged passage and cathedral void — you cannot say from down here. But the evidence of its youth is everywhere: fracture faces still glitter with exposed crystal grain, every edge is a blade, every overhang is a fracture waiting for gravity to notice it. Nothing has had time to blunt. Nothing has had time to settle.

It is warm. Not the dead warmth of insulation but the living warmth of depth — the planet's interior still breathing through every slab of stone, rising in slow thermal columns through the larger cavities. The rock radiates. Press your palm to any wall and you feel a pulse that might be your own blood or might be the mountain's.

---

## The Shape of It



The scale is wrong in the way that makes you stop speaking.

The passages run in families — tight fissures that widen into galleries that deepen into voids so large your light, if you had light, would not reach the other side. The geometry is not random but neither is it ordered. It resembles the branching pattern of lungs, or of river deltas seen from orbit, or of the dendritic crystals that form when certain metals cool too quickly. As though the earth exhaled once, violently, and this is what the interior of that breath looks like.

The walls are layered. You can feel it even without seeing — strata of different mineral, different grain, different temperature. Some layers are slick as glass (volcanic, fused). Others are rough and friable, crumbling into fine powder under your fingertips. In places, veins of a darker mineral run through the lighter rock in patterns too regular to be natural and too irregular to be intentional. They are warm to a different degree than the surrounding stone — cooler in some passages, warmer in others, as though they are plumbing of a kind, carrying something that isn't quite heat.

The floor is uneven. Tumbled scree in some places, where the ceiling shed its skin during whatever convulsion opened this world. Elsewhere the floor is a single unbroken slab, slightly concave, as though something heavy rested here for a very long time and then was lifted away.

There is almost no water. Perhaps none at all. In some of the tighter fissures you feel a faint tackiness on the wall — mineral seep, brine so concentrated it is nearly solid, salt so pure it crunches between your teeth if you taste it. But no dripping. No pooling. No sound of water. The chemistry of this place does not require it. Whatever lives here has found other solvents, other media, other ways to dissolve the world into useable form.

---

## What Lives Here



The fauna is abundant. This is the thing that catches in your throat when you first understand it — in the dark, in the dry, in the heat and silence, *there are so many of them*.

You do not see them. You will almost never see them. But you feel the air displace as something shifts in a passage you thought was empty. You hear — not hear, *sense* — a texture of small movements layered on top of each other until they become a kind of texture, a fabric of presence. The density is high. Things are near. Things are always near.

**The wall-pressers.** They are flat. That is the first thing you understand about them through touch — when you reach into a crevice and your fingers meet something that yields slightly but is not soft, something that is body-temperature or slightly warmer, something that does not pull away. They are flat the way a hand is flat, but larger, and they conform to the irregularities of the stone with a precision that suggests they have been here for a long time, long enough for the stone to forget they are separate. They feed on the mineral layer itself — you can feel the faint abrasion of their underside against the rock, a slow grinding that produces a powder they reabsorb through their skin. They do not need water. They are, as far as you can tell in the dark, not particularly concerned about you.

**The columnar things.** In the larger voids, where the ceiling is high and the air is warmest, you find them — or rather, you walk into them. They stand in clusters, rooted to the floor, rising to a height you cannot determine by reaching. They are slightly cooler than the surrounding air. They have a surface like bark but smoother, like leather but harder. They are not plants. They have no leaves, no photosynthesis, no need for anything this world does not offer. They feed on the thermal gradient itself — warm air rising past them, cooler air sinking around them, and in the differential they find enough energy to maintain their slow existence. They grow. Not quickly. Perhaps not at all in any timeframe that matters. But they are here, and they were not here before the world cracked open, and that means something.

**The drifters.** In the very largest voids — the ones where the air itself seems to have geography, where warm currents rise in invisible columns and cool air pools in depressions — there are things that move. You know this because sometimes a faint pressure brushes your face, a displacement of air too deliberate to be convection, too light to be threat. Something is navigating the thermal landscape the way a fish navigates currents. You never touch one. You are not sure you want to.

**The skitterers.** Small. Many. The sound they make is the closest thing to ambient noise in this world — a faint, arrhythmic ticking, like cooling metal, like a clock running down, like tiny stones falling very short distances very frequently. They are in the walls, in the floor, in the crevices, in the spaces between your pack and your back. They eat the powder left by wall-pressers. They eat each other. They are the base of a food chain that does not touch the sun.

**The still ones.** In certain chambers, you sense something large. Not moving. Not breathing (nothing here breathes in any way you recognize). Just *present*. Occupying space with an authority that makes the surrounding fauna give it berth. You do not investigate the still ones. Some instinct older than reason tells you they are not dead but *deciding*.

## The Artifacts



Someone was here. Someone with hands and tools and intent.

The structures are scattered — not a settlement, not a civilization, but the evidence of passage. Someone came through, tried to make sense of it, tried to stay, left marks.

**The cairns.** Stacked stone, small enough to carry, arranged in patterns that are almost regular. You find them at junctions, at the mouths of larger voids, at points where the passage changes character — becomes tighter, or wider, or turns sharply. Waymarkers. Someone was mapping this place. The stones are chosen with care — certain minerals, certain colors, certain warmth. The ones closest to the dark mineral veins are slightly cooler than the others. The selection is deliberate but the logic escapes you.

**The scratchings.** On certain walls, in places where the rock is soft enough to take a mark, someone has drawn. Not art — or not only art. Diagrams. Cross-sections of passages. Patterns that might represent the branching geometry of the labyrinth. A few figures — humanoid, but with too many limbs, or limbs that curve in ways human limbs do not. Were they drawing themselves, or what they saw, or what they were becoming?

**The shelters.** Small. Stone stacked into half-circles, roofs of flat slabs, just large enough for one or two bodies to lie down. Inside, the stone floor is worn smooth — not by many feet but by long use. Someone lived in these, for a while. Sat in the dark. Listened to the skitterers. Ran their hands along the wall-pressers. Learned the thermal grammar of the passages. Then left — or stopped leaving.

**The offering niches.** Small hollows in the wall, natural or widened by hand tools, in which objects have been placed. A chunk of the dark mineral, polished smooth. A columnar thing's outer skin, peeled away and coiled like a scroll. A collection of skitterer shells, arranged by size. Someone was paying attention. Someone was trying to understand. Someone treated this place as though it could hear them.

There is no sign of metal. The tools that made these marks were stone on stone — harder mineral against softer, edge against surface. The work is patient, careful, and shows evidence of considerable practice. Whoever did this was not exploring for the first time. They had inherited knowledge, technique, purpose. And then the chain of inheritance broke, or was interrupted, or went somewhere you cannot follow.

## The Atmosphere

The darkness is not total. It cannot be, not with this much fauna, not with this much mineral variety. There are places where the rock itself gives off a faint luminescence — not bioluminescence, but something geological, a phosphorescence triggered by the ambient heat, a glow so faint it exists only in peripheral vision and vanishes when you turn to look. It gives the largest voids a quality of depth that pure darkness would not have — a sense of *more space beyond the space you can almost see*, a darkness that has topography.

The air is warm and still but not stale. It moves — slowly, on its own schedule, driven by thermal differentials that have nothing to do with wind or weather in any surface sense. In the larger passages, you can stand in one spot and feel the air temperature change over minutes: warmer, cooler, warmer again, as though the mountain is breathing but has forgotten the rhythm. There is a faint mineral smell — sulphur, maybe, or something adjacent to sulphur but not quite, a smell that exists only in this world and has no surface analogue.

The quiet is immense but not empty. Layered beneath it — beneath your own breathing, your own heartbeat, your own footsteps on sharp stone — is the skitterer-ticking, the wall-presser-grinding, the occasional displacement-breath of something large moving in a void you cannot see. The quiet is not silence. It is the sound of a world that has decided whispering is sufficient.

---

## The Equilibrium

Nothing changes. This is perhaps the strangest thing of all, stranger than the flat things on the walls or the columnar things that eat heat or the drifters in the dark. This world has arrived at a state and *stays there*.

The temperature does not vary. The fauna does not appear to grow or shrink in number. The mineral seeps do not advance or retreat. The cairns do not topple. Even the skitterers, with their constant small motion, maintain a steady-state population — as many dying as being born, as many eating as being eaten, a dynamic system so precisely balanced it reads as stillness.

The scattered structures are not decaying. The stone tools left in offering niches show no wear from time. The scratchings on the walls are as sharp as the day they were made.

This could mean the world is young — that not enough time has passed for change to become visible. Or it could mean something else. That the labyrinth, vast as it is, exists in a kind of thermodynamic grace, a narrow window of conditions so precisely tuned that everything persists. The warmth from below exactly balances the heat lost to the dark above. The mineral exactly sustains the fauna. The fauna exactly maintains the mineral. A closed system. A held breath.

You have the uncomfortable sense that this place has been waiting — not for you, not for anyone, but simply *waiting*, the way a held breath waits, the way a loaded spring waits. The equilibrium is real but it does not feel permanent. It feels like the top of something. The crest of a wave that has not yet decided whether to break.

---

## What You Understand, Eventually

You have been here long enough — time is difficult to measure in the warm dark — to notice that the strange mineral veins are not random. They converge. In the deepest passages, the ones that require the most effort to reach, the veins thicken, multiply, draw together into a lattice that suggests a destination.

The fauna is denser there. The wall-pressers overlap in layers. The columnar things stand closer. The skitterers' ticking becomes a near-continuous hum. The still ones are more numerous, and they are less still — you feel them *orienting*, slowly, toward whatever is at the convergence.

The artifacts are there too, but different — older-seeming, or more sophisticated, or made by hands that had been down here longer. The scratchings become diagrams of diagrams, maps of maps, as though whoever made them was trying to describe something that required multiple dimensions of reference.

The air is warmer. The darkness has more texture. The quiet is different — not louder but *thicker*, as though the silence itself is a medium, a substance, something you could push against.

You do not go further. Not because you are afraid, though you are. Not because you are lost, though you may be. You do not go further because you have the distinct and unshakeable sense that the equilibrium — this held-breath stillness, this warm and quiet persistence — is not the natural state of this place.

It is a *preparation*.

And whatever the place is preparing for, it is not yet done.
