The air is the first thing you notice, and the last. It is thick, not with humidity, but with sound and the dampness of breath. It is a cool, heavy blanket that presses in from all sides, smelling of wet stone, metallic tang, and something organic, like stale sweat. There is no sun here. There is only the sky-vault, a ceiling of what feels like close, sweating basalt, a mere thirty yards above, from which a perpetual, faint luminescence bleeds down. This light has no source you can find; it simply is, a pale, grey-silver glow that coats everything, making the world a place of sharp, deep shadows where the light doesn't reach.

You are standing on a street. The term is generous. It is a narrow canyon carved through a solid mass of construction. The walls are not walls in any conventional sense. They are a fusion of material—bricks of fused volcanic glass and something like fused bone, mortared with a dark, organic-looking paste that weeps a slow, oily moisture. The surfaces are not smooth. They are a dense, intricate landscape of bas-relief, carved and built up in layers. Grotesque faces with too many eyes or mouths agape in silent screams. Geometric patterns that seem to crawl at the edge of your vision. Panels of this same bone-like material, etched with pictographs that tell a story you cannot read, of tool-wielding hands and vast, empty circles.

This is not a city built *on* a landscape. This city *is* the landscape. Everything is made. The ground underfoot is the same material, a mosaic of dark tiles worn smooth by a million footfalls, or perhaps by the constant, slow drip of condensation from above. The soundscape is a dense, layered roar that never ceases. It is the sound of dripping—a thousand distinct points of moisture hitting tile, stone, and metal, each with its own pitch. It is the groan and pop of vast, ancient materials settling, expanding, contracting in the cool. And it is a low, rhythmic *thrum*, a vibration felt more in the bones than heard with the ears. This thrum comes from the walls themselves, from the deep, unseen mechanics of the city. It is the pulse of whatever system runs beneath the surface, perhaps pumping water, perhaps powering the faint light, perhaps simply *being*.

There is no open sky. The narrow streets and alleys open into wider plazas, but even these are roofed by the same low, vaulted ceilings, their highest point in the center perhaps fifty feet up. The plazas are crossroads of sound and design. Here, the carvings are more elaborate, more frantic. Fountains, long dry, still bear the stains of the last water that trickled through their stone basins. The air in these spaces is slightly clearer, but the sound is amplified, a cacophony of echoes.

Life is a rumor. You see the evidence in the wear on the thresholds, in the faint, hollows worn into the wall where shoulders have leaned for centuries, in the clatter of something small and unseen scuttling into a dark crevice. Once, a shape like a long, pale centipede, but with a segmented shell of dull metal, moves with painful slowness across a wall before vanishing. Its trail leaves a faint, glistening sheen. The occasional cry from above might be a creature, or it might be the wind finding a gap in the construction.

The world is active. This is not a ruin. The groaning of the structure is a constant conversation. The light fluctuates in a slow, tidal rhythm, brighter for what might be a day, dimmer for a night, but never gone. The moisture is collected in channels cut into the walls, flowing in dark rivulets toward unseen drains, creating a network of tiny, moving waterways that map the city's incline. The air circulates in cool, unpredictable breezes that smell of different sectors—here of ozone, there of wet clay, there of something acrid and unknown.

This place is under construction and has been forever. In alcoves, you find half-finished carvings, tools of sharp stone and primitive bronze left as if the worker simply stepped away. Piles of brick-bone and paste wait for builders who do not come. The city grows not by expansion, but by accretion within itself. New layers are built over old, new stories are carved over the old, new pipes are grooved into the existing walls. It is a dense, saturated, living fossil, a single, vast organism of stone and memory, breathing damp air and humming its own deep song in the endless grey twilight.
