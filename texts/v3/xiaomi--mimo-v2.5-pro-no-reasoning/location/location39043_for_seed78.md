The air is a blade.

That is the first and most constant truth of this place. It is not merely cold; it is a crystalline, searching cold that finds its way through feather, fur, and bark, seeking the barest hint of warmth. The altitude is the cause, and the altitude is absolute. You are on a world that is mostly mountain, a planet-scale spine of rock recently thrust from some primordial sea, still sharp with the memory of its violent birth.

The ground is not soil. It is a latticework.

At first, you see only sharpness. The peaks are jagged, un-weathered fangs of black basalt and grey granite, their new surfaces unsoftened by millennia of rain. They split the low, bruised sky. But then you look closer, and you see that the stone is merely a skeleton. It is draped, woven, and choked with life. This is the domain of the **Arboreal Mat**, a single, planet-spanning organism of a plant that does not think of itself as individual trees.

It is a tapestry of living tissue. Thick, ropey vines, some as wide as a river, form the main arteries. They pulse with a slow, syrupy rhythm, carrying moisture from the deep places. Their bark is not bark but a hardened, cork-like shell, patterned with the fossilized impressions of a billion fallen leaves. Sprouting from these arteries are forests, but they are not forests as you know them. The "trees" are offshoots, ephemeral structures of hollow, woody tubes and broad, dark leaves designed to capture the faint, diffuse light. They grow, they photosynthesize, they die, and they are reabsorbed into the Mat in a cycle measured in decades. The ground you stand on—a thick, spongy layer of living and dead vine-matter, meters deep—is the Mat. The cliff face you climb is the Mat, gripping the rock with root-like tendrils. The very air is thick with its exhalations.

The dampness is not from rain, but from respiration. In the deep cold, the Mat breathes slowly, and its breath condenses into a perpetual, clinging mist that fills the canyons. Pockets of liquid water are rare, precious things. You find them in the cupped hands of giant, pitcher-shaped leaves, or in the hollows of the great vines where condensation trickles down and pools. This water is not pure; it is thick with sugars and minerals, the lifeblood of the Mat, and it tastes of soil and lightning.

The sound is constant, a deep and resonant **LIVING HUM**. It is the vibration of the Mat itself—a low-frequency thrum you feel in your teeth and bones, the sound of sap moving through a thousand million vessels. It is the groan and creak of the vast vines as they shift under their own weight and the pull of a moon you never see. It is the sudden, loud **CRACK** of a subsidiary "tree" trunk splitting from cold stress or internal pressure, echoing for miles in the thin air. And it is the sound of the fauna, which are not separate from the Mat, but *of* it.

The animals here are climbers, hiders, and gliders. **Shard-Wing Moths**, as big as your hand, with wings of translucent, chitinous material like frosted glass, cling to the dark undersides of the vines, their sudden flutter a loud, papery sound in the gloom. **Vine-Gnawers**, six-legged rodents with teeth of sharpened obsidian (a simple tool evolved), chew on the cork-shell, their scratching a constant, localized noise. Larger things move in the canopy-mass—shadows that flow and ripple, the **Mat-Anglers**, predators that mimic the texture and shape of the vines themselves, waiting with a patience measured in seasons.

Scattered among the living tissue are the marks of others. They are not buildings. They are **dwellings carved into the dead-growth**. You find a hollowed-out knot in a great, dormant vine, its entrance smoked black, the interior still smelling of old fires and animal fat. A few tools lie inside: a hand-axe of knapped basalt, a scraper of sharpened bone, a hook fashioned from the mandible of some giant insect. There are no signs of who made them. They are as silent and abandoned as nests. Faded ochre markings, smeared onto a smooth patch of vine-bark, depict the spiraling growth pattern of the Mat, or perhaps the path of the unseen moon. These are the scattered, faint artifacts of a people who understood they were not inhabitants of this world, but parasites upon a single, vast, and indifferent host.

The darkness is profound. The light level is barely a twilight, filtered through the dense canopy of ephemeral leaves and the perpetual mist. The only illumination comes from bioluminescence. Patches of the Mat glow with a cold, blue-green fungal light, tracing the paths of nutrient veins. The eyes of the fauna reflect this light, appearing as floating, disembodied sparks in the gloom. There is no sun here, only a slow, diurnal brightening and dimming of the grey sky, a subtle shift in the quality of the darkness.

It is a world of immense scale and profound emptiness. The spaces between the great vines are caverns of air, vast and hollow. You can travel for days, moving from one vine-highway to another, and feel utterly alone, a mote of warmth and sound in a cold, humming, and utterly alien wilderness. The Mat is active, dynamic, and all-consuming, yet it is also empty of anything that does not serve its growth. You are a ghost here, a fleeting warmth in an empire of slow, cold, green life.
