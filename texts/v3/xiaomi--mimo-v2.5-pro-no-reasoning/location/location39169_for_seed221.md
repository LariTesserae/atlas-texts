Here, the mountain does not rise from the earth; it descends from a brass ceiling.

You are inside the **Sump-Engine**, a pocket-environment no larger than a cathedral’s crypt, etched into the frozen flesh of a high-altitude glacier. But there is no stone here. There is no ice. The walls, floor, and ceiling are forged from a seamless, sweating composite of matte iron and permanently damp rubber, riveted together with bolts the size of your fist. The air is thick, smelling of ozone, animal musk, and hot, wet metal. You are standing inside a machine that thinks it is a cave.

The **Erosion** is nonexistent; this place is too new, too perfectly assembled. The "geology" is a series of colossal, interlocking cast-iron plates and structural beams, their seams still sharp-edged and raw from the foundry. There has been no time for the world to soften its edges. The **Scale** is claustrophobically intimate, a single, humming chamber carved out of a larger, unimaginable mechanism. You are in a room within a heart within a god.

And it is deafening. The **Sound** is a physical assault, a layered, multi-frequency roar that vibrates your teeth and liquefies the air in your lungs. It is the grinding of a million unseen gears, the hiss of a thousand high-pressure steam vents, the rhythmic, hydraulic *THUMP-THUMP-THUMP* of a central piston so vast its motion is felt more as a shift in gravity than a heard noise. This is the sound of an industrial process that has been running for eons and will run for eons more, yet has never once changed its pitch. The equilibrium is absolute, the noise a constant, deafening hymn.

In the **Dim** light, provided by caged, flickering vacuum tubes that sputter with a sickly green-yellow luminescence, you can see the **Water**. It is everywhere. It does not fall as rain, but weeps from the walls and condenses in thick, oily beads on every cold, metal surface. It gathers in slow, black rivulets that trace paths along the floor before pouring into grated channels with a sound like a thousand whispers being silenced at once. This is not water that nourishes; it is the lubricant, the coolant, the sweat of the machine.

The **Vegetation** is a lie. Twisted, skeletal shapes that resemble brambles or roots climb the lower walls, but they are made of copper wire and corroded tubing, their "leaves" the frayed, sparking ends of fiber-optic cables. They are static, a permanent, metallic flora. And they are crowded. The **Density** here is overwhelming. Every surface is cluttered. Pipes, conduits, levers, pressure gauges with cracked faces, and bundles of wire hang from the ceiling or emerge from the walls, creating a three-dimensional labyrinth of metal. You must push through them, their cold, damp surfaces brushing against your skin.

But the true source of the overwhelming presence, the reason the air itself feels crowded and alive, is the **Fauna**. They are not creatures of flesh. They are swarms. Teeming, skittering swarms of brass-bodied, multi-legged clockwork beetles, each the size of your thumb. They cover every horizontal surface, their thousands of metal legs making a continuous, high-pitched *ticking* that weaves into the roar of the larger machine. They are not alive, but they are animated. They crawl over the weeping walls, cleaning the condensation, polishing the rivets, repairing microscopic fractures in the metal with tiny, solder-spitting proboscises. They flow over your boots like a river of living brass, their activity frantic, endless, and utterly purposeful. They are the machine’s white blood cells, and you are the foreign object. They ignore you, but their sheer, skittering number is a form of pressure, a reminder that you are the only thing here that does not belong.

This is a place that was never born. It was built. It is a functioning organ in a body you cannot perceive, a humid, cold, loud, and utterly static masterpiece of engineering that maintains a perfect, impossible state of being. The air is water. The rock is iron. The life is clockwork. The sound is silence. And it has never, ever changed.
