The air tastes like hot stone and warm dust, though "air" is perhaps not the right word. You are beneath the surface of a world, but not in a cave. You are inside a living thing. Or, more precisely, you are inside the result of a living thing that died so long ago its corpse has become a geology.

The space is a single chamber, a cavern the size of a small house, but its walls are not rock. They are a smooth, amber-colored material, slightly translucent, with the faint, milky patterns of ancient bone or horn. It is warm to the touch, not from geothermal heat, but from a residual, metabolic warmth that has never truly faded. The temperature is constant, humid, and close.

This is the **Ossuary Garden**.

**Terrain & Materials:**
The floor is not soil, but a thick, crumbly layer ofite and phite—aite of fossilized pollen, shed carapaces, and crystalline fungal spores. It crunches softly underfoot, like walking on crushed shells and sugar. Scattered throughout this substrate are larger features: the faint, geometric outlines of what might have been foundations or pathways, now just slightly depressed lines of a harder, darkerite. These are the **scattered marks**—not built by a tool-using people, but etched by the biological processes of the Ossuary's original inhabitant, the World-Beetle. Its vein-like tunnels and organ-chambers became the architecture.

**Light & Atmosphere:**
The dimness is profound. The only light is a faint, internal luminescence. The amber walls themselves glow with a soft, biochemical phosphorescence, a tired green-gold that is enough to see shapes and movement, but not details or color. The air is thick, smelling of dried sap, ozone from static electricity, and the deep, spicy scent of the fungal growth. There is no wind, only the slow, imperceptible circulation of gases.

**The Lush, Silent Growth:**
This is where the paradox lives. In this dry, dim, stable place, vegetation is **lush**. But it is not flora. It is **Fungal Carpets** and **Lichen Mantles**. They grow not from soil, but from the very walls and floor, feeding on the ambient warmth and the mineral-rich dust. Great, velvety cushions of a dark purple fungus pulse slowly with their own inner light, like sleeping lungs. Shelves of crystalline lichen, shaped like frozen coral or complex constellations, climb the walls, their edges sharp and new (**young erosion**). They are fragile, intricate, and everywhere, yet the space between them is vast (**sparse density**). A single growth can be the size of a table, leaving yards of empty, glowing floor around it.

**The Loud, Strange Fauna:**
The sound is the most startling thing. It is a **loud**, pervasive hum. It is not mechanical. It is the sound of a billion tiny lives. The air is thick with **Glass Wasps**—insects with wings of such thin, silica-ite they hum at a frequency that resonates with the chamber itself, creating a constant, droning chord. They are attracted to the luminescent fungi, and their flight paths are like lines of sound made visible in the dim light. Larger things move in the shadows: **Amber Millipedes**, as long as an arm, their segmented bodies clicking softly as they navigate the fungal forests, grazers on the lichen. They are abundant, but slow-moving, their presence felt more as a shifting of the ambient noise than as distinct creatures.

**The Primitive Artifacts:**
Nestled in the hollows of the fungal growths or placed carefully on the darker foundation-lines are the **remains**. They are not tools of metal or stone, but of the Ossuary itself. Chitinous plates, arranged in circles. Smooth, round pebbles of concentrated mineral from the walls, stacked into tiny cairns. Woven fibers from the tough, papery lining of old larval tunnels. These are the marks of the **Dust Weavers**, a small, solitary species of mantis-like creature that uses these artifacts to cultivate specific lichens. Their "technology" is agriculture and arrangement, using what the world provides. They are rarely seen, but their work is scattered throughout, a silent testament to a patient, primitive craft.

**The Rule of This World:**
The dominant principle here is **Stasis**. The World-Beetle died in equilibrium with its environment. The fungi and lichen reached a perfect, slow-growing balance with the available energy. The Glass Wasps and Amber Millipedes have life cycles measured in centuries. The Dust Weavers are more witnesses than changers. Nothing decays quickly. Nothing erodes. The sharp edges of the new lichen growth remain sharp for millennia. The sound is a constant, the light is a constant, the temperature is a constant. It is a pocket of intense, quiet life that has achieved a timeless state—a living diorama sealed inside a fossil. The only true movement is the slow, collective breathing of the fungal carpets and the darting, humming lines of the wasps, forever tracing the same patterns in the amber twilight.
