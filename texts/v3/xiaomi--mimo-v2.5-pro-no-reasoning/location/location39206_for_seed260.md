You stand, and you have always been standing, though the memory of arrival flickers at the edge of your mind like a failing candle. The ground beneath your feet is not ground in any way you understand. It is a fine, grey dust, almost a powder, that feels less like mineral and more like ash from a fire that burned out before the concept of fire existed. It covers a plain that stretches in every direction, a vast, shallow bowl under a sky that is not a sky.

The sky is a bruise. It is a dome of shifting, iridescent purples and sickly greens, lightless and yet not dark. It provides no sun, no moon, only a diffuse, shadowless illumination that makes your eyes ache. There is no horizon line; the plain simply fades into a shimmering, heat-haze-like distortion that eats the edges of the world. This is the light of a place that has forgotten stars.

From this bruised sky, sound falls upon you. It is not a noise from a source, but a pressure. A constant, sub-sonic thrum that vibrates in your teeth and the sockets of your eyes, overlaid with a high, crystalline shriek that feels like the sound of glass being stretched until it is infinitely thin. It is deafening not because it is loud, but because it is everything. It is the texture of the air, the hum of the broken physics holding this place together. It is the sound of a world under construction—or perhaps, under deconstruction.

The air itself is cool, thin, and utterly still. It smells of ozone, hot stone, and something metallic and sweet, like old blood. There is no wind. The only movement is the slow, imperceptible crawl of the dust across the plain, driven by some unseen, patient force.

Scattered across this grey sea are the presences. They are sparse, each one an island in the emptiness. They are the vegetation, but not as you know it. They are crystalline formations, growing in defiance of the silence and the dust. Some resemble jagged geodes split open, their interiors glowing with a cold, internal bioluminescence of blue and amber light that pulses in time with the sub-sonic thrum. Others are tall, skeletal spires of a dark, glass-like mineral, branching like petrified trees but utterly bare, catching the bruised light and refracting it into painful, prismatic shards. They do not photosynthesize; they *chryso-synthesize*, drawing energy from the dissonant sound and the fractured light. Their growth is measured in millennia. You are witnessing their adolescence; the erosion of this place is just beginning, the first faint scoring on their flawless, angular surfaces.

At the base of one such spire cluster, partially buried in the grey powder, lies a structure. It is a shelter, but it is a pathetic thing. Walls of stacked, unworked rock, a roof of layered, petrified fungal plates (harvested from another, softer crystal growth). It is a hovel, not a house. Inside, you might find a fire-pit filled with cold ash, and tools: a hand-axe of sharpened obsidian so fresh its edges still scatter light like diamond, a digging stick of a fused, root-like crystal, a bowl carved from a softer, milky mineral. They are primitive, handmade, and feel recent, as if their maker just stepped out. But they are not. They are ancient. The dynamic of this place is slow. These tools were placed here a century ago, or a day ago; the distinction has no meaning. The slow processes—settling, dust-accumulation, the faint growth of a new crystal bud nearby—have barely begun to acknowledge them.

The weirdness is absolute. It is not in a single anomaly, but in the fabric. Gravity feels slightly wrong, a subtle sideways tug that you compensate for without thinking. Perspectives warp; a crystal spire a mile away seems as close as one a hundred feet away. Your own shadow on the dust is not your shape. It is a smear of deeper darkness, occasionally twitching with a movement you did not make. When you look away, things change. Not quickly, but they *change*. The spire you were watching is now oriented a few degrees differently. The bowl in the hut is now a different shape. You did not see it move. You never will. Reality here is not a consistent narrative; it is a series of assertions, and each moment is a new, slightly different assertion. The deafening sound is the sound of reality straining to hold its current form.

There are no animals. There is no water. There is only the slow, patient, luminous growth of the crystal, the gradual settling of the ash, and the profound, suffocating noise of a world that is somehow, impossibly, both brand new and in its final, entropic throes. You are an observer in a nursery for a universe that will never grow up, and a morgue for one that already died. The only movement is your own breath, the only warmth your own body, the only change the endless, imperceptible drift of the grey, grey dust.
