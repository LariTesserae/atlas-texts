# The Hollows of Silt-and-Calcium

---

You stand at the bottom of a depression — not a valley, not a crater, just a place where the land has sunk slightly, a shallow concavity maybe two hundred meters across. The floor is pale, crusted, a hardpan of calcium andite salts that crunches softly underfoot if you step hard enough, though no one is stepping. No one makes sound here. The crust is young — barely years old, or months, and the edges of every fracture in it are sharp, unweathered, as though the ground recently emerged from beneath something, exhaled upward and left exposed.

Above, the sky is not dark, but dim — a perpetual bronze-grey diffusion, sourceless, as though light is simply a property of the air at this particular altitude. There is no sun. There may have been one, once, or there may be one above the haze layer, but down here in the depression, illumination is ambient and directionless, casting no shadows. Everything is lit from everywhere, faintly.

And rising from the center of this basin: the Hollows.

---

## The Structure

It is a single construction — or a single organism, or something between — and it fills perhaps a third of the depression's floor. Imagine a termite mound, then forget that image. This is taller, thinner-walled, and architecturally more deliberate. It rises in irregular spires and buttresses, the tallest no higher than a three-story building, the shortest barely taller than you. Its color is the color of the ground — calcium-white, salt-grey, with faint veins of ochre where some mineral has oxidized slowly in the dim light. The surface texture is rough but organized: each spire is composed of thousands of thin vertical plates, like pages of a book pressed together and then fused at their bases, leaving delicate gaps between them at the top. The plates are translucent at their edges, paper-thin, and they *flex* — not in wind (there is no wind, here, no movement of air at all) — but on their own, in slow peristalsis, as though the material itself is breathing. Breathing inward. The plates draw together, then relax apart, a cycle of perhaps thirty seconds, and with each contraction, a faint pressure change moves through the basin like a silent, invisible pulse.

The construction is hollow. You can see this where a spire has partially collapsed — not from erosion (there has been almost no erosion; the features are sharp, new) but from some internal failure, perhaps a growth error — and through the gap, the interior is visible: a lattice of thin walls creating rooms within rooms, corridors that spiral upward, chambers that open onto other chambers with no doors, only thresholds, passageways barely wider than a body. The walls are thinner than the exterior plates, almost translucent, and they glow faintly from within — not from any light source you can identify, but from the material itself, some bioluminescent process that produces a cold, pale luminescence, just barely visible, just enough to make the interior navigable if you were inside.

You are not inside.

---

## The Emptiness

The Hollows have no inhabitants. This is not entirely true — but it is nearly true. Once, as you stand at the depression's edge (you have not moved; you are imagining standing, or you are standing, it makes no difference), you see something move inside one of the upper chambers. A shape, small, pale, moving slowly along a wall. It is not the structure's builder — you are sure of this, though you could not say why. It is something else. Something that has moved in. It pauses, presses itself against the interior wall, and you lose it against the faint glow. Fauna here is rare. A few organisms — small, soft-bodied, colorless — inhabit the lower passages. They move without sound. They eat the walls, you think — slowly, over days, consuming the thin interior plates and excreting something that the structure reabsorbs. A symbiosis. Or a parasitism. Or something with no word, because no one has been here to name it.

Between the spires, the ground is bare. Flat, crusted, featureless except for small circular depressions — bowls, really, each about the width of a hand, each lined with a thin film of something organic and damp, the only moisture in the entire basin, a biofilm that collects the faintest trace of condensation from the cool air. Around each bowl, a sparse ring of vegetation: low, grey-green crusts, lichen-like but not lichen, something that photosynthesizes in dim light using wavelengths you cannot see. They are patient organisms. They grow outward a millimeter per season. They will not reach the Hollows for centuries. The structure will not last that long — it is already showing signs of internal collapse, the uppermost spire leaning slightly, its plates not flexing in sync with the others.

---

## The Activity

And yet — and this is what holds you, what keeps you describing even as the words feel insufficient — the place is *alive with process*. Not sound. Never sound. But motion, change, transformation, chemical event. The ground is shifting: new crust forming at the depression's edges, mineral-laden moisture wicking up from below (there is water somewhere, deep, unreachable, saline, pressing upward through capillary networks in the substrate) and crystallizing at the surface, pushing the hardpan outward, expanding the basin's floor millimeter by millimeter. The Hollows are growing — the spires elongating, new plates budding from their tips like leaves unfurling in time-lapse, each plate rigid within minutes of emerging, stiffening as minerals precipitate in its tissue. The biofilms in the ground-bowls are cycling — colonies of microorganisms blooming, dying, being consumed by the next bloom, a food web of invisible players turning over every few days. The few pale fauna are moving through the lower passages — slow, deliberate, migrating from chamber to chamber in patterns that suggest response to something: chemical gradients, temperature shifts, the faint bioluminescent pulses of the walls.

The structure itself is a weather system. The slow flexing of the plates creates pressure differentials inside the Hollows — not enough to generate airflow (the air is still, always still, the silence is absolute) but enough to move gases through the lattice by diffusion, pulling in carbon dioxide from the basin air and exhaling something else, something that smells faintly of sulfur and old stone, though no one is here to smell it. The interior temperature is slightly cooler than the exterior — a degree, perhaps two — and this gradient drives a slow convective chemistry, minerals precipitating on the upper interior walls, dissolving on the lower ones, a constant vertical migration of material that keeps the structure in dynamic equilibrium even as it grows and collapses and grows again.

---

## What It Is

It is a city with no citizens. Or it is a body with no brain. Or it is a process that has learned to imitate architecture, mineral and biological systems locked in a feedback loop that produces ordered complexity without intention, without tools, without even what you would recognize as a central organism. The Hollows *are* the organism — the whole structure, plates and walls and biofilms and pale fauna and mineral crusts, all of it one system, one slow metabolism, one breath held for thirty seconds and released and held again. It is not alive in any way you were taught to recognize. It does not reproduce — or if it does, it has not done so here, in this basin, in whatever span of time has passed since the ground emerged. It simply *persists*, actively, dynamically, transforming itself and its small environment in an ongoing negotiation with the cool dim air and the dry mineral ground and the deep unreachable water and the silence.

The silence.

The silence is not absence. It is a medium. Sound here would be distortion — a disruption of the pressure cycles, the diffusion gradients, the slow flex of mineral-plate tissue. The Hollows have evolved in silence, or been built in silence, or emerged from silence, and they are calibrated to it. You understand, standing at the depression's edge, that if you were to speak — if there were a you, if there were a voice — the sound waves would propagate through the basin and the plates would absorb them, dampen them, metabolize them, fold them into the lattice and break them down into something the structure could use.

You do not speak.

The spires flex. The pale fauna move. The crust spreads. The biofilms bloom and die and bloom.

The Hollows are doing something, and they are doing it now, and they will go on doing it without witness, in the cool, in the dim, in the quiet, in the dry, and whatever it is — whatever the whole system is *for*, if it is for anything — it does not need you.

It does not need anyone.
