**Elevation and Water (2.4 / 2.9)**
You are not on a mountain; you are inside a cloud that has decided to become solid. The ground—if you can call it that—is a series of jagged, obsidian spires that claw upward, piercing the upper atmosphere. However, these peaks are almost entirely submerged in a "drowned sky." An inversion layer of super-saturated ocean hangs overhead, heavier than the air, creating a vertical underwater world in the open air. The distinction between ocean and atmosphere is gone. The rain does not fall; it simply is. You are walking through a three-dimensional reef of mist, where the "water" is warm, green, and heavy.

**Geology (0.4 - Emerging)**
The terrain is painfully new. The spires you cling to are raw, unweathered basalt, sharp enough to slice skin, formed only moments ago in geological time. The air screams with the smell of ozone and sulfur—the scent of rock being born. There are no footholds, no gentle slopes, only the violent, fractured geometry of land that has not yet learned how to be smooth. It feels less like a landscape and more like a wound in the fabric of the world, fresh and throbbing.

**Vegetation and Density (1.8 / 1.6)**
Vegetation here is not growing *on* the rock; it is the rock’s scab. The flora consists of massive, spongy colonial mosses and tubular ferns that look like lungs. They are lush, wet, and heavy, dragging the ecosystem down with their weight.
Because of the high density, the space is claustrophobic. Hanging vines, thick as pythons, dangle from the spires, interlocking to form a lattice of wet biomass. It is a sponge-world; every square inch is occupied by something living or dying. The jungle doesn't rustle; it gurgles, soaked through, the leaves so fat with water they look like glass bubbles ready to burst.

**Architecture and Tech (1.6 / 0.6)**
Rising from the vertical rock faces, threaded through the moss, is a settlement that defies the primitive technological level suggested by the parameters. It is an **Urban Anemone**.
The structures are made of "ite-stone"—a primitive, calcified composite mixed from coral dust, volcanic ash, and spider-silk analogs. The buildings are tall, tubular, and porous. They are organized in tiers, clinging to the spires like barnacles.
Despite the primitive tools—sharpened obsidian, bone-levers, hand-spun ropes—the engineering is sophisticated in its density. There are walkways of woven vine-steel connecting the towers, cisterns to catch the distinct flavors of the mist, and distinct districts. It is a city of wicker and wet stone, a vertical labyrinth where a thousand people live in a space meant for fifty. You see no metal, no electricity, only the intricate, labor-intensive weaving of a high-density hive.

**Atmosphere and Light (2.5 / 1.1)**
It is warm. Bathers-soaking, sweat-and-steam warm. The heat radiates not just from the geothermal vents below but from the decomposition of the endless vegetation.
Yet, it is dim. The "drowned sky" above blocks the sun, filtering the light into a perpetual, sickly twilight—a glowing emerald darkness. Shadows are deep and blue. The humidity is so dense that sound does not travel far; the world feels close, muffled, and insulated. The dominant sound is the "fat drip"—the constant, rhythmic patter of condensation falling from leaf to leaf, a drumbeat that slows the perception of time.

**Fauna and Weirdness (1.2 / 1.8)**
Animals are rare, but the ones that exist are strange. You see **Silt-Gliders**: ray-like creatures that swim not in water, but in the thickest currents of the heavy mist, their fins wide and thin. They drift silently between the towers of the city, hunted by the inhabitants not with spears, but with weighted nets.
The strangeness manifests in the density. The biology is so compressed that the lines between plant, animal, and building have blurred. The walls of the houses are slowly being digested by the moss, and the moss is growing into the shape of the walls. It is a place where the "built" environment is simply a stage in the lifecycle of the "natural" one. The city is not conquering the jungle; it is just a different kind of coral in the reef.
