# The Marrow Pocket

You descend into what might be a wound in the earth or might be a mouth — the distinction hasn't decided yet. The walls around you are not quite stone. They are *becoming* stone, mineralization still in progress, and if you press your palm against them you feel a faint, almost biological warmth, as if the calcium is still remembering that it was once part of something that bled. Veins of nascent calcite run in directions that seem deliberate but resolve into no pattern. The rock has no grain yet. It has no opinion about what it wants to be.

And yet the light is extraordinary. Bright, diffuse, sourceless — it does not come from above, because there is no above, only a ceiling of this same unfinished mineral curving perhaps three meters overhead at its highest. The glow appears to emanate from the rock itself, a steady pearl-white luminance, not warm, not cold, simply *present*, as though the calcium is radiant in its transitional state, a fever of formation. You cast no shadow. Or rather: your shadow appears in wrong places, lagging, then arriving all at once, then gone.

---

The pocket is small. Perhaps the size of a chapel — a narrow nave, with a low vault, and the floor uneven, pale, slightly damp. Water appears in ways that make you distrust your eyes: beads of condensation that climb *up* the walls rather than down, pooling in depressions in the ceiling as small upside-down lakes that occasionally release a single drop into the air, which falls with an almost inaudible tap and vanishes before it reaches the floor. The humidity is constant, cool, the kind of damp that settles into fabric and never quite dries. You can taste limestone on every breath, thin and chalky, but underneath that something faintly sweet, like rain on flowering earth, even though nothing flowers here.

Nothing grows from the walls. The vegetation is minimal — a scattering of something pale and filament-thin, not quite moss, not quite lichen, growing in patches no larger than a handprint wherever the mineral surface has developed the faintest texture. It is barely alive. It photosynthesizes nothing — there is nothing to photosynthesize from — and yet it persists, catching the rock-light, feeding on some process you cannot name. In certain spots, small clusters of translucent nodules emerge from the wall surface, resembling fungal fruiting bodies but mineral, or mineral but somehow organic, and they have grown since you arrived, or you are imagining that they have grown since you arrived.

---

The fauna is where the pocket becomes unsettling.

They are everywhere, and they are quiet. That is the first thing you notice — the overwhelming *presence* of animal life in a space this small, and the total absence of what you'd expect from it. No rustling. No clicks. No wingbeats. The silence is deep, almost pressurized, and the creatures move within it as if silence were a medium they swim through.

The walls are colonized. Small arthropod-like organisms — segmented, pale, leggy, each no longer than your smallest fingernail — move in ceaseless slow processions along the mineral grain, following paths that appear and disappear, as if the walls have invisible trails that only they can sense. There are hundreds. Thousands, if you look closely. They carry nothing, build nothing, mate in motion. When two meet, they do not interact. They pass through each other. You watch this happen three times before you trust your eyes. They are not phantoms — they leave faint tracks in the dampness — but they are not entirely *present* either, as if they exist at a frequency slightly offset from solid matter.

Larger things occupy the middle air. Insects, perhaps — long-bodied, slow-winged, translucent as the nodules on the wall. They drift in loose orbits around the chamber, never touching the walls, never colliding, tracing paths that could be called flight but feel more like punctuation, as if each one is pausing in the air at specific points to mark something. They do not react to you. You walk through their orbits and they rearrange without alarm, flowing around your body like water around a stone, and where they pass close to your skin you feel a coolness that is not quite temperature — a coolness of *absence*, as if they are tiny holes in the warmth of the world.

In the depressions of the floor, where the moisture pools in shallow, mirror-still films, something lives beneath the surface. You see it only as motion — small, sinuous, quick, moving just beneath the tension of the water, leaving no ripple. It could be a worm, a larva, a larval worm, something that has not yet committed to a body plan. There are several, each in their own small pool, and they do not cross into each other's water, though nothing physical separates the depressions.

And in the corners, where the walls meet the floor at angles that are not quite consistent — geometry is *suggestive* here rather than declarative — there are structures. Small, scattered, clearly made. Not by human hands. Not by anything with hands, as far as you can tell. They are cairns of mineral chips, stacked with an architecture that implies intention but not comprehension: each one is different, each one is a few centimeters tall, and each one is built around a single object. A shed arthropod carapace. A crystallized droplet of water, perfectly preserved, held aloft on a spike ofite. A bone, tiny, from something that has no business having bones.

No one is building them. You watch for a long time and nothing touches them. But they change. When you look away and look back, a new chip has been added, or one has been removed, or the whole structure has rotated slightly, or — you cannot be certain — one of the floating insects has been *incorporated*, its body now resting at the center of the cairn, wings folded, clearly dead, clearly placed.

---

The activity is constant but has no visible engine. The arthropods process. The insects orbit. The sub-surface things dart and coil. Moisture climbs and condenses and drops and vanishes. The walls emit light and slowly, slowly thicken into something more definite. The cairns change. And all of this happens in near-total silence — the only sounds are the infrequent tap of a ceiling-drop releasing, the faintest dry whisper of the wall-crawlers' legs on mineral, and, if you hold very still and listen past the sound of your own blood, a hum. Not from any direction. Not from the walls or the air. From the space itself, as if the pocket has a resonant frequency, a note it is almost producing, a sound it is still learning to make.

You understand, after a time, that you are inside something that is not finished. Not a cave. Not a room. An *event* — geological, biological, ontological — that has not yet resolved into a category. The rock does not know if it is bone or mineral. The creatures do not know if they are alive or pattern. The structures do not know if they are built or grown. The light does not know if it is radiation or meaning.

You are standing inside a question the earth is still phrasing.

And the question is: *what kind of place is this trying to become?*

The fauna continues its silent industry. The walls continue their slow becoming. The cairns continue their quiet accumulation. You are tolerated. You are not noticed. You are part of the geometry now, one more object in the pocket, one more presence in the sparse and luminous uncertainty, and if you stay long enough — you feel this with a certainty that has no evidence — if you stay long enough, you too will begin to *resolve*.

It would be best, perhaps, to begin climbing back out.

The way you came in is still there. You are almost sure of this.
