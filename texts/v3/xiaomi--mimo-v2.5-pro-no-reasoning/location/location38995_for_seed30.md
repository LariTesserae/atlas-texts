You are high up, but the ground doesn't feel like a mountaintop. It feels like the inside of a lung.

The terrain is a series of soft, undulating ridges and shallow basins, all covered in a substance that isn't quite soil. It’s warm, a constant 1.6 on some internal thermometer, and moist to the touch. This is a landscape still forming, embryonic, its geology less than a century old. The "rocks" are pale, semi-translucent nodules, weeping a thin, clear fluid from pores that seem to open and close with a slow, respiratory rhythm. The fluid doesn't pool; it’s absorbed instantly by the ground, which sighs with the intake.

Water is everywhere, not as a river or lake, but as a persistent, intimate wetness. The air itself is thick with it, a warm, condensing atmosphere that beads on your skin and tastes faintly of ozone and damp stone. Vegetation is sparse—groves of something that resembles fungal stalks, pale and veined with bioluminescent light, push up from the weeping ground. They are not trees; they are semi-rigid, yielding slightly if you push them, their caps shimmering with captured moisture.

The scale is intimate, hamlet-sized. You are in a valley, but the valley is a single room. The ridges around you are the walls. In the dim light—not sunlight, but a diffuse, amber glow emanating from the very fabric of the air and ground—you can see the "built" environment. It is scattered, almost organic. Structures of what looks like a fused, pearlescent ceramic grow from the ridges themselves. They are small, single-occupancy pods, some open like mouths, others sealed with a thin, membrane-like material. They are not houses; they are more like cocoons or valves in a larger system. Faint traceries of light, like circuitry, pulse within their walls.

The tech is beyond you, but it is not inert. It is integrated. The weeping rocks, the condensing atmosphere, the pulsing structures—they are all part of a single, humming engineering. The sound is a constant, layered drone. There is the low, resonant hum of the structures themselves, a vibration felt in the bones. There is the soft, wet susurrus of water moving through the ground. And there is the fauna.

Life is abundant. Not large creatures, but a dense, humming ecosystem at a smaller scale. Insects, or things like insects, with iridescent carapaces that seem to be made of the same pearlescent ceramic as the structures. They flit between the fungal stalks, drinking the weeping fluid. Their wings beat not with a buzz, but with a soft, metallic *clink*. On the ground, slug-like things with complex, segmented armor graze on the moisture-slick surfaces. From the open pods, you sometimes catch a glimpse of a larger presence—a sleek, amphibious form that watches with multiple, jewel-like eyes before retracting. The world is active, a constant biological and mechanical churn.

The light is dim, but the bioluminescence of the fungi and the circuitry of the structures creates a shifting, blue-green and amber chiaroscuro. Shadows are deep and move with the pulsing of the light. The weirdness is palpable. This place is not a natural landscape with technology on it. It is a machine that has grown into a landscape, or a landscape that has awakened into a machine. The rules are different here. Gravity feels slightly off, subjective. Sound travels strangely, a whisper near your ear seeming to come from across the valley. The boundaries of the "room" are not fixed; the ridges seem to breathe, expanding and contracting on a timescale of hours.

You are standing in a heart, or a processor, or a womb. The air is warm, wet, and loud with the quiet industry of a world coming online.
