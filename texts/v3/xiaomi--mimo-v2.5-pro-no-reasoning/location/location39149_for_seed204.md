The light is thin, the color of bleached bone. It falls from a sky of permanent, high-altitude overcast, a diffuse pallor that gives no hint of a sun's position. You are standing on the edge of something immense and silent.

You are on the **Spire**.

Imagine a mountain range that never stopped rising, thrust upward with such recent, violent force that its features are still more geometry than geography. The rock is a dark, wet basalt, shot through with veins of a pale, luminous mineral. It is a world of immense, sharp-edged planes and sheer drops. There are no soft slopes, only abrupt shelves and canyons carved not by millennia of wind, but by the fracturing stress of its own impossible growth. The air is thin and bitingly cold, carrying the smell of ozone and wet stone.

Water is everywhere, but it has no rivers. It weeps. It seeps from the porous, freshly-exposed rock, running in thin, dark rivulets down cliff faces. It gathers in shallow, perfectly still pools on the vast, flat plateaus, the surfaces so motionless they reflect the pallid sky like black mirrors. In the colder, shadowed chasms, it has frozen into thin, crystalline sheets that groan softly under their own weight. The sound is a constant, low-level percussion: the drip, drip, drip of condensation, the occasional crack of shifting ice, the whisper of wind channeling through a newly formed canyon.

And on these plateaus and in the heart of these canyons stands the city.

It is not a city of streets and squares, but of **Lances**. The structures are tall, impossibly slender spires of a seamless, bone-white material that seems to drink the faint light. They are built directly into the rock, often spanning chasms or rising from the center of the black mirror-pools. They are connected not by roads, but by a web of gossamer-thin bridges and shimmering tubes, which pulse with a soft, internal light. The architecture is alien and minimal; there are no windows, no visible doors. The buildings are less places to live than instruments, or perhaps, geological features of a different, more sophisticated order. The whole city is a single, integrated machine, built to withstand the planet's violent youth and to perform some function the human mind can only guess at.

There is no movement in the streets, for there are no streets. The city is sparsely populated, its inhabitants perhaps numbered in the thousands. They are **ghosts**. You do not see them. You sense them as faint disturbances in the air, as a momentary ripple in a pool's surface, as a pattern of light that shifts for a second on a distant spire's flank. They are not biological. They are patterns, energy-signatures, or perhaps the conscious infrastructure of the city itself, tending to its own inscrutable processes. They make no sound you can hear, only occasionally a low, sub-vocal hum that you feel in your bones, the city's own pulse.

The activity here is not of people, but of the **Spire** itself. The rock groans and shifts. New, sharp features emerge over days. Water seeps and freezes in cycles. The Lances perform their silent calculations, their internal lights pulsing in slow, complex rhythms. It is a place of immense, latent power and profound, cold loneliness. It is a city for non-people, built on a world that is still becoming a world, under a light that is the color of forgetting.
