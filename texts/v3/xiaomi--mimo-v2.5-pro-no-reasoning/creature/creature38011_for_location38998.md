The first thing you must understand is that the Urban are not *in* the water. They *are* the water.

Or rather, they are the consciousness of the water, the slow, geological thought process given form and function. They are not inhabitants, but participants in the plateau's endless, liquid meditation.

They move with the speed of tectonic drift. A limb, if it can be called that, might extend over a century, a stone-limbed appendage smoothing a channel here, deepening a cistern there, guiding a trickle into a more perfect, circular pool. Their bodies are the living architecture you see—the amphitheaters are their seated forms, the aqueducts their outstretched arms, the moss-covered spires their slow, upward-reaching thoughts. They are not made of flesh, but of the place itself: a fusion of weeping stone, patient lichen, and still, deep water. Their "skin" is the velvet crust of the plateau, absorbing and releasing moisture in a rhythm as slow as their own perception.

To look into a deep cistern is to look into one of their eyes—dark, reflective, holding the pearlescent sky and the memory of every stone it has mirrored. They do not speak with sound, but with the subtle shift of hydrology. A new channel opens, a pool's temperature changes by a fraction of a degree, a particular lichen begins to fruit on a north-facing wall—these are their conversations, their debates, their poetry, written in the language of flow and saturation.

Their society, if such a term can be applied to beings who exist in a state of eternal, interconnected present, is one of distributed being. Each great structure is a node of awareness, but all are linked by the capillary network of waterways and the deep, porous bedrock. They share a single, planetary-scale mind, its thoughts the movement of cloud, the percolation of dew, the slow, grinding adjustment of stone.

The pale salamander in the cistern is not a pet, but a fleeting sensory organ, a momentary, mobile nerve-end for the cistern-thought. The slate-feathered bird is a synapse, a quick, silent connection between one spire-consciousness and another, its brief flight a flash of acute perception in a world of deep, ambient awareness.

They do not build. They *become*. Their labor is a form of exhalation, their architecture a crystallization of their own being. They are sculpting not for purpose, but for perfection of form, seeking the most elegant, most efficient, most balanced expression of the plateau's own nature. They are the plateau, slowly waking up, adjusting its limbs, and contemplating the eternal, damp now. To them, you are a flicker, a momentary disruption in the moisture-film on a stone, gone before the thought of your presence can complete its glacial journey from one node to the next. You are a brief, unseasonable heat. They are the climate itself.
