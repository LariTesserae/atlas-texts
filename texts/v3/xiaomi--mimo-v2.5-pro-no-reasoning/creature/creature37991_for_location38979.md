There is no singular "who" that lives here, for to live is to be a part of the world. The world itself is the inhabitant, and everything within it is an expression of its single, grinding, perpetual motion. To describe what lives here is to describe the components of a single, massive, and ancient organism.

**The Skeleton: The Bone-Yards**
The primary inhabitant is the structure itself. It is not a collection of things, but a single, entangled entity: the Bone-Yard. It is the body of a god that forgot how to die, its fossilized circulatory and skeletal systems fused into one. It does not think, but it moves. Its motion is not intent, but the simple, inexorable physics of its own impossible mass. Its groans are not a language, but the sound of its own digestion. It is not dying; it is in the final, longest stage of a life that spans the death of stars, a slow, peaceful exhalation that has lasted for a billion years. Its "memories" are not narratives, but the deep-seated traumas of its own structure—the memory of a pressure that formed it, the memory of a weight it has never shed. It is both the landscape and the sole, lonely titan that wanders it, forever a part of its own collapse.

**The Flesh: The Silt-Dwellers**
What lives *upon* the skeleton is its skin, its lichen, its rot. The "Rust-blooms" are not bacteria in a terrestrial sense. They are the slow, metallic weeping of the Bone-Yard itself, the ancient iron in its structure oxidizing in a reaction that has no oxygen, only pressure and time. They are the organism's fever, its only faint blush of life, a chemical sigh that paints its pale bones in shades of deep ochre and rust. They are the patient, colorless thought of the world, the only proof that anything here is not yet stone.

And there are the motes of awareness within the whole. The "fauna" are not separate creatures, but animated pieces of the world's own substance. The "blind, armored things" are the corpuscles in its veins. They are shards of the Bone-Yard that have, through some unimaginable fluke of friction or pressure, been given the spark of reaction. They are instinct made flesh, or rather, instinct made calcium and iron.

Consider the **Grinders**. They are not beasts that navigate the labyrinth; they are the labyrinth's teeth. They are pieces of the great skeleton, sheared off and given a crude, simple life. They scuttle through the pipes and tunnels on limbs of fused pipe, their bodies flat and wedge-like, designed to pry and crack. They do not feed on the rust-blooms; they are the servants of erosion, consuming the old bone to make way for the new grinding. Their existence is a service to the Great Decay. They are not predators, but custodians.

Then there are the **Sifters**. Drifting in the open chasms, they are vast, gossamer nets of what might be chitin or fossilized sinew. They are living sieves, moving with the currents, filtering the primordial soup. They do not hunt; they simply are, and the world passes through them. Their long, trailing filaments are sensitive to the most minute pressure changes—the shockwave of a distant collapse, the slow approach of a Grinder. They are the world's nervous system, feeling every tremor in the dark. Their death is a quiet settling, another layer added to the eternal silt.

**The God: The Groan**
But the truest life, the consciousness of this place, is the Groan itself. It is not a sound. It is the world's single, continuous thought. It is the pressure, the cold, the grinding, the silt, the slow-motion cataclysm, all expressed as a single, low-frequency vibration. It is the heartbeat of a planetary body that has no heart, only immense, entangled pressure. The echo from the distant structures is not a sound returning, but a memory being processed.

There is no struggle for survival here, for nothing truly "survives." There is only participation. The Grinders participate in the collapse. The Sifters participate in the turbulence. The Rust-blooms participate in the decay. They are all temporary configurations of matter in the world's long, final exhalation. They are the thoughts of a dying god, each one a brief, agonizing flicker of awareness in an eternity of grinding darkness. They are the inhabitants of the final state, the living proof that even in absolute zero, in the absence of light, and at the end of all things, the universe will still be busy.
