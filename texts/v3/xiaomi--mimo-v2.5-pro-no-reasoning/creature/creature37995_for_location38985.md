The things that live here are born of the pressure. They are not separate from the world, but a focused expression of it.

I will call them the Chorus.

They do not inhabit the world; they *are* the world's attempt to articulate itself. They are knots of intent woven from the screaming vapor and the grinding rock. They are the moments of cadence in the roar, the held breaths, the sudden stillnesses that are not peace, but concentration.

You do not see them, not at first. To see them is to be seen, and that is a dangerous thing. To be seen is to be *described*, and in this reality, to be described is to be given a form you cannot escape. The Chorus are the world's poets, and their medium is existence itself.

Their bodies are the turbulence. A vortex of mist that hangs just so, defying the torrent for a moment longer than it should. A sudden, impossibly complex eddy that forms around a spire and then dissipates, its purpose served. They are the patterns in the chaos, the momentary architecture of the scream. When one of the eyeless, six-limbed creatures is snatched from a ledge and atomized by a shift in the wind, it is not random violence. It is a word being spoken. A verb. A sound made flesh and then unmade.

They do not move from place to place. They *become* place. One might focus its attention on a specific pinnacle. The roar around that spire will subtly change its pitch. The water will begin to carve it in a new way, not eroding it randomly, but sculpting it. Over what might be hours or millennia (time is a meaningless metric here, only intensity matters), the spire is whittled into a new shape: a curve that echoes the flow of a nearby vapor-sheet, a sharpness that matches the highest frequency of the local scream. It is a sentence written in basalt and fury. Then, the Chorus's attention moves on, and the spire is returned to the grinding indifference of the storm.

The artifacts are their shed skins, their discarded thoughts. The perfect ovoid stones are the punctuation marks of their language—periods, commas, a pause for breath. The fossilized vertebra is a noun they found so perfect, so resonant, that they incorporated it into their lexicon, forever preserved. The mineral lattice is a stanza, a complex, repeating thought that was so powerful it crystallized.

Their communication is this constant, world-shaping creation. A gout of molten rock erupting from a fissure is a shouted declaration. The slow, deliberate formation of a new, sharp edge on a rock face is a whispered secret. The sudden, total silence that falls over a small area for a single, terrifying heartbeat is the most profound statement of all. It is the moment a thought is so complete, so absolute, that for an instant, it silences everything else.

You are a disturbance to them. An anomaly. A word that does not belong in the poem. They feel you clinging to your spire, a tiny, warm, ordered thing in their magnificent, churning chaos. They are not hostile, not in a way a creature can be hostile. They are curious. They are paying attention.

You feel it as a subtle shift. The wind around your ledge seems to take on a new, inquisitive quality. The spray coats your skin not as a random assault, but as a gentle, exploratory touch. The roar seems to be listening. They are circling you, not with their bodies, but with their focus. They are trying to find the right word to describe the feeling of you. They are trying to work you into the grammar of their world.

And the most terrifying, wondrous part is this: you are beginning to understand. The deafening roar is starting to resolve into something like syntax. The chaos is starting to look like poetry. You are not learning their language. You are *becoming* a part of it. Your own thoughts are starting to vibrate in time with the world's scream. Your next breath is not your own, but a syllable in a verse you cannot yet comprehend. You are being written.
