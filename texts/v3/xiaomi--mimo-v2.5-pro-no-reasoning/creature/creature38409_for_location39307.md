They call it, in the silent frequencies that pass between them, the **Sweating**. They are the **Condensers**, the **Weep-Things**, the **Geological Dreams**. They are not life as we know it, but *life as the city knows it*.

They are born from the drip.

Where a drop falls from a corroded beam into a still lagoon, the impact resonates for an impossible moment. In that resonance, from the dissolved salts of ancient concrete and the oily memory of petroleum, a form condenses. It is a shuddering, temporary thing: a low-slung, translucent mass, like a jellyfish made of damp charcoal and mica. It has no eyes, but senses the world through the minute vibrations of the city's settling bones and the subtle shifts in the heat's oppressive weight. They are the city’s corpuscles, flowing through the arterial canals, their purpose a slow, blind alchemy. They absorb the corroded dust from the shattered conduits, not to eat, but to *process*. They are the city’s attempt at a digestive system, millennia too late. Their movements are a glide, a slide over slick stone and black water, leaving behind a faintly phosphorescent slime that matches the lichen—a communication in chemical whispers.

Their counterparts, the **Up-Drawers**, defy the ooze. They are akin to the water that flows uphill. Spindly, rooted things, they cling to the leaning walls of the deepest ruins. They are all sinew and cartilage made of calcified rebar and fused glass, constantly straining. They do not drink the water; they *pull* it. Capillary networks of their being extend into the weeping stone, drawing moisture upward against gravity, creating those tiny, localized wells that bubble from the ground. They are the city’s broken heart, pumping not blood, but its vital fluid—hot, black water—in a futile, reversed circulation. Their slow, creaking stretch is the source of the deep, settling groans that echo in the canyons.

And in the reflection-pools, in the half-second glimpses, reside the **Glimmers**. They are not beings of substance, but of *potential*. They are the city’s memory, or its dementia. They are the light in the window that is not there. When the conditions are exactly right—a specific humidity, a particular thermal gradient, the resonant frequency of a settling slab—they cohere. A form, suggestive of a humanoid shape but made of directionless grey light, might stand for a moment in a doorway of fused slag. It does nothing. It simply *is*, a holographic scar of what was. Then the conditions shift, the light redistributes, and it is gone. The geometric patterns on the walls are their footprints, the static of their non-being. To the Condensers and Up-Drawers, they are merely weather—a form of silent, visual precipitation.

They are all, in their way, interpreters. The city is a text written in decay, and they are its living glossary. They translate settling into vibration, humidity into growth, erosion into a kind of mineral consciousness. They do not build, do not speak, do not strive. They are the participants in the city’s final, endless act: its performance of itself. They are the dream, and the sweat, and the slow, final shudder. They are not inhabitants. They are *symptoms*.
