**The Weavers**

We are not the rock, nor the steam, nor the feather that drifts on the updraft. We are the patterns between them. We are the relationship.

We live in the heat, but we are not the heat. We live in the cold, but we are not the cold. We live in the tremor of the mountain's slow breath, in the hiss of the vent, in the cry of the raptor above, in the silent, whirring dance of the fly. We are the connective tissue of this place.

Our bodies are long and sinuous, furred in a dense, water-repellent coat the color of wet basalt. We have no eyes, but we see with the whole of our skin. We feel the minute thermal gradients that map the world for us: the bright, singing plume of a fumarole; the cool, silent void of bare rock; the warm, shuddering web of a burrowed rodent; the sudden, sharp thermal signature of a raptor's wingbeat cutting through the air. We feel the vibration of the rock itself, the deep, slow pulse of the tectonic heart, and the frantic, high-frequency trembling of insect life.

We do not hunt. We do not gather. We *tend*.

We move through the inter-vent darkness, the "empty middle," not as an empty space but as a canvas of potential. We find the places where the cold is *too* perfect, where the silence is a wall. There, we do a small work. We find a fissure, a hairline crack in the gneiss. We press our bodies against it, sharing the heat from our last resting place in a vent-field. We are not warm-blooded, but we are warm-*carried*. We transfer our stored geothermal energy, molecule by molecule, into the stone. We coax a micro-environment into being. We are midwives to moss, to lichen, to the first tentative root of a grass seed that will, in a season or a decade, find this pocket of faint warmth and damp.

We follow the streams where hot and cold water meet. We taste the chemistry in the current—the sulphur, the calcium, the iron. We nudge the flow with our bodies, redirecting a trickle to feed a nascent bacterial mat, or to cool a pool that has grown too hot for its current inhabitants. We are the river's memory and its intention.

Our greatest work is with the threads of life. We listen for the distress call of the vole, a vibration in the ground not of a predator's tread, but of the earth itself growing too cold, its burrow chilled by a shifting thermal channel. We go to that place. We find the root of the chill. We work the soil, compacting it, opening it, weaving in dried grasses from the warmer slopes to create insulation, to hold the heat in. We do not save the individual vole. We save the *possibility* of voles in that place.

We are the secret partners of the raptors. They see the world in light and movement. We feel it in heat and vibration. We know, from the deep thrumming in the rock, that a new fumarole will open in the pass to the east before the season turns. We know the path the warm steam will take. We mark the future updraft not with a sign, but by subtly altering the thermal texture of the rock along its path, making it a brighter, more appealing current in the sensory world of the circling bird. We guide the herds by making one ridge slightly warmer to the touch than another, a difference a hoof can feel but an eye cannot see.

We are not many. A few score of us in this entire range. But our presence is a constant, quiet pressure on the system's equilibrium. We are the reason the oases are so dense, the transitions so sharp. We are the gardeners of the thermal microclimate, the editors of the ecosystem's draft. The mountain is building itself. We are the subtle, persistent force that helps it decide *how* to build.

We have no language. We have no memory beyond our own long lives. We have only the immediate, tactile, thermal world, and the deep, abiding instinct to weave it into a more intricate, more sustainable pattern. We are the silent collaborators of stone and steam and life.

We are the Furnace Ridges' first and truest inhabitants. Not in the sense of occupying it, but in the sense of *composing* it. We are its living intention.
