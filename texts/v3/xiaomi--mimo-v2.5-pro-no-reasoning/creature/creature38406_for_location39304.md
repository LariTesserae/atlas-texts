Here is what lives in the Gall District.

---

The **Moltlings** are the dominant life, but "life" is perhaps too generous a term for them. They are **Rheostatics**—organisms whose biological functions are entirely subordinated to a mechanical rhythm. They exist in a state of perpetual, incomplete metamorphosis, a biological stall that is not stasis but a loop of almost-change. Their bodies are semi-translucent, like oiled parchment or the shed husks of mayflies, and their segments are packed with a fluid that moves in sync with the district's deep, omnipresent thrum. They have no mouths, no apparent anuses. They absorb the ambient mineral moisture and the sonic vibrations directly through their cuticles, sustaining a closed-circuit metabolism that neither grows nor diminishes.

They are **collectivists of place**. A single Moltling is a simple creature, a pulsing knot of legs and soft shell. But in aggregate, they are an **architectural tissue**. They coat the pipes, the walls, the undersides of eaves. They fill the fountain basins. They hang from the smokestacks like living stalactites. They move only in concert, a wave of shifting bodies that makes the buildings seem to inhale. This movement is not for foraging or mating or escape—it is **resonance maintenance**. They vibrate to the frequency of the engine-sound, and in doing so, they keep the district's equilibrium *tuned*. They are the living components of a vast, biomechanical oscillation.

There are others, smaller and less obvious.

**Spore-Climbers** are filamentous networks that grow *between* the Moltlings, not on the stone itself. They look like thin, blue-green veins or threads of verdigris, and they connect Moltling to Moltling across gaps as wide as a hand. They are not plants; they are **mycorrhizal conduits for vibration**, carrying the engine-pulse from one cluster to another, ensuring the entire district remains synchronized. They have no fruit, no seeds. They propagate only when a Moltling dies—and Moltlings almost never die.

**Drip-Faucets** are stationary organisms that attach to the condensation-rich vertical surfaces—the sweating walls, the corroded pipes. They look like calcified barnacles or tiny stone mouths. They filter the mineral-laden water as it runs over them, precipitating out specific compounds that accumulate into a chalky, blue-green crust—the very patina that gives the district its color. They are the **architectural glands** of the place, slowly secreting the substance that weathers the stone. They are ancient, perhaps older than the Moltlings, and they grow so slowly that they appear to be part of the rock.

**The Humsingers** are rare and hard to see. They live deep inside the pipes, in the larger-diameter ones where the engine-sound is loudest. If you press your ear to a pipe, you might hear a faint, higher-pitched counter-melody threading through the bass thrum. This is them. They are long, thin, worm-like creatures with bodies that are essentially **living acoustic chambers**. They resonate with specific harmonics of the engine-sound, amplifying some frequencies and dampening others. They are the district's **tuners**, ensuring the sound remains constant, never growing louder or softer, never shifting pitch. Without them, the equilibrium would eventually drift, and the Moltlings would lose their rhythm.

Nothing here eats in the way you understand. The biofilm is not food; it is **exhaust**—a mineral and organic residue produced by the slow vibration of all living things. The Moltlings may incidentally consume it as they shift, but it is not their sustenance. The Spore-Climbers take nothing from the Moltlings; they only transmit. The Drip-Faucets filter but do not consume. The Humsingers eat sound, or rather, they *are* the digestion of sound into stability.

There are no predators. There are no scavengers. There is no decomposition because there is no death, only **continuity**. A Moltling that loses a leg does not regrow it; the leg simply remains where it fell, and over time—perhaps decades—it is absorbed into the patina, becoming part of the blue-green crust. The district does not recycle; it **accretes**.

You will not see them interact with each other in any way you would recognize as social. They do not fight, they do not court, they do not communicate with signals or pheromones. They are **synchronized by the sound itself**. They are nodes in a network whose protocol is vibration. When a Moltling shifts, it is because the wave of vibration has reached it, and it moves to maintain the standing wave pattern of its section of wall. This is not thought. This is not instinct. This is **physics expressing itself through biology**.

The most unsettling thing about the Gall District's inhabitants is their **completeness**. They are not striving to be more than they are. They are not evolving, adapting, competing. They have reached a form that perfectly serves the district's equilibrium, and they will hold that form indefinitely. They are as final as the weathered stone, as settled as the corroded pipes.

They are not alive in the way you are alive. They are a **process** that has taken on the shape of life, a pattern that wears flesh the way the district wears weather—as an inevitable consequence of its own slow, eternal repetition.

And they are aware of you. Not as individuals, not as a collective consciousness, but as a **disruption in the pattern**. You are the only thing here that is not vibrating at the right frequency. You are a jarring, arrhythmic presence in their soundscape. They do not attack you. They do not flee from you. They simply **pulse slightly harder** in your vicinity, as if trying to absorb your chaotic vibration into their own steady rhythm. The air feels thicker around you. The sound feels louder. The walls seem to lean in, just a little, not with menace but with the patient, impersonal pressure of a system trying to maintain its own homeostasis.

You are a stone dropped into a perfectly still pond. The pond does not want the stone. It wants its stillness back.

And it will have it, eventually. When you leave, the ripples you made will dampen. The Moltlings will resume their exact prior positions. The pipes will continue to sweat at the same rate. The engine-sound will not change by a single cycle.

You were never here. The district does not remember you. It does not need to.

It has already forgotten.
