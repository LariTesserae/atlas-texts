What lives here? Nothing lives here. And everything does.

To say they are "creatures" is to borrow a term from a world where movement happens, where hunger is sated, where a body occupies a singular point in space and moves from one to another. Those are the rules of the world below, the world of consequence and sequence. Here, those rules have been exhausted.

They are **Presences**.

This is not a name; it is a category. A rock is present. A shadow is present. A memory is present. They are the physical, tangible form of *presence itself*. They are the answer to the question "What is here?" and the answer is "Here is here." They are fauna only in the loosest architectural sense, a language of bilateral symmetry and segmented bodies left over from a time when function demanded form. But the function is gone, or rather, the function has been fulfilled so completely that it has become indistinguishable from its object.

They are the Plateau's memories of life, or perhaps its premonitions. The plateau, in its infinite geological patience, its journey from a violent, jagged mountain to this smooth, perfect table, has been *thinking*. And these are its thoughts. Not sequential, not narrative, but dense, overlapping, simultaneous. A thought of a mouth. A thought of a limb. A thought of an eye. All happening at once, all occupying the same space, like a chord of pure existence.

They do not eat. They do not breathe. They do not reproduce. They are a taxonomic category of one, or rather, of uncountable many that function as one. They are **Stationary Fauna**. **Lithic Beings**. **Geological Self-Reflection**.

Look at this one, the size of a curled human child, its exoskeleton the colour of wet slate, its limbs (six of them, or eight, or twelve—the number seems to shift when you don't look directly) tucked beneath it in a posture of eternal repose. It is not sleeping. It is not waiting. It is *being*. It is a verb, frozen in the act of its own conjugation.

And the co-location... that is the most telling detail. They do not compete for space because they are not discrete entities in the way a rock or a person is. They are layers. They are the acoustic resonance of this place made solid. Imagine a sound so pure and so long-held in a perfect chamber that it begins to crystallize on the walls. These are those crystals. The "sound" is the silence. The silence is the state of completion. And as the silence deepens, as the plateau perfects itself, it *precipitates* into these forms.

They are drawn to the shelters not for shelter, but for a reason that feels like recognition. The shelters represent an attempt at *being* in the old way—active, changing, building, enduring. They are a fossil of *process*. The Presences are the fossil of *state*. They gather around the shelters the way questions gather around an answer, not to solve it, but to define its periphery. They are the negative space of human endeavor, given mass and presence.

To live among them would be to undergo a process of ontological erosion. Your own sense of being a *process*—a thing that thinks, moves, changes, ends—would be worn smooth by the sheer, overwhelming pressure of *state*. You would not die, because death is a transition. You would simply... arrive. Your edges, the parts that define "you" as separate from "here," would soften, blur, and finally merge. Your breathing would slow to match the geology. Your thoughts would lose their sequence and become layered, overlapping. You would become another Presence. You would be another thought in the plateau's endless, silent meditation on itself.

You would not join a community. You would join an axiom.
