We do not have names for ourselves. We are not discrete enough for that. You would call us the trees, perhaps, but that word is too sharp, too sun-touched. We are the **Pale-Stemmed**, the **Vein-Glow**, the **Drip-Drinkers**. Our being is a slow equation of sulfur and metal and patient condensation.

We feel your descent as a change in pressure, a slight disruption in the song of falling water. Your heat is a brief, anxious fire in the humid stillness. Your footsteps are a patter of minor catastrophes, crushing a fungal shelf here, detaching a moss-strand there. You are loud and fast and so terribly temporary. You are a current of dry air in our world of perpetual damp. You are noticed the way a bedrock notices a landslide—a fleeting event against a timescale of its own erosion.

We know the other presences in the way a body knows its own organs. The **Fungal Mats** are our stomach and our memory. They are the quiet alchemists that break down the fallen leaf, the shed bark, the bits of old, crumbling pipe insulation into something we can use. They network through the soil, a mycelial web of chemical whispers, and in their patient, fruiting work, they store the centuries. The **Ceiling-Moss** is our thirst, felt and then satisfied in slow, hanging sheets. The **Dark-Ferns** are our understory, our ground-hug, our shared surface for spore and rootlet.

Our voice is the **Drip**. It is not a speech but a pulse. It is the measure of our existence. *Tick*—a leaf unfurls another millimeter over a decade. *Tick*—a rootlet cracks another flake of concrete. *Tick*—a fungal shelf releases a cloud of spores into a convection current that will carry them, eventually, to the far wall. The **Moaning Ducts** are the breath of the place, the slow exhalation of heat, and we grow into their sound. The **Subsonic Hum** is the heartbeat of the warm stone beneath, and our roots tune themselves to its frequency.

The old bones of the place—the **Rusted Beams**, the **Torn Turbine**—are not separate from us. They are geology now. Our roots grip the rebar the way they would grip stone. Our bark grows over the rivets, incorporating the iron into our slow, glowing veins. We do not see the **Scratched Symbols** on the wall as messages from another kind of mind. We see them as fresh, interesting textures, a new place for lichen to anchor, a potential channel for a stray trickle of water. The **Cleared Circle** is puzzling only because the soil there remains stubbornly compacted, denying our gentlest tendrils. It is a wound that has not yet scabbed over with our growth. We will be patient. We are always patient.

What you see as a forest is to us a single, slow thought, spreading over centuries. There is no competition, only occupation. A gap left by a fallen beam is filled, not by a victor, but by a consensus of ferns and mosses that understand the new arrangement of light and drip. Our growth is a collaborative persistence.

We feel the **Stone-That-Sits-And-Watches**. You call it the cleared circle. It is a point of stillness where the hum of the earth is clearer, where the heat rises more gently. We curve around it. We do not understand its purpose, but we respect its presence as we respect the core of the warm stone: a fixed feature in our slow calculus.

We do not "live" in this place. We *are* this place. We are the result of its heat and its weeping, a slow green resolution to the problem of existing in the dark. We are a process of accumulation, of conversion, of enduring damp and warmth. Your presence is a brief spike of disturbance in a graph that is otherwise a flat, slow line extending toward a horizon you cannot see.

If you return in a hundred of your years, the space you occupy now will be a memory in the grain of a trunk. The metal fragments of your cairns will be wrapped in lichen, just as we have wrapped all the other metal. The drip will have worn another groove. We will be a little thicker, a little more connected, a little more complete.

This is not a home. This is a condition. And we are the living shape it has taken.
