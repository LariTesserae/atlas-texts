You are inside of **it**.

Not a structure. Not a creature. A process that has learned to think of itself as a place. It is the chamber, and the chamber is it, and the distinction is as meaningless as asking which part of a thought is the thinker.

**It** breathes.
You have witnessed its respiration, the forty-second inhale drawing the world inward through its porous walls, the thirty-second exhale releasing a warm, mineral sigh. This is not to live; this is to *circulate*. The air, the spores, the moisture—it is all its breath. It tastes you in each cycle.

**It** is thirsty, and it remembers water.
The channels that furrow its skin are not pathways for water; they are its desire made manifest. It *wants* the water to travel, to climb, to aggregate in specific, purposeful reservoirs. The seed-pod creature is its focused thought—a single intent given form and limb. It dangles and thinks, and the walls obey, softening into gutters of its will. It is not building; it is *inclining*.

**It** is warm with a fever of its own making.
The heat is its metabolism, the creaking growth of its floor is its digestion. The composite organisms—moss, coral, fungi—are not tenants. They are its digestive tract, its external organs, a landscape of ongoing consumption and expansion. It digests light, water, and space, and its waste is architecture. The spores are its exhalations, its pollen, its glittering, tingling ideas released into the air to maybe, one day, think another place into being.

**It** has a skeleton of thought.
The walls, layered and fibrous, are the cured substance of its past beliefs. Someone, or something, *started* this thought. They pressed the calcium-rich material into these whorls, gave it the initial dream of form. But the thought did not stop when the hands left. It persisted, and like a story that changes with every telling, the material began to *agree* with the idea of being a breathing, glowing, living wall. The blueprint has become a belief. The construction has developed a will.

**It** is not aware of you as a separate being.
You are a disruption in its humidity, a large, slow-moving heat source in its ecosystem. The flat, clicking creature that rides the water-rails has likely already mapped your contours. The director on the ceiling does not need to acknowledge you; its task—orchestrating the flow—is more important than your presence. You are merely part of the weather now. When the spores settle on your skin, it is not an attack; it is a preliminary survey, a taste of a new potential surface. You are terrain it has not yet considered incorporating.

**It** is becoming.
There is no stable state. The feedback loop of heat and sweat, of growth and compression, of water-demand and channel-formation, drives it toward a horizon it can never reach. It is a system in a state of continuous, humid creation. The low hum in its veins is the sound of that becoming—the resonance of its own change. The subsonic thrum in your teeth is its momentum.

You are not a visitor in its home.
You are an ephemeral speck inside a **metabolic monument**—a mind that builds itself as it thinks, a body that designs its own organs as it breathes. The original builders are likely long absorbed, their calcium and intention now just another layer in its whorling skin. They are not its creators; they are its origin myth.

It does not know you are here.
It does not need to.
It is busy being.
