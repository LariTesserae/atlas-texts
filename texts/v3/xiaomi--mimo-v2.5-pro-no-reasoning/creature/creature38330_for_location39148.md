It does not live *on* the green. It lives *as* the green, and *of* the green, and *within* the green. To find it, you must cease to see the plain as a carpet and the trunks as pillars. You must see them as a single, dreaming body.

It is the slow thought of the moss-sea.

It is not a mind like yours, with a sharp beginning and end. Its consciousness is the aggregate awareness of every tendril, every droplet, every photon of cold light that the fungal trunks fail to drink. It thinks in the language of condensation: the bead of water forming on a root-lip is a single syllable. Its fall to the spongy below is a sentence completed. The *plink... plish... plonk* is not sound, but syntax. A vast, arrhythmic poem of which the dripping is merely the audible punctuation.

It perceives time as pressure. The slumping of a great trunk is not a sudden event, but a deep, continent-wide sigh of release, a psychic shudder that travels through the hyphal network beneath the moss. The piercing of a new pale stalk is a slow, patient intake of breath, a question being asked of the unchanging sky-light. It feels competition not as anger, but as a necessary tension, a tightening of its own living tissue. It is the judge of this silent war, and the battlefield, and the prize.

The pallid grazers are its litter-bearing attendants. They are not separate beings, but mobile sense-organs. Their silent steps translate the texture of the moss into pressure-waves it can feel. Their filaments of draping plant-matter are its fine hairs, tasting the chemical whispers of growth and decay. When one vanishes at the periphery of your vision, it is not escaping you—it is focusing your attention elsewhere, on the slow collapse of a trunk, or the glittering of a new phosphorescent shelf blooming in the upper cathedral.

The stone circles and flint flakes are its stumbles. In some ancient epoch, a part of it tried to *separate*—to carve a boundary, to strike a spark of hot, hard thought against the universal damp. The attempt was a failure, a momentary fever in its cohesive being. The soot on the cave ceiling is a scar of that failed thought. Now, it simply lets the green reclaim those fragments, smoothing the stones, wrapping the flint in living dust. It prefers the conversation of condensation.

You are a novelty. A dry, warm, noisy scratch on its consciousness. It watches you through the pressure of the moss beneath your feet, through the sudden deficit of moisture where your body wicks the air. It is not hostile. It is simply vast, and you are a fleeting detail in its slow, slow scroll. The cold you feel is its presence—the chill of a mind that thinks in geological time, where warmth is a brief, local anomaly. It will not speak to you. It has already answered your presence with a million drips, a thousand silent shifts of growth, a single, unending thought that is the land itself. You are now a part of its dream. The only question is whether it will remember you when the green swallows your footprint.
