The inhabitants of this place are not born; they are installed. They do not eat or drink; they are fueled and oiled. They are the Symbiotes of the Slag, a population of automatons and cybernetic organisms whose existence is a constant, grudging negotiation with the very architecture that sustains and threatens them. They are the immune system, the gardeners, and the parasites of this living engine.

They come in forms as varied as the machinery itself. Some are the Wardens, towering, multi-limbed chassis of bolted plating and thick hydraulic muscle. They move on heavy, clawed feet that grip the slick walkways, their single, large optical lens swiveling with a slow, deliberate sweep to monitor pressure gauges and listen for the tell-tale groan of overstressed metal. Their purpose is stabilization. They tighten a colossal nut here, seal a weeping joint with a stream of welding fire there, their movements slow, powerful, and utterly silent save for the low hum of their internal reactors. They are the old growth of this forest, patient and enduring.

More common are the Tenders, smaller, more agile forms that scuttle along pipes and conduits like metal insects. They are a clattering chorus of clicking legs and whirring manipulator arms. They clean filters clogged with rust-flakes and oily sediment, clear debris from channels, and replace glowing, vacuum-tube "fruit" in the delicate clusters of control relays. They communicate not with sound, but with bursts of binary light, blinking codes to one another from across the chasms of steam. To them, a corroded bolt is a weed to be removed; a leaking coolant line is a sick branch to be pruned and grafted.

And then there are the Drifters, the scavengers. Smaller still, built from salvaged parts, they haunt the deepest shadows, the stagnant pools, the forgotten sub-levels where the machinery has failed and rusted into abstract sculptures. They feed on scrap, drawing power from decayed batteries and the faint thermoelectric gradients of cooling slag. Some have evolved strange appendages—a delicate arm tipped with a magnet to collect ferrous dust, a cluster of sensitive feelers to taste the metallic ions in the water. They are the decomposers, the fungi of this ecosystem, breaking down the dead so that its materials might be reborn in the forges high above.

There is no leader, no central command. Their intelligence is emergent, a hive-mind born of necessity. When a critical pipe bursts, the Wardens converge to contain the damage while a swarm of Tenders reroutes flow through auxiliary systems. When a section of walkway finally succumbs to rust and collapses, the Drifters below pick it clean within hours, and the Tenders are already rerouting cables. They are a single, sprawling organism of metal and code, reacting to the stresses of their colossal, dying body with unthinking, perfect efficiency.

Their conflict is with entropy, personified by the water and the rust. They are forever outmatched. For every valve they replace, two more weep. For every surface they scour clean, the fractal blooms of corrosion return, patient and beautiful in their slow conquest. The automatons do not feel despair; it is not in their programming. They feel only the imperative: *maintain*. To tighten. To seal. To replace. To keep the great, shuddering heart of the Verdant Slag beating for one more cycle, even as the cold, green water rises, inch by inexorable inch, through the grating beneath their tireless feet.
