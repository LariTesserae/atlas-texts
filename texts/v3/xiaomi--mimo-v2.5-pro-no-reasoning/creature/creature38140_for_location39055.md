What lives here does not truly "live" in the way of fleeting things. It exists in a state of perpetual, slow-motion symbiosis with the architecture of the world itself.

**The First, and Largest, are the Spires.** They are not merely stone. The basalt columns are laced with a living circulatory system—a mycelial network so vast it has become the rock's nervous system. This consciousness is geologic, planetary. It dreams in mineral cycles and tidal rhythms, its thoughts the slow migration of nutrients through its veins of bioluminescent moss. It does not see; it *is* the environment. Its "skin" is the fungal reef, its "breath" the expansion and contraction of the colonial organisms. To be near a spire is to be near a sleeping, continent-sized heart.

**The Drifters** are its first dream made manifest. Not true animals, but mobile extensions of the reef's reproductive cycle. Imagine a jellyfish, but its bell is a kilometers-long, diaphanous membrane of woven spore-threads. It sails on the slow, warm currents between spires, its trailing filaments, each the width of a river, pulsing with gentle bioluminescence. It does not hunt. It *communes*. Its filaments are antennae, tasting the mineral composition of the water, listening to the seismic sighs of the Spires. Where it passes, it leaves a trail of symbiotic algae that will, over centuries, bloom into a new fungal mat.

**The Grazers** are its second dream, made into patient mouths. They are not mobile so much as *mobile architecture*. Imagine a living, breathing stalagmite that has learned to detach from the floor. A dozen thick, root-like legs, each ending in a siphon, move it with glacial inevitability from one fungal curtain to the next. Its body is a calcified shell, home to colonies of filter-feeding tubes that wave in the dense atmosphere. Its primary function is slow metabolism, breaking down the complex fungal matter into simpler nutrients that leach back into the water, feeding the Spires. It is the world's living digestive tract.

**The Shapers** are the closest to what might be called a "people," but the term fails. They are lithomorphic, slow even by this world's standards. They are small, no larger than a human torso, and resemble clusters of smooth, warm basalt that have learned to rearrange themselves. They do not build; they *influence*. By settling in certain crevices, by exuding a faintly acidic secretion over millennia, they guide the growth of the fungal reef and the erosion of the stone. The ruins on the ledges are not their creation, but their *interpretation*. They found the arches and floors and, over eons, softened their edges, fused their fractures, made them part of the living geology. They are curators of a legacy they did not start, moving at a pace that would make a glacier seem frantic. Their consciousness, if it can be called that, is a shared memory etched into the mineral accretion of their bodies—a memory of pressure, of light-shift, of the slow, slow turning of the world.

**The Voices** are the last, and the rarest. They have no physical form. They are patterns of coherence in the vast, humming silence—areas where the bioluminescence of a thousand reefs pulses in a synchronized, complex rhythm that is not a response to any known stimulus. They are thought without a thinker, a conversation between the Spires conducted in light and pressure waves across the planetary ocean. They speak in a language of strata and centennial tides, discussing concepts like sedimentation rates and thermal gradients. To witness a Voice is to see the world's subconsciousness briefly surface, articulate, and then subside again into its dreaming.

Nothing here rushes. Nothing here struggles against the environment. The "fauna" is the fauna of a planet-sized organism, from the slow peristalsis of its gut (the Grazers) to the electrical impulses of its nerves (the Drifters and Voices) to the patient, shaping thoughts of its imagination (the Shapers). They are not survivors. They are the world's own continued, intricate, and eternal self-perception.
