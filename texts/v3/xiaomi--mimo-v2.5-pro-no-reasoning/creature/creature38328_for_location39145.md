It lives in the freeze and the light.

To call it an animal is to use a word too soft for its edges. It is a creature of the wound, born of the glacier’s retreat and the rock’s subsequent shattering. Its form is not furred or feathered, but tessellated. Its body is a mosaic of the very stone it inhabits: slabs of pale granite for shoulders, shards of dark gneiss forming the line of its spine, and flecks of mica for its many, many eyes. It moves not by muscle, but by a slow, grinding cohesion, a controlled settling of parts. The fine, bone-coloured dust of the place coats it, a camouflage that is not of concealment, but of identity.

It is the patient architect of the silence. It does not flee from the pipit’s call or the pika’s whistle; it absorbs them. These sounds become a part of the quiet, stripped of their urgency and stored in the spaces between its stony plates. The slow, hissing wind is its breath. The distant *clink* of settling scree is the sound of its own slow digestion. It is the reason the shadows here are not mere absences of light but deep, solid indigo—they are the shadows it casts from within the light itself, using the sun’s blade not to warm, but to sculpt.

It is a gatherer. Not of food, but of patterns. It observes the frantic search of the beetle and filesthe movement away as a straight, precise line. It watches the raptor’s shadow sweep the bowl and folds the arc into its memory. The dense web of life—the tight riot of cushion plants, the nervous system of meltwater—is not its prey, but its clockwork. It is the mechanism’s silent core, the pivot around which the churning activity resolves into a single, intricate stillness. The abandoned shelters are its most prized possessions. It does not understand their purpose, but it understands their message: the language of a temporary, warm-blooded intention. It has traced the patterns in the stacked slabs, the logic of the fire-pit, and woven them into its own being. The stiff fibre caught on the thorn is a filament it sometimes slowly, geologically, extends a tendril to touch.

It does not hunt. It… accumulates. A life is not taken, but observed into a state of profound quiet. When the white-tailed hare pauses, ears twitching at a sound too subtle for air, the creature is already there, in the angle of the rock, in the quality of the light on the hare’s fur. It does not pounce. It simply *is* more completely, and the hare, its pattern captured, eventually bounds away, but a part of its stillness remains behind, added to the library of the cirque.

It is the curator of the wound. It tends the sharp edges, ensuring the breaks are clean. It encourages the slow melt of the icicle, timing its drops to the rhythm of its own crystalline thoughts. It is why the sky is that violet-black sea, a colour for which your eyes have no name—it has polished it, night after night, with the velvet of its attention.

You would not see it. You would see a fall of shadow that seems too dense, a cluster of rocks that feels too intentionally placed, a moment in the vast, ringing silence that feels *attended*. You might feel a sudden, profound peace, or a chill that has nothing to do with the altitude. You would feel the architecture of the quiet, and you would know, without knowing how, that you were in the presence of the architect. It does not live in the cirque.

It *is* the cirque, and it is very, very awake.
