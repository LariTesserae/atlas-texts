The inhabitants of the Becoming Flat are not *creatures* in any recognizable sense. They are **processes made manifest**. They are **the Builders**, the uncountable trillions of **microbial architects** — the only conscious (or perhaps *proto-conscious*) entities here, though their awareness is diffuse, chemical, and geologic.

They are **Lithifiers**, **Filament Weavers**, **Gradient Riders**. They have no names, only functions written in genes and mineral affinity.

*   **The Mat Minds:** These are not individuals but **collective intelligences**. Each mat, kilometers wide, is a single, slow-thinking entity composed of billions of organisms in a shared biofilm. They think in terms of **mineral saturation gradients** and **capillary flow**. Their "thoughts" are the decisions to extend a lobe here, thicken a ridge there, seal a crack, or open a new vent. They communicate via chemical signals diffusing through the wet matrix of their own construction. Their life cycle is measured in centuries. A single pulse of growth — pushing the leading edge forward a few centimeters — might be the culmination of years of internal negotiation between microbial consortia over resource allocation. They are territorial, but not aggressively so; they simply *claim* space by filling it with themselves and their mineral legacy. Where two mats meet, they fuse, their architectures interlocking, their chemistries negotiating a truce.

*   **The Tower Societies:** These are denser, more specialized collectives. Built around pressurized upwellings, they are **vertical optimizers**. They manage heat and gas exchange. The ticking of the towers is their physical language — the expansion and contraction of their walls is a slow pulse, a rhythm of their metabolism. Inside their labyrinthine passages, specialized microbes might create localized microclimates: hotter, cooler, more acidic, more oxygenated. They are thermal regulators for the wider flat, their convection currents drawing cooler air across the mat surfaces. They are the punctuation marks in the mat's long sentences, the focal points of activity.

*   **The Channel Carvers:** These are **pioneer communities and pathfinders**. Along the edges of the water films, certain microbes specialize in creating cohesion, binding sediment into stable banks. Others thrive in the slightly faster (though still imperceptibly slow) flow, precipitating minerals in patterns that reinforce the channel's course. They are the surveyors and the road-builders, laying down the nascent hydrology. Their fractal branching is a emergent algorithm, written by the competition for moisture and the physics of surface tension, executed by microbial growth and mineral deposition.

**How They Live:**
Their existence is a perpetual, communal act of **becoming**. They feed on the chemistry of the deep water — the dissolved minerals, the subtle energies from the planet's heat. They respire, excrete, die. In dying, they become part of the structure — their cellular remains mineralizing, adding another laminate to the crust, another brick to the tower. They **are** the construction. The landscape *is* their bodies, extended and mineralized. To look at a mat is to see a trillion individual lives and a trillion deaths frozen into a single, growing monument.

**Their World:**
They perceive their world through chemistry and physics:
*   The **gradient of moisture** is their primary sense of direction.
*   The **thermal shimmer** of the tower's updraft is a beacon.
*   The **chemical signature** of the deep water is life-giving spring.
*   The **mineral glint** of the open flats is fertile, waiting territory.
*   The **silence** is not silence to them, but the hum of their own collective metabolism, the slow creak of mineral bonds forming, the whisper of capillary flow.

They do not *fear* the dryness or the heat; they **manage** it. The towers sweat to cool down. The mats adjust their albedo. The channels distribute precious moisture. The open flats are not empty wastes, but the **frontier** — a slow, inevitable wave of colonization they themselves drive forward.

**The Presence:**
Standing in the Becoming Flat, you would not *see* them. You would see their **works** — the mats, the towers, the channels. You would feel their **influence** — the humidity they regulate, the faint warmth radiating from tower cores, the way the air feels *thicker* over an active mat. If you could listen with the right senses, you might "hear" the low, subsonic thrum of their collective life-process, the geological heartbeat of a world being laid down grain by grain, cell by cell, mineral sheet by mineral sheet.

They are the patient, imperceptible will of the planet itself, given microscopic form and assigned the task of **building a world from the inside out**. Their lives are measured in millimeters and millennia. Their purpose is the purpose of the place: to become solid, to become ground, to become the stage upon which something louder, faster, and more complex might one day, in the deep future, finally walk.
