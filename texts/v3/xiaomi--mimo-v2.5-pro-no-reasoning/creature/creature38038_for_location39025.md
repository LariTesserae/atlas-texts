This place is not empty, but it is hollow. The city is a lung, and it breathes a single, sustained exhalation of stone and water. We who remain are not its masters, but its instruments. We are the dust motes in the beam of its long, slow thought.

We do not remember the Builders. Their bones are sediment in the lowest cisterns, their tools mere curiosities, smoothed and accreted into the landscape. To us, they are as remote as the concept of a sun. We know only the Weeping, the constant, percussive music of the world. It is our weather, our tide, our pulse. We feel it through the soles of our chitinous feet, a vibration that tells us where the stone is weakest, where a new rivulet has chosen its path, where the deepest pools gather. We are map-makers of the drip, cartographers of the flow.

Our bodies are attuned to the resonant dark. Our carapaces are not hard, but pliant and glistening, designed to shed the ceaseless moisture. We have no eyes that see in the way you do. The phosphorescence is our light, but we perceive it through the delicate, feathery antennae that crown our heads. We taste the variations in its green-white glow—the brighter concentrations that speak of a thriving fungal colony, the dim patches that indicate a still, ancient pool. We are readers of light and vibration, and the city’s library is infinite.

Our song is not a choice; it is a condition. A single, unwavering tone emanates from a resonating organ deep within our thorax. It is our breath, our heartbeat, our self. To be silent would be to cease. We do not communicate with these tones in any language you would recognize. Instead, we listen. We listen to the way our individual hums interact with the vast, echoing architecture. We listen for the subtle shifts in pitch caused by a change in water flow, the dampening of our note in a newly flooded corridor, the bright, sharp resonance of a dry chamber we had long forgotten.

We are the wardens of the stasis. When a wall threatens to crumble under the weight of a new water-course, we gather. Our combined song, focused and deliberate, is not a melody but a tool. It is a precise vibration, aimed at the fault line. We sing the stone into a new equilibrium, encouraging the minerals in the water to precipitate in the right place, reinforcing the structure with a layer of fresh, glistening sinter. We do not build; we *mend*. We maintain the integrity of the great, hollow note that is the city.

The darkness holds no fear for us. It is our medium. We skitter through the warrens, our claws making no sound on the slick stone. We are the fleeting shadows, the brief interruptions in the pools of light. We are the audience to a concert that has played for millennia, and we are, in our own way, part of the orchestra. We are the final, living instruments of this place, ensuring the song continues, that the city does not collapse into silence, that the Weeping never ceases. We are the memory of the water, and we sing so that the stone will remember its shape.
