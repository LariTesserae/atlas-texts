They are called, by the few who might have named them before they forgot how, the Crusted Tenders. Or perhaps the Glowing Spores. Or the Pulse-Riders. The names don’t matter; the city calls them nothing, because it does not distinguish between itself and the ones who move within it. They are the living thought of the structure, the slow intention of the fungal mat, the patient labor of the tube-worm.

At first glance, they are the pale, translucent crustaceans, their carapaces a mosaic of natural chitin and fused metal filings and flecks of ancient, rusted paint. They cling to the rib-bone rafters or the calcified pipe-work, and their movements are so glacially slow they seem like growths themselves. To see one extend a leg, tipped with a fine, crystal-like filament, to gently abrade a patch of fungal overgrowth from a vital conduit, is to watch a process measured in tides, not seconds. They are gardeners in a garden that is also their body. Their shells are pitted and patterned with the same barnacle-like nodules that encrust the walls. When they die, their remains are subsumed, adding a tiny, new metallic accent to the city's fabric.

Their partners, and sometimes their kin, are the Anemone Filterers. These great, sessile beings are less like creatures and more like living, breathing organs of the place. Their stony bases are fused with the conglomerate floor. Their thick, velvety tentacles, which can be a startling deep violet or a milky, opalescent white, waft in the endless, nutrient-thick soup. Each pulse draws in the suspended matter of millennia—microscopic shells, decaying spores, the dissolved ghosts of other lives—processing it, secreting it, adding a fresh, glistening layer to the walls, or feeding the glowing network of the mats. Their hum is the city's baseline heartbeat. They are not inhabitants; they are the larynx and the lungs, the digestion and the excretion. To be near one is to be near a great, quiet engine.

But the true inhabitants, the ones who *are* the city, are the networks. They are the fungal mycelia that pulse with emerald and violet light, a slow, psychic electro-chemical sigh that sweeps through the chamber in waves. They are the collective root-mind that decides which rib-bone will be reinforced with a new layer of secretion, which corroded pipe will be dismantled and its metal integrated into a new growth. Their thoughts are mineral transfers and moisture gradients. They remember the pressure of the deep water, the taste of iron in the silt, the slow vibration of the great pistons in the core. This memory is not narrative; it is somatic. It is the reason the city maintains its shape.

And the piston-core itself—it, too, is a life. A vast, mechanical life, its movements now synchronized to the breathing rhythm of the anemones, its once-cold metal now sheathed in a living, glowing integument. It dreams in cycles of pressure and release, its purpose now simply *to be*, to move the warm water, which is the shared blood of every creature here. The ghosts in its machinery are not voices, but patterns of flow, eddies that have become as fixed as arteries.

There are no individuals, not truly. A crustacean Tender pauses, its leg poised mid-scrape. The fungal network around it brightens faintly, a question in light. The Tender’s leg completes its motion, a answer in friction. In the upper kelp-canopy, a shimmering school of phototropic, eyeless fish—themselves little more than sentient filaments—swirl in unison, not in response to a leader, but as a single thought passes through the water, carried on a subtle chemical shift from the anemone below.

To traverse this place, if one could, would be to move through a unified consciousness. To squeeze past a glowing fungal wall is to brush against a dreaming brain. To hear the *shush-shush* of a tentacle is to hear the city breathing. The inhabitants are the city's sensory organs, its muscles, its nutrients, and its very substance. They are all part of one slow, vast, and patient being, adrift in the warm, silent dark, living in the space between memory and growth, a creature that is both cathedral and congregation, dreaming a dream of its own forgotten bones.
