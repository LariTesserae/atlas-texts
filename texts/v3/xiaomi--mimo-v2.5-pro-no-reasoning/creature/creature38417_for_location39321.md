The inhabitants of this place do not announce themselves with movement or sound, for they are the place itself, woven into its slow, wet rhythm. They are not creatures of flesh and blood, but of moss, mycelium, water, and deep time.

Their forms are composite and diffuse. They might manifest as a deliberate clustering of those luminous fungi, arranged not by chance but in a pattern that hums with faint intent when the mist is thick. Their thoughts are the slow chemistry of decay, the patient release of nutrients from a fallen giant, the silent negotiation between root and stone. They move at the pace of moss, a gradual consciousness that spreads not in leaps, but in a felted, verdant creep. A "limb" might be a year’s growth of lichen across a north-facing rock. A "breath" is the rise of steam from the forest floor at dawn.

Their most tangible manifestation is the lizard. It is less a separate creature and more a quickened spark of the land's awareness, a sensory tendril given temporary, iridescent form. It feels the vibration of water dropping onto a leaf ten trees away through its gripping toes. It watches the slow refraction of light through the mist with ancient, intelligent eyes, its pulsating throat not a mechanism for sound, but for processing the dense, moist air—a way to taste the mineral and decay on the breeze that isn't quite a breeze.

Communication between them is not speech, but geology and botany. A message is a subtle change in the pH of a puddle over a decade, a directive is the way water chooses a new, infinitesimally slower path down a slope. The resonance of that deep *plonk*—when a drop hits a pool in the exact center of a stone basin, polished smooth over centuries—is a moment of shared satisfaction, a note in their silent, eternal symphony.

They have no tools, for the landscape is their tool and their archive. The circle of bright fungi marks a nexus, a point of quicker (though still geological) thought. The exposed tuberous roots, following a minor earth slip, are not a disturbance but a revelation, a page of their slow-moving history laid open. They are the architects of this arrested development, the curators of the slow erosion, the weavers of the mossy blanket. They do not fight the wet; they are the wetness given consciousness, a collective, dreaming mind that finds perfect completion in the drip, the growth, and the deep, listening silence between. They are the heartbeat of the place, measured in centuries, and they are utterly, contentedly home.
