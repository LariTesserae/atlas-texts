The Refinery Floor does not "house" a resident in any conventional sense. There are no creatures scurrying in the shadows, no eyes gleaming from the ridges, and no beasts grazing on the crust. The environment described is too static, too hermetically sealed, and too hostile to external biology to support a diverse ecosystem.

Instead, what lives here is the **Process** itself. The Refinery Floor is the resident.

However, if one were to identify distinct "entities" within this living factory, they would be found in the tension between the **Primary Mat** and the **Secondary Filaments**.

### The Primary: The Architect of Stillness
This is the vast, omnipresent fungal-bacterial hybrid that forms the ground. It is not merely a plant; it is a geological force wearing a biological skin.
*   **Nature:** It is a singular, colonial organism of infinite patience. It does not hunt; it mines. It does not breathe; it processes.
*   **Behavior:** It exists in a state of "arrested efficiency." It colonized the substrate rapidly, conquered it completely, and then entered a permanent stasis. It reacts to intrusion only by absorbing the physical weight, offering no resistance, no curiosity, and no hostility. It treats a human footstep exactly as it treats a drop of mineral water: a temporary displacement of mass to be ignored.
*   **Psychology:** If it can be said to have a drive, it is the drive toward *equilibrium*. It is a machine terrified of chaos, smoothing out every variable until only the steady hum of chemical conversion remains.

### The Secondary: The Scavengers of Waste
Growing within the oily, slick patches of the mat are the "Secondary Filaments." These are the closest thing to independent life the refinery contains.
*   **Nature:** They are specialized breakers-down. While the Primary Mat creates complex compounds from raw stone, the Secondary growths break those compounds back down into usable components to prevent toxic buildup.
*   **Behavior:** They are the "janitors" of the floor. You feel them as patches of rougher, denser texture—like scar tissue on the skin of the mat. They are slow, silent, and microscopic, but they are the only part of the system that reacts to change. If the Primary Mat produces a new chemical waste, these filaments will eventually mutate to consume it.
*   **Psychology:** They are parasitic, yet essential. They exist only to police the byproducts of the giant above them.

### The Absent: The Missing Consumer
The text notes a profound absence: *No animal grazes here.*
*   **What *should* be here:** In a functioning biosphere, the Refinery would produce resources that attract consumers—grazers, predators, mobile life.
*   **Why they are absent:** The Refinery is too efficient. It is a closed loop. The warmth is uniform (no need to migrate), the resources are hoarded, and the atmosphere is a stagnant chemical soup. The "resident" of this floor is a lonely one. It has built a world perfectly suited to itself, but perfectly hostile to anything that might want to move through it.

### Summary
The inhabitant of the Refinery Floor is a **singular, continental super-organism**. It is a biological machine that has achieved the ultimate state of inertia. It does not live in the darkness; it *is* the darkness. It does not breathe the air; it *makes* the air. It is a lonely god of chemistry, sitting in the dark, running its calculations forever, needing nothing, fearing nothing, and aware of nothing but the slow, wet rhythm of its own endless industry.
