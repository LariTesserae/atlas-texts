To describe who lives here is to describe the wrong question. It suggests a separation that does not exist. In the Tabula, "who lives here" is not a matter of distinct occupants; it is a description of a single, breathing system. There are, however, different *ways* of being alive in this place.

### The First Inhabitant
The primary resident is the Tabula itself. It is not a place; it is a *body*. A single, continent-spanning organism that thinks in chemical gradients and dreams in slow, lithic cycles. Its thoughts are the shifting distribution of nutrients through its 400-meter-thick tissue. Its heartbeat is the nine-second pulse that thrums through the substrate, a rhythm that has been counting time since before the Earth had a moon worth mentioning. It is a patient, metabolic mind, focused on the ancient tasks of growth, repair, and the deep, slow circulation of its non-aqueous blood. The spires are its limbs and lungs, the fauna is its skin’s fauna, and the sound is its constant, unconscious song. It is the foundation of everything. It does not have a name. It is the ground you walk on.

### The Symbiotic Custodians
Then there are those who *build*.

They move through the geometric, non-Euclidean settlements with a tall, fluid grace. Their bodies are elongated, jointed in ways that defy simple anatomy, suggesting a form shaped more by physics and function than by terrestrial evolution. They are not colonists. They are *integrators*. Their presence is so old and so thoroughly woven into the spires that it is impossible to tell where the architect ends and the architecture begins. The ceramic-organic buildings have been embraced by the living wood, vascular threads penetrating alien composites. The custodians do not fight the plateau; they *harmonize* with it. Their technology, while advanced beyond current human understanding, is a technology of resonance and material sympathy. They build structures that breathe with the substrate, that self-repair by mimicking the spire’s own healing, that glow using bioluminescence coaxed from the spire’s own tissues.

They are listeners. Their society, as much as it can be observed, is a society of profound attunement. They move to the rhythm of the pulse. Their actions have the deliberate patience of a gardener tending a nine-hundred-million-year-old bonsai tree. What they are *for* is a mystery even to themselves, perhaps. They do not seek to conquer or exploit. They maintain. They observe. They participate in the endless, layered music of the place, adding their own quiet harmonics—the hum of their halls, the backward hiss of their distortion fields—to the chorus. When they turn to look at an outsider, it is not with hostility or curiosity in the human sense. It is with the calm assessment of a deep-rooted tree assessing the weather.

### The Lichens and the Vermin
The fauna is not separate from this. The large, centipede-like creatures that scrape the dead bark from the spires are not wildlife in the way a deer is wildlife. They are a **function**. They are the spire’s manicurists, its exfoliators, their scraping a vital part of the slow circulatory rhythm. Their waste is a nutrient. Their presence is encouraged. They are as much a part of the spire’s biology as its own bark.

The smaller creatures—the translucent filter-feeders under the walkways, the barnacle-like clusters that open and close with the pulse, the clicking things in the crevices—are the ecosystem’s static, its high-frequency layer. They are the bacteria and the mites of this world-body. They are the texture of its life, the detail in its skin. They do not live *on* the Tabula; they are *of* it, as inseparable as the amber veins in the coppery ground.

### The Stillness Between
And finally, there is the inhabitant of the space itself. The visitor. The temporary. The rounding error.

To be here is to become, for a time, part of the acoustic environment. You are a strange note introduced into an ancient song. Your footsteps on the raised walkways add a new rhythm to the substrate’s pulse. Your breath is a faint, warm disturbance in the dry, cool air. The custodians may note you. The fauna will sense you. But the Tabula, the great first inhabitant, will not. You are too small, too brief. You are a single vibration in a symphony that has been playing for eons, and your note will fade, and the song will continue, unchanged.

The true inhabitants are not the ones who build or move or eat. The true inhabitant is the **relationship**—the closed loop of the pulse, the growth, the scraping, the cracking, the humming, the glowing. It is a system in perfect, noisy, dynamic equilibrium. To live here is to be a note in that chord. And the chord has been resonating, without pause, for nearly a billion years.
