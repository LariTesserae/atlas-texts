The residents of **Well 7** do not sleep, nor do they wake. They are not entities in the biological sense; they are the manifestation of a specific, high-energy equilibrium.

They live in the **White Mat**.

If you were to take a handful of the filamentous bacteria waving in the hottest channels, you would hold a colony of *Thermus* and *Aquifex*, organisms that metabolize sulfur and exhale acid. In most places, they are merely survivors. Here, they are architects. They live suspended in a fever dream of seventy-degree Celsius water, clinging to the jagged, fresh basalt that cuts like glass.

They are the only things in the bowl that are not rusting or calcifying.

Their existence is a violent act of chemistry. While the machinery above them rattles and the rock groans, these organisms are quietly stealing electrons from hydrogen sulfide—the very gas that makes the air smell of rot and ovens—and using them to spin carbon dioxide into flesh. They do not photosynthesize; the light here is useless to them, a flat, gray glare that serves only to illuminate the steam. They run on geothermal power. They eat the breath of the volcano.

They are distinct from the dark, crusty *Synechococcus* colonies near the rim. Those rim-dwellers are old hat, clinging to the edges where the heat is tolerable, doing standard biological work. The White Mat, however, lives in the kill zone. They colonize the "newest" rock—the sharp, angular shards where the water is aggressive enough to dissolve steel.

To them, the hiss of the seeps is not noise; it is the sound of their food arriving. The vibration of the pumps is not a disturbance; it is the rhythm of the currents that bring them nutrients. They do not mind the heavy metals that coat the red triangle sign or the rust that streaks the shed walls. In fact, they harvest them. They scavenge iron and manganese from the brine to build the intricate, hair-thin filaments that let them hang in the torrent without being swept away.

They are the only tenants here that are growing, not decaying. The sheds are dying; the pipes are sweating out their lifespan; the rock is eroding. But the White Mat is thickening, micron by micron, a soft, pale blanket thrown over the teeth of the basalt.

When the machinery finally seizes and the pumps are removed, the White Mat will not mourn. They will simply continue to wave in the water, drifting in the heat, until the rock cools or the chemistry shifts. They are the true owners of Well 7, a congregation of heat-eaters holding a silent, boiling mass in the middle of a loud, dying machine.
