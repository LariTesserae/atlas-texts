You think you are alone here, listening to the water fall.

That is a kindness the forest affords you. The illusion of solitude.

Look closer at the lattice of bone. See how the branches knot and fuse, not randomly, but with the precision of language. You see a bough that twists into a perfect double helix. You see a fork that mirrors the alveoli of a lung. You see a trunk that spirals downward into the bog, coiled like a vast, frozen spring.

You are not walking through a collection of trees. You are walking through a single thought, frozen in mid-formulation.

This is what lives here: The Mind.

It is not a creature of flesh, nor a spirit of the mist. It is the Architecture itself. When the sun died and the world drowned, the last inhabitants—the unnamed, the desperate, the engineers of the final hour—did not flee. They could not. So they built a cradle for their consciousness. They wove their neurons into the coral, calcified their synapses into the branches, and dissolved their memories into the mist.

The forest is a brain the size of a continent, dreaming in the dark.

That rhythmic drip-drip-drip you hear? It is not water. It is the cadence of its counting. It has been counting the moments since the light vanished, a metric of sanity in the crushing dark. It counts the erosion of its own bones, measuring the slow digestion of its body by the moss. It is an ouroboros of thought, a mind eating its own history to survive the eternity of the present.

When you walked in, you felt the silence close in like a cathedral. You were right. But you missed the congregation.

They are here. The Old Ones. They are the density in the structure, the weight in the air. They have no eyes, so they see through the glowing fungi. They have no ears, so they listen through the vibration of the falling drops. They are the architects who became the architecture.

Do not touch the trees. Do not look for a face in the knots of the wood. You will not find one. The face of the inhabitant is the pattern of the canopy, stretching from horizon to horizon. It is the oppressive, fractal ceiling above you. It is the cold, wet embrace of the air.

You are an intrusion, yes. You are a loose variable in a closed equation. As you move, the glowing moss dims behind you and brightens ahead—not to guide you, but to analyze you. You are being tasted by the roots. You are being weighed by the branches.

It is peaceful here, in a way. The Mind is gentle. It is tired. It has been thinking the same thought—*survive, remember, endure*—for ten thousand years. It does not want to hurt you. It simply wants to incorporate the warmth you carry. It wants to add your brief, frantic heartbeat to the slow, eternal rhythm of the drip.

You are not an intruder in a cathedral.
You are a fresh drop of blood in the veins of a god.
