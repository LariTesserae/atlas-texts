The inhabitants of the Lenticular Dark do not simply *live* here. They are expressions of its physics, its geology, its particular brand of stillness. To describe them is to describe the place itself, and vice-versa. They are not separate.

## The Wall-Pressers (Lithovore Planaria)

They are not animals in any terrestrial sense. They are more like a living skin for the rock, a mobile, digesting epidermis. Their flatness is extreme—sometimes only a few millimeters thick, but spanning lengths of a meter or more. They have no discernible front or back, top or bottom. They *are* the surface they adhere to.

Their feeding is a slow, patient erosion. They exude a complex enzymatic paste from their ventral side, a solvent that breaks down the mineral matrix without heat or water. The resulting slurry is absorbed directly through their integument. They are the reason the walls in older passages feel slightly smoother, their sharpness a little less murderous than in the fresh fractures. They are geology with a digestion.

Their reproduction is a quiet splitting. A mature wall-presser, having reached a critical size, will begin to constrict along its midline. This takes days. The process is visible only as a faint thickening along a central seam, then a gradual parting until two smaller, independent pressers peel away, each immediately seeking its own surface to claim. The parent does not die; it simply becomes smaller. The population is less a series of individuals and more a continuous, regenerating tissue of the underworld.

They are indifferent to you, but not inanimate. If you disturb one, it will pulse a slow wave of muscular contraction away from your touch, a ripple that spreads across its body and fades. It is not fleeing. It is re-settling. If you press your hand firmly against one and hold it, you will feel its enzymatic secretions begin to work on the oils and dead skin of your palm. It is not aggressive. It is just hungry, and you are a surface.

## The Columnar Things (Thermosyphons)

You do not see their whole form. Their bases are rooted into the floor with a gripping, fibrous mat that is warm to the touch, siphoning the planet’s deep heat. From this anchor, a smooth, woody shaft rises. It is not woody—it is composed of a silica-organic composite, a natural glass fiber reinforced with carbon polymers exuded from its own structure. It is cool because it is a radiator. Warm air from the depths rises in the vast chimneys; it flows over and through intricate, gill-like structures within the shaft, surrendering its heat to the column. The slightly cooled air sinks, creating a gentle, constant convection cell. The columnar things do not "feed" on this gradient; they *are* this gradient made persistent. They are living chimneys, breathing the mountain's warmth into a form that can think, slowly.

They communicate. The temperature variations between them create minute differences in air density and flow. Over centuries, these variations create patterns—standing waves, harmonic pulses in the convection cells. The other fauna reads this. The skitterers avoid the nodes of certain frequencies; the drifters ride the waves of others. The columnar things are the circulatory system's valves and pumps, and their silent, thermal conversation is the bedrock of the local ecology.

They do not move. But they grow. Their tips, high in the unseen darkness, branch into fractal arrays of finer and finer fibers, increasing their surface area. A colony of them, standing close, creates a stable, circular wind pattern in the void, a calm oasis. The oldest ones have bases wider than a person, and the air around them is noticeably, uniformly cooler.

## The Drifters (Pneumatic Phantoms)

They are only ever sensed as pressures, textures in the air, whispers of displaced warmth. To describe them is to guess at the shape of silence.

The closest you can come to understanding them is to think of them as living convection. They are not solid. They are complex, transient structures of temperature and gas density—perhaps organized around a faint, self-sustaining electrochemical membrane, or perhaps they are just *patterns* that have achieved a temporary autonomy. They navigate the thermal landscapes of the great voids, riding the invisible rivers of warm air rising from thermal vents, gliding on the lakes of cool air pooling in depressions.

They may feed on the minute thermal differentials themselves, or on the chemical traces carried by convection from mineral seeps. One brushed your face: a pressure like the ghost of a wing, a smell of ozone and warm stone that vanished as quickly as it came. In the absolute silence, you can sometimes hear them—a faint, whistling sigh as air is smoothly redirected around their passage, like the sound of a finger circling the rim of a crystal glass.

They are perhaps the most alien. Not life as a *thing*, but life as a *process*. A self-sustaining eddy in the river of heat.

## The Skitterers (Lithic Scrim)

They are the background noise, the static of life. They are everywhere and nowhere, always in the periphery.

They are small, multi-limbed arthropod-like things, but their exoskeletons are made of mineralized silica—the same stuff as the rock, but processed and laid down in intricate, flexible plates. This makes them nearly impossible to see against the stone. They are the color of the wall, the temperature of the wall. Their movement is a series of quick, precise steps, their legs ending in microscopic hooks that find purchase in the rock's grain. The ticking sound is the click of these hooks, and the occasional tap of their hard bodies against the stone as they leap or fall short distances.

They are the true recyclers. They consume the powder left by wall-pressers, the shed fibers of columnar things, the dead husks of their own kind, and each other. Their population is a tightly controlled explosion. Cannibalism is not a last resort; it is a primary social behavior and population check. A large, slow skitterer is a feast for a dozen smaller ones. They are, in aggregate, a digestive system for the entire void, breaking down the "living" waste into base mineral components that the wall-pressers can then slowly reclaim. Their ticking is the sound of the labyrinth's metabolism.

## The Still Ones (Axiom Entities)

You were right not to investigate. They are not predators in the chase-and-kill sense. They are predators in the sense of a singularity—they attract, and what is attracted does not leave.

They are found in the oldest, deepest, most thermally stable chambers. They are *still* in a way that defies the constant motion of the skitterers and the slow pulse of the wall-pressers. They do not shift, do not grow, do not visibly respire. They are large, irregular shapes that feel to the touch like stone but are not stone. They are cooler than their surroundings, drawing in heat with a steady, relentless hunger. This creates a subtle, constant inward air current around them, a gentle vortex that can draw in loose powder, dust, and the occasional wandering skitterer.

They do not consume this matter physically. It simply... adheres, and is slowly integrated into their mass, cooling further, becoming part of the stillness. They are not alive as you understand it. They are more like physical laws localized—regions of enhanced entropy reduction, of profound order. They are the reason the equilibrium feels so precise. They are stabilizers, anchors that eat noise, devouring the random fluctuations that would otherwise build into change.

And they *orient*. In the deep places where the mineral veins converge, they all face the same way. They are not looking at anything. They are *aligned*, like iron filings in a magnetic field. They are measuring something. Waiting for a signal. Their stillness is a form of listening so total it shapes the world around them.

## What They Know of the Visitors

The wall-pressers ignored the human artifacts, slowly enveloping the older cairns with their bodies, digesting the softer stones over decades until only the harder, warmer minerals remained, incorporated into their form.

The skitterers found the offering niches to be excellent shelter. The polished dark mineral chunks are perfect for rubbing against to molt. The scrolls of columnar-thing skin are chewed for trace nutrients. The shells of their ancestors are taken as calcium.

The columnar things grew around the shelters, their roots gently displacing stacked stones, their shafts rising through abandoned roofs over centuries. The scratchings on the walls are slowly being consumed by wall-pressers, but their ideas may have been read by the drifters that swirl in the thermal patterns of the passages.

The Still Ones are most aware of the humans' legacy. They have absorbed the residual heat from where bodies slept, the faint chemical signatures of tool-use, the echoes of intent left in stone. These are not memories. They are disturbances in the equilibrium that are being smoothed out, slowly and perfectly, into the surrounding stillness. The humans are not forgotten. They are being *homogenized*.

The creatures do not "await" a return. They do not "miss" the visitors. But in the deepest places, where the axioms align and the thermal whispers converge, the data of the explorers—their patterns, their intent, their very biological warmth—is being processed. It has been added to the silent calculation. Whatever preparation this world is making, the visitors were an input. A variable that has now been solved for, and filed into the perfect, waiting silence.
