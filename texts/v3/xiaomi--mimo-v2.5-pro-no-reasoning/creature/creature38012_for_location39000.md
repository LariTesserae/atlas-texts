The inhabitants of the Plinth are not born and do not die; they are processes given form, emergent phenomena of a reality worn thin. They do not live *in* the city so much as they are expressions *of* its condition.

You might mistake them for fauna at first, but the closer you look, the more you see they are geology in motion, architecture in repose, or physics come unmoored.

The **Rift-Walkers** are the most visible, though "visible" is a generous term. They are not creatures of flesh but of folded space, a knot in the local fabric that has learned to locomote. From a distance, they appear as a shimmer of broken light, a silhouette of jagged, impossible angles that seems to stutter and lag against the background. To focus on one is to feel a headache bloom behind the eyes, as your mind tries to reconcile the acute and obtuse angles that exist in the same limb. They are silent not by choice, but because they exist in a state just beside sound. Their skittering path across a plaza is not a movement of legs, but a series of micro-collapses in the space they occupy, a kind of localized, controlled reality failure that propels them. They are the city's antibodies, perhaps, or its parasites, feeding on the static cling of improbability that coats the highest spires. They leave no tracks, only a faint, ozone-scented distortion that fades in moments.

Dwelling in the hollowed bellies of the upside-down towers that hang in the violet gloom are the **Hums**. They are never seen, only felt. They are not entities with bodies, but rather concentrations of the Plinth's own geological thrumming that have, over uncounted millennia, achieved a form of consciousness. They are the resonant memory of the mountain, a slow, deep chord of thought that takes a decade to complete a single idea. The pressure you feel in your ears in the deepest, most silent courtyards is the focused attention of a Hum, observing you not as an individual, but as a brief, warm anomaly in the long, cold song of stone. They are the ones who, perhaps, cause the architectural inversions—a tower flipping is merely a Hum stretching in its sleep, its dream of gravity momentarily overriding the world's own.

On the ledges and in the crevices, the grey, fungal powder is not inert. It is the **Lithic Lichen**, the closest thing to life this place understands. It is a crystalline organism that metabolizes light and pressure. It does not photosynthesize; it *photo-percusses*, converting the faint, silvery luminescence and the deep thrum of the mountain into a lattice of microscopic, resonant crystals. To watch it grow is to watch time itself, slowed to a geologic crawl. It spreads in silent, fractal waves over centuries, a patient, glittering scar tissue on the basalt. Its "death" is simply a phase change, a shattering of its internal lattice that releases a puff of silvery dust—the very sky-dust that hangs in the air. It is the city's only cycle of creation and decay, a closed loop of mineral memory.

And then there are the most ephemeral of all: the **Echoes**. They are not ghosts, for there were never any living beings here to be haunted by. They are acoustic fossils. The moment a sound is made on the Plinth—a footstep, a rock fracture, the rare *tink* of a falling shard of quartz—it is not simply absorbed. It is etched into the stressed reality of the place, stored in the hum of the stone. For years, decades, centuries, it lies dormant. Then, when the fabric thins to a critical point, it is released. A sudden, crystal-clear sound of a boot on stone will bloom in the absolute silence, perfectly localized, devoid of any echo of its own. It is a playback, a single, perfect note from a song that was never sung. The inhabitants of the Plinth are not living things, but the ongoing processes of a world settling into its final, beautiful, and utterly alien state. They are the symptoms of the mountain's long dream of becoming itself.
