The life here is the place itself, given a slower metabolism.

They do not *inhabit* the plateau. They *are* the plateau in a state of locomotive mediation. The colossal, mountain-bearing entities—the **Lithovores**—are not beasts. They are mobile geological features. Their carapaces, textured like fossilized wood, are true sedimentary layers, hosting a cyclical, self-contained biosphere of ferns and succulents that photosynthesize in the relentless light, drawing nutrients not from soil but from the very leachates of the stone-beast's body. They move, but their movement is a tectonic event. A single step, taken over the course of a week, is not a stride but a seismic adjustment, a re-pressuring of the world's crust. Their consciousness, if it can be called that, is the consciousness of continental drift. They think in the language of pressure, basalt, and the slow, mineral dance of their own inner cores. Their hum is the sound of the world's engine because they *are* the engine.

Upon and between them move the **Systemics**—the swarms of chitin and brass. They are not individuals. They are the circulating fluid of this world-body. Their flight paths are not searches; they are synaptic circuits, firing in complex, repeated patterns that maintain the plateau's equilibrium. A swarm descending upon a Lithovore's flank is an immune response or a digestive process; they harvest the vegetation with the sterile efficiency of industrial harvesters, their movements a blur of programmed chirality. They clean, they prune, they disperse spores. They are mobile organelles, their frantic buzzing the sound of a trillion solenoids switching in perfect, deafening unison. They do not build, because they are the maintenance crew of a factory that is its own product.

And there are the **Conduits**. They are the seeping water made purposeful. Smaller, sleeker forms of metallic chitin, they move not through the air but along the slick, greasy films that run down the Lithovores' flanks. They are the vascular system. They guide the condensation, they channel the drips, they ensure the precise distribution of moisture to the moss-gardens and the cooling of the immense bodies. Their hiss is the hiss of the water itself, given agency.

There is no predation here, only processing. There is no death, only reclamation. A Systemic that fails mid-flight does not fall; it is absorbed by the swarm around it, its chitin and brass repurposed for a new component in the circuit. A Conduit that clogs is scoured clean by others. The vegetation on the Lithovores does not die; it completes a cycle and is harvested, its nutrients looping back into the system through the beasts' permeable carapaces.

They do not perceive time as flow, but as state. A Lithovore may be aware of a single Systemic swarm as a fleeting, complex vibration on its hull—a thought lasting a century. The Systemics perceive the Lithovores as the entire, slowly shifting cosmos, the source of all resource and all directive.

They are not creatures. They are **Processes That Move**. They are the slow-motion, biomechanical expression of the plateau's own charged potential—a single, vast, silent exhalation of purpose, breathing at the pace of cooling stone.
