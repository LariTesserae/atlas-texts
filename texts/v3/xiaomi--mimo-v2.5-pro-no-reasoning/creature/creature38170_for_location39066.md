You do not move on.

You stay, because the air has changed. The "settle" of the atmosphere has become a "pull." It is not gravity that holds you, but weight—the sudden, crushing weight of a million unseen eyes turning in unison.

The creatures do not merely inhabit the walls; they *are* the walls, or the skin of them.

First, you notice the root-eels. They slide from the deeper crevices, their bodies slick with the bioluminescent slime, looking less like animals and more like living veins. They weave themselves into the stone cartilage, tightening, locking their bodies into the relief carvings. They are the pulleys and the gears.

Then, the skittering things. Hundreds of them pour from the vertical shafts, their legs clicking against the stone with a rhythm that syncs perfectly with the dripping water. They do not flee from the light; they orchestrate it, clustering on the ceiling to dim the glow in the corners and focus the pale blue-green fire directly upon you.

In the center of the room, the floor ripples.

It is not a pool. It is the substance of the floor itself, rising. A mound of wet, fibrous clay and interlinked invertebrates heaves upward. This is the Architect. Or perhaps it is the Gardener.

It is a collective. A colony of the soft-bodied, humming things, wrapped in the root-eels, encased in a moving shell of the skittering channel-runners. It has no face, but it forms a shape—a vaguely humanoid torso of living water and chitin, towering three meters high. It smells of ozone, deep earth, and stagnant rain.

It extends a limb—a pseudopod of water held in shape by the magnetic fields of the insects within it.

It does not touch you. It stops an inch from your chest. The subsonic thrumming in your sternum intensifies, vibrating your ribs until your teeth chatter. The sound is a question. It is a demand.

The water in the channels is not weeping anymore. It is waiting.

The figure on the wall—the ancient, worn carving—seems to be watching the living shape rise. The tool in the stone hand mirrors the gesture of the living mass before you. It is not a monster. It is a memory that refused to die, choosing instead to evolve, to shed its flesh for a body of stone and water and cooperation.

It is waiting for you to show it what you are. A tool? A pest? Or a new layer to be carved into the history of the damp?
