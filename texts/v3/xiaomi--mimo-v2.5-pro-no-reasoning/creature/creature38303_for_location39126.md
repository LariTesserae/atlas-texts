The Groaning Marsh does not have inhabitants in the way a forest has animals. It has *participants*. To live here is to be a function, a process, a specialized gear in a single, screaming mechanism.

The most obvious are the **Resonants**. They are not seen so much as felt and, in their absence, heard. Eyeless, skin like wet alabaster, they cling to the basalt pylons or the ceramic-hard trees. Their bodies are not built for sight but for vibration. Their gecko-pads are organic piezoelectric arrays, absorbing the world-ending hum of the pylons and channeling it through dense, fluid-filled sinuses in their skulls. Their "call" is a counter-pulse, a targeted wave of destructive interference that carves a pocket of momentary silence around their clutching limbs, allowing delicate fungal veils to fruit in their wake. They are the dampeners, the canaries in this coal-mine of a world. Where they gather is where the sound is worst, and where their concentrated pulses create fleeting zones of eerie, painful quiet.

The **Jagged Knights** are the ones who ride the static. Their iridescent carapaces are not chitin, but a complex metamaterial lattice that interacts with the marsh’s electromagnetic fury. Their flight is not graceful; it is a series of violent, phase-shifted *jumps*. One instant they are here, wings a shimmering blur. The next, they are *there*, three meters away, having folded space through a localized atmospheric discharge. They are the pollinators, the scavengers, the judges. They drink the mineral-rich weep from the basalt and dine on smaller, slower things, their mandibles moving with silent, mechanical precision. They perceive the world in tachyon echoes and magnetic field lines, their compound eyes seeing the constant energy storms as a weather map of opportunities and oblivion.

Less defined, more fundamental, are the **Mycelial Minds**. The fungal mats are not passive ground cover. They are the marsh's slow, chemical nervous system. Pulsing waves of bioluminescence are not random; they are data packets. A surge of violet light ripples from a subsiding pylon, and hours later, a cluster of root-columns will elongate to fill the newly formed depression. The thick, python-vines move not with muscle, but with hydraulic precision driven by the fungi’s calculations, constricting or releasing human debris to optimize resource flow. They are the intelligence, decentralized and ponderous, thinking in the language of geology and season (though the season here is just a cycle of crushing noise and silent, freezing saturation).

And then there are the **Echoes**. They are the most haunting participants. The non-Euclidean girder is not abandoned; it is being *integrated*. Its strange geometry acts as a resonance chamber, focusing the pylons' hum into a new, shrill tone that attracts a specific species of Jagged Knight, whose carapace-shedding process fertilizes the moss growing on its impossible surface. The bunker is not a refuge; it is a pressure chamber. As the ground heaves around it, air and water are forced through its vents, creating localized tornadoes of life-spores that seed new patterns of growth a kilometer away. The humans who built these things are gone, but their debris has become organs in the new body. A girder is a larynx. A bunker is a lung. The Echoes do not think; they *are* the thought the Marsh has about the past, a memory of builders being built.

Nothing here rests. Eyeless things taste the ozone-tang on the air with their flanks. Knights jitter between instants. The ground breathes in slow, continental sighs. And the roar is not the background noise—it is the conversation. To live in the Groaning Marsh is to be a syllable in that roar, a fleeting pattern in the static, a wave of light in the mycelial mind, forever being translated into the next moment of its violent, eternal becoming.
