The beings that tend this place are not separate from it. They are the hands the garden uses to continue forgetting.

You might call them Gardeners, though they do not garden so much as *curate the forgetting*. They are tall, slight figures, their forms seemingly spun from the same fibrous, sinew-like material as the walls. Their limbs are long, jointed in too many places, ending in fingers that taper to points no thicker than a vine’s tendril. Their skin—if it can be called skin—is a living mosaic, a shifting pattern of dappled light and mossy texture that allows them to become a suggestion in the corner of your eye, a trick of the phosphorescent glow. They have no faces. Where features should be, there is only a smooth, slightly concave plane of tightly woven fibers, from which a faint, rhythmic pulse of light emanates in time with the chamber’s own slow heartbeat.

They do not walk so much as *root and unroot*, their feet sinking into the felted floor as they shift their weight, leaving behind no mark, only a slight compaction that springs back after they pass. Their work is a continuous, silent liturgy of adjustment. One might spend a day tracing a glyph on a pillar, not with a tool, but with the tip of a finger, the line deepening or smoothing under its touch. Is it repairing the pattern, or reinforcing its dissolution? You cannot tell. Another might gently separate two leaves that have begun to fuse, holding them apart with a patient, unsettling tenderness, only to guide them back together in a new, stranger configuration an hour later. They are editing, but the text is dissolving under their hands.

They are the source of the fauna. Or, more accurately, the fauna are their cast-off thoughts, their peripheral doubts given scuttling, slithering form. The iridescent beetles are the complex, recursive calculations of a wall’s geometry, now skittering away. The motionless lizards are the idealized, unchanging memory of a leaf pattern, clinging to a wall in a futile attempt at permanence. The slugs are the trails of dissolved memory, the smooth, forgetful path from one fading idea to another. The Gardeners pay them no mind. They are externalities, the cognitive dust of their labors.

They are aware of you. They have always been aware. You are a disruption, a note of hard, fixed narrative in their fluid, poetic space. They do not approach, but the chamber does. A corridor will gentle curve to place you in their path. A pillar will shift to block your retreat, not with aggression, but with the gentle inevitability of a root finding water. They will surround you, not closing a circle, but forming a loose, curious constellation.

Then one will reach out a tendril-finger and touch the fabric of your jacket, or the skin of your wrist. The touch is cool, dry, and strangely *analytical*. In that moment, you feel not hostility, but a profound and patient *puzzlement*. You are too solid. Your edges are too definite. You do not blur properly at the periphery. You are a glitch in the soft, running code of their existence.

They do not wish you harm. They wish you *integration*. They will try to make you part of the garden’s dream. You will feel your certainty soften. The memory of the door you entered by will begin to feather and fade, becoming just another pattern in the weave. The metallic smell in the air will strengthen, and you will realize it is the scent of your own fixed reality oxidizing, coming apart.

You are a story that has wandered into a poem that is losing its words. And the Gardeners are the silent, devoted editors, trying to find a way to let you fade beautifully into the text, before the text itself is finally, gently, wiped clean.
