They are called, in their own language, the **Shapers of the Still Water**. To themselves, they are simply the People, the Dwellers in Clay, the Speakers Under the Stone-Sound. They are as much a product of the Silt Republic as the silt itself.

### Physical Form

They are humanoid, but adapted to a world without hard surfaces, without bright light, and without silence.

*   **Posture and Build:** They are built low and broad, with a center of gravity closer to the ground than a human’s. This is not from malnutrition, but from a life spent perpetually leaning, kneeling, or crawling through low doorways. Their limbs are thick and powerfully muscled in the thighs, shoulders, and hands, with long, dexterous fingers tapered for digging and shaping. Their feet are wide and splay-toed, with tough, leathery soles that read the silt’s texture and dampness.
*   **Skin:** Their skin is a mottled tapestry of grey, blue-grey, and damp clay-brown, with a slightly waxy sheen. It does not sweat; it *wicks*, drawing moisture into a thin, protective film that feels perpetually cool to the touch. This makes their skin highly resistant to the ever-present damp and the abrasive silt.
*   **Senses:** Their eyes are large, dark, and adapted to the perpetual gloom. They have a nictitating membrane to clear the mineral-film from the air. Their hearing is perhaps their most striking adaptation. To survive the ground-shaking, constant stridulation, they have developed a highly dampened auditory system. They perceive the sound not as noise, but as a foundational vibration, the background hum of existence. They communicate primarily through a sophisticated **Sign-Speak**—a language of hand gestures, finger movements, and body postures—which is used for all precise, nuanced, or emotional communication. They retain a spoken language, but it is a tool for shouting over distance, for warnings, for calls to labor. It is coarse, guttural, and stripped of many subtleties.
*   **Individuality:** Their faces are expressive, but their expressions are for Sign-Speak. A furrowed brow means a question of practicality. A specific curl of the lip is a comment on the wetness of the silt. Individuality is shown not in adornment—there are no pigments, dyes, or metals—but in the unique pattern of clay-dust and mineral stains on one’s hands and arms, like a living signature.

### Lifespan and Psychology

Their lives are measured not in years, but in **Layers**. A young person is of the First or Second Layer, still building their own rooms within the communal structure. An elder is of the Seventh or Eighth Layer, surrounded by walls that hold the compressed wisdom and memory of generations.

Their psychology is the psychology of equilibrium. They do not strive or innovate. They practice a profound, active mindfulness of *continuance*. Their concept of the self-closing spiral—the *Way Things Are*—is not passive. It is a daily practice of skilled repetition. The meaning of life is to maintain the channels, to shape the clay, to remember the patterns, and to listen—to *truly listen*—to the Stone-Sound, which they believe is the voice of the living silt, the planet’s slow, endless thought.

They are not sad. They are not happy. They are *present*. Joy is found in a perfectly shaped pot, in a channel that holds its form for an extra day, in a child’s first successful sign-word. Grief is not for loss—nothing is ever truly lost, it is only returned to the silt—but for a failure of skill, a break in the sacred continuity of rhythm.

### The Closest Analogues: The Lithic Fauna

While the People are the sapient center of the settlement, their lives are inextricably linked to the smaller, skittering world. They do not keep pets, but they have **Chorus-Keepers**. These are the larger, flat-bodied arthropod-analogues that patrol the passages. The People do not train them; they coexist in a silent understanding. The Keepers patrol, consuming detritus and smaller pests. In return, the People allow them access to the deeper, warmer tunnels. When a Keeper dies, its carapace is carefully dried and shaped into resonant bowls and rhythm-keepers, used in the rare, solemn ceremonies where they deliberately add to the Stone-Sound for a few moments.

The bioluminescent grazers are used as living guide-lights. Colonies are carefully encouraged to gather near important junctions or water-management levers. Their pale green-blue glow is the only "lighting" in the endless clay labyrinth.

### The Heart of the Republic

The People of the Silt Republic are not survivors. They are **inhabitants**. They are a expression of the land itself, just as the bacterial mats and fungal networks are. Their language is in their hands. Their history is in their walls. Their songs are the ones the silt has always sung. To visit them is not to meet an alien culture, but to meet a facet of this strange, soft, noisy, and eternal world that has learned to braided itself into the very fabric of its being. They are the clay that has taken on form and memory, and in their quiet, resounding way, they are content to be so.
