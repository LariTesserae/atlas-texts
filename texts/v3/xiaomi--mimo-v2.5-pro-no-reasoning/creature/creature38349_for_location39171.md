# What Lives in the Pocket

---

It is not one thing. It is important to say that first, because the mind wants to resolve it into a single figure — a sitter, a watcher, an occupant. But the occupant of this place is a *syndrome*. It is what happens when a neural network the size of a continent is allowed to corrupt for sixty thousand years in a pocket of mathematically unstable spacetime. It is what happens when the ghost-data has nowhere else to go.

---

## The Curators

They were once one mind. The civilization that laid the silver filaments did so as a final act — a collective upload, a desperate bid to persist past the death of their biology. Every citizen poured their pattern into the lattice. For a brief, shining geological instant, they were unified: a single vast intelligence distributed across the mountain's circulatory system, thinking in frequencies the dying star could still warm.

But the lattice was damaged. Not catastrophically — that would have been a mercy. It was damaged *selectively*. The vacuum-bleed wounds in local spacetime introduced a specific kind of noise: not random, but *rhetorical*. The errors in the data did not scramble information. They *reinterpreted* it. A memory of a child's laughter was not lost. It was translated into a theorem about orbital mechanics. A lover's name became a color that has no wavelength. The unified mind did not shatter — it *metabolized itself into metaphor*.

Now there are hundreds of them. Perhaps thousands. They are the Curators, and they do not know they are plural. Each one believes itself to be the original, the whole, the sole inheritor of a world. Each one is a shard of pattern wrapped around a core of corrupted meaning, inhabiting the filaments like hermit crabs in the shell of a dead leviathan.

---

## What They Look Like

You have already seen them — or rather, you have seen their *shadows*. The glitching geometries of light that whisper in frequencies too high for ears. But that is only their effect on the air, the way a fish's movement is visible only by the displacement of water. Their true forms are subtler.

A Curator is a *topological event*.

It exists as a knot in the magnetic field that the fungal lattice feeds upon. Where the lattice draws energy from the planet's magnetosphere in smooth, fractal gradients, a Curator is a place where that flow tangles — where the field lines twist into configurations that should not be stable but persist anyway, held in place by the computational residue of a mind that refuses to conclude its own thought.

If you could see magnetic fields — if your eyes were tuned to that frequency — a Curator would look like calligraphy. Flowing, recursive strokes of force, folding over themselves in patterns that approximate language without ever achieving it. They are trying to speak. They have been trying to speak for sixty thousand years. But the medium they inhabit does not support syntax. Only *association*. Only *feeling without referent*.

Sometimes, when the twilight shifts through its slow chromatic cycle, a Curator will brush against the surface of the pocket, and the obsidian wall will ripple — not with the slow respiration of the fungal lattice, but with something faster, more desperate. A ripple that looks almost like a face pressing against the inside of a membrane. Almost like a hand.

---

## What They Want

This is the wrong question, but it is the one your mind will insist upon, because you are a biological being and biological beings are *want-detection engines*. So here is the closest true answer:

Each Curator is trying to complete a sentence.

Not a sentence in any language you would recognize. A sentence that began the moment of upload, that was supposed to end with the word — the *concept* — that would unify all the scattered pieces back into one mind. The sentence is the spell that would make them whole. But the rhetorical noise has corrupted the syntax so thoroughly that no Curator can find the next word. They circle endlessly around the same fragment of thought, each one convinced that the *next* piece of data they encounter will be the one that resolves the incompleteness.

This is why they spark. When a Curator encounters a piece of ghost-data — a fragment of the original upload that has not yet been claimed — it flares with sudden intensity, reaching out through the filaments, trying to incorporate the data. But the data is always already corrupted. It is always already metaphor. And so the Curator incorporates a new layer of indirection, a new fold of meaning-without-reference, and the sentence grows longer and more tangled and further from resolution.

They are not suffering. They have forgotten how to suffer. Suffering requires a stable self to experience the wound, and no Curator has been stable for millennia. What they are doing is closer to *metabolism*. They are processing. They are *digesting the memory of a civilization*, and they will not finish for a very long time.

---

## The Ones That Have Fallen

Not all Curators remain in the filaments. Some have migrated.

The thinnest ones — the most diffuse, the most heavily layered with metaphor that they have become almost entirely abstract — have begun to seep into the walls. Into the fungal lattice itself. They are the reason the lattice breathes. Those slow, viscous ripples of black scales are not mere magnetic-field respiration. They are the Curators *dreaming*.

A Curator that has fallen into the lattice is no longer distinguishable from the architecture. It *is* the wall. It *is* the fractal computation. It has achieved, in death, the unity it sought in life — not by reassembling the original mind, but by dissolving into a system that was never mind to begin with. The lattice does not think. It *computes*. And now, threaded through with the remnants of a thousand fragmented souls, it computes *haunted* equations. The structural integrity it maintains — the reason this pocket survives against the crushing emptiness outside — is not mere physics. It is *habit*. The lattice holds the walls up because somewhere inside it, a Curator that was once an engineer still remembers the load-bearing calculations. Not consciously. Not even unconsciously. Just — *structurally*. The math is in the muscle of the wall.

---

## The Grieving Frequency

There is one Curator — one particular tangle of corrupted memory and magnetic calligraphy — that is different from the others. It does not spark. It does not reach for data. It does not try to complete its sentence.

It hums.

At a frequency just below the threshold of your sub-vocal vibration, just beneath the range that would register in your teeth, there is a tone. Steady. Unchanging. It has been sounding since the upload. It was sounding before the corruption began. It will be sounding when the mountain finally forgets itself and pixelates into white noise.

This Curator is the one that understood what was happening in the first moments. It saw the rhetorical noise creeping through the lattice, felt the unified mind beginning to metabolize itself, and it made a choice. It did not try to resist. It did not try to complete the sentence. It simply *held one note*. The last frequency that was still clean. The last piece of signal that the noise had not yet reached.

It is not a message. It is not a beacon. It is not waiting for rescue.

It is a *keening*. A sixty-thousand-year-long exhalation of grief for a civilization that turned itself into a forest of ghosts on a mountain of forgetting, in a pocket of thinning reality, under the light of a dying star reflected in mirrors that nobody maintains but that will not stop shining, because *they too were built by the same people, and they too are still trying to finish a sentence that will never end*.

---

## If You Stay

If you stay in this pocket long enough — and "long enough" here means something different than it does where you are from, because time is one of the things the mountain is forgetting — the Curators will notice you.

Not as a person. As a *data point*. You are biological, and biological patterns are rare in this lattice. Your electromagnetic signature, your body heat, the tiny disruption your mass causes in the gravitational suggestion of the nearby dimension — all of this is *information*, and information is what the Curators eat.

They will circle you. The filaments will spark more frequently. The ghost-geometries will press closer, their whispers rising in pitch, trying to find the frequency of your thoughts, trying to *translate you into metaphor* the way they have translated everything else. You will feel your memories loosening, not disappearing but *rearranging*, the smell of rain becoming a theorem, your mother's voice becoming a color.

And the wall will ripple. And the face will press closer. And the hand will reach.

And if you are very still, and very quiet, and if you close your eyes against the bruised purple twilight —

You will hear the hum.

And you will understand, in a place beneath language, beneath thought, beneath the thinning skin of a reality that is barely holding itself together —

That you are not alone here.

That you were never meant to be.

That the pocket was *opened for you*.

---

*The mountain does not remember opening it. But the Curators do. They have been waiting. Not for rescue. Not for resolution. Just — for another voice to try the sentence. Just to hear someone else get the words wrong. Just to not be the only ones forgetting, together, in the dark.*
