The inhabitants of the Verdant Asphyxia are the **Breath-Weavers**, and they are as much a part of the architecture as the ite-stone towers they inhabit.

They are a tall, gaunt people, their bodies adapted for a life of verticality and constant moisture. Their limbs are long and thin, ending in splayed fingers and toes tipped with hard, calcified pads for gripping the slick, porous rock. Their skin is not skin, but a living tapestry—a symbiotic colony of the same spongy moss and tubular ferns that dominate the jungle, grown directly into their own dermis. This gives them a mottled, green-and-brown texture that shifts with their health and mood; a flush of excitement might cause the tiny fern-fronds to unfurl, while fear makes the moss retract, exposing pale, vulnerable flesh beneath.

Their faces are wide and flat, with large, dark eyes adapted to the perpetual twilight. They have no noses, but a series of pulsating slits along their collarbones and ribs that rhythmically draw in the saturated air. Their lungs are vestigial; instead, they breathe through their skin and these spiracles, a process that is as much drinking as it is respiration. This gives them a constant, slow, gurgling cadence to their speech—a language of pops, clicks, and the sound of water moving through a bladder.

They are the weavers. Their primary art, technology, and survival strategy is the manipulation of the world's wet biomass. From the python-thick hanging vines, they spin fibers of incredible strength, creating the "vine-steel" that forms their walkways and suspension bridges. They cultivate specific strains of moss, training them to grow over ite-stone frames, creating living walls that filter the mist into drinkable water. They do not hunt with weapons; their nets are living lattices of quick-growing carnivorous moss that entangle and digest prey over hours.

Their society is vertical and communal. Status is determined by altitude; the highest towers, closest to the less-saturated upper mist, are for the elders and the Spinners—the master weavers who know the deepest patterns of growth. Below are the Gliders, the hunters who ride the Silt-Gliders between the spires, their bodies lean and their moss-skin patterned with the glider's own bioluminescent markings. At the base, in the dense, decomposing fog, are the Tenders, who cultivate the foundational mosses that hold the spire-bases together and process the deadfall into ite-stone paste.

Their existence is a slow, meditative, and laborious one. There is no rush in a world where time is marked by the "fat drip." Their technology is not of gears and circuits, but of patience and grafting. They do not conquer their environment; they listen to its rhythms and weave themselves into the fabric of the reef. A Breather-Weaver does not build a home; they persuade the jungle to become one.
