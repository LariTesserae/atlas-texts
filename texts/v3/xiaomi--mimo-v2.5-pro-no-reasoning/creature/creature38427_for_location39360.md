To describe who or what lives in the Silicates is to describe a world where the air itself is a physical weight—a warm, wet blanket pressing down from the thin, burning sky and rising from the dark, basalt bones of the earth. Life here does not strive; it endures.

**The Foundations: The Thermovores**
At the lowest tier of existence, clinging to the riverbeds and the scalding basins, are the **Thermovores**. To call them animals is a stretch of the imagination. They are living geometry: flat, translucent discs the size of a coin, pulsing with a rhythm synchronized to the heat of the water.

They are not social, yet they gather in mats so dense they seem to be a single, shimmering crust upon the water. They are the silence made flesh. They have no organs for sound, no eyes to see, and no minds to contemplate their existence. They are heat engines, biological batteries absorbing the geothermal fury of the world to fuel a life of stillness. When the water cools, they freeze; when the heat returns, they split. They are the baseline of this world: persistent, mindless, and absolute.

**The Grazers: The Lithovores**
Moving through the twilight zones between the lethal heat of the vents and the cooler high terraces are the **Lithovores**. They are the architects of the landscape’s texture.

Imagine a tortoise that has evolved into a walking geological formation. They are armored not in chitin, but in the very minerals they consume—layers of quartz, calcite, and metal oxides accreted over centuries. An elder Lithovore is less a creature and more a mobile hillock, a walking jewelry box of dark, studded stone. They move with a ponderous, hydraulic slowness, scraping the colorful bacterial crusts from the rock with rough, file-like tongues.

They are the chemists of the Silicates. They do not speak, but they leave complex chemical graffiti on every surface they touch. To walk the plateau is to walk through their history—a conversation written in pheromones and mineral traces, mapping the slow, grinding cycles of their lives.

**The Shadows: The Ambush Predators**
Hunting the margins, invisible against the dark basalt until the moment of attack, are the **Ambush Predators**. These are the nightmares of the hot world.

They are elongated and sinuous, possessing a disturbing, fluid grace with their six limbs folded tight against their matte-black bodies. They are living stealth technology; their skin absorbs heat and light, rendering them invisible to the naked eye. They hunt not by sight or sound, but by heat. Large, pit-like organs in their skulls scan the world in infrared, seeing the warm blood of a Lithovore or the cooler mass of a wandering creature against the thermal noise of the ground.

Their strikes are the only moments of violence in the plateau's quiet existence. It is a silent violence—a blur of unfolding limbs, a sudden compression of mass, and then a return to stillness. The only time they reveal their beauty is in the deep dark of the night cycle, when their bioluminescent displays ignite. Patterns of amber and pale blue ripple across their black skin like electric veins, a silent language of mating and territory that turns the dark into a display of shifting art.

**The Hybrids: The Industrial Fauna**
In the cracks of the settlements and the runoff of the refineries, a new tier of life has emerged. These are the **Hybrids**, creatures that exist in the liminal space between biology and industry.

Here dwell the **Glass-Shelled Crabs**, crustaceans that have adapted to live inside the cooling pipes of the human settlements. Their shells have become fused with the silica dust of the air, turning them into living prisms that vibrate with the hum of the machinery they inhabit. They feast on the mineral slicks of industrial waste, their bodies hardened to withstand temperatures that would boil soft tissue.

Alongside them are the **Drainage Predators**, pale, quick-moving hunters that haunt the aqueducts and waste channels. They are the rats of this hot world, skittish and fast, hunting the Thermovores that gather in the warm outflows. They are a testament to life’s adaptability, finding a way to hunt and breed in the hollowed-out veins of the human occupation.

**The Sentient Layer: The Human Settlements**
Finally, there are the **Inhabitants**, the humans who have carved their existence into the upper terraces. They are not conquerors here; they are tenants, living on the sufferance of the heat.

They are a people defined by suppression. Their architecture is rounded, wind-worn, and heavy, designed to hold the heat at bay or channel it for use. Their language is a thing of the lungs and hands—a complex sign language supported by breathy, vowel-heavy whispers that carry no further than arm's length. They do not shout. They do not bang.

They move through their dark, stone halls with a grace learned from the Lithovores. They have learned that the loudest noise is the one that breaks the equilibrium. They are miners, refiners, and glass-makers, turning the planet’s mineral bounty into light and structure, yet they do it with the quiet efficiency of the creatures outside their walls. They look out through tinted crystal windows at a world of white steam and dark stone, watching the bioluminescent dances of the predators at night, knowing that they are merely one more quiet animal in a world that has no room for noise.
