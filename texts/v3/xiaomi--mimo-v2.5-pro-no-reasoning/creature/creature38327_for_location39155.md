This is not a place that harbors life; it is a place made *of* life. To describe who lives here is to describe the entire ecosystem, for every resident is a gear in a clock that has already struck its final, eternal hour. There is no singular "who," only a seamless, breathing "what."

**The Substrate: The Living Rock**
First, there is the basalt itself, or rather, what has become of it. It is no longer merely stone. It is a porous, three-dimensional habitat honeycombed with micro-fauna. The rock does not erode; it is inhabited.
*   **The Lithovores:** Tiny, silicate-shelled mites and translucent springtails live within the vesicles (gas bubbles) of the rock. They do not eat the rock, but scrape the bio-film of bacteria and algae that coats the interior of every pore. They are the foundation, turning mineral surface into biological fuel.
*   **The Weepers:** In the dark crevices, pale, blind salamanders with flattened heads cling to the ceiling, waiting for the "dripping clock" to deliver moisture and microscopic prey directly to their mouths. They do not hunt; they intercept.

**The Floor: The Saturated Kingdoms**
The moss is not just a plant; it is a civilization. It is six inches deep, spongy, and teeming.
*   **The Moss-Backs:** Thousands of species of mites, beetles, and larvae inhabit the "forest floor" of the moss mat. They live in a world of humidity near 100%, navigating a jungle of rhizoids.
*   **The Fungal Shelves:** The pale, bone-colored fungus under the overhangs is not passive. It is a slow-moving predator of the moss, dissolving the lower layers over centuries, recycling them. It hosts a specific ecosystem of moths and flies that lay their eggs nowhere else.

**The Water: The Silent Gliders**
The standing pools are not empty water; they are aquaria without glass.
*   **The Basalt Eels:** These are the apex predators of the water. Long, muscular, and dark, they are not true eels but a highly evolved amphibian or fish. They possess no eyes; they hunt by vibration and the minute pressure waves created by struggling insects. They are territorial but lethargic, conserving energy in the warm water.
*   **The Striders:** On the surface tension of the pools, water striders the size of human hands skate silently. They are the scavengers of the surface, cleaning up the millions of midges that fall and drown daily.

**The Verticality: The Canopy Dwellers**
The fern-palms (the frond-plants) are the trees of this world, reaching toward the diffuse grey light.
*   **The Web-Weavers:** The spiders do not spin webs to catch food randomly. They spin architecture. Their webs are tuned to the frequency of the midge wings. They are the engineers of the air, filtering the atmosphere for biomass.
*   **The Whistlers:** The birds are small, dark, and highly specialized. They do not migrate—there is nowhere to go. They are hyper-territorial. Their two-note whistle is a declaration of ownership over a specific frond-cluster. They are in a constant, low-energy war with the spiders for the midges. It is a war of attrition that will never end because both sides are perfectly matched.
*   **The Gliders:** Large, manta-ray-like creatures that are neither bird nor bat. They drift on the thermal currents generated by the warm rock, filtering the heaviest concentrations of insects from the air. They are silent, appearing as moving shadows against the low grey sky.

**The Unseen: The Rhythmic Pulse**
The most defining inhabitants are the ones you hear but never fully see.
*   **The Chorus:** The frogs are the metronome. Their croaking is not a mating call—it is an acknowledgment of existence. It is a sonic sonar that maps the density of the basin. When one croaks, it is saying, "I am here, the density is high, the moisture is sufficient." The others respond, "Acknowledged." It is a conversation of pure data.

**The Philosophy of the Residents**
There are no "newcomers" here. There are no pioneers. Every creature is an incumbent.
*   **The Adaptation:** Every organism is adapted to the Heat (38-40°C) and the Humidity. Desiccation is a myth to them; they cannot comprehend dryness. Their skin is permeable; their eggs require standing water to hatch.
*   **The Cycle:** The midges feed the spiders. The spiders feed the birds. The birds die and feed the moss. The moss feeds the mites. The mites feed the frogs. The cycle is tight, closed, and efficient. There is no waste. There is no excess.

**The Visitor**
You are the anomaly. To the inhabitants, you are a geological event—a moving mountain of heat and carbon dioxide.
*   The midges view you as a resource to be tapped.
*   The frogs view you as a vibration to be avoided.
*   The spiders view you as a threat to their architecture.

You are the only thing here that moves with purpose. They move with necessity. You are the only thing that changes. They are the things that remain.

When you leave, the moss will cover your footprints within a week. The water will fill the depressions you leave. The system will close over your presence like water closing over a stone. You will not have been here. You were just a temporary disruption in the equilibrium, a ripple that has already smoothed out.
