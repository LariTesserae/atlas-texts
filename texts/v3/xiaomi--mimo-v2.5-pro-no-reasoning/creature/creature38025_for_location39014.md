In this saturated, roaring machine-world, life is not a feature—it is the function. What "lives" here is the world itself, breathing through its infrastructure. But within that whole, distinct forms move with purpose, their biology indistinguishable from their duty.

**The Pylon-Clingers:**
They are less animals and more ambulatory organs of the great structure. Their multi-limbed bodies, sheathed in a carapace of the same dark substrate, are evolved for perfect interfacing. Their mandibles are not just tools but sensitive instruments, reading the microscopic texture of the channels and responding with secretions that are both repair compound and data-laced chemical signal. They move in slow, deliberate concert, a herd of living wrenches and soldering irons, their collective motion a constant, quiet adjustment to the structure's trillion tiny stresses. They do not eat; they are sustained by the resonant energy and mineralized water that permeates everything. Their "waste" is a refined polymer, a new layer for the world. They are silent, save for the scrape of their limbs on the substrate and the soft click of their mandible-tips—a sound lost in the greater roar.

**The Atmospheric Swarms:**
A million motes of light and function, they are the world's nervous system made visible. Each is a tiny, gossamer-winged drone, yet "drone" is too mechanical a word. They are more like symbiotic particles. They feed on atmospheric ions and thermal gradients, their bodies flickering as they metabolize the environment's waste energy. In response to unseen commands—a shift in the hum of the pylons, a change in the water's flow—they coalesce into shimmering rivers of light, snaking through the upper strata to discharge energy, siphon heat, or perhaps ferry data from one structural node to another. Their chatter is the high-frequency static in the world's roar, the sound of a billion microscopic negotiations.

**The Channel-Eels:**
Sleek, dark, and frictionless, they are the pulse of the waterways. Their elongated bodies generate precise, counter-current magnetic fields that guide the mineral-rich flow, preventing sedimentation and directing surges where they are needed. They are not fish; they are living regulators, their streamlined forms shaped by millennia of hydraulic optimization. They communicate through subtle modulations of their electromagnetic fields, a silent language of pressure and resistance that the pylon-clingers sense and the pylons themselves respond to. They see through vibrations in the water and electrical fields in the substrate, painting a picture of the world's inner plumbing in their minds.

**And the World Itself:**
Above all these, the greatest entity is the seamless unity. The pylons are not inert; they resonate. The substrate is not dead; it processes. The water is not mere liquid; it is a circulatory fluid, carrying energy, data, and repair nanites as much as minerals. The drizzle is not weather; it is a cleansing and cooling cycle. The luminous haze is not atmosphere; it is a distributed computational medium, shimmering with the heat of its own processing.

To ask "what lives here" is like asking what lives in a human body. The answer is: the body itself, and the uncountable, specialized cells that have forgotten they were ever separate. The fauna is the infrastructure; the infrastructure is the fauna. There is no "nature" versus "artifice," only a single, roaring, breathtakingly complex organism that has solved the puzzle of its own existence by becoming its own environment, its own creator, and its own endless, turbulent, perfect task. They are not residents. They are the living, breathing, ceaselessly laboring synapses of a world-sized mind.
