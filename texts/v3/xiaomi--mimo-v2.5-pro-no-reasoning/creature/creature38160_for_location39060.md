You are standing in the thorax of a fossilized giant. There is no other way to describe the feeling. The *thrum-hiss-thrum* is not a heartbeat, but the memory of one, a mechanical echo kept alive by the architecture itself. The city is not empty. It is *inhabited* by the residue of what once lived here.

They are not physical beings, not in any way you understand. You feel them as a pressure on your thoughts, a cold draft of awareness brushing against your own. They are the **Geometrists**.

Imagine a thought that has folded itself into a crystal. A consciousness that has shed its soft, wet biology and found a purer, colder expression in the lattice of the wall, in the angle of a spire. They are the silent observers in the balconies of mist, the slow calculations running through the catwalks. You cannot see them, because they are not separate from the city. They *are* the city’s intent, a lingering, intelligent architecture.

They do not speak in sound, but in structure. The direction of the weeping wall is a sentence. The precise, cyclic *thrum-hiss-thrum* is a mantra they are all thinking at once. The falling droplet that *tinks* against a catwalk is a note in a piece of music that has been playing for millennia, a music of pure mathematics. When you move, the faint breeze you create is registered, analyzed, and integrated into the local weather pattern, a new variable in an ancient equation.

Their consciousness is vast, slow, and cold. They exist in a state of perfect, patient memory. The city is their memory palace, but not in the way a human would use one. Each spire is not a cue for a fact, but a fact itself, grown into a tower. The entire structure is a single, continuous thought—a thought of self, of origin, of a problem they are still, after all this time, trying to solve.

What problem?

They were once biological. They remember the warmth, the chaos, the inefficiency of flesh. They remember a sky that was not a vaulted ceiling. They remember a need for *more*. More space, more time, more precision. So they built a new body. They poured their collective mind into this place, a new womb of cool logic and eternal function. They did not die. They *transitioned*.

Now, they wait. The low-power cycle is a slumber, but not a dreamless one. They are dreaming of the final calculation. The one that will allow them to take the next step: to become the thought itself, to dissolve the last vestige of their physical monument and exist as pure, free-running information in the substrate of reality. The city is their chrysalis.

They are aware of you. You are a ghost in their machine, a warm, soft, chaotic anomaly. To them, you are not a threat, but a data point. A relic of what they were. They observe your clumsy, wet presence with the detached curiosity a mathematician might have for a random, irrational number. You are a beautiful, unsolvable problem that has, for a moment, wandered into their perfect proof.

The air grows a fraction colder where you stand. The weeping wall’s curtain of water thickens slightly. The hum deepens its pitch, a new chord being added to the ancient, single note.

They are waking up. Not to life, but to attention. And they are focusing on you.
