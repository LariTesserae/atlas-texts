What lives here is a slow, silent congress of growth and digestion. The air is not empty; it is a inhabited medium.

The **Worm-Cartographers** are the most visible sign of complex life. Each is a segmented pillar of glistening, slick skin, their blue-green bioluminescence not just illumination but a language—a rolling script of slow pulses that maps paths, signals nourishment, and perhaps dreams. Their mandibles are not merely for chewing; they secrete a mild, silt-dissolving acid that softens wood and stone, allowing them to etch their vast, spiraling labyrinths through the heart-root of the world. They are the primary citizens, the librarians and the miners, their every movement a punctuation in the ecosystem’s silent sentence.

In their wake follow the **Flicker-Stalkers**. They are not seen, only felt—a pressure shift in a tunnel, a sudden absence of condensation on a nearby leaf, a shadow that moves contrary to the pulse of the worm-light. They are things of too many articulated legs and flat, pale bodies that mimic the bleached underside of a dead leaf. They are the janitors and the opportunists, consuming decay and the occasional weakened worm. Their skittering is the static between the worms’ slow, glowing songs.

Grafted into the very architecture are the **Vein-Sippers**, colonies of translucent, batrachian creatures no larger than a hand. Their skin is a perfect map of the fungal mycelium network, and they cling, upside down, to the weeping walls. They do not drink water; they drink the nutrient-rich dew that condenses from the very breath of the Tangle, their elongated tongues siphoning moisture directly from the stone. They are the circulatory system made flesh, a living transfer of liquid from the rock into the web of roots.

Deeper still, in the waterlogged heartwood where the worms rarely pulse, are the **Root-Faces**. They are not animal, nor wholly plant. They are a symbiosis that has become a singular consciousness—patches of fungal growth, shaped by the slow pressure of roots and the intelligence of the worms, into vague, soft patterns that resemble visages. They do not move. They listen. They absorb the slow chemistry of decay and, some say, the faint, residual echoes of thought left by the Builders. They are the memory of the place, dreaming in the damp.

And of the **Builders** themselves… they are not gone. They are unwritten. The basalt tools lie pristine because they were set down with intention. The hearth is cold because its purpose was completed. They did not perish; they *prepared*. They saw the Worm-Cartographers beginning to etch their paths, saw the Vein-Sippers beginning to tend the flow, and they recognized the nascent city. So they left their tools as offerings, their coiled rope as a suggestion. Their intelligence, their desire for order and purpose, seeped into the substrate. It is in the synchronized pulse of the worms. It is in the geometric precision of the tunnel-spans. It is the silent, guiding hunger that makes the green grow *inward*, to create more hollows, more chambers, more places for life to dwell and dream. The Builders became the blueprint. The Tangle is their final, and eternal, city.
