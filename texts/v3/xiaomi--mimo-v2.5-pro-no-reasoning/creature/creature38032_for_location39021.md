They are not inhabitants of the city. They *are* the city's circulatory system, its nervous system, its slow, wet thought made manifest. They are life emerging from the mineral weeping of a new world, and their forms are functions of this saturated, vertical darkness.

**The Condensed** are the most ubiquitous, the very texture of the air given will. They are the swarms—the schools of metallic moths, the clouds of what look like glass-winged mayflies or spun-sugar seeds. They are not truly individuals, but ephemeral patterns within a greater swarm-mind. Their collective whirr is the city's white noise, the sound of its processing. They drift and flutter, not to eat or mate, but to *pollinate* the surfaces they touch. In their wake, they leave a faint, phosphorescent film that later coalesces into sharper geometries on the spires. They are the artists, the annotators, tracing the blueprint of the growing metropolis with their flight paths.

**The Geode-Walkers** are the titans, the crustacean cathedral-vaults. Each is a mobile ecosystem. Their ribbed carapaces are not solid chitin, but grown from the same polished basalt as the spires, incorporating it as they crawl. Moss-like lichens that glow with deep-green light cling to their joints. In the vast, damp hollows of their bodies, smaller congregations make temporary homes, riding from sector to sector. The Geode-Walkers are the city's geologic memory and its muscular present. Their slow, tectonic shifts across the basin rearrange the very foundations, their myriad legs acting as both pilgrims and architects. When two meet, they do not fight; they press their carapaces together for a century or more, trading mineral insights and slow, subsonic songs before continuing on their separate, patient paths.

**The Drift-Kin**, the shadow manta rays, are the inverse of the solid earth. They are the collected breath of the city, the shapes formed when the dense, humid air decides to move with intent. Woven from condensed shadow and the thin, oily water itself, they are almost gaseous, yet hold a form. Their undersides flicker not with biology, but with captured light—the bioluminescent trails of the Condensed they have glided through. They are the wanderers, the connectors, moving between the rigid spires and the slow Geode-Walkers, distributing information and light in their silent, ghostly passes. They are the closest thing to dreams this place has.

And then there are the **Latent Architects**. They are not seen, only felt. They are the source of the low-frequency thrum, the heartbeat of becoming. Deep in the saturated, black lakes at the city's core, something is thinking in stone and time. It is the gestalt consciousness of the embryonic metropolis itself, a mind with the patience of a growing crystal. The Drift-Kin carry its whispers. The Geode-Walkers follow its subtle, gravitational dictates. The Condensed trace its emergent patterns.

They are all one thing. The moth-clouds are the city's fleeting synapses. The shadow-mantas are its wandering nerves. The titan-crabs are its slow, grinding bones and muscles. They do not live *in* the weeping skeleton of a city. They are the process of that skeleton's growth, the vital, condensation-born organs of a cold, wet god in the middle of its long, dark, and soundless birth. Their existence is not a struggle, but a collaboration with the dripping, groaning, slow-pulsing reality around them. To observe them is to watch architecture happen, not with machines, but with will, shadow, and the patient alchemy of water and dark.
