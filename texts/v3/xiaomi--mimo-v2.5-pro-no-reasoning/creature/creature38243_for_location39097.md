It lives in the seams of the chaos.

You do not see it at first, because it is not a thing of movement. In this world of constant, grinding scuttle, its stillness is a kind of camouflage. It is perched upon one of the large, smooth stones—a stone that, from this proximity, you see is not entirely natural. Its surface has been microscopically etched with a pattern that mimics the veins in the cavern wall, a deliberate and slow masterpiece of fungal acid or patient claw.

The creature is a lithic hermit, a gleaner of the in-between. Its body is a broad, low dome of interlocking plates, not unlike the backs of the larger crawling things, but its plates are a dull, true black, like cooled basalt. They do not grow; they are accumulated. Each plate is a former shell, a segment of carapace, a piece of armored life that it has scavenged and fused to its own body with a resinous secretion from its underside. It is a walking cairn, a museum of the cacophony that flows beneath it.

From the front of this dome, two sensory clusters emerge, not on stalks, but recessed into the leading edge of its armor. There are no eyes as you know them. Instead, arrays of feathery, pale filaments wave gently in the damp air, tasting the mineral content, the humidity, the specific vibrations of the chaos. Below them, a single, complex mandible rests, not for tearing, but for manipulation. It is delicate, articulate, and looks utterly foreign on this blunt, armored body.

It is a creature of profound patience and curation. While the world churns in a frenzy of consumption, this being *collects*. It waits for a particular type of brawl—a clash between a rolling consumer and a cluster of spindly defenders—and in the aftermath, when a uniquely shaped piece of chitin is left behind, it will finally move. Its locomotion is a slow, hydraulic heave, its multiple, thick limbs extending from beneath its dome with oiled precision. It retrieves the shard, examines it with its filaments, and, if it meets some unknown criterion, secretes a dab of dark resin and affixes it to its back.

It is the keeper of the stone bowls. You see it now, dipping a forelimb into the silvery water, not to drink, but to wet the resinous pads on its undercarriage, perhaps preparing for another addition. It may be a creature that builds its own armor from the debris of other lives, a walking record of conflict. Or it may be something older—a physical memory of the place, with each added piece a neuron in a slow, geological mind.

It is not intelligent in any way you would recognize. Its consciousness is a low hum of sensory input and slow, imperatives: *gather, adhere, conserve, wait*. It is as indifferent as the rest, but its indifference is of a quieter kind. It does not participate in the tide. It observes it, takes its toll of it, and slowly becomes more itself—a perfect, armored monument to the endless, churning life it will never join. The light hums on its black shell, casting no shadow, and it sits, a stone among stones, with the weight of a billion tiny histories upon its back.
