This is a city of thresholds, not destinations. Those who live here are defined by their passage through it, their interaction with its skin, its pulse. They are not masters of this place, but participants in its endless, chthonic process.

Here is who breathes the damp, metallic air:

**The Gleaners.**
They are the most visible, if one uses the term loosely. Perhaps four feet tall, their forms are gaunt and multipurpose, built for efficiency in tight spaces. Their skin has the texture and grey-brown pallor of cured leather, seamless and tough. They move in a low, skittering crouch, their long-fingered, three-jointed hands never still. They are the historians and the scavengers. They press close to the walls, their heads tilted, not with ears (they have none) but with clusters of fine, trembling cilia where ears might be, reading the vibrations in the stone. They carry small, sharp tools of chipped obsidian and bronze, not to build, but to *record*. They etch fresh pictographs over old, not as a chronicle, but as a conversation. They will chip out a loose tile to retrieve a valuable piece of corroded metal pipe, not for the material, but for the "story" its corrosion pattern tells. Their eyes are large, milky, and lidless, capable of seeing the faintest luminescence as a blaze, and the deepest infrared heat of a settling wall as a cold black. They scavenge the drippings from the condensation channels, sipping a mineral-rich slime. They are not hostile, but they are utterly indifferent to anything that is not the city's present state. To them, you are a strange, warm vibration that will, eventually, become a layer of dust in a crevasse.

**The Lammergeiers.**
Not birds. The name is a Gleaner approximation for the sound they make—a low, grinding croak that echoes from the higher vaults. They are rarely seen whole. You glimpse a segment: a length of pale, segmented limb clinging to a ceiling, indistinguishable from a carved pipe or tendon of fused bone. They are the cartographers of the vertical. Their bodies are flat, wide, and covered in a mosaic shell that exactly mimics the city's bas-relief, making them perfect, living pieces of the wall. They move with an agonizing, silent slowness, flowing over the surface like a slime mold of stone and cartilage. They are drawn to the lines of moisture, following the slow, dark rivulets upward to their sources in sweating stone, or downward to the drains. Their purpose seems to be the maintenance of this flow. They clear blockages with a secretion that eats away organic paste, leaving the glass-bone and volcanic brick untouched. Their croak is not a voice, but the sound of their shells grinding as they shift their mass. They are the city's circulatory system, given patient, crawling life.

**The Tenebrists.**
You do not see them. You see their effects. They are the source of the rhythmic *thrum*. In the deepest plazas, in alcoves behind a curtain of ever-dripping water, they are the living engines of the place. They are not mechanical, but they are not quite flesh. They are clusters of immense, rubbery muscle and nerve, bound into the very substance of the walls, their circulatory systems merged with the city's own plumbing. They pulse, slowly, powerfully, their contractions and relaxations driving the flow of water and air through the veins of the city, generating the faint electromagnetic field that makes the ceiling glow. They have no eyes, no mouths. Their sentience, if it exists, is a deep, slow, tidal thing—the consciousness of pressure, of flow, of maintaining a single, sublime rhythm against the eternal creep of entropy. A Gleaner might leave a tribute of a shiny piece of metal at a Tenebrist's alcove, a flicker of stimulus for the vast, dreaming mind in the wall.

**The Glyptodonts.**
The living tools. They are squat, barrel-shaped, and move on six sturdy, clawed legs. Their backs and heads are covered in thick, carapace plates that are not natural growths, but sections of the city's own building material—baked clay, polished stone, fused glass—grafted onto their bodies by generations of Tenebrists and their own strange biology. They are the builders and the janitors. A Glyptodont can be seen slowly scraping encrusted calcite from a drain with its head, its body resonating with the thrum it is connected to. They shape new paste in their mouths, mixing it with their saliva, a substance with a faint, bioluminescent quality. They are docile, single-minded, and live for centuries, growing new plates and shedding old ones, which the Gleaners then collect and use as currency or building material. They are the city's hands, and they dream in geometric patterns.

**The Unseen Builders.**
The tools are left. The piles of material wait. The carvings are half-finished. The rumor is that they are not gone; they are simply on a different cycle of construction, one measured in the rise and fall of the light-tides. They are seen only in moments of transition—a flicker in the deep shadow of a just-completed archway, a shape that is all angles and non-Euclidean perspective that vanishes when the light brightens. They do not build with matter so much as with *concept*. A half-finished face on a wall is not abandoned; it is a seed, left to gestate. In a thousand years, the slow, seismic processes of the city and the unconscious chiseling of the Glyptodonts might bring it to completion. The Builders are not residents; they are the will of the city itself, the conscious dreaming of its growth, and their work is the very fabric of the world the others inhabit.

This is a symbiotic fossil. The Gleaners read its past. The Lammergeiers tend its flow. The Tenebrists dream its pulse. The Glyptodonts shape its present. The Unseen Builders are its future intention. They all breathe the same heavy air, listen to the same dripping song, and exist in the grey, eternal twilight of their own making. You are the anomaly here—a brief, warm, dry silence in the endless, damp conversation of stone.
