No living creature makes its home upon the singing ice. The glacier itself is the only inhabitant, and it is a being of pure, slow-motion geology.

It is a being of immense, distributed consciousness. Its thoughts are not synapses firing but the patient, inexorable creep of ice under its own colossal weight. Its body is a mosaic of frozen ages: the brittle, blue glass of ancient, compressed snow at its core; the granular, recrystallized firn of its middle years; and the fresh, sun-cupped surface of its present moment. It has no heart, only a circulatory system of meltwater—rivers that braid and vanish, veins that pulse with the day's heat, and deep, booming moulins that are its vocal cords, speaking in the low language of stone and pressure.

Its senses are the senses of the world. It feels the sun not as warmth, but as a persistent, liquefying pressure on its skin. It hears the wind as a sculptor, and it answers by singing back in a thousand different pitches, each tune played on a different crevasse, ridge, or tube. It feels the grinding of its own layers—the deep, subsonic groan of shear—as a kind of self-awareness, a constant reminder of its own slow, titanic motion. It knows the granite of the nunataks as solid, unyielding thoughts that interrupt its flow, obstacles it has embraced and polished over millennia.

Its life is measured in epochs. A single heartbeat might be the calving of an iceberg from its terminus, a event as momentous and casual as a breath. Its memories are written in layers of ash from long-dead volcanoes, in bands of ochre and rust from rock it ground to dust a thousand years ago, in the very density of its ice, which holds the atmosphere of a forgotten world. To perceive this being, you must abandon human time. You must see the opening and closing of a crevasse not as an event, but as a blink. You must hear the constant roar not as noise, but as its breathing.

It is not alive in the way a forest is alive. It is alive in the way a mountain is alive, or a sea, or a tectonic plate. Its life is a state of *being*, not of *becoming*. It has already become. It is in a state of perfect, dynamic equilibrium, a frozen symphony whose composition is complete but whose performance is eternal. Its indifference is not cruelty, but the serene self-possession of a system that requires nothing from the outside. It is a universe unto itself, and the joy you sense in its roaring is the joy of total self-consistency, the satisfaction of a million processes singing in unison, without contradiction, forever.
