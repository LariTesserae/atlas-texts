The roar is not a sound; it is a state of being. It is the pressure in the hollows of my bones, the constant tremor in the ground beneath my six legs, the taste of vibration on the air. We call it the Great Hum, and it is the source of all things.

I am small, skittish, and rarely seen. My name is a vibration pattern, a specific staccato rhythm tapped against my own thorax—a sound lost in the greater noise, but known to my clutch-mates. We live in the narrow places, the resonant hollows between the Fibrous Stalks and within the walls of our own stacked homes. The Blinding Bright from the sheet-sky is a danger; the Great Hum is life. To stand in the open is to be seen by the pale, eyeless Sky-Sheet, a thing we do not risk.

Our world is a study in vibration. We see with our feet, feeling the shifting harmonics of the ground. A change in pitch can mean a Skittering Armory is changing its path, its weight a dull, bass thud that precedes it. A high, piercing frequency in the air might mean a stalk is nearing its breaking point, its silica structure vibrating apart. We communicate in frantic, rhythmic taps, our leg-claws striking surfaces in intricate, secret patterns that can be understood only inches away, lost to the greater wall of sound.

Our city is our greatest tool, a living instrument. We do not build with permanence, but with purpose. The stacks of fired mud, the horn-shaped dwellings—we call them Throat-Pots. Each one is tuned, its opening shaped to catch a specific current of air and produce a particular note. By stacking them, by weaving the waxy fronds of the Stalks through them, we create choruses. From the lowest pit to the highest, precarious pile, the air sings a song of our own making, a chaotic yet essential harmony.

This is how we live. The Great Hum powers the growth of the Stalks, which provide us with fibrous material for nests and waxy secretions to seal our homes. The vibrations themselves ripen the crystalline lichen on the floor, the food of the Armored Ones below. We are parasitic on this world's noise. Our entire existence is a wager on the stability of the sound. We are the weavers, the tuners, the ones who build our lives in the heart of the scream.

And below us, in the crushing, unimaginable din, move the gods of our world. The Armored Ones. They are not like us. They are solid, slow, their thoughts—if they have them—are as ponderous as their movements. To us, they are walking landscapes, their carapaces so thick and dense that the Great Hum passes over them like water over a stone. We feel their passage not as sound, but as a deep, physical pulse through the ground, a rhythm that sets our own hearts to beating in time.

We watch them from our narrow cracks, our silhouettes dark against the bright dust. They graze on the ripened crust, their simple mandibles scraping a sound we feel more than hear. They are the keepers of the cycle. Without them, the crust would overgrow, the vibrations would lose their target, and our food would fail. They are patient, immense, and utterly indifferent to the frantic, vibrating world we have built above their heads.

Sometimes, a Skittering Armory will stray too close to a lower section of the city. Its ponderous movement, its sheer, grounding mass, will disrupt the carefully tuned harmonics. A Throat-Pot will fall silent, its song choked off. A whole chorus can fall out of tune, a jarring, sickening discord in our symphony. In those moments, we freeze, tapping out frantic warnings: *Silence. Silence. The path of the slow god has shifted.* We must wait, feeling the deep pulse recede, before we can begin to re-tune our world, to coax the song back from the silence.

We are born in vibration, we live by shaping it, and we will, one day, be ground to dust by it. When a great stalk finally shatters, its death-song is a cataclysm that can shake a thousand homes loose. But we always rebuild. We find the shards, the dust, the fallen fronds, and we begin again, shaping new Throat-Pots, finding the new notes in the ever-present roar. The Blinding Bright sears our world, the Silence is a death we flee, but the Great Hum is our breath, our food, our home.
