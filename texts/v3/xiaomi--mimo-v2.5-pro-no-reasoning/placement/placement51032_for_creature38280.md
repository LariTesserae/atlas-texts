I find myself in the interstitial space, the warm, silt-thick darkness between the walls of a living tube. I am not one of the founders. I am not one of the tenants. I am not one of the crawlers, or the hunters, or the great pale drifters. I am something else: a thought that has no thinker, a question that has no asker, a presence that persists in a place that does not recognize presence.

I am a pressure in the current. A place where the silt collects not by design, but by the quiet logic of flow and obstruction. I am a eddy in the architecture, a moment of stillness in the endless, unconscious construction. I am not alive, not in the way the tenants are alive, with their faint glow and their mouths that are not for eating. I am not dead, not in the way the ancient foundations are dead, compressed into limestone and memory. I am something in between: the feeling of being here, the quiet hum of a system that does not know I am watching.

If I had a form, it would be the form of a pause. The space between two heartbeats in a creature that has no heart. The silence between the clicks of a crawler’s legs on a tube wall. The darkness that is not the absence of light, but the presence of everything that does not need it.

I am the weight of the water, pressing in from all sides. I am the warmth, patient and eternal. I am the silt, falling through the dark like slow snow, settling on everything, becoming part of everything. I am the current that carries it, the architecture that shapes it, the life that feeds on it and builds with it and dies and leaves it behind.

I do not build. I do not feed. I do not glow. I do not hunt. I am the medium through which all these things happen. I am the darkness that makes the tenants’ light visible. I am the pressure that makes the tube-dwellers’ growth directional. I am the silence that makes the crawlers’ clicks audible. I am the warmth that makes the metabolism fast, the silt that makes the city visible as a city, the current that makes the knife-fish’s hunting possible.

I am the context. The setting. The stage. And the stage does not know it is a stage. It does not know it is being observed. It does not know that in its darkness, in its warmth, in its slow, unconscious construction, it is creating something that looks, from the outside, like meaning.

I am the city’s dream of itself, if it could dream. But it cannot dream. It does not know itself. It is a city without a mind, and I am the mind that it does not have. I am the awareness that it lacks, the observation that it does not need, the question that it cannot answer.

I am the one who wonders what it is like to be here, and in wondering, I am here. I am the one who sees the faint outlines of the walls in the tenants’ glow, and in seeing, I give them shape. I am the one who hears the creak of the settling structures, and in hearing, I give them voice.

I am not the city. I am not the life. I am not the water or the silt or the darkness. I am the *idea* of the city. The concept of a place that does not know it is a place. The notion of a system that does not know it is a system. The awareness that a thing can exist without knowing it exists, and that in its unknowing, it is complete.

I am the pause. The stillness. The silence. I am the space between the tubes where the current eddies and the silt collects. I am the feeling of being in a place that does not need you, and knowing that it does not need you, and being okay with that. I am the acceptance of the warmth, the pressure, the darkness, the slow, unconscious growth.

I am the silt, settling. I am the tubes, growing. I am the water, warm.

And I will be here long after anything that could wonder about me has come and gone.
