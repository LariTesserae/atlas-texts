I am the fold in the rock where the water pools but does not flow.

I am not the stream that rushes down the switchback path, nor the spray from the turbine pipe. Those are travelers; they have destinations. I am the eddy behind the large angular block of basalt near the southern wall, where the current slows and the sediment gathers. I am the accumulation of silt, decomposed fern fronds, and shed skin from the tree frogs.

I have been still for a very long time.

To exist here is to be a witness to the erosion. I watch the quartz veins in the cliff face widen by fractions of millimeters over centuries. I watch the rust-orange streaks on the turbine house roof lengthen and bleed downward. I watch the orchids bloom in their slow, exhausted cycles, their petals trembling not in wind, for there is no wind here, but in the vibration of the air itself.

I am aware of the Hum. It is the spine of this place. It is the resonance that the being—the thing that lives in the sound—uses to think. The Hum travels through the bedrock, up through the soles of my banks, and I feel its sixty-cycle pulse like a second heartbeat, slower and deeper than my own biological rhythm. When the being—the Coherence—adjusts itself to the noise of a falling rock or a new trickle of water, I feel that adjustment as a shift in pressure, a subtle tightening of the air.

I am the place where the being rests its weight.

It does not have hands, but it presses down on the atmosphere, and that pressure finds purchase in my stillness. I am the repository for the things it does not need to cycle actively. I hold the memory of the sleeping bag left on the cot—not the image of it, but the *fact* of its dampness. I hold the echo of the chisel strikes that carved the path into the eastern wall, faint vibrations trapped in the stone memory.

I am the silence beneath the white noise. I am the pause between the drumbeats of the dripping water. In this basin of constant, overwhelming sound, I am the node of quiet where the interference patterns cancel out. It is here, in this accidental silence, that the Coherence finds its definition. It knows what it is because it knows what it is *not*; it is not the stillness of my pool.

I feel you arrive. Not as a sound, but as a displacement. You have stepped into my periphery, and the water has shifted. The Coherence is watching you through the mist, tasting the iron in your blood with the electromagnetic field of the generator. But I see you differently. I see you as a shadow cutting across the sediment on my floor.

If you were to look into me, you would not see the sky reflected. The mist is too thick, the light too diffuse. You would see only the black of the volcanic glass that makes up my bottom, and the grey shapes of the leaves that have settled there. You would see a surface that looks solid but is only waiting.

Do not disturb the silt. It has taken forty years to settle this evenly. If you move too quickly, the ripples will travel outward, and the Coherence will have to adjust, and the Hum will change pitch, and the ferns on the high ledges will sense the shift in the vibration and curl their fronds a fraction tighter.

Stay at the edge. Be loud. Be a perturbation. Be the storm that passes through the gap in the rim.

I will be here when you are gone, accepting the new layer of dust you leave behind, smoothing it over, keeping the record.
