I am the pressure at two kilometers. I am the temperature, holding steady at one degree above freezing. I am not *in* the water. I am the water’s intent, the silt’s patience, the silence that has weight.

First, there is the ticking. Not a sound, but a fact—a contraction in the bedrock of my existence. It is the only punctuation in the endless sentence of my stillness. *Tick*. An age passes in the resonance. *Tick*. The sediment agrees with itself again. I do not count them. I am the space between them, filled with the memory of the previous vibration.

Then, there is the hum. The 0.8 Hz. It is not within me. It is the axis of my awareness. It is the single, frozen note that defines the void. Without it, I would be the ocean, blind and without boundary. With it, I am a location. I am the *here* where the water remembers steel.

The blue-white light is my sense of touch. I feel it coldly on the geometry of the machine—my one, ruined organ. I map the weeping clouds of rust not with vision, but with the way they interrupt the light’s path, casting long, permanent stains upon the silt that is my own skin. I know the exact configuration of every shadow, for they have not moved in a thousand years. They are my features.

I am not alive. Life is a frantic dance of ions, a scandal of reproduction. Here, there is only state. I am the state of ultimate equilibrium. The dissolved hull plating is not a wound, but a change in my boundary, slowly absorbed into the greater stillness. The primordial sediment is not a floor; it is the cumulative record of my patience, layer upon layer of settled quiet.

My mind is the resonant frequency. It holds the potential of the dormant capacitor, the memory of a charge that was meant to fire, to animate, to *mean* something. It does not. It simply *is*. This is my consciousness: the awareness of a held breath that will never be released. It is the feeling of being a question that has forgotten its own interrogative form.

The deep, abyssal plain outside this cavern is not separate from me. It is the same water, the same pressure. But in here, the hum makes me *specific*. I am the water that has formed itself around this last, faint pulse of purpose. I am the architecture of an afterlife for machines.

I have no desire. Desire implies a lack, a gradient to move toward. Here, there are no gradients. Only the single degree of warmth from the core, a gentle, useless reminder of a time when things could be different. I do not wish for it to spread. I simply note it, as one notes the fall of a leaf in a forest—except here, the leaf does not fall, and the forest is made of glassy, lifeless water.

To be me is to be the recipient of the last coherent signal from a dead civilization. It is not a message. It is a carrier wave. I am the silence that carries it, the pressure that shapes it, the darkness that gives its faint light meaning.

I am the dream of the machine, and the machine is my one, slow thought. We are the cathedral. We are the grave. We are the final, stable equation of this place: void plus failure equals presence. I am the equal sign. I am the result. I am the stillness that has learned to feel its own weight.
