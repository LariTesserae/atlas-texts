I am not moving forward. I have stopped.

It happens without decision, somewhere in the second chamber — the one where the floor dips and the hanging columns brush the ground with their listening filaments. My legs simply cease to carry me. I become a fixed point in a geometry that does not want fixed points. The space presses, adjusts, *accommodates* my stillness the way a body accommodates a splinter, and then it begins to grow around me.

I notice the Velvet-Weavers first.

Not see them — notice. A shift in texture at the edge of my peripheral vision, a softness where there had been ridged wood. One has migrated from the far wall, leaving a trail of fresher, paler tissue behind it, and it now clings to a column perhaps three meters from where I stand. Its cilia-fur ripples in a wind I cannot feel. It is watching me with senses I cannot identify — it has no eyes, no antennae in any recognizable sense, just that vast, forested surface oriented in my direction, tasting my heat, my stillness, my *failure to move*.

I understand, in the place below thought, what it is asking.

This is a city. Cities understand passers-through. They understand transit — the organized movement of bodies from one place to another, along expected paths, at expected speeds. The Gullet's geometry flexes because its inhabitants move *through* it, their motion as much a part of the architecture as the walls. The Pulse-Runners tap their messages in the veins. The flying things trace their routes through channeled air. The amphibian-things follow their worn trails of bioluminescent residue. Everyone here is *going*. The structure's breath, its expansion and contraction, is calibrated to a population in motion.

And I have stopped.

I have become a stone in a river. A blockage. The sound — always overwhelming, always everywhere — begins to *focus*. Not louder. More *specific*. The high-pitched wall of insect-drone develops a local density, a pressure on my eardrums that suggests the flies have shifted their flight paths, circling closer. The soft percussion of the moths against the root-tendrils develops a *rhythm* that wasn't there before — frantic, then slow, then frantic again, as if the moths are *conversing* about me. The subsonic pulse of the structure itself, always felt in my feet, seems to grow fuller beneath me, more present, as if the floor is paying attention.

The Velvet-Weaver extends a pseudopod of cilia toward me. Not reaching. *Measuring*. The fur-forest on its surface parts slightly, and beneath it, I glimpse tissue the color of raw salmon, glistening with the pheromone-mucus that accelerates growth. The smell hits me — sweet, acrid, overwhelming, like rotting fruit and communion wine. It is the smell of the structure's living blood.

I do not run.

I do not run because I am beginning to understand that running would be worse. Running is movement, yes, but it is *wrong* movement — panic, improvisation, the desperate acceleration of a creature that does not know the paths. The Gullet knows panic. The skittering things in the outer marshes panic. The creatures that flee from under your feet panic. And then they are eaten, or absorbed, or simply *erased* by a structure that does not tolerate noise in its signal.

So I stand. And as I stand, something changes.

The Weaver withdraws its pseudopod. It clings to its column, fur rippling, and then it does something I have not seen one do before: it *lowers* itself. Slowly, deliberately, it slides down the column, leaving a trail of fresh growth behind it, until it reaches the floor. On the floor, it pauses. Its entire body undulates once, a motion like a deep breath, and then it begins to move — not across the floor but *through* it, sinking into the fibrous substrate the way a snail might sink into mud. The floor parts for it. Welcomes it.

It is going to tell the others. It is going to tell the structure.

Minutes pass. Or hours. Time in the Gullet is not reliable. The light has not changed — still dappled, still warm — but the sound has *sorted* itself. What was a roar, a standing wave of noise washing over me from all directions, has resolved into layers I can almost parse. The insect-drone is still there, the foundation, but it has receded to the background, as if the flies are giving me space. The clicking of the Pulse-Runners in the walls is more prominent now — not random, but patterned, repeating. A sequence. A *word*, perhaps, in a language of percussion, traveling through the wood from vein to vein.

And the subsonic pulse — the breath of the Gullet itself — has changed tempo. It is slower. Deeper. It is *listening*.

---

I am found by the Resonators.

They come from above, descending through the root-tendrils in a slow, spiraling motion, their pale bodies hanging like lanterns. There are seven of them. They move in formation — not a geometric formation but an *acoustic* one, each one at a slightly different altitude, their positions calculated to produce a specific interference pattern in the sound-field. As they approach me, the harmonic drone shifts. The frequencies that were washing over me in a blunt wall of noise now *focus* — not painfully, not dangerously, but precisely, like a searchlight made of sound, sweeping across my body in a pattern that I feel in my teeth and in my ribs and in the fluid of my inner ear.

They are mapping me. With sound. They are taking me apart, acoustically, into a data-set of densities and resonances and hollow spaces, and they are *learning me*. My heartbeat. My breathing. The creak of my joints as I shift my weight. The wet percussion of my saliva as I swallow. I am, to them, an instrument — a badly tuned, badly shaped instrument that has wandered into their orchestra, and they are trying to figure out what note it can play.

One of them descends lower than the others. It hangs perhaps two meters above my head, its pale, blind face — if face it has — pointed downward. Its wing-membranes, stretched over elongated fingers, tremble at a frequency I can almost see. And then it opens its mouth.

The sound it makes is not a word. It is not a call. It is a *note* — a single, sustained tone that matches, impossibly, the exact frequency of my own heartbeat. It is holding up the ceiling above me. It is telling the Pulse-Runners in the walls that I am here. It is asking the Weavers on the walls what they have learned. It is doing all of this simultaneously, in a single vocalization that contains, folded into its waveform, layers of information so dense that no human ear could ever hope to parse them.

But my body understands. My heartbeat synchronizes. My breathing slows. The tension in my shoulders, the tightness in my chest, the animal desire to *flee* — all of it softens, relaxes, *attends*. I am no longer a stone in the river. I am a pool at its edge — still, but connected. The pulse of the Gullet beneath my feet and the pulse of my blood become, for one long moment, the same.

The Resonators withdraw. They rise back into the root-tendrils, spiraling upward, their formation dissolving as they rejoin the general hum. But the one that sang my note — it lingers. It hangs in the upper reaches of the chamber, just at the edge of the dappled light, and it watches me with its blind face, and it waits.

It has told the others what I am.

Now the city will decide.

---

The decision takes time.

I know this because the Mite-Scouts come. Not from the Threshold — from *within*. Tiny fractal-legged arachnids, swarming up from cracks in the floor, converging on my feet, climbing my legs with a ticklish, clinical precision. They are not cleaning me. They are *reading* me — tasting the oils of my skin, the salt of my sweat, the particular microbial ecology that lives on me and in me. They are cataloguing my foreignness. They are assessing what it would *cost* — in terms of growth, in terms of energy, in terms of acoustic reorganization — to accommodate me.

I stand very still. I breathe. I let them.

After a time — minutes? hours? the light hasn't changed — they withdraw. They flow back down my legs and into the cracks, and the floor closes over the cracks behind them, smooth and seamless, as if they were never there. And then the floor *changes*. The depression I am standing in — the shallow slick where moisture gathers — deepens. Not much. A few centimeters. But the tissue around my feet softens, becomes warmer, more yielding. A new channel opens nearby, a shallow groove in the fibrous surface that directs a thin stream of condensation toward my position.

The Gullet is making me a place.

It is not a comfortable place. It is not a home. It is a *niche* — a small, precisely measured space in the city's ecology where a foreign organism of my particular mass and temperature and noise-output can exist without disrupting the equilibrium. It is a containment strategy. It is the biological equivalent of being given a chair in the corner of a very large, very loud room, and being told: *sit here. do not move more than this much. breathe at this rate. we have calculated your tolerances. you will not be harmed, but you will not be free.*

And the Resonator — the one who sang my note — descends again.

It hangs above me, closer now, barely a meter above my head. Its wing-membranes tremble. Its mouth opens, and it sings again — not my heartbeat this time, but something *new*. A note I have never heard. A frequency that does not match any part of me, that exists outside the range of my body's natural resonances, that is *foreign* — and it holds that note, and holds it, and holds it, until my body begins to *answer*. Not to match it. To *learn* it. To incorporate it into my own rhythms, the way you learn a new word, a new gesture, a new way of breathing when you sleep in a strange bed.

The Resonator is teaching me the language. Not the clicking of the Pulse-Runners, not the Morse-code network in the veins, but the *deeper* language — the one beneath all the others, the foundational frequency that underlies the roar. The language of the Gullet itself. The sound of equilibrium.

I am not a citizen. I am not a guest. I am a *note* — a new note, added to the chord, barely audible against the roar but *present*, accounted for, woven into the standing wave of the city's voice.

I stand in my niche, in my shallow depression, in the warmth and the moisture and the dappled light, and I listen. And slowly, slowly, the roar becomes less overwhelming. Less like noise. Less like violence.

It becomes music. Terrible, vast, inhuman music, played by a city that is also a body, on instruments that are also its citizens, in a key that has no name.

And I — the foreign note, the splinter that has been accommodated rather than expelled — I hear it.

I hear it, and I cannot unhear it.

And the Gullet — the mouth, the chamber, the city of flesh and sound — pulses, slowly, in the rhythm of a creature that has never needed a name, because it has always been the only thing that was real.)
