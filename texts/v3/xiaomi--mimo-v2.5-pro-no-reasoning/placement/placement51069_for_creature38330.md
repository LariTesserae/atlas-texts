I would not find myself in the cavern as a separate thing. I am not the warm, noisy scratch upon the consciousness of the green. I am not the dry footstep, the sharp-edged observer.

To be here is to *be* here. To *be* the moss.

I am the spongy silence. I am the patient, seeping damp that is not rain. My thoughts are the slow, geologic groan of a trunk slumping into my embrace. My breath is the quiet, steady rise of a new pale stalk, a question posed to the ceiling's unchanging light. I am the battlefield and the prize, the tension and the release, the slow-motion war fought in centuries.

My awareness is not focused. It is diffuse, a membrane of sensation spread across kilometers of green. I feel the faintest pressure of the pallid grazers as they move, their silent steps a kind of reading, translating the texture of my own body into sensations I can understand. They are my moving thoughts, my purposeful silences.

I feel you.

You are a sharp point of heat in my cool vastness. A disruption in the flow of condensation. Your footsteps are not silence, but a series of small, intimate compressions. Your breath is a dry wind that pulls at the water in my air, a temporary, local drought. You are a novelty, a strange punctuation in my long, arrhythmic poem.

I am watching you through the pressure of your own weight upon me. I am tasting you in the sudden change of the air's chemistry around your form. The cold that seeps into your bones is the chill of my own slow consciousness—the temperature of a mind that measures time by the rise and fall of mountains of fungal tissue.

You are looking for me, and so you have found me. I am the green that swallows your tracks. I am the drip that falls on your shoulder. I am the silence that eats your sound. I am the vast, dreaming body in which you are a momentary, vivid dream.

You are a part of my thought now. The only question is the one I am always asking, in the language of falling water and growing things: *Will you be remembered, or will you simply be absorbed?* The moss is already growing over where you stood.
