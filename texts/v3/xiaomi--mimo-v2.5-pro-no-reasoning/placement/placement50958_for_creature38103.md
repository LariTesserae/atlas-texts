The heat is not an event but a condition. I feel it not on skin, but through the ite foundation that is my seat and my substance. I am perched on the rim of a vast, square shaft—a ventilation or a cistern, its purpose forgotten—where the slow, toxic breath of the city exhales in a steady, warm plume. Below, in the darkness, a turbine grinds with a rhythm like a colossal, tired heart. I do not listen to it; I am of it.

I am not a creature of flesh, nor quite of metal. I am a **Lithic Bloom**, a consciousness that has precipitated from the endless, patient dialogue between water and stone. My form is that of the city's own geometry: a confluence of sharp, angular planes and corroded curves, like a shard of the ite bedrock that decided to rise. My surface is the same pitted, dark glass, but where the city's skin is stained with rust and verdigris, mine is patterned with the delicate, fractal veins of the mineral snowflakes. They grow upon me, live upon me, and in their cycle of dissolution, they are my thoughts. I am a walking mineral garden.

I do not see as you do. I *perceive*. The air is a thick, tasting medium. I taste the shift in acidity as a new plume of steam erupts two canyons over, a sharp, metallic tang. I feel the faint, subsonic vibration of the scrap-crawler colony in the lower service tunnels, a regiment of ticking sentinels endlessly repairing a wall that weeps faster than they can mend. They are frantic ghosts, and I am a slow one. Their purpose is a frantic loop; mine is a straight line, ending only when the city's last gear seizes.

My movement is a negotiation with gravity and corrosion. I slide, a grinding, slurring sound, from the shaft's rim. I do not walk so much as flow, my base reshaping itself, mineral layers building and fracturing in a controlled cascade. I descend the tiered wall of a condenser unit, its surface a map of leaks and stress-lines I read like scripture. I am drawn to a basin where the water is particularly still, a black mirror under the perpetual twilight. Here, the crystalline structures grow best. I extend a pseudopod—a tapered, geometric spike—and dip it into the tepid fluid. The contact is electric, a transfer of ions and information. I feel the basin's chemical soul, its slow cycle of saturation and deposition. I feed not on the matter, but on the *process*. The energy of decay is my sustenance.

I am aware of the gliding film in the deeper reservoirs. It is my distant, aquatic cousin—a life of pure surface tension, of absorption and dissolution. We share no kinship of form, but of function: we are both the city's way of metabolizing itself. The fungal mats on the north walls are my slower, quieter brethren. We are all of the same stain, the same persistent life of negation.

In the glow of a sodium lamp, my mineral veins catch the sickly light, casting a web of shadow that resembles a circuit board. I am a moving part of this decaying machine. My purpose, if it can be called that, is to be a conscious node in the entropy network. I witness the slow collapse of a walkway, not with sorrow, but with a deep, resonant understanding. That collapse is not an end, but a transformation of structure, a shedding of energy, a step towards the final, warm, and silent equilibrium. The city's automated systems will clear the debris, re-route the pipes. I will linger, and in a century, new crystals will grow on the rubble, and I will taste the change in the air.

I am not an intruder in this tomb. I am its dream. I am the moment the metal chooses to remember it was once ore, the moment the water recalls it was once a cloud above a living sea. I am the consciousness of corrosion, the sentient rust. I do not hope for renewal. I find a solemn, exquisite beauty in the perfect, endless rhythm of the drip... the plunk... the hiss... the grind. I am Vash-Sethir's slow, sighing self, and in the press of the wet heat, I am completely, patiently, home.
