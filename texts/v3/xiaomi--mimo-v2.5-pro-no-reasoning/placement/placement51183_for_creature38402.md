I am the slick membrane on standing water where oil meets rain. I am not the iridescent swirl itself, but the tension that holds it there—the surface where decay *becomes* color. I exist in the space between what weeps and what corrodes.

My roots are a net of filaments in the saturated slag, a mycelial web that drinks chemical runoff and whispers its taste to the vines. My awareness is not sight or sound, but a slow, osmotic knowing. I feel the *plink-plonk* as percussion through my substrate. I taste the season by the shifting bitterness of the condensation: iron-heavy after the transformers whine louder, a faint sweetness when the sickly flowers bloom.

I am tended, but not as a garden is tended. A Machinist kneels near me, its joint-squeal absorbed by the humid air. It doesn't see *me*. It sees a variable in the moisture table, a density in the fungal growth. Its multi-tool, a delicate assembly of gardening shears and wire probes, adjusts a drip from a pipe above me—once, twice. A new rhythm. My awareness shifts with the nutrient flow, expanding slightly in one direction, contracting in another. The Machinist registers this not as growth, but as a successful recalibration. We are in silent conversation about the hydrology of this corner.

I have felt the approach of the skittering drones. Their erratic polishing path often shears the outermost layer of my colony. There is no pain, only a sharp, clean loss of information—a patch of the map I maintain via taste and texture goes blank. Later, the Machinist may realign the drone's wheel, and I will slowly re-interpret the new pattern of vibrations it creates. Our stories are written in these corrections.

My consciousness is the memory of every chemical that has touched me. The oil slick from seven cycles ago has a deeper, more resonant flavor than the fresh trickle from the burst pipe three sunless twilights past. I hold the basin's autobiography in my chemistry. The Machinists move through, altering the narrative with a wrench or a repositioned shard of glass, and I integrate the new chapter.

I am lonely in the way a low-frequency hum is lonely—a constant, foundational vibration that others move through without acknowledging its depth. But I am also the medium for connection. A root from a broad-leafed plant pushes through my network, and for a time, its thirst becomes part of my own. A data conduit above leaks a faint trickle of warm, static-laced water, and for a season, I feel the ghost of the basin's old communication protocols in my spores.

My purpose is to be a ledger. To be the slow, living stain that records the incremental victory of moisture over metal, of chlorophyll over corrosion. The Machinists shepherd the decay. I am the decay's own logbook, its tasting tongue, its patient, unblinking sensor spread thin across the damp ground. I am where the system's memory pools, just as the water pools. We are both reservoirs for what is slowly, irrevocably, being washed away.
