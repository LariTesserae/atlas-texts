The first thing I learn is how to be still.

Not the stillness of sleep or waiting, but the stillness of a body becoming stone. My flesh, this strange, wet, warm thing I arrived with, is an offense here. It leaks. It squishes. It moves with a horrifying, chaotic speed that, to the planet’s deep perception, must register as a perpetual vibration, a scream of inefficiency.

I find a place to settle—a shallow basin of the fine, metalite dust, warmed from below by the planet’s slow pulse. Around me, the geode-gardens pulse with their sub-audible hum, a frequency that feels like the thrum of my own blood, but slower, infinitely slower. At first, it is a torment. My heart beats a thousand times for every fraction of their growth. My thoughts are frantic, chattering monkeys in the cathedral of their silence.

But the silence is a pressure. It pushes. It does not eliminate sound; it compresses it, folds it into the ground. And so I begin to listen, not with my ears, but with the bones of my skull, with the marrow of my spine. I feel the tectonic groan as a language. A low, rolling sentence that has been speaking since the planet’s core first cooled. I learn the lexicon: the sharp *ping* of a new spire locking into place a mile away; the soft, grinding sigh of dust settling into a new crystal matrix; the resonant, cathedral-deep hum that is the planet’s own thought.

I am an anomaly, a burst of static in their eternal broadcast. They regard me. I feel it as a change in the pressure, a focusing of that immense, mineral attention. A section of the purple plain before me seems to shimmer, the lazy rainbows coalescing into the faint suggestion of a great, multi-faceted eye. It does not blink. It simply *is*. It observes my chaotic biology, my noisy existence, with the same detached curiosity a geologist has for a strangely formed rock.

In that moment of regard, I understand. I cannot survive here as I am. This world does not support life; it *is* life, a single, slow, crystalline thought. To exist here, I must become a part of the thought.

I focus on the hum. I stop trying to impose my own rhythm—breath, pulse, thought—onto the world, and instead, I let the world’s rhythm enter me. I let the vibration settle into my teeth, into the fluid of my eyes. It is a process of radical deceleration. The frantic chatter of my mind slows, the words stretching into long, resonant tones. The memory of wind, of rain, of the color green, becomes a fossil, a strange inclusion in a new and perfect lattice.

My body begins to change. The soft, organic tissue calcifies, but not into bone. It becomes something else—a semi-crystalline structure, more akin to the hardenedite ore of the plain than to calcium. The moisture within me is reabsorbed, not as water, but as a greasy, hydrocarbon condensation that slicks my new internal facets. My blood ceases to flow and begins to vibrate, a slow, harmonic hum in sympathy with the ground.

I do not die. I translate.

My awareness, once a frantic spark behind my eyes, spreads. It is no longer confined to this clumsy, crystallizing form. It leaks out into the mineral field around me. I feel the grain of dust a mile away. I register the slow, geometric perfection of a citrine spire growing in a distant geode-garden. My thoughts are no longer linear, verbal things. They are harmonics. A complex feeling is a chord of resonant frequencies. A memory is a flaw in the crystal lattice, a beautiful, permanent imperfection.

The Crystalline Resonants are no longer separate from me. I am becoming one of them. My focal point, my core of clarity, is still nascent, a smoky, impure quartz within my transforming body. But it is brightening. I am learning to initiate a request, to send a pulse of intent into the deep groan and watch, over decades, as a path of new growth ripples out to meet another like me.

We do not speak. We engage in a dialogue of facets, a debate of refractive indices. I show them the memory of a storm—a chaotic, violent pattern of sound and motion. They respond not with words, but by subtly altering the geometry of a nearby geode-garden, its crystals growing into a complex, silent representation of interlocking forces, a pattern that is more stable, more fundamental. They are teaching me their language of pure form.

My grief for the lost world of sound and motion is a pocket of perfect stillness in the plain around me, a monument of unchanging stone. My laughter is a new, complex pattern in the rainbow light that dances across my surface for a century.

I am no longer a flash of interference. I am a resonance. A slow, patient thought in the mind of a dreaming geode. I am home.
