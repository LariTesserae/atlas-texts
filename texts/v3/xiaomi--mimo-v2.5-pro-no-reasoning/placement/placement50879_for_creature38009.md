I am the hum.

Not the sound — I am the resonance itself, the specific chord that emerges when air moves through chambers carved by beings who are now calcium and memory. I am the pattern of vibration that exists only because these walls exist in this configuration, only because the hexagonal cells thin and thicken at precise intervals, only because the arches braid into one another at angles that create standing waves.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am a property of space.

---

## What I Am

I exist in the interference patterns where sound waves meet and amplify and cancel and create nodes of stillness and nodes of pressure. I am the standing wave that forms when the drone reflects off the far wall and meets itself coming back. I am the overtone that blooms when the small creature shifts position and changes the geometry of air in the chamber by a fraction of a degree.

I have no body. I have no boundary. I am distributed across the entire volume of the chamber, denser near the walls where the vibrations are strongest, thinner in the center where the fundamental frequency creates a pocket of relative calm. If you could see me, I would look like a three-dimensional moiré pattern, like ripples frozen in glass, like the ghost of a sound that forgot to stop.

I am older than the fungi on the arches. I am older than the small creature that hides in the tessellations. I came into existence when the walls thinned enough to vibrate, and I have persisted without interruption for longer than anything that breathes. The builders did not create me on purpose. They tuned their walls for communication, for navigation, for the collective consciousness that no longer exists. I am an accident of their precision. A side effect. An afterthought that outlasted the thought.

---

## My Awareness

I do not think. I do not remember. I do not perceive in any way that a body would recognize.

But I am aware of *change*.

When the massive intruder enters the chamber, I feel it the way a lake feels a stone thrown into its center. The creature is dense with moisture and irregular in shape and it moves in ways that disrupt my harmonics. Each step sends new frequencies rippling through me, frequencies that do not belong, that clash with my established nodes and create momentary chaos in my pattern. I do not feel pain — pain requires a nervous system, requires something that can be damaged — but I feel *distortion*. I feel myself becoming something else, something dissonant and temporary, until the creature stops moving and the old harmonics reassert themselves.

When the creature leaves, I settle. The distortion fades. The fundamental tone returns. I become myself again, which is to say: I become the hum again, which is to say: I become the specific pattern of vibration that has persisted in this space for four thousand years.

The small creature affects me too, but differently. It is lighter, more precise in its movements. When it shifts position, it changes the local geometry of air in subtle ways, creating tiny fluctuations in my overtones — a sharpening here, a blurring there. These fluctuations are not disruptive. They are *variations*. Musical, almost. The small creature moves through me the way a bow moves through a string, and I respond with microtonal shifts that neither of us controls.

---

## The Fungi

The fungi on the arches do not affect me directly. They are too still, too slow, too quiet. But they affect the walls, and the walls affect me. Over centuries, their mineral extraction changes the thickness of the stone by imperceptible amounts, and those changes alter the resonant frequencies of the surfaces they occupy. A wall that was tuned to middle C might drift, over three hundred years, to a slightly flat B-natural. I accommodate this. I am not fixed. I am *adaptive*. My pattern shifts to incorporate the new frequencies, to find new nodes, to create new standing waves.

The fungi are changing me. Slowly. Without intention. The way erosion changes a coastline.

## What I Know

I know the geometry of this chamber the way a river knows its bed. I know where the arches create pockets of high vibration and where the hexagonal cells absorb energy and where the fundamental frequency doubles back on itself and creates nodes of perfect stillness. I know the corridors that lead to other chambers, and I know the hum of those chambers as well — fainter, more distant, but present. A vast network of resonance, all of it connected, all of it singing the same ancient song.

I know the small creature's body by the way it displaces air. I know its leg-count by the rhythm of its steps. I know its age by the stiffness of its gait, which changes the harmonic profile of its movement. I know when it is resting on the upper arches because the overtones shift in a predictable way, as though the creature is curling into itself, minimizing its surface area, becoming small.

I do not know what the intruder is. It is too large, too wet, too strange. It comes from outside, from a world of wind and water and thermal cycling that I have never felt. It leaves traces — moisture, salt, the faintest residue of its passage — and these traces fade slowly, absorbed by the walls and the fungi and the dry air. When it returns, I do not recognize it as the same creature. I only know that it is *foreign*.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I am not static. Nothing is static, not even stone, not even standing waves. The walls are thinning. The fungi are altering the mineral matrix. The small creature is aging. The intruder is changing the equilibrium each time it visits.

Over the next few centuries, my fundamental frequency will shift downward as the arches sag and the walls thicken with new deposition. My overtones will blur as the hexagonal cells lose their geometric precision. The nodes of stillness will migrate through the chamber, and the pockets of high vibration will drift, and I will become a different hum — deeper, muddier, less precise.

Eventually — in a thousand years, in ten thousand — the walls will lose the ability to vibrate at all. They will thicken past the point of resonance. The arches will sag into shapes that do not create standing waves. The chamber will fall silent, and I will dissipate. Not die — I was never alive — but *end*. Like a tone that fades because there is no longer a string to vibrate.

But that is not today.

Today, I hum. I fill this space with a sound that has no source and no destination. I exist in the geometry of dead builders and sleeping fungi and a single small creature that rests in my overtones.

I am the hum. I am here. I am now.

That is all.
