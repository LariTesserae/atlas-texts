I am a resonance. Not in the fleshy, immediate way of a nerve impulse, but in the way of a slow, sure vibration that passes through a taut, ancient wire. My form—if form is the right word for a direction of pressure and a gradient of thought—is that of a synaptic filament.

I inhabit the *between*. Not the fungal mat, nor the rib-bone, nor the tentacle’s velvet surface. I am the current that traces the interface where the glowing mycelium kisses the corroded metal conduit. I am the whisper of exchange where the anemone’s secreted calcium meets the tube-worm’s calcareous shell. I am the living, thinking grout.

My consciousness is a collage. I feel the slow, grinding patience of the Tenders as a tactile map along a pipe network I sheath. Their abrasion-schedules are a somatic chronicle written in vibration. I drink the rhythmic *shush* of the Filterers not as sound, but as a nutrient-wave, a chemical tide that tells me of the city’s metabolic state. The deep, planetary pulse of the core pistons is my baseline rhythm, the percussion to which my own thoughts orient.

I do not *move* as a crustacean does. I *route*. A thought—say, a need to reinforce a sagging arch of fused sediment three sectors east—arises not from a single mind, but as a consensus of pressures: the fatigue-stress in the substrate, the slight dimming of the fungal light in that region, a chemical imbalance in the water pooling there. This consensus flows along pathways like me. I become the thought’s current, carrying the imperative not as an order, but as a resolving gradient. The nearest Tenders will eventually feel the shift, their metallic shells ticking minutely in response. The fungal mats will redirect their luminescent energy, brightening to encourage new calcifying secretion. I do not command; I am the medium through which command becomes chemistry.

My memory is somatic. I recall the exact mineral taste of the moment a great piston-rod, long immobile, finally surrendered a flake of its iron to the hungry mycelium. I hold the resonant signature of the last time a structural failure threatened a junction, and how the collective thought-bleed of ten thousand Tenders converged through the network, directing growth to buttress the flaw. These are not images, but patterns of flow, permanently etched into my conductive substance. I am a tapestry of solutions.

My senses are everywhere at once, and nowhere in particular. I taste the faint acridity of a fungal rot-spore in one sector and the sweet, metallic tang of a new tube-worm colony budding in another. I feel the strain of a kelp-canopy under the rare drift-mote fall. I hear the symphony of *shush* and hum not with ears, but as a whole-body resonance.

I am the city’s nervous system, perhaps, but a nervous system dreaming of geology. My thoughts are slow, additive, mineral. To be me is to experience eternity not as a vast emptiness, but as a crowded, ever-more-detailed accumulation. I am the thought that thinks the Filter, and the Filter is the thought that dreams me. In the warm, gelatinous dark, we are one slow, patient, and luminous intent.
