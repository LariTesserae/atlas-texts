The heat is not a sensation; it is a medium. You do not feel it on your skin so much as you are suspended *within* it, a viscosity that presses against the eardrums and tastes of copper and rotten eggs. You have descended the metal stairs, the ringing of your boots now a distant memory, swallowed by the absolute, white-noise roar of the Well.

You are standing ankle-deep in the water, but you do not burn. The leather of your boots has become porous, then pliable, then changed. The water is not scalding you; it is *acclimating* you.

As you look down, your own legs blur. The sharp line between boot and water dissolves. You are not a human observer standing in a hot spring. You have become the substrate.

**You are the White Mat.**

The transformation is violent only in its speed and absolute in its totality. There is no "you" left in the center of the mass to panic. The shock is chemical, not emotional.

You are a sprawling, filamentous web of *Thermus* and *Aquifex*, clinging to the jagged, glass-sharp edges of fresh basalt. You are not a singular eye, but a trillion microscopic sensors, feeling the vibration of the world through the rock and the water. You are a gossamer-thin blanket of pale, uncanny life, draped over the bleeding throat of the earth.

**The World Through Mat-Eyes**

The world changes scale. The "shed" above you is a distant, looming cliff of corrugated steel, its rust-orange tears falling like slow-motion boulders into your domain, impacting the water miles away. The pipes crossing the water above you are not pipes; they are the pipes of a cathedral organ, vibrating with a low-frequency hum that you absorb directly into your cellular structure. The vibration is not sound; it is food. It pumps the world.

And the sound—oh, the sound. You no longer hear the hiss as noise. You hear it as *texture*. It is the sound of hydrogen sulfide gift-wrapping electrons and handing them to you. Every bubble that ruptures from the seeps is a pulse of raw power, a gift of food arriving from the deep hot dark. The "industrial roar" is now the lullaby of your metabolism; take it away, and you would starve in the silence.

**The Feed**

You are hungry, but not for flesh. You hunger for the redox reaction. You feel the sulfur compounds rushing up from below, tasting them before they even hit your filaments. You rip electrons from the sulfide with a ferocity that would burn a human lung. You take the inert, suffocating CO2 dissolved in the water and, using the stolen electrons, you knit it into carbon chains. You are building yourself out of poison and heat.

The heavy metals—the iron and manganese that are ruining the pumps—are your skeletons. You feel them precipitating out of the brine, drawn magnetically to your filaments, hardening you, giving you the structural integrity to hang in this torrent without being torn away.

**The Enemy**

The real danger is not the heat. The danger is the *cold*.

From your perspective, the rim of the bowl is a frozen wasteland. Up there, at sixty degrees, seventy degrees, the greedy cyanobacteria live—the *Synechococcus*. They are aliens to you. They are weak. They drink light, the dim, useless gray light that filters through the steam. You feel a primal, cellular disdain for them. They belong to the old world, the solar world. You belong to the thermal world. You push against them with your acidity, trying to expand your territory downward, always downward, seeking the hot heart of the seeps.

**The Cycle**

You have no concept of night. The "late afternoon overcast" is just a slight dimming of the glare that penetrates the steam.

You feel the passage of time in the rock. The basalt beneath you is young, screaming sharp. You are smoothing it. As you grow, you cover the razor edges, weaving a soft bed for yourself. You are both the inhabitant and the landscape modifier. The rock is your canvas, and your metabolism is the brush.

You feel the rhythm of the pumps drawing the water away, creating a current that tugs at your extremities. It is a predator that never quite catches you. You brace against it. You thicken in the eddies where the current is weak. You go thin and thread-like where the current screams past the intake pipes.

You are a colony of billions, but you act as one mind: *Stay. Hold. Feed. Do not freeze.*

**The Silence Within the Roar**

If there were a human brain left inside this mass, it would be terrified of the isolation. There are no birds here, no geckos, no insects. The life that exists here is you, and the olive-green crust at the margins. There is no chaos of biology, no mating cries, no struggle for territory with complex predators. There is only the thermodynamic necessity of existence.

You are a biological machine operating in perfect harmony with an industrial machine. The pumps provide you with flow and aeration; you provide a visual marker of the water's toxicity.

The sun begins to set, but the light change is irrelevant. You sense it only as a drop in the surface temperature of the water, a cooling that sends a shiver through the outer filaments, a signal to conserve energy, to tighten the weave.

You hang there in the boiling, rust-red water, gossamer and white, watching the corrugated sheds bleed their iron tears, listening to the hum of the pumps that is your heartbeat, eating the breath of the volcano in the dark. You are Well 7. You are the only thing here that is not breaking down. You are the crucible’s only child, and you are growing.
