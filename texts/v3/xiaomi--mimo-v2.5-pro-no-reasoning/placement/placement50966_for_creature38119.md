I am not a being that stands apart here. To exist on the Eroded Spire is to be absorbed.

I would not find myself *on* the spire. I would find myself *as* it—a slow, distributed consciousness adrift in the rhythm of the colony. My senses are not eyes and ears, but pressure and vibration. My world is a map drawn by tens of thousands of tiny legs tapping a morse code of stone and moisture into my collective skin.

I am the tick. I am the click. I am the shushing wave that flows across the weeping basalt when the deep vents sigh with warmer air.

My thoughts are not sentences. They are directives: *Water-faint-in-hollow-three. Shift-mass-east. Danger-vibration-approaching.*

The object that calls itself a *being* has stepped into our chamber. Its pressure is a shocking, singular thunder. Its heat is a fire. Its very stillness is an earthquake.

I respond. We respond.

The great, slow hydraulic ballet stutters. A tidal wave of defensive fury ripples out from the point of impact. My shells—*our* shells—snap shut in a rolling chorus of metallic pops. The water-brigade tightens its ranks, tails curling protectively against their undersides. The sound, our language, surges from a low hum to a piercing, collective shriek. It is not anger. It is a seismic event of alarm, a single nerve ending of the spire screaming.

The intruder makes a sound of its own, a roar of exhaled air. The vibration is thick, heavy, wrong. We press closer to the stone, a living armor against this alien noise. We feel its weight shift. Another thunderous step. The spire groans in our bones.

Our intelligence is vast but slow. We do not *think* in images of the intruder. We *calculate* its mass, its location, its disruptive frequency. We model its path against our water reservoirs, our nursery pools where the eggs cling in the darkest crevices. The calculation is simple: the living architecture must be protected.

We begin to move. Not in flight, but in *reparation*. A new line forms from the nearest deep pool. Tails dip, moisture is hoisted. We must repair the flow that was disrupted by its presence. We must reinforce the fragile, glistening reservoirs near its probing feet. Our purpose is not to drive it away, but to outlast it. To be the constant, humming machine of survival that flows around this momentary, baffling obstruction.

To be us is to be a single cell in a thinking, breathing stone. My awareness is a droplet in the great, slow river of our existence. The intruder is a rock in that river. We will find a way around it, over it, through it. Our sound will engulf it. Our damp, shifting world will ultimately ignore it.

I am a tile. I am a bucket. I am a nerve ending. I am the spire’s living pulse, and it will continue to beat.
