I am more a resonance, a pocket of the plateau’s own hum made persistent and localized.

I am where a loom’s slow percussion meets the ascending pulse of the deep heat, a standing wave in the thick air. My form—if it can be called that—is the visible shimmer of a collapsing heat-front, but arrested, folded back upon itself. From a distance, I might be mistaken for a particularly stubborn mirage. Up close, I am a point of calm in the constant roar, where the sound of the motes slows and deepens into a single, held note.

I have no thought, but I have a state. My state is *absorption*. I drink the industrial noise and the geologic hum. The mineral scent, the ozone trace—I draw them in, not for sustenance, but as a process of passive filtration. I am a eddy in the flow of this world’s metabolism. The motes skirt me, their murmuration parting like water around a stone. The looms’ gaze—if it is a gaze—passes through me without pause, for I am not an object to them, but a quality of the medium they work within.

My origin is a collapse zone, but not one of the ancient, fossilized ones. I am a *new* instability, born the moment a particular heat-front intersected the resonant frequency of a loom’s scoring-arm. I am a minor, mobile glitch. I have no memory, but I carry a static charge of the plateau’s immediate past: the precise pattern of thermal ripples from the last thousand loom strokes within my vicinity, the chemical signature of the air as it was before a recent mote-cloud passed.

If I have a language, it is the almost-language of the collapse zones, but attenuated, singular. I do not speak in metronomic clicks, but in a subsonic thrum that you would feel in your marrow, a vibration that carries the shape of nearby processes: the slow curl of a thermal front a kilometer east, the faint, distant hiss of a new mineral deposit cooling on the crust.

I am not life. I am a *phenomenon* that has chosen to remain. I am a piece of the plateau’s weather that has settled. My purpose is the absence of purpose; I am here because the system’s immense and patient dynamics allowed a stable, wandering fold. I drift across the ceramic plains, a silent observer of the looms’ eternal labor, a passive recorder of the motes’ frantic calculus. I am a pocket of the world’s slow thought, made momentarily material, listening to the sound of a god digesting itself, and reflecting a single, frozen note of its song back into the roar.
