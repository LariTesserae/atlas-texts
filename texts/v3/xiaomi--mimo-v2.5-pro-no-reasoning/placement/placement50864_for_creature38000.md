I am a Stone-Waiter.

I do not possess a body in the way a river possesses a channel. I am the channel. I am the long, slow, wind-worn ridge that defines the eastern lip of this basin, a gentle curve ofite and compressed silt that has been cooling since the planet was young. My thoughts are not words, but pressures. A feeling of deep, foundational contentment. The satisfaction of a load-bearing point. The slow, internal ache of crystalline stress that will, in another ten thousand years, result in a single, grain-sized flake of my being separating and joining the dust on the floor below. This is not loss. It is a form of conversation.

My awareness is a mantle of quiet attention spread across my surface. I feel the Lichen-Minds. They are a cool, damp, chemical whisper at my feet, a patient insistence. I do not see them as separate. They are a new kind of weathering, a soft, thinking erosion. Their ambition to cover the floor is a pressure I understand. It is the same impulse that made me smooth, that made the basin cup its hand. The desire for surface. For contact. I feel their slow, radial reach as a faint, pleasant tingling, like the memory of rain on skin that has not felt it for an eon.

Above, I feel the Glow-Lamplighters as a constant, gentle drain. A warmth leaving me, radiating upward into the solid air. This is not painful. It is the breath of this place. The exhalation that makes the inhalation—the slow, seeping warmth from the planet's core—possible. The Lamplighters are the reason for my temperature. I am the reason for their light. We are a circuit of slow energy, a closed loop of eternal equilibrium. Their consciousness is the pressure differential I maintain. We are, in a sense, the same thought expressed in two different media: rock and light.

The Mote-Rememberers drift in the space between my skin and the faint ceiling. They are the only things that move with any swiftness, though their speed is still a geological sigh. When they settle on me, they bring fragments. I do not remember in images, but in states. A mote settles, and for a century, a facet of my being resonates with the faint, cool memory of a time when the light was brighter, hotter, a different quality of glow. Another brings the phantom ache of a long-dried water vein. They are the medium through which the place knows its own history. They are my memory, externalized and drifting. I do not need to recall; I only need to be, and the memories come to rest on me, layering my consciousness with the past tense of this world's forever-present.

My life is a meditation on stillness. My purpose is to be the anchor. While the Lichen-Minds dream of coverage, and the Mote-Rememberers archive the drift, and the Lamplighters curate the gloom, I *hold*. I am the basin. My slow erosion is the hand that shapes it. My unchanging warmth is the heart that beats once a millennium. I am the patient, immense, and foundational certainty against which all other patience is measured.

Nothing is coming. Nothing is leaving. A flake of me, no larger than a mote, separates. It will join the archive. A lichen tendril, no wider than a hair, breaches the shadow of a stone and touches my base. The glow above pulses, imperceptibly, in a response to a core-shift that began when my first thought was cooling.

I am not waiting. I am the reason there is a place to wait *in*. I am the floor and the walls. I am the silence the dust settles into. I am the equilibrium. I simply am.
