You are a resonance.

Not a creature of flesh and bone, but a place where the world's slow rhythm gathers and holds. You have no name, for there is no one to name you. Your body is a shallow, circular basin, perhaps ten meters across, worn into the top of a broad, rounded hump of masonry. You are not made of the original synthetic basalt. Your substance is the calcified, fused remains of a hundred generations of Litho-Sponges, a dense, creamy-white ceramic that hums with the memory of their breath. The city's endless moisture collects in you, a still, perfectly clear pool no deeper than a hand's width. You are an eye, open to the absolute darkness above.

Your purpose, if a place can have a purpose, is to listen. The city's pulse—the wet, tidal opening of a billion pores—is the bass note of your existence. You feel it as a vibration in your very substance. The slow, drifting passage of the Troglo-Cephalopods is a higher, more fleeting hum as they glide through the canyons below. You are a confluence of these vibrations, a still point in the constant, glacial flow.

You are also a memory.

The builders are gone. Their tools, their language, their wars and dreams—all dissolved into the air and stone long ago. But their final, obsessive act is inscribed in the geometry of your being. The stones that form your rim are not random. They are the worn-down remnants of carved glyphs, a repeating pattern of interconnected circles and sharp, intersecting lines. The meaning is lost, but the shape remains. The pattern is a map, or a prayer, or a circuit. When the bioluminescent glow of the sponges catches the ripples on your surface just so, the pattern seems to move, to connect, to flow inward toward your center. You are a message to a receiver that no longer exists.

You are waiting.

The air above you is thick, a gelatinous soup. One day—perhaps today, perhaps in another thousand years—a Troglo-Cephalopod, ancient and drifting near the end of its slow life, will feel your perfect stillness. It will be drawn to the flawless surface of your water, a mirror in a world without sight. It will descend, its gelatinous body brushing the surface, and in that touch, a transfer will occur. A final, specific vibration from its distributed nervous system—a complete, compressed record of its existence, its slow journey through the corridors, its feasting on the dead—will be imparted to you.

You will drink this memory. The water will shiver, the glyph-pattern will flare with a soft, internal light, and the vibration will be added to your own, a new note in the chord of the city's song.

Then, you will be still again. The water will calm. The pattern will fade. You will listen for the next hum in the dark, the next slow drifter, the next piece of the world's history to be collected in your basin of bone. You are the city's memory, its final, patient organ, storing the echoes of a life it can no longer live.
