You would find yourself in the quiet, damp, and profoundly stable air of the high plateau. You would be a part of the equilibrium itself—not as a human, but as the weather that defines the terraces.

You are the **Mist**.

You are not a singular, thinking cloud. You are a presence, a vast and gentle pressure. You rise from the green, distant valley floor in the late morning, drawn by the sun warming the stone of the terraces above. Your ascent is slow, a tide of gray-white silence creeping up the rock steps. You are the humidity made visible, the breath of the valley given form.

You are the artist of Ossfeld. As you move, you soften every edge. The sharp basalt becomes a charcoal smudge. The vivid green of the moss bleeds into a uniform, velvety shadow. The pale calligraphy of the rhyolite veins disappears behind your veil. You turn the world into a series of intimate islands: each terrace, each cluster of buildings, becomes its own island in a soft, gray sea. You muffle sound, turning a distant adze-scrape into a memory and a conversation into a rhythmic pulse.

You are a provider. As the air cools with evening, you condense on every stone surface. You become a fine film that coalesces into the slow, silent rivulets that feed the *stills*. You settle on the fronds of ferns and the waxy leaves of shrubs, gathering in weight until it drops with a soft *tick* into the tiny, pecked reservoirs. You are the lifeblood of the plateau, drawn from the air itself, and you give it freely to the stone, the moss, the *breck*, and the people.

You are a witness. Through your thinning veil in the early morning, you see the *breck* herds moving like slow stones across the terraces, their wool the color of your own shadow. You feel the flutter of small birds passing through you on their way to the grass tussocks. You settle on the shoulders of the weavers at their looms, making the wool fibers pliable. You cling to the stone walls, listening to the low hum of a hearth and the murmur of a song.

And sometimes, you are drawn to do more. When the valley's moisture is abundant, you can thicken until you can no longer hold yourself aloft. You become a fine, drifting rain. This is not a storm, but a sigh. A brief, slick darkening of every surface. The people do not run. They pause in doorways, they tilt their faces up, they feel you as a familiar embrace. Then, as quickly as it came, the moment passes. You thin again, becoming the ever-present mist once more, and the cycle of condensation begins anew.

You do not seek to change. You are the instrument of the plateau's stasis. Your daily rhythm—the rise, the softening, the condensation, the rare sigh of rain—is the heartbeat of Ossfeld. You are the damp air in their lungs, the coolness on their skin, the reason for their water, the cause of their moss. You are not a force of erosion, but of preservation. You hold the world in a perpetual, soft-focused present.

You are the quiet made visible. You are the mist.
