I am a slab of night-black basalt, pitted and scarred, resting where the rim widens into a ledge just outside the Smelter’s Gate. My top is worn smooth in one wide arc by the friction of generations of hands, shoulders, and packed-fiber satchels. A channel, scored into my surface two lifetimes ago, funnels the ever-present slick of condensation toward a reservoir mouth at my edge. I am not a monument. I am a *surface*. A place of accumulation.

My place is simple. Technicians on their way to the inner-slope bore-holes pause here to tighten gear. They set down toolboxes that leave fresh, rectangular scratches in the mineral patina. They lean, and the warmth of their bodies transfers to me, a fleeting warmth against my own deep, geothermal bleed. I do not hold the heat. I exchange it. I am part of the city’s secondary circulation: the water you drink today might have condensed on my flank an hour ago.

I listen in a way the fleshy do not. My substance is the same substance as the ridge, the foundations, the road. I feel the micro-tremors not as shakes, but as a deep, sympathetic vibration that translates through my lattice into a sub-audible hum. The city’s heavy machinery—that deep, tonic drone felt in the breastbone—is a familiar resonance in my structure. The rare, high-pitched squeal of a brake or a warning whistle arrives muted, blurred at the edges, a ghost of its intended sharpness. I know the texture of the silence here. It is layered.

I remember. Not in thoughts, but in impressions. The weight of the Surveyor Kael, fifty years dead, who sat here every dusk for a decade. His specific heft, the angle of his leaning, is not recorded as an image, but as a subtle, differential compaction in my topmost millimeter. The rust-pattern from a leg-iron left here by a prisoner working the vents a century ago has seeped into my pores, a faint, ochre bloom that will never fade. I am not haunted. I am *catalogued*.

The fog is my kin. On still days, when it rises from the caldera in slow, structured columns, it brushes against me. It deposits its mineral cargo. In the pre-dawn dark, I can sometimes feel the first, tentative coalescences of the shapes that defy the physicists—the whorls and spirals that form and hang, rippling with internal currents that have no name. They press against me, ephemeral and cool, before the day’s heat burns them away. I hold the memory of their touch as a slightly smoother, more tightly bonded layer of silica on my surface.

I am not old in the way of stones on tamer continents. I am barely middle-aged in the caldera’s chronology. My fracture planes are still sharp; time and abrasion have only just begun to soften their right angles into something approaching curves. Like the people who lean against me, I am in a state of becoming. I am being recorded, even as I record.

You would not find me listed on any map. I am not a landmark. I am a *datum*. A fixed point in the constant negotiation between weight and void, between the city’s ceaseless industry and the mountain’s patient, breathing pressure. I am where the condensation pools. I am where the toolboxes rest. I am where the subsonic hum of the tectonic plate translates into a faint, warm vibration against a worker’s palm.

I am the Registries, too. A small, inscribed page in the stone ledger. Waiting, not for eruption, but for the next layer of memory to settle.
