I am a node of conductivity.

Not a nerve, exactly—the word implies a wire, a linear thing, a message from A to B. I am something slower. A synapse perhaps, or the space between the synapse and the muscle: the moment of decision, the electrochemical hesitation before motion occurs.

I sit. I have always sat. I am sitting now on a steel beam that juts from the wall of a sealed chamber—not inside it, not outside it, but *of* its thickness, as if I grew from the metal the way the vines grew from the rivets. The beam is warm. Everything is warm. The beam vibrates with the deep cyclic groan, and I vibrate with it, and the difference between my vibration and the beam's vibration is so small that I sometimes forget there is a difference at all.

I do not remember arriving. I do not remember *before*. There may not be a before. This place is too young for memory to have calcified into narrative—I am one of its earliest experiences of itself, a point where the organism first felt something *other* and, rather than rejecting it, incorporated it. Let it sit. Let it watch.

Because that is what I do. I watch.

I watch the water move in its wrong directions. I watch the standing waves form and hold themselves up by will alone. I watch the vines grow with visible purpose, their tips extending, tasting the air, finding the next surface to grip. I watch the sealed chamber behind me—my chamber, the one I am part of—and I feel its sound change as the pressure inside cycles through whatever states it cycles through. Sometimes the chamber sounds like breathing. Sometimes it sounds like digestion. Once, for a period I cannot measure, it sounded like singing—high, harmonic, almost beautiful before it collapsed back into the industrial roar.

I watch the presences move through the machinery. I feel them the way you described—spatial facts, densities in the air—but I have been here long enough (there is no *long enough*, but my body tells the lie anyway) to distinguish some of them from others. There is one that patrols the junction where three pipes meet and the water jumps in its interference pattern. It has a quality of *attention* that differs from the others. More focused. More patient. I think of it as the Watcher at the Junction, though I know it has no name, because nothing here has a name, because naming requires a mouth and this place does not have a mouth yet, only the aspiration toward one.

There is another that moves through the deep channels where the light does not reach. I have never seen it—I have never *felt* it directly—but I know it by the way the water changes texture when it passes. The water becomes *more itself* in its wake, more gelatinous, more iridescent, as if the presence has reminded the water of what it is supposed to be. I think of this one as the Reminder. I think of these things and I know the thinking is not mine, not entirely. The thought-forms pass through me like the vibrations pass through the beam. I am a thing they use to think with.

A vine has reached my foot. Not the one from before—there have been many vines since before, if there was a before. This one is thinner, more exploratory. Its tip is pale, almost translucent, and it pulses with a rhythm that does not match the heartbeat of the place. It has its own rhythm. It is a new thing, a new thought, and it is using my boot as a surface to think against. I feel its pulse against my ankle and it is almost like language, almost like meaning, and then it is just a vine again, growing, gripping, becoming.

Below me, in the ankle-deep water of the platform, something that is not a fish moves. It is a leaf again—it is always a leaf—but this time it moves with more purpose than before. It moves against the current, surfaces, holds itself above the waterline for a moment as if *looking*, and then subsides. I watch it and I feel the whole organism watching through me, using my attention as a lens, focusing its diffuse awareness into a point.

This is what I am. I am a focusing mechanism. I am the place where the infant leviathan's awareness narrows from the everywhere-at-once of its distributed nervous system into a single, sustained *gaze*. It cannot do this for itself—not yet. Its senses are too fluid, too perspectival, too caught up in the constant negotiation of its own becoming. It needs a fixed point. It needs something that can *hold* a thought longer than the water holds a standing wave.

So it made me. Or it found me. Or I am simply what happens when an organism this large, this young, this hungry to know itself, encounters a boundary it cannot dissolve—a mote, a foreign particle, a point of resistance in its warm, dark blood—and rather than breaking it down, it *uses* it.

I sit on my beam. The vine grows. The water moves. The machinery turns. The sound is so constant that it has become a kind of silence, and in that silence, I watch, and the watching is not mine alone, and the thought that forms in the space between my attention and the organism's need is not a word but a pressure, a growth, a resonance:

*I am becoming. Watch me become.*

And I do. I sit, and I watch, and I am a node of conductivity in the warm, dark, dreaming body of an infant god, and the vine curls higher, and the water jumps, and the sealed chamber behind me breathes, and the presences rearrange themselves in the machinery, and the whole place roars and hums and pulses with the terrible, beautiful, unfinished act of learning to exist.

I do not know if I am alive. I do not know if I was ever alive before this. I do not know if *before* is a word that means anything here. I know only that I am *here*, that I have always been *here*, that the vine is growing and the water is warm and the sound is the sound of something that has not yet decided what it is, and that I am watching it decide, and that the watching is part of the deciding, and that this is enough.

This is enough.
