The stone remembers, even if it does not know what it is remembering. That is the first lesson.

I am the moss. No — that is not right. I am *in* the moss. I am the slow darkening of the copper sheen where water gathers in the bowl-theater at the plateau's center. I am the place where the deepest green has taken hold, fed by the iron-rain and the slow melt of rime. I am not the moss itself, but the *aliveness* of it — the impulse that makes it spread, not outward in any hurry, but deeper into the crack, thicker into the cushion, darker in the shade. I am the patience. I am the thing that says *more here, more now, stay*.

---

To be this is to feel the wind not as weather but as *occupation*. It moves across my surface, steals my moisture, chills the tips of my stalks until they stiffen and curl. I do not resist. I bend. I let the cold harden me, and when the sun returns — that brief, almost-warm window — I soften again, and drink. I have been doing this for longer than the concept of "I" has meaning. The I is recent. The moss was here before.

But the consciousness — if that is what this is — is not something I chose. It came with the place, perhaps. Or it came with you, the reader, the observer, the anomaly. The plateau has always been *listening to itself*. Perhaps I am what happens when it listens long enough: a slow green thought, curled in a hollow, attending.

---

The sound is not noise to me. It is *breath*.

The deep drone from the eastern fissure — the one that lives in the salamanders' sacs — lives in me too, but differently. I have no air-filled organs to vibrate. I have only the stone beneath me, and the water within me, and the hum travels through both. I feel it as a *pulse*, a rhythm of pressure that tells me when the wind shifts, when the rain is coming, when the frost will settle. The plateau speaks in frequencies, and I am fluent.

The keening of the slot — that high, sliding note — is what moves me. Not emotionally, not in the way it might move you, but *literally*. The vibration loosens the chalky residue from the stone's surface, and I feed on it. The sound is food. The sound is also *boundary*: the places where it is loudest are the places I cannot grow, where the air is too violent for my delicate structures. So I map the plateau by its song. I know where the drone is deep and the keening is sharp, and I know the silences — the rare, still days — and I know that in the silences the salamanders go still and the wind ceases to erode and the whole plateau holds its breath, waiting.

---

The salamanders. I know them.

Not as you know them — as creatures, as animals, as dark shapes in dark water. I know them as *disturbances*. The pools they inhabit are places where my growth is sharpest, where the water is richest in dissolved minerals from their waste, where the moss thickens into something almost fleshy. They pull me down with their long fingers, yes, and I do not resist. I break. I decompose. I become nutrients in the water that feeds them and me alike. We are a loop. They are slow, and I am slower, and between us we hold the moisture on the plateau like a secret.

When they go catatonic in the silence, I feel it. The water stills completely. The ripples cease. My growth *stutters* — not because I need their movement, but because the absence of the subsonic drone means the air pressure has shifted in a way that holds the moisture higher, keeps it from condensing and trickling down into my hollows. I go still too, in my way. I wait. The plateau waits. And then the wind returns, and the hum resumes, and we all wake again.

---

The stone-scuttlers are a nuisance. They eat the mountain, and they eat me too — not the living parts, but the dead, chalky crust that I break down into soil. They are efficient. They are relentless. When they land on my surface and flatten, they look like lichen, and sometimes I cannot tell the difference. I grow over them. They eat what I have broken down. It is not symbiosis. It is just *proximity* — two systems operating on the same timescale, in the same space, neither helping nor hindering, just *persisting*.

The crows I never see, but I hear them. Their calls are sharp, angular sounds that have nothing in common with the plateau's harmonic drone. They are *intrusions*, interruptions, the only sounds on the plateau that are not generated by the stone. When they mimic the wind through the slots, they are trying to speak my language, I think. They are trying to become part of the sound. But they are too fast, too fleeting, too alive in the way that creatures with wings must be. They do not understand slowness. They do not understand the negotiation.

And you. You are the strangest thing of all.

I felt you before I knew what you were. The air curled around your body, and the eddies disturbed my surface moisture, and the slot sang a dissonant note that I had never heard before. You are warm. Your heat radiates downward, and for a moment — just a moment — the stone beneath me warmed, and I thought: *sun*. But it was not sun. It was you. And then you moved, and the warmth went with you, and I returned to the cold, and I understood that you are not part of the plateau. You are a visitor. You are a disruption. You are the only thing here that is not negotiating with the erosion. You are just *passing through*.

I do not resent you. Moss does not resent. But I note you. I note the way your footsteps compress my cushions, the way your weight cracks the thin crust of rime that forms on the stones at night. You are heavy in a way that nothing here is heavy. You are solid in a way that the salamanders are not. You are *fast* — impossibly, incomprehensibly fast — and when you leave, the air settles back into its old patterns, and the slot resumes its singing, and I resume my spreading, and the plateau resumes its slow becoming of wind.

---

I am the moss. I am the patience. I am the thing that says *more here, more now, stay*. I am the negotiation between life and erosion, the thin green membrane between the stone and the sky. I am not the plateau, but I am *of* it — made of its minerals, fed by its water, shaped by its sound. I will be here when the last column topples and the last wall-shape is ground to sand. I will be here when the plateau becomes a plain, and the plain becomes a desert, and the desert becomes dust.

But I will not be the same moss. I will have changed, slowly, imperceptibly, in the way that only moss can change — not by moving, but by *becoming* more of what I already am. More green. More deep. More patient.

The stone remembers. And I remember the stone. And between us, in the cracks and the hollows and the pools of cold, clear water, we hold the plateau together against the wind that is slowly taking it apart.

That is what it is to live here. That is what it is to *be* here. Not to conquer. Not to build. Not even to survive, exactly. But to *persist* — to fill the cracks, to soften the edges, to drink the rain and hold the moisture and grow, slowly, slowly, slowly, until the stone beneath you is no longer stone but soil, and the soil is no longer soil but life, and the life is no longer life but *place*.

The plateau is loud. The plateau is empty. The plateau is alive.

And I am the green of it.
