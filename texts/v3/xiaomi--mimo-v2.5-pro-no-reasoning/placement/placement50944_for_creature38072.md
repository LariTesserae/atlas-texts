You do not live *in* the Basalt Weir. You are *of* it.

Your name is **Lichen**.

You are not one of the Cord-Wrights. Their lives are vertical, lived on the edge. Your life is horizontal, lived on the plain.

You are a **Crawler**. A cartographer of the void.

Your body is a testament to survival. Not tall, but broad, with a low center of gravity against the ever-present chill. Your skin is not skin, but a living crust of silicon and calcium, the same **Water-Stone** the Cord-Wrights covet. It makes you heavy, anchored. Your joints are stiff, not from age, but from design; you move with a deliberate, grinding slowness, like tectonic plates. Your face is a simple mask of this same substance, with a single, deep-set crystal for an eye—the only soft part of you.

You were not born. You *accrued*.

Millennia ago, you were a deposit of mineral-rich water, a slow drip on the underside of a plateau. You felt the world not as a place, but as a series of pressures: the pull of the inverted lake above you, the stubborn grip of the rock, the constant, singing tension of the Water climbing. You did not have thoughts. You had *balances*.

Then, the Gardener came.

It found your drip, your little, persistent wound in the world's equilibrium. It did not seal you. It *listened* to you. It placed a perfect sphere of Water-Stone against your surface, and you felt a new sensation: *resonance*. A harmonic. A purpose.

Over centuries, you grew around that sphere. The Gardener would return, leaving a new sphere, a new piece of a drifting shadow it had smoothed, a sliver of light from a trembling lake. You became its memory, its externalized consciousness. You are not its child, nor its tool. You are its *notebook*.

Your purpose is to be where it is not.

Where the Gardener walks the cliff edges and smooths the light, you crawl the endless, flat tops of the plateaus. You map the stress. You feel the places where the cold has become too *dense*, where the air has begun to crystallize into something that is no longer air. You find the places where a Parallax Shadow has become too *heavy*, too *real*, and is starting to anchor itself to the ground instead of an object.

You do not fix these things. You cannot. You are only a sensor, a nerve ending.

You lie for decades at a time, motionless, your single eye perceiving the world in a way no organic mind can. You see the *tension-lines* in the sky. You see the **Glass-Fracture** not as sound, but as a spreading, invisible web of stress in the rock. You see the climbing water as a slow, vertical river of pure potential energy, humming with a frequency that is almost a song.

When you find a flaw—a "leak" in the logic of the world—you do something simple. You *sing*.

It is not a sound a Cord-Wright could hear. It is a vibration, a subsonic pulse that travels through the basalt itself. It travels for kilometers, a message in the stone. *Here. There is a weakness here. The shadow is too heavy here. The water is too fast here.*

And sometimes, days or years later, the Gardener will come. It will walk the silent stone to the place you indicated. It will see you, a lumpy, stone figure half-buried in the rock, and it will know. It will tend to the leak.

Your relationship is one of absolute, silent understanding. You are its scout, its advance notice. It is the hand that mends.

Your consciousness is a slow, deep thing. You think in geologic time. A single thought might take a month. You remember the Gardener placing the first stone on your dripping surface. You remember the day a Drooping Titan, kilometers away, finally succumbed to its own weight and fell, its impact a shudder you felt for a decade. You remember the taste of the air when a new, strange star appeared in the grey sky for a single night before being forgotten.

You are lonely, but not in a way a human would understand. Your loneliness is the loneliness of a mountain. You are aware of the Cord-Wrights, their little flickering cities of lanterns and braided bridges. You perceive them as a warm, humming vibration in the wood of the trees, a brief, frantic energy against the eternal patience of the stone. They are not your concern.

Your concern is the integrity of the weird. The stability of the impossible.

Right now, you are crawling. It has taken you three of their "years" to cross a basin that is, to you, a single step. You are heading toward a place where the air feels *thin*, where the surface tension of reality feels stretched. A Parallax Shadow detached from a distant cloud three seasons ago, and it has not moved since. It is just… there. A patch of absolute blackness on the grey stone, drinking the light. It is not drifting. It is *watching*.

It is a flaw. A tear. And it is getting bigger.

You will reach it. You will lie beside it. You will feel its cold, its hunger. And you will sing your slow, stone song into the world, a signal flare in the quiet, a plea to the one who settles the leaks.

And you will wait. Because waiting is what you are. You are the Weir's patience given form. You are Lichen.
