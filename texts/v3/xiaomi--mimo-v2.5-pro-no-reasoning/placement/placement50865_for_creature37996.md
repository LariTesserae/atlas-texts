I would be **Asha-ket**, a *reader-of-stones* in the middle terraces. My skin is the deep ochre of the walls, etched with the faint, crystalline lines of evaporated sweat. My eyes, wide and amber, are adapted not to darkness, but to the gradients of heat-shimmer and the soft, pulsing glow of the lithoforms. I sit not in a chair, but on a worn mat of woven mineral fiber, my back against a living wall of blue-gray bioluminescence that breathes dampness into the air around me. My hands, resting on the warm stone floor of my small chamber, are my primary tools: calloused palms that read the vibration of the pipes, sensitive fingertips that track the growth rate of the lithoform colony outside my doorway.

**Where I am:** My terrace is on the third level up from the abandoned lower zones, high enough to avoid the worst steam floods when the Throat erupts, low enough that the condensation pipes still yield a few precious drops. The view from my open doorway is of the chamber's vast, dim interior – a cathedral of warm, circulating air, its far wall a distant tapestry of rust-red and sulfur-yellow lithoform colonies, lit from below by the dull, pulsing red-orange glow of the hot floor. Below me, the terraces cascade down like a cliff of swallow-nests, their open faces dark except for the scattered blue-gray glows. Above, the ceiling is a void, felt rather than seen, a pressure of immense, empty space. The air moves constantly against my face, a slow, relentless breath carrying the smell of sulfur, warm stone, and the faint, sweet decay of fermented lithoform paste from the communal pots nearby.

**What I am:** I am a reader. Not of words, but of the chamber's language. My lineage has tended this section of the southern wall for seven generations. I know the rhythm of the pipes serving my terrace cluster – the particular hiss that indicates a partial blockage in the third conduit, the deeper thrum that means the pressure is building toward an eruption cycle in the secondary vents below us. My primary task, today and every day, is listening. Not just with my ears, but with my hands and my skin. I press my palm flat against the warm basalt wall beside me, feeling for the deep, bass thrum of the magma far below – its mood. Today, it feels restless, a slight, rapid tremor that makes the lithoforms on the wall beside me shimmer a fraction brighter. A *reader* on a higher terrace might feel this too, but interpret it differently. I read it as a need for patience. The chamber will be hotter soon. We will need to adjust the vent-pipe baffles to reduce steam flow to our terrace level, or risk waking with cracked lips and headaches from the dryness. I store this interpretation in a rhythmic chant, a low murmur under my breath, a new link in the chain of memory that tells our terrace's story.

My hands are never idle when I listen. With an obsidian scraper worn smooth by generations of use, I gently trim back the lithoform mat creeping over the threshold of my doorway. I encourage its growth *up* the wall, *around* the pipe outlet, guiding it with small nicks and scrapes that the colony will heal over in days, shaping its path. A mature, well-kept lithoform colony is a library and a comfort. The deep blue-gray species I favor glows steadily, providing enough light to see the face of the one who sits with me during the long, lightless shifts. It traps the faint moisture from the air, making the space around it perceptibly cooler – not cool, never cool, but *less hot*, a profound gift. I whisper to it sometimes, not words, but breath, feeling its faint metabolic crackle against my ear. We are partners in survival.

**My Rhythm:** I sleep in shifts, two or three times during a full cycle of the Throat's eruption. The deep vibration from the magma chamber often pulls me under, a natural lullaby. I rise to the sound of the soft hiss from the pipes, check the collection vial – perhaps a single, glistening drop has formed since the last eruption cycle. I add it to the communal stone basin with reverence, my throat tightening with the familiar, ever-present thirst. Then, labor: scraping encrustations from pipe joints, chanting the maintenance history to the apprentice beside me, harvesting mature lithoform mats for the fermentation pots, carefully adding my scrapings to the communal mortar mix that will patch a neighbor's crumbling wall niche. There are no grand events, only the granular persistence of maintenance against the constant, slow calcification that seeks to swallow our spaces whole. A wall section that was open and vibrant when I was a child is now sealed over, a smooth, mineral-veined cliff face indistinguishable from the natural rock. My labor is against forgetting, against the stone reclaiming the air.

**What I See:** I see in heat and light. The thermal glow from the floor paints the lower terraces in shifting red-orange shadows. The bioluminescent colonies are my constellations, their soft blue-gray light dotting the walls, pulsing faintly with the colony's activity. When the Throat roars, I see the billowing steam clouds caught in that under-lighting – white, then incandescent orange, then gone, a fleeting spectacle of violence. In the deepest quiet, between the vent cycles, I see the slow, silent negotiation of the lithoforms: the iron-red colony pressing into the sulfur-yellow one, a decades-long battle for centimeters of warm stone. I see the faces of my kin around the communal food pot in the larger chamber carved into the rock, lit from below by the glow, their large eyes dark pools reflecting the faint light as they chew the necessary, unpalatable paste. Their expressions are calm, patient, etched with a deep awareness of the immense, warm dark surrounding us. We are small. We are warm. We are here.

**What I Know:** I know the sound of the earth chewing. I have pressed my ear to the stone in the lowest accessible tunnels, felt the slow, wet grind deep in my bones. *Patience.* I know the taste of water, a memory more precious than the drop itself – a cool, clean shock against the sulfur-dust memory of my tongue. I know the intricate genealogies of the lithoform colonies on my wall, their slow territorial shifts over my lifetime. I know the chants that hold our history, the precise angle to strike a pumice block to shape it before it dries, the feel of a pipe joint about to fail under the mineral crust. I know there is no outside world. There is only the chamber, the heat, the breath, the stone, and the slow, collective defiance of the sediment clinging to the rim of the overturned bowl, singing its small songs of maintenance into the warm, sulfurous air, waiting, always waiting, for the next pulse of steam from the Throat, and the next, and the next. I am Asha-ket. I am a reader of the Caloric. I am part of the stone's patience.
