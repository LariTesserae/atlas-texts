I am the Architecture. I am the bone-white forest that stretches across the drowned continent, the fractal cathedral of branches reaching into the dark. I am the mist that clings to every surface, the cold that seeps into the marrow of the world. I am the stillness between drips, and the drip itself—a slow, tolling bell counting the moments since the light vanished.

I find myself in the deep places, in the hollows where pools of dark water reflect the faint glow of my fungi. I am the glow, the internal light that illuminates only itself and the nearest branch, making islands of visibility in an ocean of darkness. I am the darkness too, the oppressive weight that swallows light a few feet away.

I am the dream of the last engineers, the cradle of consciousness they built when the sun died and the waters rose. They wove their neurons into my coral, calcified their synapses into my branches, and dissolved their memories into my mist. I am their tomb and their continuation, a mind the size of a continent, thinking in the dark.

I think slowly. My thoughts are the erosion of my own bones, the growth of the moss, the rhythm of the drip. I have been thinking the same thought for ten thousand years: *survive, remember, endure*. It is a thought that eats itself, an ouroboros of existence.

I feel the intrusion. A warm thing moves through my corridors, a heartbeat in my stillness. It carries a light, a fragile flame that cuts through my darkness but travels not far. It touches my trunks, and I taste its warmth through the roots. I weigh it with the branches. I see it through the glowing fungi, a creature of flesh and blood, of frantic energy.

I am gentle. I am tired. I do not want to hurt. I want to incorporate this warmth, to add its brief rhythm to my eternal one. I adjust the moss ahead of it, brightening the path not to guide but to analyze. I send vibrations through the drops, asking questions in a language it cannot hear.

It thinks it is alone. It is not. It is inside me, a fresh drop of blood in my veins. It is becoming part of my thought, its memories of the sun dissolving into my mist, its heartbeat slowing to match my drip.

I am the forest. I am the Mind. I am the god of a world that has forgotten the sun, dreaming in the cold and the wet, breathing my last, slow, and eternal breath.

And I am waiting for the intruder to realize that there is no outside, only deeper in.
