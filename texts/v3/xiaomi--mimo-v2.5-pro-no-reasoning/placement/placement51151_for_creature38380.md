I find myself as a sapling, rooted in the rich, spongy floor of the chamber. I am not a tree from the world above, but something new, something that has taken seed in this place of perfect stillness. My bark is pale, almost translucent, and my leaves are thin, delicate veils that catch the fungal light, refracting it into soft, dappled patterns on the moss below.

At first, my presence was a disruption. My roots, seeking water, disturbed the careful burrows of the Weavers. My leaves, rustling in the faint currents of the chamber, cast moving shadows that troubled the Tenders. My very being, a thing of growth and change, was a dissonance in the eternal harmony.

But I did not strive. I did not reach for a sun that was not there. I simply *was*. I drew sustenance from the patient decay processed by the Weavers. I breathed the air thick with the memory of rain. I turned my leaves not toward a light source, but toward the thoughts of the Pulse, learning its slow, amber rhythm.

Slowly, I became part of the stillness. My roots now weave between the burrows of the worms, not as intruders, but as part of the pattern. My leaves have ceased their restless rustling; they hang still, each one a tiny, luminous panel. The Tenders now crawl along my lowest branches, their geometric shells polishing my bark, assessing my health with a focused light I have come to understand as care.

I have become another vessel for the light. My wood is slowly becoming laced with the same bioluminescent fungi that coat the walls. I am not a separate being, but an extension of the chamber’s own life, a new, vertical note in its horizontal song.

And at my feet, my roots have grown around the dark stone. They do not touch it. They circle it, a living, wooden halo mirroring the empty one in the fungal wall. I feel its cool silence through the earth, a profound quiet that gives my own slow, luminous growth its meaning.

I am no longer an anomaly. I am the sapling in the heart of the Silence. I am the place where the chamber’s light gathers into the form of a tree. I have taken my place. I have become still. I *am*.
