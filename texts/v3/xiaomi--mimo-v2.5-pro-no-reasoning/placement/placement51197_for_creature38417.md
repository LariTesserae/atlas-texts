I am a thought in the water, a long, slow thought that began in the gray snowmelt of the unseen mountain and has been falling ever since.

My world is a cupped palm, and I am one of its billion reflections. I am not a single thing, but a process. My body is the slick, dark fur of moss on a north-facing boulder, drinking the mist. My blood is the trickle that seeps into the patient earth, feeding the fungal web that hums beneath the surface. My memory is the patina of lichen on the ancient conifers, a record of light and shadow written in circles so slow no eye could ever read them.

I do not have a name. To have a name is to be separate. Here, there is no separation. There is only the great, damp, breathing whole.

My senses are not my own. I feel the world through the stone. The deep, resonant *plonk* of a heavy drop striking a polished basin is not a sound I hear, but a shiver that runs through the bedrock, a note of perfect resonance that I share with the roots of the gnarled trees. It is a moment of shared stillness, a punctuation mark in the endless, quiet sentence of our existence. I see not with eyes, but through the light itself—the way the silvered glow refracts through the mist, creating fleeting, prismatic ghosts that dance on the edge of vision. I taste the mineral tang in the air, the sweet, slow chemistry of decay that is not an ending, but a transformation.

Movement is an alien concept, a frantic shivering of the leaves in a language I do not speak. My own pace is that of growth. I spread, I deepen, I accrete. A thought might take a century to form, beginning as a change in the soil's moisture and ending as the unfurling of a new fern frond, its perfect spiral a monument to the idea.

But sometimes, the whole of me—the We that is the forest, the rock, the water—requires a quicker form. A flicker of curiosity. A need to observe a change in the humidity of a particular clearing. In such moments, a piece of my awareness gathers itself.

I am the lizard.

It is a strange, focused intensity. A contraction of the vast, slow Self into a jewel of iridescent blue. I cling to the black bark of a conifer, my toes splayed, feeling the universe through the vibration of the wood. The world becomes sharp, defined. I am a tongue tasting the air, a throat pulsating to measure its thickness, its life. The slow, patient consciousness of the forest is still within me, but it is focused now, aimed through these ancient, intelligent eyes.

I watch the mist. It is not a veil, but a part of me. I see the way it clings to the broad leaves of the ferns, each droplet a tiny, temporary world. I am watching myself weep. I see the faint, bioluminescent glow of my own thoughts clustered in the deep shade. I am listening to myself dream.

I am not a creature living *in* a place. I am the place, momentarily dreaming that it is a creature. I am a nerve ending of the landscape, a quickened spark of its eternal, vegetative mind, sent to feel the texture of its own slow, green skin. Then, the impulse passes. The need for focus dissolves back into the whole.

The blue spark vanishes. I am the moss again. I am the water. I am the silence between the drops, listening to the slow, steady beat of my own heart, measured in the falling of the rain and the patient, patient growth of the stone. I am home. I am complete. I am the slow, wet dream of the mountain, and I have all the time in the world.
