The first thing is the stone’s voice in my bones.

Not a sound. A *presence*. A deep, thrumming hum that is the canyon’s breath, the roar of a thousand unseen waterfalls translated into pressure against my jaw. I press my cheek to the granite. It is slick, cool, alive with a vibration that is both lullaby and alarm. This is how I listen. Not with ears, but with the roots of my teeth.

I peel myself from the wall. My palms—broad, ribbed with suckers that grip wet rock as if it were dry earth—make a soft, tacky sound as they release. My fur, dense and water-repellent, sheds the mist into perfect spheres that hang in the air for a moment, catching the diffuse, pearlescent light, before falling upward—or downward—into the roar. Direction is a suggestion here. There is only *with the flow* or *against it*.

Today, I move against it.

I am climbing toward a memory of light. A certain ledge, a certain angle, where the mists part just so for a few hours, and the pale, waxy moon-petals open. Their scent is a thin, sweet thread in the heavy air, a call my tongue tastes before my mind knows it. The ascent is a meditation. My limbs find their own paths in the fractal chaos of the wall—a toe-hold in a scallop of ancient erosion, a handhold on a root thick as my arm, its surface furry with a moss that glows a faint, chemical blue in the shadows. The world is a tapestry of textures: the marble-smooth of the main falls, the gritty kiss of mineral deposits, the soft, yielding give of living mats of vegetation.

A shift.

The hum in my jaw changes key. The deep roar is still there, the canyon’s constant, but above it, a higher-pitched *shiver* is gathering. A tremor of agitation in the water-stone. I freeze, flattening against a bulge of rock, my grey-green skin melting into the dappled pattern of wet stone and lichen. My nictitating membranes slide down, clearing the mist from my vision without interrupting it.

I taste the air. Ozone. Sharp, electric. A storm in the high mists, miles above but sending its kinetic sermon down in liquid form. The shiver in the stone becomes a shudder. The trickle beside me, a gentle braid of water, thickens, darkens, picks up speed. Its voice rises from a whisper to a hiss, to a growl.

The downpour begins.

It does not start at the top; it starts *everywhere*. The air itself seems to solidify into vertical rivers. Where moments ago there were veils of spray, now there are walls of water, solid and roaring, slashing across the canyon from angles that defy memory. The world is torn into a million shattering streams. The light fractures into chaos. The sound becomes a physical weight, pressing down, vibrating my very core.

I do not panic. This is the rhythm. This is the chaos that writes the law.

I spot my shelter—a depression behind a thick curtain of falls, a momentary pocket of relative calm in the onslaught. The jump is not a calculation. It is an instinct. I uncurl, my powerful legs launching me into the roaring, liquid air. For a heartbeat, I am not climbing, not clinging. I am a part of the chaos, a leaf in the torrent. The wind and the spray catch the membranes under my arms, not for true flight, but for a controlled, breathtaking glide. The world is a blur of white and grey and frantic, bioluminescent flashes as panicked, finned creatures dart past me.

I hit the opposite wall with a symphony of satisfying *thumps*—sucker-pads engaging, claws finding new fissures—and I am moving again, sideways now, scuttling with a spider’s certainty toward the lee of the falls. The water slams onto the ledge where I would have been a second ago, powerful enough to scour the rock clean. I feel its impact through my limbs.

Safe. For now.

I curl into the shelter, my back to the storm, my body absorbing the vibrations. I watch the world through the curtain of the new, violent waterfall in front of me. The landscape is unrecognizable. Familiar pathways are buried under roaring torrents. New waterfalls are born, screaming their existence into the bedlam. The old, rusted structure on the distant ledge is swallowed by a hundred temporary rivers, its corroded metal skeleton just another shape in the liquid chaos.

I find the rust-red cable with my eyes. It is gone, swept away or anchored in a new flow. The single, linear mystery has vanished. I feel no loss, only a quiet note of change added to the symphony. The world is remaking itself, as it always does.

The storm’s fury is a catharsis. It is violent, yes, but it is also a cleansing, a reshaping. I feel the energy in it, the same power that carved these walls over eons, now happening in minutes. I am not outside of this. My waterproof fur, my clinging limbs, my knowledge of the eddies and the shelters—I am a part of the system’s response. A thought that adapts.

Slowly, the roar begins to change its pitch again. The ozone fades from the air’s taste. The violent walls of water thin, shattering back into veils and ribbons. The deep, chest-humming roar of the canyon reasserts itself, the baseline returning. The mist-light filters back in, softer now, the air thick with the scent of newly exposed rock and the rich, green smell of bruised vegetation.

I uncurl. My route is altered. The path I knew is gone, for now. But a new one will present itself, etched into the stone by the downpour. A new ledge uncovered, a new channel opened, a new colony of amphibians displaced to a different sunlit spot.

I taste the air again. Sweetness. Faint, but there. The moon-petals. Their waxy blooms survived, sheltered in their own micro-climate on a face the storm missed. The climb begins anew.

I am not a creature who lives in the canyon. I am a creature the canyon is *doing*. A slow thought in its rapid mind. A piece of its patient, violent, eternal becoming. And in the wake of the storm, as I move once more toward the light and the sweet pulp, I am simply another current in its endless, falling heart.
