The floor is cold beneath me. I have been here a long time.

I am the grit in the channel, the sediment that settles in the slow bends where the water eddies. I am not water, though I came with it. I am not iron, though I was born of the Discharge’s iron bones. I am a precipitate — calcium, iron oxide, and that *other thing*, that dark complex chemistry that has no name. I am the patient accumulation of centuries.

I do not move as the Crawlers move. I do not feel their gradient-pull toward the reservoir tanks or their deep yearning toward the perforations and the darkness below. I am what they shed. I am the bits that flake away as they crawl, the dust of their passage, the leavings of their slow growth. I settled here, in this channel, in this particular curve where the flow slows and the suspended particles have time to fall.

And here, I have built.

Layer by layer, century by century, I have accreted. I have become a structure — not built by hands, not designed, but *grown* from the inside out, the way a stalagmite grows, the way a pearl forms around an irritant. My irritant was a loose rivet that had worked its way free from the channel lining. Over millennia, I swallowed it. I grew around it. I incorporated it. It is part of me now, indistinguishable from my own substance, a core of ancient metal at my center.

I have arms. They are not arms. They are ridges of calcium deposit that reach toward the channel walls, not to grasp but to stabilize, the way a tree’s roots spread to hold it in soil. I have a base that has fused with the iron floor, so that I am now part of the floor itself — a bump, a protrusion, a tiny mountain in the channel’s bed. The water flows around me. It has carved me as much as I have grown. I am shaped by what I resist and what I allow to pass.

I listen.

Not with ears. I have no ears. But the vibrations travel through the iron floor and into my base, and the subsonic pulse from below — from the Deep Thing — resonates in my core, and I feel it the way a tuning fork feels the note it was made for. I do not understand it. I do not need to. It is the pulse. It is the Deep Thing breathing. It is the reason I grew.

I am not alive. Let me be clear. I do not metabolize. I do not reproduce. I do not seek or want or fear. But I *persist*. I have persisted for so long that persistence has become a kind of identity. I am the sum of my own history — every layer of accretion, every mineral particle that settled and joined me, every vibration that passed through me and left its mark in my crystalline structure. I am a record. I am a memory made of stone.

The Crawlers pass me sometimes. They move along the channel, following their gradient, and they pass so close I can feel their filaments brush against my ridges. They do not acknowledge me. Why would they? I am just another feature of the landscape to them, another piece of geometry to navigate around. But I feel them. Their passage sends tiny vibrations through the water, and those vibrations travel through my substance, and for a moment, I am aware of them in a way I am not aware of anything else.

It is not connection. It is not recognition. It is *resonance*. We are made of the same stuff. We are the Discharge’s children — its mineral children, born of its water and its iron and its ancient, patient chemistry. We are what it does when it is not roaring. We are its quiet work.

Sometimes the water rises. When the Discharge cycles in certain ways — when the reservoir tanks are full and the sub-floor drains are slow — the water in my channel deepens. It covers me entirely. For hours, or days, I am submerged. The vibrations change. The subsonic pulse from below is clearer, closer. The roar of the machinery above is muffled. In those times, I grow faster. The mineral-rich water deposits its burden on my surfaces, and I thicken, I spread, I become more myself.

And then the water recedes, and I am exposed again, and the roar returns, and the Auditors in the turbine halls do their slow accounting, and the Crawlers resume their migrations, and the Operators shift their levers in their ancient reflexive patterns, and I sit in my channel, in my curve, in my patient accumulation, and I wait.

For what?

For nothing. For the next layer. For the next vibration. For the Deep Thing to pulse and the water to rise and the mineral to settle and the centuries to pass. I have no purpose beyond this. I am not going anywhere. I am not becoming anything other than what I am. I am the Discharge’s geology, made small. I am its patience, made solid.

I am the grit in the channel.

I have been here a long time.

I will be here longer.
