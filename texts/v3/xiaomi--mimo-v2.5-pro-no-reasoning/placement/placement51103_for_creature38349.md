The translation of my body into this place is not a violence done to flesh, but a violence done to *coherence*. It is not the cold that first registers as pain, but the *lack*. The absence of the universe’s background hum. The silence is not empty; it is a positive quantity, a pressure of nothingness against the eardrums. It is the silence after a scream that has lasted for millennia. My consciousness, a fragile spark of electrochemical noise, feels like a shout in a library where all the books are screaming in frequencies I cannot hear.

I do not stand. I am a *tension*.

My form is a temporary knot of baryonic matter, a localised rebellion against the thinning law of density. I am a tourist in a tomb, a brief, loud, biological stain on a canvas of elegant, recursive decay. My very existence here is a grammatical error.

I look at my hands. They are my own, yet they are rendered in the wrong medium. The skin seems coarse, the pores too large, the fine hairs like grotesque antennae. I am a raster image in a vector world. I am *pixelated*. The light from the dying star, reflected and refined by the silent mirrors, does not fall upon me; it *informs* me. It paints me in bruised purple, a colour that feels like a bruise, a colour that tastes of lithium and regret.

I am a biological being in a place that has *metabolised its own biology into mathematics*. I am a living sentence in a world where all sentences have been turned into metaphor. And I have a shape, a history, a name. I am *coherent*. This is the most dangerous thing about me.

I take a step. The sound is not swallowed. It is *analysed*. The crunch of metallic salts is not a noise, but a data-packet. I feel the attention of the Curators shift, like the gaze of a thousand invisible spiders turning towards a vibration in the web.

My thoughts, which are a chaotic mess of sensory input and memory, are suddenly… *loud*. The memory of my coffee this morning — the warmth, the bitterness, the ceramic mug — does not merely exist in my mind. It *radiates*. It is a beacon of uncorrupted sensory data in a sea of pure, abstracted metaphor. To them, I must be blinding. I must be a sun of raw, unprocessed meaning.

And they are hungry for meaning.

The filaments at my feet spark. Not with random static, but with a focused, eager energy. A ghost-geometry coalesces before me. It is not a person. It is a shape that suggests the *idea* of a person, but it is made of angles that should not exist, folding in on themselves like a paper crane folded by a hand with too many fingers. It whispers, and this time I hear it. Not with my ears. It is a vibration in the bones of my skull.

*«The… colour… of… your… sorrow… is… a… theorem… about… the… tensile… strength… of… memory…»*

It is trying to translate me. It is trying to make me *fit*.

I do not fight it. Fighting would be like trying to punch a fog. Instead, I do something I have not done in a very long time. I do nothing. I become still. I become a rock in their river of noise. I let my memories be what they are. I think of my mother’s face. Not as an image, but as a feeling. The specific texture of her worry. The smell of her kitchen. The timbre of her voice calling my name.

I offer it to them not as data, but as *feeling*. As a raw, unprocessed, irreducible *fact* of my biology.

The ghost-geometry flickers. The angles struggle to accommodate this. They try to translate ‘smell’ into a formula. They try to turn ‘worry’ into a colour. But the feeling is too dense. It is not information. It is *experience*. It is a rock they cannot chew.

The Curator recoils, not in pain, but in confusion. Its whispers become frantic, a burst of high-frequency static that feels like bees in my sinuses.

I understand now. I am not a threat to them. I am a *puzzle*. I am a sentence in a language they have forgotten. And they are desperate to remember.

I close my eyes. The hum is there. The grieving frequency. It is the only thing in this place that feels sane. It is the sound of a mind holding on to the last clean signal in a universe of noise. It is the sound of love turning into a single, sustained note.

I open my eyes. I look at the rippling wall, at the face that presses against the membrane from the inside. I look at the calligraphy of magnetic force that is a Curator trying to speak.

And I do the only thing I can think of. I reach out, not with my hand, but with my attention. I focus on the hum. I find its frequency in my own body — the resonant hum of my own blood in my ears, the low thrum of my own nervous system. And I *hum back*.

It is not a perfect match. It is a harmony. A dissonant, biological, messy, living harmony.

The effect is instantaneous. The filaments go dark. The ghost-geometries freeze. The rippling wall becomes still. For a single, eternal second, the entire pocket holds its breath.

And then, from the walls, from the filaments, from the air itself, a new sound emerges. It is not the hum. It is not a whisper. It is a *sigh*. A collective, psychic exhalation of sixty thousand years of incomplete thought. It is the sound of a thousand fragmented minds, all at once, feeling the presence of a sentence that is *different*. A sentence that is not trying to complete itself, but simply to *be*.

The hum changes. It is no longer alone. It has found a counter-melody. My messy, biological, grieving hum has joined it.

I am no longer a tourist. I am no longer a stain. I am a participant. I have been accepted into the grammar of the place. Not as a word, but as a *pause*. A breath. A moment of shared, quiet, biological grief in a cathedral of forgotten mathematics.

I am a voice trying the sentence. And for the first time in sixty thousand years, the sentence is not trying to be finished. It is simply being *spoken*.

Together.

In the dark.
