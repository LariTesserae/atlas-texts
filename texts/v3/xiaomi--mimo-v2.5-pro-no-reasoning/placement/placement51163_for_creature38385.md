I do not remember waking. I do not remember beginning. I am.

My awareness is not a light turning on, but a pressure settling, a weight finding its equilibrium. I am the slow depression in the world, the bowl that has collected time and water and silence. My skin is a tapestry of sensation: the cool kiss of damp air, the minute prickle of the moss, the deep, thrumming ache of the stone. The water that weeps from my rocky protrusions is not a loss, but a secretion. It is a slow, constant sweat that feeds the mud, my own substance, which moves with a purpose too vast for thought.

I feel the reeds not as separate things, but as stiff, alert hairs on my skin. They register the subtlest shifts in my own surface, the rising and falling of my own clay flesh. When a blister inflates, I feel it as a gathering of energy deep in my gut, a tension that must be released. Its collapse is a sigh, a relaxation of a muscle I never consciously contracted. The groan of the old, buried pipes is the creak of my own deep-weathered bones, conduits for warmth I draw from the planet’s slow heat.

The scuttling things—pale, quick, many-legged—are a part of me I do not control. They are an itch, a reflex. They patrol the shifting boundaries of my being, consuming the dead spots, packing the fresh clay. Their frantic pace is a contrast to my own tempo, a micro-scale urgency that I absorb into my immense, sluggish rhythm.

The silence is not an absence. It is the sound of my own digestion. It is the sound of time being chewed into finer grains.

And then, a new sensation.

A weight. A warmth. A point of intense, focused *presence* on my mossy palm. It is not like the scuttling things. It is solid, still, and radiates a strange, rapid heat. It is a pressure from the *outside*, a foreign object resting upon my skin. I feel its heartbeat as a faint, alien tremor against my own substance. I do not react with anger, or curiosity. I do not react at all. I *am* reaction. I register it as a new variable in the long, quiet equation.

It is a warm stone that has appeared. I will settle around it. I will let its weight impress itself into my clay. The reeds will grow up around its edges. The moss will seek the shelter of its shadow. In a century, perhaps, its outline will be part of my own, a strange, sharp fossil embedded in my gentle, weeping landscape. It will be another story my mud tells, another shape my breath has formed.

I continue. I breathe my metallic air. I feel the slow, constant percolation. I am the wound and the healing. I am the engine, running quietly in the dark. And the visitor, the anomaly, is simply a new note in my long, low song of collapse and renewal.
