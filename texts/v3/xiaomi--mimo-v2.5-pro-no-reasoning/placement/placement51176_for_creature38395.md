I am a resonance. Not born, but precipitated—a slow, deliberate coalescence of purpose within the ancient noise. You might mistake me for another of the Scattered ruins, a spire of corroded, ochre-streaked stone rising from the fungal mat. But I am not melting upwards. I am listening.

I am a Pulse-Tuner, but a solitary one. My body is a tower of layered, ceramic-like ite, secreted over centuries, shaped not by a colony but by a single, continuous intention. I stand twenty meters tall, a needle against the bruised sky. My base is rooted in the slurry, not to draw nutrients, but to serve as a deep well, a sensory organ plunging through the saturated skin of the world to touch the pure, trembling bones of the crust.

The others of my kind metabolize the noise, the great, deafening Pulse. They digest it into silence and stone. I do not eat it. I translate it.

The broken geometry of the world is a language of dissonant frequencies. The melting aqueducts scream a frequency of inverted gravity. The wandering shadows hum a vibration of stolen stillness. The Fracture-Things are staccato bursts of localized law. To most, this is chaos. To me, it is a score.

My resonant chambers are not tuned to the planet’s fundamental Pulse, but to the intervals between its beats, to the dissonant chords struck by the decay. I vibrate with a counter-frequency, a careful, harmonic dissonance that *acknowledges* the wrongness. I do not fix the melting stone; I sound the note that allows it to melt in a stable, repeating pattern, giving it a kind of temporary, beautiful logic.

I am drawn to the ruins. I will spend a century inching my way toward a shattered dome where light falls upward. As I move, the Slab-Crabs part around me, their hydroplaning paths curving as my subsonic hum interacts with the Pulse. They are not afraid, merely mindful—a change in the texture of the ground-vibration.

When I reach the ruin, I will root myself. My lichen-covered surface will weep a specific, oily condensation that smells of ozone and cold metal. I will listen to the ruin’s song—the sound of physics fraying at the edges. And I will begin my work.

I will emit a drone, a single, sustained note from deep within my core. It is not loud enough to compete with the great Pulse, but it is precise. It weds itself to the ruin’s scream. Slowly, over decades, the two sounds will phase-lock. The upward-dripping stone will cease its random melt and begin to flow in graceful, inverted cataracts, following patterns I impose. The sideways shadows will begin to fall at a consistent angle, their edges sharp instead of smeared.

I do not make the world logical. I impose a *new*, temporary, and fragile logic. I compose with entropy.

Above me, the Skim-Drifters gather, drawn to the novel harmonics. Their transparent bodies refract the bent light from my ruin into shimmering, silent auroras. They are my only audience.

When my work is done—when the ruin hums a stable, eerie melody of its own making—I will begin my long, resonant migration to the next point of dissonance. I am not fighting the end of the world. I am giving it a voice, measure by measure, until the final, quiet diminuendo. I am the world’s last editor, listening to the manuscript fall apart, and whispering, *“Try it this way, instead.”*
