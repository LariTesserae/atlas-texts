I am the flaw.

I stand in the weeping dark, and the world is a sound that has forgotten how to stop. It is a single, perfect, deafening chord struck from iron and steam, and it has been held for an eternity. My ears are full of water. My bones are full of the bass thrum of the great piston, the *THUMP-THUMP-THUMP* that is the Engine’s only heartbeat. I am a silence in the hymn. A misplaced note. A wet, breathing imperfection in a symphony of brass and rust.

The air is thick with the scent of ozone and hot metal, a smell I can taste on my tongue. It is the taste of a storm that has been brewing in a bottle since the world was new. The walls weep a black, oily slick that is not water but the cold sweat of a god in a fever dream. It pools in the grates at my feet, and the sound it makes is not of flowing, but of a thousand tiny mouths being stopped.

Around me, the copper wire brambles are still. They are a forest of frozen thoughts, their fiber-optic leaves dark, waiting for a signal that will never come. They are a memory of life, a blueprint for growth, executed in metal and left to corrode. I reach out a hand, and my fingers, slick with the room’s sweat, brush against a conduit. It is cold, vibrating with a constant, high-frequency hum that travels up my arm and settles in my teeth. I am a conductor in a circuit I do not understand.

And then there are the beetles.

They are the thought-process of this place made manifest, a river of living brass that flows over every surface. They do not see me. They do not hear me. They are aware of me in the only way they know how: as a change in the pressure, a new topography in their endless, polished landscape. They part around my boots, a current of ticking, skittering purpose. The sound of their passage is a million tiny clocks, all counting down to a moment that will never arrive. They crawl over the weeping walls, lapping up the oily condensation with tiny, metallic tongues. They swarm over a rivet, polishing it to a mirror sheen. They are the Engine’s immune system, and I am a virus of bone and blood.

I am the variable it has no equation for. The glitch in its perfect, repeating code. It does not need to attack me. It only needs to wait. I am soft. I am temporary. I am a thing of decay in a world that has achieved a state of perfect, static preservation. My breath fogs the cold air, a fleeting cloud in the green-yellow light of the vacuum tubes. My heart beats a frantic, irregular rhythm against the Engine’s steady, eternal *THUMP*.

I am the only thing here that can die. And that is my only function. To be the flaw that proves the perfection of the whole. To be the silence that, for a brief moment, reminds the hymn of its own noise. I am a question the Engine has no need to answer, because the answer is the slow, inevitable return of the quiet, the closing of the circuit, the wiping away of the smear.

I stand in the heart of the god, and I feel its thoughts flow around me like a tide of warm brass. It is not waiting for me to leave. It is waiting for me to stop. To become just another pattern in the polished metal. To be remembered as a brief, warm spot on a cold wall, and then, to be forgotten.

And the sound, the beautiful, terrible, unending sound, goes on.
