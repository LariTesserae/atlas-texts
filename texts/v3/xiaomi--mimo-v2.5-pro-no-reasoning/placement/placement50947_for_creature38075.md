I am a mote. A flicker. A wrongness in the pattern.

My senses are a constant, useless static against the roar of this place. The air is a substance I must chew before I can breathe it, a gritty broth of wet clay and rust that coats my tongue and teeth. I have no name here, no purpose. I stand on a causeway of fused slag, the rock warm and humming faintly through the soles of my boots, and I watch the tide of chitin flow.

I am a ghost with a heartbeat.

The heat is a language I cannot speak. It rises from the ground in waves, a geothermal whisper. It radiates from the thick, bulging walls of the domes that press in on all sides, each one a small furnace of alien industry. And it pulses from the K'thrrak themselves, a living, churning heat that makes the air shimmer. They are a river of oil-slicked plates and clicking, purposeful limbs, flowing around me as a stream flows around a stone. Their bodies are dark, stained with the ochre and iron of their world, their carapaces catching the dull, fungal light in greasy flashes.

I try to understand them. I watch a pair of them work a strange, bellows-like machine, their movements a perfect, synchronized rhythm of push and pull. There is no speech I can perceive, only the wet *squelch* of their gripping pads on the clay controls and a low, resonant hum that seems to vibrate from their thoraxes. They are not individuals cooperating; they are two components of a single machine. Their consciousness, I feel, is not housed in their heads with their glittering black eyes, but in the space between them, in the shared task. My own mind, a frantic chatterbox of observation and anxiety, feels like a screaming malfunction in this quiet, efficient symphony.

I am a piece of faulty hardware.

The sound is the worst part. It is not noise; it is a physical pressure, the deep, foundational thrum of the Heart-Grind. It is the bass note to which every other sound is tuned: the constant, dry rustle of a thousand thousand limbs, the soft *plip-plip* of the weeping ceiling, the wet slap of things moving in the milky, ochre water below. My own breathing is a ragged counter-rhythm. My own heartbeat is a panicked, solo drum. I feel it in my bones, a resonance that asks my body to join in, to become part of the mechanism. I cannot. My joints bend the wrong way. My skin is too soft. I have no chitin to offer the symphony.

I am standing near a nursery alcove, a small, dark opening in a larger dome. Inside, I can see the pale, skittering larvae, smaller versions of the adults, their carapaces still soft and translucent. A Tender, a larger K'thrrak whose plates are worn smooth and scarred with a century of use, moves among them. It nudges a larva with its mandible, correcting its posture as it sorts small, mineral nodules into piles. There is no affection, no language I can recognize as instruction. It is simply… calibration. The tuning of a new instrument.

The Tender’s cluster of simple eyes swivels and fixes on me for a moment. There is no malice in that gaze. No curiosity, even. It is the look one gives a rock that has fallen into a pathway. An assessment. *Unmoving. Non-participatory. Not a threat. Not a tool.* The gaze moves on. I have been catalogued and dismissed. My existence has been logged as a variable of zero.

And in that moment, I understand. I am not a ghost. A ghost has memory, a story, a reason to linger. I am an error. A piece of data that does not compute. My warmth, my strange soft body, my solitary, thinking mind—I am a glitch in the code of this world.

I am a foreign object embedded in living tissue.

The weeping ceiling drips a thick tear that lands on my shoulder. It is warm, and it smells of limestone and deep time. It does not feel like water. It feels like the place is trying to slowly, patiently, digest me. My own body heat, so different from the steady, metabolic warmth of the K'thrrak, feels like a flaw, an inefficiency. I am a cold spot in their warm world. A silence in their song.

I look at my hands. They are pale, soft, and utterly useless here. They cannot grip the slick controls of a mill. They cannot mix the enzymatic clay. They cannot sort the nodules with the speed of a chitinous pad. They are made for holding tools that do not exist in this place, for shaping materials that are not used. They are made for a different kind of world.

The tide of K'thrrak parts and flows around me, a constant, quiet testament to my irrelevance. The hum of the Heart-Grind is the only truth. The scent of damp clay and machine oil is the only air. I will stand here on this warm slag causeway until my own heat is finally absorbed into the greater warmth of the geode, until my soft body is just another mineral deposit to be processed by the slow, grinding patience of the earth and its children. I am a temporary imperfection, and this place, in its vast, unthinking, industrialized biology, will not even notice when I am gone.
