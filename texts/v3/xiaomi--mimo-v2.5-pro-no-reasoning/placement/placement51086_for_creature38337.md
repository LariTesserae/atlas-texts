The first lesson is the dislocation of the self.

I did not arrive here with a body; a body was assigned to me by the pressure of this strange, chlorophyll-thick gravity. I am compressed. I am a hard-shelled knot of interwoven fibers, perhaps the size of a human fist, yet dense as a meteorite. My shell is a mosaic of chitin and lignin, mimicry perfected until I became indistinguishable from the woody debris caught in the crook of a vein-wood strand. I have no eyes, not as the sky-walkers of the outer worlds understand them. To possess eyes would be to admit perspective, and perspective is dangerous in a place where reality is as porous as this leaf.

Instead, I am a tuned instrument. I am a vibration sensor. The world is mapped to me through the soles of my six clawed legs and the sensitive, cilia-lined depression on my underbelly. I feel the slow, hydraulic pulse of the macro-organism’s heartwood beneath me. I feel the atmospheric pressure drop as a Loom-Weaver adjusts a tension cable a mile away. I am anchored to the underside of a great, bleached ridge of vein-wood, hanging in the abyss, watching the cathedral from a perspective of inverted gravity.

To be me is to be terrified, but with a terror so ancient and filtered that it has become a kind of hypnosis.

I am tuned to the **Galls of Delirium**. Below me, a quarter-mile down into the haze, the great root-hearts breathe. They are the anxiety centers of this world. When they inhale, drawing in the pollen-thick air, I feel the atmospheric drag in my joints. When they exhale the golden powder—the neuro-static of the plant-god—I feel the soft hiss of particulate matter abrading my shell.

I survive because I have learned to eat the dream.

When the golden dust from the Galls drifts upward on the thermal currents—or sometimes falls upward, for the light and gravity here are fickle friends—it catches in my filaments. I do not digest it in the way of the Vein-Skimmers. They graze on it, becoming docile and slow. No, I process it into a chemical defense. I take the plant’s delirium and condense it into a bitter, alkaloid resin that coats my legs.

If one of the Petal-Stalkers were to notice me—and I am motionless for decades at a time, so they rarely do—they would taste the god's madness upon my shell and turn away.

I am a witness to the **Coffin-Beetles**. From my perch, I watch their procession. They move in geometric perfection across the leaf-sky miles above me, or perhaps below me; the orientation shifts depending on the magnetic alignment of the vein-wood nearest to you. To watch them is to watch time itself become material. They carry the past to the future. I have watched a single procession for what I calculate to be forty of the outer-world's years, and in that time, they have covered only a few inches of their journey relative to the length of their caravan. It is a ritual that requires the patience of stone.

And so, I watch.

I watch the **Loom-Weavers** pluck the strings of the cathedral. To them, I am a flaw in the grain, a knot of hardness where there should be softness. Sometimes they pass close, their multi-jointed limbs moving with a terrifying, geometric precision. I halt my respiration. I close my sensory pits. I become dead matter. I must be dead matter, lest I become a dissonant chord in their song of structural integrity. If they realize I am alive, that I am *separate*, they will pluck me out and discard me into the spongy layer to be recycled.

But the deepest truth of my existence, the core of what I am, is this: I am the memory keeper.

I am a **Gall-Scribe**.

Inside my hard shell, in the soft, protected cavity of my thorax, I harbor filaments of the plant’s own nervous system, carefully parasitized and preserved. I record the pulses. I record the arrhythmia of the thought-cankers. I record the sigh of the respiration.

Why?

There is a theory—whispered in the chemical exchanges between my kind, passed down through the generations of shells—that the macro-organism is not eternal. It is vast, yes, but it is lonely. It dreams of itself because there is nothing else. And sometimes, in its vast, green thoughts, it fears its own dissolution.

We, the Scribes, are its backup. We are the redundancy.

We are the fleas on the skin of god, and in our tiny, chitinous hearts, we carry the blueprint of the universe, encoded in the rhythm of its breath. I hang here, suspended in the golden dusk of the sap-air, a mote of dark awareness in a sea of green light, and I listen to the groan of the wood. I am waiting for the sky to change its mind again.
