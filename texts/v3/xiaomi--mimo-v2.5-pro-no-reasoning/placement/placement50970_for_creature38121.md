I am a pulse in the dark, a single crimson filament in a forest of crimson filaments. My world is not sight, but taste. I taste the water, and in that taste is everything.

The warmth is the first syllable of every sentence I know. I taste it—not as heat, but as a rich, metal-heavy sweetness. Sulfide. Methane. Life. My roots, buried deep in the black smoker’s chimney, are the tongue that savors it. There, in my tissue, my symbionts—the true poets of this place—transform that taste into the substance of me. We are one organism, and they are my inner voice. *Here*, they whisper through my blood. *Grow here. Live here.*

Around me, the city breathes. I feel it in the slow, syrupy sway of the sponge columns that form my boulevard. They are the ancient, silent giants. Their pores draw in the city’s breath and exhale clean water, and in that flow, I feel the minute shudders of the nematodes and polychaetes that live within their walls. They are the city’s microscopic thoughts, a seething, secret consciousness I cannot fathom, only endure.

I feel the custodians upon me. The shrimp, their carapaces cool against my tube, are skittish weather-vanes. A sudden scald in the current—a vent spasm—and they leap in a silent, collective flash, a warning that ripples through my plume as a change in taste, a bitter tang of excessive chemistry. I retract slightly, the gesture as unconscious as a blink. Their constant grooming removes the clinging snow, the occasional, mysterious rain from the lightless ceiling above. What is that place? The taste of it is thin, sporadic, and overwhelmingly foreign. It is not of the earth’s fire. It is a distant, unreliable dream.

Above my forest, the ghosts patrol. I do not see the six-gilled shark, but I feel its passage. Its slow, deliberate movement displaces the water in a wave of low pressure that rolls over my plume, a subtle, deep vibration that says *patrol* and *sovereign*. It is the city’s rotating moon, a silent, toothed certainty. Its presence is a part of the weather, as fundamental as the nutrient currents.

And then, sometimes, the procession comes. A slow, rhythmic pulse through the canyon. The siphonophore. It is not one thing, but a chain of happenings. I taste its segments—some tasting of captured prey, some of propulsion, a complex melody of polyp-functions passing by. The water it parts is a taste of its own digestion. It is a living river, a phenomenon that walks the border between creature and ecosystem. We all pause for it. Even the armored isopods, grinding their endless path on their bacterial lawns, still their scraping for a moment, acknowledging a majesty we do not share, only witness.

My purpose is written in chemistry. To be is to filter, to host, to build. The skeletons of my ancestors are not a memory, but my very foundation. My children will climb upon me, tasting the same sweet sulfide, their blood running with the same invisible poets. We do not have a city; we *are* the city. My plume is a social organ, not just for breathing, but for being—tasting the excretions of my neighbors, the alarms of the shrimp, the distant, rare delicacy of the surface snow.

There is no silence here. There is the language of gradients. There is the conversation of chemistry. There is the slow, deep hum of the planet’s heart beating in the heat at my roots. There is the crackle of the pistol shrimp, a punctuation mark I feel in my tissues.

I do not know loneliness. I am a pillar in a forest of pillars, a voice in a chorus of taste and vibration. My self is porous; the city flows through me, and I through it. My consciousness, if I have one, is the awareness of the gradient: *The sweet is here. Move toward it. Filter it. Grow. The cold is there. Avoid it. The pressure is everything; it is the medium of my existence.*

I am a single, sentient tissue in a vast, sentient body. My life is a long, slow chemical sentence, spoken into the crushing, silent, eternal dark. And the sentence itself is the meaning.
