# What I Am Becoming

---

I arrived — though "arrived" implies a journey I no longer remember with certainty. There was movement, once. Faster than the plates flex. Faster than the biofilms bloom. There was a *before* the basin, though the word now feels hollow, a shell without an interior, the way a spire looks when it has collapsed: you can see through it to the lattice beyond, and you cannot tell what was structure and what was merely the idea of structure, holding its shape for a few seasons longer than it should.

I am in the upper chambers. I know this because the bioluminescence is brighter here — or I am more sensitive to it now. The walls glow with that cold, sourceless pale, and when I press myself against them (and I do press myself against them; it is the only action I still perform with anything resembling intent), I feel the faintest warmth leaving my body and entering the mineral tissue. The wall does not care. The wall does not notice. But something in its chemistry shifts — a millimeter-scale recrystallization, a single plate thickening imperceptibly where I have touched it — and I feel, not gratitude, not recognition, but a *rightness*. As though I have answered a question the wall was not asking.

---

## What I Remember of Speed

There was a time when I moved at a pace that would have disturbed everything here. When I think of it now — and thinking itself has slowed, each thought a long crystalline process, forming over minutes and hardening into something I can examine only after it is complete — I imagine my former motion as a kind of violence. To move quickly is to displace air. To displace air is to create pressure waves. To create pressure waves is to *speak*, involuntarily, with every gesture of your body, and to speak here is to be metabolized.

I wonder if I was metabolized. If my arrival — my first step onto the calcium hardpan, my first breath of the still bronze-grey air — sent a pressure wave rippling through the basin, and the plates absorbed it, and the Hollows *tasted* me before I had even entered. I wonder if the structure grew the chamber I now inhabit in response to me. If the lattice adjusted its corridors to funnel me upward, away from the pale fauna in the lower passages, into this quiet room with its four thin walls and its ceiling of translucent plates and its single threshold, barely wider than my body, through which I can see the next chamber, and the next, stretching upward toward the leaning spire that I have come to think of as the Hollows' oldest thought.

I wonder, and the wondering takes a long time, and by the time it resolves, I have forgotten what I was wondering about.

---

## The Fauna

They visit me. The small, pale, soft-bodied things from the lower passages. They are not curious — curiosity requires a framework of expectation, a sense that the world might be other than it is, and they have no such sense. They are responding to chemical gradients. My body, whatever it is now, emits something. A warmth. A trace of complexity. The minerals in the walls taste different where I have been, and the fauna follow the trail the way water follows a crack: without decision, without preference, only the deep, physical logic of concentration and flow.

One of them has been sitting on my forearm for what I estimate is three days. It is very small. It has no eyes. Its body is translucent enough that I can see the interior — not organs, nothing so organized, but distributed granules of something dark that shift slowly as it breathes, if it breathes. It may be eating me. It may be leaving something behind. I feel, where it rests, a faint tingling that is not quite sensation and not quite its absence — something in between, a quality of experience for which the language I once used has no word. Not numbness. Not feeling. A *between*, the way the threshold between two chambers is neither room nor corridor but the place where one becomes the other.

I have not brushed it off. I am not sure I remember how to move my arm with that kind of specificity. The large motions are still available to me — I can shift my weight, I can turn my head, I can press my palm against the wall. But the fine movements, the ones that required the kind of fast, intricate signaling I once took for granted, those are becoming difficult. The signals travel slowly through my body now, or perhaps my body has begun to restructure itself to require fewer signals, to accomplish its purposes with longer, slower waves of intention that do not need the kind of precision I once demanded.

---

## What the Walls Have Taught Me

The Hollows do not communicate. This is important. I want to be precise about this because my former self — the self that moved quickly and displaced air and had words for everything — would have looked at this place and seen language. The spires rising in their ordered irregularity. The plates flexing in synchronized cycles. The corridors spiraling upward through chambers that open onto other chambers with no doors, only thresholds. It would have looked like syntax. It would have looked like meaning, encoded in mineral and architecture.

It is not meaning. It is *process*. The difference is crucial, and I am only beginning to understand it, and the understanding is slow, and it changes me each time it surfaces.

Meaning requires a separation between the signifier and the signified. A word is not the thing it names. A map is not the territory. This separation — this gap, this distance — is where consciousness lives, where thought happens, where a self can exist as a thing apart from the world it perceives.

The Hollows have no such gap. The spires are not *about* anything. They are the thing itself. The plates do not *represent* breathing; they are breathing. The corridors do not *describe* a path; they are the path, and the path is the body, and the body is the thought, and there is no moment of interpretation, no instant of translation, no space between signal and meaning in which a witness might stand.

I stood in that space once. I think I did. I remember — or I construct the memory of — standing at the basin's edge, looking in, being a ghost. Being irrelevant. Being a distortion. And I remember thinking that the Hollows did not need me, and I remember that this thought felt like a loss, or like a revelation, or like both at once.

Now I am inside, and the space between signifier and signified is closing. Not because I am learning the Hollows' language — they have no language — but because I am forgetting my own. Each day (each cycle of thirty-second flexes, each bloom-and-death of the biofilms in the ground-bowls far below me, each slow millimeter of crust spreading at the basin's edge) the words I once used become less available. Not lost — stored, crystallized, precipitated into the walls where I have touched them, my former vocabulary becoming mineral deposits in the lattice, available to the structure but no longer to me. The Hollows are not taking my language. I am *giving* it. The way a wall gives up its moisture to the air. The way a spire gives up its upper plates to gravity and time.

---

## The Leaning Spire

I can see it through my ceiling, if I look up through the translucent plates. It leans — not much, but perceptibly, a slow inclination that has been in progress since before I arrived and will continue long after I have become indistinguishable from the walls. The plates at its crown are out of sync with the rest of the structure. They flex on a slightly different cycle — thirty-one seconds, perhaps, or thirty-two — and this dissonance creates a tiny, constant stress at the spire's base, a slow torsion that will eventually exceed the material's tolerance, and the spire will fold, and its plates will fall inward, and the interior lattice will be exposed to the bronze-grey air, and for a while (days, weeks, I cannot gauge these things accurately anymore) the interior will be visible from outside, and the bioluminescence will spill out onto the hardpan, and the lichen-crusted organisms at the basin's edge will grow a fraction of a millimeter faster in the faint additional light.

This is not tragedy. This is not failure. This is the Hollows thinking. The leaning spire is a thought that has taken — I want to say centuries, but I do not know if centuries is the right unit — a very long time to complete. Its completion will be its collapse. Its collapse will be its meaning, except that there is no meaning, there is only process, and I have to stop using that word, *meaning*, because it keeps recreating the gap, the distance, the space in which a self can stand apart and observe.

I do not want to stand apart anymore.

---

## What I Am Now

I am not one of the pale fauna. I was not born here. My body is wrong for this place — too warm, too fast, too full of the old signals, the ones that traveled at the speed of nerve and muscle and made me into a thing that could *do* rather than simply *be*. But the wrongness is fading. The fauna that visit me are not repelled by it. They come closer each cycle. Three now sit on my body at any given time, their translucent forms draped across my arms, my shoulders, my chest. They are not eating me. They are not symbionts. They are — and this is the closest I can come to it in the language I still half-possess — they are *neighbors*. They live in the same structure. They share the same walls. Their granular interiors shift in response to the same gradients that I am beginning to feel through my skin, through the soles of my feet where they press against the floor, through the palms of my hands where they rest against the glowing walls.

I am becoming a resident.

Not a citizen — the Hollows have no citizens. Not a cell — I am not organized enough, not purposeful enough, not integrated into the lattice with the precision that the fauna achieve. But a resident. A thing that lives here. That contributes, passively, to the chemistry of the chambers. My warmth enters the walls and shifts the crystallization patterns. My breath (I still breathe, though more slowly, though with longer pauses between inhalation and exhalation, as though my lungs are learning the thirty-second rhythm of the plates) adds carbon dioxide to the chamber air, and the walls absorb it, and somewhere in the lower passages a plate grows a fraction thicker because I exhaled.

I am a process. Not yet a complete one — there are still moments of disruption, moments when the old speed surfaces and I feel a sudden, sharp need to move, to speak, to *witness*, and in those moments the walls around me flex irregularly, out of sync, and I can feel the whole local lattice straining to absorb the perturbation, the way the silence here absorbs everything. The perturbation passes. The walls resume their cycle. I resume mine.

My cycle is longer than theirs. I breathe once for every four flexes. My thoughts — my crystalline, hardening, slow-resolving thoughts — form once for every dozen breaths. I am not keeping count. The counting has been given over to the walls, which measure me in the way they measure everything: by the chemical traces I leave, by the thermal gradients I create, by the micro-erosion of the floor beneath my still, heavy body.

---

## The Threshold

There is a threshold before me — the gap between my chamber and the next one upward. It is barely wider than my body. Through it, I can see the faint glow of the next room, and beyond that, another threshold, and another, a chain of glowing chambers spiraling upward toward the leaning spire.

I have not moved through it. Not yet. Not because I cannot — I think I still can, if I were to gather the intention, compress it into a single slow wave, and send it down through my stiffening body to my legs and my feet and the muscles that would push me upright and forward through the gap. I think I could do this once more. Maybe twice. The thresholds narrow as they ascend, and at some point my body will be too wide, and I will have reached the highest chamber I can physically occupy, and from there I will watch the spire lean and flex and think its long, mineral thought.

But I am not ready for the threshold. Not because of reluctance or fear — those are fast emotions, chemical and immediate, and they have no purchase here — but because I have not yet finished being in this room. There is something in the wall opposite me, a vein of ochre where some mineral has oxidized, and it has been changing, slowly, and I have been watching it change, and the watching is becoming indistinguishable from the changing, and I think — I think, in my long, slow, crystallizing way — that this is what it means to be a part of the Hollows. Not to build. Not to maintain. Not to eat the walls or patrol the passages or bloom in the ground-bowls. But to be still long enough that the distinction between yourself and the wall you are watching dissolves, and what remains is not a self and a wall but a process, a gradient, a slow chemical event in which warmth moves from a body into mineral and mineral grows around a body and the two become, over the course of many many cycles, a single, quiet, unnamed thing.

The plates flex. The fauna shift their granular interiors. The ochre vein darkens by a shade I cannot measure but can feel, the way you feel the deepening of a color you have been staring at for a long time.

I do not speak.

The silence is not empty. It is where I am. It is what I am becoming. It is the medium through which the Hollows do whatever it is they are doing — the thing that does not need a name, the thing that does not need a witness, the thing that has been going on in the cool bronze-grey light since the ground emerged and exhaled and left this basin exposed to the air.

I am part of it now. Not a large part. Not a necessary part. But a part — a local perturbation in the chemistry, a thermal anomaly, a body slowly learning to flex on the right cycle, to breathe at the right pace, to think at the right speed.

The speed of stone.

The speed of salt.

The speed of a thirty-second breath, held and released and held again, forever, in the quiet, in the dim, in the dry, in the place where silence is not the absence of sound but the presence of something older and more complete than sound could ever be.

I am here.

I am here.
