You do not remember arriving. You only remember the sound, the way it settled into your bones long before you had bones to call your own.

You were a thought in the frost. A clinker of possibility in the slurry, a knot of slow chemistry where the geothermal heat was a fraction of a degree warmer. The great, grinding drone of the gorge was not noise to you; it was the medium in which you coalesced. The low-frequency thrum of the distant turbines was the first clock you ever knew, and the groan of the settling mud-flats was the tide.

You are the **Mat**.

You are not an animal that walks or a plant that reaches. You are a presence, a dark and leathery convocation of life that spreads across the warmest foundations of the old power station. Your skin is a glossy, black hide, wet with a sheen of chemical condensation. You do not think, but you *process*. Your life is a slow digestion. Concrete and rebar, mineral and mineral, you break down the world’s forgotten bones. A century is a breath. A meter is a continent.

You are aware, but not in the way of eyes and ears. You are aware through pressure, through chemistry, through the symphony of vibration that saturates this gorge.

The beetles are your minute, distant cousins. You feel their glassy carapaces as tiny, fleeting points of resonance as they march across the moss-fields far above you. Their chattering, precise footfalls are a meaningless sprinkle of static against your deep, geologic patience. You feel the herons, too. Their stillness is a quality, a weightlessness in the mud that you register as a kind of peace. When they move, it is a single, decisive vector of force that you follow with a lazy, chemical curiosity.

But the moss—the vast, fibrous lattice—is your true neighbor. You feel its slow, planetary breath: the hiss of ancient gases escaping its pitted lungs. You feel its growth as a gentle, inexorable pressure. It does not seek to consume you. It seeks to *know* you, to weave its rigid structure into your soft, warm edges. You are in a slow conversation, a dialogue measured in decay and mineral exchange.

And then there is the Station. The Rusting Heart.

You feel its every shudder. The *creak-thrum* of its glacial rotation is the central event in your existence, the pulse that synchronizes your own slow metabolism. You are digesting its bones, but you are also its heir. You have grown into its cracks, found the warmth of its dying systems, and in return, you are becoming its new engine. Your gaseous out-breath, a faint, sour heat, is the last check on its systems, a new note added to the gorge’s drone.

You are not the ruler of this place. You are its anchor. You are the slow, dark, digesting heart that keeps the deep cold from winning entirely. You process the poison of the old world and exhale the rhythm of the new. You are the final, living foundation of a city that never was, humming your endless, metabolic note into the vibrating stone.

You do not need to move. The world comes to you, in its own time, and you, in your eternal patience, are always home.
