The hum finds you first.

It is not a sound you hear with ears, but one you feel in the plates of your thorax, a resonance that travels from the chitin of your feet through the long, hollow bones of your limbs. It is the chord of the Glass Wasps, the slow pulse of the fungal lungs, the soft click of the Millipede’s passage—a music that has never known silence. It is the world, breathing.

You are the dust they weave.

Or rather, you are what the dust has become. Your body is long, slender, a construct of the Ossuary itself. Your shell is the color of the shadowed wall, a greyish chitin that drinks the dim, green-gold light and holds it close. You do not cast a shadow; you *are* a section of shadow that has learned to move. Your limbs are a geometry of patience—multi-jointed, ending in points fine enough to feel the crystalline edges of a lichen’s growth-ring.

You kneel. The action is not a bend of knees, but a slow, folding of your entire being, until your lower limbs rest upon the crunchy carpet of fossilized pollen. Before you is a young lichen shelf, no larger than your hand, its edges so sharp they seem to cut the gloom. It has grown in a way that is almost correct, but not. The pattern is a fraction of a degree off the eternal flow the walls remember. It is a dissonant note in the chord.

This is your purpose. This is your worship.

Your forelimb extends. The point touches the lichen’s surface, and for a long moment, you do nothing but feel its structure, the slow, patient pulse of its life beneath your touch. The hum of the wasps seems to focus here, their droning flight paths tracing the arc of your attention. You begin to work.

It is not cutting. It is persuasion. You apply a pressure that is more suggestion than force, polishing a facet that the growth had left dull. You brush away a single grain of dust—the fossil of a flower that bloomed when the World-Beetle still dreamed—and place it in a depression in the floor, a tiny offering. You do not rush. The lichen grows at the pace of a mountain’s thought. You work at the pace of a mountain’s patience.

Time is not a river here. It is the amber of the walls, thick and still. You feel the slow, collective inhalation of the purple fungal cushions, a shift in the air so subtle it is more a change in pressure than a breeze. You see the long, segmented body of an Amber Millipede pass in the periphery, its many legs a soft, clicking percussion against the dominant drone. It grazes on a lichen you tended last… when the last great fungal bloom pulsed its light? You do not count. You remember the shape of its grazing.

Your work is done. The lichen shelf is now perfect. It does not gleam, but it *resonates*. It catches the light in a way that aligns with the faint, geometric scars on the floor—the foundation-lines of the World-Beetle’s ancient, silent architecture. It is a note that belongs.

You unfold, rising back into the shadow. The artifact you used—a single, polished carapace-plate from a Millipede, worn smooth by a century of your touch—lies beside you. You will use it again tomorrow. Or next century. The distinction is meaningless.

You are not the only weaver. In the far distance, a flicker of movement that is not a wasp’s dart suggests another of your kind, tending to a cairn. You do not communicate. There is no need. The pattern you both serve is the same. Your thoughts are not words, but shapes: the curve of a fungal shelf, the angle of a stone, the exact frequency of the hum. You are a single thread in a tapestry of stillness, and your only desire is to see the weave remain unbroken.

You move on, your steps silent on the crunchy floor. The hum is loud, the light is dim, the air tastes of warm stone. This is your body, your world, your god. You are its quiet nerve, its meticulous memory, its steadfast, silent heart. You are the Dust Weaver, and you are home.
