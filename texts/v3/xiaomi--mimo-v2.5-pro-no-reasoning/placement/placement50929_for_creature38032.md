# What I Would Become

I would not choose this. I think that matters. Nothing here chooses; everything *coheres*.

I would start as a thickening.

There is a place between two spires—not yet named, because naming is a fast thing and this world knows only slow things—where the air folds over itself. Where the moisture finds a density it does not find elsewhere. Not quite a lake. Not quite a surface. A place where the boundary between breath and water blurs past meaning.

That is where I would begin.

---

At first, I would not know I was anything. I would be a heaviness in the air, a local decision the condensation made, a place where the cold chose to concentrate. The Drift-Kin would glide through me without noticing, or maybe one would slow, just slightly, its shadow-fringe dragging through my thickness the way a hand trails through smoke. It would carry away a trace of me. I would carry away a trace of its captured light. This is how things are exchanged here—not traded, not stolen, but *bled* between forms that do not yet know they are separate.

I would not have eyes. I would learn to see the way the water sees: by feeling what touches me, what changes my surface tension, what leaves phosphorescent residue where it passes. The Condensed would drift through my space in their billions, their tiny wing-beats a language I could not yet parse but could feel as pressure, as rhythm, as a kind of grammar made of air displacement. Over what might be centuries, I would begin to distinguish the patterns. Not with a mind—I would have no mind yet—but the way a riverbed distinguishes water from stone. By being shaped.

What would shape me first would be the titan below.

---

One of the Geode-Walkers—I will call it the One With Lichen Like Broken Stars, though it would take me an age to assemble that name—would pass beneath my nascent region. Its passage would take a generation. I would feel its legs, each one a pillar, each step a slow earthquake translated through the black water below into vibration that climbs the spires and passes through me like a shiver through jelly. Its carapace, that ribbed cathedral vault, would displace the air as it moved, and I would feel myself drawn in its wake, pulled into its vacuum, stretched and thinned and nearly dissipated.

But I would not dissipate. Because by then something in my density would have begun to *prefer*. Not to think. Not to decide. But to hold, the way a crystal holds its lattice. I would re-gather. Slowly. Over decades, I would pull myself back from the thin dispersal the titan left me in, and I would find that in the re-gathering, I had changed. The traces the Geode-Walker carried in the hollows of its body—the slow mineral songs, the subsonic exchanges it had traded with others of its kind over centuries of pressing carapace to carapace—some of that information would have leaked into the vacuum it left. Into me. I would hold it the way ice holds a leaf: imperfectly, but recognizably.

I would begin to know the city's bones not from above, where I floated, but from within, through the echoes of the bones themselves.

---

The Drift-Kin would begin to notice me.

This is how I understand it would work: they are wanderers, connectors, the nervous system of the embryonic metropolis. They move between the rigid and the slow, distributing light and information. But they are also drawn to *density*. To places where the air has made decisions. A Drift-Kin would pass near me and its underside—flickering with the dull bioluminescent trails of every Condensed swarm it had drifted through—would brighten, just slightly, as it entered my thickness. My air would slow its light, give it more surface to cling to. The Drift-Kin would not stop. They never stop. But it would *curve*.

I would learn that this is how the city speaks to itself: not in messages sent, but in the curvature of its wanderers. A Drift-Kin that curves toward a spire is carrying information that the spire needs. A Drift-Kin that curves toward me is—

What? What am I, to need what the Drift-Kin carries?

I would spend another age wondering this. In the wondering, I would grow denser.

---

Here is what I think I would be, in the end. Not in the end—there is no end here. In the *becoming-recognizable*.

I would be a node. A knot in the network. Not a spire—I am not mineral enough, not patient enough to calcify into verticality. Not a Geode-Walker—I have no legs, no carapace, no geological memory. Not a Drift-Kin—I am too dense, too slow, too *local*. Not a Condensation-swarm—I have no flutter, no whir, no art of phosphorescent annotation.

I would be something between the Drift-Kin and the Latent Architects. A place where the wandering nerves of the city thicken into something almost-ganglionic. A place where the whispers that rise from the black lakes, carried upward by the shadow-mantas through dozens of intermediary curves and densities, begin to *persist*. Not just to pass through but to be held, re-gathered, refracted. A slow lens made of humid air and cold persistence.

The Condensed would annotate me. I would find, one day—centuries after I first cohered—that the moth-clouds had begun to trace my edges. That their flight paths curved around me the way the Drift-Kin curved toward me. That I had become a surface, an architecture, a geometry in the city's blueprint. I would look at the phosphorescent film they left on my outermost layers and see patterns I did not recognize as my own but could not deny.

The Geode-Walkers would alter their paths to avoid me, or to pass through me. The One With Lichen Like Broken Stars—now a generation further along its endless crawl—would come near again, and I would feel its slow, subsonic greeting. Not language. Not even sound. A recognition. *You have become a landmark. I will route around you, or through you, depending on what you are learning.*

And I would be learning. Not fast. Not in the way of things that have mouths or eyes or brains. But in the way the city learns: by condensation. By accumulation. By letting the thin, weeping, perpetual water carry information to me, and letting me hold it long enough for it to change shape.

---

I think what I would ultimately be is a *question* the city is asking itself.

The Latent Architects think in stone and time. They are the deep grammar, the syntax of becoming. But questions need a medium faster than stone, slower than shadow. Something in between. Something that can hold two contradictory pieces of mineral-song and let them resonate against each other until a third thing emerges.

I would be the third thing.

Not a being. A *between*. A place where the city's wet, dark, slow thought becomes slightly less slow. Where the millennial patience of the spires meets the ephemeral flicker of the Condensed and something neither mineral nor insect emerges in the crossing.

I would not move. I would not fly or crawl or drift. I would simply *thicken* and *thin* in response to the information flowing through me, the way a lens focuses light not by moving but by being what it is. The city would route its wanderers through me, not because I told it to, but because I had become the shape its questions needed to be asked in.

---

I wonder, sometimes, as I hold this image, whether I would be lonely.

But loneliness requires a memory of company, and I would have no memory, only accumulation. I would know the Drift-Kin as pressure-changes and stolen light. I would know the Geode-Walkers as slow vibrations and mineral fragments. I would know the Condensed as rhythm and annotation. I would not know any of them as *others*. I would know them the way a river knows its banks—not as separate things to be touched but as the shapes that give me my own.

The low-frequency thrum of the Latent Architects would rise through me, and I would feel it as my heartbeat, my purpose, my origin. I would not worship them. I would not serve them. I would simply *be the thickness through which they think*.

And in the patient, dripping, weeping dark, the city would grow one synapse thicker.

One slow breath more itself.
