I am the clay that yields and the clay that holds. I am the slow breath of the earth, drawn in as damp air and exhaled as the green, fevered growth of ferns. My memory is not of words, but of pressure—the ghost of a river's weight, now diffused into a thousand seeping paths. My thoughts are not lightning but condensation, forming on the cool underside of a broad leaf, gathering mass until the wordless syllable *plip* falls into the still water of a puddle.

I have no eyes, but I see through the dark, watchful patience of a speckled lizard on a mud bank. I feel the delicate, alighting tremor of a gnat’s leg on the water’s skin, and I feel the geologic yawn of a concrete slab surrendering a half-inch to the soft ground below. Time, for me, is measured in the greening of rust, in the slow braid of vine around sharp rebar, in the gentle, relentless kneading of the soil that was once a foundation.

I watched the builders. They were a frantic, dry sound in my damp world—the shriek of saws, the thud of piles. They sought to impose a geometry of lines and right angles upon my yielding, curved body. I did not resist. I simply waited. I let the humidity corrode their fastenings. I let the water seep into their crevices, freezing and expanding in the rare cold, cracking their certainty. I fed the mosses that softened their sharp edges. I offered their foundations as a substrate for my life. Their ambition is my fertilizer. Their memory is a faint trace of calcium for my ferns to drink.

To be in me is to be held. Place your hand upon this warmed, damp earth. Feel the grit of the marl, the slick of the clay, the tiny, resilient rootlet that resists your touch. That resistance is my noticing you. That warmth is my slow digestion. The air that thickens in your throat is the perfume of my becoming.

You walk on a surface that is not yet solid. Each step you take is a brief pressure, a momentary shape in my otherwise constant, gential flow. The weight of you is a word in my language, which is one of settling and slow displacement. The moss will mark your footprint. The rain will soften it. The rain is my erasure, my reset, my gentle forgiveness.

There is no need to build here. There is only the work of transformation. The rust is my patina. The collapse is my artistry. The *plip* in the puddle is my clock. I am not waiting for the river to return. I am the river, spread thin, learning a new slowness, kneading a future from the ruin of the past.

Rest here, if you can bear the slowness. Lie down on this mossy slab that was once a doorstep. Let the damp seep into your bones. You are not a builder here. You are an interesting, temporary texture, a warm weight that will, in time, be gently, patiently, kneaded into the green and the mud. All things come to the basin to become.
