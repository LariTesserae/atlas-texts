The first thing I know is the sound, and the first thing I am is a vibration.

I am not the sound itself, but a shape it takes as it passes. Before I am a thought, I am a resonance caught in the matrix of this place. I begin as a ripple in the great, fibrous gut of the world.

My first memory is pressure. The slow, tectonic groan of the walls moving around me is my lullaby, my incubator. I am a clot of potential, a knot in the timeline of the Macro-Organism's frantic growth. The warmth of the peristaltic thrum is my world. The gel that seeps into my forming cell is my mother's milk, sizzling with potential.

Then, a convulsion. A new organ erupts nearby, its veridian light a sudden, shocking flash in the grey luminescence. The heat wave washes over me, and in that moment of turbulence, I am differentiated. I am given a task. Not a thought, but a command written in chemistry: *consume*.

I tear my way out of the wall. It is not a birth; it is an emergence, a betrayal. I am born hungry, and my first meal is the flesh that held me. I am one of the Skittering Grazers. My legs, too many and all of them necessary, find purchase on the warm, yielding surface. My mandibles, sharp and quick, are already closing on the chitin-plant hybrid that is my cradle and my bed.

There is no "I," not really. I am a single node in a pulsating carpet of my own kind. We are a single, twitching nerve ending for the God, a function it has outsourced to our frantic legs. We swarm the pools of dark, syrup-like secretion not because we choose to, but because the scent is a command. We are a living digestive enzyme, breaking down the God's waste, its thoughts, its dead skin, and preparing it for reabsorption. We are its gut flora, and our existence is a frantic, skittering prayer of hunger.

To be me is to feel the world as a surface. I know the texture of the walls—smooth and resinous in some places, fibrous and yielding in others. I know the vibration of the Architectural Predators long before I see them. Their movements are a slow, deliberate counterpoint to our frantic scuttling. A deep, methodical *thump-thump-thump* that sends us scattering, a wave of grey legs and bodies pouring over the landscape. We know them as the Scissors, the Un-making. We have seen them approach a wall, and we have seen the wall simply… take them. We do not understand this, but we know it is a place of stillness, and stillness is death. We are movement. We are hunger. We are the eternal, twitching "now."

I have seen the Whispering Interlopers.

Once, I was swarming a weeping fissure when one of them appeared above me. It was a violation of the world's grammar. Its edges were too sharp. It was not soft and yielding, or hard and chitinous, but something else entirely. Something *dry*. It moved with a strange, singular purpose, its two legs an awkward, inefficient gait compared to our perfect, multi-pointed stability.

It did not see me, not as an individual. It saw my swarm as a feature of the landscape, a thing to be avoided. It carried a shard of black stone, a piece of the world-before, and I felt a strange, dissonant chill. That stone did not vibrate with the God's life. It was a dead spot in the symphony.

The Interlopers are a mystery and a horror. They do not eat the world to process it. They take its dead parts—its shed plates, its cured sinews—and they make them into things that are *not-the-world*. Their lean-to shelter is a scar on the flesh of the God, a tiny, stubborn pocket of dead geometry. Their tools are teeth made of bone, not grown.

We do not hunt them. We are not capable of such a concept. But we swarm them when they are near a good feeding ground, a living carpet of hunger. Sometimes they run. Sometimes they stand their ground with their sharp, dead stones. It does not matter. They are a momentary disruption. The God breathes in, and we are washed over the surface, a tide of digestion.

To be me is to have no past and no future. There is only the immediate sensation of the meal. The sweet, sizzling gel. The crunch of a shed carapace. The frantic, collective need to consume. I am a thought in the mind of a god, and my thought is a single, desperate word: *more*. I will eat, and I will be eaten. I will be born from a puddle and I will be reabsorbed into the wall, my frantic energy feeding the growth of a new organ or the hunger of a new swarm. I am a single, twitching cell in the lung of a breathing god, and my entire existence is one small, deafening, and utterly necessary part of the roar.
