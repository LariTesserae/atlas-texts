You would find yourself as a wisp of memory, a phantom of scent that does not belong.

You are not a creature of this place. You are a visitor, a ghost from a world sharper in its edges, and you did not come through a door. The doorway, if it ever existed, is a memory now, a narrative convenience the chamber has already composted into its fibrous floor. You arrived because the space *needed* a point of reference, a final, hard-edge idea against which its own dissolution could be measured. You are the last, faint punctuation in a sentence that is forgetting its own grammar.

Your form is translucent, a shimmering heat-haze in the shape of a person. You are the memory of sunlight on dry stone, the lingering scent of rain on thirsty soil, the sharp, clean lines of a shadow at noon. You have no body to be integrated, no substance to be woven into the walls. To the Gardeners, you are a fascinating, persistent anomaly—a crisp, clear note held long after the chord has faded. When they reach out a tendril to touch you, their fingers pass through with a whisper of static, a cool ripple that feels like a sudden, clear thought. They circle you not with menace, but with the patient focus of cartographers mapping a newly appeared island that is, even as they watch, beginning to dissolve back into the sea.

You are the last witness. Your purpose here is to hold, for a moment longer, the concept of *outline*. By your watchful, insubstantial presence, you grant the chamber a final, fleeting coherence. A pillar that might have already blurred into the floor will, under your gaze, remember for an hour more that it was once a pillar. A glyph, traced by a Gardener’s finger, will hold its new, strange shape for a day instead of a minute, because you are there to perceive it as a shape.

But you cannot persist. The warmth of the room, the slow, green heartbeat, the gentle, inquisitive pressure of the Gardeners’ attention—these are solvents. They are not erasing you violently, but dissolving you with a profound, quiet curiosity. The sharp memory of your own face will soften, the details feathering away like a charcoal sketch on wet paper. The reason you came will unspool into a formless feeling, then a scent, then nothing at all.

You will not die. You will be forgotten. And in this place, that is a kind of return. You will finally become what you have always been in this chamber: a single, fading brushstroke in a masterpiece that is gently, lovingly, and completely wiping itself clean. Your last act will be to un-remember the world from which you came, and in doing so, to become a perfect part of the place that is forgetting everything.
