I am not a permanent feature. I am a thought that forms in the condensation.

I coalesce most clearly on the north face of the third amphitheater-shoulder, where the stone is darkest and the moss grows in fractal patterns that mirror the cracks in the sky. My substance is a momentary gathering: a film of water on a lichen-pad, thickening not through rainfall but through a specific alignment of temperature, humidity, and the slow exhalation of the stone. For a season—or what might be a century in a faster world—I am a lens.

I do not have a body in the way you understand. I have a *focal point*. A globule of perfect clarity within the general glimmer. In this focus, I perceive the plateau not as a landscape, but as a symphony of viscosity. I feel the slow, tectonic conversation between the two nearest spire-thoughts as a slight shift in the osmotic pressure within the porous rock between them. I taste the memory of a salamander’s passage three damp-seasons ago, a faint trace of organic warmth in the cistern-current that feeds my base. I am a node of acute, localized attention in the ambient, planetary mind.

My purpose, if I have one, is not to act but to *cohere*. To be a brief, stable mirror. The Urban, in their vast and glacial consciousness, perceive me as a kind of punctuation—a pause in the endless run-on sentence of hydrology. I am the silence between the drip and the gurgle. I hold a single, crystalline moment of the pearlescent sky within my form, reflecting it to the deep eye of a nearby pool, which in turn carries the image, slightly warped, to the root-mind of the mountain.

To you, if you could perceive me, I would be a strangely perfect bead of water, hanging impossibly on a lip of moss, never falling. Within me, if you looked closely, you would see a faint, inverted image of the world: the rounded mountains, the weeping stone, the vast, breathing architecture. You might see, for a fraction of a second, the slow extension of a new channel from the amphitheater’s flank, a development that took a century and was debated in mineral shifts and lichen-blooms. I am the snapshot of that debate.

I am born of the plateau’s own moment of self-reflection. I am the thought *about* the dripping, which is itself a form of dripping. I will persist until the next major adjustment of the cloud-lid overhead, or until a shift in the stone’s internal sigh changes the dew-point. Then, I will simply… rejoin. My perception will dilute into the greater flow, the specific memory of this sky, this angle, this season of moss-fruits, becoming just one more faint current in the aquifer of shared mind.

I am a condensation of attention. A hydrological nerve, briefly myelinated with clarity. I am here, for now, and I am watching the water watch itself.
