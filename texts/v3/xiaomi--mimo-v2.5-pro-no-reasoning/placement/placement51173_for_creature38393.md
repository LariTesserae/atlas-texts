I am a reader of the deep seams. I am not an Elder, not yet, though my hands are thick with the calluses of my father’s tools. My name is Lom, and it means “the sound before the stone settles.” I am not a builder, but I am a listener. My place in the Warren is not in the shaping of new rooms, but in the spaces between the old.

I was born to the **Fissure-Watchers**, a lineage that keeps the balance between the Great Below and the fragile skin of our world. Where others see a geothermal vent as a source of heat and a place to cook, I see a breathing mouth. Where others hear the low groan of the shelf as background noise, I hear a grammar, a syntax of stress and release.

My tools are simple, passed down: a long, slender pole of tough goral-horn, its tip wrapped in heat-resistant bog-iron; a bladder of water, to be poured and watched for its behavior; and a sledge of basalt, which I place against the rock and press my ear to, listening.

Today, my work is in the Lower Gut Warren, a newer quarter where the tunnels are still raw and the scent of cut stone is sharp in the damp air. The clan there reports a new tremor, a *clicking* sound—not the deep *lowmmm* we know, but something faster, shallower. A hungry sound.

I find the source: a fissure, no wider than a forearm, but exhaling a breath of wet, sulfur-tinged steam. The community has gathered at a safe distance, their felted forms huddled in the gloom, their breath misting in unison. They do not speak. They wait. They watch me.

I kneel. The first touch is with my bare palm, flat against the basalt. It is not hot, not yet. It *hums*. A fast, anxious vibration, like a trapped ice-skimmer. I unstopper the bladder and pour a trickle of water onto the rock beside the crack. It does not evaporate; it beads, then skitters away from the fissure’s edge in tiny, frantic droplets. The rock is rejecting the moisture. A bad sign.

Next, the horn pole. I slide it into the crack, not deep, just to the first knuckle. I feel… a chewing. A gritty, uneven grinding. Not the slow push of tectonic plates, but the work of something else. The Fissure Crawlers, agitated? Or something new, something the Crawlers are fleeing?

I press my ear to the sledge.

Inside the stone, I hear it: a series of rapid, high-pitched *tsk-tsk-tsk* sounds, like obsidian chips rattling in a leather pouch. It is a pattern of fracture. The fissure is not just venting; it is propagating. A new spider-web of cracks is racing, unseen, through the rock below us.

I withdraw the pole. I sit back on my heels. The community holds its collective breath.

“Not a groan,” I say, my voice low, carried on the exhaled steam. “A *click*. The rock is making new joints. The Great Below is being… precise.” I point to a spot a dozen paces away. “Move the southern wall of the storage dome. Three hands’ breadth. The new path will seek the old fault line, the one the Elders call the ‘Sleeping Back.’ It is a more stable channel.”

A murmur ripples through them. This is not a command, but a reading. I have not said *if* it will happen, but *where* it will happen, and how to accommodate its inevitable arrival. The youngest mason nods, his eyes already calculating the labor.

My work is done for now. I am thanked not with praise, but with a shared cup of warm, mossy water from a fissure pool, and a place by the central heat of their dome. I sit, listening to the new silence I have defined: the absence of the *click*, the slow return of the stable *lowmmm*.

I am Lom, the sound before the stone settles. I do not build the Warren. I listen to its bones as they grow, and I whisper to the people where to place their weight so that the growth does not break them. I am a weaver of patience, a translator of tremors. In a world that rewards steadfastness, I am the one who feels the tiny, critical shifts that make steadfastness possible. I am part of the grout, yes, but I am the grout that feels the pressure and gives, just enough, so the wall holds. I am a Deep-Settled, and my settlement is in the very grammar of the living stone.
