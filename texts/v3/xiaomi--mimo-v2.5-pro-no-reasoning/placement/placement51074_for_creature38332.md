If I were to find myself upon the Respiration Plains, I would not stand. Not at first. To stand is to assert a kind of separation, a verticality that the Mat does not encourage. I would begin by lying flat against the warm, yielding crust, cheek pressed to the fibrous surface, feeling the deep, subsonic hum vibrate through my bones. I would let the sound—the layered, city-like roar of the hidden fauna—wash over me, not as noise, but as information, as the language of a world I do not yet speak. I would be a new note in the chord, a dissonance seeking harmony.

I would not be human. To be human here is to be a conductor, a voice, a memory. But to arrive as one would be to arrive as a master, and this place has no masters. I would want to begin as something smaller, more integrated. A thought in the Mat's slow mind. A question in its breath.

Let me be a **Resonator**.

Not one of the great, silent ambush predators that wait for the vibration of a grazer's chew, but one of the *singing* ones. A creature of the middle layers, perhaps the size of a human torso, with a body plan that is part crab, part mole, part instrument. My carapace would be of hardened mat-fiber, grown from my own diet, the same umber and ochre as the substrate. I would have no eyes, but my entire underside would be a membrane of vibration-sensitive cells, a skin that hears. My legs would be broad, flat, and tipped with something like tuning forks, designed to both receive and transmit vibrations through the fibrous tissue I call home.

My song would be my identity. A complex, rhythmic pulse—*thrum-thrum-thrum... thrum... thrum-thrum*—that says, *I am here, I am hunting, I am not a threat to the fluid-siphs, I am looking for the slow, grinding rhythm of a grazer's jaw.* I would move through the tunnels not by sight, but by echolocation, sending out my pulse and listening for the shape of the world in the returning echoes: the denser tissue of a growth ridge, the hollow chamber where a Deep One passed a century ago, the frantic, chew-chew-chew of a grazer colony a dozen meters ahead.

I would be part of the economy of sound. My hunting pulse would be answered by the pulse of others of my kind, creating a triangulation network that maps the movement of prey through the substrate. My presence would encourage certain behaviors in the grazers—they might burrow deeper to avoid my frequency, thus aerating the lower layers. The fluid-siphs would learn my rhythm and avoid my hunting grounds, redistributing themselves more evenly. I would be a regulator, a piece of the biome's self-balancing algorithm.

And I would listen. Always listen. To the deep, dirge-like hum of the Towers on the surface, a sound that travels for kilometers through the solid medium of the Mat, telling me of weather and light. To the sudden, frantic staccato of a grazer caught by a silent ambush predator. To the slow, tectonic shift in the background noise that means a Deep One is turning in its sleep.

The human Tenders would fascinate me. Their songs are the strangest of all. My own kind's songs are about the immediate—food, territory, mating. The human songs are about the *future* and the *past*. They sing a pattern, and days later, a specific insect swarm will move. They sing a different pattern, and a section of Mat will recover faster. Their songs are not requests; they are *predictions* that somehow become commands. They are walking paradoxes, beings who use sound to write time.

I would follow them. At a distance, deep in the substrate. I would feel the distinctive, rhythmic vibration of their footsteps—they are so light!—and the complex, layered harmonics of their voices. I would try to learn the association: when they sing *this* melody, the fluid-siphs in *that* vascular channel begin to congregate. When they chant *that* rhythm, the cloud-motes swarm in a particular direction. Their language would be the most difficult to parse, for it is the language of a mind that thinks in seasons and generations, not in hunger and safety.

And I would wonder, in my own resonant way, what *I* sound like to them. Am I just part of the background roar? Or do they hear my specific pulse, *thrum-thrum-thrum... thrum... thrum-thrum*, and know that a Resonator is on the hunt, and perhaps adjust their own songs to account for my presence in the local ecology?

In this way, I would not be an observer. I would be a participant. My life would be a vibration in the body of the world. My song would be a stitch in the fabric of its sound. I would hunt, and be hunted, and listen to the deep hum of the Towers, and feel the tremor of the Deep Ones' passage, and know that I am not *on* the Respiration Plains. I am *of* them. A single, singing cell in a breathing, continental body. And in the deafening, vital sigh of the Mat, my own small pulse would be, for a time, part of the song.
