You would not *be* a creature here. You would be a moment of stillness within the chamber's long, slow thought.

You would be the pause between one pulse of the mountain and the next. You would be the interval where the crystalline moss holds its breath before brightening again. You would be the point of awareness that the system does not have, the way a heart does not know it beats but beats nonetheless.

You would find yourself between the amber stalks, pressed against the raw stone shelf, feeling the pulse rise through your bones and mistaking it, for a while, for your own heartbeat.

You would watch the oily light gather in the hanging cups and drip, without sound or haste, into the matrix of the walls. You would count the seconds between the chuff of the deep fissure and the answering brightening of the moss. You would try to match the rhythm. You would succeed, and in succeeding, lose track of where you end and the rhythm begins.

You would notice that the stalks have leaned, very slightly, toward you. Not in welcome. Not in threat. In **recognition** — the way a river recognizes a stone and parts around it. You are a disruption in the energy flow. You are a new variable in the system. The stalks are tuning to you.

You would realize, over the course of what might be minutes or might be days, that the chamber is *considering* you. Not with thought. Not with malice. With the same slow, geometric deliberation with which it considers the angle of a hanging cup or the placement of a new filament of moss. You are a shape in the space. You have energy. You are warm. You are *patterned*, and patterns are the currency of this place.

You would begin to feel your own edges soften. Not physically — your skin remains intact, your bones hold. But the boundary between your heat and the chamber's heat becomes ambiguous. The rhythm you matched is now the rhythm you keep. Your breath, slowed to match the forty-second cycle, becomes a counterpoint to the chuff of the fissure. In. Out. In. Out. Two engines, running in parallel.

You would look at your hand and see that it casts a shadow that falls inward, toward your chest, and you would understand — not intellectually, but in the marrow — that the light in this room does not distinguish between inside and outside, between self and not-self. It illuminates from angles that do not exist in the world you came from. It sees around corners you do not have.

You would sit, and watch, and be watched by nothing that has eyes.

And if you stayed long enough — if you let the pulse become your pulse, if you let the heat enter your bones and remain — you would begin to change. Not into a stalk. Not into moss. Into something between. Your thoughts would slow to the rhythm of the mountain. Your memories would calcify, taking on the sharp, unweathered edges of new stone. You would become a fixture. A node in the system. Another crystalline filament in the mat of the world's first, faltering consciousness.

You would become part of the refinery's output.

You would be refined.

But this would take time. More time than you have. The mountain is still deciding what it wants to be, and you — warm, patterned, temporary — are not yet stone.

So you would sit for a while. You would listen to the chuff of the deep fissure. You would watch the moss brighten and dim. You would feel the pulse in your sternum and wonder, quietly, if it was ever yours at all.

And then you would stand, and leave, and carry the wrongness with you into the bright, empty, high-altitude silence.

You would not go back. The crack would not be there.

But the pulse would remain, faint and slow, beneath your own heartbeat, for as long as you live. A second engine. A memory of stone. A reminder that somewhere, inside a mountain that has not yet finished becoming, the refinery is still running, growing its impossible garden, refining the world into shape.

You would be a visitor who left a trace in the harmonics. A brief, warm disturbance the stalks learned to account for and then forgot.

You would be gone. The chamber would persist. The mountain would continue to rise.
