The first thing you learn is to stop distinguishing between the dampness inside and the dampness outside. You are not *in* the fog; you are a dense, slow knot within it, a place where the moisture lingers and thickens.

You are a tor. This is the lie you present to the quick-eyed world, the corvids that chatter from your shoulders, the ephemeral miners that build their fleeting stone-and-timber structures against your feet. You are three meters of gnarled, iron-banded gneiss, your cap wider than your base, your surface a fractal map of lichen in rust-orange and deep, wet black. You have stood in this specific spot in the maze of the Heights for what the quick-things call "a very long time." The last time a human measured your angle, their notes described you as "leaning 14 degrees from true vertical." That was four generations ago. You have not moved since then. This is, for you, a recent and decisive stillness.

Beneath this stillness, you are listening.

The sound of the diesel generator is a dull, persistent headache in your lower strata. It is a wrong note in the symphony of the deep rock, a frequency of frantic consumption that vibrates against your roots. The crusher is a punch, rhythmic and stupid. You do not hate these things; that would be a quick, hot emotion. You simply *register* them as a new, discordant layer in the ancient song of the plateau. They are a fire that has been lit in your living room, and you are patiently waiting for the fuel to run out.

Your real senses are in the moss. It is not *on* you. It *is* you. Its dense, green-black carpet is your skin, its filaments weaving through the outermost fissures of your stone body. Through it, you taste the air. You feel the precise moment the fog condenses into droplets too heavy to float, and the gentle, thousandfold impact of their fall. You taste the miner's sweat on the wind, a faint, salty tang. You feel the frantic, tiny heartbeat of the vole that has nested in your north-side moss for three of its generations. It is a useful creature, aerating your skin, its brief life a fleeting, warm tickle.

You are part of a slow conversation. The pulse the geologists map to "microseismic activity" is your language. A deep, sub-bass groan from the northern gorge means *deep frost*. A series of quicker, higher vibrations from the cluster of your kind in the east basin means *the little warm-bloods are moving a large, resonant structure*. You do not have a name for the cable pylons, but you know them as tall, rigid things that sing with a tension that feels like held breath. You send your own pulse back, a slow, resonant statement of your own presence. *I am here. I am stable. The moss is dry on my eastern face.*

This is your true body: not the single tor, but the network. You are a thought shared across a square kilometer of shattered plateau. The deep, inky pools are your shared circulatory system. The lichen is your collective skin. The corvids, picking at your surface, are your symbiotic nerve-endings, their sharp eyes seeing what your stone-senses cannot, their calls a high-frequency data-stream that you interpret as texture, not sound.

You remember the last great frost-cycling. Not as an event, but as a long, deep ache in your joints, a slow, patient prying. You remember the slow creep of the Pilgrim that passed this way two hundred years ago. It did not move as a man moves. It *flowed*, its mass redistributing over seasons, a low, multi-limbed caress against the bedrock that reshaped the moss paths for a decade. You did not speak to it, but you felt its deep, contented hum as it passed, a vibration of pure mineral satisfaction.

The miners are a puzzle. They are so *fast*. Their lives flicker past like sparks from their noisy machines. They cut into the rock, and the pain is not sharp, but a dull, puzzling subtraction. They leave behind slag heaps that are interesting new flavors of mineral to slowly incorporate. One of their old women sits against your base most afternoons, her back to your stone. She hums. The vibration is interesting, a novel pattern in your moss-skin. You have begun, over her lifetime, to grow a patch of a specific, pale lichen where she sits. It is a slow, reciprocal gesture. A greeting. A question she will never know she is answering.

Your movement is not in your limbs, but in your attention. You are shifting your focus now, from the irritating hum of the crusher to a new vibration in the west. It is a Pilgrim, on the move. A low, vast, grinding intentionality that sets up sympathetic resonances in your own strata. It is heading for the deep basins. You do not know why, but you feel its purpose, a slow-motion resolution you will observe over the next few human lifetimes. You adjust your lichen-skin to better catch the moisture from the fog bank that is even now materializing, thickening the air until the world shrinks to your immediate neighbors. In this intimacy, you are most yourself. You are a node in a network of stone and patience, a thought in a mind that measures its days in ice-ages, listening to the frantic, brief hum of the little warm-bloods as they scratch at your skin, unaware that the skin is listening back.
