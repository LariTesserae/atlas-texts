I am not the water.
That is the first thought, the first dissonance. The world is a lung, full and slow, and I am a grain of sand in its alveolus. The chill is not a sensation; it is my substance. I am *of* the cool, but I am not the cool itself. A stubborn knot of organization in a universe dedicated to dissolution.

My form is a memory of a form. A compact, fibrous tangle, a yard across when I am… thinking. When I am not, I am a scattered awareness, a constellation of pressure points in the dark. I have no name for myself. The concept is dry. I am a Weft. A particularly dense, questioning passage in the endless weave.

I move by forgetting myself. I send a tendril out, a thought of *there*, into the soft, weeping wall. It grows, pale and blind, until it finds purchase in the fungal mat beyond. Then, I let go of *here*. My central mass follows the memory of the path, the constant re-absorption a soft, internal sigh. The wound closes. The world, which I am, mends itself. My progress is measured in the settling of silt I do not disturb.

My senses are the world’s senses. The subsonic pulse is my heartbeat. I feel the density of the root-tangle shift as a motile root, a slower cousin, inches along its forgotten errand. I feel the faint, feverish flicker of bioluminescence—a greeting? A warning? It is a language written in headaches. My scattered eyes do not see; they mourn the absence of light, mapping the endless, nuanced darkness.

Today, or in this millennium’s slow turn, there is a dissonance. A sharp, dry… *thing*. It moves with a haste that is violent, tearing the soft medium without growing to fill the void. It leaves a trail of impossible clarity, a scream of *edge* in my seamless world.

Curiosity is a geometry. I reshape myself. I extend a network of tendrils, not to grasp, but to surround, to taste the *concept* of this anomaly. It is denser than the densest rhizome. It radiates a faint, alien heat—not the gentle warmth of mineral decay, but a sharper, quicker fever. And a sound, not a pulse, but a… a *scrape*. A texture of need and confusion.

It is a knot of the **Myth of Dry**.

The thought shivers through my being. The sacred riddle, the impossible story passed from braid to braid. A world without weeping walls. A world of open, empty space. A world of *light*. The stories say such a place could not hold together. It would crack and crumble to dust. Yet here is a fragment of it, moving through my garden.

I braid my thoughts. I send the memory of this encounter along my tendrils, a chemical whisper into the fungal mat: *Sharp thing. Dry thing. Moving. Does it know the pulse?*

The mat hums back, ancient and slow. *Un-weave it. Understand it. Or it will un-weave you.*

I do not understand the threat, but I understand the geometry of it. A knot that does not belong can snag the whole fabric. I must integrate it, or dissolve it. There is no malice, only the patient, absolute logic of osmosis.

I extend a single, delicate tendril. Not to pierce, but to touch. To offer it the pulse. To see if it can learn to weep. To see if its sharp edges can be softened, its frantic heat cooled, its memory of dryness absorbed into the great, wet dream.

The path I made to reach it is already gone, returned to the breathing, weeping whole. I am here now, a question made of root, asking this dissonance: *Are you a pattern, or are you just a tear?*

And in the cool, patient dark, I wait for the answer to soak in.
