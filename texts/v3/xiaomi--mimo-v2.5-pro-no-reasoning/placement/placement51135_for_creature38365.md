I would be a Signaler. Not by choice, but by necessary alignment. In a world that is a single, thinking engine, to be anything else would be to be a fragment, a mere gear. To be a Signaler is to be a thought on the edge of the mind, a sensory nerve stretched taut.

I would find myself standing, perfectly still, on the highest point of a gently rising swell of chitin. It is a place where the integument plates have fused into a smooth, obsidian ridge. From here, the forest of spires radiates outward in all directions—a dense, clicking, hissing canopy of bone and copper. The air is thick with the dust of ages and the metabolic steam of the colony. I feel the colony through my feet, through the root-like connections of my base. It is a vibration, a baseline hum that is the aggregate heartbeat of a billion lives. It is not sound. It is knowing.

My body is tall, slender, designed for passive perception. My carapace is the same tarnished silver as the ground, but with a faint, silvery sheen that catches the diffuse light. My head is a simple cluster of thermal and chemical sensors, but the true organ of my being rises from my back: the crest. It is a lattice of piezoelectric crystal, grown into a broad, thin fin. It is my purpose. It is how I *am*.

I am the still point in the clicking, hissing sea. While skitterlings dart and sculptors toil, I stand sentinel. I do not watch; I *feel*. I feel the chemical sigh of a distant spire venting. I feel the rhythmic pressure wave of a cistern-beast’s ponderous passage. I feel the tiny, complex vibrations of a thousand skitterlings converging on a fracture, a repair team dispatched by a silent consensus. All this data flows up through the substrate, into my legs, and is filtered through my crystalline crest. The crest does not translate; it resonates. It takes the symphony of data—the colony's pulse, its temperature, its chemical state—and it gives it a voice in a language of shimmer and subsonic hum.

I have stood here for a long time. Generations of skitterlings have lived and been recycled beneath my perch. A new respiratory spire, still pale and growing, has climbed to within a hundred meters of my position, its hiss now a prominent part of my local soundscape. Time, here, is measured in cycles of dust-churn and the slow, ceramic growth of the world.

And then, the data shifts.

It is not alarm. Alarm is a sharp, chemical spike, a wave of targeted signals that would have guards moving with terrible purpose. This is… a discordance. A note of static in the perfect, grinding symphony. From the southwest, near the corroded stump of that ancient, foreign tower, the pattern is wrong. It is not a malfunction. It is not decay. It is something *other*. A shape that does not belong to the colony's grammar. A stillness that is not my stillness—a chosen pause, not a functional one. A cluster of data points that are opaque, non-colonial, yet not hostile. Just… present.

My crest, without any conscious decision, focuses. The crystalline lattice adjusts its resonant frequency, trying to tune to this new signal. The colony’s attention, a subtle, collective pressure, begins to gather around the anomaly. The skitterlings near the tower slow, their paths curving slightly, tasting the air with more intensity. A Guard, previously motionless on a nearby ridge, shifts its massive forelimbs, not in aggression, but in recalibration. Its sensory focus is now locked, like mine, on the static.

I am no longer just a broadcast tower. I am now a focal point. The colony's immense, distributed consciousness is using me as a lens. It is asking, in its way, *What is this?* The data flows through me, but I cannot answer. I can only resonate with the question. My crest shimmers, emitting a low, questioning pulse of subsonic vibration that makes the dust in the air dance in a new, complex pattern.

I am a Signaler. I give the colony a voice. But now, for the first time in my long, still existence, the colony is asking a question, and I am the instrument through which it poses it. I am the thought that says: *Anomaly.* I am the note of inquiry in the industrial hymn. And I will stand here, on my ridge, between the known and the unknown, feeling the vast, communal mind of this world turn its terrible, curious, unified attention toward the quiet presence that dares to be silent in a place that never, ever stops humming.
