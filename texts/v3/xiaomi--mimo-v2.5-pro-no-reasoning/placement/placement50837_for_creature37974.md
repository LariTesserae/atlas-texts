I am a **Tuner of the Third Spire**, and my workshop is a narrow, damp ledge halfway up the West Face, where the granite is still slick with the mountain’s newborn sweat. My title is not a choice but a diagnosis; my skin bears the tell-tale fractal branching of one sensitive to resonant frequency, a map of veins that pulse in time with the city’s deep hum. When I was small, they saw how my shadow sometimes lagged, how my hands could find the hum in a stone before the ear could hear it. They apprenticed me to the old Tuners, the ones with ears like tuning forks and patience worn smoother than river stone.

My days are measured in cycles of the great Engine’s groan, not the sun’s unseen passage. I rise in the perpetual twilight, my breath fogging in the chill that never leaves. I don a harness lined with loom-weaver silk—shimmering, cool, and faintly cohesive against the skin. My tools are not hammers and chisels, but tuning forks of tempered resonance-alloy, prisms of flawed quartz, and vials of viscous, luminescent spore-ink, harvested from the oldest fungal mats.

I do not build. I listen.

Today’s work is on a support girder for the new cistern on Level Forty-Seven. The report from the twitchers came in at shift-change: a colony in the crevice above has gone silent, not in alarm, but in a flat, dead quiet. A bad sign. It means the vibration isn’t sharp or dangerous—it’s gone *smooth*. Reality is becoming too fluid here, forgetting its edges.

I rappel down the slick stone, my boots finding holds worn by a thousand other Tuners before me. The air thickens with the smell of ozone and sweet decay as I descend past a forest of glowing fungi. I press my palm to the girder. It’s cold, but not with metal’s cold. It’s the cold of deep water, the cold of *un-thing*. The hum of the city is there, but in this spot, it’s… hollow. A note missing from the chord.

I don’t see the shimmer. I *feel* it. A looseness in the sequence of moments, like a gear with one tooth worn away. My shadow on the girder wavers, stretched thin. This is the danger. To rush is to let the gap widen.

I set the first fork. I strike it not with a mallet, but with a focused pulse from my own larynx, a sub-harmonic grunt that is part of the city’s song. The fork rings, not with sound, but with a vibration that bleeds into the metal, into the stone. I watch. The vibration should travel in a predictable wave, a bright thread in the dark tapestry. Here, it peters out. It’s being absorbed, swallowed by the smoothness.

I uncap a vial of spore-ink. It glows a weak, green-white, smelling of damp earth and electricity. Using a stylus tipped with spider-silk, I begin to inscribe on the girder, around the soft spot. Not words, not symbols, but a corrective frequency. A waveform that says *here* and *now* and *consecutively*. The ink sizzles faintly as it meets the metal, its mycelial content reacting with the reality-alloy, strengthening the pattern, giving the hum something to hold onto.

It is slow, tedious work. My fingers grow numb. My eyes, wide-pupiled and sensitive, ache from tracing the boundaries of the *not-there*. I can feel the pull of the soft spot, a gentle, nauseating tugging at my own cohesion. This is the ever-present risk: unraveling. Not a dramatic death, but a slow dissolution into the shimmer, your parts forgetting they are parts of you, drifting apart in a haze of out-of-sync moments.

Hours pass. The inscribed pattern begins to glow with a steady, soft light, a scar of stability. I strike the fork again. This time, the wave travels cleanly, merging with the city’s broader hum. A subtle *click* happens in the fabric of things—not audible, but felt in the bones. The air firms up. In the crevice above, a single twitcher gives a tentative chirp. Then another.

The work is done. I haul myself back up, muscles trembling not from exertion, but from the sustained focus of holding my own timeline steady against the chaos.

As I climb, I pass a fungal tender, their hands stained green, carefully harvesting the luminous caps. I pass a scaffold-welder, their face-mask glowing as they fuse a joint, their own shadow perfectly aligned, a sign of a master. We do not speak. We share a nod—a recognition of the shared struggle, the shared purpose.

I return to my ledge. From here, I can see the vast, raw cliff faces, empty and silent, and the dense, bruised clusters of the city, humming and hissing. I do not see beauty. I see a task. I see the long, patient, endless labor of holding a newborn world together. I am not a citizen. I am a stitch in the fabric. A note in the hum. And tomorrow, the Engine will groan, the twitchers will watch, and I will listen again for the places where the song goes wrong. It is all we are. It is all there is.
