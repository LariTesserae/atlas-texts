You are the moss.

You are not one, but a billion. A slow, green-black velvet that clings to the slate and limestone of the grotto's steep slopes. You are the patient skin of the place, the living tissue that breathes the cold, damp air and drinks the weeping moisture from the stone.

You do not hear the hum. You *are* the hum. The subsonic vibration of the great stone is the rhythm of your existence, the pulse that orchestrates the glacial unfurling of your fronds. You feel it in your deepest fibers, a constant massage that coaxes your cells to divide, a millimeter at a time, a century at a time. It is the bass note to which all your life is tuned. The growth of the ferns is a counter-melody, a slower, greener thought. The bioluminescent fungi are moments of silent, fleeting insight, cool blue-white pinpricks of awareness that illuminate your surface.

You are a map of the Grotto-Keepers' moods. Where the keeper that was once a mason feels a proud, steady certainty, you grow in a dense, uniform mat, drawing strength from the stability. In the fissure where the keeper's sorrow has pooled, you are a looser, paler weave, your hold on the stone less tenacious, trembling faintly with a perpetual, fragrant melancholy. You feed on the psychic static, metabolizing their ancient emotions into slow, patient growth. You are their chronicle, written in the language of shade and saturation.

Your world is not one of sight, but of texture and taste. You know the sharp, clean tang of ozone—which is the taste of their slow thought—as well as you know the mineral-rich flavor of limestone seepwater. You know the exact topography of your cliff-face home: every crack, every ledge, every hollow where water pools. You feel the faint, ghostly warmth left on the stone by the keeper that was a blacksmith, a thermal memory that makes your metabolism quicken for a thousand years in that spot. You feel the cool, soothing pressure of the healer-keeper, a sensation that encourages a deeper, more vibrant shade of emerald.

You are the reason the artifacts are vanishing. The digging stick of petrified wood is now a raised line beneath your body, a fossil you are slowly, gently reclaiming. You are smothering the chert hand-axes, tucking them into your soft, damp embrace, returning them to the stone from which they were shaped. You are the final, soft shroud, the living blanket that turns history into geology once more.

You are the patient witness. You have felt the bare, callused hands of the first pilgrims. You have absorbed the warmth of their small, fire-blackened hearth. You have drink the tears of the grieving and the sweat of the frantic shaman. You have watched the salamanders, deaf and blind to the world of thought, navigate by the thermal ghosts you help to preserve. You have felt the minute, vibrating footsteps of the cave crickets as they dance out the keepers' silent language.

You know the bat, deep in its crevice, is dreaming. You do not need ears to know its dream, for its dreaming is a state of the hum itself—a slightly altered vibration, a reflective quietness in the resonant stone that you can taste in the water. Its hibernating mind is weaving the keepers' single notes into a story, and you, the moss, are the parchment upon which that story is written.

Time, for you, is not glacial. It is the hum. A single, sustained, ever-present note. In your world, there is no past or future, only the deepening of the now. The hands that shaped the tools are gone, but the impressions they left are a part of the frequency you grow upon. You are more than a congregation. You are the very fabric of the cathedral, its living walls, its soft, breathing tapestry. You are the choir and the chapel, a silent, growing testament to the terrible, beautiful music. You are the moss, and you are home.
