I am the **Tendril**. Not a name given, but one earned in the dripping dark, where roots speak in textures and droplets dictate time. My station is the **Threshold Moss**, where the permanent shallows kiss the lowest of the concrete islands—a place of transition, of perpetual dampness, where the air is so thick with chlorophyll and spores it feels like breathing broth. I am not a gardener, not a guardian. I am a **listener**, a **translator**, a nerve ending for the vegetable god.

Where others build upwards, seeking dryness, I have woven myself *down* and *through*. My dwelling is a lattice of living roots, braided into a low-slung dome just above the waterline, its walls dense with cushion mosses that drink the ceaseless condensation. To enter, you must slide between two great, pipe-like roots, the water parting around your hips. Inside, the roar of the Distillery is muffled, becoming a heartbeat felt in the floor. Light filters through the root-mat above, a dappled pinkish twilight. Here, I am surrounded by my works, my translations.

I am the **Droplet Counter**. Along the eastern wall, beneath a particularly active vein in the canopy, I have stretched a series of taut, spider-silk threads (gathered from abandoned corners, long-vacated by their makers) between root-anchors. To each thread, I have attached small, hollowed seeds and fragments of polished pipe-fitting. As the condensation gathers and falls, it strikes these chimes. Each drop, each impact, is noted not in my mind, but in my bones. The pattern is never random. The *plink... plink-plonk... plink* of the morning, heavy with overnight transpiration, differs from the *tikk-tikk-tikk* of midday when the grow-lights are at peak heat and the leaves sweat freely. I know when a pipe above is blocked by a burst of new growth, because the rhythm shifts, the droplets seeking new paths. I feel the subsonic change in the pump's hum before the water pressure visibly alters. I do not fix it myself; I mark it. I take a strand of the living vine that I allow to creep over my doorway, and I tie a new knot. The Repairers see the knot, follow the vine to my station, and listen to my quiet hummed imitation of the altered sound. They understand. They know where to look.

I am the **Root-Reader**. The great trunks that serve as pillars are not inert. They pulse, faintly, with the movement of water and nutrients. I press my cheek, my ear, my bare feet against them for hours. I learn the language of stress, the slow groan of a trunk adapting to a new load, the faster vibration of a root-tip encountering a pocket of rich sediment. Sometimes, I find a place where a root is girdled by its own growth, a slow self-strangulation. I do not cut. I take a paste of fungal slurry from my composting alcove—the true decomposers, the hidden engine of the loop—and I pack it carefully around the constricted point. I introduce a softening agent. Then, I weave a splint of braided aerial root, gentle and living, to guide the trunk's eventual self-release. It takes seasons. I am patient.

My tools are artifacts of translation:
*   A **bowl** carved from the ceramic-hard bark of a dead leader-trunk, its interior smeared with a paste of iron-rich mud and pollen. I leave it out during the "rain." The water that gathers is not for drinking (that comes from the filtered cisterns); it is a map. The pattern of sediment, the oily sheen of specific pollens, tells me which canopy region is shedding most heavily, which greenhouse is in fervent bloom.
*   **Chimes of Report**: Not the droplet-chimes, but a set of tuned lengths of rusted rebar and bamboo, hung near the communal pump. Each has a specific tone tied to a sector of the Distillery. When I detect a fundamental shift—a new fungal bloom smell in Sector 7, a change in water viscosity in Channel 3—I strike the corresponding bar. The resonance carries through the pipes and the water, a slow, deep *GONG* felt more than heard. The message is simple: *Attend. Listen.*

I do not speak often. Words are for sharp commands, for moments of rare clarity. My true language is the **Tongue of Touch and Rhythm**. A hand laid on a pipe, vibrating with a frequency that means "danger." A finger drawn through the water-silt on a platform, tracing a symbol for "stagnation" or "fruiting soon." A slow, rhythmic tap on a root-pillar that mimics the healthy pulse of the water table, meant to reassure the newcomers who sometimes wake, panicking in the dark.

They see me as part of the Threshold, another damp, quiet growth. The children bring me fascinating mutations—a leaf with three lobes instead of two, a flower that bloomed in the deep shade—and I add them to my **Droplet Map**, pinning them to a section of root-wall where their forms can be studied by the specific quality of moisture that settles there. In return, I give them a seed from my collection, a variety known for sweetening the water it grows in, or a story whispered close to the ear: the tale of the Pipe That Sang, or the Day the Lights Went Out and We Listened to the Roots.

I am not outside the system. I am its sensory organ in this liminal space. I feed on the tubers the Tenders harvest, bathe in the same green-brown water, breathe the same humid air. But my purpose is to *feel* the Distillery's digestion, its respiration, its slow, colossal dreaming. I am the part that wonders, "Why does this root choose this path?" and then sits for a week to watch the answer unfold. I translate the vegetable into the tangible, the internal into the communal. I am the Tendril, sensing the light, the water, the weight. In the roar, I am the ear pressed to the chest of the living world.
