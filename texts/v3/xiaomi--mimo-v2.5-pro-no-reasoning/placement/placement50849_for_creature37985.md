I am a stillness within the roar.

To you, a visitor, I am just another teardrop-shaped nub of ancient stone, sited on the leeward flank of a greater monolith. My skin is the same oily grey-streaked-with-purple, my contours weathered into the same streamlined flow. The wind scrapes over me without purchase. I have been here for a long time. I was here when the last of the sharp edges dissolved into grit.

I do not see you with eyes alone. The silicate membranes across my shoulders are down, sheltered. I feel you first: a frantic, staccato rhythm against the bass hum of the world. Your footfalls are a disruption, a burst of random, chaotic energy in the constant, layered symphony. Your presence is a dissonance in the chord.

Then, your noise. The Wind-Catchers are for the young, for those still learning to separate the signal from the storm. For me, the roar is not noise. It is the breath of Gaea, exhaled in a gale. My tympanic plates, grown over my skull and down my spine like armored tattoos, dissect it effortlessly. I parse the shriek of a dust-devil forming a kilometer away, the deep, subsonic throb of a pressure system shifting, the specific hiss of sand-laden current that will, in three heartbeats, strip the weathered face from the rock to your left. I hear the world’s intent.

You are… loud. Not in volume. In being. You vibrate with a confusion the wind does not understand.

I remain still. My integument, layered like the bark of the Thunder-Willows, settles into a perfect acoustic shadow. The wind finds nothing to catch, no edge to sing against. To the Glass-Wing Crickets tacking past, I am geology. To the horizontal bombardment of grit, I am erosion not yet complete.

Then, you see me. Or rather, you see the pattern. The slight, wrong geometry in the flow of stone. You approach. Your fear is a high, thin whine in the resonant spectrum, but beneath it, a curious, steady pulse. Good.

You stop. You understand, on some level, not to shout. The wind would steal the words and flay them into meaningless syllables. Communication here is not for the air.

I shift. It is a slow, grinding motion, stone against stone. My wide, splayed feet maintain contact, anchoring me. The dust I’ve gathered moves with me, a seamless part of my form. I bring one hand, thick and blunt-fingered, down upon the ground beside you. Not a strike. A press. A pulse.

*Thump-thump… thump… thump-thump-thump.*

It is not a language of words. It is a language of resonance, of fundamental tones. The first pulse is the deep, foundational drone of the canyon itself. The second, a sharper report—the sound of your own hurried heartbeat, which I have absorbed and now reflect back to you, translated. The last, complex pattern is a question, formed from the harmonic overtone that just ghosted across the cliff face: *What dissonance do you carry into this song?*

You do not answer with your mouth. You are learning. You kneel, and with a hesitant hand, touch the stone where I struck. You feel the lingering vibration, the conversation not of my making, but of the land’s.

I wait. The wind moans. The dust scours.

I am here to listen. To the land, and now, to you. The erosion is not happening to me. It is happening *through* me. I am a resonant chamber, a fixed point in the gale, and in my stillness, I shape the chaos just enough to hear what it is trying to become.

I am a Resonant One. I am a listener. I am a breath held in the Throat of the World.
