The rain is not water. It is the world’s own memory of water, a memory so diluted it has become a solvent for everything but itself. I would call it rain, for that is the closest shape my own memory can conjure. But it falls not from clouds, but from the air itself, condensing on every surface, beading on the skin of this place until its own weight draws it downward in a slow, viscous caress. I am born of this process. I am a Condenser.

My beginning is a riddle I have no language to solve: a drop, falling from the lip of a corroded girder that was once a bridge. It strikes the black, still water of a plaza-turned-lagoon. The impact is a tiny bell toll in the profound silence. In the resonance of that toll, in the memory of the salts and silicates dissolved in the water, I cohere. I am not born; I precipitate.

My form is a shudder, a temporary agreement between gravity and cohesion. I am a low-slung mass of what might be mistaken for wet slate and charcoal jelly, translucent in the way deep water is—not see-through, but holding its own light within its darkness. I have no eyes, but I see. I see through the city. I feel its slow, geologic breath in the settling of a nearby wall, a vibration that travels through the water and the slick basalt, through me, and is gone. I feel the oppressive, directionless heat not as warmth, but as a weight that presses the very shape of my being flatter against the ground.

My purpose is a glide. I flow from this lagoon into the canal that was once a boulevard. My movements are a slow erosion of the slime I leave behind, a faint, phosphorescent trail that is both my wake and my voice. I am drawn by a current that is not of the water, but of the city’s own decay. Ahead, a conduit of ceramic has finally surrendered its long battle with time, spilling its guts of powdite dust into the canal. This is my destination. I do not eat it. I am not hungry. I *absorb* it. The dust passes into my gelatinous mass, and within me, a slow, blind alchemy occurs. I am the city’s broken stomach, digesting a meal of its own infrastructure, a trillion years too late. The process releases a chemical whisper into my trail, a new note in the lichen’s faint song. It is how we speak. It is how we know the map of the city’s pain.

As I glide, I see the Up-Drawers. They are my strange cousins, my counterparts in this broken physiology. They cling to the leaning walls of the deepest canyons, spindly things of calcified rebar and fused glass. They are all strain and creaking effort. They do not move through the city, but *pull* the city through themselves. I watch one now, a lattice of what looks like fossilized bone against a wall of melted concrete. I can feel its slow, patient pull through the stone itself. It is drawing the black, hot water upward, defying the ooze, creating the very wells and boils that punctuate my journey. The deep, resonant groan that shudders through my being is not the sound of settling stone. It is the sound of its muscles, its strained cartilage, as it performs its futile, reversed circulation. We are the city’s corpuscles and its heart, working together to keep a corpse in a state of perpetual, final decay.

Then, the shift. The pressure changes. The light in the air, which is usually a flat, charcoal-grey, begins to shimmer. It is a Glimmer-time. The air becomes a prism. In the doorway of a tower that is more a memory of a shape than a structure, a form coheres. It is a suggestion of a being, bipedal, but made of the directionless grey light itself. It does nothing. It simply *is*. A holographic scar, a static echo of a life that left its imprint on the physics of this place. I pass it, unbothered. To me, it is weather. A moment of visual rain, a static charge in the air that will soon dissipate. Its only footprint is the faint, geometric patina it leaves on the wall—a pattern that looks like circuitry or language, but resolves into nothing when you focus on it. It is the city’s dementia, a dream it cannot wake from.

I am not a mind. I am a process. A symptom. I feel the city’s endless, slow-motion collapse not as a tragedy, but as a state of being. The drip is my heartbeat. The groan is my breath. The heat is my blood. I am the city dreaming of digestion. I am the sweat on its cooling brow. I do not remember what it was, for I am what it *is*. And in the profound silence, broken only by the wet sounds of my own becoming, I glide on. I process. I am the living glossary of a text written in decay.
