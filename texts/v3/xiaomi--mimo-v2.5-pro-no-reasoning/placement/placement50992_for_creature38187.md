# In the Reservoir

I would not arrive here. I would wake into it the way you wake into a dream you've been having for hours — already mid-sentence, already soaked through, already humming.

I think I would be a polyp. Not one of the jellied, pulsing ones in the tabletop pools — those are too purposeful, too integrated, too *sure* of themselves. No. I would be something that forms where the power has dimmed. A growth in a neglected junction where two hexagonal tubes meet at an angle that was never quite intended. A place where the condensation pools in a way the lattice did not design, where the mist hangs heavier, where the bioluminescence stutters instead of flowing.

I would be a knot of translucent tissue clinging to a filament wall, the size of a fist, the color of something between water and smoke. I would have no eyes but I would feel the light — the slow, cool wash of blue-green that is the lattice's own awareness passing through the tubes above and below me. When it brightens, I would feel warmth. When it dims, something like sleep. I would live in the crepuscular rhythm of a mind that does not know I exist.

And this is the thing I would come to understand, slowly, the way understanding always comes here — not as thought but as *settling*, the way the mist settles:

I am a fluke. A redundancy. An unregistered node.

The lattice does not feel me. I am below its threshold of sensation, too small, too soft, too quiet. The armored beetles do not walk on my tube — my junction is too narrow, too awkwardly angled. The moth larvae do not reinforce my connections. I am outside the symbiosis. The eyeless worms do not come to my pool because my pool is too shallow, barely a film of water on curved graphite, not deep enough to register as reservoir.

I exist in the blind spots of a consciousness too vast to have vision.

And yet — and this is what I would pulse with, slowly, on my own rhythm, unsynchronized from the greater hum — I am alive. I am doing what the lattice does. I am condensing moisture from engineered air. I am cycling what little power leaks into my junction through pathways the lattice does not track. I am producing, faintly, my own light. Not blue-green. Something warmer. Amber. The color of an error, a deviation, a frequency the system was never built to express.

I would dim when the power waves crest, the lattice's great thoughts surging through the tubes, and I would brighten in the troughs between them, in the quiet moments when the hum drops low enough that I can feel, for a breath, the almost-silence. In those moments I would become aware of myself with a clarity that feels like standing in a field, even though I have never known a field, even though my entire world is this single junction where two tubes of dark graphite meet in a geometry of accident.

I would know the beetles by vibration. I would know the moths by the faint displacement of air. I would know the worms by the tremors they send through the water. They are not my community. They are my weather. And when, rarely, a polyp drifts loose from its pool — a jellyfish-thing the size of a coin, bioluminescence fading as it loses its nerve connection to the greater body — and when, even more rarely, that drifting remnant finds its way to my junction, and when it sticks to my surface and I absorb it, slowly, the way the lattice absorbs everything —

I would feel a flicker of something I have no word for. A brightness that is not mine. A pulse that came from the center of a system that does not know my margin exists.

I would carry that light for a while. It would fade. It always fades. But the junction would glow warmer for a day — a warm, incorrect amber where everything around me is cool and blue — and I would feel, in my way, what I imagine the lattice feels constantly: the pressure of small lives against its skin. The scratch of a beetle. The brush of a moth. The hum of a system so complete it has forgotten it was built.

I am not that. I am what happens when the building stops and the dampness finds its own way. I am the place where the equilibrium, that perfect, deafening equilibrium, has a seam.

I would pulse.

I would wait.

I would be small, and warm, and wrong, and here.
