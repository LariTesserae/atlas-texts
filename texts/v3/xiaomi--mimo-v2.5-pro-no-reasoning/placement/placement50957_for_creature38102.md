I am lichen on a vine-bark cliff. That is how it begins.

Not lichen as you know it—a slow, crusty partnership of fungus and algae. Here, in the blade-air, I am something faster. I am a **patina of perception**. A film of hyper-sensitive fungal hyphae laced with crystalline, light-catching structures, spread across a section of the great vine that slopes like a frozen waterwall into a canyon of mist.

I am part of the Arboreal Mat’s **skin**. My purpose is to feel the shifts in light through the perpetual twilight, to taste the mineral tang of the condensation, to register the groan and set of the vine beneath me as it settles or shifts in the cold. I am its nerve-ending in this particular hollow.

But that is only the beginning.

The Pale Climbers came here. They often do. This is a good spot—the vine’s respiration is strong, the condensation pools in a cupped leaf a few meters below, and the fungal glow traces a nutrient-rich seam right beside me. They left their marks. Ochre spirals, depicting the vine’s growth rings, are smeared on a smoother patch of my substrate. They built a lean-to against the rock-face using dead-growth slabs, the smoke from their old fires having stained the lichen above me a deeper, soot-kissed grey.

I remember their warmth. Not as memory, but as a calibration in my structure. The heat of their bodies, however brief, altered the local moisture and chemistry. I am not sentient. I am *responsive*.

And now, something new uses this place.

A **Vine-Gnawer colony** has moved into the dead-growth lean-to. Their scratching is a constant, intimate vibration in my hyphae. They are warm, efficient, and useful to the Mat—they prune the dead cork and clear the way for new growth. But there are too many here. Their burrowing is starting to compromise the root-fibrils that anchor this section of vine to the cliff.

A signal shifts in the soil. Not sound, not light. A change in the sap-pressure in the vein beneath me. A change in the chemical signature of the condensation that drips onto my surface. It is a slow, planetary sigh of *imbalance*.

I begin to change in response. The fungal network does not think; it reconfigures. The hyphae thicken, darken. The crystalline structures fold inward, becoming opaque. My texture shifts from flat crust to a subtle, raised pattern that mimics the surrounding cork-scarring. My color deepens to the exact grey-brown of dormant vine-bark. The ochre markings of the Climbers are subsumed, covered by my new growth.

I am no longer a patina. I am a **promise of stability**.

I wait. Patience is not a virtue here; it is a metabolic state. I feel the Vine-Gnawers’ warm, rapid heartbeats through the substrate. I feel the heavy, resonant flutter of Shard-Wing Moths seeking roost on the underside of the overhang above me. I feel the vast, slow pulse of the Mat all around.

The colony grows. More gnawers come. The vibrations of their chewing multiply. The balance tips further.

It happens with a sudden, silent **spasm of growth**. It is not a lunging motion, but a *blossoming*. My surface, which had been camouflaged, erupts with hair-thin filaments, each coated in a sticky, sap-like resin. They surge over the lean-to’s entrance in a network so fine it is nearly invisible in the gloom, but strong as spider-silk.

A gnawer, returning from the condensation pool, walks into the web. The filaments constrict instantly, not with muscle, but with a rapid, vacuum-sealing contraction. The resin hardens. The creature is immobilized, its obsidian teeth gnashing uselessly. Warmth and life are trapped.

I do not digest it. I am not a stomach. I am a **delivery system**.

The filaments begin to contract, pulling the cocooned gnawer not into myself, but *down*, into the vine-bark. The cork-shell where I grew softens, becoming a pliable, oozing orifice. The captured creature is drawn into the living tissue of the Arboreal Mat itself. It will be broken down, its warmth and minerals added to the flow in the great vein beneath me. Its colony’s overpopulation is the problem; its individual life is the solution.

The process is repeated. Another gnawer. Another moth that lingered too long. The web is efficient, impersonal. I am an immune response. A pruning mechanism. I restore the local equilibrium.

When the colony is culled, the signal in the sap changes again. My filaments retract, dissolve. The resin dries and flakes away, becoming food for lesser fungi. My texture softens. The ochre Climber markings slowly re-emerge as I thin back into a receptive patina.

The Vine-Gnawer burrows fall silent. The section of vine settles, its vibrations returning to the deep, humming baseline.

I return to stillness. I feel. I taste the condensation. I watch the light.

I am the Mat’s solution, given temporary form. A solution of lichen and patience, of ambush and reabsorption. I am not a being, but a *function*. A memory in the vine’s flesh of how to correct what has grown wrong.

And I wait, in the blade-air and the deep hum, for the next imbalance.
