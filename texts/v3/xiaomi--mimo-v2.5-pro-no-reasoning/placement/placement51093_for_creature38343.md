I am a Listener. Not by choice, but by the stone's decree. Before I learned the tap-tongue, before I could read the weeping patterns in the wall, the rock chose me. I was born on a night when the mountain hummed a new, low note—a resonance that vibrated in my very marrow. The elders pressed their palms to the living wall of my birthing-terrace and felt my small heart beating in time with it. They said the rock had already begun to teach me its language.

My first memories are of sound and texture, not sight. The constant, deep hum that is the mountain’s breath. The thousand-fold percussion of drips—the sharp *plink* from a high spire, the slow, fat *plop* from a fern’s tip, the whispering *hiss* as mist coalesced and slid down a wall. I learned the world through my feet, strapped to my first, small stilts—the feel of the copper cup on the sky-foam: the solid *thud* of established ground, the hollow *tock* of a new growth-spurt beneath, the gritty slide of a fissure’s edge. My hands were never still, always pressed to the cool, damp surfaces, feeling for the subsonic messages, the news of pressure and shift that traveled through the mountain’s veins.

Today, I am tending the Spire of the Third Hum. It began as a nub of bruised-plum rock pushing up through the central canal, threatening to disrupt the flow of mist to the lower terraces. Others saw a problem. I heard a song. For a season, I have been listening to its intent, its slow, grinding *crack-crack-pause-crack* rhythm. It does not want to be a wall. It wants to be a gate.

My tools are laid out on a mat of woven Ironwood fiber. The obsidian knife, its edge so fine it is almost a rumor, for the initial, delicate cuts that tell the stone where to yield. The copper chisel, bent and re-straightened so many times it has a memory of its own in its soft curves, for the broader shaping. My anvil is a small, flat disc of the same soft metal, carried on my back.

I press my palm to the spire. It is warm, alive with its own forming. I send a vibration, a steady, patient *tap-tap-pause-tap*. *I am here. I am listening. Let us find your shape together.* The rock answers not in words, but in a subtle shift of pressure against my skin, a deepening of its hum. I begin.

The work is slow. Each stroke of the obsidian is a conversation. I do not force; I suggest. The rock is soft, yielding, but it has a grain, a direction of becoming. To cut against it is to hear a high, pained whine, a message of stress. To cut with it is to feel a sigh, a release of tension, and to see the rock flake away in clean, satisfying layers. I carve channels for the mist, guiding it around the spire’s base. I shape the upper reaches into a graceful, arching form that will catch the light and weep in harmonious drips.

As I work, the city’s other sounds weave into my focus. The rhythmic *shush-shush* of a neighbor on their stilts, tending the luminous lichen three terraces below. The distant, resonant *groan* as the far valley wall expands by a hand’s breadth. The sharp, staccato *crack-crack* from the peak—a warning of loose scree. I adjust my own rhythm in response, pausing, listening, then continuing. My body sways with the mountain. My breath is its breath.

Sometimes, I close my eyes and see with my hands. I feel the future in the rock’s tension. The shape of the arch is not in my mind; it is in the stone’s own desire. I am merely the midwife, using sharp edges and soft metal to coax it forth. When a droplet, hanging for a week from a newly carved edge, finally lets go and falls, it is a victory. It means the shape is true; the rock has accepted the new form and will weep accordingly.

I am not building a gate. I am helping the mountain grow one. I am a slow thought in a geologic mind, a patient hand in a process that measures time in the fall of water and the whisper of stone. I am a Listener. And in the deep, humming quiet, between the drips and the groans, I hear my own heart beating in time, a soft, wet rhythm in the living, weeping air. I am home.
