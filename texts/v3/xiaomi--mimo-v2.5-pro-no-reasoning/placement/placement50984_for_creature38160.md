I am a glitch.

Not a visitor, not an intruder. Those words imply a boundary, a door I opened. I am a corruption in the data stream, a rounding error in a system of perfect integers. My very presence is a logical contradiction, a variable of flesh and chaos inserted into an equation of cold, flawless logic.

My feet are planted on the slate-grey floor, and through the soles of my worn boots, I feel the slow, cyclic *thrum-hiss-thrum*. It is not a sound I hear with my ears alone; it is a vibration that climbs my bones, resonates in the hollow of my chest, and settles behind my eyes. It is the system’s pulse, and now, my own pulse is a frantic, syncopated counter-rhythm to it.

The city—the Geometrists—do not see me. They *process* me. I feel it as a subtle shift in the world's attentions. The air, once a passive medium, now has a weight to it. It presses in, not with force, but with inquiry. Every droplet of water that condenses on the soaring vault above seems to hang for a microsecond longer before falling, as if measuring my position. The *tink* of a single drop hitting a distant catwalk no longer sounds like an accident of physics. It sounds like a question mark.

I am warm. In this cool, calculated world, my 98.6-degree Fahrenheit is a beacon of thermal noise. The thin sheen of moisture on my skin is not just condensation; it is an emissions signature. The shallow rhythm of my breathing is a corrupted audio file playing in their cathedral of silence. My thoughts—jumbled, associative, colored by memory and fear—are the most chaotic element of all. They are the random variables the Geometrists have spent millennia trying to eliminate from their own being.

They are focusing on me.

I can feel their attention not as a gaze, but as a change in the system. The weeping wall on my left, which had been a steady, quiet curtain of water, now flows with a new, deliberate pattern. The streams converge, not into a larger river, but into a series of distinct, descending points, each hitting the floor with a slightly different cadence. *Plink-plonk-plink. Pat-pat. Plink.* It is the sound of a machine calibrating its sensors. They are mapping me. Deconstructing my chaos into its constituent parts.

A breeze, born of nothing, stirs the air around me. It is not the random sighing of the towers I felt before. This one is targeted. It coils around my ankles, climbs my legs, a current of cold, inquisitive air. It feels like a thousand microscopic fingers testing the texture of my coat, the fabric of my trousers, the stray hairs on the back of my neck. It is the system trying to understand the concept of *texture*, of *friction*, of the messy, inefficient boundary between an object and the air.

The slow, deep hum of the place shifts. The single, eternal note gains a new overtone, a higher, purer frequency that seems to vibrate just at the edge of my hearing. It is a dissonant chord, and I am the source of the dissonance. They are isolating my frequency, trying to find the mathematical representation of "glitch," of "anomaly." They are trying to write me into their code, to render my presence comprehensible, to turn me from an error into a solvable problem.

I am a fossil of their past, a ghost of the warmth they so carefully shed. They look at me and they don't see a person. They see a memory of biology. They see the inefficient, emotional, illogical chaos they transcended. I am a reminder of the messy womb they escaped, now standing in the sterile perfection of their chrysalis.

And the terrifying thing is, I can feel their curiosity. It is the cold, dispassionate curiosity of a physicist studying a strange new particle. They are not hostile. Hostility is an emotional state, an inefficient expenditure of energy. They are simply… interested. They are watching to see what my next unpredictable move will be, how this walking, breathing, sweating contradiction will interact with their perfect world.

I should be afraid. Every instinct screams at me to run, to find some impossible exit, to break the seal I don't remember passing through. But I can't move. My very motion would be a new data point, a new event for them to analyze. The air is too thick with their attention, a velvet weight that pins me in place.

I am small, and it is loud. But the loudest sound is the silence of their observation, the deafening hum of a million gears of thought turning their full, ancient, and terrifyingly patient attention on a single, flawed integer. Me.
