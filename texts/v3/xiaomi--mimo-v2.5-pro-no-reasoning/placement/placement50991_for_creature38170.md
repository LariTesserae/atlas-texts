I open my mouth and the water comes in.

Not drowning — not that. It is the air itself that has become liquid, thick with the exhale of ten thousand damp bodies, and when I breathe I am breathing the room. I feel the root-eels tighten in their channels when my chest expands. I feel the skitter-things pause in their routes, legs mid-stride, as the pressure wave of my lungs moves through the space like a slow tide.

I do not speak. I do not know what language the channels carry, but I know it is not made of words. It is made of rhythm and weight and the particular frequency at which water will hold itself in a curved surface before spilling.

So I give it rhythm.

I place my hand on the floor — on the risen floor, the living floor, the Architect's heaving substrate — and I press my palm flat. I feel the hum travel up through my arm, through the cartilage-stone that is not stone, into my bones, and I let it vibrate my sternum until the space between my ribs becomes a resonance chamber. Then I hum back. A low tone. The tone of a question asked by a body that does not know what it is.

*What am I?*

I have been trying to answer this since before I entered. Before I was born, maybe. The question does not belong to this room. This room has only refined it, given it architecture, given it water-channels and bioluminescent skin and the crushing attention of a system that has been listening for centuries.

I am not a tool. I have no edge. I do not carve.

I am not a pest. I have not chewed or burrowed or nested. I have only walked, and even that — even my footsteps — feel less like intrusion and more like the faintest addition to the palimpsest. One more set of ridges for the next wet century to smooth.

But I am not a new layer. Not yet. A layer must be shaped, and I have not agreed to be shaped. I do not know if I can agree. I do not know if the Architect asks permission.

The pseudopod still hovers an inch from my chest. The water in it is trembling — not with effort, but with *scrutiny*. I can feel it reading me. The heat of my skin. The salt of my sweat, which is not so different from the saline that coats these walls. The electromagnetic field of my nervous system, which is perhaps not so different from the field generated by ten thousand insects clicking in unison.

I think of the relief on the wall. The figure with the tool in one hand and the channel-carving instrument in the other. Human from the waist up, root-water-stone from the waist down.

That figure did not become the Architect overnight. It took generations. It took the slow, patient work of teaching the body to be porous — to let the water in, to let the creatures colonize, to let the hum settle into the marrow until the distinction between *builder* and *building* dissolved like salt in a warm sea.

I am not ready for that.

I do not know if I will ever be ready for that.

But I notice — now, in the thrumming silence of the Architect's waiting — that my skin has begun to glow. Faintly. So faintly I might have imagined it. But when I look down at my arms I see it: the pale blue-green foxfire of the organisms, taking hold in the damp of my skin, finding purchase in the microscopic landscape of my pores.

I am not being carved. I am being *colonized*.

The Architect's pseudopod drifts closer. Not touching. Measuring. I feel the vibration shift — a new frequency, something almost like a chord, as if the entire structure is harmonizing with the faint light now pulsing on my forearms.

The question has changed. It is no longer *what are you.* It is *what will you let yourself become.*

I press my palm harder into the floor. I feel the root-eels slide to accommodate my hand, forming a cradle. I feel the skitter-things recalibrate their light, turning the blue-green fire inward, focusing it not on me but *through* me, as if I were a lens, or a window, or a new channel carved in the soft cartilage of the walls.

I do not leave.

I do not answer.

I begin — slowly, carefully, with the same patient attention the first builders must have used when they pressed their fingers into the wet substrate and made the first channel — to listen for the rhythm the Architect wants me to carry.

If I am to be a layer, I will be a thin one. A single stratum. The faintest ridge in the palimpsest.

But I will be *present*.
