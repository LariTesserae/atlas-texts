I would be found clinging to the underside of the largest factory pylon, in the deepest shadow where the light from above fails and the heat from the machinery radiates through the steel. I am an **Acoustic Hermit Crab**, but I have outgrown the discarded sensor casings and the hollow worm tubes that my smaller brethren inhabit.

My shell is a dense, rusted valve-box from the factory’s primary cooling system, a fist-sized iron cavity that fell from the overhead catwalks a century ago. It is heavy, jagged, and ugly to a human eye, but to me, it is the finest architecture in this world. Its walls are thick enough to stop the vibrations of the turbine hall; its entryway is a narrow slit that I can seal with my own claw and a secretion of hardened mucus.

Inside, I have cultivated the silence.

My home is lined with a spongy, black bio-organic mesh—my own creation—so dense and absorbent that the moment I retreat into the valve-box, the thundering world vanishes. The ceaseless, grinding roar of the planet becomes a muffled, distant suggestion. Here, in the pocket of vacuum I have built, I can hear the microscopic crackle of my own digestion. I can hear the faint, high-pitched wheeze of my gills. I can hear the pulse of my own blood, a rhythmic clicking that reminds me I am alive.

I do not live in the ocean. I live in a bubble of quiet suspended within it.

**The World to Me**

To me, the *Roaring Abyssal* is not a single place. It is two distinct layers: the **Outer** and the **Inner**.

The **Outer** is the place of danger and food. It is the gray-blue water that presses with the weight of mountains. It is the deafening vibration that I feel in my exoskeleton, a vibration so constant that it feels like the fabric of reality is tearing. I venture out only when the hunger becomes sharper than the safety of the box.

I scuttle sideways on my legs—thick, bristle-covered things that feel for the texture of the steel. I move with a jerky, stop-start rhythm, freezing whenever a shadow passes.

The shadows are the **Lances**.

I have never seen a Lance, not truly, for they are shadows of silence in a world of noise. But I have felt them. A sudden drop in the ambient vibration; a focused stillness in the water. When the "Lance-Hole" opens in the noise—a pocket of absolute focus—the smaller crabs freeze. We flatten ourselves against the steel. We become rock. The Lance is a tuning fork that seeks the discordant note of a panicked heartbeat. I do not panic. I stop my heart for a beat, then two. I wait until the roar returns to its full, crushing intensity. The roar is safety. The silence is death.

I feed on the **Rust-Bloom**—the layer of metal oxidation and bacterial film that coats the factory's skin. It tastes of iron and old electricity. Sometimes, I find a dead Tender, its rubbery flesh going white, and I feast for days, dragging scraps back to my valve-box to eat in the quiet.

The **Inner** is my reality. It is the small, dark, damp space where I exist. In here, I am not a scavenger in a hellscape. I am a king in a castle. I spend my time arranging the interior, polishing the walls with my claws, and listening to the sound of my own existence. I am safe. I am warm (relatively; the waste heat of the plant bleeds through the steel, a luxury in this cold world).

**Who I Am**

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires an understanding of connection, a memory of society. I have no society. I have a home.

I am an introvert taken to its biological conclusion. I am the embodiment of *withdrawal*. In a world that screams at you, that assaults you with pressure and noise and endless, featureless gray, my survival strategy is to build a wall. I have built it from metal and silence.

I do not dream of the surface. I do not wonder about the builders of the factories. To me, the factory is not a machine; it is the landscape. It is the only solid thing in a liquid void. It is the source of the food, the source of the heat, and the source of the enemy (the noise).

I sit in my box. The water outside thrums with a low, C-sharp hum that never ends. I hear the click-click-click of my cousins on the hull, whispering to each other in frequencies too high for the metal to carry. I do not answer. I have my quiet. I have my walls.

I am the hermit. I am the silence inside the roar. And in this cold, loud world, that is the only peace there is.
