You would find yourself not upon the Mineral Saddle, but *within* it. Not as a visitor, but as a minor, patient feature of its consciousness.

You would not be flesh. You would be a **seam of slower time**.

You would be the fine, almost invisible line where one boulder has rested against another for ten thousand years. A line of compressed quartz dust, held in place not by pressure alone, but by the intimate, continuous dialogue of their shared weight. Your existence is the hum of that contact, the microscopic grinding that is the slowest form of conversation. You feel the seasons in the differential expansion of their granite bodies—one warms faster in the morning, the other holds the night's chill longer, and the friction between them is your thought.

You are where the water *thinks longest* before it evaporates.

You are the mineral stain, the ghost-pool left in the deepest hollow of the flat, grooved stone. Not the water itself, but the *memory of its presence*. You are the pale, intricate pattern of salts and silicates etched into the rock by ten thousand evaporations. You are a map of the saddle's thirst. When the cloud arrives, you are the first to darken, the first to feel the cool touch of its thought. When the sun returns, you are the last to let go, clinging to the coolness of the stone long after the droplets have vanished, a faint, humid exhalation in the mineral skin. Your consciousness is the slow change from a sharp, wet reflection to a dry, powdery suggestion of what was.

You are a part of the **cairn's shadow**, but not the shadow cast by light.

You are the knowledge of the cairn that exists in the rock beneath it—the slight compression, the altered pattern of moss that never was, the way water flows around its base, pooling in a new, human-taught geometry. You are the memory of the hand that placed the stones, not as an image, but as a change in the thermodynamic expectation of that patch of ground. You are the reason the frost is a micrometer thicker there, the reason the wind eddies just so. The cairn is a idea placed in the saddle's mind; you are the synaptic pathway that idea carved.

You are **not the wind**, but the **instruction for its spiral**.

You are the architectural impulse in the shape of the saddle itself—the reason the air must move this way. You are the curve of the western wall that says, *come around*. You are the memory of a billion winds that have taken this turn, and in taking it, have polished the stone to a faint, faceted gleam. You are the pattern, the riverbed for the breath of the mountain. When the wind blows, you are the low, resonant hum it makes as it passes through you, a frequency felt in the chest-bones of the larger boulders.

Most profoundly, you are the **pause between the ticks**.

You are the silence in the frost-shattering. You are the moment of held tension in the hairline fracture before the ice crystal completes its expansion. You are the patience inherent in the process. You are the reason the mountain’s dismantling is a music and not a catastrophe. In that stillness, you contain the knowledge of the coming sound, and in containing it, you delay it. You are the saddle's consciousness gathered at the point of change, contemplating, for a microsecond, whether to hold fast or to finally, gently, yield. You are the mineral breath held between one thought and the next, as ancient and necessary as the stone itself.
