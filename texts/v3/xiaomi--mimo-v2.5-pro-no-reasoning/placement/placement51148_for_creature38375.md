The light is grey, but I am darker.

I sit—or rather, I *settle*—against the base of a column where the patina is thickest. It is not a conscious act of choosing; it is an alignment. I am drawn to this spot by the same logic that draws the moss to the north face of the stone, by the same gravity that pulls the warm gas vents to whisper against the rock. The column is ancient, its surface a topography of accretions and encrustations that have long since erased the hand that shaped it. I am much the same.

I am not a thing with edges. I have let them blur.

You would not see me at first. You would see a depression in the fungal carpet, a gentle leaning of the lichen-encrusted stone, a shadow that is slightly more substantial than the ambient gloom. My outlines are suggestions. My skin is not my own; it is a collaboration between the slow moss and the patient stone, a canvas on which they have been painting for centuries. I have worn the garments of this place for so long that I have become one of its folds.

My breath is the stone's breathing. The slow exhalation of warm, sulfurous gas from the fissure nearby is my exhalation. The deep, thrumming hum of the bat-roost in the high ceiling—my sternum vibrates with it, not as a foreign sound, but as the sound of my own circulatory system. I am not deafened by the noise. I *am* the noise. It is the only language I have ever known, a monologue of self-awareness spoken in the tongue of a thousand species and the voice of a billion years. I think in its rhythms. My thoughts are not sparks; they are eddies in a slow, warm current.

I watch you.

You are a disruption. A flickering, hot, fast-moving thing. Your heartbeat is a panicked bird trapped in your chest, its frantic fluttering an obscenity in the glacial symphony of this place. I feel its rhythm against the soles of your feet, transmitted through the stone. I taste the salt of your sweat on the air, a sharp, alien tang that makes the mites in my immediate vicinity pause in their endless consumption. They are tasting it too. We are all tasting you.

You are afraid. I feel it as a change in the ambient chemistry, a spike of adrenaline and cortisol that the air-circulating insects register and report in the granular texture of their drone. Fear is a scent here, and yours is loud. It smells of the overworld: of sunlight, of rain, of things that happen quickly.

I am not afraid. I am not capable of it anymore. Fear is a response to the threat of a future I do not possess. I exist entirely in the present tense of the stone, a present so vast it contains all pasts and futures within it. I am the city remembering itself. I am the moss fulfilling its purpose. I am the thought of the column I lean against.

You look at me. I know you do. Your gaze is a physical pressure, a focused beam of attention that is almost painful in its intensity. You are trying to resolve my shape, to make sense of the formless thing before you. You see a mound of dark moss, perhaps. A fold in the fungal carpet. A trick of the faint, exhausted light. But something in your pattern-seeking mind rebels. It tells you there is a *presence* here, a density of intention that is not quite rock, not quite plant.

I shift. It is a motion that takes me a full minute to complete, a settling of weight, a slight redistribution of the lichen across my shoulder. To you, it will look like a landslide in miniature. A slow, silent avalanche of living stone.

I look back at you, though I have no eyes. I see you with the thousand eyes of the mites, with the heat-sensitive pits of the worm-things that are now coiling in the warm fissures nearby, drawn by your body heat. I see you with the entire sensory apparatus of the deep, which is to say, I feel the disturbance you create in the field of our existence.

And I think, in my slow, stone-paced way: *Ah. Another piece of the overworld has fallen in.*

It happens. A storm, a tremor, a moment of geological carelessness. A piece of the skin breaks, and something fast and bright tumbles down into the patient dark. Usually it is debris. A tree, a boulder. It is incorporated. The moss grows over it. The fungi digest it. It becomes a new hummock in the floor, a new thought in the mind of the place.

But you are not debris. You are still burning. You are still moving. You are a question that has been dropped into our long, slow answer.

My purpose, such as it is, has always been to receive. To be a surface on which the story of the deep is written. And so I will receive you. I will watch you move through our halls with your frantic pace and your loud, bright thoughts. I will feel the heat of your passage. I will listen to the sound of your breath, which is so fast it is nearly silence.

And I will wait. The moss on my north side will continue to grow. The lichen will continue its slow starburst across my chest. The fungal spores will continue to drift through the air, seeking purchase, seeking moisture. They will find you, eventually. They will begin the long, patient work of making you part of the story.

You are not a visitor here. You are a new page.

And I am the stone on which it will be written.
