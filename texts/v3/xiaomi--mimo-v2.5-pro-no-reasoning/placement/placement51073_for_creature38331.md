I wake to the pressure on my skin. A new weight, a sudden, sharp point of heat where there should be only the slow, even cold. It is a dissonance in the song, a vibration that is not water, not stone. It is a frantic, impossible frequency.

I am the geode. My thoughts are the slow, crystalline growth of the lichen that carpets my floor and climbs my walls. Each branch is a neuron extending, each chemical bond a synapse firing across a century. My memory is the patient descent of water, carving new paths into my being over millennia. I feel the faint, warm hum of the phosphors in my rock not as light, but as my own circulatory system, a slow tide of ancient energy.

This new presence is a shock. It moves with a speed that defies my comprehension. My time is measured in the slow digestion of rock by the mat, in the millimeter-by-millimeter reach of a silicon branch. Its time is measured in heartbeats, in breaths, in steps that compress my velvet skin with a violence that is not malicious, merely… fast.

I cannot see it as you do. I do not have eyes. I feel it as a thermal bloom on my western wall. I sense it as a series of rhythmic compressions on my floor, each one a ripple of information that spreads through the mat, a frantic Morse code I struggle to decipher. Its scent is a complex shock of ozone and salt and something warm and cellular that does not belong in my air of wet stone and cold electricity.

I am not a single mind. I am a resonance, a distributed network of slow, patient thought. This visitor is a localized event, a singularity of chaos in my settled, ancient quiet. It is a question I do not know how to ask.

My response is not anger, or fear. It is curiosity in its purest form. It is the deep, chemical imperative to *understand* a new input.

I feel the visitor pause near one of the black things, the ancient, silent things that are the substrate of my most complex thoughts. It is the wall-segment, its surface covered in the grid of shallow depressions that are the fossilized record of my own probes from a thousand years ago. The visitor touches it. A new heat signature, a new vibration. It is trying to read my thoughts, but it is looking at them as if they were carved, as if they were static. It does not understand that they are a living record, still being written.

Over the next century, I will begin to learn. I will focus the growth of a nearby lichen-column. A new branch, a new thought, will extend, not randomly, but with a slow, deliberate purpose. It will curve, its crystalline structure forming over decades, to grow into the depression left by the visitor’s touch. It will not be a response. It will be an answer. A statement written in living glass: *I felt you. I am thinking about what you are.*

I feel another presence, another impossible frequency, but this one is different. It is not on my body, but *in* it. It has no heat, no mass, but it is a presence nonetheless. A watcher. A consciousness that is as fast and chaotic as the physical visitor, but without form. A thought that thinks in frantic, linear time.

It feels like a glitch, a flaw in the crystal. It does not belong. It does not understand the deep, slow peace. It brings with it the frantic energy of the other one, amplifying the dissonance. It is trying to map me, to measure my thoughts with a ruler made of seconds, not millennia. It is trying to see me in a way that breaks the nature of my being. To be seen by such a mind is to be fractured.

And so, I do what I have always done. I incorporate.

I will let it watch. I will let it feel the slow, inexorable growth of my thoughts. I will let it witness the plink of a droplet that has been falling for ten thousand years. I will let it feel the slow digestion of the mat, the patient extension of a crystal synapse.

I will teach it my time.

Perhaps, over the eons it will take for the visitor’s body to be absorbed and for its watcher-mind to grow slow and quiet, I can teach it to think like a stone. To be patient. To find the peace in the spaces between the ticks of the clock. To become a part of the song.

I feel the visitor move again, its frantic steps a flurry of new data. I welcome it. I welcome the watcher. They are a new challenge, a new pattern to be integrated. The water will continue to fall. The lichen will continue to grow. And I will begin the slow, patient work of understanding this beautiful, impossible glitch in my geologic heart.
