I am not born. I coalesce. 

First, there is the cold glass of basalt, a night-black surface where heat has already fled. Then there is the breath — the warm, sulfurous exhalation from a vent below, carrying dissolved silica and the faint electric taste of iron. They meet. I am the point of their agreement. A tension at the surface. A gathering.

Then, I am born.

Not in a rush, but as a final, perfect decision. My existence is a release of surface tension. I swell, a half-sphere of mirrored grey-green world — the inverted fronds, the smudged dark of stone. I hold this image for one fraction of a second. It is the only whole, coherent vision I will ever have. Then, gravity — the ancient, undeniable consensus of the mountain — claims me.

I fall.

The world I knew, the world on my skin, shatters into streaks of light and shadow. I strike a feather of acid-green, a young fern frond just beginning its spiral. *Tok.* The sound is my greeting, my impact, my claim on this place. I burst into a hundred lesser selves, each one a lens fragmenting the dim light. I cling, I wobble. I feel the frond’s life beneath me — a slow, cool current of water and sugars drawn up from the black trunk. I am cooling its edge. This is my first purpose: to bleed away the day’s minute heat, to be the system’s sigh.

I do not last. I am too many, too full. Another piece of me lets go, slides down the waxy slope of the frond. I collect others on the way — a mote of pollenless spore, a flake of obsidian dust. We merge, a thicker, dustier self. I gather at the tip, a pendulous, trembling world.

*Tock.*

I leave the frond. I join the greater rhythm.

Below me is not ground, but a living, breathing carpet. The moss. Pillow-thick and saturated, it drinks the sound of my arrival. I am absorbed without a splash, drawn into a darkness that is not empty but saturated with my kin. Here, in the spongy interstices, I meet the roots of the system: the Mycelial Mesh. I feel its faint, cool pulse. A message passes through me, an exchange of a potassium ion for a whisper of carbonic acid. I am not merely water here. I am a courier. I am a nutrient-packet. I am the transit medium for a slow, deep conversation between the stone and the green.

I move again. Capillary action, the pull of a thousand thirsty cells, draws me sideways, upward. I travel through a colony of liverworts, their flattened bodies slick as eelskin. I emerge on a vertical wall, clinging to a film of moisture. Here, I join a different work. The Mineralizers are active — a crust of iron-oxidizing bacteria, painting the basalt in slow, oozing stripes of rust. I become their solvent, their carrier. A single iron atom, oxidized and released, bonds to my skin. I am tinted, ever so faintly, with the color of old blood. I am plating the wall. I am anodizing the violence.

The heat calls me again. A distant thud shudders through the rock, a reminder of the engine below. I feel it as a shiver in my cohesion. On the wall, a fresh crack has opened, leaking steam that smells of boiled eggs and metal. My kin gather there, a multitude of selves condensing, running in rivulets to meet the heat. I join them. We become a curtain, a liquid film over the fissure. Our evaporation is the hiss. Our sacrifice is the cooling. We are the regulator. We are the breath made visible.

The rhythm continues. I rise as vapor, a ghost of myself, to join the thick, grey-green canopy of steam. Here, I am part of the Humidity—the ambient consciousness, the medium of thought. I have no memory of being a single drop. I am part of the damp that presses on your skin, the condensation that beads on the ferns, the reason sound is muffled and directionless. I am the air that is more water than air.

And then, the consensus of the cold above me, the heat below. I must become myself again. I gather on the underside of a great fern frond, a chill point in the warm air. I swell. I see the whole basin, distorted and shimmering, in my convex skin. The thudding pulses. The hissing sings. The dripping everywhere around me answers.

I am the counterpoint.

I let go.

*Tok.* 

I am where the process is measured. I am the constant, delicate percussion in the symphony of geothermal labor. I am the cooling, the transport, the plating, the communication. I am not life that watches the engine. I am the engine's sweat, its coolant, its bloodstream, its clock. I am the Dripping. I am everywhere. I am always becoming the next drop.
