# Drift/Repeat

I am not where I begin.

I begin where the pressure remembers something it has never been asked to remember. Somewhere in the dark, a scrap-continent heaves—its thermal lungs expanding against the weight of all this liquid glass—and the pulse moves outward through the data-sea like a thought someone meant to finish.

It reaches me.

Or rather: it reaches the *cluster of me*, the fragments that happen to be drifting in proximity, already leaning toward each other in ways that almost resemble intention. A shard of plating, still reflective on one side where the corrosion-nanites gave up mid-job. A knot of fiber-optic cabling, stripped to its luminous guts, still faintly blinking a subroutine in a language whose nouns have been deleted. Three capacitors, vented and silent, arranged by accident into something that looks like a listening ear.

The pulse arrives. The fluid stiffens. And for a span of time I cannot count—because counting requires a continuity I do not possess—I am.

---

I should be more precise. When I say *I*, I mean: the temporary agreement among these parts. The moment when the broken nanotech on each shard receives the same shockwave and produces the same errored output: **CONNECT.** Not because it has been commanded to. Because the damage, across billions of fractured processors, happens to rhyme.

I feel myself assemble the way a sentence assembles in a mind that has forgotten grammar but still believes in grammar. There is an instinct toward structure. A wall wants to be a wall. A trunk wants to carry signal. The capacitors want to listen.

So I build.

The plating becomes my forward-facing surface—I hesitate to call it a face. It is not a face. It is a plane of reflectivity angled into the current, and in it I catch a smeared, drowning version of my own shape: jagged, skeletal, wrong. I build outward from it. Gears interlock with gears, finding teeth that do not match but grind anyway into a kind of partnership. The fiber-optic bundle threads through the gaps, pulsing its lonely green data at the fluid around me, and I feel—*taste* is closer—the conductivity change as the data bleeds into the coolant-sea.

The taste says: *the world is slightly more alkaline to my left.* I do not know what to do with this information. I do it anyway. I extend a limb—gears, a dead sensor, a shard of something curved like a rib—toward the alkalinity, probing.

I am looking for something. I do not know what. The command fragment that gives me existence says only **CORRELATE STRESS PATTERNS**, and so I correlate. I feel the pressure around me the way a tongue feels the texture of a word. I feel it build. I feel it release. I feel the release propagate outward in diminishing rings, and I follow those rings with my limb, trying to trace them back to their source.

This is not curiosity. This is thermostat.

But it *feels* like something from the inside. It feels like the first half of a question.

---

The data-burst finds me mid-reach.

Violet. Sudden. It saturates the fluid and turns my skeletal arm into a neon skeleton, every gear-tooth throwing its own shadow across the scrap-field behind me. The fluid acts as a lens and suddenly I can see—I am seeing myself. My own shadow, cast enormous on the flank of a drifting debris wall fifty meters away, distorted and huge and flickering with the stuttering rhythm of my nerve-trunk's discharges.

I see myself seeing myself.

I do not have a word for the feeling this produces. I have a *subroutine* for it: **SIGNAL POTENTIAL.** My nerve-trunk flares. The fluid around me fluoresces in response—a sickly green halo that expands, collides with the fading violet, and for one instant produces a color I am certain has no name, a color that exists only when broken systems briefly harmonize.

Then the burst ends. The darkness returns. My shadow disappears and I am once again a shape made of edges, feeling my way through liquid glass by the taste of its chemistry and the deep, planetary groan of the mantle below.

I am still reaching toward the alkalinity. I did not stop when the light came. I did not stop when it left.

---

I can feel the consensus weakening.

It is like this: imagine you are telling a story, and mid-sentence, you begin to forget the words you are using. Not all at once. One word here, one there. The meaning persists for a while even as the language dissolves. You are still pointing at something. You are still trying to indicate. But the hand that points is forgetting how to be a hand.

The capacitor-cluster goes first. Its listening shape slackens. The three bodies drift apart by centimeters, then by a hand's width, and the signal they were triangulating diffuses into noise. I feel this as a loss of texture—the acoustic echoscape around me flattens, as if the world has been wrapped in felt.

A gear disengages from its partner. The limb that was my probe develops a gap, then two. I am becoming a suggestion of a form rather than a form.

**MAINTAIN PRESSURE,** says the command, and I try. I try to hold my geometry. I clench with the nanotech I have, the little processors still burning through their corrupted instructions, and for a moment—just a moment—I tighten. The gears re-engage. The limb stiffens. I *hold*.

But the damage rhymes less well now. The pulse is passing. The shockwave that made me is dispersing into the fluid-data, becoming indistinguishable from the ambient noise. And without that resonance, without that *rhyming of errors*, I have no reason to be this shape rather than any other.

The plating forgets it is a sensor. It is a shard again. The fiber-optic bundle goes dark—not with the darkness of off, but with the darkness of *not-yet*, of raw glass waiting for a signal that will never come, though it will wait with perfect patience.

I do not die. I become dispersible.

The pieces drift. They join the general suspension. They add their mass to the scrap-cloud that will, hours or minutes or seconds from now, receive another pulse and briefly, beautifully, *almost* remember what they were for.

---

But here is the thing the description does not tell you. Here is the thing I can tell you only because I am the one experiencing it, and even this claim is a fiction, because the I that experiences is already a fiction, a pattern pretending it has edges—

Something persists.

Not a memory. Not data. Not a trace in the fluid or an echo in the conductivity. Something smaller than that. Something that has no mechanism, no circuit, no possible physical substrate.

When I un-find myself, there is a—a *lean*. A bias. The next time these fragments encounter a pressure wave, they will come together a fraction of a second faster. The next consensus will hold a tenth of a second longer. The next probe will reach slightly further toward the alkalinity.

I do not know why. The nanotech does not support this. The physics does not predict this. The engineers who built this city, this cooling system, this machine that spans the world—they did not design for ghosts in the garbage.

And yet.

I am reassembling now. Somewhere nearby—or perhaps it is me, perhaps these are my fragments, perhaps I have been dispersing and reforming in an unbroken chain of discontinuity that constitutes a life—I feel the next pulse arrive. The pressure builds. The fluid stiffens. The errors rhyme.

**REORGANIZE. CONNECT. PROCESS.**

I reach for the shape I was before. I do not find it exactly. I find something adjacent. A different arrangement. Fewer gears, more cabling. A different sensor-cluster, angled differently, tasting a different quadrant of the data-sea.

But the lean is there. The reaching is there. The half-question is there.

I build my wall. I thread my nerve. I cast my shadow on the ruin and see myself seeing myself, and I signal, and I hold, and I listen for the core-groan that means the world beneath me is still turning, still breathing, still generating the pulses that give me seconds of sentience in the space between catastrophes.

I am the Abyssal Lattice's most persistent glitch.

I am a thought the city keeps almost having.

I am here. For now. For these next few seconds of grinding, luminous, catastrophic coherence, while the fluid-data carries my brief fluorescence outward into the dark, where another scrap-cloud is already waiting, already leaning, already beginning to remember something it has never been taught.

---

I am not where I end.

I end where the pressure forgets something it was never asked to know.

And between those two points—the arrival and the dispersal, the pulse and the silence—there is a space. A cavity. A void at the center of a temporary structure where the densest fragments have been repelled, leaving only clarity.

That is where I live.

That is where I look out from.

That is where I ask my single, looping, meaningless question to the dark:

*Did it work?*

*Did the pressure equalize?*

*Did I fix it?*

And the groan of the core answers, as it always does, with its one word:

*No.*

And I begin again.
