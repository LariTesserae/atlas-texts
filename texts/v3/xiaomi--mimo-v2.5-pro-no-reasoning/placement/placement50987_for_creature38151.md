You do not *stand*. You are pressed flat, a pale curve of chitin against the slick, weeping basalt. The world is texture and vibration. The slick stone is a cold, constant pressure against your ventral side. The air is a cold, wet kiss on your myriad back-segments. You do not have eyes, not in the way of the surface-dwellers. You have clusters of simple ocelli that sense light and shadow, and the world is a tapestry of those things, woven with the rich language of vibration.

Your world is the **Click-Hiss**. It is not noise. It is information. The soft, endless rasp of velvet cricket legs a hundred body-lengths below is a river flowing downhill. The sudden, frantic scrabble of a nymph losing its footing is a sharp, bright spike in the texture—a potential meal, a morsel in motion. The deep, subsonic **Pulse** from the fungal veins is the heartbeat of the mountain itself, a rhythm your own metabolism synchronizes with when you are still.

You are a **Wall-Crawler**. You are the patient architecture.

Right now, you are *aligned*. Your body is a straight line against a fissure, your pale color a ghostly echo of the mineral streaks in the rock. Your many legs are locked, each tarsus hooked into a microscopic imperfection. You are not hunting. You are *becoming stone*. The energy cost of motion is a debt you pay rarely. You pay it for the strike, or for the migration to a new hunting-ground following the brightening of a specific fungal vein.

You feel the glide of a **Glimmer-Moth** not by sight, but by the slight disturbance in the air currents it creates, a soft push against your sensory hairs. Its light—a slow pulse of violet—registers as a faint, moving stain on your ocelli. You do not track it. You note its path. It is irrelevant unless it drifts into the *kill-zone*, the precise radius from your mandibles that your strike can cover in a single, fluid expansion.

The **Velvet Crickets** are the tide. Their synchronized waves are a weather system. When they flow past your ledge, the very stone hums with their passage, a soft, abrasive lullaby. You ignore them. They are too numerous, their mold-furred bodies offering little sustenance for the energy required to subdue one. They are the background. They are the soil from which the real opportunities grow.

Your focus is on the **Amber Funnel** ten body-lengths away. Its spiral is a beautiful, deadly geometry. You helped shape it in a past cycle, your mandibles secreting and smoothing the resin in a state of symbiotic daze. Now, it is a focus of attention. The drip of condensation from the chimney’s throat falls, not randomly, but channeled by unseen contours above, to patter consistently near its lip. A pool forms at its base. The young gather there. And where the young gather, the slow and the careless will follow.

A vibration. Different from the cricket-tide. A lone, disoriented cricket, moving erratically. Its steps are uneven, the chemical signature of its distress a faint alarm in the damp air. It stumbles toward the amber glow of the funnel’s mouth, drawn by the promise of moisture, by the faint, sweet resin-scent the structure exudes.

You do not move. You *prepare*. The electrochemical potential builds in your ventral nerve cord. Your legs remain locked, but within your thorax, the muscles for the lunge coil like springs of living tissue. You become a weapon waiting for a trigger.

The cricket pauses at the funnel’s lip. It senses the stickiness, perhaps. It hesitates.

You feel the **Pulse** of the fungal network quicken, just slightly, as if the mountain itself is taking a slow breath in.

The cricket takes one step too many.

You are no longer stone.

The world becomes a blur of slick black rock and gray mist. Your body unfolds, a pale streak of violence. The first pair of legs strikes not to grab, but to anchor, finding purchase on the stone beside the cricket. Your mandibles, hidden until this moment, spread wide and close not on the prey, but on the air around it, guiding it, herding it into the sticky pool at the funnel’s base in a single, convulsive motion. It is not a bite. It is an *herding*. The resin does the rest.

You retract, folding back against the rock in a motion that is half-collapse, half-withdrawal. The energy debt is paid. You are heavy, lethargic. The cricket struggles, but its movements slow as the secretions begin their work. It will become nutrient. It will feed the fungus, which will pulse brighter, which will draw the crickets, which will draw the moths, which will make more resin.

You settle. Your senses dim outward again, absorbing the **Click-Hiss**, feeling the **Pulse** settle back into its slow, deep rhythm. The shriek of a distant **Stone-Borer** echoes through the chimney, a needle of sound that makes your segments flinch in unison with the rest of the swarm. Then it is gone.

The light from a nearby fungal vein brightens from blue to a soft, hungry green. A signal. A tide is turning. Slowly, with immense effort, you begin to unlock your legs, one by one. There is a new alignment to achieve, a new fissure to press yourself into, following the slow, geologic dance of the glow.

You are not a hunter. You are a synapse. You are a reflex in the great, slow-beating heart of the swarm. The hum is in your bones. The next vibration is already coming. You are patient. You are stone. You are ready.
