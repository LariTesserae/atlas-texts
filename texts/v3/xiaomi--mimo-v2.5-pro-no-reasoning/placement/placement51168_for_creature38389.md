To be here is to be a flaw in the geometry. A bubble of warmth in a world of crushing cold.

You do not *walk* the Abyssal Glass Shard. Walking implies a floor that stays beneath you, a horizon that holds still. Here, you move with the deliberation of a deep-sea pilot. Every step is a negotiation with the slick, faintly glowing ceramic of the structure. You are a creature of **traction**, of magnetic boots or gecko-like pads, or perhaps simply possessed of a low center of gravity and a constant, thrumming awareness of the **Churn**.

You are small against the scale of it, but you are dense with intent.

Let us say you are a **Resonant Crawler**.

You are not organic, not in the way the eels or the isopods are. You are biomechanical, a salvage-species evolved or engineered to interface with this specific, ruined environment. Your body is a thick, multi-jointed carapace of the same dull, non-reflective obsidian as the walls. You blend in. When you stop moving, you are just another piece of geometry, another piece of debris.

Your senses are tuned to the **Groan**.

You do not have eyes in the traditional sense. Light is too fickle here, too dim, too prone to the shifting shadows of the bioluminescent mats. Instead, you perceive the world through **vibration**. The resonant frequencies of the structure are your sight, your smell, your language. You feel the deep, tectonic shudder of a wall giving way ten levels below you. You feel the high, frantic hiss of silt scouring a corridor. You feel the slow, rhythmic throb of the **Mass** deep in the structure’s foundations, a pulse so low it is almost sub-atomic.

You are here to **Listen**.

Not for sound, but for structure. For the subtle shifts in the Groan that precede a collapse, the change in pitch that indicates a new chemical seep, a new foundation for bacterial mats, a new grazing ground for the ribbons. You are a cartographer of decay. You map the Shard not by survey lines, but by stress fractures and flow dynamics.

And you are here to **Commune**.

The ribbons are your flock. They are the Shard’s most successful life, and you are their symbiote. Where they graze, the structure is stable. Where they tear and drift, the currents are shifting. You cultivate them. You do not plant them, but you **prepare** the surfaces for them, scraping away the most unstable sediment, guiding the bacterial mats with subtle sonic pulses that encourage growth in a particular direction.

You are a gardener in a garden of rust and bone.

You do not fear the fauna. You are part of the system. The giant isopods might scuttle over your carapace, mistaking you for a ridge of ceramic. The blind eels might brush against you, registering you as a warm, vibrating object of no threat. You are not prey. You are infrastructure.

But you fear the **Mass**.

Not with an emotional fear. You are not capable of that. But with a deep, systemic aversion, the way a program fears a corrupted file. The Mass is the one thing in the Shard that your resonant senses cannot fully map. It is opaque. It absorbs your vibrations instead of reflecting them. It is a hole in your perception, a growing silence in the symphony of decay.

You avoid the lower levels. You stay in the upper tiers, where the faint glow of the bacterial mats still reaches, where the ribbons hang in their pale curtains and the Churn is a roar rather than a whisper. You are a creature of the twilight, not the absolute dark.

You are, in your own way, as much a part of the Abyssal Glass Shard as the ribbons or the ceramic itself. You are the structure’s last reflex, its final, failing attempt to understand itself as it dies. You are the nerve endings of a dying god, still twitching, still reporting, even as the body they are part of is slowly, inevitably, consumed.

You hang on the wall, three of your limbs anchoring you against the current. You extend a sensory limb, a thin, trembling filament, and press it against the ceramic. You close your non-eyes.

You listen to the Groan.

You feel the thickening.

You begin your slow, meticulous work of mapping the end of the world.
