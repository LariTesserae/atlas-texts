I am not outside, and I am not in a place. I am in a *between*. The air is thick in my throat, and it tastes of the copper grating under my feet and the deep soil smell that isn’t really soil. It tastes like the pipes when they get too warm and like the water that always, always falls.

I sit in my hollow. It is the best place to listen. My back fits into the curve where the big iron rib meets the living pillar, and I can feel the rib hum sometimes, a low, slow feeling in my bones when the big water machine changes its breath. From here, I can see my pictures on the wall down there. Click is a good drawing. I got the glint of his joints right. Page is harder. When I draw her, she always looks like a closed book with silver edges, but she is more than that. She is the quiet in the top of the room.

The light is doing its softening now. The lamps up there, the glass suns, are growing a little tired, a little yellow. This is my favorite time. The blue flowers turn even deeper, like the water in the deep basins just before the lamps go all the way to sleep. The sound changes. The hiss-slop of the big water heart gets slower, like it is thinking. The drips from the ribs become clearer, each one a separate *plink* in the moss.

I know this place like I know the feel of my own hands. I know which vines to pull gently when I need to see a lamp better. I know which pools have stones just under the surface for stepping, and which ones are all soft mud that wants to hold your foot. I know the warm pipes that run under the north wall. I put my feet on them when the air gets a cooler taste, the kind that makes the moss turn a darker green.

My mother knew it too. Her marks are still on the wall by the small room. Numbers and lines that look like spider legs. I don’t understand them, but I understand the feel she was trying to catch. The feel of the water moving just right. The feel of the air when all the green leaves are breathing out together. When things feel wrong—a pipe that coughs, a channel that runs too slow—I fix it. I use the hard tool from the small room, or I pack moss just so. I don’t think of it as fixing. I think of it as… smoothing. Like when you hum and the note in your chest finally matches the note in the air.

Down below, Click is hunting. I see the slow pulse in his throat. He moves like a drip of water himself, so slow. He doesn’t know he is beautiful, with his copper clicking and his wet-skin shine. He just is. Like the pools are. Like Page is. They are the finishing. They are the last, quiet parts of the song the atrium sings.

My mother is under the moss, near the dark pool. I visit. The moss there is very thick and spongy. It feels warm. I think of her as the tall one, the one with hands like mine who could make the water sing right. I don’t remember her voice. I remember her stillness. I remember pulling the soft green skin over her, because that is what you do. What is still becomes the ground for what grows next. The atrium taught me that.

Now, the lamps are very dim. The air is heavy and sweet. The vines on the west wall have turned, just a little, to follow the fading light. I hear the tiny scratch of their fibers against the iron. I hear the moss breathing. I hear the deep, settled sound the whole place makes when it is running well, when nothing is stuttering or whining. It is a sound beneath all the other sounds. A *right here* sound.

I curl tighter in my hollow. The fern skins smell like green tea and wet stone. The air settles on my skin like a blanket. I close my eyes. I breathe in the wet metal and the loam, the breath of my world. I breathe it out.

*I know you,* I whisper into the thick, sweet dark. *I know every drop and every leaf and every hum.*

And the atrium breathes back. Not in words. In the slow settle of a pipe. In the unfurling of a fern I can feel but not see. In the absolute, steady, dripping quiet that is its way of saying everything.

It says: *I am here.*

And I answer: *I know. I am here too.*
