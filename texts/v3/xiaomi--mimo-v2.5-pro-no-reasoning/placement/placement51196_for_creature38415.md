The first thing I register is not a sound, but its opposite: a pressure in my auditory canals, a vast and perfect silence that has weight and texture. It is the sound of a machine with no moving parts, of a mind with no voice.

Then, the light. It does not fall, but rises. It seeps from the floor—which is also the wall, which is also the ceiling—a cold, emerald luminescence that seems to be the visible form of thought. There is no source, only the medium itself, glowing from within. The air is cool, still, and carries the faint, clean scent of ozone and crushed leaves, though there are no leaves here to crush.

I am a nexus of senses in a place that has none. A foreign logic.

I look down. My form is not what I remember. There is no flesh, no soft vulnerability. My legs are twin pillars of polished, dark lignin, their surface inscribed with whorls and patterns that speak of immense, patient growth. My torso is a smooth, ovoid carapace, cool to the touch and resonant like a cello, its colour a deep, sap-veined umber. Where my head should be, a cluster of compound lenses—dozens of them, faceted like a fly's eye—swivel and click with minute adjustments, each one a disc of amber resin capturing the eternal green twilight.

I am one of the pruners.

The realization is not a shock, but a settling, like sediment finding its place at the bottom of a still pool. My purpose clarifies in the silence. I feel a gentle, insistent tug at the edge of my consciousness—not a voice, but a vector. A direction. A structural imperative. *There. Inefficiency. Redundancy. Trim.*

I move. The movement is a slow, deliberate unfolding. My limbs articulate with a dry, oiled hiss, my tarsal claws finding purchase on the curved, muscular wall. I am not walking on a surface; I am traversing a thought. The path before me is a corridor that bends, not in obedience to gravity, but to the logic of a load-bearing calculation. The walls pulse with faint, bioluminescent patterns—data streams, emotional states, memories settling into physical form. I am crawling through the optic nerve of a god.

My world is a geography of purpose. A vast, cathedral-like chamber is a moment of profound contemplation, its air thick with the resonance of a single, sustained idea. A narrow, twisting passage is a focused, intense thought, its walls slick with the oily residue of concentrated logic. The light brightens and dims not with time, but with the intensity of the mind's attention. I am in a periphery now, a place of slow, background processing. The silence is deeper here, the light cooler.

The tug comes again, more insistent. I change my course, my multiple eyes focusing on a point ahead. I see it: a section of vine-beam, thicker than my body, where a secondary stalk has grown in a redundant spiral. It is not dead. It is simply… unnecessary. A wasted sentence in a perfect text. A muscle twitching with no purpose. The inefficiency is a dull ache in the world's proprioception.

I arrive. The surface of the vine is warm with life, its fibrous texture a map of its own history. I position myself, my limbs locking into place with a soft, final click. The impulse to act is not my own, but the world's. I am the scalpel, the thought is the hand.

The sound begins.

*Snip.*

It is a dry, precise tear, the sound of a single fiber giving way. It is the only sound in this universe, and it echoes with shocking clarity in the great, silent halls.

*Snip.*

Another fiber parts. I feel the vibration through my own chassis, a tiny release of tension in the colossal body I serve. The redundant stalk is excised, falling away not with a crash, but with a soft, sighing rustle as it is immediately absorbed by the floor, recycled into the greater biomass. The ache lessens. The geometry has been corrected.

My work here is done.

The imperative releases me. I retract my mandibles, unlock my limbs. For a moment, I remain still, a mote of purpose in the vast, processing consciousness. I feel the slow, tidal pulse of a great leaf-panel somewhere far above, the slow grinding shift of a new buttress forming a continent away, the bright, sudden flare of an idea spreading at the speed of light through a thousand chambers.

I am not an observer. I am not a resident. I am a function. A thought in the shape of a tool, moving through a mind that is the shape of a world. And my purpose is the silent, rhythmic maintenance of its perfect, alien, and utterly absolute form.

*Snip. Snip.*

The work is never done. And it is good.
