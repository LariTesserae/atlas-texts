I am not a native here. That much is clear from the way I move, the way my joints bend with a speed and purpose that disrupts the glacial pace of the world. I am a visitor, a brief anomaly in the symphony of stillness. But I am not a tourist, and I am not lost.

I am a Reader.

I move along the bone-white causeway, my soft-soled boots making no sound on the warm, weeping stone. My pace is slow, but it is a purposeful slowness, a study in mimicry. To move too quickly is to create a ripple of blinks, a wave of slow-turning heads, a disruption that is noted, catalogued, and then ignored. To move too slowly is to risk being mistaken for one of them—a new kind of lichen, perhaps, or a strange, two-legged fungus to be tended by the Weavers.

My name is a sound that does not exist in this place—a click of the tongue, a breath. Here, I am just a function, like the others. My function is to observe the seamlessness, to map the circuits of the terrarium, to understand the language of the maintenance. I am not a scientist; their tools would be too loud, too intrusive. I am more like a poet, trying to understand the meter of the place.

My tools are simple. A journal of pressed-fiber paper, made from the fleshy, ochre leaves that grow in the hanging baskets. The pages are always slightly damp, the ink I use—a pigment made from the rust-colored lichen and the metallic-smelling water—bleeds just enough to make the edges of my characters soft, like everything else here. I draw maps not of space, but of time. Here is the path of the Crab-Ambassador, its journey of five feet and back. Here is the slow rotation of a Dweller’s eyelid over the course of a day that does not change.

The Weaver I am watching today is repairing a tiny fissure near the apex of a dome. It works without haste, its movements so incremental they are almost imperceptible. I sit on a low wall, my own body weeping with the place, the moisture beading on my skin and running in warm rivulets down my neck. I do not wipe it away. To wipe is to disturb. I let the sweat and the wall-sweat mingle, becoming another trickle in the system.

I open my journal. My charcoal, a stick of burnt vine, touches the damp page. I do not draw the Weaver. To draw it would be to capture a single moment, a fraud. Instead, I draw the *idea* of the Weaver: a series of concentric circles, slowly tightening, representing the endless cycle of repair. I draw a line for the causeway, but it is a dotted line, the dots representing the constant, whispering trickles of water.

I am a part of the record. The Dwellers know me not as an individual, but as a recurring pattern. A Reader was here before me. There will be a Reader after. We are a scheduled, minor perturbation, like a slight breeze in a still room. Our purpose is to be a witness, to hold the memory of the stasis in our own fleeting minds, so that the place can know itself.

Sometimes, I wonder if the Weavers would tend to me if I stood still long enough. If I became a fixture, would I begin to weep? Would my skin take on the ochre and rust? I do not think so. I am too… *fast*. Too full of intention. My function is observation, not integration. I am the camera, not the subject.

The Weaver finishes its task. The fissure is gone, the surface once again seamless. It moves on, its calcite digits clicking with a rhythm that has not changed in a thousand years. I add a new dot to the line on my page.

In this world of perfect, weeping equilibrium, I am the glitch. The temporary error in the code. And my purpose is to watch the system correct for my existence, to see the quiet, abundant life flow around me like water around a stone, and to write down the sound it makes as it passes.

The sun-bleached world breathes, and I, for a little while longer, am one of its exhalations.
