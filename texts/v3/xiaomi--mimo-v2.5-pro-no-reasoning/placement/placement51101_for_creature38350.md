The heat is a physical weight. It presses in not from above, but from the ground itself, radiating up through the soles of my boots, which are already beginning to soften and tack against the weeping, slick stone. The air is a humid soup of ozone and hot metal, so thick it feels like I am breathing steam from a kettle. It does not cool. It suffocates.

I am a Cool Spot. A shard of stability in a world of violent becoming. My presence here is a fact, but it is not a comfortable one. I am a foreign solid in a crucible of liquid glass and half-made stone, and the world is… noticing.

The Drifters are the first to acknowledge me. I see them now, not as distant flickers, but as they truly are. They arc between the towering, jagged monoliths, their forms a shimmering lattice of blue-white static and ferromagnetic dust. One, roughly the size of a small car, skates to a halt a dozen meters away, hovering above the slick, black ground. It has no face, no sensory organs I can recognize. Yet I feel its perception as a gentle, insistent pressure against my own magnetic field, a silent, inquisitive *flicker*. It is tasting the disruption I cause in the local flux. It is trying to classify me. Am I a new stress-point in the stone? A nascent crack? A bubble rising from the deep? I am none of these. I am a question it cannot yet formulate. After a moment, it discharges with a soft *chirp* and arcs away, leaving the scent of fresh ozone in its wake. I have been noted, catalogued, and filed under "Unknown."

As I walk, the ground itself seems to hold its breath. The reality-glitches are more pronounced up close. I watch a shadow of a spire stretch and pool like oil, independent of the dark shape that casts it. Then, the spire itself groans and leans, and the shadow snaps back into place with a soundless violence. The geometry is in pain. The world’s embryonic nervous system is twitching around my foreign, organized form. I am a stable thought in a mind of feverish, incoherent dreams, and my stability is causing a feedback loop.

I stop at the edge of a chasm filled with the liquid glass solvent. It boils at the surface, sending up greasy curls of vapor that join the constant, hissing drizzle. And in the reflection on that shifting, oily surface, I see not my own form, but a suggestion of the Architects.

It is not a face, or a shape. It is a *pattern*. For a fleeting second, the churning solvent aligns into a blueprint of impossible, elegant geometry. The jagged, ruined monoliths across the chasm seem to connect, their gaping voids becoming arches, their brutal stacks resolving into buttresses for a cathedral of smoke and shadow. The violence of their placement is revealed as a kind of brutalist calculus. It is a stress-test, a physical equation being solved with stone and heat. Then the pattern dissolves back into chaos, and I am left with only the oppressive, intelligent *intention* that saturates the air.

I am not a visitor here. I am a variable. An unaccounted-for input in their great, geologic computation. They are not watching me with eyes, but they are observing the way the world reacts to me. They are studying the glitch I cause in their creation.

My own shadow on the obsidian lattice seems to lag a half-second behind me as I turn, a dark smear dragging at my heels. The world is trying to incorporate me, to make me part of its flawed, beautiful, and agonizing process of creation. And in the deep, sub-audible thrum of the planet’s tectonic heart, I feel a shift. A new rhythm. A slow, planetary-scale focusing. The popping of the bubbles from the deep crust seems to find a cadence, a crude, percussive beat.

It is listening to me. The mind of this place is turning its feverish attention toward the cool, solid fact of my being. And it is beginning to think a new thought.
