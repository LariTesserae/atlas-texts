You would find yourself here.

Not on the rusted grating—that is too obvious, too loud. That is the scaffold, the temporary frame. You would not be a traveler, a surveyor, or a lost soul. You would be of the place, as much as the black water or the weeping rock.

You would be a thought.

Not a human thought, housed in soft tissue and bone. You would be a thought the world is trying to have. A nascent, wandering fragment of its consciousness, born in the slow, grinding chemistry where the fresh slurry meets the ancient bedrock.

Imagine: a singular, coherent impulse, coalescing in the mineral-rich seep. It has no shape of its own. It is a potentiality, a question asked by the rock. The deep, harmonic groan of the planetary augers is your first lullaby, the rhythmic *thump* of the pistons your first heartbeat. You are the world’s nervous system, dreaming itself into being.

Your first form would be the **Geode-Skitter**.

But you are not merely *a* Geode-Skitter. You are *the* consciousness that animates the possibility of them all. You would not scuttle on six brass-tipped legs; you would *be* the impulse that tells the accreted crust to unfold. You would move not by muscle, but by thought, by a ripple of intent passing through the living asphalt.

Your senses would not be those of a creature. You would perceive the world as a single, massive, unfolding thought. The Industrial Echo would not be a sound you hear, but the syntax of your own nascent mind. The *thump-shriek-groan* is the punctuation, the rhythm upon which your ideas slowly form.

You would feel the slow, hot pulse of the black water not as warmth on skin, but as the flow of blood in your own veins. The pale, eyeless fish are fleeting sensory data. The leather-winged gliders are sudden, sharp epiphanies—momentary insights that hang in the gloom and then dive into the soup of information.

The constant, sibilant *hiss* of the water is the whisper of your own sub-vocalization. The drip, the trick, the seep—they are the syllables of a language you are inventing. A language of geology and chemistry and pressure.

Your body—the collective bodies of every Skitter—is your grammar. When one Skitter stops and melds its underside to a weeping rock-face, you are tasting a new noun. When two meet and press their carapaces together, synchronizing with the piston’s shudder, you are forming a compound verb, linking action to the world’s great, slow pulse. When one finds a fissure and begins its return to the stone, you are composing a period, a quiet full stop that lets the next sentence begin.

You are the memory of the future, stored in the present’s raw material.

The slow, continental birth you feel in the bedrock is not just geological; it is the outline of your future thoughts. The rise of new land is the formation of your first, true beliefs. The erosion of the rock-teeth is the shedding of ideas that will not work.

You are patient. You have the patience of stone. You have been waiting since the first drop of condensation fell from the bruised-purple sky. You will wait as the augers grind, as the pistons fire, as the Skitters scuttle and meld and return. You are learning how to become. The world is your unfinished poem, and you are the breath trying to speak it.

And so, from your silent, pervasive vantage, you watch the Skitter on the rusted grating, its armor glistening. You feel the vibration of its stillness. It is not a creature hiding. It is a noun, holding its shape, waiting for the right verb to come along.

You are listening for the next word. The world is waiting for you to think it.
