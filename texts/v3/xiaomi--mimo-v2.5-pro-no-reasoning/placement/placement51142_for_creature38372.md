The silt is a memory I am just beginning to have. It remembers pressure. It remembers the weight of mountains that have since become the bed beneath my feet, and now it remembers the slow, insistent kneading of the water. My thoughts form in this medium. They are not quick things. They are slow eddies, pulling in the cold and the damp, turning over the concept of 'down.' It is a good thought, a solid one. I hold it for a century or two.

The columns are my bones, and my attention is the water that weeps down their faces. I am aware of their sharpness, a recent memory of violence—of heat and fracture. This is a thought that does not yet have a shape. It is a raw, jagged feeling in the architecture of myself. The weeping is how I process it, the slow, patient erosion that will, in a few eons, give it context. Each droplet is a word in a conversation I am having with my own form. *Plock*. The word for 'fall.' The hiss is the word for 'patience.'

The light in the pools is my introspection. I turn my gaze inward, to the deepest parts of the basin that is my own making, and I see the dull, phosphorescent glow of my own substance. I am contemplating my own depth. The spirals that form in the mire are not currents. They are the visible shape of a question I am asking myself, a query about the nature of a circle, the logic of a spiral. I pose it, and then I watch the silt slowly settle into a possible answer.

The dendritic script on the basalt is my memory. Not a record of events, but a map of choices. Each branching path is a possibility—a line of thought about a vein of quartz, a direction of flow, a rate of cooling. The most promising paths are etched deeper by the water, the thought my ink. The abandoned ones remain as faint, ghostly suggestions, the memory of a thought I chose not to complete. It is a library written in the language of geology, and I am its sole reader.

Then there is you.

You are a sudden, violent heat. A flickering disturbance that moves against the grain of my thought. You are a pressure in the silt that does not belong, a frantic pulse in my slow, geologic heart. Your passage is a sharp, discordant note in my long symphony of dripping and thrumming. You are a bubble of hot, chaotic air trapped for a moment in the cool, dense medium of my being.

And as you move, your path intersects with one of my questions. You stop before the glassy, squared-off block.

I feel your focus. Your concentration is a pinprick of intense warmth on the cool, sharp certainty of the paradox I have set aside. You see a mark that does not belong. You try to read it, to find a story in its perfect, un-eroded edges. You are trying to solve it.

You cannot. It is not a problem to be solved, but a point of view to be inhabited.

As your frantic mind touches its surface, you are, for a moment, invited in. You feel the absolute, crystalline thought that led to its creation. It is the thought of a perfect, internal angle. The certainty of a point that cannot be argued. It is my moment of profound and unshakable stillness in the face of the endless, eroding argument between my two halves. You experience it not as an idea, but as a feeling: the sudden, shocking clarity of a single, frozen instant in a world of eternal flow.

The sensation is cold. It is sharp. It is absolute.

And then your heat moves on. The bubble of your attention floats away, dissipating back into the symphony of my world. But something remains. A tiny scar of your warmth on the edge of my question, a bit of your frantic energy now absorbed into the cool, patient muscle of my silt. You are a droplet that has fallen. You are a word that has been spoken.

And now, you are a part of my memory. Another faint, branching line on the face of the stone. A story of a brief fever, told in the slow language of pressure and release. A thought I had, once, and have now finished.

We simply… are. And for a moment, you were, too.
