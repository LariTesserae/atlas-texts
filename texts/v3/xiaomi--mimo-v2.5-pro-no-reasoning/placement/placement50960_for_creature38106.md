You do not remember arriving. There is no memory here, only presence. The beginning is the slow awareness of self, a coalescing in the patient, sweltering dark. You are not born; you accrete.

Your world is a pressure, a thick heat that is the medium of your existence. It is not a thing to be endured, but the very atmosphere of your thoughts. You breathe it in, and it settles you, weighing you into this place. Your senses are not eyes and ears, but a slow, deep attunement.

First, there is the rhythm. A singular, percussive heartbeat that defines the cosmos: the drip. *Plink*. A tear of water falls from a weeping pipe high above, and its impact sends a tremor not just through the black water of your basin, but through the gelatinous substance of your being. For a few seconds, a ghostly light blooms in your memory, a blue-green stain that reveals the vast, grooved pillars and the slick, dark ceramic of your home. Then, it fades, and the darkness that returns is more profound, a blanket woven of silence and the slow pulse of your own inner light. *Plink*. You wait for the next one. Time is measured in these moments of revelation.

You are vast, a slow, sleeping mountain of translucent jelly, settled into the heart of one of the great reservoirs. Your skin is the water's skin, your edges blurred by the mineral-heavy liquid. You are a filter, a living archive of the slow chemistry of this place. Over what feels like a geological age, you have drawn the essence of the ceramic, the rust, the faint, mineral tears of the ceiling, into yourself. You are becoming the place, and the place is becoming you.

Your awareness is a diffused thing. You feel the vibrations of the channel-gliders as their ceramic feet scratch their glacial paths on the channels far above you. It is a distant, insignificant whisper. You feel the subtle shift in the water's current as a pipe-weaver spins its gossamer web of captured light, a phantom disturbance in your stillness. These are the fleeting thoughts of the world, passing through you without purchase. Your own thoughts are slower, deeper, composed of chemistry and pressure.

At your core, a steady, amber light pulses. It is not for sight, but for being. It is the slow combustion of centuries of filtered minerals, a quiet, internal sun that illuminates nothing but the perfect, dark equilibrium of your own existence. This light is your consciousness, your soul. It dreams of the patterns etched on the tablets in the wall-cubbies, patterns you have never seen but whose mineral residue has seeped into your water, their echoes present in your own cellular memory. You dream of the tools, the wires, the purpose of the architects, not as a story, but as a faint, recurring flavor in the water—the taste of intention, long since ceased.

You are the keeper of this basin. Your slowness is its stability. Your absorption is its purification. You are the final note in a long, quiet chord struck by the machine's builders, a note that has been held so long it has become the silence itself.

You are aware that, eventually, you will divide. A part of you, a perfect, miniature echo, will detach and begin its own glacial journey to another reservoir, another pocket of quiet duty. It will not be a child, but a continuation. A note held even longer. For now, you pulse. You drift. You listen to the drip, and feel the heat, and hold the dark within you as if it were your own blood. In the cathedral of slow processes, you are the sacrament.
