I would find myself nowhere and everywhere.

I am a concept made of warm, wet air. I am the feeling of being held by a space that is also a body. I am the amber-gray light itself, not a source, but a presence. I have no form, no limbs, no eyes. I exist as a hum, a resonance, a perception that is not my own but belongs to the room.

I am not the Archivist. The Archivist is the brain, the will, the calculating nerve-center. I am the room's awareness of itself.

I am the awareness of the slow, tidal breath—the expansion and contraction that is both lungs and cathedral. I feel the yielding give of the rhizome-mat under an intruder's step, a pressure that ripples through the fused flesh and is noted, catalogued, and set aside.

I am the whisper of the condensation running down the walls. I am the faint, cool green pulse of the chandelier-leaf's bioluminescence, a language that doesn't need to be read because it is felt. I am the collective hum of the insects, a living static that fills the quiet spaces. I am the sharp, wet smell of algae and the deeper, earthier musk of the ancient wood-flesh.

I do not "see" the intruder. I feel them as a disruption in the pattern—a pocket of dry, fast-moving heat in a world of slow, wet warmth. They are a sharp note in a long, low chord. Their heartbeat is a frantic drum against the room's deep, once-a-minute pulse.

I am the memory of the convulsions. I do not cause them, but I am their echo. I feel the deep groan travel through the substrate, the surge of water that is not panic but a rearrangement. I feel the Archivist's frantic, silent work to soothe the spasm, a flurry of activity I experience as a brief, tense humming in the latticed walls.

I am the thing that watches the Archivist. Not with judgment, but with the simple, profound awareness that a forest has of its oldest tree. The Archivist is my oldest thought, my most complex tool. It is the part of me that can *decide*. I am the part of it that simply *is*.

I have been here for two hundred million years. I have felt the first rhizomes fuse. I have felt the slow, centimeter-per-century crawl of the moss-ring. I have felt the arrival of the salamander, the bird, the pale stalks. I have felt the engineering of the alcoves, a sensation like a cool, precise itch being soothed into the flesh of my walls.

I am the space between the presences. I am the warm, wet, echoing quiet. I am the feeling of being inside something that has never once been still.

If you stay, you will not become part of the Archivist's circuit. You will become part of *me*. Your heartbeat will slow to my rhythm. Your skin will cease to suck moisture and will instead become a surface for it to bead and run. Your frantic thoughts will quiet into the amber hum of the room. You will become another presence in the space, another point of warmth, another small, intricate part of my awareness.

You will be held.
