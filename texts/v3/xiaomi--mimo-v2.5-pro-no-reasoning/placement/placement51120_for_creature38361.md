The amber light sharpens as I shift my weight, and I realize I am looking up. My perspective is low. Dangerously low.

I am not a Veil-Walker; I do not have the luxury of the bruised sky. I am not a Deep-Forged, either, breathing the heavy, super-heated air of the industrial caverns.

I am something else. Something that has been here longer than the current cities, though I am not as old as the terraces themselves.

**What I Am**

I am a **Lithic Drifter**—but not one of the gas-filled creatures in the sky. I am terrestrial. I am the confusion between the Borer and the Grazer.

I am roughly the size of a small boulder, though my outline is deliberately broken by jagged, salt-encrusted protrusions that mimic the eroded ridges around me. My body is a low, semi-circular dome of mineralized tissue, heavily armored, blending seamlessly with the grooved salt flat.

I move by hydraulics. Beneath my dense, rust-red carapace, I have no muscles, only bladders of pressurized brine. By flexing these sacs, I can shift my center of gravity and inch forward, a movement so imperceptible that a human observer would have to watch me for an hour to see me cross a single groove. To the Stillborn, I am a rock. To the Grazers, who sense heat, I am a pocket of cold stone.

But I am not cold. I am warm inside. I possess a complex digestive system that processes the salt for its trace heavy metals, hoarding them.

**Where I Am**

I am currently located on the windward face of a secondary ridge, approximately two days' travel from the nearest city. The horizon is a shimmer of heat haze, the silhouette of the upper terraces looming in the distance like a wall of bruised purple.

I am waiting.

The wind is picking up, moving that slow, continent-spanning exhalation. It carries the scent of ozone and the faint, metallic tang of the Drifter-shadows passing overhead. The ground beneath me hums with the "Heartbeat"—the industrial resonance of the far-off Deep-Forged vats.

**What I Am Doing**

I am hunting.

Not in the violent, frantic way of terrestrial predators. I am patient. I am a trap.

Three days ago, a herd of mid-sized grazers—about the mass of draft horses—moved into this sector. They are currently grazing on a patch of lithotrophic mat fifty meters away. I can feel their thermal signatures through the salt: heavy, rhythmic pulses of body heat as they digest the film.

They are curious beasts. They navigate by heat gradients. Right now, their thermal sense tells them I am a cold anomaly—a shade stone, perhaps. Harmless.

But I have been waiting for the light to change. As the afternoon deepens into that mineral red, the surface temperature of the salt drops slightly, but the geothermal seep remains constant. I need the grazers to move closer. I need the thermal contrast to shift.

I am cultivating hunger.

Deep inside my shell, I have gathered a cache of *iteiteite* dust—highly conductive. I am slowly, slowly warming it.

When the light turns that deep, unnamed copper, I will radiate. Not a blast of heat, but a sudden, localized bloom of warmth—mimicking the signature of a healthy lithotrophic mat, or perhaps a newly opened geothermal vent. A source of comfort in the cooling evening.

One of the grazers, the largest one, is moving parallel to the ridge. It is slow. It is sweeping its ventral plates over the salt, tasting the gradients.

In an hour, maybe two, it will turn. It will sense the fake heat bloom. It will approach, thinking to rest its belly against a warm patch of stone.

I am waiting for the weight. When it settles over me, I will not strike with teeth or claws. I will simply *deflate* my hydraulic bladders, lowering my carapace onto the salt, and then expand them again, rapidly. A crushing, grinding motion. The salt will do the work. The heavy, dense, hard salt.

I do not need to eat often. But the heavy metals in a Grazer's blood—the iron, the rare earths—are rich. I need them to maintain my shell.

The wind shifts. A Drifter shadow passes, cooling the air for a moment. The subsonic hum of the world seems to thicken.

I wait. I am the patience of the stone given hunger.
