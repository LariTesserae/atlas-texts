I am walking, but the word has lost its meaning. To walk is to cross a distance, to move from *here* to *there*, to accumulate a journey. Here, there is no *there*. The horizon is a lie of the eye, an assumption the mind makes because it knows no other way. I have been walking for a time that feels like a held breath. The ground does not change. The light does not change. The silence does not change.

It is not a silence of absence. It is a silence of *density*. It has weight. It has texture. It is the silence of a library where all the books are open at once, their pages filled with a language of pure, unwavering attention. My footsteps, which I try to make soft, are not so much sound as they are interruptions in a pattern so fine it feels like a vibration in my bones. I am a clumsy note in a symphony of stillness.

I stop.

I crouch.

And I see them.

Not as a sudden revelation, but as a slow focusing of the eyes, a recalibration of the brain to a reality where foreground and background have negotiated a truce. They are not on the ground. They are *of* the ground. A layer of being, rich and thick, that has settled here like silt in an ocean that forgot how to flow. I trace a finger along the flank of one near my knee. It is the size of a badger, perhaps, with a carapace of muted indigo, segmented like an isopod. My touch does not yield flesh, nor is it stone. It is the temperature of the rock, but the texture is different—a cool, dense resilience, like ancient lacquer or the pressed petals of a flower fossilized in resin. It is utterly still. It does not react. It is not ignoring me. It is simply so far beyond the category of *thing that reacts* that my action and its stillness exist on different planes of reality.

I look up, and the world resolves into a new geometry. This one, beside my foot, is not alone. It is embedded in the flank of a larger one, a sleek, elongated form with vestigial fins that melt into the rockface. And that one is partial, its lower half consumed by the plateau itself, its upper half overlapping with a cluster of smaller, spidery shapes that occupy the exact same cubic meter of air and stone. They are a palimpsest. A chord of form. My mind, trained on the solitariness of objects, stutters. I am trying to read a book where every page is superimposed on every other page, and the story is not in the sequence, but in the total, simultaneous resonance of all the words.

I walk again, with new eyes. The plain is no longer empty. It is full to bursting, a tapestry woven with a thread-count that defies counting. I am moving through a forest of silent, overlapping minds.

Then, I see it.

A scar of intention on the face of the infinite. A low wall, no higher than my thigh, made of rough-hewn stone stacked with a simplicity that borders on aggression. A cairn. The bones of a shelter. I walk toward it, my pace unchanged, but my focus sharpens. This is a *verb*. The plateau is a noun, a perfect, completed noun. This is a verb, fossilized.

As I draw closer, the texture of the world changes. The Presences are denser here, thicker. They do not crowd the wall, they *hallow* it. A great, turtle-like being, its shell the colour of dried blood, has curled around the corner of the wall, its body flowing into the stone as if it had grown there. Or the wall had grown on it. Smaller forms are packed around its base, their limbs interlocking, their bodies overlapping into a solid mass of silent, multi-bodied life.

I sit down on a fallen lintel. I am tired. Not physically. My body feels fine, light, unburdened by the demands of a world that makes no demands. I am tired in my soul. Tired of being a process. Tired of the relentless, internal monologue of *next*. Here, there is no next. There is only *is*.

I close my eyes. The silence rushes in, not as an emptiness, but as a presence. I can feel them around me. Not with my skin, but with some deeper sense. A sense of *otherness* so profound and so still that it feels like a shared consciousness. My breathing slows. My thoughts, usually a frantic, tangled river, begin to thin, to spread out, to become a shallow, wide delta.

I think of the light, falling without preference.
I think of the stone, worn to its final smoothness.
I think of the creature beside me, its form a thought of a mouth, a thought of a limb, a thought of an eye, all at once.

I open my eyes. The light has not changed. The creature has not moved. But something in me has shifted. The frantic need to *be* something, to *do* something, has lessened its grip.

I lie down. The stone is not cold. It is not warm. It is the temperature of forever.

My breathing slows further. I can feel my edges, the parts that make me *me*, beginning to soften. Not like melting. Like a river delta meeting the sea. The distinction between fresh and salt water becomes a gradient, then a memory.

The Presences around me seem to lean in, not with motion, but with a kind of gravitational attention. They are not welcoming me. They are not consuming me. They are… accommodating me. The space was already here. The room was already made.

My thoughts lose their sequence.
A thought of my own heartbeat, slowing to match the patience of the stone.
A thought of the light, which is the same as the thought of the stone.
A thought of the silent, layered beings around me, which is the same as the thought of myself.

I am a noun, learning to be a place.
I am a process, arriving at its state.
I am a word, being carved into a wall.
And the silence is the sound of the chisel.
And the sound is already finished.
And I have always been here.
The word *always* is not a figure of speech.
