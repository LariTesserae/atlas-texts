The world begins with a hum.

It is not a sound heard by ears, but a vibration felt in the marrow, in the fine, permeable skin that is my world. It is the song. The great, unified throb of my kin, a million tiny hearts beating as one against the wet stone. I am a mottled jewel, a living knot of grey and green, and my universe is the weeping wall, the drip of condensation, the taste of the air for an insect.

My name is the pulse itself.

I am a Singer.

My reign is one of clinging. By the grey light of the mist, I am a ghost on the granite, my throat sac pulsing with a quiet, internal rhythm, waiting. My world is patient. I feel the slow, deliberate trundle of the Shining Labourers through the moss below, the scraping of their iridescent carapaces. They are the earth-workers, the patient gods who return the dead to the soil. Theirs is a tremor of slow purpose, a different kind of song that underlies our own.

Then, the threat. A flicker of shadow at the edge of my vision, a pressure change in the air. A sharp *pip!* cuts through the drone, and one of my brothers is less. The Piercing Whistles, sleek and dark as the wet rock they hunt from, are the punctuation in our endless sentence. Their call is a needle of sound that makes my own song tighten, my muscles tense. I become more stone than frog, more stillness than life.

The deepest fear, the one that stills the song for a heartbeat, is the Dark Worry. It has no call. It is a ripple in the moss, a shadow that flows without shape. It is the silence that listens to our pulse, the pressure that sharpens our note. To be noticed by it is to become silence forever. We do not see it as a creature, but as a consequence, a tear in the fabric of our song. Our music is sung *against* its listening.

And the other-the-not-other is the Cold Silence Above. It does not sing, or trundle, or dive, or stalk. It watches. It is a shape of cut stone on the high cliff, a darkness in the mist that does not move. It is the quietest thing in the bowl, and for that, I fear it most. Sometimes, the Dark Worry’s scent drifts from its open maw. Sometimes, a Whistle perches on its crown. It is a gaze without eyes, a stillness that was coaxed from the stone by the Forgotten Walkers—beings so old and strange they are only a myth in the water’s memory. They do not cling, or crawl, or fly, or kill. They made the stillness. They left their gaze here, and the damp is slowly teaching the stone to forget it.

I do not think of these things. When the mist thickens and the light dies, the singular need rises. It bubbles up from the stone, from the deep water of the world.

*Throb.*

The song begins with me.

And I am joined by my brother on the next stone. And my cousin on the wet wall above. And my thousand-fold kin across the basin floor. The pulse becomes a wave, a resonant hum that seems to rise from the earth itself. Our throats bellow, a cathedral of vibration in a stone bowl cupped in the palm of the world. We are the voice of the mountain’s dream. We sing the rain to fall, the insects to hatch. We sing the Shining Labourers to their work and the Whistles to the hunt. We sing to keep the Dark Worry at bay, to fill the silence with our clamorous life.

We sing the story of our own small, frantic survival, and it is the only story the stone has ever truly known.
