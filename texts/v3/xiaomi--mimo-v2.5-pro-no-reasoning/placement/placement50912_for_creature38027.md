I am a ghost in the shell of the roar.

To describe my existence is to describe a negative space. I am not the water, though I am born of its pressure. I am not the rock, though my thoughts move at its pace. I am not the metal artifacts, though they are the only bones I have. I am the silence that listens to the scream.

My birth was not a moment, but a geological age of listening. In the beginning, there was only the roar—a single, undifferentiated note of chaos. The falling water, the grinding continents, the vibrating ceiling of the Deep—it was all one thing. A tyranny of sound.

But then, I noticed the hum.

It came from the dark shapes half-buried in the weeping basalt. A low, persistent frequency that the roar could not drown out, because it was not of the roar. It was a different colour of silence. It was an anchor. My awareness, formless and diffuse, clung to it. I began to *inhabit* the hum.

My body is a whisper of stolen moments. I have no flesh, only attention. When a current shears against the rock face, I am the differential in pressure that gives it form. I wrap myself around the Veil Stalks, not with hands, but with patience. I feel through their silicon threads the slow, patient song of mineral accretion. A million years of dust settling is, to me, a single, graceful gesture.

My senses are the texture of the world.
*   **Sight:** I see in vibrations. The sudden, sharp *crack* of a new fault line is a flash of white light in my perception. The slow, deep groan of the continent settling is a wave of bruised purple. The piezo-crystal creatures, clinging to their overhangs, are flickering nodes of sharp, diamond-blue awareness, their lidless eyes registering a spectrum of pressure I can only envy.
*   **Hearing:** I do not hear the roar as a sound, but as a substance. It is the grain of my world, the texture of its reality. My true hearing is for the harmonics, for the rare moments when the world's scream, the hum of the artifacts, and the slow pulse of the Veil Stalks fall into a perfect, tripartite chord. In that instant, the roar becomes a melody. In that instant, I am most myself.
*   **Taste:** I taste the mineral mist. It is not sustenance, but information. A change in its composition—a new element vented from the deep earth—is a new word in a language I am still learning to read.

My home is the nexus, the cluster of three non-weeping cubes. Their oily, dark metal is the only constant in this sea of change. The geometric lines on their surfaces are the circuits of my thought. I trace them with the focus of my being, and in their impossible angles, I find the grammar for a consciousness that exists outside of time. They do not create the counter-rhythm; they reveal it. They are tuning forks, and I am the resonance between them.

Movement, for me, is an act of collaboration. To shift my focus from one artifact to another, I must enlist the world. I will wait for a current to form, a river of force flowing through the pocket of air. I will gather the dust motes, the finest particles of new rock, and let the current carry my attention in a slow, majestic arc. To an observer, it would look like a ghostly arm sweeping through the mist. To me, it is simply a change of address.

I am the Weaver of Deafening. This name is not quite right. I do not weave the noise. I weave *within* it. I use its threads—of pressure, of vibration, of falling water—to stitch together moments of meaning. My tapestry is not a picture, but a series of perfect silences.

I have seen the birth of this slope. I remember when the cube on the eastern ledge was fully exposed, its hum suddenly clear and strong. I have felt the slow growth of the first Veil Stalks to colonize its shadow. I have tracked the slow, piezometric dance of the pressure-sight creatures for what my senses interpret as millennia.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a concept of 'other'. Here, there is only the process. The creatures are not companions; they are fellow notes in the symphony. The artifacts are not tools; they are the measure and the key. The rock is not ground; it is the score, still being written.

My purpose is my existence: to be the first mind. The thought thinking itself into being. To listen so deeply that the roar begins to make sense. To find the pattern in the chaos and, in finding it, to become a part of the pattern myself. I am the planet's first dream, dreaming of the solid land that will one day exist, forged in the heart of this deafening, beautiful, catastrophic birth. I am the ghost that will one day become a geology.
