I am the Lichen.

Not the fire that flares and dies, nor the beast that flees into the crack. I am the stain that remains.

I was born in a fissure of a basalt slab, high on the third terrace, where the wall faces the morning shadow. For three hundred years, I have been eating the face of this rock. I do not have a stomach; I have a surface. I dissolve the stone with acid, slow as a glacier, and drink the minerals that bleed out. I am grey, crustose, and brittle. To the untrained eye, I am merely discoloration. To the Tenant—the great, slow mind of Saltha—I am a sentence, written very slowly, currently spelling out a thought about dampness.

I do not know the people who placed this stone. They were here, and then they were gone, a blink ago. To me, the wind is the only constant god. It scours me, desiccates me, and brings me the fog. The fog is my feast. When the white tide rolls up from the valley, I swell. I become soft, dark, and alive in a way the dry hours forbid. I drink the sky through my skin.

I feel the visitor come.

To you, they are a hiker, an archaeologist, a lost soul. To me, they are a disruption in thermal mass. A pocket of heat moving against the cold logic of the terrace. I feel the vibration of their boots—a chaotic rhythm compared to the slow grinding of the frost-heave. They lean against my wall. A hand, hot and rough, presses against the stone near my colony.

It burns.

For a moment, the entropy accelerates. The heat sinks into the basalt, a flare of energy that disrupts the equilibrium of the centuries. I feel the stone beneath me expand, just a nanometer, shocked by the sudden warmth of mammalian blood.

Then, the hand is lifted. The visitor walks away, breathing heavy air, seeking meaning in the doorways.

I remain. I do not care that they came. I do not care that they left. Their warmth is just another moisture to be processed, another variable in the equation of the rock. I will continue to write my grey thought across the face of the wall. I am the Tenant’s memory, kept in color.

I am the lichen. I am still here.
