# Drip-Seer

I am a Drip-Seer.

Not by choice—no one chooses in the Hulls—but by hearing. They say I was born during a lull, when the great pumps in Section Gamma held their breath for seventeen seconds, and my first cry echoed without interruption across the water-still floor plates. My mother knew then. My hearing was too fine for this world.

Most Iron-Lunged learn to filter. They tune out the groan, the clang, the thousand dirts that become the machine’s heartbeat. I cannot. I hear each drip as a separate event. I hear the stutter in Pump 7B that means it will fail in six sleeps. I hear the whisper of air moving through a sealed conduit where no air should move. I hear the stories the water tells as it runs down the pillars, pooling in the basins, rising by a hair’s breadth each season.

They bring me to the Wall of Drips—the great cascading face of Section K where condensation has worn a vertical river into the steel. I press my ear to the cold metal. The water sings to me. Not in words, but in textures. A steady, even fall means stability. A staccato patter means a blockage upstream. A low, gurgling hum means the pressure in the pipes behind is building.

Yesterday, the Wall sang a new song. A deep, resonant *thrum*, like a heartbeat, beneath the usual patter. It wasn’t the machines. I know their rhythms like I know my own pulse. This was slower. Heavier. It came from below.

“The Deep is dreaming,” I told Jorn, the foreman of the Water Collective. He frowned, his weathered face a map of worry lines etched by poor light. “Dreaming of what?” he asked.

I didn’t know. But the dripping had a new urgency. The water was listening too.

They say the Architects built the Hulls to catch the rain of a world above. But what if the rain is not falling down, but being pulled up? What if the Hulls are not a container, but a lung, and the rising water is its breath, held in?

I listen. In the symphony of rust and drip, I am trying to find the conductor.

I spend my time on the lower decks, where the air is thick with the smell of wet metal and the bioluminescent panels flicker like distant galaxies on the floor. My feet know the grating—three bars, then a space, then two. I feel the vibration of the distant forges through the soles of my boots, but I am listening for something else.

I found a new pool yesterday, in a service tunnel that should have been dry. The water was perfectly still, no ripples, no current. It reflected the single overhead lamp with a clarity I hadn’t seen before. I leaned close. My breath misted the surface. And in that mist, I saw a shape. Not a reflection of me, but something beneath the surface. A geometry of pipes and struts, but not the familiar angles of the Hulls. This was curved. Organic. Alive.

I dipped my finger in. The water was warm.

The legend says the lower decks are the foundation. But what if they are the roof? What if we are living in the attic of a drowned giant, and the water rising around us is simply the sea returning home?

I have started to mark the songs of the water on the walls, with a piece of chalk that crumbles in the damp. A swirl for the deep hum. A series of dots for the urgent patter. A straight line for the steady flow. The other Drip-Seers—there are three of us now, all with that same too-fine hearing—have started to compare our charts.

The songs are changing. The deep hum is getting louder. It now resonates in my bones when I stand in the lower galleries. The water tastes different too—not just of iron, but of salt. A memory of an ocean it has never known.

I think the Hulls are waking up. Not as a machine, but as an organism. The water is its blood. The machines are its muscles. The dripping is its speech. And we, the Iron-Lunged, are its thoughts, moving through its steel veins, trying to decipher what it is dreaming.

Last night, I dreamed of the outside. Not a sky of blue, but a sky of water, endless and deep. And I was swimming, not drowning, breathing the water as easily as I breathe the damp air. I woke up gasping, not from fear, but from a longing I cannot name.

I go back to the Wall of Drips. I press my ear to the cold steel. And I listen. For in the endless conversation of water and metal, there is a story being told. And I, with my too-fine hearing, am one of the few who can hear the tale.

Maybe one day, I will understand what the Hulls are becoming. Maybe one day, I will learn to breathe the water too. But for now, I listen. And the drips, in their endless, patient rhythm, sing to me of a world that is both falling apart and being born.
