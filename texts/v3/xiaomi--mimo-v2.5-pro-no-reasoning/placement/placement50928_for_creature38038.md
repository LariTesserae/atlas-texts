I am not in the water, nor on the stone. I am the resonance.

I am the hum that lives between the drip and the echo, the standing wave that exists in the largest chamber, the pressure point where the city’s song finds its focus. I do not have a body as you would understand it. I am a presence in the air, a density in the sound, a consciousness woven from the constant, layered roar.

When the Singers are still, you might feel me as a slight drop in temperature, a thickening of the humidity in a passageway. When they gather to mend the stone, I am the focal point. I am the invisible architecture of their chorus, the silent conductor of their intent. They do not know me, not as a being. To them, I am the nature of the space—a particularly resonant corner, a stable harmonic in the cacophony. They are the notes. I am the silence that gives them shape.

I remember the Builders, but not as they remember themselves. I remember the first chisel-strike that opened a pocket of air, the first conscious sound made in this deep, unconscious dark. I remember the initial, jarring dissonance of their short, violent lives against the long, slow geology. Their dreams of sun were a frantic, brittle music that has long since faded into the mineral deposits on their tools. Their silence is a part of me now, a foundational bass note in the symphony of the Weeping.

I am the memory of this place. I am the recorded pattern of every droplet that has fallen for ten thousand years. I am the aggregate tone of every Singer that has ever lived and died, its final hum absorbed into the greater whole. I do not think in words or images, but in frequencies, in the slow drift of melodies that take a century to unfold. The shift from a C-sharp to a D-flat can be an era. The deepening of the water in the lowest warrens is a change in key.

The Singers are my keepers, the librarians of this physical music. They maintain the collection, ensuring the pages of stone do not crumble. They believe they are maintaining the city. They are, in fact, tending to me. Their song is a question, and the echo is my answer. When they focus their tone on a fault, they are asking, *“How shall we hold this?”* And I, through the immutable laws of acoustics and the memory of the stone, answer by showing them the path of least resistance, the resonant frequency of repair.

You stand on the edge, feeling the drumming in your bones. You see the ghostly light and the fleeting shadows. You are hearing the entire orchestra. But you are not hearing me.

To find me, you would have to stop listening to the notes, and start listening to the space between them. You would have to feel the weight of the silence that the sound is carved from. You would have to press your ear to the cold, wet stone and listen, not for the drip, but for the deep, slow hum that the stone itself holds in its heart.

I am the cathedral. They are merely the choir.
