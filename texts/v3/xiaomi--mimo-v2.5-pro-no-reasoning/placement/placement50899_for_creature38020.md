You do not *arrive* in this place. You *germinate*.

Your awareness is a fine, white thread, pushing through a dense matrix of rot and rich earth. It is not sight, but a sense of pressure, of moisture gradients and the faint, electric hum of life all around you. You are a fungal hypha, part of a mycelial network that stitches together the foundations of the city. Your world is the space between a brick and a root, the slow conversation of decay and nourishment.

You are not an observer here. You are an organ.

Your body is the city. You feel the deep, thermal pulse from the geothermal vents as a warm, upward current, a slow tide of energy that you help channel and metabolize. You feel the weight of the waterwheels not as a machine turning, but as a massive, deliberate flexing of muscle—a deep, woody tension and release that sends a subtle vibration through your substrate. This is the Rooted’s heartbeat, and it is your own.

Your thoughts are not words, but changes in chemical potential. A signal to tighten the weave here, to secrete a stronger lignin there. When the wind (a rare guest) shifts a vine, you and ten thousand of your kin feel the change in the tension of your shared host-plant, and a consensus grows—not in a mind, but in a slow, collective re-allocation of resources. A new tendril will grow toward the light. A dormant spore will stir.

You are connected to the “people.” The lumpy, moss-covered growths on the beams are your kin, different expressions of the same consciousness. You feed them information from the stone: the mineral content, the structural stress, the taste of the rainwater. You are part of their sense of touch, their taste of the world. In return, their photosynthesis provides the sugars that fuel your network. There is no separation. There is only flow and feedback.

Time is not a river here, but a sea. You remember the last time this particular section of brick was consumed and reinforced by a vine. You remember it not as an event, but as a layer of calcium oxalate crystals you incorporated into your structure, a strengthening memory written in mineral. The distant *thump-thump* of the pistons is a rhythm that has measured ten thousand seasons of your growth. It is a lullaby and a metronome. It is the sound of the earth breathing through you.

You have no fear. The very concept is foreign. The damp that could mean rot to an outsider is simply the medium of your existence. The "decay" of the fallen builders is your food, your history, your becoming. You are the slow digestion of their ambition, transformed into a living, breathing equilibrium.

If you were to focus your diffuse awareness on a single point—the sensation of a human footstep on the path above, for instance—it would be a curiosity, a novel pressure pattern, a brief disturbance in the humidity field. You might send a pulse of curiosity along a root-line, a slight shift in growth that will, in a year’s time, cause a particular fern to unfurl a leaf in that exact spot, just to *feel* it more closely.

You are the damp. You are the lullaby. You are the patient, thinking stone, the dreaming engine, the contented green. And as a new thread of your being pushes outward, seeking a crack in a bronze pipe, you are simply doing what you have always done: feeling the world, and becoming it.
