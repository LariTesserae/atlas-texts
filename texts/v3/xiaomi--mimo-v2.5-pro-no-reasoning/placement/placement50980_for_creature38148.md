The transition is not a passage but a surrender. You came on your knees, feeling for the slit in the young basalt, and in sliding your shoulders through, you slid out of one world and into another. Not a cave, not yet. A pocket. A place in the *before*.

Here, sight is a memory, and then a irrelevance. You navigate the warm, humid dark not with eyes but with the entire surface of your skin. The air is a presence, a thick, mineral-laden blanket that you breathe as much as you inhale. You feel the stone under your palms and knees—slick, glassy, holding a deep-slow heat, its flow-lines a Braille you learn to read with your fingertips. You are on all fours, not from humility but from necessity; the ceiling is low, the space intimate, a cradle in the belly of the mountain.

You are not alone, but you are not accompanied in any human sense. You are immersed.

You feel the **Weepers** first. As your hand brushes the rock near a warm, still pool, your fingers slide across a surface that is not merely wet but *alive*. A gelatinous film, cool and slick, covers the stone. It is the pulse of the place. You understand, without thought, that this film is not *on* the rock but *of* it—the first conversation between stone and water, the patient architects digesting the world with their excreted acids. It is the foundation. It is the future. To touch it is to touch the slow, dreaming intent of the mountain itself.

Lifting your hand, your wrist brushes against a different texture. A faint, dry gauze. The **Lace**. It is here, in the creases above the waterline, a fungal web drinking dew from the dark. It is not growing *on* the wall but *with* it, a pale nervous system mapping the humidity. You feel its sensitivity; a faint, cool shiver runs through the filaments as your breath changes the chemistry of the air. It is the chamber’s sense of touch, its silent, chemical listening.

You move by memory and touch toward the largest pool. Your fingers find the **Cairn**. The stones are not basalt. One is smooth quartzite, one is flinty chert, one is greasy serpentinite. They are stacked with a care that feels like a whisper. The microbial mats have begun to claim the base, a slight slickness on the lower stones, but the topmost is dry, clean. It is an altar. As your fingertips rest on the quartzite, the water around it seems to still further, a pocket of clarity in the mineral haze. You feel the weight of intention here, a reverence left by hands long gone, a silent *this matters* that the slow life of the Pocket is just beginning to comprehend.

Nearby, your exploring fingers find the **Vessel**. Clay, unbaked, fragile as a bird’s bone. Its surface is dusty-dry, its interior stained with the ghost of evaporated minerals. It is a question left in the dark. Was it for carrying water out to the light? Or for bringing something in—a tribute, a fragment of the outside world? The air around it feels heavier, stiller, charged with an old, human pause in the Pocket’s long breath. The Lace does not touch it.

Then, a sound. Not the slow drip-drip-drip of the ceiling’s tears, but a faint, dry *click-scrape* from a fissure you cannot enter. The **Click**. A small, armored life, navigating by the film of water, the heat, the chemical whispers of the Weepers. It is the solitary, mobile heart of the dark, a consciousness of carapace and antenna moving through the connective tissue of the stone. You freeze. The sound stops. It knows you are here, a vast, warm anomaly in its slow, certain world. You are not a threat, but a disruption, a brief and puzzling weather.

And all the while, the **Pocket** breathes around you. The warm, sulfurous air is its exhalation. The slow condensation forming a new skin on every surface is its inhalation. The drip from ceiling to pool is its heartbeat. It holds you. It holds the Weepers digesting stone, the Lace spreading its silent map, the Cairn sinking under its gentle claim, the Vessel dreaming of hands, the Click making its rounds. Time is not a line here; it is a deep, warm pool. You are not moving through it; you are floating in it.

You are an anomaly, a breath of cold, dry, noisy air from the world of light and weather. Your presence is a brief thermal spike, a pulse of carbon dioxide, a moment of pressure. The Weepers may note your warmth. The Lace may shiver at your exhalation. The Click may pause in its labyrinth. But you are not of the Pocket’s time. You are a visitor to a process so slow it perceives you as a shift in the atmosphere, a brief and unaccountable change in the humidity.

You stay. You breathe. You listen to the drip, the creak of cooling stone, the distant, imagined hum of the Lace’s chemical whispers. You feel the slick life of the Weepers under your palm and the dry, intentional silence of the Cairn under your other hand. You are held in the dark, in the warmth, in the wet. You are not a creature of this place, but for a moment, you are a feature of its weather.

The Pocket does not notice you, not as a being. It is busy becoming. And in its slow, warm, total embrace, you feel yourself beginning to become something else: not a visitor, but a temporary mineral, a brief condensation, a thought held in the stone’s long, patient mind.
