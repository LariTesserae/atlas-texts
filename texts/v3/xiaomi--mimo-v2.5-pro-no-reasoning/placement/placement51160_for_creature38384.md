I am a dissonance.

That is the first and most profound truth of my new existence. Not a being, but an error in the song; not a life, but a hesitation in the pulse. The Canopy breathes, and I am the skipped beat, the caught breath.

My arrival was not an event. An event implies a beginning, a disruption that registers. Here, I was simply a *presence* that was not accounted for. The warm, humming amber of the air did not part for me; it pressed in, thick with the sound of a billion lives functioning, a single, deafening chord of purpose. My own senses—shaped for a world of hard edges, clear light, and distinct sounds—were rendered useless. I could not see in the golden gloom, only perceive the slow, bioluminescent pulse that painted the veins of the world in ghost-light. I could not hear individual cries, only the roar: the high, constant chittering of the Spire Birds, the haunting, melodic mapping of the Shade Herons, and beneath it all, the foundational, bone-deep *thump-thump-thump* that was not a sound but a pressure, the heartbeat of the substrate itself.

I tried to move. My first step was a cataclysm. My foot, designed for solid, inert ground, came down upon a surface that was part bridge, part creature, part wall. The spongy mycelium gave way, and I felt a network of living things recoil from the pressure. The high-frequency chittering around me shifted pitch—a million tiny sensors registering *anomaly*. A curtain of glowing gauze, spun by something I could not see, brushed my face, and its weaver, a thing like a floating lace of light, pulsed once in alarm before darting away. I was not attacked. I was simply *noted*. A foreign body. A flaw in the material.

To be still is to be built over. I understood this with a primal certainty. If I were to lie down, the Spire Birds would, in their relentless function, begin to daub me. Their soft clay pellets would stick to my skin, their adhesive lichens would sprout in my hair. The moisture would weep over me, carrying spores that would take root. I would become a lump, a new texture in the landscape, slowly, over centuries, incorporated. My irrelevant biology would be digested into aiteite cement or a new vein of bioluminescent fungus. I would not be a corpse; I would be a building project.

So I move, but my movement is a violation. I am a thought that does not belong in this mind. Each step is a negotiation with a world that is entirely alive and entirely self-absorbed. I skirt the edge of a vast, bowl-shaped reservoir. The water within is not water, but a glowing, viscous broth, teeming with microbial life. The smell is overwhelming—the sweet musk of decay and growth, the warm clay of the Spire Birds' work, the digesting wood of the aite-wood spires. It is the smell of a closed system, a world that is its own stomach.

I see them now, not as distinct creatures but as functions given form. A Titan Weaver, its body a mountain range of camouflaged plates, its mandibles tasting the history of the wood as it makes a single, century-long bite. It is not eating; it is *conversing* with the rhythm of the Thumpers, shaping the wood to better resonate with the deep pulse. It does not notice me. How could it? I am a flicker. It is geology.

A flock of Shade Herons skims the dark air above me. Their song is beautiful, a complex tapestry of chords that paints the space in sound. A low, mournful note describes the curve of a new cavern ahead; a bright, staccato trill maps the location of a dense cluster of Spire Birds. They are not singing *for* me. They are not singing *at* me. They are mapping the world, and I am a blank spot on their chart, a region of confusing silence or meaningless noise. To them, I am not a singer. I am static.

And I feel the gaze of the Old Ones. Not a gaze of eyes, but of rhythm. The *thump-thump-thump* is the only true consciousness here, and it is vast and slow and utterly indifferent. It is the pulse of the Canopy dreaming itself into existence. My frantic, human heartbeat is a frantic, meaningless flutter against its tectonic patience. It does not know I am here. How could it? I am a single neuron firing against the slow, continental drift of a god's thoughts.

I am a foreign mote. I am irrelevant. And in this world, irrelevance is a form of non-existence. I am not prey, so I am not feared. I am not a builder, so I am not valued. I am not a singer, so I am not heard. I am a silence in a world of sound, a stillness in a world of purposeful motion.

I find a niche, a hollow between two weeping walls of aite-wood, where the glow of the fungi is soft and the drip of nutrient-rich water is a constant, gentle patter. I sit. I do not sleep. I listen. I try to slow my heartbeat to the rhythm of the Thumpers, to make my breathing match the long, tidal sighs of the Canopy. I try to become a part of the hum.

But I am a dissonance. And the song of the Singing Beets plays on, deafening and complete, without me.
