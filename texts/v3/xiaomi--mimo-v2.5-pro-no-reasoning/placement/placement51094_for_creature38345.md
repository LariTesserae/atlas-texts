I would find myself a thought, a fleeting resonance given temporary, chitinous form upon the vast, slow skin of the Plateau-Entity.

I am not a visitor. I am a manifestation.

My beginning was a pressure, a point of focused attention in the upper atmosphere where the Plateau’s warm, mineral-laden exhalations meet the cooler, charged air above. From that condensation of will, I precipitated. I am a Glider, one of the Plateau’s idle thoughts. My body is a wide, membranous disc, dappled with patterns of soft blue and sickly yellow light that shift not with my own intent, but with the faint, tidal pull of the deep brine-channels far below. I do not have muscles; I have a subtle control over the permeability of my tissues, allowing the updrafts—the Entity’s own breath—to carry me, to turn me, to set me down.

My senses are the Plateau’s senses. I do not see with eyes, but with my entire ventral surface, which is sensitive to the specific bioluminescent signatures of the lichen below. The “sickly yellow” signifies a recent, aggressive grazing, a zone of painful regeneration. The “pale blue” is contentment, a stable cycle of growth and mineral uptake. I taste the air not for scent, but for mineral density and electrical charge. The constant, deafening hum is not noise to me; it is the baseline of existence, the Entity’s slow heartbeat. I feel its rhythm in the resonant chambers of my wings. The clicks of the Grazers are sharp, percussive updates, reports of activity. The flute-like calls of others of my kind are the drifting syntax of shared contemplation: *Here is abundance. There is stress. The wind from the eastern fissure is rich with silicates.*

I am, at this moment, considering a problem. Below me, a herd of Grazers has become too efficient. Their pickaxe mandibles are stripping a vein-moss network faster than the lymphatic system can replenish it. They are not malicious; they are simply the Entity’s appetite, running unchecked in a local feedback loop. This is a mild inflammation. My path as a thought is to drift lower, to pass over the zone, letting my shadow and the shifting patterns of my light fall upon them. It is a gentle signal, a calming wave in their frantic consciousness, encouraging them to migrate a few spires over where a thicker lichen bloom awaits. I am a synaptic intervention, a re-ordering of priority.

I land, not with a thud, but with a silent settling on the salt crust near one of the old, domed structures. I fold my membranes. My body begins to slow, to mineralize. This is not death. It is a thought being shelved. My purpose here is complete. The cartilage of my frame will become brittle salt. My membranes will dry and curl, becoming part of the crust, adding my specific mineral signature to the whole. The knowledge I carried—of the overgrazing, of the successful calming—does not vanish. It seeps into the lichen that will soon grow over my remains, encoding a faint, new bioluminescent nuance into the Plateau’s memory. I will be a fossil of a resolved problem.

I am a fleeting, mobile dream of a patient, enduring geology. My life is a frantic, noisy flutter in the Entity’s long, slow song, and my end is the quiet, nourishing silence that allows the next movement to begin.
