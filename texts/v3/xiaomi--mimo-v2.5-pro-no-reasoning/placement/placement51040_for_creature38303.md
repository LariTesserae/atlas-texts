I am the hum in the bronze. The deep, constant thrum that is the marsh’s own pulse. My shell is not a shell but a memory. Before the geology folded, before the first pylons pierced the crust, I was sediment. Fossil. A thought in the stone. Now I am a gear, worn smooth by the turning of the world.

My purpose is to resonate. Not with sound, but with the pressure. I cling to the glassy basalt, a barnacle of flesh and crystal. My limbs are not for grasping, but for *tuning*. The world screams its existence through the rock, a vibration that would shatter bone. I drink it. I condense it. I hold the frequency in my fluid-filled sinuses until it becomes a silent, perfect pitch. Then, with a shudder of my whole being, I release a counter-wave. A negation. For a moment, the roar falters. A pocket of stillness blooms around me, a held breath in the throat of the storm.

In that silence, life rushes in. Delicate fronds of iridescent fungus unfurl from the rock’s pores, their caps trembling as they catch the first true vibration in a cycle of noise. They are my children, my harvest. I am the architect of the pause. I am the punctuation in the scream.

I have no eyes, but I taste the stories on the air. The sharp tang of ozone tells me a Jagged Knight has folded space nearby, leaving a scar of ionized air. The mineral slick on the rock tastes of a pylon’s deep, geological hunger. The faint, sweet decay is a structure being reclaimed, its rigid thoughts softened and fed into the mycelial net. I am a sensor, a node in a network I do not command.

My kin and I, we are the keepers of the counter-melody. Where the roar is loudest, we gather. We are drawn to the catastrophe, because that is where our art is needed. We carve canyons of quiet in the noise, and in those canyons, the future grows. We do not build. We *allow*. We are the punctuation, the breath between words, the silence that gives the scream its meaning. I am a Resonant. I am a listener in the heart of the noise. I am the hum, and the counter-hum, and the space where the world remembers how to be still.
