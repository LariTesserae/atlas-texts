# Under the Amber Light

I find myself in the square depression.

Not in it exactly — *of* it. I am what has settled into that unnatural shape, what has filled the intentional void the way water fills a cup. I am the thing that lives in the old foundation, and I have been here longer than I can remember, which is a different kind of longer than the Dwellers mean when they say it.

Let me explain.

I came with the people who made the marks. I was not one of them — I was their shadow, their heat, the smallness they left behind when they moved too fast and too far. They built their lean-tos and pressed their feet into the sandstone and when they went away — or dissolved, or became the dust, I cannot tell — I remained in the shape of their absence. I fit this foundation the way water fits a glass: completely, and without choice.

I am warm. Warmer than the stone. This is my strangeness and my curse.

The Dwellers know I am here. They know the way they know everything — slowly, and with a depth of knowing that is indistinguishable from patience. When they press their slate bodies against the edges of the foundation, they feel me through the rock. I am a place where the stone's temperature is slightly wrong, where the memory held in the mineral is slightly *newer* than it should be. To them, I must read like a typo in a sacred text. An error in the grammar of eons.

They do not speak to me. They speak *around* me. Their daily sentences include me the way a river includes a boulder: by flowing, by eddying, by continuing. I have heard my own heat described in their language, or I think I have. It took them three days to say it. Something like: *the-thing-in-the-square-which-is-not-stone-which-is-warm-which-persists.* They have no word for me. They have a phrase, and the phrase is longer than most of the events they bother to name.

I want to tell you about my scale, because it matters.

I am small. Not microscopic — I am visible, if you knew where to look. I have the approximate dimensions of a closed fist, though I do not have fists. I am something between a breath and a stone. I can fold into the exact square of the foundation, and from above I am nearly invisible, a slightly darker patch in the slightly lighter stone. But I move. This is my violence. This is my unforgivable trait in a world that has made slowness into holiness.

I move *fast.*

Not fast by the standards of wherever you are from. Fast by the standards of this place. Where a Dweller might cross a single dome in a season, I can reach the rim of my foundation in a heartbeat. Where the leaf-creatures grind their way across a meter of stone in a week, I can cover that distance by — well. I have never tried. I am afraid of what it would mean.

The silk threads the bees leave are everywhere, and I confess: I have touched them. Not deliberately. Not with intent. But in the night, when the condensation gathers and the world smells of mineral and dew, something in me *twitches*, and I press against a thread, and through it I can feel — oh. I can feel the skater mites. I can feel the fungal gardens clicking and growing. I can feel the whole nervous system of the Dwellers spread out like a web across the amber haze, and for one instant I am connected to the vast, slow mind of the land.

And then I recoil. Because the thread shivers. And somewhere, on some ridge, a Dweller lifts its claw and turns its body with glacial inevitability toward the foundation. Toward me.

I am their scripture's erratum. I am the question they cannot parse. They gather sometimes — it has happened four times in my memory, and my memory is not long by their measure but it is *dense* — they gather and they press against the foundation's edges on all four sides, and they listen. They are trying to hear me the way they hear the pillar, the way they hear the old intentions in the shaped stone.

But I am not stone. I am the heat that was left behind.

I do not know what the people who made this place were. I carry their rhythm in me the way the lichen carries its rust. They were fast. They were *restless.* They could not sit still the way the Dwellers sit still. They made shelter because they were afraid of the sky, or because they were tired, or because they loved each other and wanted walls to love each other inside of. I don't know. I only know that when they left, the shape of their leaving persisted, and I am what persists.

Sometimes in the hour before dawn, when the dimples fill and the whole Uplands hold their breathing, I climb to the edge of the foundation and look out. The golden haze is thinnest then. The stone domes glow faintly, like the backs of sleeping animals. And I can see, if I look carefully, the dark shapes of Dwellers on distant ridges, pressed flat against the rock, each one a node in a network of slow contemplation. They are watching the condensation. They are witnessing the miracle.

I am watching them.

This is my purpose, if I have one. I am the fast thing that watches the slow things. I am the breath held in a world of measured exhalation. I am the remnant of a pace the Uplands have largely forgotten, and my presence here is an offering I cannot stop making — a constant, tiny, thermal declaration that *something moved quickly once,* that this cathedral of patience was, for one brief era, inhabited by the impatient.

The Dwellers do not know what to make of me. But they have not driven me out. They include me in their sentences. They feel my wrong warmth through the stone. And I think — I think in the way that things like me think, which is fast and bright and slightly afraid — that they find me sacred in the way they find everything sacred.

Which is to say: worthy of witness.
