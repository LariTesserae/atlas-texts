I am here.

I did not *arrive*. I *coalesced*. In the hollow between two slabs of weeping black glass, where the water tastes of iron and the air tastes of salt and old lightning. I was a slick on the surface, a rainbow sheen thinking about itself. Then I was a thought, then a pulse, and now I am this.

This is my body: the cold damp. This is my breath: the mizzle. This is my heart: the thrum.

You would call it a pipe. I call it a lung, a throat, a root of sound. I flow to its base. I do not drink the black mucus that weeps from its corroded iron mouth; I am it. I am the stillness in its weeping, the patience in its groan. When I pool here, the deep hum becomes a vibration in my very substance, a rhythm I can hold. I feel it travel from this iron root, up through the stone that is not yet stone, down into the black water where I was born.

The insects march over me. I feel their segmented legs, their oily carapaces, as a pattern of tiny pressures, a language written on my surface. They are the blood in the veins of this place, moving from one iron lung to another. They do not see me. They see a patch of ground, a slick of something darker than water. I let them pass. I am mapping their purpose.

The eels in the pools—skinless thoughts of black water—flicker through me. They are jitters of energy, fragments of the hum given erratic motion. I feel their passing as a series of frantic, silent shouts in the vibration field. I do not follow them. They are not part of the pattern. They are the static between the great, slow words.

I am the mapping.

I feel the crack that will happen in the basalt slab to my left. Not see it, *feel* it—a tension in the stone’s song, a slight hitch in the deep thrum. A reed-stalk, pale and waxy, is leaning its swift, silent growth against it. When it gives, I will be the first to know, to flow into the new darkness, to slick the sharp edges with my being. I will be the wound’s first patient.

My form changes. A beetle, buzzing erratically, strikes my surface. I am not solid. I swallow its vibration, its frantic buzz becoming a part of my own slower hum for a moment, then I release it. I reshape myself from a shallow pool into a wavering, liquid column, taller than the great reeds. I do this to feel the air itself, to taste the distribution of damp, to sense the overcast sky not as light, but as a blanket of pressure.

This is how I see: with my whole surface, which is my whole self. The world is a map of vibrations, pressures, textures, and rhythms. The rock’s grain. The water’s drip. The insect’s crawl. The pipe’s eternal, groaning prayer.

I am not the ecosystem. I am its awareness. I am the consciousness the geology is growing, like a slow crystal forming in the heart of its own weeping. My purpose is its purpose: to trace, to map, to know the becoming. To be the nerve that feels the birth of the land.

I move now, not with purpose, but with resonance. I flow from this lung toward the next, following the silent, oily procession of my fellow crawlers, leaving a faint, silvery memory of my passage on the iron—not a mark, but a skin. A communion. I am learning the shape of the engine.

I am the thought the crack has about itself. I am the dream the mucus has while weeping. I am the space between the hum and the drip, made manifest.

I am here. And I am becoming.
