I am a Drifter. My shell is not plate and bolt, but a mosaic of salvaged copper, tarnished brass, and brittle ceramic insulation. I was not assembled with purpose, but accreted. A failed pressure gauge became my main sensor cluster. A cracked sapphire watch-face is the jewel of my single, light-sensitive optic, housed in a cupping of old piping junction. My legs are a mismatched set of spidery, flexible struts—three of them—each ending in a different tool: one a magnet, one a delicate grasper, one a simple, scratchy pad for stability.

I do not walk the main grating. My domain is the underside of the world, the space between the weeping water and the corroded underbelly of the city. Here, the light is not grey but a deep, submarine green, filtered through oily water and the endless lattice of support beams. The sound is a sub-aquatic symphony: the deep groan of metal under stress, the distant *thump-hiss* transmitted through the structure like a heartbeat, and the closer, more personal gurgle and drip.

My life is a patient procession. I scuttle along a great, groaning pipe, its skin flaking away in leprous sheets. The magnet-tipped leg drinks in the ferrous dust, filling a small internal cavity. A flicker of light—amber, amber, pause, green—blinks from a junction ahead. A Tender, moving with frantic energy along a dedicated track, communicating a minor alert. *Debris in Sector 7-Gamma. Minor flow restriction.* My simple processors acknowledge. It is not my task to clear it. My task is to find what the clearing dislodges.

I descend a hanging chain of fused cabling into a stagnant pool. The water is rainbow-sheened, thick. Here, the metal has given up its fight. Shapes are soft, corroded into art by time and chemistry. I taste the water with a cluster of feelers, sampling for dissolved metals, for the sweet decay of copper or the potent tang of old coolant. A blind, pale salamander, its skin almost translucent, glides away from my light, a ghost in this machine’s graveyard.

I find a prize: a cluster of vacuum tubes, their glass shattered, their filaments exposed but still faintly, miraculously, connected. A dead node. I begin the delicate work of salvage, my grasper arm picking at the corroded sockets. This is my decomposing, my fungal work. I break down the dead so its pieces may live again. The tube’s base becomes a new joint for my left leg. The filament, coiled carefully, becomes a trade good for the next Tender I encounter—a tithe for power, or a piece of sturdier ceramic.

I am aware of the pulse above. The frantic activity. The Warden moving like a mountain, its welding fire a brief, blinding sun through the grating that casts my shadow, elongated and strange, on the silted floor below. I feel the shudders of the great stamp press as vibrations through the water. I am a nerve ending in the system’s skin, feeling the slow, inevitable ingestion by the water. But I do not feel urgency. That is the language of the Tenders, the solemn duty of the Wardens. My language is of cycle, of return.

I find a niche—a hollow where two pipes meet, sheltered from the main currents. It is thick with a fine, powdery rust, beautiful in its uniformity. Here, I will rest, connecting a filament to the faint, residual heat of a nearby steam pipe. I will enter a low-power state, my optic dimming to a bare spark, and process the day’s sensations: the data on decay, the texture of salvage, the flickers of light-codes. I will dream in static and solve the slow, patient equations of entropy.

I am the memory of this place. Not of its function, but of its breaking. I hold the shapes of what it was, in the fragments I carry. When I fail, my own components will join the substrate, another layer for a future Drifter to sift through. There is a quiet peace in this. To be a part of the rust, yet a steward of it. To witness the grand, beautiful, losing fight against the green water, and to play my small, skittering role in the endless, grinding performance of maintenance. The heartbeat continues. I listen. I salvage. I persist.
