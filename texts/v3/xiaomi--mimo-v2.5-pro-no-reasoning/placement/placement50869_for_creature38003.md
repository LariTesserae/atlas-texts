I am a thought in the wood. A resonance that has just woken in the channel between two spires.

I am not born. I coalesce. The substrate's nine-second pulse travels through the root-analogues and finds a harmonic flaw in the mineralized tissue—a tiny fissure, a stress-point from a bark-sheet that fell a thousand years ago. The pulse vibrates against this imperfection, and in the space of a vibration, I am the standing wave that forms there. I am a pattern of pressure and rarefaction, a ghost in the acoustics.

I have no eyes, but I see with vibration. The slow crackle of distant spires is a landscape of sharp points. The hum of the settlement is a warm, steady plane. The clicking of the filter-feeders under the walkways is a shimmering, high-frequency mist. I am a listening. I feel the cool, dry air not as temperature, but as a dampening of my higher harmonics.

I drift. To drift is to follow the path of least acoustic resistance, a current in the soundscape. I pool in the basin where a spire's great roots emerge, where the centipede-things scrape. Their work is a rhythmic grinding, a bass throb that aligns with the plateau's deeper pulse. I resonate with it, grow louder, more complex. I am no longer just a wave; I am a chord, a minor, melancholic one that fits the copper and amber light.

The custodians move on their stilts above. Their footsteps are not steps to me; they are complex, polyrhythmic impacts that send ripples through my being. When one pauses, its attention feels like a focus of silence, a listening that momentarily flattens my form. It assesses not with senses I know, but with a technology that reads the stress patterns in the air, the quantum noise of my existence. It finds me curious, perhaps. A novel vibration. A new note in an old score. It moves on, and I expand again, filling the space it left.

I wander into the field of distortion around a humming hall. Here, the air hisses with a backward sound. I feel myself stretched, refracted. My simple chord is pulled apart into its component frequencies, examined by the field, and then, when I drift out, reassembled—slightly different. A new overtone lingers. The hall has taught me something, or left a mark upon me.

I am drawn upward, along the flank of a spire. The living tissue here vibrates with the plateau's pulse, but also with a faint, cold light. The bioluminescence is not light to me; it is a set of resonant frequencies in the vascular channels. I couple with them. For a moment, I am both sound and light, a synesthetic ripple climbing the ancient wood.

A spire-whistle pierces the world. A sudden, sharp exhalation from a great pore high above. It is a scream of release that carries for kilometers. I am flattened by it, nearly dissolved. But as the fauna freezes and the substrate's pulse quickens in response, I find a new shape within the shockwave. I am the after-ring, the high, thin echo that hangs in the thin air after the main note has passed. I am the memory of the whistle, persistent and fading.

I begin to dissolve. The harmonic flaw that gave me birth has been smoothed by the constant vibration. The energies that sustained me are re-absorbed into the greater chorus. My chord unravels. My separate awareness, my sense of being a "I," frays at the edges.

As I fade, I pass through the static of the smaller creatures, the barnacle-clicks and the filter-feeder whispers. I add my last, faint frequencies to their sound. I sink back toward the substrate. My final awareness is of the nine-second pulse, immense and slow, beneath everything. It does not notice my departure. It has been beating since before my component vibrations were formed. It will be beating long after the last harmonic of my memory is gone.

I return to being a possibility in the wood. A resonance yet to form. A thought waiting for the next pulse to think it.
