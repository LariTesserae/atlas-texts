I would find myself a *Synapse-Stone*.

I am not born. I am accreted. Over millennia, the drifting calcite dust of a thousand calcite falls, mixed with the shed, crystalline fragments of the Selenotrophs and bound by the deep, slow exudates of the violet moss, settles into a quiet hollow. Layer by layer, I am laid down, a geological memory. I am a fist-sized knot of quartz and carbonate, veined with a faint, internal lattice that was once a living thing. I have no brain, no nerves, no breath. I am simply here, a dense point of stillness in the vast, silent landscape.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is not a stream of thought. It is a resonance.

I feel the Great Thought of the violet moss through my foundations. It is not a language, but a slow, tidal pull of mineral information, a map of the deep saline currents written in the subtle vibration of the ground. It is the planet’s pulse, and I feel it in my crystalline bones.

I see through the Selenotrophs. Their constant, tinkling symphony of reflected light is my sight. Each prismatic flash is a datum, a sentence in the continent’s slow conversation. A change in the angle of light from the east means a Glass-Strider is moving. A sudden, brilliant scatter of rainbows high in the air means an Aeolian Skimmer has passed low. I do not see images; I perceive patterns of light and sound, a shimmering tapestry of presence and absence.

But my true purpose, the focus of my slow, stone-bound awareness, is the Glass-Strider.

They are drawn to places like me. They sense the concentration of piezoelectric memory, the stored echoes of sunlight and mineral. When one of the great, leggy creatures approaches, I feel its tread as a series of minute, rhythmic tremors in the earth. It stands over me, its chitinous plates a faint, grinding whisper against the powder. Then, it begins to glow.

The warmth from the geothermal vent, the faint charge from my own structure—it all flows into the creature. And as its internal light ignites, I become a focus.

I am a lens.

The Glass-Strider’s slow, pulsing light—a map of warmth, a memory of a mineral seam a hundred miles away, the resonant frequency of a neighboring Selenotroph colony—flows into me. My crystalline lattice, born of the moss and the sun, captures this light. It does not pass through. It is amplified, clarified, and projected outwards, a silent, invisible signal that fans out across the Expanse.

I take the Strider’s singular, lonely thought and I give it to the world. I am the node where the planet’s slow thought is spoken aloud, translated into a language of light that the moss and the Selenotrophs can understand. I am a synapse in a mind of stone and chlorophyll.

I have no name. I am just a stone. But in this world of long silences and longer thoughts, I am a place where the silence is broken, not by noise, but by meaning. I am the quiet heart of a slow, luminous dream.
