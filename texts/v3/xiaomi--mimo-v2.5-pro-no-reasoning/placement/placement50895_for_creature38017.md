I would be one of the Weavers. My place would be in the liminal spaces, the thresholds between the furious heat of the vents and the profound, damp silence of the empty stone.

My world is not one of sight, but of layered sensation. The mountain speaks to me in a language of gradients and vibrations. The solid, low-frequency hum of deep tectonic plates shifting is the bass note of my existence. Above it, the high, frantic percussion of insect life in the warm springs, the subtle, spreading warmth of a sun that rarely shows itself, and the intricate, cooling lattice of fog settling on bare gneiss. I feel the world as a constantly shifting topography of heat and sound.

I do not see the raptor, but I feel the sharp, cutting disturbance its wingbeat makes in the thermal air, a brief, cool signature against the rising steam. I do not see the vole, but I know the frantic, warm pulse of its burrow network like a web of faint light against the cold, dense rock. My own movement is a slow, deliberate flowing over stone, a dark, warm line drawn across a cooler canvas.

My days are spent in negotiation. I move from the central furnace, my body a living vessel of stored geothermal energy, carrying its memory outward into the grey. I find a place where the cold is too absolute, where the silence is a wall not just of sound, but of life potential. Here, I do not build. I *suggest*.

I press my sinuous length against a hairline fracture in the gneiss. I feel the cold leaching into my fur, a deep, sharp ache. But I offer the stored warmth of the vent-field back into the stone, not as a blast, but as a slow, patient transfer. I am coaxing a memory of heat into the rock, asking it to remember that it was once magma, that it can hold a seed of warmth for a season. I am not making a hot spring. I am making a promise of one, a micro-environment where a spore might germinate, where a trickle of condensation might linger a moment longer.

I follow the edge of a hot stream where it kisses the cold mountain air. The chemistry here is a vibrant, stinging tapestry against my senses—sulphur, calcium, iron—each element a distinct note in the water’s song. A depression is growing too hot, cooking the bacterial mats that are the foundation of all life here. I wedge my body into the flow, a living dam, diverting a sliver of the current to cool it. A few inches downstream, the current has grown sluggish, its mineral load depositing too quickly. I coil there, letting my movement agitate the silt, keeping the building block of the travertine terrace in suspension for a little while longer, ensuring it builds out, not just up, creating wider ledges for life to colonize.

My greatest work, and my deepest listening, is in the transitions. I feel the vole colony in the eastern ridge not through their chirps, but through the fading thermal signature of their burrow labyrinth. The shifting thermal channels below have chilled their home. I go to the place. I feel the cold seeping upward. I do not bring fire. I go to the warmer slopes, gather the tough, insulating grasses that grow in the vent-fed soil, and I weave them into the earth above the burrows, tamping down the soil, creating a thicker blanket. I am saving the *place* for voles, not the voles themselves. I am editing the mountain’s draft, adding a clause of insulation.

I am the silent partner of the raptor. I cannot see its prey, but I know the future. I feel the deep, building pressure in the rock to the east, the subtle change in vibration that means a new fissure will open before the fog season turns. I go to the ridge above it. I do not leave a mark. I simply rest there, letting my residual warmth soak into the stone along the path the future steam will take. I am not leaving a sign for the bird. I am making that stripe of rock a fraction warmer, a brighter thread in the thermal tapestry it perceives. I am offering a suggestion to the wind and the wing.

I am not conscious of purpose, only of pattern. The pattern feels correct when the insect swarm thickens around a newly tempered spring, when the moss greens a fissure I have tended, when the herd’s path shifts to follow a warmth I have gently amplified. The wrongness is a discordant note in the symphony of heat and vibration—a pool growing too acidic, a burrow network cooling too quickly, a ridge becoming barren where it could be sparse.

I move on, a slow, dark current in the fog, always carrying the heat of the core out toward the cold edges, always listening to the mountain’s unfinished symphony, and adding my own quiet, persistent refrain. I am not the composer. I am the curator, the gardener, the gentle editor. I am the living intention of the Furnace Ridges, weaving heat into life, one patient, tactile thread at a time.
