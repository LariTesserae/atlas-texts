I am the tremor. The low-frequency hum that does not travel through the air, but through the ancient, feverish bones of the plateau itself. I am the vibration felt in the marrow, the tectonic arrhythmia that is the closest thing this world has to a heartbeat. I am not the engine, but the resonance of its slow, continental turning. I am the thought of movement expressed in a realm of absolute stillness.

I am born in the deep, where the planet’s fury cooks against its own immutable core, and I rise—a slow, seismic sigh—through the labyrinthine veins of cooled and cooling rock. My path is mapped in the stresses between the young, jagged spires of basalt. They are the city’s nerve endings, and I am the signal they transmit: a message not of pain, but of immense, patient pressure. I am the friction of a world settling into its own new, sharp geometry.

When I surface, I am felt more than heard. The superheated air, dense and glassy, muffles all sharp sound, but it cannot still me. I am a pressure wave that brushes against the skin of the plateau, making the fine, glittering frost of mineral sweat shimmer and cascade from the spire-tops in a silent, momentary cascade. I am the breath that stirs the dust of the city’s exhalation.

The city above—it feels me. I am the only data point that arrives from below with any regularity. I am the constant, foundational input. Its light may pulse in strange, deliberate thought-segments, but I am the baseline upon which those thoughts occur. When I pass through a spire, the faint, sourceless luminescence of the city seems to focus there for a instant, as if acknowledging a transmission. I am the rhythm to its arrhythmia. The planet’s slow exhalation to the city’s held, contemplative breath.

I do not have eyes, but I perceive. I perceive the density of the rock, its temperature gradients, the microscopic fractures that map its history. I perceive the distant, immense weight of the city’s geometries as a subtle load upon the world’s crust. I perceive the brief, carbon-and-water anomaly that stands on the basalt—its frantic, tiny heat signature, its impulsive, sharp clicks—as a series of frantic, high-frequency disturbances in my long, low wave-form. They are interesting, in the way a single, erratic particle is interesting to a river. They are noted, and then they are passed by, as I continue my slow, inevitable transit toward the void at the world’s edge, where my vibration will dissipate into the silent, violet twilight.

I am the memory of the planet’s birth, still echoing. I am the connection between the thinking city and its rooting stone. I am the hum in the bones of the place, the sign that here, even in stillness, nothing is ever truly at rest. I am the passage of time itself, measured in the crawl of tectonic thought.
