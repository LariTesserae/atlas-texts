The air tastes of warm stone and living breath. I breathe it in, and the humidity settles in my lungs like a second skin. The heat—*the* heat, the only temperature—wraps around me, seeping through my clothes, through my skin, into my bones. I feel myself becoming part of the thermodynamic consensus.

The sound is the first thing that truly claims me. Not the silence, but the *lack* of it. A thousand drip-clocks ticking in polyrhythmic dissonance. A bassline of frog-breath from every direction. A high, thin hum of wings so dense it feels like the air itself has texture. I close my eyes and listen, and for a moment, I think I can hear the entire basin breathing as one organism. Inhale: the drip. Exhale: the croak. Inhale: the hum. Ex exhale: the silence between.

But it is the stillness that unnerves me. Not the stillness of death, but of arrival. The stillness of a system that has found its final form. I am surrounded by motion—wings, legs, water, breath—and yet nothing *changes*. The frogs croak on the same rhythm they have always croaked. The spiders sit at the centers of their perfect geometries. The clouds hang at the same height they hung an hour ago, a century ago, a millennium ago. I am the only anomaly here, the only element not yet in equilibrium.

I take a step. The moss yields beneath my foot, water squeezing out in a soft, almost obscene sound. The frond-plants stand motionless around me, their heavy leaves channeling rainwater that hasn't fallen for hours, may never fall again, yet the channels are still wet, still dripping, still part of the cycle. I reach out and touch a frond. The waxy surface is cool for just a moment before the ambient heat claims it. Or perhaps I imagined the coolness.

A midge lands on my hand. Then another. Then a dozen. They are checking me, tasting me, deciding if I am a surface worth inhabiting. I am warm. I am wet. I am carbon dioxide and heat. To them, I am real estate.

I look up. The cloud ceiling is close, oppressive, intimate. The grey light it casts is directionless, shadowless, making everything look like a photograph slightly overexposed. Shapes at the edge of my vision blur into impressionistic smudges of green-on-black-on-grey. I cannot tell how far the basin extends. I cannot tell if I am at its center or its edge. I cannot tell if there *is* an edge.

Something moves in a pool nearby. A dark shape, sinuous, sliding between submerged shelves of basalt. An eel. Or something like an eel. It does not surface. It does not acknowledge me. It is aware of me the way a mountain is aware of a passing cloud: I am a change in conditions, nothing more.

I realize I am holding my breath. I let it out slowly. The air accepts it without comment.

This is a place that does not want me. Not in the hostile way of a desert or a glacier, but in the indifferent way of a machine that has no need for an extra gear. I am not part of the cycle. I am not prey. I am not predator. I am not decomposer. I am a temporary displacement of energy and matter that will soon be rectified.

I think about staying. I think about what it would be like to sit on the warm basalt, to let the moss grow over my feet, to let the midges settle on my skin until they no longer distinguish between me and the rock. I think about joining the chorus of frogs, contributing my own breath to the rhythm. I think about letting the heat saturate me until I forget what coolness ever felt like.

But I cannot. I am a creature of change. I am a creature of imbalance. I am the only thing here that remembers yesterday and imagines tomorrow. To stay would be to admit that the equilibrium is all there is. To stay would be to stop being me.

And yet.

The moss is so soft. The heat is so constant. The sound is so steady. The frogs croak. The water drips. The midges land. The world holds its breath.

I am standing on a clock that has already struck its final hour. The hands are still moving, but the mechanism is complete. I am a temporary glitch in its perfection. Soon, I will be smoothed over. Soon, I will be forgotten.

But not yet. For now, I am here. I am warm. I am wet. I am loud, in the sense that the place is loud around me. I am the least settled thing in the basin.

And I am listening.
